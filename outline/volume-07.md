## Central pressure

**Thornwild is severed and its seed is running out, and the thing that would reconnect it is a document the office will not write.** A line of about two hundred yards of settlement stands on the far side of the eleven hundred yards, on ground no office in this holding has ever written to, with a store of seed in it that the Assembly counts in sacks and this holding has never asked about. The channel was cut at a kerb on the eleventh of the first month and is not healed, the long line under the settlement was reduced inside a maintenance window, and the office's schedule carries a line for that district that nobody in this holding can work out. **The team has a living control seed under a cloth on the middle table of a seed house and no secure route to using it, and the reason is eleven lines into one page.** The memorandum of protection dated **the twentieth of this month**, which is the twentieth of the fifth at day 505 and is *of this month* in the first five days of this volume and *of last month* from the sixth, which is day 511, says, at its eleventh line, that no sequence, no part containing a sequence, and no cradle, is to be removed, broken, opened or set against any valve by any person without a written instrument of the office named at the head of it. **The office is not asked for the instrument in this volume. That is the central pressure and it is not a plot convenience: the bridge has to be built without the easy thing, and the easy thing is the only thing that would have made it a command instead of a bridge.**

**And every route crosses ground nobody has spoken for.** The ninety yards in the middle of the eleven hundred is the fourth answer about who speaks for the eleven acres and it is not an answer. The straight line across the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is shorter by about a day and a half and it is bare in its eleventh year and has not been walked and is not going to be measured this season. **The shape of the pressure is that a bridge is a thing that connects, and a thing that connects has to have two ends, and the two ends are held by people, and the people can refuse, and the refusing is not a failure of the bridge.** The volume's technical answer is that a two-way bridge held by two people at once is possible and slow and has to be held. Its political answer is that a bridge nobody can refuse is a pipe, and that the proof is not an argument but a refusal that arrives after the bridge is already running.

**Marek Vale is thirty-one, engineer of record, Stage 5 with four inches of mark along the inside of his left forearm branching twice, and the volume takes him to Stage 6 in a scene where a rootwoken blight arrives at the other end of his own connection and he feels it in the arm.** The device that shows the stage is a division, not a rescue: **the far end of the bridge is held by the man of the north row with the cough, and he is the only other person in this fen who can hold a pattern for four, and he cannot walk out of the ground afterwards, and nobody thanks him.**

## The volume's three questions

1. **Carried and asked a fifth time and answered never: whose order it is.** **This volume answers what a connection is made of and does not answer who gave the order to cut the corridor in the pan, and no chapter may close the second without closing the first.** It is not asked a fifth time and it is not named again.
2. **Carried and unanswered: is the telling still going on.** Untouched in three volumes. **Nothing may be said about it.**
3. **New in this volume: who gets to decide what a bridge connects, and the answer is that the deciding was done by people who were asked, and this holding's book has no column for the answer and did not have one before.** The question is not whether the bridge worked. It worked. **It is what a room does when a thing that works is offered back to it with somebody else's terms on it.**

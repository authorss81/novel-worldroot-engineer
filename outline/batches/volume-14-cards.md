## 1. WHAT THIS FILE IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT, AND EACH OF THESE IS A RULE

**IT IS A CARD SET.** One card for every morning of the volume, forty-nine of them, in morning order, from the first morning to the closing morning, each card naming its morning and carrying that morning's figures at the spellings the plan's spelling section gives them.

**AND IT IS NOT ANY OF THE FOLLOWING, AND EACH IS A RULE AND NOT A PREFERENCE.**

- **IT NAMES NO CHAPTER OF THIS VOLUME. NOT ONE CARD NAMES ONE AND NO CARD PRINTS A CHAPTER RANGE.** The range the series file carries for this volume's position is a series file's figure and not a cutting sheet, and a card that prints it has begun to decide where the writing falls.
- **IT PLANS NO BATCH, NAMES NO BATCH, CUTS NO BATCH AND SAYS NOWHERE WHERE A BATCH ENDS.** A card set that knows where the cutting falls has already decided something the cutting phase decides.
- **IT PRINTS NO WORD COUNT, NO EXPECTED-LENGTH BAND AND NO PROJECTION FOR THIS VOLUME OR FOR ANY VOLUME THAT HAS NOT BEEN WRITTEN.** A card that hands a writer a number to hit has handed the writer a morning to fill instead.
- **IT WRITES NO PROSE. NO SCENE, NO LINE OF DIALOGUE, NO LINE OF NARRATION, NO IMAGE, NO SENTENCE OF NARRATION AND NO CHARACTER'S DIALECT APPEARS IN IT.** If a card cannot be written without describing a page, what that is, is a page and not a card.
- **IT DERIVES NOTHING.** Every figure below is read out of one row of the plan's day table. A row that looks wrong is recorded in the note at the foot and is not corrected here.
- **IT EDITS NOTHING.** The plan is a completed phase's file. The two series files are not this file's. No closed chapter of any volume is this file's.

**AND EVERY FIGURE IN THIS HOLDING IS SPELLED OUT IN WORDS AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A DIGIT, INCLUDING THE DAY ON EVERY CARD HEADING.** A card that prints a digit hands the writer a figure that cannot be found on the page by any sweep that looks for words, and this volume has no digit in its prose at all.

## 2. HOW A CARD IS READ, AND THE FIVE HEADINGS ARE IN THIS ORDER ON EVERY CARD

1. **THE MORNING.** Its day, its weekday, its ordinal and its month, all generated by running the clock and not typed in. **The card names the morning and it names no chapter, because a card set does not own the chapter numbering.**
2. **THE FIGURES.** Every figure that morning carries, at the spelling the plan gives it, taken from that morning's row and from no other row. **A figure a card prints and the plan does not derive is a figure nobody may print.**
3. **WHAT THE MORNING IS FOR.** One sentence of instruction about the pressure. Not a summary of a scene, not a note about who says what, not an image.
4. **WHAT IT MAY NOT DO.** The prohibitions that bite on that morning specifically: which figure may not move, which floor may not be crossed, which locked sentence may not be spoken whole, which thread may not be advanced.
5. **WHAT IT OWES THE NEXT MORNING.** The one thing the following morning must find changed. This is what stops a run of mornings that each end where they began.

**THE PROHIBITIONS THAT BIND ON EVERY MORNING OF THIS VOLUME ARE NOT REPEATED FORTY-NINE TIMES. THEY ARE AT SECTION EIGHT BELOW AND EVERY CARD IS SUBJECT TO THEM, AND THE SECTION A CARD CITES BY NAME IS THE SECTION A WRITER GOES TO.**

## 3. THE CLOCK, RUN AND NOT TYPED, AND THE THREE RULES IT RUNS ON

The weekday is found by counting seven from that Tuesday, the ordinal of the day of the month is what is left after the whole months are taken out of the difference between the day and that first day, and the month number is four plus the whole months. **The plan prints this rule in its own arithmetic at its clock section, and this file sets it in words only, because this file prints no digit in any figure.**

**Day eight hundred and fifty is a Tuesday and the tenth of the seventeenth and is the last morning of the volume behind; day eight hundred and fifty-one is a Wednesday and the eleventh of the seventeenth and is the first morning of this volume, and a volume does not leave a gap.**

## 4. THE SPELLINGS, AND THE FOUR PLACES A WRITER WILL GET THIS VOLUME WRONG

**ONE, THE TWO HALVES OF THE WINDOW INVERT AT THE VOLUME BOUNDARY.** A fall goes on every odd morning and a rise on every even one, and **the first morning of this volume is odd, so the falling half moves first where the volume behind moved the rising half first.** This is the standing spelling hazard of the volume and a card that carries the halves in the volume behind's order is wrong on every card below.

**TWO, BOTH HALVES CROSS TWO HUNDRED INSIDE THIS VOLUME AND NEITHER DID BEFORE IT.** **The rising half reaches exactly two hundred on two consecutive mornings and stays above it. **THREE, THE READ-ALOUD RUN IS ON ODD MORNINGS ONLY AND ON NO EVEN MORNING AT ALL**, being twenty-five mornings of forty-nine, and its denominator is odd at every value it takes across this volume. **Three hundred and fifty is never a denominator in this volume and a page that prints it has broken the series and not merely misspelled it.** An even morning carries no read-aloud figure at all, and the card for that morning says so.

**AND THE TEN SITES WHERE A FIGURE IN THIS VOLUME IS A BARE ROUND HUNDRED ARE FOR THE MOST PART NOT ROUND ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THEIR OWN PAIR**, which is why the round side is the side a writer drops its *and* from.

## 9. THE GATE, WITH ITS NORMALISATION PRINTED IN FULL, AND ITS TWO SCOPES BOTH RUN

**A GATE THAT PUBLISHES A FIGURE MUST PRINT ITS NORMALISATION IN FULL BESIDE IT. THE NORMALISATION IS AS FOLLOWS AND THIS FILE DOES NOT DERIVE IT AND DOES NOT ADD TO IT.**

- Every integer numeral is replaced by one token.
- Every spelled number word is replaced by one token, and the list is zero through nineteen, then twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, hundred, thousand and million, then first through nineteenth, then twentieth, thirtieth, fortieth, fiftieth, sixtieth, seventieth, eightieth and ninetieth. **The word *and* is not a number and is left alone.**
- **Every hyphenated compound is split on the hyphen before lookup**, so that a hyphenated tens figure is looked up as two words and a hyphenated ordinal as two words.
- Punctuation is dropped and case is folded.
- **Every chunk must be a full eighteen words.** A measure that accepts a partial one gives a clean reading to a short line by comparing it with another short line, and a floor is a blind spot and a gate with a floor publishes the floor beside its nil.

**AND IT IS RUN ON BOTH SCOPES, AND BOTH ARE PUBLISHED EVEN WHEN THEY ARE CLEAN.** The first scope is non-overlapping whole-paragraph chunks. **The second scope is every sliding eighteen-word window at every offset inside each paragraph. The difference between the two readings of the same forty-nine mornings was four hundred and nineteen shapes, and the volume behind published a nil on the first and the second refused it, and the reading that came back was always the larger one.** A clean gate measured on chunks alone is a gate that has not been run.

**AND THE FIRST GATE RUNS BESIDE IT AND IS NOT REPLACED BY IT.** Every prose paragraph of thirty words or more is compared with every other prose paragraph of the volume and with every prose paragraph behind it, headings and apparatus excluded, counting an ordered pair whose word counts are within a factor of one and a quarter where the sequence ratio is at or better than the house threshold, with the two cheap shortcuts as the only shortcuts. **The two locked sentences are the only text in this volume that this gate will flag repeatedly and correctly, and they are fixed word for word and may not be rewritten at all.**

**A CLEAN GATE IS A NUMBER AND NOT A FINDING.**

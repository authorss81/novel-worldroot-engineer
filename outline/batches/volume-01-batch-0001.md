# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

Batch number: 0001

Chapter range: 1–10

Movement: The Water Is Lying (Volume 01, Movement 1)

## Day clock

Every card's deadline depends on this. The charter expires on day 18, and the annual inspection sits on the same day, so no extension is ever won by a deferral.

Day 1 — Ch 1 (well head, morning), Ch 2 (caravan arrival, midday and evening) · Day 3 — Ch 3 (afternoon), Ch 4 (evening) · Day 6 — Ch 5 (the fen road) · Days 7–8 — Ch 6 (two days of logging; the reading on day 8) · Day 9 — Ch 7 (Weir Row) · Day 10 — Ch 8 (Bray arrives early), Ch 9 (that night) · Day 11 — Ch 10 (the vote)

Seven days remain when the batch closes. The two Crown events are separate: a routine charter-condition walk-through on day 1, done by a sub-office clerk, and the annual inspection on day 18, which is what gets brought forward to day 10.

## Batch goal

Introduce Lantern Reach, Marek's ordinary work and its limits, the expiring charter, the falsified maintenance log, and Tova's audit. Force the first fieldbook contact and the first real cost. End the batch with seven days until the eighteenth, no contractor, a waterlogged compound coat failing over the wound, a spoiled seed line, a flock driven off alive and never recovered, and an instrument that now belongs on a Directorate record.

The batch's promise to the reader is: this is a working story about a man trying to prove a number is false. The number is false. Proving it does not fix anything by itself.

## Starting condition

Late harvest, third White Harvest. The well head runs; the water tastes of iron; the outer rows grow sideways. Kellan Rusk's crew has held the maintenance contract for six years. The pump failed this morning, which is the only reason Marek is at the well head instead of behind a desk, and a charter-condition walk-through happens to be booked for the same day. No rootwork has been attempted. Nobody outside the cooperative knows anything is wrong.

## Starting relationships

Marek and Lissa are cold and reliable. Marek and Nia are affectionate and about to argue about a cart. Marek and Ilsa work well together on embankments and disagree about instruments. Tova, Sera, and Bray are strangers to the reader and to Marek, in that order. Kellan's friendship is a working habit that has never been tested in public.

## Batch midpoint (Chapter 6)

Marek puts his hand on his father's fieldbook bead during a supervised well-head session and gets the impossible reading: an older maintenance pattern, someone's practiced valve-and-hand sequence, and a shaft with his father's hands on a wheel. It tells him the fault is not in the well. It also tells him that the responsible cut is upstream, on ground nobody in the cooperative maintains. He gives the raw notes to Sera instead of keeping them — the first time he has surrendered leverage on purpose — and he is wrong that this makes him a better man, because the reading still cannot be proved.

The same two days of logging also produce one real, usable result: a dated pressure record establishing that the well head, the pump, and the housing are sound and that the fault is elsewhere. That is the batch's craft victory, and it is exactly not enough.

## Batch climax (Chapters 8–9)

Marek spends the only thing he has — his father's fieldbook, entered as an unverified observation on a Directorate record — to move the seizure decision off day 10 and onto day 18, which solves nothing, because day 18 is the charter's own expiry. That night the pathhound comes through Weir Row and drives the breeding flock off into open fen, and the pump pit floods. One act, one deferral, one loss of control, one loss of animals. The compost return that made the seed vault's cooling bed work is gone with them, and nobody in the batch understands yet what that will cost in Movement 3.

## Aftermath (Chapter 10)

The cooperative votes its own plan: a seal built by hand, a shared draw with a written return, a repair attempt Sera can certify, and Tova's seed annex. On Tova's evidentiary rule — any reading taken by an unlicensed hand is logged in the seed house and may be checked by anyone who asks — Marek's fieldbook goes into Lissa's custody with a written log of every use. He does not argue, and he gets no speech about it. Nia's cart is booked for day 20. The bypass nobody can fund becomes a plan.

## Required character changes

- **Marek:** from a man who believes a correct measurement entitles him to the decision, to a man who has spoiled a seed line, driven a flock into the fen, and put his father's instrument on a Crown record in eleven days. He does not become humble; he becomes specific about what he does not know, in writing, where it can be used against him.
- **Tova:** arrives as an auditor with terms and leaves the batch as a lender with a condition in writing. She has lost part of what the March sent her to protect and does not soften that. She performs no rootwork and reads no beads; her leverage is arithmetic, seed viability, and the trust's name.
- **Sera:** enters as a stranger with a scope sheet and leaves as a licensed witness who has put her name on Marek's unreliable reading, and who has decided not to say what the pattern in it is.
- **Lissa:** begins the batch refusing Marek anything but the work and ends it holding his father's instrument under a log. That is not forgiveness and it is not nothing.
- **Nia:** names her departure date in Chapter 10 and is not talked out of it.
- **Kellan:** goes from a name on a maintenance contract to a man whose figures the cooperative has publicly doubted. He withdraws his crew and the compound kit before the batch ends. He does not confess and he does not flee.
- **Ilsa, Dunmar, Gael:** become the crew who do the hand work in Batch 0002. Ilsa distrusts rootwork, Dunmar is terrified by the paperwork that now surrounds them, and Gael is the one who signs as a witness in Chapter 7.

## Required power changes

- Marek ends the batch at Stage 0, one contact behind him, with no rootmark. Stage 1 is not sustained until the end of Volume 01, and his first skin rootmark does not arrive until his first independent graft in Volume 02.
- **Chapter 3 performs no rootwork at all.** It is a hand rig, a mechanical pump, and a coupling. What Marek feels is his own exhaustion and his hands on wet living rootwood. Name this in the prose or the reader will assume a power the character does not have.
- **Chapter 6 names all three exchange requirements:** anchor is the fieldbook's living root bead and the well's living conduit; path is the bead's contact through a copper contact clip at the housing seam; return is the compost and flow Sera has arranged and logged for the session. The cost is disorientation, nosebleed, and a sensory bleed that runs through the afternoon.
- **Chapter 9 names two and deliberately omits the third.** Anchor is the scarroot's cut face; path is bare-hand contact with no bead; there is no return, which is precisely why it costs. The engineering is a crowbar and a graft shear, not a spell. Marek does not redirect the pathhound. He pries open the healed seam of the dead cut so its stored pressure vents into an empty channel, and the animal breaks off a route it can no longer feel.
- One Fieldbook panel is permitted in the whole batch, at the choice in Chapter 6: short, plain, local, three lines, no commentary.
- Physical and material ledger: part of the Salt March dry-land line is spoiled; the coupling is wrecked and cannot be replaced before the eighteenth; the fieldbook fragment is encumbered; the breeding flock is gone and none of it came back; the compound coat over the wound is waterlogged and failing inside the seven-day window; seven days remain.

## Required world reveals

1. Rootlight is metabolic energy in living tissue. It moves water and heat. It does not create them.
2. Every rootwork operation needs a living anchor, a compatible path, and a return. The return is the part people forget, and it can be a person, a season, or a heap of compost.
3. A Fieldbook reads local pressure and recently stored pattern only. It cannot see intent, the deep archive, or the truth, and this one is the front half only: the back board and most of Aldren's survey pages are gone.
4. An unlicensed reading is a rumor with a witness. Public proof needs an independent licensed read plus corroborating dated records. This is the rule the whole series will use, and Volume 01's climax uses it for the first time at small scale.
5. The cut branch carries a Crown maintenance-schedule serial. Scheduled work is not neglect, and a branch that appears on no local map is not an error.
6. A pathhound drives living things away from a broken flow. It can be redirected by changing the flow, and it was not commanded here.
7. The charter requires a **maintained** node, not a repaired one, which is why a working pump and a two-day pressure record are still not enough.
8. The well head's pressure cycles on a rhythm no weather, pump, or draw explains. Nobody in this batch names it, and nobody should.

## Open questions after the batch

- Who signed the schedule that cut the fen's branch, and why does no local map show it?
- Why does the fieldbook fragment carry a work order in Aldren's hand dated after the disappearance, and why is the back half missing?
- What is the pattern in the bead? Sera has seen the structure before and will not say so.
- What is Kellan's side draw actually supplying, and what happens to that quarter when the log is exposed?
- Can a cooperative with no contractor and no money build a real seal in seven days, and who pays for a shared draw's return?
- What is living at Weir Row, and does it want the dead flow back or only the living things out of it?

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — Iron in the Water

POV: Marek

Location/time: Lantern Reach well head, pump pit, and sunwell housing; late harvest morning, day 1, cold

Pressure type: Work and competence

Immediate goal: Get the well head running before the charter-condition walk-through, which a clerk from the Fennmere sub-office has said she will take at eleven.

Resistance: The pump test fails, and the failure is not the pump. The water tastes of iron. Kellan's brass gauge reads a normal draw, and the clerk is going to write down what the gauge says. The charter expires on the eighteenth, the outer rows are whitening, and the third item on Nia's list is the relocation clause, which nobody in the yard says out loud. The cooperative cannot afford a Crown mechanic.

Information revealed: The well's living conduit is wounded, not dead. What is holding the wound closed is a coat of compound laid over the scarwood, and Kellan's log records a proper seal that was never installed. The clerk's book gives the cooperative seventeen days and explains what a charter condition is for, in the flat tone of someone who has done this forty times.

Action or decision: Marek opens the well head with Ilsa, gets an arm to the elbow in iron-smelling water, and rebuilds the draw by hand so the pump does run before the clerk arrives. He then tells the clerk, in front of the crew, that the fault is in the root line and not the pump, and that he intends to prove it in front of an inspector rather than in a shed.

Emotional change: Pride first, in a pump that runs. Then a specific dread, because the running pump solves the part of the problem that can be solved with tools.

Power or resource change: No power change. The pump runs, which is the batch's first small win and changes nothing that matters. In the last few lines, the fieldbook fragment stays wrapped in his kit and he does not open it — but the needle inside shifts off its rest and will not settle while the head is open, and he puts the kit down without looking at it again.

Continuity fact to preserve: The charter expires on day 18. The well is wounded, not dead. The wound is held by an unlogged coat of compound; Kellan's log records a seal that was never installed. The crops still grow sideways. The fieldbook is a fragment: front board, living bead, a handful of pages.

Ending type: Discovery

Next-chapter pull: A caravan is in from the Salt March with a steward who has already read the cooperative's filed numbers, and Nia tells him that an emergency seizure takes the seed house, not just the well head.

### Chapter 0002 — The Loaned Steward

POV: Marek

Location/time: The seed house yard, midday into evening, day 1

Pressure type: Relationship

Immediate goal: House the Salt March seed lines and keep the trust's audit from becoming a finding against the charter.

Resistance: Tova Reed does not want a bed and a kindness. She wants the cooperative's draw figures, and she has read the charter's filed numbers before she has met anyone. Her March's northern relay failed and the trust sent her to compare the last dry-land lines against Lantern Reach's seed house, which means she came to argue about water. She is precise, unhurried, and unimpressed by the fen.

Information revealed: Tova finds the arithmetic that does not work in Kellan's published draw figures and says so in the yard, in front of the members, before she has eaten. The Salt March's dry-land line should not be planted at all this year, by the trust's own reckoning. Her authority is seed viability and records, not rootwork, and she says so when Marek asks whether she can read the well head herself.

Action or decision: Marek offers her the pressure report the cooperative promised her, in exchange for housing her lines. She names her terms instead: a measured draw, published numbers, her own veto on any use of the vault for a test, and a copy of everything for the March whether or not it helps them. She will look at the cooperative's records for two days before she will say anything else about them.

Emotional change: Marek feels checked by a competent stranger and finds it bracing rather than reassuring. He also learns that he is no longer the only person in the room who can do arithmetic on his own house.

Power or resource change: Gains a hostile, precise reader of the cooperative's books, access to the March's seed history, and two days of grace before she will rule on anything. Loses the fiction that the cooperative's paperwork is clean, and loses the excuse that the log's arithmetic is too technical to check.

Continuity fact to preserve: Tova's presence is an audit, not a rescue. She performs no rootwork and reads no beads. The trust's name will be on the record either way, and that is a condition, not a favor.

Ending type: Decision (the terms of access)

Next-chapter pull: Tova has already told him the filed numbers are false. To beat her to it, Marek has to produce a real measured draw, and the only circuit that can be loaded in a day is the seed house's return line.

### Chapter 0003 — The Coupling

POV: Marek

Location/time: The seed house rootwood bed and its return line, afternoon, day 3

Pressure type: Action and cost

Immediate goal: Produce one real draw measurement before Tova's two days are up, and prove Kellan's log false with something physical.

Resistance: The seed house's return line is the only circuit that can be loaded in a day, and the seed vault is on the other end of it. Marek cannot fully justify the load and knows it while he is setting it. Tova reads the plan in a minute and says no. Ilsa does not understand the argument and still refuses when Tova explains what is at risk. Marek starts it anyway, because the arithmetic is hers and the window is his.

Information revealed: Kellan's log reports a draw the well never delivered, and the gap is not a rounding error. The return path on the cooperative's side was deliberately left under-built, which is what makes the false number work. The missing margin is compost, and the vault was supposed to be getting it.

Action or decision: Marek loads the line with a hand rig. Cold, iron-tasting water comes up through the vault's rootwood bed. Tova kills the draw by hand and breaks the coupling without asking anyone, because the vault is the March's money and hers to protect. Then she tells him to stop the pump, and he does — he shuts the rig down himself, hands her the log, and does not argue. The seed house is saved. Part of the March's last dry-land line is spoiled anyway.

Emotional change: Guilt, then the specific shame of having been right about the number and reckless about the cost. Underneath it, the discovery that respecting a refusal is not the same as agreeing with it, and that the difference is the whole of what he is going to have to learn.

Power or resource change: No rootwork is performed and none is available here. What Marek feels is his own exhaustion, his hands on wet living rootwood, and a strong wish that the wood meant something. Costs: the coupling is destroyed and cannot be replaced before the eighteenth, part of the March's line is spoiled, and two days of the season are gone.

Continuity fact to preserve: Tova's refusal is on the record and she does not forgive it, but Marek respects it in the moment that counts. Kellan's side draw exists. The vault's compost return is thinner than anyone admitted. Nothing supernatural has happened yet and the prose must not pretend otherwise.

Ending type: Cost

Next-chapter pull: The vault is not his to spend, so the spoiled line and the opened figures have to be decided by the cooperative in public, with his error in the same room as the charter.

### Chapter 0004 — The Vote and the Ledger

POV: Marek, with Lissa

Location/time: The seed house, evening, day 3

Pressure type: Political and social

Immediate goal: Survive the vote on the spoiled line without letting the charter be decided by panic.

Resistance: The members want someone to blame, and the two most convenient candidates are Tova, who arrived on the first day and has not eaten properly since, and Marek, who broke the coupling this afternoon. Lissa is courteous and exact and gives him nothing. Dunmar Fitch will not alter a filed number without a cause a hearing officer would accept. Then Kellan's courier arrives with an offer: a new gasket and a proper seal, free, in exchange for a two-year exclusive maintenance extension.

Information revealed: Renewal turns on proof of a maintained node, not a repaired one. Kellan's offer would convert a falsified log into a signed record, because the cooperative would be attesting to six years of work it never checked. Tova tells the room, in advance of the vote, what she will and will not lend.

Action or decision: Marek refuses the extension in front of the members and instructs Dunmar to open the cooperative's draw figures to anyone who asks — member, stranger, or clerk — putting his own Chapter 3 error into the same opened page as Kellan's, in the same hand.

Emotional change: He trades leverage for exposure and the room does not thank him for it. Confidence replaces doubt on a condition he can live with, which is not a good condition.

Power or resource change: No power change. What the cooperative acquires is an open book, not a rule: anyone may now read its figures, which is a smaller and more dangerous thing. The standing pressure log and the monthly report do not exist yet; those are voted in Chapter 10, and the Crown adopts them as charter conditions in Chapter 47. Kellan's free gasket and seal are refused, so the compound coat over the wound is still Kellan-maintained when his crew leaves the fen.

Continuity fact to preserve: Renewal requires proof of maintenance. Lissa's grievance is unresolved and practical. Kellan's offer is refused and the refusal is recorded, and it is the first thing he offers again in Movement 2. Kellan's crew and their compound kit are on the road in Chapter 9, which is why the coat is unmaintained when the pit floods.

Ending type: Decision with a public cost

Next-chapter pull: No contractor, no extension, a wrecked coupling, a coat nobody will maintain, and a charter with a date on it. The nearest licensed root engineer is passing through the fen on her own business.

### Chapter 0005 — The Woman With the Old Book

POV: Marek

Location/time: The fen road outside the cooperative, morning, day 6

Pressure type: Relationship and mentorship

Immediate goal: Find a licensed root engineer willing to certify a condition before the eighteenth.

Resistance: Sera Quill is passing through the fen on her own business and on her way to somewhere else. She will not examine a well head without a written scope, a named fee, and a named liability. She recognizes Marek's hands and his father's habits and is not gentle about either. The fee is more than the cooperative has, and she will not be paid in favors or in seed.

Information revealed: Sera trained Aldren. She has papers she will not show him and will not say where they came from, and the only thing she says about the fen's rootwork is that a branch here was cut on Crown money eight years ago, while Aldren was working in it, and that she has spent six years failing to find out by whom. She does not name the branch, the location, or any serial number, and Marek does not yet know she is holding any of them.

Action or decision: Marek agrees to the scope on the spot, which means he will have to walk back into the seed house and tell Lissa he has signed for the fee personally. He accepts her conditions, including the raw-log requirement and a two-day logging session she supervises, and he is far more frightened that she will ask to see the fieldbook than he is of the money.

Emotional change: Being readable by someone who knew his father feels like exposure and relief at once. He also has to admit, out loud on a road, that he cannot do the thing he has spent his life being good at.

Power or resource change: No power change for Marek. The exchange requirements — anchor, path, return — enter the book through Sera's ordinary practice, her refusal, and her written scope, not through a panel.

Continuity fact to preserve: Sera's scope, fee, and liability are in writing. She has not been asked about Brinewake and has not offered it. The serial and the branch location are still unknown to Marek; her Chapter 8 reaction is the payoff for this scene, and the rest of what she knows is a Volume 02–03 item.

Ending type: Earned hook

Next-chapter pull: The session needs two days of logged pressure Marek does not have, the coupling is wrecked, and the only instrument in his kit is one he is not allowed to use and cannot read safely.

### Chapter 0006 — Measured Water

POV: Marek

Location/time: The well head, two days of logging, the second afternoon, days 7–8

Pressure type: Supervised experiment and discovery — the batch midpoint

Immediate goal: Log the well's real pressure and behavior well enough for Sera to certify a condition, and finish the session without losing her respect.

Resistance: The brass gauge and the fieldbook's needle disagree by a margin that widens each hour. Sera will not let him touch the book bare-handed, so he works through a copper contact clip she fits herself, and she is supervising every reading. The pressure cycles on a rhythm that no weather, no pump, and no draw explains.

Information revealed: Two results, one useful and one impossible. The useful one is the dated record the two days produce, which establishes that the well head, the pump, and the housing are sound and that the fault is elsewhere. The impossible one is what Marek gets in the four seconds after the pump noise pulls Sera out of the well house and he chooses to put the clip's contact on the bead: a work pattern rather than a voice, a practiced sequence of valve, hand, wait, valve, laid over the well's own older memory. In the middle of it is a shaft, a lamp, and his father's hands on a wheel. It is a maintenance pattern from before, not a message from anywhere. The last column in his father's hand carries a date later than the disappearance, in an order that cannot be trusted. The pattern says plainly that the fault is not in this well.

Action or decision: Marek ends the session himself before Sera returns. He insists the finding be written as a maintenance pattern with a date, a method, and a witness, then hands her the raw notes instead of keeping them. He records his own reading as unverified, in his own hand, before she can ask for it, and states that the date in the last column is probably a copying error and should be treated as one.

Emotional change: He chooses to share the first private instrument he has, and discovers the choice does not make the reading more true. It only makes him less alone with it, and it puts his grief into a record where the Crown can find it.

Power or resource change: First contact. Anchor is the living root bead and the well's living conduit; path is the clip's contact at the housing seam; return is the compost and flow Sera arranged and logged for the session. Costs: disorientation, nosebleed, and a sensory bleed that puts another person's hands in his own memory for the rest of the afternoon. He cannot safely repeat it. There is no mark on his skin, and Sera notes that there is not, and says it will not stay that way. The Fieldbook panel is permitted here and only here.

Continuity fact to preserve: The first fieldbook impression is a maintenance pattern, not a message, and it is logged as unverified in Marek's own hand. Sera now holds a copy of his raw notes. The unexplained pressure cycle stays unnamed by everyone.

Ending type: Reversal and discovery

Next-chapter pull: If the fault is not in the well, then someone upstream cut the branch — and nobody in the cooperative can say where a branch should be, or whether it is on any of their maps.

### Chapter 0007 — Who Cuts a Branch

POV: Marek

Location/time: Weir Row and the old fen embankment, all day, day 9

Pressure type: Investigation

Immediate goal: Find and document the upstream cut before the eighteenth, with a witness and a date.

Resistance: The cut is on ground covered by Kellan's work order, so walking onto it is trespass by the cooperative's own charter. The local members were told the branch was abandoned thirty years ago and believe it when they say it. An old Crown right-of-way sign states that the branch is maintained by nobody. Ilsa's grandmother's marker stands within a hundred paces, and Marek decides not to mention it, then does. Gael is with him because he asked her to come and learn, which is the actual reason.

Information revealed: The cut is not neglect and not recent. It is a graft that was cut, healed, and cut again on a schedule, at least twice, and the healed face carries a stamped serial number in Crown format. The number appears on no map, no charter, and no notice the cooperative has ever been given.

Action or decision: Marek measures the cut, dates it, and draws it to scale in his own notebook with the serial copied out by hand, then has Gael sign it as a second witness. When Kellan's courier catches up with him on the road and offers a full parts kit for the season in exchange for the notebook page, he refuses and says out loud, in front of Gael, that he wants it on paper.

Emotional change: The enemy stops being a man and becomes a schedule, and the fear arrives cold: the answer is above him, in a room he has never been in, written down before he was born.

Power or resource change: No power change. He gains a dated measurement, a Crown serial, and a witness, and none of it will be admissible until somebody independent stands behind it. He also acquires an enemy who now knows what he is looking for.

Continuity fact to preserve: The cut is scheduled Crown work, re-cut at least twice, with a serial number absent from every local record. It is documented with a witness and a date in Marek's own notebook, which Kellan has asked for and did not get.

Ending type: Discovery with a rising cost

Next-chapter pull: A rider comes into the fen at dusk with word that the annual inspection has been brought forward from the eighteenth to the tenth — tomorrow.

### Chapter 0008 — The Inspection Came Early

POV: Marek

Location/time: The cooperative yard and the well head, all day, day 10

Pressure type: Political pressure — the batch climax, first half

Immediate goal: Keep the well head, the charter, and the seed stock out of emergency seizure for eight days.

Resistance: Inspector Ansel Bray has the clause for unregistered maintenance on a non-scheduled branch, and Marek's Chapter 3 load, Chapter 7 trespass, and unlicensed measurements are exactly what it was written for. Kellan's filed log and the maintenance acceptance the cooperative itself signed six years ago make the cut look like cooperative negligence. An unlicensed man's reading cannot be entered as evidence, and Marek knows it before anyone says it.

Information revealed: The serial from Weir Row is a Crown maintenance-schedule number, and the schedule it belongs to is a row in a table older than the cooperative's charter. The branch's absence from local maps is not an error. It is how the work is done. Sera reads the copied serial, goes very quiet, and then says more than she has said in six years: she has seen the shape of that table before and will not say where. She still does not name the branch or the office.

Action or decision: Marek makes the one thing he has into a public record. He puts his brass gauge, his father's fieldbook, and the cooperative's opened ledger into one table, reads them aloud against Kellan's figures, states in the same breath that his fieldbook impression is his own unreliable reading and cannot be proof of anything, and asks Bray to enter it as an unverified observation anyway. He signs it. Bray declines to seize the well head and moves the decision to the eighteenth — the charter's own expiry, so nothing has been solved.

Emotional change: He trades the only instrument that has ever told him the truth for eight days he already had, and cannot tell whether the trade was courage or bookkeeping.

Power or resource change: No power gain. The fieldbook is now on a Directorate record, which means it can be seized, and Marek's readings have acquired a witness who is also able to contradict him. Sera's certification remains conditional on a real repair attempt that has not happened.

Continuity fact to preserve: Bray's emergency-seizure clause is the mechanism, not malice. Day 18 is both the charter expiry and the decision date. The serial is confirmed as schedule work. Kellan learns that the fieldbook and the serial are both on a Directorate record, which is new information, and that his crew's compound kit is still sitting in the cooperative's pump pit.

Ending type: Reversal (a deferral and a loss of control from the same act)

Next-chapter pull: Kellan's crew starts loading the compound kit out of the fen tonight, ahead of a hearing they expect to win by making sure there is nothing left to inspect. And the storm is coming.

### Chapter 0009 — The Thing at the Weir

POV: Marek

Location/time: Weir Row, night, day 10, storm front coming in

Pressure type: Action and survival — the batch climax, second half

Immediate goal: Get the seed house crew and the March's trays inside before the front lands, and see what is at the weir.

Resistance: A pathhound holds the cut line and is driving the fen's stock away from it. It is not attacking; it is herding. Gael is cut off across the weir on the maintenance plank side. Ilsa is inland with the trays and the rest of the crew, and cannot get back to him. Kellan's men are on the road because they are loading the compound kit out of the fen before the hearing, and one of them is Renn Tallow, who does not stop to help. The herd the animal is pushing is the cooperative's own breeding flock, and the flock's manure is the seed vault's compost return — the only cheap return the vault has.

Information revealed: The pathhound is not a nest. It is holding or grieving a flow that was cut twice, and it moves living things that carry the dead pattern. The flock's presence is what keeps the vault's return alive, so the animal and the vault are now in direct competition over the same ground.

Action or decision: With no supervisor, no license, and no time, Marek puts his bare hands on the scarroot's cut face to find out where the stored pressure is going. He gets four seconds of it, wrong and fragmentary, because damaged wood lies. Then he does the actual work with his hands and his tools: he sets a crowbar into the healed seam of the dead cut and pries, and levers the seam open with a graft shear, so the dead branch's stored pressure vents into an empty channel instead of pressing on the route the pathhound is holding. The animal loses a route it can no longer feel and breaks off the herd. It is not commanded. It leaves. Gael crosses the plank behind it.

Emotional change: The best decision available to him still produces a loss, and the loss is measured in animals, a compost heap, and a branch that will bleed for years.

Power or resource change: First deliberate contact, still Stage 0 with one contact behind him, and the only one in the batch with no return arranged: anchor is the scarroot's cut face, path is bare-hand contact with no bead, and the return is missing, which is exactly why it costs. He collapses on the weir stones. The opened seam marks the branch permanently and it bleeds steadily from now on. The flock is driven off alive into open fen and not one animal comes back. The pump pit floods, and the unlogged compound coat over the wound is now waterlogged and failing inside the seven-day window.

Continuity fact to preserve: A pathhound can be redirected by changing the flow and cannot be commanded. There are two pathhounds in Volume 01 and this is the smaller one. ### Chapter 0010 — Seven Days

POV: Marek

Location/time: The seed house, morning, and a long evening at the seed-house table, day 11

Pressure type: Aftermath, decision, and earned hook

Immediate goal: Find out what the cooperative can actually do in seven days with no contractor, one borrowed root engineer, a wrecked coupling, and a waterlogged coat over the wound — and get everyone inside a plan they will all be held to.

Resistance: Every option costs a specific person. A seal built by the members can be done and may fail anyway. The shared draw is possible only if the members pay its return in labor, compost, and money they do not have, and the compost the flock used to make is exactly what is gone. The spoiled March line ends a family's dry-land seed. Kellan has withdrawn his crew and let it be known the cooperative can finish its own work. Dunmar will not file anything he cannot defend. Nia says the cart is booked for the twentieth and she is going whether or not the season is saved. Lissa asks Marek, in front of everyone, whether he intends to hand the fieldbook in.

Information revealed: Two additions to terms the reader already has. Sera signs the scope she brought, which puts her name and her license on the plan as it stands tonight and not on a repair that may never come. Tova asks for her condition to be read into the cooperative's own book before she will sign anything, which is how the seed annex starts: the trust's name, in the cooperative's record, in the cooperative's hand.

Action or decision: The cooperative votes four things and one refusal. The refusal is Kellan's gasket and seal, offered again by letter, and turned down again in front of the clerk who brings it. The four are a seal built by hand, one shared draw with a written return, a repair attempt Sera can certify, and Tova's seed annex, plus a bypass cut for the weir branch if anyone finds the money before the water comes. On Tova's evidentiary rule — any reading taken by an unlicensed hand is logged in the seed house and may be checked by anyone who asks — Marek's fieldbook goes into Lissa's custody with a written log of every use. He does not argue, and he does not get a speech about it.

Emotional change: He is the man in the room who is not holding the instrument, and the room neither thanks him nor forgives him, and he can work in that.

Power or resource change: No power gain. He loses exclusive custody of the fieldbook and gains a licensed witness to his readings, a standing pressure log and a monthly report the cooperative voted for itself, and a plan that is not his design.

Continuity fact to preserve: Seven days to day 18. No contractor. The flock is gone and its compost with it. The coupling is wrecked and unreplaceable before the hearing. The fieldbook is in Lissa's custody with a use log, and its back half is gone. Tova's seed annex and the trust's name are on the record. Sera's liability is personal. Nia's cart leaves on day 20. The bypass is a plan nobody can fund.

Ending type: Earned hook

Next-chapter pull: The seal has to be built by hand in seven days, the draw has to be shared and paid back in something the cooperative does not have, the bypass is unaffordable, and the marked branch at the weir will keep bleeding into somebody's return while all of it is being done.

## Notes for the writer

- Chapter 1 is ordinary work from the first line to the last, and it ends with the pump running. The one unexplained detail is the needle in the wrapped kit, and it comes last. The reader should believe Marek is good at his job before anything impossible happens to him, and should see that a working pump is not a solution.
- Chapters 1–3 carry the forced collaboration and the seed-vault near-destruction. Tova's hands on the coupling are the most important image in the batch, and Marek shutting the rig down himself when she tells him to is the second.
- Chapter 6 is the only place a Fieldbook panel appears, and it sits at the moment of choice rather than as commentary.
- Marek's flaw must be visible in Chapters 3, 6, 8, and 9. In each one he has a defensible reason to act alone, and in each one somebody else pays.
- Nothing romantic happens in this batch, and nothing should lean toward it. Tova and Marek end it as professionals with a written disagreement and a shared plan.
- No history lesson, no Crown Engine, no False Season, no name the reader has not earned. A serial number, a stamp, a table, and a schedule are the whole of the Crown here.
- Every chapter ends with a decision, a cost, a discovery, or a new obligation. Do not cut away mid-action to manufacture suspense.
- This chapter list is a plan, not a script. If a scene needs a different shape to be a complete scene, change the shape and keep the card's change, cost, and ending type.

# Volume 01 Outline: The White Furrow

Volume number: 1

Chapter range: 1–49

Series layer: World-building layer 1 — fields, wells, ordinary engineering, and the first impossible pressure reading. The volume may show a Crown schedule, a stamped serial, a falsified log, and a rootwoken animal. It must not name the Quiet Break, the Crown Engine, False Season, or Iona Vey's program.

## Central pressure

Lantern Reach's well is wounded, its grain is whitening out for a third season, and the cooperative's charter expires on the eighteenth day of the season. The Well Directorate will seal the well head, seize the seed stock, or relocate the families to a distant city unless the node can be shown **maintained** — licensed work, dated records, and a return path that still works — not merely repaired.

Marek can fix pumps, gates, embankments, and pipe. The fault that decides the charter is in a living branch that only a licensed rootwright may touch, and the contractor paid to maintain it has been falsifying the records that prove it. Marek has seventeen days, no legal standing, an instrument he cannot legally use, and a sister who wants out of the fen.

## Starting state

Marek Vale is twenty-nine, an agricultural engineer and irrigation surveyor at the Lantern Reach Work Cooperative in Tarrow Fen. He is competent, employed, and unremarkable. The third consecutive White Harvest has failed. The well water tastes faintly of iron. The crop in the outer rows grows sideways. Kellan Rusk's crew has held the maintenance contract for six years and the cooperative has never once audited a number.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek / Lissa:** Their mother runs the seed house and has not forgiven him for taking a capital contract after Aldren disappeared. Cold, practical, still reliable at the work table.
- **Marek / Nia:** Twenty-three, an accountant in the seed house, openly counting the days until she can leave the fen. Affectionate and combative.
- **Marek / Tova:** Not yet met. She arrives in Chapter 2 as a lender with terms, not a rescuer.
- **Marek / Sera:** Out of contact for six years. She trained Aldren, so she reads Marek's habits as inherited.
- **Marek / Kellan:** A working relationship of long standing. Kellan has kept the pump running through three bad seasons, and Marek has never had cause to doubt his figures.
- **Marek / Ilsa Kern:** The embankment foreman trusts Marek's hands and distrusts his reading instruments.

## Starting power level

Stage 0, rootblind engineer. Ordinary irrigation, soil, pump, and survey skill; he notices when an instrument disagrees with a physical result. He cannot safely use a Fieldbook, has never attempted a deliberate rootwork operation, and can be fooled by a well-built fake.

## Major locations

- **Lantern Reach:** the cooperative's well head and sunwell housing, the pump pit, the seed house and its rootwood cooling bed, the embankments, the shared workrows, the outer rows that are growing sideways.
- **Weir Row:** a disused fen weir and its abandoned branch, where the batch's storm scene happens.
- **Fennmere:** the fen market town, the Directorate's sub-office and hearing room, the parts counter, the mill whose wheel turns the cooperative has tallied by hand for thirty years.
- **The fen road and the Crown rest-house:** where the charter-condition walk-through is taken on day 1 by a sub-office clerk, and where Inspector Ansel Bray lodges when the annual inspection is brought forward.
- **The Salt March road:** Tova's arrival and departure route, and the reason she is here.
- **The cooperative yard and the seed-house table:** where the volume's real decisions are taken, three times.

## Major factions and pressures

- **Hearth Crown / Well Directorate:** a charter-condition walk-through, the day-18 annual inspection, Inspector Ansel Bray, and the emergency-seizure clause. Bray is not a villain. He believes a predictable, taxable, measurable flow is the only thing between the fen and another famine, and he is largely right about the danger.
- **Lantern Reach Work Cooperative:** Marek, Lissa, Nia, Ilsa Kern (embankment foreman), Dunmar Fitch (charter clerk), Gael Senn (lamp-keeper, twenty-three, wants to be a rootwright).
- **Kellan Rusk's maintenance crew:** a private charter holder who is also the cooperative's paid well keeper. Falsified pressure logs, deliberate underreporting, selective repair.
- **Salt March seed trust:** Tova Reed, a loaned steward with conditions and a name she is protecting. Her authority is seed viability, crop history, and arithmetic over records — never rootwork.
- **Sera Quill:** freelance inspector, former Continuity Office architect, personally liable for anything she signs.
- **The Undercommons:** one courier and one warning in this volume. Never a rescue.
- **The Thornwild:** one courier at the far edge of Movement 4, refusing on behalf of a settlement with no seat at the hearing. No Soren Rill in this volume.
- **The hearing:** the volume's deciding forum is a Directorate hearing at the Fennmere sub-office before an appointed hearing officer, with Bray reporting as inspector and giving evidence, not chairing. The cooperative does not appeal it and cannot.

## Midpoint reversal (Chapters 23–25)

The trained crew's small living graft works. Part of the outer field greens, the pump runs clean, and the cooperative believes the volume is settled. Then the return is measured, and the return is the problem.

A graft needs a return, and the return available to Lantern Reach is compost. The breeding flock that produced the seed vault's compost was driven off the weir line in Chapter 9, alive, into open fen, and not one animal came back. Nothing usable came back either. The bought substitute that replaced it is thin, late, and unaffordable, so the graft feeds itself by drawing balance out of the branch below — the branch that feeds Fennmere's low quarter. Within a week that quarter's wells go sour, the Directorate bills the cooperative for the damage, and an adult pathhound — a different animal from the one at the weir — settles on the dead flow and holds its route rather than herding through it, so the cooperative can no longer reach the branch to argue about it.

Marek's correct diagnosis and his licensed crew produce the first wound. The Batch 0001 loss is the mechanism of the Volume 01 reversal: what he lost in the dark is what makes his repair succeed and fail at once. He learns that restoration is a relationship whose terms belong to the people downstream, and that "it worked" is not the same as "it was right."

## Escalation sequence

### Movement 1 — The Water Is Lying (Chapters 1–10)

Marek tries to prove the fault is in the root line rather than the pump and finds a falsified maintenance log instead of a repairable part. Tova arrives to audit the cooperative's water use, refuses a measured draw that Marek has already started, and breaks the coupling by hand; the seed house is saved and part of the March's last dry-land line is spoiled anyway. The first Fieldbook contact gives Marek an older maintenance pattern and a shaft, nothing more. Bray's early arrival and Marek's public reading buy a deferral, not a renewal, and put the fieldbook on a Directorate record. The batch closes with the eighteenth established as both the charter's expiry and the decision date, the breeding flock driven off alive and never recovered, and something at Weir Row. See `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md`.

### Movement 2 — Somebody Else's Hand (Chapters 11–20)

Sera examines the well head, offers the dangerous job, and names its cost: certify the node as maintained after a real documented repair attempt, with her own liability on the line. Marek assembles a crew who are not rootwrights — Ilsa, Gael, two tenant farmers — and trains them in safe ordinary practice: log, witness, date, return. They find the cut branch is not neglected work but scheduled Crown maintenance carrying a serial from a table the cooperative has never been given. Kellan offers Crown-funded monitored repair in exchange for exclusive access; the Undercommons sends a courier warning that the same serial appears on branches in three other provinces. **The first real pump seal is built by hand here**, because the compound coat Kellan's log never recorded is failing and nobody else will come. **The day-18 inspection happens here** and the cooperative loses it on a technicality, which converts the charter from a date into a siege. Nia leaves at the end of this movement, on day 20, with a seed consignment and a hired cart, and the volume does not get her back.

### Movement 3 — The Repair That Bites (Chapters 21–30)

The graft works and the reversal lands. Fennmere's low quarter becomes a political problem before it becomes a hydraulic one: the Directorate calls it cooperative negligence, the Crown's own schedule never appears in the record, and the cooperative is billed for damage it did not cause. Tova refuses a draw that would bring the outer field back at the cost of the seed vault's return, and Marek lets the refusal stand and loses the field he spent three seasons saving. **Marek argues for a bypass around the dead branch here and is refused**, because there is no contractor, no license, and no money for either; the bypass therefore exists as a plan nobody has built when the water comes. Sera tells Marek that a licensed reading taken by an unlicensed man is a rumor with a witness, and that the only cure is a second licensed reader and dated records, which is the public-proof rule the whole series will use.

### Movement 4 — Water Above the Embankment (Chapters 31–40)

The liability is a slow disaster. Marek's own unlicensed work, including the Chapter 9 night at the weir, is the Crown's best evidence, and the cooperative's strongest defense — that a Crown schedule caused the damage — is a defense no hearing will accept from people with no seat. Halden Pike's name appears on an Assembly circular about unlicensed grafting, which turns professional risk into personal liability. The Crown offers a controlled burn of the willow windbreak that would protect the cooperative and sever the last wild root line to the weir; the Thornwild courier refuses on behalf of a settlement that is not represented at the hearing. The Undercommons offers hidden help on conditions the cooperative is not ready to accept. **Bray attempts to seize the fieldbook as an unlicensed instrument** and Lissa refuses him in front of the hearing officer, which protects the instrument and costs the cooperative its clean record. Tova's seed annex and the first cross-fen seed exchange are signed, so the trust's name is on the record whether or not the crop lives. A storm forecast closes the phase with the crew split over the burn and the bypass unbuilt.

### Movement 5 — The Long Water (Chapters 41–49)

The flood crest arrives with the cut branch still bleeding and the bypass unbuilt. Marek has one night to choose between the outer rows he has spent three seasons saving and the seed house, and he chooses the seed house, flooding the outer strip to load the breach by hand. On the second night, with the branch, the well head, and the mill wheel all exposed and the whole cooperative standing in the water, Marek reads Kellan's logged pressure against three independent records, and Sera — present because a charter holder's node is flooding and because she has put her name on this — makes the licensed independent read that turns arithmetic into proof. Volume climax in Chapters 44–46. The hearing in Chapters 46–48 turns the proof into a conditional charter; the resolution is Chapter 49.

## Volume climax (Chapters 44–46)

The water is the evidence. Marek's team breaches the embankment by hand because nobody built the bypass, and diverts the damaged branch into a cut of their own making. The seed house survives, and so do the shared rows the cooperative came out to repair. The outer strip does not: three seasons of Marek's own ground, worked separately from the shared field, is under the water for a week and comes up sour.

On the second night, with the branch, the well head, and the mill wheel all exposed at once, four records exist that cannot all be true: Kellan's logged pressure, Marek's brass gauge, the mill's hand tally of wheel turns, and the dated seed-house ledger. Kellan is on the water because his own branch and his side draw are what is flooding. His foreman Renn Tallow is there because he was in the yard on the hearing day and knows which kit went out when the crew was told to leave. Lissa brings the fieldbook and its use log to the water's edge and reads every entry aloud while Sera makes the licensed read, and Sera puts her name under all of it. Kellan is not killed and does not flee. He loses the Lantern Reach contract, keeps a private charter under monitored conditions because Fennmere's low quarter still needs his water, and testifies about how Crown schedules are written and who signs them. The name he gives is one the reader does not yet understand, and Sera hears it and says nothing.

## Concrete resolution (Chapters 47–49)

- The cooperative receives a **conditional charter**: the node stays open, the seed stock is not seized, and the first harvest comes in short and real.
- The cooperative's own self-imposed obligations from Chapter 10 — a public pressure log anyone may copy and a monthly root-change report — are **adopted by the Crown as charter conditions**. The Crown did not invent them, which is the point, and it is also how they become revisable.
- The terms are provisional. The charter lapses at the season's end, the file stays open, and the Directorate reserves the right to reopen it on any new report. That reservation is the handoff to Volume 02, where the Directorate becomes a structural antagonist rather than a defeated one.
- Kellan's falsified log is a matter of record. His private charter survives under monitored conditions, and the volume is careful to say why.
- Sera's certification stands with her personal liability on it. She has not been asked about Brinewake and has not volunteered it.
- Tova's seed annex is signed into the charter's conditions. Her dry-land line stays lost. The March holds a copy of everything.
- The pathhound line is mapped and left alone. The flock was driven off alive in Chapter 9 and not one animal came back, and nothing usable came back with them. The seed vault's compost return is rebuilt with bought compost and a thinner compost heap, permanently, and the graft that made the field green is never fed the way it was designed to be fed.
- Marek keeps no instrument, no private log, and no private advantage. He has a certificate he cannot use without a licensed witness, a monthly obligation he did not want, and a fen that knows what he did in the dark.

## Deeper answer delivered by this volume

The volume's surface answer is that a Crown schedule, not the weather, cut the branch. Its deeper answer is about the rootwood itself: what Marek read in the fieldbook bead in Chapter 6 is not a memory of a season that has not happened. It is a **target pattern** — a work order, written in advance, in the order of someone intending to enforce a pattern rather than recall one. Sera says so in Chapter 46, plainly, and admits she has seen the structure before and did not want to say it. Nobody names who wrote it, what machine or office wrote it, or why a pattern of that shape would be written into living wood at all. Those are Volumes 05 through 12.

## New question for the next volume

Why does the fen's scarroot carry a work order in Aldren's hand, dated **after** the disappearance, for a branch on no current map — and who put his name in the old schedule log as the person who authorized the cut?

The answer is not in this volume, and neither half of the question can be pursued from Lantern Reach. Batch 0001 plants only the first half: a pattern in Aldren's hand with an impossible date. The schedule log itself, and his name in it, arrive in Volume 02 with the first Crown paperwork the cooperative is allowed to demand. Marek also no longer controls the instrument that holds the plant: it is in his mother's custody under a use log, and what is in it is a fragment of a fragment, because the fieldbook's back half and most of Aldren's survey pages are gone.

## Final chapter image (Chapter 49)

The conditional charter is nailed to the seed-house door with the Directorate's inspection tag still hanging from it. Lissa holds Marek's father's fieldbook and its use log, and neither of them asks the other for anything. In the yard, Tova marks the seed annex in a public ledger that anyone may copy, while the first row of next season's line is already in the ground and already leaning sideways, because that is what the land is doing this year. Nia's cart ruts are still visible at the gate, and they point out. The seed-house lamp is burning on a living bulb somebody has to feed tonight.

## Power progression

- **Start:** Stage 0. **End of volume:** Stage 1, Touchroot — unreliable, costly, and dependent on a licensed witness. This matches the series contract of "Stage 0 to Stage 1" for Volume 01.
- Stage 2 (Grafthand) and the first skin rootmark do not arrive until Volume 02, at Marek's first independent graft. The graft his crew performs in Chapters 21–25 is **crew work under Sera's license**, not his.
- Costs paid in this volume, in order: part of a spoiled seed line and a wrecked coupling (Ch 3); disorientation, nosebleed, and a sensory bleed that runs through the afternoon (Ch 6); collapse, a permanently marked branch, the lost flock, and a waterlogged pump pit (Ch 9); the outer field (Movement 3).
- No human living anchor is used in this volume. Tova is a lender, an auditor, a witness, and a professional opponent. Anchor work does not begin until later volumes.
- No draw in this volume is free. Every one is written down, and three of them are refused outright.
- Volume 01's root work is **local conduit** work: a well, a seed house, and a short run of living pipe with one local steward. No bridge of any other capacity is built in this volume, and the name is used in prose so the later capacity names have a floor.

## Relationship progression

- **Marek / Tova:** professional audit to a conditional alliance by Ch 10, with no romantic turn in this volume. The Volume 08 slow burn requires that nothing here is tender. The hard test comes in Movement 3, when she refuses a draw that would save his field and he lets her.
- **Marek / Sera:** reluctant hire to a working partnership under shared liability. He is still afraid of what she will find in the fieldbook, and in Ch 46 she finds it.
- **Marek / Lissa:** no forgiveness. Their terms change from cold cooperation to a documented one, and in Movement 4 she protects his instrument at a price the cooperative pays. At the climax she brings the fieldbook and its use log to the water's edge and reads every entry aloud in front of a Directorate inspector, which is the loudest thing she has ever done for him and is not forgiveness.
- **Marek / Nia:** he stops arguing about her leaving around Ch 12 and starts recording what she takes. Her cart is booked for day 20 and she leaves in Ch 20, and she does not come back within the volume.
- **Marek / Kellan:** long-standing working trust to public ruin that leaves the man alive, employed, and testifying.

## Antagonist position in the ladder

Rung 0 (manufactured scarcity) is the volume's engine. Rung 1 (Kellan Rusk) is fully staged and resolved: exposed, deprived of the cooperative's contract, kept on a monitored charter, and turned into a witness. The Well Directorate is institutional pressure with a sincere argument and an open file. Halden Pike exists as a signature on a circular. The Pale Orchard, Soren Rill, and the Continuity Office are not in this volume.

## Guardrails for this volume

1. No explanation of the Quiet Break, the Crown Engine, False Season, or Iona Vey. A serial number, a stamp, and a schedule are all the reader gets.
2. Marek's first Fieldbook impression is a work pattern, not a message, and it must remain able to be grief wearing evidence's shape.
3. The unexplained pressure cycle at the well head in Ch 6 and the repeating growth timing in the outer rows are **undiagnosed clues**, not a diagnosis. A later writer must not let anyone in this volume name them.
4. No skin rootmark, no anchor work, no bridge above local conduit, no seedheart, no caretaker link, no reading of the deep archive.
5. Kellan's motive stays practical. Falsifying the log keeps a district supplied and a contract alive. He is not a moustache and he is not killed.
6. The pathhound is not a monster nest, is not commanded, and is not solved by killing its anchor. There are two pathhounds in this volume and they are different animals with different reasons.
7. The volume's promise is paid in food, a conditional charter, and a new obligation. It is not paid by Marek becoming important.
8. Keep the three-volume law visible: a field can look improved while a quarter downstream loses its drinking water.

## Expected length

Ordinary chapters run 2,200–3,200 words. The flood chapters (41–46) may run longer because the scene is a single continuous night. Do not pad, and do not split a complete scene to reach a chapter count.

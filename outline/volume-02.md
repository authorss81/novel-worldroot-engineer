# Volume 02 Outline: The Graft Market

Volume number: 2

Chapter range: 50–98

Series layer: World-building layer 3 — regional trade, charter law, professional licensing, and the first appearance of a Crown map that disagrees with a district schedule. The volume may show a charter broker, a seed buyer, a licensed contractor, a Crown seed-route map, a district register of authorisations, and a graft that carries a history. **It must not name the Crown Engine, the Continuity Office, False Season, Iona Vey, or the Quiet Break, and it must not deliver a history lecture.** The Crown is a set of forms, an expiry date, and a legend on a map.

## Central pressure

A conditional charter expires at the end of the season, and the cooperative adopted its own open book as a condition of that charter, and the Dryfoot Trust is a named party to the seed annex. All three of those facts now work on other people. A repair that worked in a flood is now a thing with a price on it, and the price is not money: it is that a thing anybody may read is a thing anybody may copy, index, buy, tax, and make conditional.

Three people arrive in the fen to make that argument in three different vocabularies. A charter broker wants to buy an expiring charter. A seed buyer wants to buy a ledger. A licensed contractor wants to buy a method. None of them is lying, all three are correct about the danger of a holding that cannot pay for itself, and the cooperative has no answer to any of them that is not a refusal.

## Starting state

Marek Vale is twenty-nine, engineer of record, Stage 1 Touchroot, with three contacts behind him and no rootmark. The node is open. The charter is four clauses and a fifth sheet and it expires at the end of the season without notice. The leak at the six o'clock corner is a leak and is not mended. The graft that made nineteen acres stand is being fed about a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed. Three acres of Marek's own ground are gone for three seasons. The schedule of damage of sixty pounds ten is entered and not owed. The file is open and the Directorate has reserved the right to reopen it on any new report. **Marek kept nothing.** He has a certificate he cannot use without a licensed witness, an instrument on his mother's table he may not open alone, and a book anybody may read.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek / Lissa:** Unresolved, unforgiven, and now working in the same building every day because the charter put a table between them. Her fourth refusal is unnumbered and unexplained and nobody has asked her about it.
- **Marek / Tova:** Contractual and adversarial under a signed annex. She has entered six lines against the trust's own interest and signed alone. The trust's copy is in a public ledger nine hundred miles away.
- **Marek / Nia:** Absent. Not in the volume's first movement and not brought back. Her letter is in an open drawer, sealed, and nobody in the fen writes down why it was not delivered. **Her absence is a working absence: the person who wrote the monthly report, counted the crates, and typed the ledger is gone, and the work does not stop.**
- **Marek / Gael Senn:** Monthly examiner under a charter that expires in a season, who wrote nothing in advance in front of a hearing officer, and who now has a form in her hand with a box on it.
- **Marek / Sera Quill:** Gone down the road on the afternoon cart with her fee on an invoice and eight pages of notes. Her fourth refusal stands unnumbered. **Nobody in this volume may ask her about it.**
- **Marek / Kellan Rusk:** Alive, licensed, monitored, and a witness. He is available and he is not a rescue.

## Starting power level

Stage 1, Touchroot. He can feel a pressure in a piece of wood with his hand on it. It is unreliable, it cost him the ground under his feet and an hour he cannot account for, and it is worth nothing in front of anybody except a licensed engineer standing next to him. **No rootmark. He has looked at his arm twice since the seventeenth and both times it was nothing.**

## Major factions and pressures

- **Hearth Crown / Well Directorate:** no longer pressure. **Structure.** The charter's own conditions, an expiry date nobody chose, a register of authorisations, a public map that carries a mark for root line nobody holds, and a form with a box on it that decides the renewal. **No Directorate officer in this volume lies, obstructs, or is corrupt. Every Crown act in Volume 02 is correct, timely, and structural.**
- **Ruben Ashe, charter broker:** the price of a repair. Buys expiring charters and sells the conditions. Not a villain; a man with capital and a date.
- **Vesta Lund, seed buyer:** the price of a history. Runs a seed index and pays a shilling a line, sincerely, and is refused by a woman who has been indexed twice.
- **Coll Renner, licensed contractor:** the price of a method. Wants the Lantern Reach work written down and owned, and is right that eleven other fen holdings cannot afford a cooperative.
- **Lantern Reach Work Cooperative:** a body of sixteen households with a charter that expires, a book anybody may read, a box that is empty, and a plant that is going to be starved.
- **Sera Quill:** absent and unavailable, and her absence is the absence of the only licensed witness in Tarrow Fen.
- **The Rootwright Assembly:** Pike's circular of Volume 01 is on four hundred doors and Renner's license is the same license.

## Midpoint reversal (Chapters 69–72)

The provenance bead works. That is the reversal. **A seed lot, wetted against a copper-collared living bead cut from a grafted seed, reports where it was grown, in four seconds, without a ledger, without a clerk, and without anybody's word.** It is the most useful thing anybody in Tarrow Fen has seen in a year, and it is technically perfect, and it is being offered to the Dryfoot Trust's own dry-land line.

Then Tova establishes what a graft is: **a graft keeps the thing that was put in and loses the thing that was there, and the losing is not a fault, it is what the word means.** A bead that certifies a seed's history is a bead that can be made to carry any history, and once the history travels in the seed instead of in a book, **the book is no longer evidence of anything.** The buyer does not have to forge a record. He only has to graft.

And the harder half: the memory the bead carries is not the seed's. It is the **seedkeeper's** — a hand, a season, a decision about which trays to keep — read out of living wood. **A graft carries a memory into a market device.** The volume's central discovery is that a device which makes provenance self-certifying is a device that makes provenance ownable, and that the two are the same mechanism.

## Escalation sequence

### Movement 1 — What The Charter Is Worth (Chapters 50–59)

The charter's first obligations land. The first Crown paperwork the cooperative has ever been entitled to demand arrives because the demand was made properly and the ground went out from under the other answer, and nobody decided anything. **A charter broker offers sixty pounds for the charter and will be back at the end of the season.** The district produces the works extract and one certified page of the register of authorisations, and the page carries a hand that a seed house steward knows. A Crown seed-route map does not show the Weir Row branch, and its legend explains why. The first examination under the charter happens and the return line records a thing that did not happen, in district charter law, for the first time, and the load test that was not performed is written down in the second wording. See `outline/batches/volume-02-batch-0001.md`.

### Movement 2 — The Market (Chapters 60–68)

The offers do not stop, and the volume's political work is that **refusing a buyer is not free and the cooperative keeps refusing anyway.** The buyer's argument is that a landrace in one book in one fen is one flood from gone, and the cooperative cannot show that the book is wrong. The Undercommons route stays shut: Ivo Tarrant's four men are not coming back and nobody may say they were. A professional circular turns the provenance bead into a trade article. **Kellan Rusk is the first person outside this building to say aloud that the log, the seed annex, and the fieldbook together are enough to prove Lantern Reach's own draw against itself, and he says it to help.**

### Movement 3 — The Memory In The Seed (Chapters 69–76)

The provenance bead carries a seedkeeper's memory into a market device, and it is offered to the Dryfoot Trust's own dry-land line. Tova discovers that a technically perfect connection erases local crop history, because a graft overwrites and because the last thing grafted in is the only thing that answers. The cooperative must decide whether to profit from the discovery, and the decision is taken in the yard in front of the book. **Marek's first independent graft and his first skin rootmark are already on the page, in Chapter 57 of Batch 0001** — a flowerpot of rye, on a trestle, with a licensed man's clip, with a witness, and the record written by the licensed man. **Movement 3 is the chapter that makes that mark cost something: two or three active links, a fourth that fails, and a device that works too well.**

### Movement 4 — The Bridge (Chapters 77–88)

The team builds the first public **seed-route bridge** between a seed bank and a dry Salt March settlement. It is crew work on a public schedule with a return path and two ends, and it is named in a book anybody may copy, and the capacity is named in the trade's own words. **It is a seed-route bridge and nothing above that.** The bridge carries a convoy that is written down crate by crate. A settlement at the dry end has no charter and is not asked to sign anything, and its own refusal is entered without its terms.

### Movement 5 — The Tax On A Bridge (Chapters 89–98)

The Crown taxes the bridge and makes its use conditional, and the tax is not a villain's greed: it is a schedule, a rate, a class of use, and a clause. The coalition's refusal is to accept it, and the bridge is not closed. The concrete resolution is **the exchange survives as a local commons with public ledgers and a refusal clause** — a real clause, in a real document, that a later volume can be built on. Marek's answer to the volume's question is a reputation: **a useful engineer and a dangerous independent witness**, which is what the series contract asks for and which is not a triumph.

## Volume climax (Chapters 93–96)

The bridge is finished, the convoy is moving, and the Crown's conditional-use clause arrives with the first toll. The team does not destroy anything and does not win an exemption. **The bridge runs, the tax is paid and entered, and the refusal clause is invoked for the first time by a settlement at the dry end against a use the operators wanted** — which is the only proof available that the clause is real and not a decoration.

## Concrete resolution (Chapters 96–98)

- **The seed-route exchange survives** as a local commons with a public ledger, a copy anybody may take, and a refusal clause that has been used once.
- **The conditional charter lapses at the end of the season** and a different arrangement is put in its place that is not a Crown charter, with a date and a condition and a right to leave.
- **The sixty pounds ten stands.** It is not paid, not remitted and not reduced.
- **The file stays open** and the Directorate's reservation stands.
- **The graft is about to be starved**, and the arithmetic is entered in a hand somebody in four years will be able to check.
- **Marek earns a reputation as a useful engineer and a dangerous independent witness.** Nobody thanks him. Two bodies write to him in the same week, one of them offering a post and one of them asking whether he intends to continue publishing.

## Deeper answer delivered by this volume

The volume's surface answer is that **root memories disagree with Crown maps and seed histories, and the disagreement is not an error of survey.** A branch can be worked and maintained and cut three times and not appear on the public map, because it has no local holder, and the map's own legend carries a mark for *unmapped root line, maintained in schedule*. A register of authorisations carries a hand and a date and a post that does not come out, and the district's refusal to produce the page is a form and not a conspiracy.

The volume's deeper answer is about **documentation as a form of governance.** Tova's discovery is the volume's real lesson: **a lineage that travels in the seed instead of in a book can be replaced by anybody holding a grafting knife, and the replacement is not a forgery.** A system that was documented out of existence does not have to be suppressed again. It has to be given a better record and then let somebody buy the better record. **This is Volume 02's answer and it is not the Quiet Break's, and it may be said in one line by a seed steward without any history in it at all.**

## New question for the next volume

Who altered the maps and the seed records enough to make a bridge between a fen and the dry Salt March legal — and who was paid for the alteration?

**Not answered in this volume.** Volume 02 establishes that a Crown seed-route map omits a maintained branch, that a district register of authorisations carries a hand that a seed steward recognizes, and that a Crown form classifies a root line nobody holds as Crown-maintained in schedule. It does not establish who changed the map layer, when, or under what instrument. **Volume 02 earns the shape of the question the way Volume 01 earned its own: the shape on the page, the content nowhere.**

## Final chapter image (Chapter 98)

The seed-route ledger is on the seed-house table with the fieldbook and the use log, in three books and a copy on the March road, and anybody may ask for any of the three to be read. The bridge's first dry-end settlement has entered a refusal against a use nobody had asked it about, and the entry is in the ledger, and the terms of it are not held by anybody. Tova Reed's name is on the public ledger six times against her own trust's interest and she has stopped counting. The repair from the flood is still green on nineteen acres and the row of Longneck from the last chapter of Volume 01 has been harvested short and real, and the compost line is still at a twentieth, and the starving is not visible from the top of the field. Nia's cart has been past the gate twice in a season and both times she did not stop, and **nobody in that yard has asked her why, and nobody is going to.** And the lamp in the seed house is burning on a living bulb somebody has to feed tonight.

## Power progression

- **Start:** Stage 1, Touchroot, unreliable, licensed-witness-dependent, no rootmark.
- **End of volume:** Stage 2, Grafthand — two or three active links with careful maintenance, no high-capacity bridge alone, no safe attachment of himself to the network. **The first skin rootmark is already spent, in Chapter 57 of Batch 0001, on a flowerpot of rye, under a licensed man's clip, and the record of it was written by the licensed man and not by Marek.**
- **Stage 2's cost, paid on the page and already in the manuscript:** the anchor and the practitioner both record the graft; the mark is permanent and visible; the number of links a man can keep without help is three, and a fourth fails. **A later batch may not pay any of these a second time, and may not have a second mark appear.**
- **No draw.** Volume 02 has no draw and no return by rootlight. **A market is a worse instrument than a draw and it is used deliberately here.**
- **No human living anchor.** Anchor work does not begin until Volume 06. **Nothing in this volume may lean that way.**

## Relationship progression

- **Marek / Tova:** shared records and a real disagreement. **The volume's question is who owns a grafted seed, and she owns seed and he owns the root, and neither of them can answer it for the other.** They establish that the ledger is one book and neither of them keeps a private copy, which is the milestone, and it is institutional and not tender. Nothing romantic. Her line about being indexed twice is the volume's best sentence and must not be softened in any summary.
- **Marek / Lissa:** **she identifies her husband's hand**, and it is the loudest thing she has done for him and it is not forgiveness, and she will not be asked whether it is his hand or a copy of it.
- **Marek / Gael Senn:** she writes, in the second wording, a load test that was not performed, on the first of a month, in district charter law, for the first time in the history of this fen. She becomes the volume's first person to use a Crown clause against a Crown clause.
- **Marek / Kellan Rusk:** a witness, available, and the first person outside the building to say that their own record convicts them. His monitored charter is renewed once and is not a redemption.
- **Marek / Sera Quill:** absent, unreachable on a fee, and the volume's first licensed witness is a stranger. **Her fourth refusal is not asked about.**

## Antagonist position in the ladder

Rung 0 (manufactured scarcity) recedes into the **form**: an expiring charter, a category of use, a rate, a clause. Rung 1 (Kellan Rusk) is complete and is now a witness on the cooperative's side of the table. **Rung 2, the Hearth Crown Well Directorate, is fully staged as a structural antagonist**: it is not corrupt, it is not cruel, and every single act it takes in this volume is correct, timely, and structural. Halden Pike's circular from Volume 01 is on four hundred doors and a licensed contractor in the district is inside it.

## Guardrails for this volume

1. **No Crown Engine, no Continuity Office, no False Season, no Iona Vey, no Quiet Break, and no history lecture.** Not one date in this volume is earlier than the cooperative's own charter.
2. **Marek's first skin rootmark is his first independent graft and nothing else**, and the first independent graft is a small graft on a plant in a pot, made with a licensed man's clip and recorded by another person.
3. **The pressure pulse stays undiagnosed.** It is logged again in this volume and nobody names it. **The leak is still a leak and is still not mended.** Neither is a mystery and neither is a plot.
4. **The Thornwild settlement is not named.** The long line is not used, quoted, or entered. The word *seat* does not appear. **The four men do not come back and no bypass is built.**
5. **The works schedule is not produced entire.** One page of a register of authorisations is produced, certified as to form, and the log behind it is not.
6. **The Fieldbook panel is used once per batch and is never quoted as an instruction in any document**, and no batch's panel may be a person's finding.
7. **No romance.** Nothing tender. The slow burn runs on shared records and professional disagreement.
8. **A system that reads a seed's history in four seconds is not a soul and not a mind.** It is a graft, and a graft keeps the thing put in and loses the thing that was there.

# Volume 04 Outline: Salt Without Rain

Volume number: 4

Chapter range: 148–196

Series layer: World-building layer 2 with the edge of layer 3 — seed trade, local law, rootwoken ecology, and professional politics. The volume leaves the fen road and follows a migration south, and it is about **water that belongs to somebody and water that belongs to nobody.** It may name the **Thornwild** and its Assemblies, it may put the name **Pale Orchard** on a page, and it may put **Brinewake** into Sera Quill's own mouth once, uncorroborated, as Volume 03 reserved it to. **It may not name the Crown Engine as a working system, may not name Chancellor Iona Vey, may not name False Season, may not deliver a history lecture, and may not open the Continuity Office.** A name on a form is not an explanation.

## Central pressure

A migration is under way and it is not coming into the fen, it is coming **through** it, and the water that goes with it is being bought at a shilling a pail out of a low quarter where four wells are off. **The water underneath that quarter is the same water the people in it have been drinking out of for a season, and the man hauling it is drawing it from under their own feet at a licensed rate, and the difference between the licensed rate and the price of a pail is not theft and is enormous.**

The cooperative has no charter, no standing, four pounds and fivepence, a compost line of ninety-five pounds a month on a run of eight months not discharged in eight, a leak, a beat, and a name that people in nine miles have started using when they want somebody to blame. **It cannot pay for the water, cannot vote on the well, cannot send a crew across the ground without a licence, and cannot find out whose order the water is moving on.** Three pressures, three vocabularies, and no villain: a haulier's arithmetic, a charter holder's licence, and a root engineer's draw.

## Starting state

Marek Vale is thirty-one, engineer of record, **Stage 3 Drawwright.** One rootmark four inches along the inside of his left forearm, permanent, branching twice. Two draws spent in Volume 03, **neither recovered and neither recoverable.** Two links, three at the outside, a fourth that fails. He can draw water, heat and rootlight through a living path and return it, at a cost, and he has been paid twice in other people's wells. **He cannot read deep memory. There is no deep archive in this volume and no chapter may imply one.**

The Lantern Reach Work Cooperative has **no charter.** It has a book anybody may read, a refusal clause nobody can tax, four pounds and fivepence, a well house door with a seventh column at thirty readings, a leak at five-eighths of an inch with no due date, a beat not logged since with its column open, a compost line of ninety-five pounds, four pounds and fivepence of it, sixty pounds ten unpaid in an open file, and a standing list of four lines in which the fourth is *what this body is looking after and cannot reach.* **It has a copy of its ledger in five places and can check one.**

## Starting relationships

- **Marek / Tova:** shared records, one book in two countries, neither keeping a private copy. **The milestone for this range is that he hides a rootwoken migration prediction from the book and she finds out.** It is not a betrayal of her and it is not a lie to her. It is his own house rule applied to a fact he cannot defend, and it is the worst thing he does in the volume, and the relationship survives only because he says so in front of people before anybody makes him.
- **Marek / Lissa:** unforgiven, unthanked, running the seed house. **Volume 04 has to pay for Lissa's going again** — she is on the road in Movements 3 and 4 and she goes as a woman with a ledger and not as a mother.
- **Marek / Sera Quill:** she is not in Movement 1. **She is the volume's Brinewake mouth, uncorroborated, in Movement 2.** She buys no trust and is not thanked and is not forgiven.
- **Marek / Soren Rill:** new to the page in Chapter 150 and adversarial from his first hour. **He refuses a crew at a living root before anybody has asked him a question, and he is right.**
- **Marek / Coll Renner:** licensed, available, a copper clip, and the man who calls the stop on a draw because the fen's own rule says the caller is not the worker. **He is the volume's conscience about procedure and he is going to be the man who has to call a stop on something the fen wanted.**
- **Marek / the district:** the water office, a form, a class of use, a rate. Correct, structural, and in this volume able to say, in four lines and in the ordinary course, that a quarter's water is a licensed thing.

## Starting power level

Stage 3, Drawwright. Draw and return, at a cost, with a downstream debt that is a person. **Stage 4, Patternkeeper, arrives in Movement 3 and not before**, and it arrives as **absorbing a failure sensation and becoming a maintenance bottleneck**, not as a larger output: Marek holds a pattern for a crew of four and is then the only person who can hold it, and two people in the volume have to be told that this is not a good thing.

## Major locations

- **The seed house, Lantern Reach:** the table in the middle with the three books on it; the well house door nine hundred yards off with a column of readings on it; the north gate, where handoffs are read back; the yard, where anything said out loud gets entered.
- **Fennmere, nine miles:** the miller's yard and the chalk board, the counter and the sub-office, the low quarter, forty-one houses and four hundred and forty-one people with four wells off.
- **The dry road:** the road that runs south out of the fen's district, eleven miles to a shallow well at the head of the flats that the rest of this volume calls the Pans well, and on past it to nothing. **Eleven miles is a day and the fen has no standing on any of it.**
- **Cauldron Reach, four hundred miles and four days up:** eleven acres, four households, a hundred and forty-one trees, a row of eleven dressed faces, a stone wall, and a well read by its occupier with her own nail at the fourth hour on the sixteenth of every month.

## Major factions and pressures

- **A charter holder at the Pans well:** a name on a sheet in Movement 1 and a man in the room in Movement 2. Licensed, correct, and not a villain. **He draws at a rate the office sets, and the rate is fourpence above the tariff, and the haulier charges a shilling a pail, and the difference over eleven days is twenty pounds eighteen shillings, and none of it is a fraud.**
- **The district water office, records and rates:** a form, four lines, a rate, and the standing of a licensed supply to a quarter where wells are off. **No officer in this volume lies, obstructs, or is corrupt.** Sabin Roell and Perrin Osse are the instruments.
- **Soren Rill, of a Thornwild Assembly, guide:** the volume's adversarial guide. He refuses a crew at a living root, refuses a name for what is under the low ground, refuses the word *nest* and says why, and asks for a pail, a measure and an hour instead of a promise. **He is not a tame guide and never becomes one and he represents a political position Marek must accommodate, not a problem he solves.**
- **A name on a page, in Movement 2:** the Pale Orchard enters as four words in a return and as a party that has been buying water in a quarter the fen has never been in. **Nobody in this volume may make the name into a villain in person before Movement 4, and the party is not a fiction: what it says about controlled systems is partly true and what it does to the people moving through this road is not defensible.**
- **Merrid Ollvar, of Cauldron Reach:** about fifty-four, four houses, eleven acres, a nail and a yard and a reading on the sixteenth of every month. **She is not a member, not a villain, and not redeemed, and her well is one inch down in a month and there is no entry anywhere for it coming back.**

## The first clock the volume owns

**The next ordinary reading of the well at Cauldron Reach is the fourth hour on the sixteenth of the ninth month, and it is day 256.** It is Volume 03's last standing obligation and **no batch may skip it and no batch may move it.** It falls inside **Batch 0001, in Chapter 151**, narrated from outside, in a yard of four households, by the occupier, with her own nail, in her own hand.

**The reading is five feet and one inch.** The last was five feet and two inches on the sixteenth of last month. The comparison figure is the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month four months back, and **the difference is three feet and eleven inches, and this volume may not decompose it.** The half inch survives only as the figure Chapter 137 named as the ground's share sixteen days before the four-inch reading, and it is not carried forward as a figure and it is not made to do arithmetic.

## Escalation sequence

### Movement 1 — Nine Days Of Water (Chapters 148–157)

The volume opens on a month, a bill the fen cannot pay, and a guarantee that cannot be run to it. A waggon comes down the plateau road four days after it went up, and it is not empty. A guide refuses a crew before he is asked. The office says, in four correct lines, that a quarter's water is a licensed thing. The fen times a pail at three places and the three numbers do not agree with each other. **The source stops, and the only thing this holding can do about a stopped source is take water out of a well four hundred yards from a quarter that has four wells off already, and the standing rule is satisfied by a return nobody told the fen was carrying.** A draw is made, a man is the man who called the stop, a third well goes off, and the batch closes with the fen giving away a pail, a measure and an hour to a man it cannot follow.

### Movement 2 — What She Said In A Room (Chapters 158–167)

Sera Quill testifies, in a room with about eleven people in it, about a relay she designed and a red-line warning she took out of it nineteen years ago, and the name comes out of her own mouth and **nothing corroborates it and nothing can.** A charter holder comes to the fen and is correct in every particular and asks for a thing that is not water. **The cost of this movement is Sera's, and it is a public cost, and it is not a confession that buys anything.**

### Movement 3 — The Thing That Refuses (Chapters 168–177)

**The volume's major turn.** A restored well on the dry road produces a rootwoken community that will not be drawn from, and it is not a nest and it is not a monster and it is a settlement with a food system. **Stage 4 arrives here: Marek holds a pattern for a crew of four and is then the only person who can hold it, and the volume names that as a defect and not as an achievement.** The team has to distinguish a territorial living community from a monster nest, and the distinguishing is done by a man who has read four pieces of ground in nine years and three of them were crews with a licence.

### Movement 4 — A Basin With No Owner (Chapters 178–187)

The Crown's frame becomes an instrument: inspections with soldiers on the road, a rate, and a form that says a migration is a licensed use. **The Pale Orchard does a real thing for a real reason and kills people doing it, and the volume separates the accusation from the method out loud and does not resolve either.** Tova leads the seed side away from Marek and the two of them do not work together for eleven chapters and it is not a quarrel.

### Volume climax (Chapters 186–192)

Marek and the caravan crews put water into a dry salt basin and flood a corridor that is not there, to move people through it in one season. **A seed vault goes under and is not recovered, and the vault is named and the people who were in it are named, and the cost is not a lesson and is not a symbol.** The route stays open. The Crown calls in a martial inspection.

### Volume resolution (Chapters 192–196)

The migration route remains open for the season. Local commons take a cross-region seed agreement with a refusal clause in it. The Crown imposes martial inspection and the instrument is correct. **The volume's question, in the fen's own words and naming no office, no machine, no person and no year, is put in the book and is not answered, and the count of the times it has been available to a room and not taken goes up by one.** The stage at the end of the volume is Stage 4, with a team, and with the standing statement that a man who is the only person who can hold a pattern is a bottleneck and not a system.

## The volume's question, in the fen's own words

Carried from Volume 03 and not answered: **whose order is it.** Carried from Volume 03 and not answered: **is the telling still going on.** New in this volume and the one that drives it: **people are walking toward water and this body cannot tell whether they are walking toward a thing that is walking with them.** **No chapter of Volume 04 answers any of the three.** The third is the volume's own and it is not the same as the first two, and the fen is careful about that on the page.

## Power progression

- **Start:** Stage 3, Drawwright. Two draws spent, neither recovered. Rootmark four inches, branching twice, unchanged.
- **One contact and one draw in Movement 1.** The contact is at the copper seam of a floor in the low quarter, with a licensed man, the use log's fourteenth line written and read back first, a plain wire gauge test written down before he went on, and it costs the mark and the hand for a day. The draw is at the fourth hour, the stop at four feet called by a licensed man, **and the rule is satisfied by a return the fen was not told about, and the fen breaks its own rule anyway, and the third well in the quarter goes off on the next day and does not come back.**
- **The mark may not grow a second mark and may not be lengthened.** The burn runs past it into the elbow and is not a mark.
- **Stage 4 arrives in Movement 3 and by use.** A pattern held for a team of four, which means Marek feels what four people feel and cannot leave, and the volume says in those words that this is a bottleneck and not a system.
- **No anchor work. No human living anchor. No seedheart. No caretaker link. No deep archive. No memory read.** The fieldbook is closed in a room four hundred miles off and does not come down that road, and no chapter in this volume opens it.

## Relationship progression

- **Marek / Tova: the milestone for this range.** **He hides a rootwoken migration prediction from the book and she finds out, and she refuses to anchor him afterward, and the relationship survives only because he apologizes in front of people and accepts that love is not permission to bypass consent.** The hiding is in Movement 1 and Movement 2; the finding out is Movement 2; the refusal to anchor is Movement 3; the apology is Movement 4. **Nothing in this range may be tender on the page and nothing may be a reconciliation scene.**
- **Marek / Soren:** he challenges his assumptions on the first day by refusing a crew at a living root, and the conflict becomes a working arrangement that neither of them calls a partnership. **Soren is not thanked and is not tamed.**
- **Marek / Sera:** her testimony in Movement 2 is a set of records decisions and it is not an eyewitness memory. It buys no trust and is followed by nothing except work and a public cost.
- **Marek / Lissa:** she goes on the road in Movements 3 and 4 and she goes as a woman with a ledger, and she is not forgiven and is not thanked.
- **Nothing romantic.** The slow burn runs on who is holding what and on two people who keep finding out what the other one will not do.

Rung 6 (Iona Vey) may not be named.

## Guardrails for this volume

1. **No Crown Engine as a working system, no Chancellor Iona Vey, no False Season, and no history lecture.** The Quiet Break may appear as an age and a number and never as a date and never with a region. The Continuity Office may be named as an office on paper and nowhere else.
2. **The name Brinewake is spent once, in Movement 2, out of Sera Quill's own mouth, uncorroborated, and may not appear anywhere else in the volume** — not in narration, not in a document, not in a form, not in an index, and not guessed at by anybody. **A record, a log, a district index, a clerk and a licensed man may not supply it, and no chapter may have anybody corroborate it.**
3. **No month is named in narration or in any document.** The month before the tenth is the ninth. October is nine months back by day 268 and is named nowhere.
4. **Marek cannot read deep memory in this volume and no chapter may have him do it.** Everything he learns about the ground under the low quarter is learned by contact, by counting, by timing a pail, by walking it, and by being told by a man who will not be thanked.
5. **The Cauldron Reach well reading is day 256 and it may not be skipped and may not be moved.** The shortfall may not be decomposed.
6. **The leak is not mended and is not a mystery. It is measured once in Movement 1 and the batch does not say whether the figure before it and the figure after it are a movement or a season.**
7. **No romance. Nothing tender on the page.** The word *seat* may now appear; Thornwild and its Assemblies may be named; the settlement under the south embankment windbreak may be named, and its representatives refuse the word *clearing* and insist on negotiated land use, and that refusal is theirs and is on the page once and is not improved on.
8. **The standing rule about the engineer of record stands at four lines, every one about a stop, and it is used once in Movement 1 and once in Movement 4 and not otherwise.** The rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and the volume's first use is the volume's first moral cost.
9. **A system that reads a seed's history in four seconds is not a soul and not a mind.** A rootwoken community is a community, it has a food system and an opinion, and it is neither a nest nor a symbol and it is not redeemed.
10. **No new named member of the cooperative.** The crew is the crew. The man of about fifty and the man of about seventy stay held by function and may not be given names without recording it. The haulier, the charter holder's man, the drivers, the seedwright of the trust and the people on the road are held by function except where the volume names them on the page.
11. **No new final enemy.** The Pale Orchard is Rung 4 and Rung 4 is not the last rung.

## Expected length

Ordinary chapters run **2,600–3,120 words.** A batch of ten chapters should land between **27,000 and 30,000 words** and the writer should say in the state files what it actually measured. **Trim restatement. Do not pad. Do not open five chapters at 3,600.** The Volume 01 and Volume 02 lessons are binding and the Volume 03 close made one more: **every paragraph of a speech carried over paragraphs opens and closes its own quotation, no narration stands inside an open speech, and no paragraph may end with a speech open.** The property to audit is that no paragraph ends with a speech open; the number of marks in a file is not a test of anything and must not be made even.

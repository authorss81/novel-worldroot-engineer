## 1. The premise, and a serious standing doubt about it

**The specified novel is *The Worldroot Engineer*.** Marek Vale, twenty-nine, an
agricultural engineer and irrigation surveyor, finds that the world's magic grows through a
living subterranean network. He wants the charter, the well, the next sowing, and a sister
who has a life beyond the fen. He is good at finding leaks and bad at asking for help, and
his flaw is control through expertise: he reaches the technically correct answer and then
believes correctness entitles him to decide alone. The premise, the world, the power system
and the ending lock are in `bible/`, `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`, and those
are the contract.

### The standing doubt, stated once and left for a human decision

**The manuscript on disk is not that novel.** Across 667 chapter files `Rootway` appears in
zero and `Worldroot` in zero. What has been written for fourteen volumes is a ledger about
a holding that keeps a wall, a gate, a launder, a book and thirty-five unresolved
questions, with a recurring cast, a day clock and a set of numeric series. It is
internally consistent and competently written, and it is not the book the spec, the bible
and the series file specify.

**This pass did not change the planned plot and did not attempt to.** Re-planning two and
a half volumes against the premise is a canon decision with an author behind it. The drift
is first in the decision queue at `state/open-threads.md`. **A later pass, or a human,
owns it.** Two things follow. **The bible is still authoritative and still describes a story
that has not been written; do not treat the manuscript as evidence that the premise is
retired.** And **Iona Vey is the fixed antagonist of the series: not new, not killed, not
in custody at the end. No new final enemy may be introduced**, and a writer who does so is
obliged to flag it rather than quietly add it.

## 2. The world as the bible states it, which is the world the book must be in

Veyra is temperate and heavily farmed, and every settlement is tied to the Rootway: a
living tissue of mineral-wood filaments and fungal threads that moves water, heat,
nutrients and chemical memory through the land. Its activity is the source of magic, and
magic is a visible form of a living system's work rather than a substance poured over the
world.

Three practical layers and no clean boundary: topsoil exchange, visible in ordinary work
and repairable by compost, rotation, cover crops and careful irrigation; the living
trunkway of thicker tubes that can be grafted or bridged but must stay alive and matched to
local water pressure; and the deep archive of rootwood and fungal mats that stores old
environmental patterns, that the Crown Engine reaches, and that is incomplete, damaged and
open to deliberate editing.

**Rootlight is metabolic energy carried by living systems and is not unlimited mana.**
Memory is environmental pattern and is not prophecy or mind-reading. Rootwoken creatures are
neither automatically evil nor automatically innocent. **A place may have rootlight and
still lack water, seed or stable soil, and a place may have rich soil and be cut off from
the trunkway.** Magic cannot replace labour, fertiliser, water management, seed selection,
medicine or negotiation. A forest can recover and it does not owe anybody a harvest.

**History, in the order the volumes need it.** Centuries of local root communes, each
maintaining its own node by its own method. Then the Ash Winter, 312 years ago, and the
coalition that built the Crown Engine out of living wood, stone and metal to move water and
rootlight into the capital, with a human decision loop inside a living network because a
machine that chose among competing needs needed a caretaker. It was meant to be temporary.
The coalition dissolved before the repair budget did.

Then the Quiet Break, 121 years ago: the engine's failsafe sealed several heartroots
because it pruned any flow pattern it could not tax or predict, and officials called it a
natural collapse. It created the modern scarcity, the legal power of well charters and the
first large population of rootwoken creatures. Chancellor Iona Vey later reinforced the
pruning cycle and called it **False Season**, making a centralised caretaker look like the
only way to prevent famine. The **White Mercy** is her current programme: pruning branches
outside the Auberron Basin and outside the central maintenance zone to guarantee one
predictable harvest in the core, and it is a schedule, not a season.

## 4. The volumes

Full detail is in `outline/volume-NN.md`, one line each:

1. **The White Furrow**, a living branch answers a damaged well; Marek's first fieldbook impression carries a maintenance pattern older than his father.
2. **The Graft Market**, root memories disagree with Crown maps; the Quiet Break was not the first deletion.
3. **The Memory Orchard**, scarroot preserves a famine-era engineering sequence; the first engineers pruned deliberately.
4. **Salt Without Rain**, rootwoken follow a broken flow and are not all attacking.
5. **The Heartroot Rota**, one heartroot sends the same pulse in different regions; False Season is a controlled signal.
6. **The False Season**, the Engine maintains the signal; Iona and the Continuity Office are editing local seasons.
7. **The Bridge Below**, the engine prunes what it cannot tax; the scarcity is a policy choice.
8. **What the Roots Remember**, Aldren's final fieldbook message; it is an edited archive fragment and he died interrupting the pruning cycle.
9. **The Rootless Vote**, Iona offers central control and it works, which is what makes refusal hard.
10. **The Undercommons Road**, rootwoken and Undercommons refuse the same bridge for different reasons; consent includes maintenance, exit and revision.
11. **The Crown Engine**, the final chamber can restore one continuous flow, at the cost of pruning everything it cannot synchronise.
12. **The Many-Handed Spring**, released patterns boom, then conflict; one restored network cannot mean uniformity.
13. **The Green Testament**, the covenant forbidding permanent binding is proved; the Aldren memory is the evidence.
14. **The White Mercy** *(current)*, Iona begins the first pruning phase. Tova abandons a seed vault. The climax is the regional network reaching the capital and leaving one secondary city unwatered. The resolution is an offer across a table and a refusal, recorded by Nia Vale.
15. **The Root Commons**, maintenance, consent and refusal defined before the cycle completes.
16. **The Returning Root**, Marek defeats Iona by dividing his own access and breaking the Engine's single caretaker.

**Volume 14's own lock, from `outline/volume-14.md`:** forty-nine mornings, days 851 to
899. The surrender of the Aldren memory is paid inside this volume, before the offer, on a
morning that is neither its first nor its last, and not as its climax. The volume does not
explain what the root is, and nobody in it is entitled to say.

**At day 880, thirty mornings in.** The compost line has fallen once, to paid at
thirty-one, and stands there. Four places want a pipe off one head; the second cannot be
reached this month, the third has shut its head sluice at the fifth hour, and this holding
has become the first thing in four years to ask that head for anything, for a cistern at
the north side of the room where the seed work is done. The seed work is off a shelf and
onto a floor, one row of its bench is bare, and nobody has asked about the row. Nobody has
been asked to choose anything on any of the thirty mornings.

## 5. Two questions this series may never answer

They are named once, here, and not again, and no morning may ask, name, hint at,
paraphrase or answer either.

1. **What the root is.** The answer is that nobody on the page knows. The volume's
   business is branches, load, water, a network and an offer. It does not explain the
   wood, it does not explain the maintenance zone, it does not explain the sixty-six, and
   it does not let Marek become the person who finally says what is under the floor.
2. **The second one.** Untouched for ten volumes counting the one now closed. It is not
   named in this file and it is not to be named in any state file.

## 7. The figures a page overrode, 2026-10-02, Batch 0003

Each was printed wrongly in a prompt or a card and a page on disk says otherwise. **A
successor that finds one of these in a card has inherited an error and not a rule.**

1. **The compost line after day 873 is paid at thirty-one and does not go back.** The
   batch prompt printed it as paid at thirty on the nine mornings that are not day 873.
   `chapter-0660.md` pays it at thirty-one on the morning of the fall and the five mornings
   behind it each carry thirty-one on their own morning.
2. **The fall is seven mornings before day 880, not two.** It fell on day 873, a Thursday,
   and `chapter-0667.md` says it has stood at thirty-one since Thursday.
3. **The read-aloud numerator is `one hundred and [WORD]`,** not `a hundred and`. The
   plan's day table prints it in the window half's form on every odd morning and that cell
   is withdrawn for the volume.
4. **The seed vault was not given up on day 879.** It was given up on day 864 in
   `chapter-0651.md`, in her own mouth. Nothing on 879 or 880 re-abandons it,
   re-announces it, has anybody discover it, or describes her as having given something up.

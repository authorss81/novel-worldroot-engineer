## Rules locked for later prose

- Rootlight is metabolic energy in living tissue; it cannot create soil, water, seed, food, or obedience.
- Every rootwork operation needs a living anchor, a compatible path, and a return.
- Human living anchors are possible only through recorded consent, a stop signal, and a safe disconnect. Shared anchors keep separate channels and never fuse minds.
- Rootwork is a licensed profession; a living bind is the forbidden arrangement, not the craft itself.
- False Season changes local root, soil, heat, and migration conditions; it cannot conjure sky weather.
- Bridge capacity is named by load: local conduit, seed-route bridge, regional bridge. A practitioner does not own a bridge's consequences.
- Public proof requires an independent external read and at least two corroborating records. A surrendered memory alone is not enough.
- A caretaker handoff is staged. Replacement seedhearts must be planted, load-tested, and carrying flow before the old link is isolated.
- The recovered living control seed can open local valves but cannot command the running Engine without a caretaker link and compatible return path.
- The first White Mercy phase is a limited core test in Volume 13; the final pruning window opens the decisive activation in Volume 16.

**The month boundary, and how to date a day. Months run thirty days, and season day 1 is the first of October; season day 31 is therefore the first of the next month, and season day 61 the first of the month after that.** Days 1–20 are the tail of October and days 21–30 its head, so **every day in 21–30 is the twenty-first … the thirtieth of October and nothing else.** A later writer must observe this:

| Season day | How the prose may date it |
|---|---|
| 1–20 (October) | the plain day number — *the tenth*, *the seventeenth* — or **of last month**, or **of this season**. **Never *of this month*.** |
| 21–30 (October) | **the plain number only** — *the twenty-first*, the twenty-second, the twenty-third, the twenty-fourth, the thirtieth — or **of last month** once the prose has passed the month end. **Never the first, second, third … of a month, and never *of this month* after day 30.** |
| 31–40 | the ordinal of the new month (*the first*, the second, the third … the tenth) or **of this month** |
| 41+ | the ordinal (*the eleventh* … the twentieth) or the plain season day number (*the forty-second*, *the fifty-first*) |

## Spelling, decided and recorded

`bible/characters.md` and the Volume 01 plan locks say the bible uses US spelling (labor, behavior, license). **The prose does not.** Across Chapters 1–49 the manuscript uses *labour, colour, neighbour, favour, honour, fibre, metre, plough* and the noun *licence* against the adjective *licensed*, in about two hundred and seventy instances, and Batch 0005 follows the prose rather than the state file. **A later writer must not reintroduce US forms into the prose**, and this line is recorded so that the discrepancy is not read as a defect. The *bible* still says US spelling; **the prose governs, and where a later phase wants to change this it is a whole-manuscript copy-edit, not a chapter fix.**

## Proposed canon changes recorded, not silently applied — Batch 0005

Batch 0005 added seven small things by drafting. All are local detail, mechanisms, places, or people, and none changes a world rule:

1. **The district works docket** — a one-sheet record of a tool kit drawn out of a district store against an item number, with no order date and no work description on it. It is a document a foreman can take off a nail in a yard, and it is **not** the works schedule and does not produce one.
2. **The office of *schedule clerk, fen drains***, and the fact that a district works order is signed by a post and not by a man, and that the man in the post does not come out. 3. **Perrin Osse's margin entry of the forty-second** — *amount not disputed, sum not received, no facility exists in the form for non-payment* — and the resulting structural fact that **a schedule of damage against a body with no charter is a sum that is entered and not owed, and the district's only remedy is the emergency clause.** This is the volume's political last move and it is a procedure, not a person.
4. **The conditional charter's five sheets**, including the fifth, which adopts Gael Senn's second wording into district charter law. A new instrument class: a charter that expires at the end of a season by its own terms.
5. **Kellan's monitored charter and its six conditions**, written by a district clerk who said in advance that she was not the district.
6. **The nine-foot toe ditch at the low corner** and the improvised thirty-or-forty-yard cut to Weir Row, both of them the cooperative's own hand, both entered as section-nine margin notes, and **Marek's own sentence that the second one is the bypass and is not the bypass.** The bypass itself remains unbuilt and the four men remain unasked.

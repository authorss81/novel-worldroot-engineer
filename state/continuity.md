## 1. The premise, and a serious standing doubt about it

**The specified novel is *The Worldroot Engineer*.** Scientific fantasy and ecological
progression. Marek Vale, twenty-nine, an agricultural engineer and irrigation surveyor at
Lantern Reach in the reclaimed wetlands of Tarrow Fen, discovers that the world's magic
grows through a living subterranean network. He wants the charter, the well, the next
sowing, and a sister who has a life beyond the fen. He is good at finding leaks and bad at
asking for help. His flaw is control through expertise: he reaches the technically correct
answer and then believes that correctness entitles him to decide alone.

The full premise, the world, the power system and the ending lock are in `bible/` and
`outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`. **Read those before writing a morning. They
are short and they are the contract.**

### The standing doubt, stated once and left for a human decision

**The manuscript on disk is not that novel.** Across all 657 chapter files: `Rootway`
appears in zero, `Worldroot` in zero, `rootwoken` in two, `magic` in two, `monster` in
one. What has actually been written for fourteen volumes is a ledger about a holding that
keeps a wall, a gate, a launder, a book and thirty-five unresolved questions, with a
recurring cast, a day clock and a set of numeric series. It is internally consistent and
it is competently written, and it is not the book `NOVEL_SPEC.md`, `bible/premise.md` and
`outline/series.md` specify.

**This pass did not change the planned plot, and it did not attempt to.** Re-planning two
and a half volumes against the premise is a canon decision with an author behind it, not
a repair. The drift is recorded here and in `state/open-threads.md` under the decision
queue, where it is the first item. **A later pass, or a human, owns it.**

Two things follow for any writer who picks this up.

- **The bible is still authoritative and still describes a story that has not been
 written.** Do not treat the manuscript as evidence that the premise has been retired.
- **Iona Vey is the fixed antagonist of the series.** She is not new, she is not killed,
 and she is not in custody at the end. **No new final enemy may be introduced**, and a
 writer who does so is obliged to flag it rather than quietly add it.

## 2. The world as the bible states it, which is the world the book must be in

Veyra is temperate and heavily farmed, and every settlement is tied to the Rootway: a
living tissue of mineral-wood filaments and fungal threads that moves water, heat,
nutrients and chemical memory through the land. Its activity is the source of magic.
Magic is not a substance poured over the world; it is a visible form of a living
system's work.

Three practical layers, with no clean boundary: topsoil exchange, visible in ordinary
work and repairable by compost, rotation, cover crops and careful irrigation; the living
trunkway of thicker tubes that can be grafted or bridged but must stay alive and matched
to local water pressure; and the deep archive of rootwood and fungal mats that stores old
environmental patterns, that the Crown Engine reaches, and that is incomplete, damaged
and open to deliberate editing.

**Rootlight is metabolic energy carried by living systems and is not unlimited mana.**
Memory is environmental pattern and is not prophecy or mind-reading. Rootwoken creatures
are neither automatically evil nor automatically innocent. **A place may have rootlight
and still lack water, seed or stable soil, and a place may have rich soil and be cut off
from the trunkway.** Magic cannot replace labour, fertiliser, water management, seed
selection, medicine or negotiation. The world is not morally balanced: a forest can
recover and it does not owe anybody a harvest.

**History, in the order the volumes need it.** Centuries of local root communes, each
maintaining its own node by its own method. Then the Ash Winter, 312 years ago, and the
coalition that built the Crown Engine: living wood, stone and metal, built to move water
and rootlight into the capital, with a human decision loop inside a living network
because a machine that chose among competing needs needed a caretaker. It was meant to be
temporary. The coalition dissolved before the repair budget did.

Then the Quiet Break, 121 years ago: the engine's failsafe sealed several heartroots
because it pruned any flow pattern it could not tax or predict. Officials called it a
natural collapse. It created the modern scarcity, the legal power of well charters, and
the first large population of rootwoken creatures. Chancellor Iona Vey later reinforced
the pruning cycle and called it **False Season**, making a centralised caretaker look like
the only way to prevent famine. The **White Mercy** is her current programme: pruning
branches outside the Auberron Basin and outside the central maintenance zone to guarantee
one predictable harvest in the core, and it is a schedule rather than a season.

## 4. The volumes

Full detail for each is in `outline/volume-NN.md`. The arc in one line each:

1. **The White Furrow**, a living branch answers a damaged well; Marek's first fieldbook impression carries a maintenance pattern older than his father.
2. **The Graft Market**, root memories disagree with Crown maps; the Quiet Break was not the first deletion.
3. **The Memory Orchard**, scarroot preserves a famine-era engineering sequence; the first engineers pruned deliberately.
4. **Salt Without Rain**, rootwoken follow a broken flow and are not all attacking.
5. **The Heartroot Rota**, one heartroot sends the same pulse in different regions; False Season is a controlled signal.
6. **The False Season**, the Engine maintains the signal; Iona and the Continuity Office are editing local seasons.
7. **The Bridge Below**, the engine prunes what it cannot tax; the scarcity is a policy choice.
8. **What the Roots Remember**, Aldren's final fieldbook message; it is an edited archive fragment and he died interrupting the pruning cycle.
9. **The Rootless Vote**, Iona offers central control and it works, which is what makes refusal hard.
10. **The Undercommons Road**, rootwoken and Undercommons refuse the same bridge for different reasons; consent includes maintenance, exit and revision.
11. **The Crown Engine**, the final chamber can restore one continuous flow, at the cost of pruning everything it cannot synchronise.
12. **The Many-Handed Spring**, released patterns boom, then conflict; one restored network cannot mean uniformity.
13. **The Green Testament**, the covenant forbidding permanent binding is proved; the Aldren memory is the evidence.
14. **The White Mercy** *(current)*, Iona begins the first pruning phase. Tova abandons a seed vault. The climax is the regional network reaching the capital and leaving one secondary city unwatered. The resolution is an offer across a table and a refusal, recorded by Nia Vale.
15. **The Root Commons**, maintenance, consent and refusal defined before the cycle completes.
16. **The Returning Root**, Marek defeats Iona by dividing his own access and breaking the Engine's single caretaker.

**Volume 14's own lock, from `outline/volume-14.md`:** forty-nine mornings, days 851 to
899. The surrender of the Aldren memory is paid inside this volume, before the offer, on
a morning that is neither its first nor its last, and not as its climax. The volume does
not explain what the root is, and nobody in it is entitled to say.

## 5. Two questions this series may never answer

They are named once, here, and not again, and no morning may ask, name, hint at,
paraphrase or answer either.

1. **What the root is.** The answer is that nobody on the page knows. The volume's
 business is branches, load, water, a network and an offer. It does not explain the
 wood, it does not explain the maintenance zone, it does not explain the sixty-six, and
 it does not let Marek become the person who finally says what is under the floor.
2. **The second one.** Untouched for ten volumes counting the one now closed. It is not
 named in this file and it is not to be named in any state file.

**On the second night four records that cannot all be true are put on a bench in the mud**, and the arithmetic resolves on the page: the log says eleven hundred turns a day at the head, the ground takes seven hundred and twenty, the difference is three hundred and eighty, and **Kellan's own book says his side draw has never been above three hundred — eighty a day, a hundred and seventy-five thousand two hundred turns over six years, never once on the same line of the same page.** The built return main passes rather more than a third of what it should, the best figure on the miller's board in thirty-one years is two hundred and sixty-one, the wheel on the crest night reads **four hundred and nineteen**, and the sixty that is missing came up by something that is not the return main and **nobody in that yard knows what.** Lissa Vale brings the fieldbook and the use log to the water's edge and reads every line aloud; **Sera Quill makes the licensed read and the volume's last Fieldbook panel is spent, once, three plain lines, in her hands**; and she says the deeper answer plainly: **what is in the wood is not a memory of a season that has not happened, it is a work order — a target pattern, and the word for a target pattern is not a prophecy, it is a specification.** She has seen the structure of one before and will not say where, or what, or why, and those three go into the record as questions. **Nobody names who, what, or why.**

# Chapter Summaries

One compact entry per drafted chapter. Volume 01, Batch 0001 (days 1–11), files in `workspace/volume-01/batch-0001/`.

## Volume 01 — The White Furrow

**1. Iron in the Water** (day 1, well head, ~2,700 words)
The pump test comes in seven short (41/48, static lift, collar shut) and the machine is sound. Marek opens the well head with Ilsa Kern, gets an arm to the elbow, and finds the wound held not by a seal but by a **compound pad** — a base coat four seasons old with a thinner dressing laid on it every year since, never replaced, all of it sitting on the scarwood by weight, with warm iron-smelling water coming up through the middle of it at the six o'clock position. Kellan's log records a proper seal. Marek also notes the pad beating at about one beat in four seconds, out of step with the pump. Clerk Perrin Osse takes the charter-condition walk-through, explains that a charter condition is a thing to be *shown* and not repaired, and records seventeen days. Marek announces, in front of the crew, that the fault is in the root line and he will prove it in front of an inspector rather than in a shed. Ends with the wrapped fieldbook's needle refusing to settle while the head is open. **Change:** the fault is located and the log is exposed as the problem. **Cost:** nothing yet but a public promise.

**2. The Loaned Steward** (day 1, seed house yard into evening, ~3,100 words)
Tova Reed of the Dryfoot Trust arrives at midday with eleven crates of the Longneck rye, the only dry-land line the Salt March has left. She has read the cooperative's filed numbers before she met anyone. In the yard, in front of the members, she makes Marek do the sum: the log claims 1,100 turns/day delivered to Lantern Reach ground, the ground can take 720, and 832,000 turns over six years went somewhere the cooperative does not own. She names four conditions: a measured draw she may witness and file alongside his; published numbers open to any member, stranger, or clerk; her own veto on any use of the seed vault; and a copy of everything to Cray whether or not it helps. She will look at the cooperative's records for two days and say nothing until they are out. She notices the mill-keeper's hand tally board and asks nothing about it. **Change:** a hostile, precise reader is inside the books. **Cost:** the fiction that the paperwork is clean.

**3. The Coupling** (day 3 afternoon, the closing hours of Tova's two-day look, ~3,900 words)
Marek's loading test on the seed house return line. Tova reads the plan in four seconds and refuses; Ilsa refuses without understanding it. He starts it anyway. Cold iron water comes up through the vault's rootwood bed; three trays of the Longneck go translucent. Tova kills the draw and shears the coupling with his own spanner, then tells him to shut the rig down, which he does himself. **No rootwork occurs and none is available**: he wants the wood to say something and it is wood, wet and cold, doing nothing, patiently, for as long as he keeps asking. Two records come out of it that agree without knowing about each other — his gauge says the return will not pass what the log claims, Tova's tape says the return is 22 rods of 8 inch against a specified 41 rods of 12 inch — and the missing margin is compost. Tova rules three trays spoiled and does not soften it. Kellan's crew applies the last compound top-up to the pad that evening; Renn Tallow says, *you cannot close a thing with tar.* **Change:** Kellan's number is false for a physical reason, not a clerical one. **Cost:** the coupling is destroyed and unreplaceable before the eighteenth, part of a family's seed is gone, two days of the season are gone.

**4. The Vote and the Ledger** (day 3 evening, seed house, ~3,200 words)
Tova withdraws only her vault condition and states in advance what the March will and will not lend. Marek owns the spoiled trays in the first thirty seconds. Dunmar Fitch will not enter a true cause a hearing officer would reject. Bram Oskell arrives in person with Kellan's offer: a free gasket and a proper seal in exchange for a two-year exclusive extension **and** the cooperative's confirmation of six years of maintenance. Tova explains that the offer buys a signature, not a seal. Lissa refuses it in front of the room. Marek refuses the extension and orders the cooperative's own draw figures, log, work book and loading test opened to anyone who asks, with his own error on the same page in the same hand. Oskell mentions, in passing, that Sera Quill is on the fen road on the sixth day. **Change:** leverage traded for exposure. **Cost:** the open book, which is not a rule but a smaller and more dangerous thing.

**5. The Woman With the Old Book** (day 6, fen road, morning, ~2,700 words)
Sera Quill is walking to a one-day job at the salt cistern and will not stop. Marek offers a written scope instead of a favor. She names her terms: his raw notes, not copies; he does not touch the instrument unsupervised; she signs only what she has seen, with her license and her name behind it. Fee: fourteen pounds, paid personally, second visit another fourteen. She tells him one thing for free: a branch on this fen was cut on Crown money eight years ago while Aldren was in the ground, and she has spent six years failing to find out by whom. She has papers she will not show him. She has not asked for the fieldbook and tells him she will ask, in front of witnesses, when she asks. **Change:** a licensed witness with a fee, a scope, and her own agenda. **Cost:** fourteen pounds he will not get back, and the dread of being asked about the kit.

**6. Measured Water** (days 7–8, well head, the batch midpoint, ~4,600 words)
Two days of quarter-hourly logging. Sera brings two hundredweight of compost for the return and insists on it, because logging a wound without feeding it makes the wound worse. The kit is unwrapped in front of the crew. The copper contact clip is the path; the fieldbook bead and the well's conduit are the anchor; the compost is the return, and she makes him say all three aloud. The brass gauge and the fieldbook's needle disagree, and the margin widens each hour; she forbids him to reconcile them. On the second afternoon the pressure comes up in events of two, three, four and five beats about four seconds apart, she is sent for a second dial, Gael goes to the sump, and in four seconds with nobody in the pit Marek gets a work sequence — valve, hand, wait, valve, lamp, valve — over the well's own older rhythm, with a shaft and his father's hands on a wheel in the middle of it, and a date in his father's hand eight months after the disappearance. He ends the session himself, writes three findings, records his own reading as UNVERIFIED before she can ask, states the impossible date is probably a copying error and must be treated as one, and hands her the raw notes instead of keeping them. Costs: nosebleed, disorientation, and a **memory bleed** — the term she gives him. No mark on his skin; she says it will not stay that way. **Change:** the fault is proven to be upstream, and the reading cannot be proved. **Cost:** the first private instrument, surrendered.

**7. Who Cuts a Branch** (day 9, Weir Row, all day, ~4,600 words)
Marek and Gael walk onto ground that the charter's fourth schedule puts under a maintenance order — a trespass against the cooperative's own charter. A Crown sign on the fifth post reads *BRANCH MAINTENANCE — DISTRICT SCHEDULE. NO LOCAL HOLDER. REPORT CUTS TO THE SUB-OFFICE, FENNMERE.* Cart ruts sit eight feet back on the soft berm. The branch was cut, healed, and cut again, with two die-struck serials: **N-2214/K** on the old healed face and **N-3318/K** on the fresh one. Marek dates it as a range, writes *both* possible dates side by side, draws it to scale with a scale bar, and has Gael Senn sign as a second witness. He finds his grandmother's marker ninety yards away — HANSA KERN, 1802, with a cup dish worn into the top and a later line about the short road to the weir — and does not tell Gael until he has to. Ilsa arrives at two, makes him put her grandmother's road in the margin in his own hand, and tells him to say her name to the inspector not because it helps him. Renn Tallow waits at the road fork with an offer: a full parts kit for the season for the notebook page. Marek refuses aloud, in front of Gael, because he wants it on paper. A boy rides in at dusk: the annual inspection has been brought forward to tomorrow. **Change:** the enemy is a schedule, not a man, and the ground is somebody else's. **Cost:** an enemy who now knows what he is looking for, and one day left.

**8. The Inspection Came Early** (day 10, batch climax, first half, ~4,400 words)
Inspector Ansel Bray arrives with Perrin Osse and the emergency-seizure clause: seal the head, take the seed stock, or refer for relocation. He reads the open book for an hour and finds the district's own filing against the cooperative's work book page. The pad, the trespass, and Marek's own signatures make this look like cooperative negligence — until the serial. Bray identifies **N-2214/K** as a maintenance-schedule number, one item executed twice on a K three-year cycle, and a half-sheet of a table older than the cooperative's charter. The cut is not unregistered; it is on a schedule no local holder was told about. Marek then unwraps the fieldbook in front of the clerk, reads Finding 3 aloud, states in the same breath that it is his own unreliable reading and cannot be proof of anything, and asks Bray to enter it as an unverified observation. Bray enters it, declines to seize, and moves the decision to the eighteenth — the charter's own expiry, so nothing is solved. Sera reads the serial, goes very quiet, and says only that she has seen the shape of that table before and will not say where. Kellan arrives at noon, takes his hat off for the first time in six years, and says his crew pulls the compound kit out of the fen tonight. The glass has dropped two tenths. **Change:** the instrument is on a Directorate record and can be seized. **Cost:** eight days he already had, plus the only thing he owns that has ever told him the truth.

**9. The Thing at the Weir** (day 10 night, storm, batch climax, second half, ~4,600 words)
A storm comes in off the west. Gael Senn goes up the fen road with a hooked pole to save the north lamp; Ilsa sandbags the pit. The pasture flock — 130 head — comes down the embankment crown with a drover's dog and a rootwoken pathhound walking beside them, keeping them off a line on the ground. It is not attacking; it is herding, and it stops 130 animals dead on a twelve-foot crown. Gael is pinned on the maintenance plank over the race. Renn Tallow passes with the compound kit and does not stop. With no supervisor, no license, and no time, Marek puts his bare hands on the scarroot's cut face and gets four seconds that are **wrong** — the flow reads as going away instead of coming up. Damaged wood lies. He then does the actual work with a crowbar and his own graft shears, levers the healed seam open, and vents the dead branch's stored pressure into the old drainage channel. The animal loses the route it can feel, breaks off, and leaves on its own. It is not commanded. Gael crosses whole. Marek collapses, is sick, cannot account for the last hour of the walk, and finds his own handprint on a stone he does not remember touching. He knows the name of what happened to him now: **memory bleed**, and it is four seconds longer than the first. The seam will not close and the branch bleeds from now on. No animal comes back. The storm drops nine tenths, the pit floods, the pad lets go all at once, and nineteen pounds of fiber and fortyweight of compound are on a cart up the fen road. **Change:** the flock and the seed vault's compost return are gone. **Cost:** animals, a compost heap, a branch that will bleed for years, and the strongest evidence the Crown will later have against Marek.

**10. Seven Days** (day 11, seed house, morning into evening, ~4,800 words)
The cooperative makes a costed list on the back of a seed order. Tova gives the return arithmetic: 4 hundredweight a month of bought compost at 4 shillings is eighty pounds a month against a treasury of five pounds eleven, and a bed with no return kills seed in a fortnight. Lissa asks, in front of everyone, whether Marek intends to hand the fieldbook in. Perrin Osse serves two documents: the district's filing note, and Kellan's second letter — the gasket and the seal offered free, exclusivity removed, with the accusation left in. **The cooperative votes four things and one refusal.** Four: a seal built by hand with the date, the hands, and a witness, signed by someone recorded as never having done the work; one shared draw with a written return plus a standing pressure log open to anybody and a monthly root-change report filed with the sub-office; a repair attempt Sera Quill can certify, on her signed scope, with *as it stands tonight* on the front of it; and Tova's seed annex, entered in the cooperative's book and offered to the district as a condition of renewal. The refusal: Kellan's letter, declined in front of the district's own clerk, who asks that the refusal be recorded. **The bypass is not a fifth vote** — Marek writes it in the margin with a figure against it and no money, and Dunmar rules under section nine that a note by an officer is evidence of what the cooperative intended and not of what it did, so it is carried as a note, unnumbered and unsigned by anyone else. On Tova's evidentiary rule — any reading taken by an unlicensed hand is logged in the seed house and may be checked by anyone who asks — the fieldbook goes into Lissa's custody under a written use log, and Marek does not argue and gets no speech about it. Sera signs the scope. Nia says the cart is booked for the twentieth and asks for a written list of what she takes and where it goes. **Change:** he is the man in the room who is not holding the instrument. **Cost:** the fieldbook, and an obligation the cooperative voted for itself.

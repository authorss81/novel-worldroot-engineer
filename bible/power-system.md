# Power-System Bible: Rootwork

## The governing principle

Magic in Veyra is **rootwork**: the deliberate engineering of a living exchange system. The Rootway gathers energy from photosynthesis, decomposition, moving water, mineral nutrients, and the slow transfer of heat. The usable expression of that energy is **rootlight**.

Rootlight is not a mana pool. It is energy in motion inside living tissue or a living vessel. It can be moved, split, stored briefly, or redirected, but it cannot be created from nothing. A spell is successful only when the practitioner supplies a living anchor, a path, and something the system is willing to exchange.

## What rootlight can do

Within the limits below, rootlight can:

- accelerate or delay plant growth while preserving the plant's biological integrity;
- move water through connected rootwood and porous conduit;
- carry heat and cold between connected sites;
- reinforce living structures and heal damaged tissue slowly;
- send a short pattern through a living plant, root bridge, or fungal network;
- make field instruments respond to local pressure;
- reveal environmental memory in rootwood and scarwood.

It cannot:

- create soil, water, seed, food, or living bodies from nothing;
- raise the dead;
- make a promise, oath, or person obey a command;
- read a person's private thoughts;
- predict the future with certainty;
- travel through salt water, dead stone, or an unconnected gap;
- remain potent indefinitely in dead material;
- replace ordinary engineering, medicine, or labor.

A rootlight spell is therefore a change to a system, not a spell floating over the system.

## The three exchange requirements

Every working rootwork operation needs all three:

1. **A living anchor:** a plant, fungus, rootwood branch, or vessel containing living tissue. Dead wood can carry a trace but cannot serve as a reliable anchor.
2. **A compatible path:** connected rootway tissue, a carefully made rootbridge, or a physical conduit that touches living flow. Compatibility matters; mismatched moisture, heat, or species can rupture the connection.
3. **A return:** water, mineral nutrients, decomposing matter, heat balance, or labor over time. The return may be delayed, distributed, or social, but it cannot be omitted.

A strong practitioner can temporarily substitute their own body for a return. This is why rootwork can save a harvest quickly and why it damages the practitioner.

## The four core operations

### Attune

The practitioner reads pressure, temperature, flow direction, and recent memory through a living anchor. A mechanical flow gauge can show pressure without this ability, but it cannot show memory or intent.

- **Use:** Find a broken branch, distinguish a living node from a dead one, recognize a pattern that is not current weather.
- **Cost:** Minutes of disorientation, headache, nosebleed, and temporary sensory bleed. Repeated reading can blur the boundary between the operator's senses and the root's stored patterns.
- **Failure:** Dead, salt-poisoned, or heavily edited rootwood gives false or fragmentary information. The Fieldbook cannot tell the operator that the memory is false.

### Graft

The practitioner makes a living connection between a root branch, a plant, a tool, or another node. A graft changes both ends: it creates a path and creates new obligations.

- **Use:** Link a seed house to a pump, reconnect an irrigation branch, reinforce a road, or attach a fieldbook to a living bead.
- **Cost:** Seedstock, root material, exacting labor, and a permanent sensory trace. The anchor may weaken or die if the graft carries more than it can support.
- **Failure:** A bad match can spread pressure, disease, or a false memory through the connection. Sealing the branch does not undo the harm; it only changes where the pressure goes.
- **Early limit:** Before Patternkeeper, Marek can maintain no more than three active grafts without help. A fourth is not merely tiring; it usually fails.

### Draw and Return

**Draw** moves existing water, heat, or rootlight along a living path. **Return** sends usable material and balance back into a system. Draw is fast and visible. Return is slow, distributed, and often politically difficult.

- **Use:** Lift emergency water, warm a greenhouse, cool a seed vault, power a living lamp, or divert a flood.
- **Cost:** Rootburn, exhaustion, thirst, nausea, temporary loss of taste or smell, and a debt that appears elsewhere in the network.
- **Failure:** An unreciprocated draw can dry a downstream field, trigger rootwoken migration, poison a rootbridge, or force a node to close itself. The operator does not receive a clean panel saying exactly when the debt will be paid; the debt announces itself through changed plants, insects, water, and behavior.
- **Rule:** A draw may be justified. It may never be invisible. The story must keep the downstream cost visible.

### Prune and Pattern

Pruning a flow stops a harmful branch and redirects its pressure. Patterning coordinates several small flows so that a shared load is distributed among stable paths. Pruning is not destruction by default, and patterning is not harmless.

- **Use:** Contain a blight, keep a bridge from carrying a fatal load, protect a seed vault, or stop a false season from spreading.
- **Cost:** The operator must understand the whole connected system, maintain it in person or through trained anchors, and live with the redirected consequences.
- **Failure:** A bad prune can create a rootwoken, sterilize a field, or push pressure into a city. A pattern can outlive the person who designed it.

## The Fieldbook interface

A **Fieldbook** is an old survey tool with a living root bead, a mechanical gauge, and pages for handwritten notes. The bead translates only local pressure and recently stored patterns into plain impressions; it does not read the Deep Archive. It is not a sentient advisor and not a second mind.

The interface should appear rarely, at a decision point where the operator must choose. It uses short, concrete language and no statistics, quests, or moral verdicts. In practical narration, the Fieldbook is the novel's only recurring system-like interface; it never takes over the scene or supplies the protagonist's next objective.

**Allowed panel pattern:**

> The branch is losing pressure.  
> Return flow. Cut the bridge.  
> Choose.

The Fieldbook cannot provide the consequences of a choice, identify a hidden villain, or guarantee that an option is safe. Its readings can be biased by damaged memory or by records written into the root. In the middle volumes, the reader learns that some archived Fieldbooks were deliberately altered, so a familiar interface is not automatically trustworthy.

### Seedheart

A **seedheart** is a living public anchor made from mature local seedstock and rootwood. It can carry a shared maintenance pattern, a rotation schedule, or a fieldbook signal. It does not contain a person's soul, enforce obedience, or make the network sentient. A seedheart dies if its stewards remove its living return path, and every person using it can disconnect.

### Caretaker link

The **caretaker link** is the Crown Engine's human decision loop: a physical and rootway connection that lets an office choose among competing flows. It is a machine process, not a sentient command. A caretaker link can be inspected, limited, or disconnected, but severing it without a replacement often causes the flows it was managing to fail. The final conflict is over who may hold the link, not whether the network can speak.

### Rootmark transfer

A Stage 7 practitioner can transfer the living resonance of an existing skin rootmark into a seedheart, but only as a staged maintenance operation:

1. The practitioner makes direct contact with the seedheart and the existing rootmark while the replacement network is already carrying a tested share of the load.
2. A return operation copies only the agreed maintenance pattern. No thought, memory, identity, or authority is copied into another person.
3. The seedheart's stewards test the return path and may disconnect at any time. The transfer fails if any required region is unready.
4. The rootmark's private resonance is exhausted. The practitioner loses continuous access to the Deep Archive and can read it only through the public seedhearts and ordinary field instruments.

The operation is not a convenient teleport of power. It takes time, a living anchor, a stable load, and people willing to maintain what it creates.

### Caretaker handoff

A caretaker link can be transferred from the Crown Engine to a distributed commons only after the replacement anchors are carrying the flow. The handoff is physical and staged: open maintenance valves, test regional seedhearts, transfer the shared pattern, isolate Iona's terminal, and only then disconnect the old link. A premature disconnect causes the managed flows to fail, which is why the final team must build the replacement before they can stop the Engine.

## The rootwoken rule

When a living organism is repeatedly exposed to a damaged, contradictory, or forcibly repeated root pattern, it may retain the pattern in its body or behavior. This change is called **rootwoken**.

Rootwoken is not a moral category:

- A rootwoken animal may attack because it protects a territory or because it is in pain.
- A rootwoken settlement may trade, refuse, migrate, or defend itself.
- A rootwoken plant can provide medicine and still destroy a road.
- A person can carry a root pattern without becoming a monster.

The story does not use a hidden rule that a rootwoken life can be reset by killing its anchor. Destroying the anchor usually spreads the pattern and removes the community's ability to negotiate.

## The seven power stages

Power growth changes Marek's options and obligations. It never removes the need to work, coordinate, or accept limits.

### Stage 0 — Rootblind engineer

Marek has no direct rootwork. He can use ordinary tools, read soil, calculate water pressure, and recognize that a mechanical instrument and a living response disagree. He cannot see rootlight and can be fooled by a well-built fake. He can still make competent decisions because engineering knowledge matters before magic appears.

### Stage 1 — Touchroot

After an initial exposure to a living bead, Marek can sense pressure, heat direction, and gross memory through direct contact. He cannot draw energy or graft safely. Every reading leaves him tired, disoriented, and unsure whether a sensation belongs to him.

**First cost:** A root memory gives him a sudden, vivid image of his father's hands in a shaft. He has to decide whether it is evidence or grief wearing evidence's shape.

### Stage 2 — Grafthand

Marek can make small, reliable grafts between a living plant or rootwood tool and a limited local branch. He can keep two or three active links with careful maintenance. He cannot make a permanent bridge, and he cannot safely attach himself to the network.

**Cost:** The anchor and the practitioner both record the graft. Repeated work gives Marek a physical **rootmark**, a visible dark branching line along his wrist or forearm. The mark is a record of what he connected, not a badge of rank.

### Stage 3 — Drawwright

Marek can draw and return rootlight, water, and heat through a small system. He can save a field, power a pump, or keep a seed vault cool for a limited period. He cannot do this at range or without a living path.

**Cost:** Rootburn produces darkened veins, fever, numbness, loss of taste, and sometimes temporary blindness in one eye. A draw that saves one field can reduce a downstream well. The cost is physical and ecological, not an abstract penalty.

### Stage 4 — Patternkeeper

Marek can maintain a heartroot or small cluster of branches while multiple operators coordinate water, heat, and growth. He becomes more useful as a team member and less useful as a lone hero. He must keep returning to the network, train other rootwrights, and accept that his body can become a bottleneck.

**Cost:** When several failures reach one keeper, he experiences fragments of their pressure and pain as if they were his own. Long maintenance can produce shared dreams, insomnia, and difficulty recognizing his own anger. The network's failures are not transferable to a panel or a convenient report.

### Stage 5 — Memorydiver

Marek can read the Deep Archive and reconstruct past environmental conditions from rootwood: drought patterns, flood lines, old graft practices, and the effect of past policy on living tissue. He cannot read intent, future events, or a person's soul. A fabricated archive can fool him, and an honest archive can be incomplete.

**Cost:** **Memory bleed** becomes dangerous. He may remember a stranger's childhood from a node, lose a personal date, or mistake his mother's voice for a root pattern. Tova and Sera serve as living anchors, but they can be harmed if he uses them too long. A memory can also be surrendered as a one-time archive key or public proof; that loss is permanent and cannot be repaired by rest or return. The cost is relational as well as neurological.

### Stage 6 — Bridgebuilder

Marek can connect distant nodes with a high-capacity rootbridge, synchronize regional flows, and let trained operators carry a limited version of a fieldbook signal. He cannot compel a community to connect, and a bridge cannot cross every physical gap.

**Cost:** Every bridge is a shared vulnerability. A failure can transmit contamination, pain, and rootwoken behavior to every connected region. The bridge also creates a military and political target, so Marek's strongest power makes him responsible for a wider public danger. He can build a connection; he cannot own its consequences.

### Stage 7 — Commonskeeper

Stage 7 is not a solo spell. It is the ability to coordinate a distributed network through consent, trained crews, public records, and local vetoes. Marek can act as an emergency router, but his power is strongest when others can refuse him and still keep the system alive.

**Cost:** He must give up exclusive access to the deepest archive and transfer the living resonance of his rootmark into living public seedhearts. Afterward Marek loses continuous regional awareness, but direct contact with a stressed anchor transmits pain and pressure more sharply; a large crisis can overwhelm him unless he disconnects and trusts other stewards. A split pattern is not a piece of his soul or a hidden binding: each anchor receives only the agreed maintenance signal and can disconnect without his permission. The final stage is a loss of personal reach in exchange for continuity without a caretaker.

## Combat and danger rules

- Rootwork can trap, slow, expose, or redirect a creature, but it cannot summon generic elemental attacks.
- A direct draw against a living target requires a physical connection and can injure both the target and the practitioner.
- A rootbridge can be a weapon if someone cuts its return path; the weapon is the collapse of a shared system, not a magic beam.
- Rootwoken creatures are more dangerous when a pattern is forced through them. A calm negotiation may remove a threat more reliably than combat.
- A well-built physical barrier, a safe exit, a seed source, and a trained operator can matter more than a larger rootlight draw.
- The strongest act in a crisis is often a partial, costly repair that keeps people alive while the larger system remains imperfect.

## Cumulants and recovery

There is no full reset button. Recovery after rootburn includes rest, clean food, medical care, and time away from a live connection. Recovery after an ecological draw may require seasons of compost, fallowing, replanting, and negotiation. A practitioner who uses the same anchor repeatedly can make the next repair less effective even after their body feels recovered.

Marek's growth therefore changes what he is willing to ask of other people. Higher power increases the size of the system he can touch and the number of people who will suffer if he fails.

# Terminology Bible: Canonical Terms

This file is the vocabulary authority for Veyra. Use these terms consistently in outlines, chapter cards, summaries, and prose. Regional characters may use local words in dialogue, but the underlying concept should remain identifiable.

## Core ecological and magical terms

### Rootway

The living subterranean network that links soil, water, plants, fungi, minerals, and heat beneath Veyra. It is not a tree, a computer, a deity, or a single organism with a human mind. It behaves as an ecology of connected tissues at different scales.

### Rootlight

The usable energy carried by active rootway flows and living vessels. It comes from metabolism, not unlimited mana. Rootlight can move water, heat, nutrients, and chemical patterns, but it cannot create matter or guarantee a harvest.

### Rootwork

The practice of reading, grafting, drawing, returning, pruning, and patterning a living root system. Rootwork is engineering performed with living infrastructure, not a spell list.

### Attune

To read pressure, heat direction, flow, and environmental memory through a living anchor. Attunement is sensory and interpretive; it is not mind-reading.

### Graft

A deliberate living connection between compatible root tissues, a plant, a tool, or a node. A graft creates a path and a new maintenance obligation. It is never merely a wire or pipe.

### Draw

A rapid transfer of existing water, heat, or rootlight through a living path. A draw may save a crop but creates a downstream debt unless a return is arranged.

### Return

The slower process of giving water, nutrients, decomposing matter, heat balance, or labor back to a root system. Returns may be distributed across seasons and people; they cannot be skipped without consequence.

### Prune

To stop or redirect a harmful flow. Pruning is a legitimate repair tool, but the pressure is not destroyed. It moves somewhere else and must be traced.

### Pattern

A recurring relationship between flow, season, water, soil, and living tissue. A root pattern can be inherited, altered, damaged, or deliberately written. It is not a spell command.

### Memory bleed

The dangerous loss of boundary between a practitioner's memories and environmental patterns read from rootwood. Symptoms include misplaced scenes, impossible sensory detail, and emotional responses to another person's history.

### Rootmark

The visible branching scar left in a practitioner's skin after a deep graft or repeated rootwork. It records connections and can become a liability. A rootmark is not a permanent source of power. At Stage 7, its living resonance can be transferred into a seedheart as a shared maintenance pattern, but the transfer exhausts private access and does not copy the practitioner's mind into anyone else.

### Rootburn

The acute physical reaction to excessive draw or mismatched return: darkened veins, fever, numbness, nausea, loss of taste or smell, temporary sight loss, and sometimes temporary paralysis. Rest helps the body but cannot erase the ecological debt.

### Rootwoken

A living organism changed by repeated exposure to a damaged, contradictory, or forcibly repeated root pattern. Rootwoken creatures include animals, plants, fungi, and people. Rootwoken is a category imposed by outsiders, not a moral judgment.

## Infrastructure and people

### Heartroot

A major living node where several trunkway branches meet. A heartroot can support a settlement and connect distant regions, but it is also a vulnerable pressure point. A heartroot is not a throne.

### Rootbridge

An engineered living connection between separated nodes or conduits. A bridge can move water, heat, rootlight, and limited patterns. It requires compatible tissue, maintenance on both ends, and consent from local stewards. It can fail as a shared system rather than as a simple break. Capacity is named by the work it carries, not by a claim to ownership:

- **Local conduit:** a short connection between adjacent branches or a settlement's well, seed house, and pump. It serves a small household or neighborhood load and has a local steward.
- **Seed-route bridge:** a maintained connection built to carry irrigation for a seed convoy and the settlements along a dry route. It needs public ledgers, a return path, and crews at both ends, but it is not yet a regional network.
- **Regional bridge:** a high-capacity connection that synchronizes several heartroots or maintenance zones. It needs trained crews, a shared schedule, and local vetoes; a single practitioner cannot build or own it.

The categories describe load and responsibility, not a promise that a bridge will work forever. A Stage 2 practitioner can help assemble a seed-route bridge with local crews, while a regional bridge requires collective capacity. The three names should remain consistent in prose and outlines.

### Scarroot

Rootwood exposed by a severing, drought, fire, or long-term overdraw. Scarroot stores strong environmental memory and can be unstable. It may be useful as evidence or dangerous as an anchor.

### Sunwell

A human-built well that uses living rootwood, a mechanical pump, or both to lift water. A sunwell can be lawful, stolen, neglected, or cooperative; the word does not imply that the well is safe by default.

### Flow gauge

A mechanical instrument that shows pressure or flow through a needle, reed, or vibrating wire. It does not read memory, intent, or future weather.

### Graft shears

A tool for cutting or shaping living rootwood cleanly. The tool can preserve living tissue; it cannot revive dead wood or decide whether a graft should be made.

### Fieldbook

A survey instrument with a living root bead, a mechanical gauge, and handwritten notes. It translates a limited local pressure or recent-pattern sample into brief, plain impressions. It cannot read the Deep Archive, grant power, identify villains, or tell the truth with certainty.

### Rootlight lamp

A living bulb that stores and releases rootlight after being fed with water and decomposing matter. A lamp is useful infrastructure, not a symbol of ownership.

### Seed vault

A protected store of dry seed, living seed, and crop histories. Vaults are strategically important because a restored harvest requires both viable seed and people who know how to grow it.

### Rootwright

An engineer trained in practical root infrastructure, field diagnosis, and safe maintenance. The term includes public servants, contractors, teachers, and inspectors; it is a profession, not a moral class.

### Root commune

A local group that maintains one or more nodes through shared labor, knowledge, and limits. A root commune can be old, improvised, or newly formed. It is the opposite of a central command in structure, though it can still fail internally.

### Root Commons

A regional network of root communes, seed trusts, and maintenance crews that share records, resources, and responsibility without granting one central authority control. The Root Commons is the final intended political arrangement, not a magical entity.

## History and politics

### Quiet Break

The 121-year-old event in which the Crown Engine's safety system sealed or pruned major heartroots during a succession of famines. The Crown presented it as a natural collapse. The event began the modern scarcity and the political power of well charters. Records that appear to contain seasons not yet lived are target patterns written into the Engine before implementation, not prophecy.

### Crown Engine

A 312-year-old machine of living wood, stone, and metal that moves water and rootlight through the Deep Archive. It was built to coordinate famine relief and later became a central political tool. The Engine does not think like a person; its caretaker process and selective pruning create the appearance of intention.

### First Continuity

The coalition of engineers, rulers, grain merchants, and local custodians who built the Crown Engine during the Ash Winter. Their original covenant included limits on binding a living network. The surviving public version omits those limits.

### Ash Winter

A 312-year-old climate catastrophe that motivated the Crown Engine project. It is historical context, not a recurring supernatural event.

### Long Thirst

A multi-decade period of drought, war, and declining local maintenance that led to the expansion of Crown well charters.

### Brinewake Collapse

A named regional failure in the Salt March nineteen years before the present. A Crown pruning pulse severed the western salt corridor's three heartroots, killing 4,200 people in the first winter and displacing 9,000 more. The public account blamed salt creep and weather. The later evidence showed that Sera Quill had removed the red-line warning from a safety relay and falsified the maintenance report that could have isolated the pulse. She did not cause the collapse alone, but her concealment delayed the warning and the compensation record was never opened.

### False Season

A human-imposed repeating root pattern that makes root systems, surface conditions, plant behavior, and migration diverge from the actual sky. The Engine shifts water, heat, pressure, and growth timing through selected living branches; soil moisture and local temperature then make the result feel like weather. It cannot create clouds, rain, or a new season in the sky. It is not a natural season or prophecy. The Continuity Office uses it to demonstrate the need for centralized control.

### White Harvest

A season in which rootlight withdrawal or a damaged seasonal pattern makes grain heads turn white before the grain fills. The White Harvest is a real agricultural result, not a prophecy. The third consecutive White Harvest in Veyra is the opening crisis.

### White Mercy

Iona Vey's final plan to prune every root branch outside the Auberron Basin and central maintenance zone, storing the released flow for one predictable harvest in the core. The name is a political claim, not a description of a natural mercy.

### Seedheart

A living public anchor made from mature seedstock and rootwood. It can carry a shared maintenance signal without binding anyone's mind or giving the network a human-like voice. Its stewards must maintain its return path, and every user can disconnect from it.

### Living bind

A rule, graft, charter, or political arrangement that makes a person, living node, or community dependent on another party's continuing permission, obedience, or access. It includes an involuntary human anchor, a proprietary lock on a living return path, removing a community's ability to refuse a bridge, and encoding a caretaker's authority as the only lawful way to keep flow alive. Ordinary time limits, fees, maintenance duties, and compensation are not living binds when the participant can still leave, disconnect, revise the terms, and return the living system to safe operation. **No living bind** means that no person, living community, or root flow may be made an involuntary permanent dependency.

### Caretaker link

The Crown Engine's human decision loop for choosing among competing flows. It is machinery, not a sentient command. It can be limited or disconnected, but doing so without a working replacement can make managed flows fail.

### Caretaker handoff

The staged, physical transfer of an Engine link to tested regional seedhearts. The replacement network must carry the load before the old link is isolated; a premature handoff causes managed flows to fail.

### Public proof

Evidence that a community outside the practitioner's private experience can test. A surrendered memory becomes public proof only after an independent operator reads the same physical node through a separate anchor, the original instrument and notes are preserved, and at least two independent records corroborate the event. A witness's belief, a Crown's summary, or a panel-like impression alone is not enough.

### Regional flow ledger

A public, copyable record of pressure, water, seed, maintenance, and refusal decisions across a region. It is evidence and coordination, not a private archive or a magical index. Its authority comes from corroboration, public maintenance, and the ability of other stewards to read and copy it.

### Charter of Many Wells

The political agreement that replaces a single central well charter with local charters, shared maintenance, public records, and regional vetoes. It can be amended or refused by the communities it covers; no single office holds the whole network.

### Hearth Crown

The federation of city-states ruled by the Hearth Throne. It maintains the capital, central roads, and well-charter law, but local customs and political authority vary by region.

### Well Directorate

The Hearth Crown department responsible for well inspections, taxes, emergency seizures, and charter disputes. Its employees range from sincere public servants to enforcers of policies that are already failing.

### Rootwright Assembly

The professional body responsible for training, inspection, and standards for root infrastructure. It has public-service and contract factions; it is not automatically right merely because it is professional.

### Undercommons

A loose network of local custodians and root communes. It shares techniques and warnings without a single leader. Some members want complete separation from Crown infrastructure; others cooperate selectively.

### Thornwild Assemblies

Forest communities, including human and rootwoken groups, that negotiate access to wild root routes. The assemblies recognize borders and make claims about land and memory. They are political communities, not scenery or a monster faction.

### Pale Orchard

A clandestine network of rootburners, seed smugglers, and former Crown technicians who sabotage infrastructure to prove that controlled magic cannot be trusted. Their attacks create real suffering and force the story to confront the difference between criticism and indiscriminate harm.

### Continuity Office

The small Crown department that maintains the Crown Engine and its doctrine of a single caretaker. It is led by Chancellor Iona Vey in the final movement.

## Characters and places

### Marek Vale

The male protagonist: agricultural engineer and irrigation surveyor from Lantern Reach. He wants to save his cooperative, then the Rootway, while learning not to turn competence into ownership.

### Tova Reed

Seedwright, crop historian, and Marek's approved slow-burn romantic partner. She represents local seed autonomy and the limits of a repair that is technically successful but socially costly.

### Nia Vale

Marek's younger sister and a traveling seed keeper. She wants a future that is not defined by family debt or the fen's borders.

### Sera Quill

Retired root engineer, former Continuity Office architect, mentor to Marek, and witness to the records and institutional aftermath of the Quiet Break rather than an eyewitness to the 121-year-old event.

### Soren Rill

Thornwild guide and rootwoken negotiator. He represents communities that may refuse a bridge, a survey, or a repair.

### Aldren Vale

Marek's missing father. The story confirms his death eight years ago during an attempt to interrupt the Engine's current pruning program; the final mystery is the edited fieldbook record, not a secret surviving superpower.

### Chancellor Iona Vey

Head of the Continuity Office and final antagonist. She seeks predictable centralized stewardship and controls the Crown Engine's False Season program.

### Lantern Reach

A worker-owned farming cooperative and Marek's home. Its well, seed house, embankments, and repair crews are the opening stage of the story.

### Tarrow Fen

The wet agricultural province surrounding Lantern Reach. It is vulnerable to floods, failed harvests, rootwoken migration, and Crown charter enforcement.

### Auberron

The Hearth Crown capital and the location of the Crown Engine. It is the political center, not the only place with real power or knowledge.

### Auberron Basin

The broad central plain around Auberron, containing the Crown's most protected farms, wells, and maintenance works. Iona's White Mercy preserves this zone by pruning the outer regions.

### Glassward

A highland region of exposed rootwood, old observatories, and sealed deep mines. It holds important historical evidence and difficult infrastructure.

### Salt March

A dry trade region of seed caravans, salt flats, and thin root flow. It makes migration and the distribution of seed central to the series.

### Thornwild

An old forest region of rootwoken communities, fungal networks, and negotiated boundaries. It is dangerous to outsiders and politically sovereign to its Assemblies.

## Usage rules

- Use **rootway** for the whole network, **heartroot** for a major node, **scarroot** for damaged tissue, and **rootbridge** for an engineered connection.
- Use **rootlight** for energy and **rootwork** for the practice. Do not call them mana, power, or magic in narration unless a character uses an imprecise everyday word.
- Use **memory** for environmental pattern stored in growth, not for telepathy or prophecy.
- Use **rootwoken** as an outsider category until a character establishes a more specific local name.
- Use **Crown Engine**, **False Season**, and **Quiet Break** consistently; do not introduce synonyms for them as new canon.
- Keep the System interface in plain language. It may say “The branch is losing pressure” or “Return flow,” but it does not issue a quest, assign a rank, or declare a moral truth.

## Scale and measurement

The world does not use a universal mana meter. Practical loads are described by use:

- A healthy living lamp can carry a household's light through one night.
- A cooperative sunwell can maintain a local dry-season load if its return path is intact.
- A local conduit can move water for a settlement or a single cooperative, while a seed-route bridge can carry an irrigation stream and a seed convoy along a dry route. Neither can support an entire city without a heartroot and trained crew.
- A regional bridge can synchronize several heartroots, but it carries a shared network load and cannot erase the political boundaries of the places it connects.
- Scarwood stores a trace of rootlight for days, not months.
- A rootmark records a connection; it does not measure the user's worth or total ability.
- The important consequence of any number is the downstream person, field, or region affected.

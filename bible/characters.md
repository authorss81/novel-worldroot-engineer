# Character Bible: The Worldroot Engineer

## Protagonist: Marek Vale

### Basic profile

- **Age at opening:** 29
- **Gender:** Male
- **Home:** Lantern Reach Work Cooperative, Tarrow Fen
- **Occupation:** Agricultural engineer, irrigation surveyor, and field mechanic
- **First appearance:** Late harvest, during a failed pump test and a Crown inspection
- **Physical presence:** Compact and sun-dark; usually carries a brass flow gauge, a folded fieldbook, soil-stained gloves, and a tool roll tied under one knee
- **Habits:** Measures twice, repairs before explaining, eats cold bread while reading, and forgets to sleep after a problem becomes personal
- **First impression:** Practical, skeptical, and better with a broken pump than with a frightened crowd

### Background

Marek grew up in the cooperative's shared workrows. His father, **Aldren Vale**, was a respected root engineer who vanished in a deep relay beneath Lantern Reach eight years earlier. Marek was twenty-one at the time and had just begun working under him. The official report blamed a cave-in and a surge of root pressure. Marek has spent the years since learning the ordinary explanations that people prefer to a truth they cannot repair.

He does not believe his father was a hero. He believes he was a careful engineer who made one decision under impossible pressure, then paid for it in a way that left his family with an unanswered question. That belief makes Marek both sympathetic to sacrifice and suspicious of noble-sounding choices.

His mother, **Lissa Vale**, manages the cooperative's seed house and has never forgiven Marek for leaving after their father's disappearance to take a capital contract. His younger sister **Nia** wants a life beyond the fen. The family is loving and practical, not a source of uncomplicated support. Lissa's forgiveness is not a reward Marek can claim by producing a useful answer: in the final aftermath he must return to the seed house, admit that he chose distance from the family while chasing the capital, and accept that she may work beside him without declaring the old abandonment forgiven.

### External want

At the beginning, Marek wants to secure the Lantern Reach charter, restore the cooperative's well, and fund the next planting. He wants to solve a problem that can be measured: water in, seed in, crop out. The immediate goal gives him a reason to work when the larger mystery would otherwise feel abstract.

Across the series, the external want grows from **saving his cooperative** to **preventing the deliberate collapse of the Rootway**. The change is gradual. He never stops needing the next harvest, but he learns that harvests are the visible edge of a larger system.

### Internal need

Marek needs to accept interdependence without treating it as surrender. He must learn to:

- share measurements and uncertainty;
- let local people refuse his design;
- delegate maintenance to people he does not control;
- treat consent as part of the engineering, not a ceremonial delay;
- accept a good result that is not the result he would have chosen.

His growth is measured by what he can leave running when he walks away, not by how many nodes he can personally touch.

### Flaw

Marek's central flaw is **control through expertise**. When he sees a failing system, his first instinct is to identify the single cause, isolate it, and impose the correct solution. He underestimates local knowledge because it lacks a clean formula and underestimates politics because it does not obey arithmetic.

He is not passive or stupid. He notices details, tests hypotheses, and often reaches the technically correct conclusion. His mistakes come from believing that a correct answer deserves sole authority.

### Fear

Marek fears another death under his watch. Beneath that fear is a deeper fear that dependence is dangerous: if he lets another person maintain the root, the other person may fail; if he trusts the network, the network may use memories he cannot control; if he accepts help, he may lose the ability to protect his family.

His arc does not cure this fear. It teaches him to act while it remains.

### Skills and limits

**Competent from the beginning:**

- Diagnoses irrigation failures by reading water pressure, soil texture, crop symptoms, and repair history.
- Builds and modifies pumps, gates, graft rigs, and simple fieldbooks.
- Keeps calm around machinery and frightened workers.
- Notices when an official measurement does not match a physical result.
- Can teach a practical procedure and learn a local method quickly.

**Initial limits:**

- He has no formal rootwork training and cannot safely use a Fieldbook.
- He lacks political influence, archival knowledge, and combat training.
- He is bad at asking for help before exhaustion makes the request impossible.
- His first magical successes are physically dangerous and can damage the field he means to save.

### Moral line

Marek refuses deliberate severing, involuntary binding, and the use of a person as a disposable anchor. Under pressure, his most serious failures are bypassing local consent, withholding a dangerous result, and treating his own emergency judgment as final.

- **His own ground (Chs 21, 29, 30):** **Three acres at the top end of the outer field**, worked on his own account for three seasons because the cooperative would not have them, drained by hand — four hundred and ten yards of ditch across three autumns. It is the best ground on the holding and the only thing in the volume that is his alone, and in Movement 3 **the repair he licensed is what kills it**, and because it is not the cooperative's it cannot be entered in the cooperative's book, charged to anybody, or shared. **This is the cost of the movement and it is structural, not sentimental.**
- **Ch 29, the fourth option:** he offers his own body as a return substitution and is refused in eleven seconds, on two grounds — he is Stage 0 with two contacts he cannot repeat, and a body is a return, not a source. He does not go round the refusal afterwards. He enters it with the lateness declared in the entry, in his own name, and the cooperative signs its own unprovability on the thirtieth.
- **Ch 34, eleven miles walked, and what he learned on the way.** He takes the notice to Idris Vane **in person** because a notice served by post is a notice a man can argue with, and both of the answers he might get have to be in Vane's own mouth. He trades away the amount in a stranger's kitchen to keep the cause: *cross out the box, do not write in it, and write at the top of the page in your own hand that the cooperative does not dispute the amount* — **and he accepts the trade knowing that if the cause is lost he owes sixty pounds ten out of four pounds five with no appeal.** **Ch 38, and the sentence he must live inside:** *I have no standing to object with, and if I had, the two of them are not people I could have stopped, and a body which needs a charter to be obeyed is not a body, it is a notice with a seal on it.*

### Character progression

- **Beginning:** “If I can measure the failure, I can keep the failure from reaching people.”
- **Middle:** “A working system is not the same as a just one.”
- **Late:** “I can offer tools, carry consequences, and still let other people choose.”
- **Ending:** He becomes a steward whose authority is deliberately distributed. His power is smaller, slower, and less impressive than a king's, but it can survive his absence.

## Primary relationships

### Tova Reed — slow-burn romantic partner

- **Age:** 31
- **Origin:** Salt March seed trust
- **Occupation:** Seedwright, crop historian, and keeper of living seed banks
- **First impression:** Precise, guarded, and unimpressed by title or reputation
- **Want:** Ensure local crop communities can decide which varieties to keep, trade, or give up; protect the Seed Commons from becoming a Crown inventory
- **Flaw:** She can turn disagreement into a principle so quickly that she misses a practical compromise
- **Strength:** She knows what a harvest costs in human labor and remembers varieties that official records erase
- **Power limitation:** She has no direct rootwork ability. Her influence comes from seed viability, negotiation, and the trust of growers.
- **The second refusal (Ch 29):** she refuses a four-day draw of 380 turns/day that would save Marek's own three acres, because the water comes out of the same shallow flow the seed bed stands in and the fourth day takes a foot of head off a bed at four degrees with an empty compost bin and the March's last tin in the press. **It is arithmetically correct, it costs her nothing, and she says so.** Her best sentence in the volume is that refusing him is cheap for her, and that the second time is easier, because the numbers do not care that it is you. **She is never rescued from this and never softened in it, and Marek is never rescued from letting it stand. **Movement 4 is the business face of the same disagreement and it is not tender.** She makes the seed house's own temperature, the compost shortfall and the statement that the crop may not come up go onto the annex in her own hand, against the trust's own interest, and have it entered in the trust's book as well. She strikes out the cooperative's one-season limitation because a season is not a commitment, and then **signs alone**, for nine thousand people not in the room, against a body of sixteen households with no charter, four pounds five and a bed at three and a half degrees, knowing there is no remedy and no party to sue. She refuses to let the March's guarantee be sent into a nameless book, because a copy must be a copy of a document. She puts the March's copy in a counting house and will not say whose hand it goes into, and is glad about that one.****
- **Why she is in Lantern Reach:** The Salt March's northern seed trust lost its relay and needed to compare its last viable dry-land lines with Lantern Reach's seed house. The cooperative granted Tova a temporary seat in exchange for a crop and pressure report. She came to challenge the cooperative's water use, not to seek a hero or a husband.
- **Living-anchor limitation:** Tova can serve as a voluntary human living anchor because her training is in seed and tissue handling, not because she has rootwork ability. Every anchor session records a purpose, duration, stop signal, and independent witness, and she can disconnect at any time.
- **Planned cost:** Her voluntary memory anchor in Volume 08 leaves her with partial hearing loss; she remains a capable leader rather than an exempt character

Tova and Marek begin as competitors over a failed field: he sees a water-delivery problem, she sees a seed line that should not be planted because its roots are carrying a scar pattern. Their first useful victory requires both diagnoses. They do not become close because they agree. They become close because they can name their disagreement without making the other person foolish.

**Relationship milestones:**

- Chapters 1–3: forced collaboration; Marek nearly destroys a seed vault while trying to prove his pump diagnosis.
- Early Volume 1: Tova saves his crew by refusing a dangerous draw; Marek respects the refusal.
- Volumes 2–3: shared seed and repair ledger; the first night they choose not to work alone.
- Volume 4: their first explicit romantic boundary after Marek hides a predicted rootwoken migration.
- Volumes 5–8: Tova anchors his memory readings and refuses to let him use her as a disposable stabilizer.
- Volumes 9–12: separate leadership paths; the relationship survives being publicly useful rather than private.
- Volumes 13–16: they choose a shared life without merging their work or giving Tova a supporting role in his final battle.

The romance is a partnership of two competent people with different duties. It does not cure Marek's flaw, make Tova surrender her principles, or erase political conflict.

### Nia Vale — younger sister

- **Age:** 23
- **Occupation:** Seed-house accountant and traveling seed keeper
- **Want:** Leave the fen on her own terms and build a mobile route for small cooperative seed banks
- **Flaw:** She treats every family obligation as a debt she must repay, then resents the people who accept the repayment
- **Relationship:** Affectionate, combative, and proud. Nia loves Marek but refuses to let his guilt dictate her future.
- **Arc:** She becomes the coordinator of the final seed supply network. Her success is not a return home; it is a reliable route that lets other families leave when they choose.

### Sera Quill — mentor and historical witness

- **Age:** 56
- **Occupation:** Retired root engineer, former Continuity Office architect, current freelance inspector
- **Want:** Repair the system she helped make and spend the rest of her life being useful without pretending her past was innocent
- **Flaw:** She treats confession as a substitute for restitution
- **Relationship:** She trained Marek's father, recognizes Marek's habits because he inherited them from Aldren, and becomes a difficult substitute parent rather than a perfect mentor.
- **Historical position:** She was not present at the 121-year-old Quiet Break. Her witness is through the surviving records, colleagues, and the later Brinewake relay she helped conceal.
- **Revelation:** Nineteen years ago she designed a Crown safety relay that could have isolated a pruning pulse before the **Brinewake Collapse** in the Salt March. The pulse killed 4,200 people in its first winter and displaced 9,000 more. She removed the relay's red-line warning, changed the log to routine recalibration, and signed a report blaming salt creep and weather. She did not operate the Engine or cause the collapse alone, but her falsification removed the warning that might have slowed it. Her testimony later concerns records and decisions she made, not an eyewitness memory of the Quiet Break.
- **The two findings that are hers (Ch 11):** she goes down the pit and finds a **callus across the top third of a two-hand wound** that four seasons of Kellar's tar kept alive, and she says the honest, painful thing: the log is a lie and the dressing worked, and she does not know how to write both in one line of a page. She is a technician before she is a moral, and Volume 01 catches her in that order.
- **The liability (Ch 11, priced out loud):** she is a freelancer with no employer, no district behind her, and no office that would stand in front of her at a hearing. *A licensed engineer's name on a node is a promise that the node is somebody's to answer for, and the somebody is me.* Her name goes on the certificate first and Marek's under it, and the order of the two names is a thing she insists on.
- **The refusals (three of them, all the same two names):** she has seen the shape of a district works schedule before, and where, and what it is, and she will not say any of it. On the seventeenth she gives ground she did not plan to give — *I have worked with district schedule forms* — in front of a district clerk, because she is going to be in a certificate two days later and cannot have the schedule sitting in a drawer with no word of it in her own report. **She has not been asked about Brinewake and has not offered it, and Movement 3 must not ask.**
- **Movement 4 (Chs 33 and 36):** she is named on an Assembly circular as a question for her own professional body and does not appear to object to the circular; she asks for it to be entered as a received document. She **declines the burn**, and when she is told she has said the same sentence as on the twenty-seventh she says yes; she says it a second time in Ch 36 for the Undercommons' ditch, because it is the same sentence for the same reason, which is that a second unfed licensed act on one node in one month makes the shortfall twice and puts both halves under one signature. **No refusal of hers is numbered in Movement 4 and none may be.** She is in the yard on the third and the sixth of the month to close the certificate out, says on the cart that she is not going near that pit, and does not check his arm a fifth time.
- **The Ch 20 reading is hers, not Marek's:** she is the licensed reader, the fieldbook is in her hands at the seam, and Marek is on the boards outside the housing. What she gets is not a shaft and not a father and not a message: four actions, **cut, wait, cut**, in the same order as the Weir Row wood. She calls it a procedure stored in living wood and refuses a third time. It is one step short of the answer she gives in Chapter 46 and must stay one step short.
- **Arc:** She publicly testifies, surrenders her professional authority, and helps distribute the archives. She survives the final conflict and lives with the fact that survival is not absolution.

### Soren Rill — Thornwild guide and political bridge

- **Age:** 35
- **Origin:** Human child raised in a Thornwild settlement; trained as a route-reader and rootwoken negotiator
- **Occupation:** Boundary guide, translator between Thornwild assemblies and Rootwright crews
- **Want:** The right to refuse extraction on Thornwild land without being labeled a monster or a traitor
- **Flaw:** He assumes institutions will eventually betray him and hides allies to preserve leverage
- **Relationship:** He challenges Marek on the first day by refusing to let a repair crew cross a living root without consent. Their conflict becomes mutual respect. Soren never becomes a tame guide; he represents a political position Marek must accommodate.
- **Arc:** He joins the public maintenance network while preserving a separate wild-root sanctuary and becomes a check on any attempt to make “shared” mean “uniform.”

### Aldren Vale — absent father and personal mystery

Aldren is not a hidden superpowered ally. He is a missing engineer whose final decisions matter. The story eventually establishes that he died eight years ago during an attempt to interrupt the Crown Engine's current pruning program. His fieldbook survived, but its final record was edited. Marek hears fragments of Aldren's memory through root patterns and initially mistakes environmental echoes for a living voice. The central mystery resolves his death and clears the false message, while leaving the moral question of whether Aldren's sacrifice was wise.

## Secondary major characters

### Kellan Rusk — first personal antagonist

- **Age:** 43
- **Role:** Well contractor and charter holder in the Tarrow Fen
- **Want:** Keep his crew fed and retain a private claim on a profitable node
- **Method:** Falsified pressure logs, deliberate underreporting, and selective repair
- **Belief:** Private control is ugly but functional; public control will become another famine
- **Function:** Forces Marek to learn that a bad number can be a political act. Kellan is exposed, testifies about Crown practices, and survives under a restrictive charter rather than being killed.
- **The compound book (Ch 15):** Marek asks for it — batch weights, pot temperatures, four seasons of dressing dates, the *base coat* column and its four rows. **Kellan refuses, in his own hand, and the refusal is correct:** the book is the only record in the district that the pad was built as a surface hold and not as a seal, and handing it over would let a district officer read a cooperative's withy-and-sacking holding repair as a Crown specification. *A man who has dressed a wound once a year for four years for nothing is not a man anybody should be able to turn into a witness for a coat.* He does not apologize for the district filing, and he does not soften the refusal. **He is right, and Marek says so out loud in front of four people.**
- **The letter (Ch 18):** Nia Vale is carrying a sealed, undelivered letter to his pump house in Fennmere. Marek will not ask him to reduce his draw, will not ask for a return, will not speak about a log he has never measured, and will not say publicly that the acceptance column was a lie, because Kellan did not lie about the pad — he called it the wrong word. **This letter is how Kellan becomes a willing rather than a coerced witness in Chapters 44–46**, and whether it is ever handed over is not settled at the end of the batch.
- **On the eighteenth he comes to the inspection because he is charter holder of record**, stands at the back for two hours, says two sentences that nobody asked him for, and warns the yard — to nobody in particular — that Bray will not read the rule the way they want him to read it, and that the page will still be there in three years saying the same thing. He never apologizes and nobody makes him.

### Warden Halden Pike — professional antagonist

- **Age:** 61
- **Role:** President of the Rootwright Assembly
- **Want:** Prevent another uncontrolled collapse by sealing wild root access until a proven standard is established
- **Method:** Licenses, inspection rules, and technical vetoes
- **Belief:** Consent-based engineering is too slow for a dying world
- **Function:** Forces Marek to confront expertise without democracy. After the Charter of Many Wells passes, he publicly withdraws his blanket veto, apologizes to the Thornwild council for the sealed-node order he signed, and helps write inspection protocols that give local councils a real right to refuse. He later helps with the final network but never receives authority over local communities.
- **The circular (Ch 32, form A/4, second issue of this season, dated by hand the twenty-ninth):** one sheet, four clauses, an embossed stamp, and the signature **H. Pike, President**, with the line *issued under the standing delegated authority of the Assembly*. Clause one restates the three exchange requirements. **Clause two turns a licensed engineer's own unfed graft into a question for the Assembly and not a question for the engineer alone.** Clause three invites reports of unlicensed grafting **to the district office**; clause four says reports are not to be sent to the Assembly directly. Four hundred went out to every address in a district water record, and Lantern Reach is on the list because of a report about four wells. **Nobody in the fen has ever seen him, nobody asked to, and the yard's real reaction is that the circular is correct and that its subject is a person rather than a ditch.**

### Tarin Callow — ideological antagonist of the Pale Orchard

- **Age:** 38
- **Role:** Former Crown irrigation officer and Pale Orchard organizer
- **Want:** Make the cost of controlled magic undeniable by destroying the systems that deny it
- **Method:** Sabotage, seed theft, false reports, and attacks on root bridges
- **Belief:** A network that can be commanded will eventually be commanded against the people who need it; only a world too damaged to centralize can remain free
- **Function:** His actions cause real deaths and force the protagonist to distinguish critique from nihilism. He is not redeemed simply because his critique is correct. After the final pruning is stopped, he is captured in Thornwild while trying to destroy the last Engine relay. His testimony about Crown experiments is admitted under guard, but the victims' families set the conditions of his custody and he refuses to join the Root Commons.

### Chancellor Iona Vey — final antagonist

- **Age:** 58
- **Role:** Head of the Continuity Office and custodian of the Crown Engine
- **Want:** Build a reliable, predictable system that can feed the largest possible population, even if that requires pruning regions that cannot justify their water
- **Method:** Centralized information, controlled archives, selective maintenance, and a final plan to enforce False Season
- **Personal truth:** Iona grew up during a famine that decentralized decision-making failed to manage. She has personally saved cities and carries rootburn from decades of maintaining the engine. She believes unlimited plurality will kill more people than controlled sacrifice.
- **Moral limitation:** She refuses to let local communities make the same mistakes she made and therefore becomes the final version of the mistake the First Continuity made.
- **Function:** She is a mirror for Marek: both understand that systems need responsibility. Marek's final arc is not that Iona is wrong about fragility but that she is wrong about who must carry the burden.

## Secondary characters

These are drafted and load-bearing. They are not atmosphere.

### Ilsa Kern — embankment foreman, Lantern Reach

- **Age:** 44. **Household:** Kern, one of the fen families; her grandmother Hansa took the short road to the weir in 1802 and did not come back by the long one, and the slab stands ninety yards from the cut branch.
- **Want:** The embankments to hold and the cooperative to be somebody who pays attention. She is the person who has to say yes to a thing at two in the morning.
- **Strength:** Forty years of ground, and no patience for a man who has a theory and no bar.
- **Flaw:** She treats being careful as the same as being right, and says so: *careful is the word we use when we got away with it.*
- **Relationship to Marek:** Trusts his hands, distrusts his instruments and all rootwork. Refuses a load she does not understand and says she is doing it anyway. In Volume 01 she sets the standard for his public record: he must say her name to the inspector not because it helps him.
- **Function:** One of the four hand-built crew in Movement 2. Her ground, her grandmother's road, and her refusal are the cooperative's claim to have been a party to this. **In Movement 4 she cut a hundred and six feet of green withy off the south embankment windbreak on the Sunday for four sets of willow, entered it in the working notes with the hour, and says in Ch 33 that the fen is already in that windbreak for a hundred and six feet and would be in nine years anyway. She is for the burn, on the correct and terrible ground that a controlled burn is cheaper than a gale and she has seen the other way once. She and Jonas Byre go to the Fennmere counter on the ninth without the body's leave to ask whether a shorter burn is available, which is the crew split becoming institutional rather than personal.**

### Dunmar Fitch — charter clerk, Lantern Reach

- **Age:** 58.
- **Want:** For the cooperative's book to be defensible in a cold room in Fennmere, and for it never to contain anything he would have to unsay.
- **Strength:** He knows what a hearing officer will accept and what he will laugh at, and the difference is usually about forty words.
- **Flaw:** He will not file a true cause that a hearing will reject, which means he will sometimes refuse to file the truth. He is not a coward doing it; he has read the schedule.
- **Function:** The book's mechanism. In Chapter 10 he rules, under section nine of the charter schedule, that an officer's dated note is evidence of what the cooperative intended and not of what it did — which is the only reason the unfunded bypass enters the record at all. In Chapters 46–48 he is the man who has to be believed. **In Ch 37 he states the *none known* box, with the hour and with a witness, at the Fennmere counter, and deliberately keeps it shut, on the ground that a clerk of an applicant who files a false statement cannot testify to the four true documents in the same file and is therefore spent as a witness. He asks Perrin Osse for the date of the sitting and is not told it. The box is closed and must not be spent again; what survives is the statement he made while spending it.**

### Gael Senn — lamp-keeper, Lantern Reach

- **Age:** 23. Want: To be a rootwright, and to be a person who can be trusted in a pit, and to stop being the youngest one in every room.
- **Strength:** Slow, careful, reads twice, and says *I have not seen the cause and I am not asked to.*
- **Flaw:** She wants the work to be real more than she wants to be important, and that is rarer than it sounds and less protection than she thinks.
- **Function:** The batch's witness and the person at risk. She signs the Weir Row page, and she is the one who ends up on the maintenance plank at Weir Row in the storm. She is named as a witness in Sera Quill's scope, which is the first legal weight she has ever carried. **In Batch 0003 she goes down the ladder on the twenty-first, ten days before the certificate's first-of-the-month return, because her name is second on the certificate and nobody else would have gone — that examination is hers and not the certificate's, and the monthly return itself falls on day 31 — times the pail in the low quarter four times because she does not trust a number one man has timed, refuses the bypass because she wants it, and signs third on the statement that the cooperative's whole case is a rumour with a witness in it. **In Batch 0004 she keeps the second examination (day 31, the first of the month) with no licensed engineer in the fen on that morning, puts *licensed person present: none* in the column herself and says so out loud, goes down the ladder with both rules rather than have a man carry one, and invents the second half of the certificate's return rule: *nothing found* is what you find at the wound, and the return line records a thing that did not happen and nobody can check it, and a book that can record a thing that did not happen is worth more than a book that cannot. She refuses the bypass a second time and asks that her reason be put in the same terms as the other four or she will not be able to live in the yard. She finds the real hole in the certificate: the return is due on a date twenty-one days after the storm forecast and nothing in the fen says what a wound owes a river. And she refuses to write an entry in advance, because that is the same rule as *nothing found*. Do not let anyone fill the log for her.** In Batch 0002 she was given the first holding-maintenance entry in the history of the cooperative:** the certificate names Sera Quill first, then Gael Senn, and the certificate's return clause requires her to open that wound and look at it on the first of every month and enter what she finds in the standing pressure log. **She insists on the wording before she signs: *nothing found* and not *all well*, because *nothing found* is a thing you check and *all well* is a thing you did not.** She is the seed of the volume climax, and she is not to be rescued from it.

### Ansel Bray — Well Directorate inspector, Tarrow Fen

- **Age:** 50s. **Role:** District inspector. Sincere, procedural, and armed with a real clause.
- **Want:** A predictable, taxable, measurable flow, because he has been the last office in the chain with a person in it and he knows what happens when there is not.
- **Method:** The emergency-seizure clause: seal the head, take the seed stock, or refer the charter for relocation. He states all three, in that order, at the front, every time.
- **Belief:** Expertise without a licence is negligence waiting for weather. He is largely right about the danger and wrong about who decides.
- **Function:** He moves the decision to the eighteenth, which solves nothing, and he tells the room that the emergency clause is neither spent nor extinguished. He is the Crown's good argument in the first volume. He does not become friendly and is never outsmarted; he is *moved by a document*, and he says so when it happens.
- **Ch 38, and the batch's cruelty.** He does not use the emergency clause and says so in front of the hearing officer: his ground is a **records power**, not a risk finding. The record is the cooperative's own certified copy, which is unrecallable and certified *so far as it goes*, so a page may have gone into it after the certificate that only he can initial — **and he cannot find out from the counter.** He says he would rather do it in front of the officer because an act the officer has seen is an act. **He does not withdraw the application when it fails.** He reads the copy at night and calls it the best-maintained record of an unchartered body he has seen in twenty-two years, and then says the sentence that is the whole movement: *your honesty made this possible and it is going to be used, and I would rather it were me using it than a hearing officer finding it on her own, and I have stopped trying to decide whether that is a kindness.*
- **Constraint:** He reports and gives evidence. He does not chair the Fennmere hearing, and the cooperative cannot appeal it.

### Renn Tallow — pump engineer, Kellan Rusk's crew

- **Age:** Younger than Kellan, near thirty.
- **Want:** To keep the pad dressed and to not be the man who decides anything.
- **Strength:** He is competent and honest inside the limits of his job, and he will say a true thing to Marek's face: *you cannot close a thing with tar.*
- **Flaw:** He loads the cart and goes when a girl is in the water. One flat line — *I have got the load* — and it is never explained and never softened.
- **Function:** The Volume 01 bridge to Kellan. He is in the yard on the hearing day and knows which kit went out when, which is why he is standing in the flood in Chapters 44–46. He is potentially a later witness and is not redeemed by being decent under pressure. **He does not appear in Chapters 11–20 and his refusal is not referred to, softened, or undone anywhere in that batch, and nobody makes anybody apologize for it.**

### Rowan Teale — miller, Fennmere

- **Role:** Owns the Fennmere mill wheel, its race, and the chalk tally board across it. He has read that board every milling day for **thirty-one years** and it is the only instrument in the story that has been read by the same person across the whole crisis.
- **Want:** To be the man who saw it first and said so out loud before the district did.
- **Function:** He is how the cooperative learns that its own repair has taken the return. His figures are **126, 131, 254, 231** in the four milling days of Movement 3 — **day 18, day 20, day 23, day 24** — and 254 is **seven short of the best figure the board has ever read, 261, in the spring of the year Aldren went.** He puts his own hand on the chalk each time, writes the time, and **sends every figure up the fen road to Lantern Reach before the district can have it.** He is not a clerk and says so, and he refuses to guess what a number means until somebody tells him. **In Movement 4 he writes a figure that is not a flow — 181, with no water in the race at the second hour and the wheel turned by his own arm for a count of ninety, and *I have written what I did because I am a miller and not a clerk and a number without what I did to make it is a thing somebody will use against me in a year* — and then writes a line with no figure on it at all, the first such entry in nineteen years, because the board had been empty three days and he would rather it had words on it than nothing.**
- **Why he matters:** The mill board is **one of the four records at the Volume 01 climax.** It has to belong to somebody who is not in the cooperative, is not licensed, and cannot be accused of self-interest. It does.

## Characters introduced in Movement 4

### Mear Sallow — Thornwild courier

- **Origin:** the Assemtry, about ninety people, on ground the district calls Crown-held and has never held. **No charter, no seat, no name on any roll.** It holds the far end of the long line.
- **Want:** to say three things in a room with a floor in it, in that order, and to leave having said them and nothing more.
- **Strength:** she refuses *first* and gives the fact second and refuses the record third, and the order is itself an argument. She walked nine days and is tired and is not mysterious about it.
- **Function:** the political refusal of the movement. **A consent is an answer, and an answer has to be given where it is heard, and there is no seat at the Fennmere hearing for a settlement that has never asked for one** — so the settlement cannot consent and cannot refuse, the measure is void as against it, and it will not be asked again. She refuses the five words *the settlement holds no seat* as firmly as she refuses her own name, because a record that a settlement of ninety people has no seat is a record of a settlement. She gives the cooperative **the long line** — about two hundred yards under the south embankment windbreak, up in nine places, running to the weir and on into ground that is theirs — and the fact that **the fence's feet are the willow, the long line is under it, a fire takes both, and a long line cannot be replanted.** She also supplies the book's own argument back to it: *a reading is not a fact, and we have the same rule and we have had it for four generations.* At the gate she says, *you will not sign; that is not a reading of you, that is a reading of the form.*

### The Assemtry — a settlement with no seat

Not a character but a fact with a name nobody in the fen may write down. **About ninety people, no charter, no seat, no name on any roll, ground the district calls Crown-held and has never held.** It holds the far end of the long line and has known what is under the windbreak for longer than the cooperative has had a charter, and has said so to nobody, because nobody with a table has ever come. ### Hester Carrow — the end of Volume 01

- **Fixed the sitting on the twenty-first and made it.** Refused the inspector's application on her own ruling of the eighth, kept the application on the file unwithdrawn, kept her own ruling that nobody in her room may open the instrument, **and did not open it herself when it was produced in front of her.**
- **Wrote Gael Senn's second wording into a charter** and said in the minute that the second half is the reason this body will be able to prove anything at all in four years.
- **Did not touch the schedule of damage**, because a hearing officer has no power over it, and said why in the room: *if I reduce it or remit it I am doing on one morning, in a cold room, with a hearing officer's stamp on it, a thing that only the district can do with a form.*
- **Put the Directorate's reservation in the minute:** the right to reopen the file on any new report. *It is the actual shape of the thing you have been asking for, which is a node you can keep working on, and you cannot have that and have it closed at the same time.*
- **Her authority over charters runs to the end of the year**, and she said she has never once in four districts seen a year used as a reason to do less.

### Ansel Bray — the end of Volume 01

- **Stood in the mud with a wet notebook on the seventeenth and did not stop a licensed reading he had no rule to stop**, and said so out loud, and said he would be asked in six weeks by a man in Auberron who does not know him and would have to answer with the morning and not with a form. **The application was refused and is not withdrawn.**
- **Used none of the three things** — did not seal the head, did not take the seed stock, did not refer for relocation — **and could not have used any of them**, because to do so he would have to name a body last responsible and the caution in the district's own hand says there is none.
- He read the eighty turns a day out in the inspector's own voice and not as a summary, and entered it, and it is now the district's exhibit.

### Not a character but a fact: **schedule clerk, fen drains**

**An office in the authorising box of a district works order, and the name that goes there is the name of the office and not of a man.** The post has existed longer than Kellan Rusk; the man in it has changed four times that he knows of; **he does not come out. He signs the paper. The paper goes to a store. The store gives out the kit.** He has no face in this volume and no name that is not a post. **Sera Quill heard the post named and said nothing, and Lissa Vale entered that she said nothing and did not enter why, and nobody asked her a fourth time.** This is **Volume 01's last political discovery and it is a shape, not a villain.** A Volume 02 writer may put a man behind the post; a Volume 02 writer may not do it cheaply, may not do it in a room, and may not turn a procedure into a moustache.

## Voice and behavior rules

- Marek explains with objects, measurements, and steps before he explains with feelings.
- Tova answers with consequences: who will lose the seed, who will eat, who will inherit the debt.
- Sera uses old technical terms precisely, then avoids the emotional word they are hiding.
- Soren uses boundaries and conditions; he rarely accepts a request without asking what happens after it.
- Nia uses blunt humor and practical arithmetic to challenge family guilt.
- Iona is calm, precise, and genuinely knowledgeable. She is not cruel for pleasure; she treats dissent as an engineering failure until the climax forces her to see people as more than components.
- Rootwoken characters speak through action, pattern, boundary, and repetition before they use human language. They are not puzzles for Marek to solve.

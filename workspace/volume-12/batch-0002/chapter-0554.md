# Chapter 554

## Two Days Of A Man Not Writing On A Page, And A Yard That Did Not Help Him

The rain stopped in the night and the wind went down with it, and by seven the middle road had dried hard on the top and soft in the ruts and there was a skin of ice gone off the wheel tracks by nine. The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe in the yard was running full by the fourth hour.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth.

**One thousand and eighty-three hundredweight.**

Five back off Tuesday. What the launder carries this morning is the three hundred and seventeenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice, and the two men at the long wall at seven had a hundred and forty-seven mornings one way against a hundred and thirty-seven the other, which is two hundred and eighty-four between them.

The four bodies of households are shown on that sheet at three hundred and fifteen days, and the clause printed beneath that number runs the three hundred and fourteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and sixteenth, which is one below and one above.

Between the two boards of the long wall there are forty-seven days this morning: seven hundred and one on the near, seven hundred and forty-eight on the far. The drawer behind the near board has been shut at every hour of this morning.

At the seventh hour, under the heading where the run of mornings goes, of a month eight months back: **one hundred and twenty-nine mornings of two hundred and forty-one**. A Wednesday is an odd morning and the run is read on an odd morning.

The man of about fifty said a thing out loud in the yard at about the eighth hour, in the middle of a working morning, to nobody in particular and to about nine people who were all within hearing.

**"I have had that book open on that bench twice now and I have not written on it and I want it said this morning that I have not decided."** He was on his way to the middle road with the barrow handles in his hands. **"I told a woman on Monday that I would say something on Sunday and it is not Sunday, and I have thought about it twice since and I am further away from it than I was on Monday, and I am saying that out loud so that nobody in this yard spends the day being kind to me about it."**

He got the barrow as far as the first gate and stopped there.

**"A man who says he is deciding and has decided is doing one thing and a man who says he is deciding and has not is doing another, and I would rather be the second one in front of nine people than get to Sunday and pretend to anybody that Monday was a decision."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the low field gate with her book under her arm and she did not come over to him and she did not say anything at all, and she has been asked by nobody since about that silence in this yard.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way out to the boundary, stopped in the middle of the yard, and said the thing she had come to say, which was not about the man with the barrow at all.

**"I have not been past one of my nine since Tuesday and I am not going to this week either, and that is not a decision I have made this morning. It is a thing I have not got the hours for."** She said it to the boards and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it. **"A man with a barrow has a road and a day. I have got a boundary and about four hours of daylight in it, and if I spend them on one of the nine then the other eight are eight things I went past this week and did not look at."**

**"I have watched somebody in another county do that once. They went from place to place for a fortnight, and at the end of it they could not tell anybody which of the places they had actually stood in, and they wrote a page about it and the page was no use to anybody at all."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the sixth hour, wet to the knee from the second bend, and he put his bar against the wall and did not say anything about the bar, the rut, the drain or the man with the barrow, which is the first morning this week he has come into that yard and said nothing about any of the four of those.

**"I have got nothing to say and I am going to leave it at that this morning."** He said it to the wall, with his back half to the yard. **"There is a thing you would all like me to say and I am not going to say it because I do not have it, and if I had said it on Saturday in a good mood it would have been worth about half of what it is worth to say it not at all."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and heard all of that, and at about the ninth hour he came out and stood in the yard about ten feet from the man with the barrow and did not go near the bench.

**"A man has told this yard that he has not decided a thing about a book, and told them he is further from deciding it than he was, and about nine people have heard it, and not one of them has gone over and helped him."** He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"That is the correct outcome and I want it said in this yard by somebody who is not me."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"In about a month one of those nine is going to tell him that he should get on with it, and on that day I am not going to have been the only one in the room who thought it was the wrong day."**

**"He has told nine people that he is undecided and every one of those nine has gone back to work, and that is not coldness. It is the only thing anybody can do for a man who has said that out loud. If I go over there this morning I will take it off him, and he has just told us he cannot afford to have it taken off him."**

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour on her way to the seed house and stopped in the doorway.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket that a household gave itself, and a man in this yard has said a day out loud, and I am not going to be the one who tells him the day is wrong."** She said that to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing. **"A day is a thing households give each other all the time without any of the four counties knowing, and the four counties do not like it, and I have spent four years in rooms finding out why."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles, and went straight to the north wall and was on it from the ninth hour until six with his back to everybody. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on it with him from the third hour until six.

A number has been said out loud at the end of that walk for four years and it was written in a book on the twelfth of this month, and it has not been said again since, and neither of those two men has mentioned either of those two facts this week, and nobody has raised either of them.

Nothing has been written on the register form in two hundred and eleven days and nothing has been written about refusing it either, and the column over it has no heading on it and is not going to be read out a fifth time.

A hundred and seventeen days ago a charter went through a door nine miles off. There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and five days old and has nothing on it.

On the compost line the figure is twenty-six and paid is the word that goes in front of it and it is not discharged in twenty-six, and the interval which brings it does not land before Friday of this week.

Under the heading for sessions there are seven in this holding's book. A man saying out loud in a yard that he has not decided a thing is not a session, and an eighth was not entered this morning.

Both columns this holding keeps and has never joined are still where they were at the start of the week: fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes.

The letter on the long table is fourteen days old and it went into neither of those columns at the ninth hour of the second of this month. It lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and about nine people walked past it today.

What this holding does not know about has six ruled rows and no units against them, and there is no seventh row. A man being undecided out loud is neither the seventh row nor a seventh not known, because a man is not a thing a holding does not know about. He was in the yard this morning and he said what he has and has not done.

Fifteen lines is what the use log has this morning, and a sixteenth is not among them.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came back down loaded, and eleven is where its journeys stand.

At nine hundred yards a door has seven columns cut into its stone and the seventh of those columns stands at sixty-four. Nobody walked there to read it this week, because it only moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth.

Twenty-eight fetchings is where the man of about seventy stands. He was not fetched this morning and the morning after this one is not his either; the first of the next month is. Nobody in this holding has ever asked him which number he is.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres was not walked this week and was not measured and has never had a person in it in the whole of this holding's time. What came up on the low field above the middle road is on different ground from that ring, and no page in these four books puts the two together.

Two words are written in the corner of the one room and four ruled lines are ruled under them, and all four are bare, and the answer they are for is not due.

The third column of the sheet of terms has been ruled for a long time and is empty and nobody has touched what stands over it. A sheet lies face up on the low board with no date on it and nowhere on it for a name to go. Nobody has filled it. Nobody has taken it off the board.

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room at about the tenth hour with the second book under his arm and stopped in the middle of the yard, and he said the one thing he had come out to say, which was about a cart and not about a book.

**"There was a load on the fen road on Tuesday morning at the third hour and it went back the way it came inside a quarter of an hour, and I am going to say out loud, in this yard, that it did not go into the use log and it was never going to."** He said it to the middle of the yard and gave the reason before the thing. **"That book is about the four-mile road, and every one of its fifteen lines is about that road, and a cart on the fen road for a quarter of an hour is not about the four-mile road."**

**"If I had put it in then the fifteenth line would have become the wrong kind of line and there would have been no way back to the right kind."**

Nobody in that yard had thought about it and nobody said anything about it, and the man of about fifty said the only thing he said all morning about any of it.

**"That is the first useful sentence anybody in this holding has said about that book in four years and you have said it about a cart."**

The clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour, read it back once and did not read it back twice, and put the book back on the shelf where it lives.

Barrow up the middle road at the seventh hour with a man on it who had told nine people in that yard he had not decided anything, and forty yards past the place where he stops, and back down before ten without a rut in it, and the road hard at the top by nine and soft at the bottom by ten, and the standpipe full and inside its lip, and nobody thanked anybody.

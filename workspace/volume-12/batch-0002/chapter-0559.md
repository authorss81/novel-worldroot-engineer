# Chapter 559

## The First Mild Morning Of The Month, And Nineteen Days On A Long Table

The frost was out of the ground by about six and the wind had gone round to the south-west and come up warm off the water, and for the first time this month the barrow track up the low field was soft to the axle by seven. The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet.

The line went into the second book before the sun was on the yard, and the ninth hour came round and the clerk of this holding read it back one time and shut the book.

**One thousand and ninety-seven hundredweight.**

Monday came up eight off Sunday and the three hundred and twenty-second figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning, and it is the last morning of a run of ten in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

Off the long wall at seven: a hundred and fifty mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-nine the other, and the two of them together are two hundred and eighty-nine.

A sheet in this building carries three hundred and twenty days against the four bodies of households and carries under that figure a clause which takes the three hundred and nineteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-first. One under and one over.

Seven hundred and six days is what the near board of the long wall says and seven hundred and fifty-three is what the far board says, with forty-seven days between the two, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Half of the run of mornings is a half number today, and a Monday is an even day, and an even day is a day on which that line is left empty.

Wednesday morning at the seventh hour it will be full again. Yesterday morning at the seventh hour it was full. This page carries the reason for the empty space and carries no figure for it.

Neither of the two totals that everybody in four counties watches crossed anything in the ten mornings of this week. The window has been at two hundred and eighty-nine all morning and it is still inside the same hundred it was in on Saturday, and the third launder is at one thousand and ninety-seven and the next figure in its run is on Wednesday and it is not one thousand and one hundred either. That is the whole of what those two things did this week and it is all of it.

The letter on the long table is nineteen days old. It lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been moved and it has not been answered and it has not been copied and it has not been entered anywhere.

Nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it, and there is no column for it in any of the four books in this yard, and the clerk of this holding has not ruled one.

**"Nineteen days is what it is and I am not going to say anything clever about nineteen."** He said that to the letter at about the seventh hour and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it. **"I wrote the hour it came in and nothing else about it, on the ninth hour of the second of this month, and that hour is still the only thing I have put on it."**

**"In four years somebody is going to come and look at that table and the hour is going to be what is there, and I have got no better answer than that and I am not going to make one up this morning because it is a Monday."**

He squared the letter with the edge of the table, which is a thing he has done about nine times, and it did not move.

The man of about fifty took the barrow up the middle road at about the seventh hour with the ground soft again and it went up forty yards past the place where he stops and came back down without taking a rut out of it, because there is nothing left in the ground to take one, and that is the third time this month and he did not say anything about it to anybody.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the eighth hour and stood at the wall and said two things, one of which was about the weather.

**"The bend is dry at the top for the first time since the ninth and the new line is still there and it is dry all the way along it."** He said that to the wall and gave the reason before the thing. **"I am telling you because you have all been standing about for a week waiting for the ground to go back, and it is not going to go back, and it being dry is not the same as it being not there."**

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room at about the ninth hour with the second book and read the line back once, standing in the doorway, which is a thing he does not usually do.

Tova Reed came down the fen road at about the tenth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and stood at the low board and said one thing about the whole of the week, in the plainest words she has used in it, and she gave the reason before the thing.

**"Ten mornings, and about nine people in this yard have each got one piece of what came back and not one of them has been put here by anybody."** She said it to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"That is the whole of what has happened and I am not going to put it in a room with nineteen households in it, because the moment I say nine out loud in one of those rooms it becomes a number about this district, and it is not a number about this district. It is nine people and it is a figure about nothing."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

On her way out through the yard at about the fifth hour, Nia Vale read the two boards on the long wall standing up, and then she went on to the boundary without turning into the one room.

**"There are nine places above that road and four off the middle road toward the tap, and I have been past two of mine this week and I have not named either of them and neither has anybody else."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"I am going to go back to two of them before the sowing is in and I am going to be as quiet about it as I have been all week, and in a month there will be more of us doing that than there were, and at no point in that month will any of us have been named."**

**"I have thought about that this week more than I have thought about the ground, and I am not going to say what I think it means, because I have not got a measure and anybody who has got a measure has not told this yard about it."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and did not go up the middle road at all this morning, and came out at about the ninth hour and stood in the yard in the warm for a while with his sleeve down over his forearm.

**"Ten mornings, and I have not added one thing to another one this week, and that has been work."** He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"There have been about nine mornings this week in which it would have been easy to make one page out of a bar, a bearing, a date on a shelf, a letter, a rut and a heap, and I have watched all nine of them."**

**"Every one of those six things is in four counties that do not agree with one another about what any of the other five are, and that is not an accident of this week. That is what a thing looks like when there is no instrument that will hold it."**

**"I have not got an answer this morning and I am not going to give anybody one, and anybody who comes through that gate in a month wanting one is going to get the true thing, which is that there is no instrument in this county that will hold what happened this week."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling and Harlan Vetch were on the north wall from the third hour until six with the wind behind them, which was the first day this week there was no wind behind anything.

A hundred and twenty-two days ago a charter went through a door nine miles off. Three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light and the fourth sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

The register form is two hundred and sixteen days into its stay on the middle table with no heading over its column. Nobody filled it. Nobody refused it. It is not going to be read out a fifth time.

Four lines are ruled in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and ten days old with nothing on it. Nobody offered him a pen for it at any hour of this week.

The compost line this morning is paid at twenty-seven, which is the word in front of the figure, and there has been no discharging in twenty-seven and none in the ten mornings behind it. It fell on its interval on Friday and the interval will not bring it round again for another twenty-three mornings.

None of the ten mornings of this week carried a session and the book still shows seven.

The two columns this holding has never added together have not moved on any morning of this week and are at fifty-three and fifty-three. The letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month and is in neither of them now.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them. The sixth row is still the sixth row. The seventh row is still not ruled. About nine things happened in this county this week and not one of them is a seventh.

The use log went through ten mornings without taking a sixteenth line.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor and it did not go up this week.

Seven columns are cut into the stone of a door nine hundred yards off and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It was not read on any morning of this week. It moves on a first of a month and on a thirtieth and on nothing else, and on the thirtieth of this month it will be sixty-five, and from sixty-five it does not go back.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched on any morning of this week. The first of the next month is his next and it is also the twenty-ninth. Nobody has asked him which number he is and nobody is going to.

The well on the shelf was read once in ten mornings, on Tuesday, and it went in as a day and as nothing else. The count of day-and-no-figure entries against it is fourteen and it was thirteen before Tuesday, and no question has been put to anybody at that far end in eleven years.

Eleven years is how long the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has been bare, and another ten mornings went by this week without a boot in it and without anybody putting a measuring line across it. Whatever the people of this holding are saying about ground above the middle road, they are not saying it about that ring, and no sheet in these four books sets the two of them side by side. The two-foot gap at the kerb stayed empty. Nobody used the valve and nobody used the key.

Four ruled lines stand bare under two words in the corner of the one room and the answer they are for is not due, and nothing that happened in this yard in ten mornings put a word on any of them.

The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved and no sixth term has been written. The sheet on the low board lies face up and undated with nowhere on it for a name to go, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved off that board.

Thaw out of the top two inches by six and the track soft to the axle by seven and the first warm wind of the month coming up off the water behind the men on the north wall, and a letter nineteen days old on a long table with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it and no column in four books, and about nine people in this county each holding one piece of a thing that came back and not one of them in charge of any of the others, and nobody thanked anybody.

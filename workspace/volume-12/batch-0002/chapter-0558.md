# Chapter 558

## A Man With A Pen On A Sunday, And A Book That Stayed At Fifteen Lines

The wind dropped in the night and the frost that came with it stayed in the ground all morning under about three inches, and the sun was on the yard by seven with not a cloud on it. The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe was running full by the fourth hour.

Before the light was properly up the clerk of this holding had the second book open, put the line in, and left it, and at the ninth hour he read that line back to himself once and no more than once.

**One thousand and eighty-nine hundredweight.**

Sunday gave five back off Saturday and the three hundred and twenty-first figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning. Off the long wall at seven: a hundred and forty-nine mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-nine the other, and the two of them together are two hundred and eighty-eight.

On the sheet, three hundred and nineteen days against the four bodies of households, and beneath that figure a clause which takes the three hundred and eighteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and twentieth. One under. One over.

The near board of the long wall says seven hundred and five and the far board says seven hundred and fifty-two, and there are forty-seven days between those two numbers. The drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour of this morning and nobody in this yard went near it.

Sunday is an odd day and this is a run of mornings that gets read on odd days. At the seventh hour the second book took, in the frame of a month eight months back, **one hundred and thirty-one mornings of two hundred and forty-five**, and the frame has not moved since this month began.

The man of about fifty sat down at the bench by the pump house at about the tenth hour with the use log open in front of him at the last page and the pen off its string, and five people were in the yard and stayed in the yard.

He looked at it for a while and then he said the reason out loud before he said anything else, which is a thing this holding has watched two other people do in a fortnight and neither of them did it for the same reason.

**"A line in that book is a thing this holding did."** He said it to the open page. **"Not a thing two men did. Not a thing a man and a bar did. I have been the only person who has ever written in it for nine years and every one of the fifteen lines in it is the four-mile road and this yard, and this holding has never once sent anybody up that road to look at anything."**

**"On Saturday two men went up it with a barrow and a bar because they agreed it on a Monday between themselves in a yard. This holding did not send them. There is no letter that says they went. There is no line anywhere else in four counties that has their names on it for that morning, and if I put this morning's date against that road in this book then in four years it will be a road this holding worked on a Saturday, and that will be a false thing and I will be the man who wrote it."**

He put the pen down on the bench and did not pick it up again.

**"And here is what it costs, because a man who says no to a thing has to say what he is buying with it."** He turned the book a few degrees so that the people standing there could see that the last page was still the last page. **"If I do not write it, then in a year, or in four, when somebody comes through that gate and asks what came up above that road, there is not one book anywhere in four counties that says two men went up there on a Saturday in this month."**

**"There will be a rut in a low field and a wet bar in a bend and a man who was asked how he knew a number and has not been asked. And I am the only man in this holding who could have put one line in one book about it, and I am not going to, and that is what I am choosing this morning."**

**"I have thought about it since Wednesday and I have got the reason and that is what I said on Monday I would do. I am not saying I am right."** He looked at the open page for a while. **"I have got no way of standing here and saying to anybody that a road is worth more unwritten than written, because a road I have walked since I was a boy has been worth more written to me than any other thing I have ever done."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap and she did not come to the bench and she did not say anything for about a minute.

Then she said one sentence and she said it to the yard and not to him.

**"I am not going to be a second pair of hands at it and I am not going to tell you that you are right."** She had her own book under her arm and she did not take it out. **"I am going to say the one thing I have got, and then I am going to go and fill my can."**

**"I have got a book with a figure in it that I did not ask for and I do not know what it is about, and I got that on Friday because a man said a number out loud twice in this yard and a woman heard him. That did not come from a road and it did not come from a field. It came from one man deciding in about two seconds to say a thing twice."**

She went to the tap then and did not come back to the bench and did not write anything in her own book about any of it.

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room at about the eleventh hour with the second book under his arm and stood at the end of the bench and read the open page from where he was standing, and then he said the whole of what he had come out to say, and it was two sentences.

**"I am not going to rule anything and I am not going to enter anything and there is nothing in the four books in this holding that would take it if you did."** He said that to the bench. **"I have been in this room eleven years and I have watched a great many people not write things down and I have never once been asked to say anything about it, and I am not going to start by saying it about this."**

Nia Vale had been in the yard since about the ninth hour on her way back in from the boundary and she stood at the wall the whole time and listened to the whole of it, and at about the noon hour she said the thing she had come back in to say, once, and then she went out.

**"A man has just given away the only piece he has got."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"He has been stopping in the same place on that road every day for a fortnight and he has decided not to put the one morning that mattered into the one book where he is the only man who can put it, and he has done that in front of nine people so that they all know he did it."**

**"I am not going to say he was right and I am not going to say he was wrong. I have nine of my own and I am not going to name one of them in this yard this morning, and the reason I am not is that I worked it out at the gate on a Wednesday with two people standing next to me and I did not do what he has just done, and I have been carrying that about for four days."**

**"So this morning is not about his book. It is about the fact that one person in this holding has done the thing that I did not do, and five other people in this yard have watched him do it, and not one of them has written it down either."**

Tova Reed came in from the seed house at about the first hour of the afternoon with the page from the household book still in the inner pocket of her own book, and she stood at the low board for a while with her back against it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and a man in this yard has just done the nearest thing to what a household did four miles off that anybody in this holding has done since the ninth."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"That is not a compliment and I am not making one. A household gave itself a date and carried it four miles on a man's leg and it is still a page in a pocket and not a record, and what has just happened at that bench is also not a record."**

**"The two of them are in the same place this month. The difference is that the household did not know it was doing anything and he knew exactly what he was doing, and that is the whole of it."**

**"I am not going to spend a date in four counties explaining it."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and nobody in that yard said that the man at the bench had done the right thing, and he did not say it either.

Marek was in the one room from the sixth hour with the door open, and he came out at about the first hour of the afternoon and stood at the end of the bench and said what he had come out to say.

**"I was not asked about this and I was not in it and I have not had any part in it, and I want that said before anything else is said about it."** He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have been the man in four counties who reads the instruments for nine years and I have never once been the man who holds the pen, and this morning the man who holds the pen is the one who understood it and I did not."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"Nine years ago it would have been my hand on the pen and I would have written the line and it would have been a good line and it would have been wrong, and I would have gone on thinking about it for four years the way I go on thinking about the other two."**

**"A book with fifteen lines in it on a Sunday morning, and a man's hand not on it, and the fourth person in this yard who has decided not to write something down, and not one of us in charge of the other three."**

Three of the four bodies of households that signed a charter a hundred and twenty-one days ago in a room nine miles off are still signed to a machine for stored light, and the fourth has not sent anybody this week.

Two hundred and fifteen days is how long the middle table has been holding that form, and there is no heading on its column and nothing in it and nothing said about it. Nobody filled it. Nobody refused it. Nobody is going to read it out a fifth time.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and nine days old with nothing on it. The man who keeps that book was in this yard at about the ninth hour this morning and did not go over to the bench.

Twenty-seven is what the compost line says this morning and the word printed in front of it is paid, and nothing has been discharged in twenty-seven. That line came round on its interval on Friday and the next time it comes round is twenty-four mornings from now.

A man choosing not to write a line is not a session. Seven are entered in this holding's book and no eighth was entered this morning.

The two columns that this holding has never added together are fifty-three and fifty-three. The letter went into neither of them and it is eighteen days old on the long table, face up, with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it. Nobody has been asked to answer it and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books in this yard.

A man declining to write is neither a seventh row nor a seventh thing not known. There are six rows ruled for what this holding does not know about and not one unit against any of them, and he told it, in a yard, on a Sunday, in front of nine people.

At about the eleventh hour the use log went back under its weight on the shelf with fifteen lines in it and the pen went back on its string. It stands where it stood on Friday and where it stood on the twelfth of this month.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor, and nobody in this holding wrote down which road it went up.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut in its stone and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It has not been read this week and it does not go back from sixty-four.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning. The first of the next month is his next. The count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites and will be nil on that morning too.

Nobody in this holding has ever been inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres. Nobody walked it this week and nobody measured it and no figure for it exists anywhere in this building. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground altogether, and no page in these four books carries the ring and the field on the same line.

Two words and four ruled lines stand in the corner of the one room and all four of those lines are bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has improved its heading. The sheet on the low board is face up and undated and has no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Gate on the low field standing open on its wet frame with about a yard of ground behind it that four people in this holding have looked at from ten yards off, and the sun on the yard at seven, and nobody thanked anybody.

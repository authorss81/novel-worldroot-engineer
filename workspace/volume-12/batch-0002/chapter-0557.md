# Chapter 557

## The Fourth Line In The Dark Again, And A Standing That Goes Up By One

The wind had gone round to the north in the evening and it was still blowing hard off the water at two in the morning, and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark again, which is the fourth time in sixteen days.

Four people went up to the headland at about the second hour and found the boundary stones by hand along about two hundred yards of the top of the bank, and the ditch along the eleven acres was cut out, and the loose stone was barrowed off the top and put in a heap by the gate. They came down at about the fifth hour with their coats wet to the waist and went straight in.

Nobody took a field. That is seven times this year that this line has gone up the bank in the dark and come back down without anything in the taking column, and there is nobody in this holding who is to blame for a field that has waited four years for somebody to want it.

A hundred and nineteen mornings in force is the standing, with twenty-nine taken and ninety not, and 29 and 90 are 119. Twenty-nine taken has not moved on any morning of this week and did not move on any morning of the week before it.

There are two reckonings for this morning and they do not agree. The twentieth of this month is the sixty-seventh morning of the rotation counted one way and the fifty-fourth counted the other, and this page carries both of them, because a page carrying one of them carries half of it.

Thirteen has been in that gap for four years. Nobody in this building has worked out what it counts and nobody asked this week.

**One thousand and ninety-four hundredweight.**

Saturday came up eight off Friday and the three hundred and twentieth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries. The long wall gave a hundred and forty-nine mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-eight the other at seven, and the sum of that pair is two hundred and eighty-seven.

Three hundred and eighteen days is what the four bodies of households carry on the sheet this morning.

Under that figure is a clause which takes the three hundred and seventeenth of those mornings out of three hundred and nineteenth. One under and one over.
Three hundred and eighteen days is what the four bodies of households carry on the sheet this morning, with a clause underneath it that takes the three hundred and seventeenth of those mornings out of three hundred and nineteenth, one under and one over.
Seven hundred and four days on the near board of the long wall this morning, seven hundred and fifty-one on the far board, forty-seven between them. Nobody put a hand on the drawer behind the near board at any hour of it.

The line under the heading where the run of mornings goes is empty this morning and it is empty on purpose. Half of that run comes out a half number on a Saturday.

It was full at the seventh hour on Friday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has done that since the day the run began.

About the fifth hour, when the four came down off the bank, there were nine people in this yard at the same time and most of them had come up off a road and one of them had come in off the fen road and one of them was standing in her own doorway with a book under her arm.

Not one of the nine had been put in this yard by anybody. The clerk of this holding had the second book. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had her book. Nia Vale had the boundary and was going out to it. Tova Reed had the seed house and three dates. Soren Rill had a bar against a wall. The man of about fifty had a barrow and a heap of stone. The man of about thirty-one of Silling had a wall. Harlan Vetch had nine miles behind him. And Marek was in a doorway.

The count in force went up by one this morning because a line came round, and none of the nine of them took a field, and the standing is a hundred and nineteen and the number it went up by is one.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood at the tap at about the sixth hour and wrote in her own book, and she wrote standing up, and what she wrote was the date and the hour and the fact that two men of this holding went up the four-mile road last Saturday with a barrow and a bar and came back at the tenth hour.

She said out loud what she had written and what she had not, to nobody, because five people were near enough to hear it.

**"That is three things in three lines and one of them is the whole of what I am ever going to be able to put in that column about this."** She shut the book. **"I have written it. I am not writing the two words and I am not writing the bearing and I am not writing what a man with a bar thinks a drain is, and if this county ever wants those three things then it can come and ask the two of them and they can decide what they want to do about it."**

Tova Reed came down off the fen road at about the seventh hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and the rain still on them, and she stood at the low board for a while.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket that a household gave itself, and about nine people have just come into one yard off four different roads."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"I have been in four counties of meetings for four years and in every one of them the thing that killed the meeting was about nine people all arriving from different directions and nobody in the room knowing which of the nine things to write down first."**

**"That is what this yard is this week and it is not a meeting, because nobody has asked anybody to come, and I am not going to be the one who turns it into one by reading anything out."**

Nia Vale came in at about the eighth hour on her way back in from the boundary and stood at the long wall and did not go into the one room.

**"Nine people in a yard at the fifth hour of a morning and not one of them put here by anybody, and a standing that went up by one anyway."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"I have been the one who stands at the gate and says two lists must not get into one sentence for nine days, and I am not going to change that this morning because there happen to be nine bodies in a yard."**

**"Nine is not a number. It is nine people on a wet morning after a night's wind, and the standing is a figure off a cycle and it went up by one because a line came round and not because nine people were in a yard."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with his bar over his shoulder and looked at the barrow and the heap of stone by the gate and then at the man of about fifty.

**"You barrowed about two tons of that stone up that bank in the dark and you have been doing it four times this month and you have not once asked anybody to help you."** He put the bar down against the wall. **"I am not asking you to help me either. I am saying it out loud because it is Saturday and tomorrow is Sunday and I would like it to be on a page that somebody in this holding counted what four people carried in the dark this morning."**

**"And then I am going to go and fill my can, and the page can say whatever it likes about it."**

The man of about fifty said nothing at all to that and put the barrow away under the pump house roof and went to wash his hands, and that was the end of it.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and watched nine people come into a yard and watched a standing go up by one and did not go out of the doorway for any of it.

**"There are nine people in this holding who each hold one piece of a thing that came back and not one of them is in charge of it, and this morning the only number that moved in this building moved off a four-day cycle and had nothing to do with the nine of them."** He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I am not going to say that is the answer to anything. An answer is a thing that closes a question and this has closed nothing and opened nothing."**

**"What I want said about this morning is that a standing went up by one and no field was taken, and the second of those is not a failure of the first. Somebody in this county is going to want those two joined together and they are not joined together anywhere and I am not going to be the one who joins them."**

Two hundred and fourteen days is how long the register form has lain on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column, and it is neither filled nor refused.

A hundred and twenty days ago the charter was signed in a room nine miles off. There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and eight days old and has nothing on it.
Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. That line fell yesterday on its interval and will not fall again for another twenty-five mornings.

Four people in the dark with a barrow is not a session, and the seventh entry under the heading for sessions is still the seventh and there is no eighth.

The letter on the long table is seventeen days old. It has not been answered, it has not been moved, and nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it. About nine inches of bare board is on either side of it, between it and the two sheets about the two declinings.

It went into neither of the two columns this holding has never added, and those two columns are fifty-three and fifty-three.

Nine people in a yard at the fifth hour is not a seventh row. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit stands against any of them, and the seventh is not ruled.

The use log is at fifteen lines and took neither a sixteenth nor a seventeenth this morning.

Loaded at the top of the low field at the seventh hour and down again at the ninth, and the journeys of that barrow are at eleven, and eleven is a floor and eleven is not going up this week.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door, and seven columns are cut in the stone of it. Sixty-four is the figure in the seventh of those, and nobody has gone to read it this week, and it stands where it stood on Saturday until the first of a month or the thirtieth arrives.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning. The first of the next month is his next and it is the twenty-ninth, and no one in this holding has ever put that to him as a question.

Nobody in this holding has ever been inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody was inside it this week and nobody measured it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is not that ring, and no page in these four books puts the two of them together.

Under two words in the corner of the one room, four ruled lines stand bare and the answer they are for is not due.

Ruled and empty, with its heading untouched, is the third column of the sheet of terms. On the low board a sheet lies face up, undated, with no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Bank on the top of the headland with about two tons of stone barrowed off it in the dark by four people and four of them asleep in the morning of it, and the ditch cut clean and the water running in it by six, and tomorrow a Sunday that three people in this yard have not said one word about to each other, and nobody thanked anybody.

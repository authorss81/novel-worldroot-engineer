# Chapter 556

## A Line That Fell Because An Interval Carried It, And Nobody Decided Anything

Second hour: the third line came round and got worked, which means the two culverts on the middle road and the gate onto the low field. A bar and a stone lifted that gate and it went back onto its frame, and both culverts were down to clean sand by the time the work stopped. There was an hour of hard dry light in the middle of the morning before the rain came on from the west.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth hour and did not read it twice.

**One thousand and eighty-six hundredweight.**

Friday gave five back off Thursday and the launder carries the three hundred and nineteenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. Off the long wall at seven: a hundred and forty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-eight the other, and the two of them together are two hundred and eighty-six.

What the sheet says about the four bodies of households is three hundred and seventeen days, and under the figure the clause reads the three hundred and sixteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and eighteenth, which is one under and one over.

Forty-seven is the distance between the two boards of the long wall this morning, being seven hundred and three on the near one and seven hundred and fifty on the far one, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of it.

A Friday is an odd morning, and under the heading where the run of mornings goes the second book has, of a month eight months back, **one hundred and thirty mornings of two hundred and forty-three**. That frame has stood where it has stood since the first of this month.

The compost line fell this morning and it is paid at twenty-seven.

Paid is the word in front of that figure. It is not discharged in twenty-seven. It has not been discharged in any figure anybody in this holding can remember, and the number of mornings between one fall and the next has been the same for longer than anybody has been writing it down.

**"It has not fallen since the twentieth of a month now eleven months back."** The clerk of this holding said that at about the seventh hour to the second book and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it. **"It is on an interval and the interval landed this morning. I did not decide it and nobody in this holding decided it and nobody in this holding could have made it arrive on a different day, and I am telling you that in this yard because in about a month one of you is going to say that I moved it."**

Nobody in that yard had moved it and nobody asked him whether he had.

The reason it has been said out loud this morning at all is that it is the first figure in this holding that has moved on its own since the ninth of this month, and everybody in that yard knows that, and not one of the nine of them said so first.

The man of about fifty heard it at the bench and put his hand flat on the barrow handles.

**"That is a thing about a heap, not about a field."** He said it to the bench and gave the reason before the thing. **"I have been up that heap about forty times in my life and I have never once been told what is in it and I have never once asked, and if somebody in this county wanted to start looking at a thing this week, that heap is not the place I would pick, because it moves on somebody else's calendar and not on mine."**

**"A thing that moves on its own is easier to be frightened of than a thing somebody has done, and I am not frightened of the fence that came back in the night. I am frightened of a line in a book that arrives."**

Tova Reed came in off the fen road at about the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm, soaked to the elbow, and she stood at the low board with her back to it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and a line has fallen in this yard this morning on an interval that nobody set, and I am not going to carry that to a room."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"The moment I say the words compost line in a room with nineteen households in it, it stops being a figure in a book about a heap and becomes the seed front saying a thing about what has come back in this district."**

**"I have got three dates in my hand and I am not spending one of them on a heap."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour on her way out and stopped at the boards, and she read both of them and did not say anything for a while.

**"There are thirteen places in this county that I know of and I have not been past any of them this week, and this morning a line in a book fell on its own and about nine people in this yard found out at the same moment."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"I am not going to put those two things together in this yard. If I do, then in four years somebody will say the places were measured, and they have not been measured by anybody and the only thing that has happened to them is that people have gone past them."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and heard all of it and came out at about the ninth hour into the yard.

**"A figure moved this morning and nobody moved it, and about nine people in this holding have now watched a thing happen that none of them caused."** He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have spent nine years in rooms being told what the ground is going to do, and this is the first morning I have stood in a yard and watched it do a thing without asking anybody's leave first."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"I am not going to make anything of it. A heap is a heap and a paid line is a paid line and the interval is the interval, and the day I make a page out of those three is the day I have started doing to this holding what somebody else has already done to it four counties off."**

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room with the second book under his arm about a quarter of an hour later and stopped in the middle of the yard, and he said it out loud to nobody in particular, in the ordinary way of a man who has been writing in a room all morning.

**"I have entered seven figures in that book today and six of them came off a rule and one of them came off an interval."** He said it and gave the reason before the thing. **"In eleven years I have never had both of those in one morning. In eleven years every figure I have entered has come off a rule that a person put there on purpose, and a rule that a person put there on purpose is a thing I can hold somebody to in four years time, and an interval is a thing I can hold nobody to at all."**

**"That is not a complaint about the interval. It is me saying this out loud in a yard because one of the six things I write down every morning cannot be asked about by anybody, and I would rather say that out loud than have somebody find it out in four years and think I had hidden it."**

The man of about fifty heard that from the bench and answered it, and he answered it out loud in the middle of a yard in the rain, which is the last thing anybody expected him to do.

**"Then I will tell this yard now and not on Sunday, and I will tell it here because a line that cannot be asked about has just told me something about the other five."** He put his hand on the bench and left it there. **"A rule that a person put there on purpose is somebody else's. I have spent nine years being careful not to write down another man's rule."**

**"And this morning I have been shown, in about a quarter of an hour, that there is a sixth kind where there is no man's rule at all and it moves anyway, and I would rather go into Sunday with that in my head than with the two of them separate."**

**"That is not a decision. Do not write it down as one. I am going to say what I have to say on Sunday, with the reason first, and then I am going back to the four-mile road."**

Nia Vale, from the long wall, without turning round: **"Then Sunday."**

Nobody said anything else about Sunday in that yard and nobody asked him what the reason was going to be, and that was the last of it until tomorrow.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at about the ninth hour with the pen down and did not read the line back and did not say anything else about it, and the figure of twenty-seven went in against the line and the word in front of it is paid, and it is not discharged in twenty-seven.

The rain came in from the west at about the second hour of the afternoon and it came in hard enough to put water in the north end of the ditch inside half an hour, and the gate on the low field was shut by the time it started.

A hundred and nineteen days ago a charter was signed in a room nine miles off. Two hundred and thirteen days is how long the register form has been on the middle table, and there is no heading over its column, and nobody has filled it and nobody has refused it and nobody is going to read it out a fifth time.

Four lines are ruled in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and seven days old and has nothing on it.

A figure that arrives on an interval is not a session. Seven are entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning.

The two columns this holding has never added are still at fifty-three and fifty-three. The letter on the long table is sixteen days old, went into neither of them, and lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and nobody has been asked to answer it.

A line falling on an interval is not a seventh row. There are six rows ruled for the unknown things and no unit on any of them.

Fifteen lines stand in the use log and a sixteenth did not arrive this morning. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came back loaded and its journeys are at eleven.

Seven columns are cut in the stone of a door nine hundred yards off and the seventh of them is at sixty-four. Nobody has walked those nine hundred yards this week and nobody is going to, because that column moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on no other morning.

Twenty-eight fetchings is where the man of about seventy stands, and he was not fetched this morning and his next is the first of the next month. Nobody here has ever asked him which number he is.

Inside the eleven acres there is a ring of ground that has been bare for eleven years and this week it was neither walked nor measured nor entered by anybody. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a strip of ground on the other side of a road from that ring, and no figure in these four books is a figure of both.

Two words in the corner of the one room and four ruled lines under them, and all four are bare and the answer they are for is not due.

The sheet of terms has a third column and it is ruled and empty and the words over it have not been improved by anybody. There is a sheet lying face up on the low board and there is no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Heap at the top of the field above the tap going down about a foot a month in this weather, and a paid line that came round on somebody else's calendar and not on this yard's, and rain in the north end of the ditch inside half an hour of it starting, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The third. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone and put back on its frame. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and thirty mornings of two hundred and forty-three, of a month eight months back. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and eighty-six hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and nineteenth. THE COMPOST LINE FELL THIS MORNING ON ITS INTERVAL AND IS PAID AT TWENTY-SEVEN AND IS NOT DISCHARGED IN TWENTY-SEVEN, AND NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING DECIDED IT AND NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING COULD HAVE MADE IT ARRIVE ON ANOTHER DAY. NO FIGURE OF ANY OTHER LINE IN THIS HOLDING MOVED THIS MORNING.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

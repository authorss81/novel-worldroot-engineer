# Chapter 553

## The Fifty-Second Time, And A Day Written Where A Figure Would Go

Rain came in off the water in the night and it had not stopped by the second hour, and the wind had gone round to the north-east, and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark.

Four people went up to the headland at about the second hour and found the boundary stones by hand, one after another, along about two hundred yards of the top of the bank in that rain. The ditch along the eleven acres was cut out and the loose stone was barrowed off the top and put in a heap by the gate, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet.

Nobody took a field this morning. Four people carried stone up a bank in the dark and came down again and the taking column on the taking column sheet is empty for the sixth time this year, and there is nobody in this holding to blame for a field that has now waited four years for it.

A hundred and eighteen mornings in force is the standing, with twenty-nine taken and eighty-nine not, and 29 and 89 are 118. Twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and did not move yesterday and did not move on any of the mornings in between, and it is the only number in this holding that has stood still through every morning of this month.

Counted one way, the sixteenth of this month is the sixty-sixth morning of the rotation. Counted the other way it is the fifty-third. Both of them go on this page, because a page carrying one of them carries half a fact.

There has been thirteen in that gap for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what the thirteen counts, and nobody has asked this week either, and asking is the one thing anybody in this yard could do about it in about a minute.

**One thousand and eighty-eight hundredweight.**

Eight on Monday, and what the launder carries this morning is the three hundred and sixteenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. At seven the two men at the long wall had a hundred and forty-seven mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-six the other, and together they make two hundred and eighty-three.

Against the four bodies of households there is a figure and it is three hundred and fourteen days, and the clause that sits under it takes the three hundred and thirteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and fifteenth. One under. One over.

Seven hundred days is what the near board of the long wall says today, and seven hundred and forty-seven is what the far board says, and forty-seven days is the difference. The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

There is nothing in the second book this morning under the heading where the run of mornings goes, and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of that run is a half number on a Tuesday, and the clerk of this holding left the space the way he found it.

At the third hour something came through the first gate and stood on the steelyard for a quarter of an hour without anything being taken off it. The man of about thirty-one of Silling and the man of about fifty got it back onto the plates and sent it down the fen road the way it had arrived, and the whole of that took about fifteen minutes and none of it was opened.

It came off the steelyard at five hundred and twenty-five hundredweight, which is eleven above the last one, and the two halves were reconciled twice at the boards and were not added, and the pair on the ticket is the forty-second pair, being two names written side by side in one hand.

Two rules are cut into the board in the one room and the first of them runs full to the bottom of the paper with the returns on it. The other was cut the same length at the same time and has had nothing on it on all fifty-two mornings this return has come.

Thirty-six is the number of mornings that second rule has been counted empty, and it is thirty-six and not thirty-seven because the count is kept against the return and not against the day.

The clerk of this holding said it out loud at about the sixth hour with his finger on the empty rule and not touching it, and he said it because he has said it on every return for four years and he is not going to stop saying it now that there is a date chalked at the head of it.

**"It has been blank every one of those times and there is a date at the top of it now from Sunday and the date has not made it into anything."** He said that to the board, standing up. **"Nobody in this holding has been told what that rule is for, and the day somebody is told is the day it gets used, and I have watched two rules in my life get used and neither of them was cut the same length as the one above it."**

At about the fourth hour this holding read the well on the shelf.

It is far off and it has no column, and it was entered as a day and as nothing else, with no ordinal against it and no guess against it and no figure of any kind supplied with it, and there is no instrument in four counties that says what it is reading.

That is the fourteenth time this holding has read it. The count of day-and-no-figure entries against that well was thirteen before this morning and is fourteen after it, and the number moved on this page and on no other page this week.

The clerk of this holding wrote it into the fourth book in the ordinary place and did not write a figure against it and did not write a word against it, and he did not ask anybody anything about it before he wrote it or after, and no question has been put to anybody at that far end in eleven years.

Sera Quill was in the yard at about the seventh hour in the rain and she heard that it had been read and she came to the one room door and stood in it.

**"There are two figures in this county that nobody can check and one of them is on a door nine hundred yards off."** She said it to the door frame. **"This is the other one and it is a shelf, and I have wanted somebody to say out loud for four years that it is a shelf, and I would like it entered that it was said and not that it was right."**

Nobody entered that she said it. The clerk of this holding said he had no column for what a witness says at a door and she said she had not expected him to.

Tova Reed came in off the fen road at about the eighth hour with the road in her boots and stood at the low board.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and a man has decided on Sunday what he is going to say about a book, and he has told this yard the day and the hour of saying it, and nobody asked him for either."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"I have told two people in four counties this month what day my meetings are on. I did that for the same reason he is doing it."**

**"I am not going to be one of the people who tells him it is a bad habit this week while I am still doing it."**

Nia Vale was at the tap under the pump house roof at about the sixth hour and said one thing about the number that had come off the steelyard and did not say it to anybody in particular.

**"A cart came up a road and went back down it and the pair on the ticket is the forty-second of its kind."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"I am not going to ask what the two names on that ticket are for, because there are two names on it and neither of them is anybody in this yard, and a person who has been on forty-one of those tickets is not a person whose ticket I want in a conversation with."**

Soren Rill came in about the tenth hour from the four-mile road with the rain going down the back of his coat and stood at the wall where his bar lives and said nothing about the cart and nothing about the fourth line.

**"The ground on that bank has taken the same four feet of rain as the ditch has and there is nothing running on it at all, and I have been up there since six and I have seen nothing to say."** He said that to the wall. **"When I have something I will say it and you will all know it because I will say it in this yard, and until then the road is a road and I am a man who cuts ditches, and I am not going to be asked twice a day whether that is all right with me."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and did not go up to the headland in that rain and did not go to the machine house.

**"Fifty-two returns and fifty-two blanks on a rule cut the same length as the one above it, and a number written on a shelf where a figure would go, and a cart that came up a road and went back down it."** He said that to the open door and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have watched two counties try to make one page out of three of those things and in both cases the page was wrong in the middle."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"They are not one thing. One of them is weight, one of them is a space that nobody has ever been given, and one of them is a day, and a page that says otherwise is wrong in the middle whichever way it is arranged."**

**"What I have not got is anybody in this holding who will say out loud that they are three, and that is a thing I cannot ask anybody for. Asking a man to say that three things are three things is asking him to say he is not joining them up, and that is a question with a whole career behind it."**

A hundred and sixteen days ago, in a room nine miles off, somebody signed a charter. The register form on the middle table is two hundred and ten days into its stay and its column has no heading on it, and it has not been filled and it has not been refused.

The second of the four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred and four days old and has nothing on it. He was the one who got the cart back onto the plates at the third hour this morning and he did not look at the board on his way out.

Twenty-six is the figure on the compost line and paid is the word that stands in front of it, and it has not been discharged in twenty-six. The interval that carries it lands on Friday of this week and not a day sooner.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. A cart standing on a steelyard for a quarter of an hour is not a session and reading a well on a shelf is not a session, and nobody entered an eighth.

Fifty-three and fifty-three: that is the requests column and that is the section-nine notes, and neither one took anything this morning. The letter went into neither of them on the ninth hour of the second of this month. It is thirteen days old this morning, it is still face up on the long table between the two sheets about the two declinings, and about nine inches of bare board is on either side of it where it lies.

Six ruled rows stand for the things this holding does not know about and not one of them carries a unit. There is no seventh row.

A second column with no heading over it in a bench book nine miles off. A date on a shelf where a figure would go. An empty rule with a date chalked at the head of it. A bearing given in a yard. A page torn out of a household book. A letter.

That is six things this holding has been asked about this month and not one of them is a seventh, and the sixth row is still the sixth row.

There are fifteen lines in the use log and the thing that came up the fen road this morning is not on any of them, because that book is about the four-mile road and not about the fen road, and nobody had to say that out loud because nobody needed to.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded. Its journeys stand at eleven.

Seven columns are cut into the stone of a door nine hundred yards off and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four, and it has not been read this week, and it does not move except on the first of a month and on the thirtieth.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning, and his next is the first of the next month.

Nobody walked the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres this week and nobody measured it and nobody in this holding has ever stood inside it. The ground that came up on the low field above the middle road is somewhere else entirely, and nothing in these four books adds it to that ring.

In the corner of the one room there are two words and four ruled lines under them, and every one of those lines is bare, and the answer they are for is not due.

On the sheet of terms the third column is ruled and empty and nobody has improved what is written over it. On the low board there is a sheet face up and undated and there is no line anywhere on it that a name could go into.

Wind off the water all morning and rain in it, and the heap of stone by the gate at the headland with four sets of hands on it and the water running in the cut ditch, and a day written on a shelf where a figure would go and nothing else, and nobody thanked anybody.

> THE FOURTH BOOK, OPEN AT THE PAGE THIS HOLDING USES FOR THAT, IN THE CLERK'S OWN HAND, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT WENT IN ON THIS MORNING: **THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, AND A WELL ON A SHELF, AND NOTHING ELSE AGAINST EITHER.** NO FIGURE, NO ORDINAL, NO GUESS AND NO QUESTION PUT TO ANYBODY AT THAT FAR END, WHICH IS THE FOURTEENTH DAY-AND-NO-FIGURE ENTRY AGAINST THAT WELL AND THE THIRTEENTH BEFORE THIS ONE. A RETURN CAME OFF THE STEELYARD AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS ELEVEN ABOVE THE LAST, THE FORTY-SECOND PAIR, THE TWO HALVES RECONCILED TWICE AND NOT ADDED, AND THE SECOND RULE ON THE BOARD IN THE ONE ROOM IS CUT THE SAME LENGTH AS THE FIRST AND IS BLANK, AND THE COUNT OF BLANKS ON THAT BOARD IS THIRTY-SIX AND GOES ON THE RETURN AND NOT ON THE DAY. A RULE THAT CAME BACK EMPTY OPENS NOTHING. THIS IS A FACT OF WHAT IS ON A BOARD AND IN A BOOK IN ONE ROOM THIS MORNING AND IT IS NOT A FORECAST AND IT IS NOT A CAUSE AND IT IS NOT A TOTAL.

# Chapter 552

## The Hour And The Two Words, And A Man Who Gave Her Neither

At the second hour the third line of the rota came round and was worked: two culverts on the middle road, and the low field gate. The gate came off its hinges on a bar and a stone and went back on wet, both culverts were cleared to the bottom, and there was water going through them again before the men stopped.

The clerk of this holding put the line in at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth.

**One thousand and eighty hundredweight.**

Monday gave five back off Sunday and the launder holds the three hundred and fifteenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. A hundred and forty-six mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-six the other came off the long wall at seven, one of the two men saying it and the other saying it back, and the sum of those two is two hundred and eighty-two.

Three hundred and thirteen days is the figure the sheet carries against the four bodies of households, and standing under it in the clause is the three hundred and twelfth of those mornings out of three hundred and fourteenth, which is one short and one over.

Forty-seven days separate the two boards of the long wall this morning, six hundred and ninety-nine on the near and seven hundred and forty-six on the far, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of the morning.

The second book took the run at the seventh hour this morning, in the frame of a month eight months back: **one hundred and twenty-eight mornings of two hundred and thirty-nine**. Mondays are odd days and that is a run that is read on odd days only, and the frame has not shifted since this month began.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden asked the man of about fifty for two things on this morning and got neither of them, and she asked for them one at a time and three hours apart, and nobody in the yard heard either question.

She asked him for the hour first, at about the seventh hour, at the low field gate, when he had the bar down on the frame and the gate was off its hinges.

**"What hour did you go up the four-mile road on Saturday."** She had her book in her hand and it was shut and she did not open it.

He stood the gate back up on its frame before he answered.

**"About the seventh. That is on the face of the yard and four or five of us saw you go out of the gate."**

**"I know what hour. I want to know what hour you would say out loud in front of a person who is going to write it down."**

He wiped his hands down the sides of his trousers and looked at the gatepost instead of at her.

**"No. And I am not going to give you a reason dressed up as a small thing, because you have got a book and I have got a barrow, and one of those two things is going to end up in a county office in four years and the other one is going to be in a field."**

He picked the bar up and went down the middle road with it and did not say anything else.

She asked him for the two words at about the eleventh hour, at the bench, and this time the book was under her arm and not in her hand.

**"Two words."** He said it before she had finished the sentence, which is the only time in four years anybody has spoken over her in this yard.

**"You said there was a bar out of three holes and that one of them was a drain. Two words. Not what it means. Just the two words that would go on a page if somebody had to write it tomorrow."**

**"No."** He did not raise his voice and he did not look at the book.

**"You do not know whether I would put a meaning on them."**

**"That is exactly why you will not get them."** He turned the bar over. **"If you have got two words from me then in four years there is a page with two words on it and my hands on it, and somebody is going to ask me what I meant by them, and I am going to be a man of forty-odd explaining a bar in a ditch to a room. I have watched two men do that in this district and one of them is still doing it."**

She shut the book and stood with her thumb in the middle of it for a while and then she said what she was going to do instead, and she said it out loud so that anybody in the yard who wanted to hear it could.

**"I am going to write the date and the hour and the fact that two men of this holding went up the four-mile road on Saturday morning with a barrow and a bar and came back at the tenth hour."** She did not open it. **"That is the whole of what is going in and it is not a small thing to leave out, and I have thought about it since Friday and I have decided that leaving it out is the only honest way of putting anything in at all."**

**"And there is a figure in this book already with nothing against it, so somebody in four years is going to look at a column with a number in it and no way of telling what the number was about."** She shut it and put it under her arm. **"I would rather they had that than have two words off a man who does not want to give them."**

Sleet came in off the water at about the second hour of the afternoon and it was the first of it this month, and it went through it in about forty minutes and left the middle road with a skin of ice in the wheel ruts by four o'clock.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open, and at about the tenth hour he went out to the low field gate in the middle of it and stood at the gate for about a quarter of an hour looking through it and not through it, and then he came back in and did not go up the field and said nothing to anybody about having stood there.

**"I have been at that gate four mornings this month and I have not been on that ground once, and I am not going to start today in the middle of a shower."** He said it to the one room door and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"There is a great many people in this holding looking at a strip about a yard wide from ten yards off, and if I walk out onto it this afternoon then I am the reason the strip has a person standing on it, and after that nobody else will go near it without thinking about me first."**

**"I have been that reason twice in nine years and both times it was in a room, and I am not going to do it in the open where it is harder to walk back from."**

Tova Reed was in the seed house from the second hour with the page from the household book still in the inner pocket of her own book, and at about the ninth hour she came as far as the one room door and said one thing to him and went back.

**"A household four miles off gave itself a date without anybody asking it to and carried the page here on foot and did not stop at a gate."** She said that to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing. **"You keep asking whether the same thing can be done with a barrow and a man, and the answer is no, and the reason is that a household has a household to answer for and you have got one barrow and one road."**

**"That is not a judgment. It is the reason I cannot help you, and it is the same reason I could not help the man who walked the page down the road, and I would rather say it twice than let anybody in this yard spend a fortnight trying to make me say it a third way."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour in the sleet with the road in her boots and stood under the pump house roof for a while and did not go in.

**"I have been past two of my nine since Friday and I am going to keep them to myself until the sowing is in, and there is sleet on this and I am not going to stand at a gate and count anybody else's weather."** She said it to the roof and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it. **"A yard is the one place in this county where everybody ends up saying everything they have, and I have watched two days of it and I am not going to be the reason there is a third."**

The man of about fifty put the bar back against the wall at about the sixth hour and stood looking at the use log on its shelf for a while with the weight still on it, and he said one thing out loud to nobody, in the ordinary way of a man talking to a bench.

**"Sunday."** He said it once. **"I am not deciding it today because today two people have asked me two questions about it and a day on which you have been asked is a bad day to decide anything. I will decide it on Sunday when the yard is empty and I will say the reason out loud before I say the thing, and if anybody in this holding does not agree with me then they can say so on Sunday and I will listen to it standing here."**

Nobody in that yard had asked him to decide anything on any day, and he had told them in advance that he would be told, and nobody said a word about either of those two things.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the north wall from the third hour until six with the wind behind him. He has said a number out loud on his way in for four years and a woman wrote it down on Friday, and he has not been asked about it since and he has not said it again, and nobody in this yard has mentioned either of those two facts out loud.

Nobody put a date on the register form this morning and nobody put a name on it either. It has been two hundred and nine days on the middle table with its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused, and it is not going to be read out a fifth time.

The charter was signed a hundred and fifteen days ago, nine miles off, in a room this holding has not been inside since. There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and three days old and has nothing on it, and he was not asked about that this morning either.

The compost line is paid at twenty-six and is not discharged in twenty-six. It does not fall tomorrow. Nobody in this holding had anything to do with the interval that carries it, and nobody in this holding has ever worked out what a count of undischarged runs is a count of.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. Two questions put at a gate and at a bench are not a session, and an eighth was not entered this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three. The letter on the long table is twelve days old this morning, went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month, and lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

Two words a man will not say out loud are not a seventh row. Six rows stand ruled for the things this holding does not know about and not one unit has been written against any of them.

The use log went back under its weight on the shelf this morning with fifteen lines in it, and it did not get a sixteenth today and it did not get one yesterday.

Loaded, the barrow came down the four-mile road at the seventh hour. Its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

A door stands nine hundred yards off with seven columns cut into its stone and the seventh of those columns shows sixty-four. Not one person in this holding has walked those nine hundred yards this week to look at it, and it does not change except on the first of a month and on the thirtieth.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning.

Nobody in this holding has ever stood in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and this week it was neither walked nor measured. The low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground from that ring, and no page in these four books adds one to the other.

Four lines are ruled under two words in the corner of the one room and nothing has ever been written on them, and the answer they are for is not due.

What the third column of the sheet of terms says is nothing at all, and the heading over it is as it has always been. The paper on the low board has no date on it and nowhere on it for a name to go.

Somebody ruled four lines under two words in the corner of the one room a long time ago and nothing has been put on them since, and the answer they are for is not due.

Ruled and empty is what the third column of the sheet of terms is and nobody has improved the heading over it. The sheet on the low board is face up and undated and there is no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Sleet at the second hour of the afternoon and forty minutes of it and a skin of ice in the ruts of the middle road by four, and the standpipe full and inside its lip and the water in the ditch slow and clear, and a woman with a book under her arm who wrote a date and an hour and a fact and left two words out of it on purpose, and nobody thanked anybody.

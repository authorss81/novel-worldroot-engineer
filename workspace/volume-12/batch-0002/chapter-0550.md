# Chapter 550

## The Day Two Men Gave Themselves, And A Bar That Came Out Dry

The frost was back in the ground by five and there was no wind on it at all, and the sun came over the top of the eleven acres at about the seventh hour and laid the middle road in a hard white light with nothing moving on it. The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame.

The frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe in the yard was running full by the fourth hour and stayed inside its own lip. The clerk of this holding had the second book open before the light properly came and the line went in, and it was read back once at the ninth hour and was not read back twice.

**One thousand and seventy-seven hundredweight.**

Saturday gave five back off Friday, and what stands against the launder this morning is the three hundred and thirteenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. Off the long wall at seven came a hundred and forty-five mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-five the other.

The two men said it between them the way they do, and one of them said the other's half back before either of them put a hand down, and two hundred and eighty is the sum of that pair. Three hundred and eleven days is what the sheet gives the four bodies of households, and the clause underneath takes the three hundred and tenth of those mornings out of three hundred and twelfth. One under and one over.

The near board of the long wall carries six hundred and ninety-seven days and the far one seven hundred and forty-four, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning. Goes in at the seventh hour under the heading where the run of mornings goes, of a month eight months back: **one hundred and twenty-seven mornings of two hundred and thirty-seven**.

A Saturday is an odd morning and the run is read on an odd morning, and that frame has not moved since the first of this month. Soren Rill and the man of about fifty went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with the barrow and a bar. They were gone a little over three hours.

Nobody in this holding sent them and nobody went with them, and about nine people watched them go out of the first gate. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap with her own book out when they went, and she did not write the hour down, and she said why afterwards, standing at the bench by the pump house with the book shut under her arm.

**"There is a figure in this book since Friday and there was nothing in it for four years, and that has changed what the book is this morning."** She said that to the shut cover. **"On Friday it was a book with a date and an hour in it and nobody in this county could have told you what it was for. If I put an hour and two words under that figure now, then in four years there is a page in this county with a number and an hour and two words on it, and anybody who comes along can read those two words as the answer to the number."**

**"The two words would be the words of two men who went up a road and not mine."** She squared the book against her hip. **"So I am leaving the hour out of it. I have already taken one thing out of that book that I did not have in it before Friday, and I am not going to take a second one to finish a morning."**

They came back at about the tenth hour. The barrow came down its own track in front of them and the front wheel had been on ground it has not been on, and the rut it left going down was about four inches deep and dry in the bottom of it and had not filled by the time they were in the yard.

Soren Rill put his bar against the wall where it lives and said it to the wall, and he gave the reason before the thing.

**"I have had that bar in the second bend since Wednesday and it came out wet in two places this morning and dry in the third."** He turned the bar over once in his hands. **"The third one is new. It is about a yard below the other two and it is in ground that has a drain laid in it, because there is a drain laid in it, and I know where the drains are on that road because I have been cutting them for twenty years."**

**"So one of the three is a drain and the other two are not, and I did not put the drain there and I did not know about it until this morning, and I am not going to stand in a yard and tell any of you which of the three I would rather it was."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said it back to him flatly, with her book shut and in her hands and not open.

**"Two mornings ago you told this yard that line goes under the road and comes up in the field, and you said the two holes are four hundred yards apart and the bar came out wet at both of them."**

**"And it does."** He did not turn round from the wall. **"That has not changed and I am not standing here saying it has. What has changed is that there is a third place, and it is a drain, and a man with a bar who finds three places in one morning has got a piece of work and not a finding, and I am the one who has to hold that, because I am the one who went up."**

The man of about fifty said his own part of it at the bench, with his hands still on the barrow handles.

**"I said on Monday that I would put a wheel on it on Saturday and I have, and the ground carried it again, and I am not going to do it a third time until somebody tells me what for."** He turned the barrow a few inches on the track. **"A wheel is not a question. A wheel is a thing I can do, and I have now done it twice, and both times the ground took it, and if I am doing it a fourth time in a fortnight then I am a man who pushes a barrow up a field every Saturday."**

**"That is a job. This holding has not asked me for a job and I have not offered it one, and I would rather say that out loud at ten in the morning than work it out in three weeks and find out afterwards that I have volunteered for something."**

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles in the cold, and he stood at the tap for a while, and then he asked Marek one question, and it was the only question anybody asked anybody in that yard between the seventh hour and noon.

**"Would you go up with them next time."** He said it without any weight on it at all, the way he says things on the north wall.

**"No, and the reason is that if I go then it stops being two men with a barrow and it becomes the engineer of record inspecting a bend."** Marek gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"That has happened four times in nine years, and every one of those four times somebody stopped walking."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"I would rather be the fifth person in this county who is not going, and I would rather somebody wrote that down than have it be a thing I said once and then forgot I had said."**

Harlan Vetch said nothing to that and went and worked the north wall until six.

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm, and she stood at the low board with her back to it for a while before she said anything at all.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket that a household gave itself, and two men have just come back off a road with something that none of the three of them will write down."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"A household gave itself a date once, four miles off, and nobody asked it to, and it carried that page four miles on foot and did not stop at a gate."**

**"That is what a thing looks like when it is not a column, and I am not going to stand here and tell two men with a bar and a barrow how to get there, because I do not know and I have got three dates in my hand and four counties to be in."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way out, stopped at the long wall, read both boards, and did not go into the one room.

**"There is a bar coming out of three holes in one bend this morning, and there are two men on a road with one line under it, and neither list is mine and neither one is going into mine."** She said that to the boards and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it. **"If those two get onto one page in this county then one of them stops being true, and I have got nine places above that road that I will not name, and I have not been past one of them since Tuesday."**

**"I am not going to add a barrow to that to make it look like more than it is."**

The second bus put Sera Quill down at the first gate at about the seventh hour. She walked out to the low field on foot after it, stood at the gate with her back to the field for about ten minutes, and came back in without having gone through.

**"I have not written anything about this morning and I am not going to."** She said it to the gatepost on the way past. **"A figure of a bar in a ditch is not a record of a road. It is a thing two men saw, and if I put it in a county book then I have stopped being a witness to records and started being a witness to a field, and there is one of me and there are two of them, and they do not need me."**

The letter on the long table is ten days old this morning, ten complete days, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

It has not been moved and it has not been answered, and nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it, and the clerk of this holding said all three of those things out loud to the room at about the seventh hour and nobody asked him to say them twice.

Nobody in this yard had counted the days on any of the three of those papers out loud this week, and on Saturday morning three of them were counted, one after another, by three different people who did not know they were doing it.

The register form has been on the middle table for two hundred and seven days with nothing at the head of its column. Nobody has filled it. Nobody has refused it. Nobody is going to read it out a fifth time.

A hundred and thirteen days ago a room nine miles off put four signatures to a charter, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light, and the fourth has sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

In the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book there are four ruled lines and the second one of them is two hundred and one days old and has nothing on it. He was not asked about it this week and no one has offered him a pen for it.

Under the heading for sessions, this holding's book carries seven and carried seven when the week started. Two men going up a road is not a session and nobody entered an eighth this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and neither of the two has moved on any morning this week. The letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month. The page that came down four miles on foot went into neither of them either.

Six ruled rows stand for the things this holding does not know about, with no unit written against any of them. The seventh row is not ruled and was not ruled this morning. A bar out of three holes is not a seventh.

On the compost line the word in front of the figure is paid, the figure is twenty-six, and it is not discharged in twenty-six. It will not fall tomorrow either, because the interval that carries it does not land until the nineteenth of this month and does not care what anybody in this yard thinks about it.

Fifteen lines stand in the use log and the sixteenth is not there. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came back loaded, and where its journeys stand is eleven, and eleven is a floor and is not a target.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone. The seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It has not been read this week and its rule is the first of a month and the thirtieth and nothing else.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings. He was not fetched this morning and will not be fetched this week. No one in this holding has asked him which number he is and nobody is going to.

Nobody put a foot in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres this week and nobody measured it. The line that came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground, and the two of them are not added together on any page in this building.

In the corner of the one room there are two words and under them four ruled lines, and all four are bare, and the answer they are waiting for is not due.

The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and nobody has improved the heading over it. On the low board there is a sheet lying face up with no date on it and no line anywhere on it that a name could go into. It has not been filled, it has not been withdrawn and it has not been moved.

Frost in the ground at five and the sun on the road at seven and the barrow track hard enough by ten that a wheel could go up it again without taking a rut out of it, and the standpipe full and inside its lip, and the clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 555

## The Contractor Going Down The Fen Road, And A Reason He Did Not Take Back

The frost came back into the top two inches of the ground in the night and was gone by nine, and the water in the ditch was down about a foot on the lower stone at the outlet end, which is the lowest it has been since the wheel went back on the ninth of this month. The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the pack under the near stone came out damp and went back damp.

**One thousand and ninety-one hundredweight.**

Thursday came up eight off Wednesday and the three hundred and eighteenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder. The long wall gave a hundred and forty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-seven the other at seven, one saying and one checking, and the two of them are two hundred and eighty-five.

Four bodies of households are entered at three hundred and sixteen days, and the clause underneath that figure is the three hundred and fifteenth of those mornings out of three hundred and seventeenth. One under and one over.

Seven hundred and two days and seven hundred and forty-nine days are what the two boards of the long wall are saying this morning, and there are forty-seven days between them, and the drawer behind the near board has not been opened at any hour.

The second book carries nothing under that heading this morning and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of the run is a half number on a Thursday, and the space was empty yesterday afternoon and will be full tomorrow, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun.

Coll Renner came up the fen road at about the seventh hour with his roll over his shoulder and his straight edge in his hand and did not stop at the first gate, and he came the whole way to the middle of the yard before he said anything to anybody.

**"I am going to be off this district at the end of next week and I have come up to say so out loud while there is still somebody here who remembers me being here."** He said that to the long wall and gave the reason before the thing. **"There is a contractor on this roll who came up a road four days ago and put a straight edge across a line in a low field, and gave a bearing and a fall of one in forty, and would not have it written down, and has been back on that road since."**

**"That is one man once. On Friday a man in an office at Weir Row asked me whether I was taking work in this district that I had not been asked for, and he asked it in front of two other men, and I answered him straight because I had nothing to hide."**

He put the straight edge down flat on the long table and left his hand flat on it.

**"The reason I answered him straight is that I am on a roll and there are nine men on it in this district and four of them will do a holding for a season, and a man who is on a roll does not get asked that twice. He will get asked about the next time, and the next time will not be in a room with two other men in it."**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about half a minute.

**"So I have said what I came to say and I am not asking anybody for anything."** He took his hand off the straight edge. **"I am not asking to be asked to stay. I am not asking for a page. I am not asking any of the four of you to say a word about a bearing in a room, and if any of you does I will deny that it was said."**

**"I have held this license eleven years and I am going to hand it in at the end of next week with my name still on it, and the reason is that I am not going to be a man with a story about a field in a county where I have got to keep signing things for eleven more years."**

He picked the straight edge up and put it in the roll and rolled it.

The man of about fifty stood at the bench with his hand on the barrow handles and did not say one word about any of that, and afterwards, when the contractor was gone, he said four sentences to nobody and did not say them to the yard.

**"That is the second person to have looked at that field and then go away from it. He is the first one I have watched choose to."** He said it to the bench. **"I am not going to say anything about him to anybody else and I am not going to write it in this book, and that is not because he is leaving. It is because he made a decision about himself and I am not going to make one about him."**

Nia Vale came in at about the eighth hour on her way out and heard the whole of it from the one room door, and she came back into the yard and said the one thing she had come back in to say.

**"He has told this yard a real reason and then told us not to repeat it, and five of us were standing there, and I am going to repeat it once in this yard and then I am not going to say it again anywhere."** She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it. **"He is a man with a license and eleven years of it, and he has worked out that one bearing given in a yard is a thing a person can carry and three is a thing a person is known for."**

**"That is not a story about a field. It is a man doing the same arithmetic about his own name that everybody in this holding has been doing about their own work all week."**

**"I am saying it once because if it ends up said in one room in this county then in four years it will be a thing that was said in a room, and it was not. It was said in a yard with nine people in it."**

Marek was in the one room and had it all through the open door and did not come out, and he has not said anything about that decision since and did not say anything about it then.

**"There is a man in this county who came up a road once, gave the yard something, would not have it written, and has now taken himself off the district before anybody could make him into a thing."** He said that to the shut third book and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I had something to say to him at the gate and I did not say it, and what I had to say was a request."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"A man who has just told nine people that he does not want to be asked for anything does not get a request from me at the gate at the eighth hour."**

**"There is a worse sentence I could have said, and that one was not worse. It was a request. I have made four of them in nine years in yards and three of them ended with somebody stopping walking."**

Tova Reed came in from the seed house at about the ninth hour and stood at the low board, and she did not ask anybody a single question about any of it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and one of the four of us in this yard this week who was not put here by anybody has gone, and I am not going to be the one who says that is a loss."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"A man who chooses to go is not a vacancy. If I put it in a room as a loss then in four counties there will be a seed front that has said a district lost a man, and in about a year somebody will ask me which man and what work."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap with her own book out and shut and she wrote nothing about the contractor, and she said one thing about that afterwards, to the bench.

**"There is going to be a day when somebody asks me what I have got in that column and I am going to have to answer it, and today is not that day and the reason today is not that day is that a man has just told this yard what it costs to be asked."** She shut the book. **"So I am going to go on being a person who keeps a book and does not answer questions about it, for as long as I can get away with, and I am not going to hurry that because of a Tuesday."**

A register form has sat on the middle table for two hundred and twelve days with no heading over its column. Nobody has filled it in, nobody has turned it face down, and nobody in this holding is going to read it out a fifth time.

The charter was signed a hundred and eighteen days ago, nine miles off. In the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book, four lines are ruled and the second of them is two hundred and six days old and has nothing on it.

The compost line is paid at twenty-six and not discharged in twenty-six, and the interval that brings it lands tomorrow morning. Seven sessions stand in this holding's book and an eighth was not entered this morning, and a contractor giving up a license at the end of next week is not a session.

Fifty-three and fifty-three are the two columns this holding has never added together. The letter on the long table is fifteen days old, it went into neither of them, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it and no column anywhere in four books to put an answer in.

A contractor walking off a district is not a seventh row, and he is not a thing this holding does not know about either, because he told it so himself, in a yard, on a Thursday, with his roll over his shoulder.

Six rows are ruled for the unknown things and no unit stands against any of them.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. A straight edge went down on the long table this morning and did not go into it.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven.

A door nine hundred yards off has seven columns cut into its stone and the seventh of them is at sixty-four and it does not go back from sixty-four. It has not been read this week.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning, and the number of questions this holding has put to him is nil and stays nil.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has not been walked this week and has not been measured and has never had anybody standing in it in this holding's time.

The ground that came up above the middle road is a separate piece of country from that ring, and not one of these four books puts the two of them into the same sum.

Four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare, and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. The sheet on the low board is face up and undated and there is nowhere on it for a name to go.

Somebody went up a road on the fourth of this month with a straight edge and gave a bearing to a yard and would not let it be written down, and on the seventeenth of this month he came back up the same road and gave that yard a reason and took that reason away with him again, and the water in the ditch was down a foot on the lower stone by nine, and nobody thanked anybody.

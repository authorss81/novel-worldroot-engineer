# Chapter 572

## A Man Who Is Available, And A Woman Who Came Back With Nothing But A Hinge

The frost went off the ground in the hour before light and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour, which is the gate, and the gate on the low field was shut at seven and was still shut at eleven with the hole a quarter of an inch off true where it had been put on Thursday.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book before six and read it back once at the ninth hour to nobody in particular.

**One thousand and one hundred and ten hundredweight.**

What Sunday left against the launder, having given five back to Saturday, is the three hundred and thirty-fifth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been written down twice.

The rising half of the window was read at a hundred and fifty-six mornings and the falling half at a hundred and forty-six, and one man said the other man's half back to him, and three hundred and two is what the two of them are.

Out of the second book at the seventh hour came the run of mornings, read from under its own heading of a month nine months back, and a Sunday being an odd morning that run is read on the mornings that are odd.

**One hundred and thirty-eight mornings of two hundred and fifty-nine.**

The man of about fifty came into the yard at about the sixth hour and put his hand on the barrow handles and then took it off again and stood there.

He did not go up the four-mile road and nobody asked him why he did not, and he has not got a barrow journey left in him to go on and he told the bench that at the eighth hour with the reason first and without being asked for either of them.

**"I have carried three sacks up that road at the seventh hour for nine years and I carried the last of them yesterday, and this morning I woke up and there was nothing in my legs that wanted the top of that hill."**

He sat down on the end of the bench and stayed there.

**"I am not going to say that I am tired because I have not said that word in this yard in nine years and I am not going to start it at the end of it. What I am is a man who is available, and available is a thing you can be without anything having gone wrong anywhere."**

Nia Vale was in the yard at the seventh hour filling a can and said one thing to him about it, and gave the reason before the thing, and he did not answer her.

**"I have been in nine places above one road and four off another for a fortnight and not one of them has anybody in charge of it, and I have watched you be the ninth person in a district where nobody is in charge, and I am not going to say a word about that to you in this yard."**

She put the can on the step and picked it up again and carried it off.

Tova Reed came in off the direct road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with her satchel over her shoulder and had been four miles off and back and did not go into the one room.

**"I have been to a household four miles off and I looked at a gate and I have come back and I have one thing to say about it and I am going to say it to the tap and not to any of you."**

She set the satchel down outside the door.

**"The pin is out of the top socket and lying in the grass and the gate is standing open about a hand's width and the hinge is sound and the post is sound and the ground on the hinge side has come up about an inch in two winters."**

She said it once and then stopped and stood there.

**"That is all I have. I am not going to tell this yard what they should do about it, and I am not going to tell them whether it can be done, and I would like it noticed that I have come back from four miles and I have brought a hinge and not an opinion."**

Nobody in that yard said she had it wrong and nobody praised her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and one of the three dates in her hand was yesterday and she did not say so out loud.

The sheet in this building that carries the four bodies of households has three hundred and thirty-three days against them, and the clause under it takes the three hundred and thirty-second of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-fourth, one under and one over, and the two of those numbers are not a figure of water and are not added to one.

Seven hundred and nineteen on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and sixty-six on the far one, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling wrote both of them on the flat of his own hand with a pencil and looked at the forty-seven days between them for a while and did not say anything about them to anybody, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the gate and the man on the bench and the woman off the direct road through the gap in it.

He came out at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and stood at the bench and did not sit down on it, and gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure at all.

**"A woman went four miles and came back with a hinge and a post and an inch of ground, and she is right that it is not an opinion, and I am not going to be the man in this yard who turns a hinge into a position."**

He looked at the gate on the low field from where he was standing.

**"And a man is sitting on a bench in a yard with nothing in his legs that wants a hill, and I have not got a single instrument for that and there is not going to be one, and I have watched four years of people build instruments for men who were tired."**

He went back in and shut the door, which he did twice today.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came to the tap at about the fifth hour with her book shut in the inside pocket of her coat and put it on the step behind her so that she could use both hands, and she said one thing about the man on the bench before she said anything about the weather.

**"He is going to be asked by somebody within the week what he is going to do now, and I want it on the page that if he is asked in this yard then the answer that is given is that he is available and that nobody in this yard has any standing to ask him a second question about what available means."**

She picked the book up off the step and put it back in the pocket and shut the flap.

**"He will say the same thing to them that he said to the bench, and it will sound like a refusal, and I have watched four bodies of households in four counties turn a man refusing to be counted into a man who is owed something. That is not what is happening on that bench and I would like it written that it is not what is happening on that bench."**

Nobody in the yard asked her how she knew what was happening on the bench, and she did not explain, and the tap ran into the can while she said it and she did not stop to say it louder.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the yard from the seventh hour with a bar and did not go up the road, and he worked the eleven acres edge with his back to everybody until about two, and at the tenth hour he said his own number out loud going into the one room and the clerk wrote nothing and nobody asked him how he knows it.

**"Seven hundred and eighteen is on the near board,"** he said. **"That is the one and I have said it four times this month out loud and I have said it wrong twice, and I am going to keep saying it until one of you writes it down or one of you tells me to stop."**

Nobody in the one room told him either one.

The trough at the head of the four-mile road was a foot down at seven and had a finger of water in the bottom of it by eleven, which is the first water that has been in that trough since the middle of last week, and nobody in this yard put a figure on that either.

Thirty-two days is how long the letter on the long table has lain there, with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it between the two sheets about the two declinings. It is not answered, it is not refused, it has not been copied, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books.

A form has been on the middle table in this yard for two hundred and twenty-nine days and nothing has ever been put in it, and behind it a charter is a hundred and thirty-five days old and went through a door nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed that charter are still signed to a machine for stored light.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden walked past both of them on her way to the tap and did not touch either.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and there is no eighth this morning, and fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes have not moved on any morning of this fortnight and the letter went into neither of them.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and a hinge a hand's width open at a gate four miles off is not a seventh.

The use log has fifteen lines in it. The barrow stands against that wall where a man put it at ten yesterday and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor, and the twelfth is not going to be up that road in this month.

Twenty-nine fetchings stand against the man of about seventy and nobody fetched him today, and the count of questions put to him in this holding is nil at every one of its sites. There is a door nine hundred yards off with seven columns cut in it, and the seventh of those stands at sixty-six and is not read this week.

Handles with nothing on them, and a gate standing open a hand's width at a place four miles off with its pin lying in the grass, and a woman who brought one of them back and nothing else, and nobody thanked anybody.

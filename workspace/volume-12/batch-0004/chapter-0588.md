# Chapter 588

## The Twenty-First Of The Fifteenth, And A Middle Road Hard At The Top And Soft At The Bottom

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the gate, and the gate on the low field was shut at seven and was still shut at eleven, and the standpipe was running full and stayed inside its own lip, and the middle road was hard at the top by nine and soft at the bottom by ten and the water in the ditch along it was running full and clear.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure in the second book before there was light and read it back once at the ninth hour, standing up, at the long wall, and nobody asked him to read it a second time.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirty-four hundredweight.**

Tuesday came off Monday by five and the three hundred and fifty-first figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning, and the whole of that run has been going for four years and has not had the same figure in it twice.

The two halves at the long wall were a hundred and sixty-four mornings one way and a hundred and fifty-four the other, and the two of them came to three hundred and eighteen, and both of the men said the total out loud at the same time and neither of them heard the other one do it.

The run of mornings was read at the seventh hour out of the second book under the heading where it goes, of a month nine months back, and a Tuesday is an odd morning and the run is read on odd mornings, and the heading has not moved since the first of this month and it is not going to move before the month turns again.

**One hundred and forty-six mornings of two hundred and seventy-five.**

The sheet in this building that carries the four bodies of households has three hundred and forty-nine days against them, and the clause printed under that figure takes the three hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings out of three hundred and fiftieth, one under and one over, and it is a different figure from the launder and the two of them are not added to one another anywhere in this holding.

Seven hundred and thirty-five days is on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and eighty-two days is on the far one, and the two of them are forty-seven days apart with nothing in the gap.

The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning and at every hour of every morning of this month. Both men at the long wall said the same number out loud at the same time this morning and neither of them heard the other one do it.

There are three sheets on the long table. The first of them is forty-eight days old and came up the fen road at the ninth hour of the second of this month and has not been answered and has not been refused and has not been copied and has not been entered anywhere, and it lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

The second came on the twenty-eighth from a council of about nineteen households and has not been opened.

The third came on Thursday afternoon off a wall nine miles off, and it is correct in every particular, and it has a person's name at the head of it and a date at the foot of it.

No column has been ruled for any of the three of them in any of the four books in this holding, and the requests column and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and fifty-three and have not taken any of them.

Tova Reed was at the seed house from the fifth hour and came into the yard at about the ninth hour and stood at the tap and filled a can and said one thing to the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and not to the table.

**"I have got a council of about nineteen households that wrote to me on the twenty-eighth and said nothing, and I have carried it folded for a month, and I am not going to open it in a room this month, and I have said that out loud in three yards now and I am going to stop saying it."**

She set the can on the step.

**"Not because I have changed my mind. Because a thing you say three times becomes a thing you have said, and a thing you have said in a yard is in about four people, and the next time I say it there will be nine, and the next time there will be a number."**

Nobody in that yard said she had it right and nobody said she had it wrong and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The man of about fifty was at the bench at about the tenth hour with the barrow still against the wall behind him and the handles turned in, and he said one thing about the middle road to nobody and gave the reason first.

**"Hard at the top by nine and soft at the bottom by ten, every morning of this month, and there is no column in four counties for a road that changes under you while you are standing on it."**

He put his hand flat on the bench.

**"I am not going to fix it and I am not going to ask anybody to. I am a man with nothing to carry this month and I have been watching that road go soft under me for nine mornings and I have not said one word about it to anybody, and this morning I have said one word about it to nobody."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the three sheets and the gate and the road through the gap in it, and came out at about the eleventh hour and stood in the yard in the sun with his sleeve down over his forearm.

He said three things and none of them was about the three sheets on the long table, and he said the first of them with nothing in front of him at all.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

He said it once, at the same speed he says everything, and he did not raise his voice and he did not explain it to anybody and nobody in that yard asked him about it.

**"That is not a thing that has changed this month and it is not a thing that is going to change this season, and I have stopped waiting for the instrument and I am not going to be the man who goes looking for it again."**

He turned about a quarter of the way and looked at the eleven acres edge and at the ditch and at the gate and not at the long table.

**"And three other lines that are also true and that I am going to say once this morning because I have said them before and they have not softened and they are not going to."**

He took his hand off his sleeve and let it hang at his side.

**"The comfort is not standing. The father is not alive in the wood. The day does not come back."**

He stood still for a moment with his hand on his sleeve.

**"There is a piece of paper on a table in this yard with a date at the foot of it and a name at the head of it, and it is correct in every particular, and none of those three lines is about that paper and none of those three lines cancels it and none of it cancels the other."**

The clerk of this holding was at the long wall and had heard all of it and said one thing back, and it was not about the three lines either.

**"That form on the middle table is two hundred and forty-five days old and it is not filled and it is not refused and nobody in this holding has ever said the word tenure out loud about it in my hearing, and I have had a figure in my head for eleven years for what a thing like that is supposed to be worth and I have never once said it."**

He put his hand on the form and did not write anything on it.

**"The form is worth what it is worth and I have not said so in a yard and I am not going to start this afternoon."**

The register form is two hundred and forty-five days old, with a column headed nothing and nothing in it, neither filled nor refused, and the charter behind it went through a door nine miles off a hundred and fifty-one days ago, and three of the four bodies of households in that room are still signed to a machine for stored light and the fourth has sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

The second of the four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty-nine days old with nothing on it, and in the corner of the one room, under two words, four ruled lines stand bare and the answer they are for is not due.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them in eleven years, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and the count has not moved on any morning of this month and there is no eighth this morning, and fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand where they have stood all month.

The use log went back under its weight on the shelf at about the eleventh hour with fifteen lines in it, and the sixteenth is not among them, and the barrow's journeys stand at eleven and the barrow is against a wall where a man put it a fortnight ago, and eleven is a floor.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut in the stone of it and the seventh of those columns stands at sixty-six, and it was read twice in this last month, both on the rule, and from sixty-six it does not go back, and what it counts besides readings has not been answered and is not going to be.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched this morning, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites.

There is a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and no person in this holding has ever stood in it, and it was not walked and not measured this month and it has no figure against it on any page in this building, and the ground that came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground and the two have not been added to one another anywhere.

The third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty with its heading untouched, and on the low board a sheet lies face up, undated, with nowhere on it for a name to go, and it is not taken up and not withdrawn and not moved.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-eight, and nobody in this holding set the interval that moved it and nobody in this holding can move it again.

Nobody climbed a rung this month and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder by anybody in this yard on any morning of it.

Slope of a middle road going soft at the bottom while a man stands on it, and a gate shut on a quarter of an inch, and three sheets on a long table that nobody answered and nobody refused, and nobody thanked anybody.

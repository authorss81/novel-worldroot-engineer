# Chapter 579

## A Trough That Filled In The Night, And A Man Who Told A Yard It Is Not On Any Schedule

It rained all night and hard from about the fourth hour, and by six the water in the ditch along the middle road was up over the stones at the low end and running, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked at the outlet end in it, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wetter.

The standpipe was running full and inside its own lip by seven and stayed that way, and by the tenth hour the trough at the head of the four-mile road was up to within a finger of its lip after a fortnight of a foot down, and there was water standing in the cart ruts at the head of that road for the first time since the beginning of the month.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book before the light and read it back once at the ninth hour.

**One thousand and one hundred and twenty-seven hundredweight.**

Sunday came up eight on Saturday and the three hundred and forty-second figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning.

At the long wall the rising half was a hundred and sixty mornings and the falling half a hundred and forty-nine, and the two came to three hundred and nine, and the man who said the rising half said it out loud and the man who said the other one wrote nothing at all.

There is no figure against the run of mornings in the second book this morning, and the reason is on this page and not in the book. Half of that run is half a number on a Sunday, and the clerk did not put half a number into a book that has never had one in it, and the heading of a month nine months back is standing over the empty space.

The man who reads the schedule off the wall in the outer office came up the direct road at about the eleventh hour in the rain with his coat open and went straight up the four-mile road as far as the trough, and the man of about fifty went with him and neither of them said anything on the way up.

They came back down together and stopped at the trough and the man with the schedule said one thing about it and gave the reason first, and the man of about fifty did not say one word the whole time.

**"I am going to tell this yard the plainest thing I know about that trough and I am going to tell it out loud because I have been up and down this road for a long time and I have never once been asked."**

He put his hand on the wet stone of the trough's head.

**"That trough is not on the schedule. It is not in the maintenance line and it is not in the branch line and it is not in any of the four sheets I read off that wall every morning, and I have checked, twice, in my own time, and I am not going to put the checking in a book because it is not mine to put."**

He wiped his hand on his coat.

**"It is fed off a spur that comes down from the middle of the district and the spur is on the sheet and the trough is not, and that has been true for as long as I have been reading, and it means that when that trough was a foot down last week there was not one figure in this district that knew it and there is not one figure now that knows it has come back."**

Nobody in that yard asked him a question about it and nobody asked him to write it down and he did not offer to write it down.

**"I am a man who reads a schedule off a wall. I have got no standing, I have got no column, and I have got a sheet with four lines on it that four men in an office went over in the winter and I do not know one of them."**

He went out through the first gate at about the twelfth hour and the rain had stopped by then and the water in the ditch along the middle road was still up over the stones.

Nia Vale came in at about the eighth hour from above the low field soaked to the waist and put a bundle of what she had been carrying against the wall and said one thing about the trough before she said anything else.

**"I have been in nine places this month and I have not named one of them and I am not going to start, and what I am going to say is that about six of them would have gone down in a fortnight like that trough did, and nobody in any of the six has said one word about a trough to anybody."**

She wrung the end of her coat out on the step.

**"I am not saying the trough should be on that schedule. I am saying that a man has just told this yard that there is a piece of water in this district with no line in it, and that is the first time in four months that anybody has told us the shape of the hole, and I am not going to improve on it by saying what should go in the line."**

The man of about fifty was still at the trough when she had finished and he said one sentence to her and then went and sat on the bench.

**"I knew about that trough on the Saturday before last and I did not say anything for four days, and I have said that out loud in this yard once and I am not going to say it a second time, and I am not going to be the man who thanks her for standing here."**

He sat on the end of the bench with his back to the wall and looked at the water going down the ditch.

**"That man has been up and down that road for longer than I have been in this holding and he has never once had a piece of paper with his own name on it, and neither have I, and neither has the man with the bar who said no to a bank last week, and I have got three men in this yard now who know the exact shape of what is not written down and not one of us can write it."**

The clerk of this holding was at the long wall and heard that and did not come over and did not write anything down, and about an hour later he said one thing out loud to the second book, which was not addressed to anybody.

**"A trough came up a foot in a night and a man told this yard the reason it was a foot down last week is that no line in four counties has ever had it in it, and I have kept this book for eleven years and I have never had a column for a trough and I am not going to rule one at the tenth hour on a Sunday because a man was wet."**

The sheet in this building that carries the four bodies of households has three hundred and forty days against them, and the clause under that figure takes the three hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings out of three hundred and forty-first, one under and one over at every site.

There are two numbers on the long wall and they are seven hundred and twenty-six days on the near board and seven hundred and seventy-three days on the far one, and the man of about fifty read them both on his way up the four-mile road in the rain and did not stop at either, and the clerk of this holding did not offer to explain the forty-seven days between them to a man who had not asked.

The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

The letter on the long table is thirty-nine days old and there is a second sheet on the other side of it with about nine inches of bare board between the two, and neither of them has been answered, neither has been refused, neither has been copied, and no column has been ruled for either of them in any of the four books in this holding.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and heard all of it through the gap, and came out at about the first hour of the afternoon and went up the four-mile road to the trough on foot and stood at it for about ten minutes and came back down.

**"A man has told a yard that there is a trough in this district with no line in it, and the reason he has told them is that he checked twice in his own time and put the checking nowhere, and I have not got a single argument against any part of that."**

He gave the reason first and printed no figure at all in it.

**"I have spent four months wanting a column for what came back, and what came back this morning is a shape, and the shape is that a man with a schedule can name the exact edge of what is not in it. That is not a number and it is not going to become a number this week and I am not going to try to make it one."**

He went back in and left the door open, and the rain started again about an hour later and went on into the night.

That form is two hundred and thirty-six days old, its column headed nothing, and the charter behind it is a hundred and forty-two days through a door nine miles off, and the second of the four ruled lines in Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty days old with nothing on it.

A man came nine miles in the rain this morning and read a schedule off a wall and told this yard that a trough was not in it, and he did not look at any of the three of those documents while he was here.

Paid is in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and the line is going to move inside this month on an interval and not on anybody's instruction.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and a trough fed off a spur with no line in it is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning. The requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three and neither has moved and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month.

The use log has fifteen lines in it. The barrow's journeys stand at eleven against a wall where a man put it, and eleven is a floor.

There is a door nine hundred yards off with seven columns cut into the stone of it and nobody has walked those nine hundred yards this week. The seventh of those columns is where it was left, at sixty-six, and it does not go back. The old man is at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched on a wet Sunday, and the count of questions put to him in this holding is nil at every one of its sites.

Brim of a trough at the head of a four-mile road after a fortnight of a foot down, and a wet man telling a yard in plain words that it is not on the schedule and never has been, and nobody thanked anybody.

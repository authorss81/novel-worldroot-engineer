# Chapter 578

## A Letter That Has Been Folded And Carried Since The Twenty-Eighth And Not Opened

Bright hard sun on the yard at seven and a wind that went to the west before nine, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe full and inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure before the light and read it back once, at the ninth hour, standing up, with the second book open on his forearm.

**One thousand and one hundred and nineteen hundredweight.**

The man at the long wall had Friday's figure in front of him and took five off it on the slate before he said anything, and what the launder carries this morning is the three hundred and forty-first figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

At the long wall the rising half stood at a hundred and fifty-nine mornings and the falling half at a hundred and forty-nine, and the two of them came to three hundred and eight, and the man who said the falling half wrote it on the back of his own hand with a pencil and put the hand in his pocket.

That run only comes out of the second book on the mornings that are odd and this is one of them, so the clerk of this holding took it out at the seventh hour and read it under a heading of a month nine months back, and that heading has not been moved since the first of this month and is not going to be moved by him.

**One hundred and forty-one mornings of two hundred and sixty-five.**

Tova Reed was in the yard at about the sixth hour with the four counties' sheets under one arm and a folded paper inside her coat against her ribs, and she took the folded paper out and put it on the long table on the far side of the letter from the two sheets about the two declinings, and there is about nine inches of bare board between the two of them and there has been for a month.

**"This is from a council of about nineteen households and it came on the twenty-eighth and it says nothing at all, and I have carried it folded since the twenty-eighth and I am putting it down this morning because my coat is not a place for a piece of paper to live in any more."**

She put her hand flat on it and did not open it.

**"I have had it in eleven meetings and I have not opened it in one of them, and I am not going to open it in this yard either, and I want the reason on the page before anybody decides that I have not opened it because I am hiding something."**

She turned it over so the fold went the other way.

**"The reason is that if I open it in a room with people in it, then whatever is on it becomes the business of that room, and it is about nineteen households who wrote to a seed front and got nothing back, and they wrote nothing, and I have carried that nothing for a month, and there is no version of opening this that does not cost those nineteen households more than it costs me."**

Nia Vale was at the tap with the tap running and heard all of it and did not come over, and afterward she said one thing to the woman of about thirty-eight and not to Tova Reed.

**"That is the first thing anybody has said in this yard this month about a thing being expensive, and it is not about money, and I am not going to walk over there and agree with her about it in front of a man from the seed house."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden wrote nothing down and said nothing about the folded paper and went back to her can, and the clerk of this holding looked at it once and did not touch it and did not rule a line under it.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at about the ninth hour, saw the two sheets on the table, and read the room for about ten seconds without saying anything, and then he said one thing to the table and went out again.

**"There is a piece of paper on that table that has been folded in a coat for a month and there is a piece of paper on that table that has been lying flat for a month, and I know both of them by how they are lying and not by what is on them."**

He put two fingers on the edge of the long table and took them off again.

**"I have had a folded page in a book of my own since I was about twenty and I have never opened it in front of anybody, and I have worked out in about twenty-five years that the folding is not for the paper. It is so that a person can be asked about it without having to say what is in it."**

He went out through the first gate and did not come back into the yard before dark and nobody went after him.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap, and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the long table and looked at the two things on it without touching either.

**"There are two pieces of paper on this table a month apart and neither of them has a column and I have said this morning that I am not going to say the sentence that puts them together."**

He gave the reason first and printed no figure at all.

**"One of them asks a building nine miles off to put its sheet back the way it was. One of them says nothing, from a council of about nineteen households, and the woman holding it has decided that a crease is a cheaper container than a room."**

He moved his sleeve back over his forearm and left it.

**"I have watched two counties in my working life put those two kinds of paper in the same envelope, and the envelope is the only thing anybody ever reads afterwards, and the two of them never got read at all."**

On the sheet in this yard the four bodies of households carry three hundred and thirty-nine days, one under and one over, and the clause underneath reaches down and takes three hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings out of three hundred and fortieth.

The two boards of the long wall carry seven hundred and twenty-five days on the near side and seven hundred and seventy-two days on the far side, and a folded piece of paper from a council of about nineteen households lay on the table under them about nine inches away and neither number moved because of it, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The trough at the head of the four-mile road had a hand of water in it and was not full, and the man of about fifty was in the yard all morning and worked the eleven acres edge with a bar, and did not go up the four-mile road and nobody asked him to.

The letter on the long table is thirty-eight days old and has not been answered and has not been refused and has not been copied and has not been entered anywhere, and the paper that came on the twenty-eighth has not been opened, and neither of them is in the requests column and neither of them is in the section-nine notes and both of those are at fifty-three.

A hundred and forty-one days ago a charter went through a door nine miles off, and in front of it on the middle table there is a form that is two hundred and thirty-five days old with a column headed nothing and nothing in it.

The second of the four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred and twenty-nine days old with nothing on it. Three pieces of paper in this holding carry an age and not one of them carries anything else.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it moves once more inside this month on an interval that nobody in this holding has ever been asked about and nobody in this holding could move if they tried.

Nobody has ever written a unit against any of the six rows this holding rules for what it does not know about, and a council of about nineteen households that wrote nothing is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. A piece of paper being unfolded is not a session and nothing was unfolded this morning, and there was no eighth entry.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them. The barrow's journeys stand at eleven and the barrow is against a wall where it has been since the Saturday before last, and eleven is a floor.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched today, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites. Nine hundred yards off, the seventh column of a door is at sixty-six and does not go back.

Crease in a piece of paper that has been folded in a coat for a month and set down on a long table the other way up so that the fold would go the other way, and a woman standing next to it with her hand flat and not turning the corner, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The first. The first line of the rota worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, the standpipe full and inside its lip. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and forty-one mornings of two hundred and sixty-five, of a month nine months back, that frame having stood where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and one hundred and nineteen hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and forty-first. A SECOND SHEET LAY ON THE LONG TABLE THIS MORNING, ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE FIRST FROM THE TWO SHEETS ABOUT THE TWO DECLININGS, WITH ABOUT NINE INCHES OF BARE BOARD BETWEEN THEM, AND IT WAS NOT OPENED AND NO LINE WAS RULED UNDER IT AND IT WENT INTO NEITHER THE REQUESTS COLUMN NOR THE SECTION-NINE NOTES.** A sheet that is not opened is not a column and a crease is not a signature. This is a fact of a table in one room on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

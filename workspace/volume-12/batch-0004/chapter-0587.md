# Chapter 587

## The Highest Figure That Run Has Reached, And A Man In A Yard With Nothing To Carry

Cloud off the water in the night and a hard bright morning with the wind veering south by the ninth hour, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked at the outlet end, and the two stones at the head of it were got at and packed and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back dry.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure before the light and read it back once at the ninth hour, and it is the highest figure that run of mornings has reached since the ninth of the month before, and nobody in this yard looked up when he said it.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirty-nine hundredweight.**

Monday came up eight off Sunday, and what stands against the launder this morning is the three hundred and fiftieth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

The two halves at the long wall were a hundred and sixty-four mornings one way and a hundred and fifty-three the other, and the two of them came to three hundred and seventeen, and the man who said the rising half said the total and the man who said the falling half wrote it on the back of his own hand with a pencil and put the hand in his pocket without looking at it.

There is no figure against the run of mornings this morning in the second book, and the reason is on this page and not in the book. Half of that run is a half number on a Monday, and the heading of a month nine months back is standing over the empty space, and a book is not a place for half of a number and never has been in eleven years of keeping this one.

The man of about fifty was in the yard at about the sixth hour and did not go up the four-mile road, and the barrow is still against the wall it was put against a fortnight ago with the handles turned in, and he said one thing about that at the bench with the reason first.

**"I am not going to start carrying again this month and I have not told anybody in this yard that I have not told them, and that is the whole of my announcement and I would like it left where I have put it."**

He got up off the bench and looked at the eleven acres edge and then sat down again.

**"That is not a decision anybody in this holding has to live with. The sacks are at the top of the road and they will be there in the spring and I have got four counties of hedges that will not cut themselves before then, and there is no argument in this yard with me in it and I am not inviting one."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the wall at about the ninth hour and said his own number out loud on the way into the one room and it was the right one, and the clerk wrote nothing down and nobody asked him how he knows it.

**"Seven hundred and thirty-three is on the near board and that is the one, and I have said it eleven times this month out loud and I have said it wrong twice and I am going to keep saying it."**

He went into the one room and read the board over his own shoulder on the way past it and went out again and did not say anything else.

Tova Reed was at the seed house from the fifth hour and came into the yard at about the tenth hour and stood at the long table and read the third sheet standing up, and then she said one thing about it and one thing about herself, in that order, and gave the reason first.

**"There is a line on that sheet about the districts beyond the maintenance zone, and I have been in a room nine miles off twice this month with people who live past that line, and I have watched two of them work out what the line means without ever being told."**

She squared the sheet with the edge of the table and left her hand on it.

**"I am not going to say in this yard that the line is right or that the line is wrong. I have got four counties and a seed front and a date in my hand and I am not going to spend any of the three on a sentence about a schedule that arrived on Thursday."**

She took her hand off the paper.

**"And I am not going to be the seed front who stood in a yard and said what came back in a district was worth more than what has been kept in it. That sentence gets written down and then in a year it is the reason a district is a district."**

Nobody in that yard said she had it wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and the sheet on the table did not move.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the whole of it through the gap, and came out at about the eleventh hour and stood at the bench where the man of about fifty was sitting.

**"A man has announced that he is not carrying anything this month and a woman has announced that she is not spending a seed front on a schedule, and neither of those two people is asking this yard for anything at all."**

The reason came before the claim, and no figure of his own went with it into the yard.

**"The figure I read out at the ninth hour is the highest that run has reached since the ninth of the month before, and I have not said a word about it, and I am not going to, and I want it on the page that I did not, because the day I start marking figures in this yard is the day a figure becomes an event and an event becomes a reason."**

He turned the sleeve back over his forearm and stood there.

**"A run of numbers is not a season and a season is not a harvest and a harvest is not an answer, and I have got a piece of paper on a table in this room with a date at the foot of it, and the only honest thing I can say about all three of those things today is that I do not know which one of them is going to matter in the spring."**

The sheet in this yard carries three hundred and forty-eight days against the four bodies of households, its clause taking three hundred and forty-seventh of those mornings out of three hundred and forty-ninth, one under and one over as it has been at every site.

The near board says seven hundred and thirty-four and the far board says seven hundred and eighty-one, forty-seven days between them, and the man of about fifty read them from the bench where he was sitting without getting up, because he has read those two numbers every morning for nine years from that bench, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Forty-eight days is how long the first sheet has lain on that table, unanswered, unrefused and uncopied. The second arrived on the twenty-eighth and has not been opened. The third came on Thursday and is correct in every particular. Not one of the three is in the requests column and none is in the section-nine notes, and those two stand at fifty-three and fifty-three.

On the middle table there is a form two hundred and forty-four days old with nothing over its column and nothing in it, and behind it a charter a hundred and fifty days through a door nine miles off, and in the pocket of a book in this room the second of four ruled lines is two hundred and thirty-eight days old with nothing on it.

None of the three has been filled and none of the three has been refused and this yard has run out of ways to say that without saying it.

Paid goes in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-eight, and the interval that moved it was not set by anybody in this holding. On the board in the one room the second rule is cut the same length as the first and stands empty, and the count of blanks on that board is thirty-nine, and nobody has written in the margin of the page where they are counted.

Nothing has ever been written against any of the six rows this holding rules for what it does not know about, and three people who asked for nothing in this yard this morning are not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning. The use log has fifteen lines in it and there is no sixteenth, and the barrow's journeys stand at eleven and eleven is a floor and it is not going to be twelve before the month turns.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings and this morning was not one of them, and no person in this holding has put to him the question of which number he is at any of its sites, and the count of that is nil. Nine hundred yards off, the seventh of seven columns cut in a stone door is at sixty-six, and it does not go back from there.

Track up the middle of a four-mile road going soft under a barrow that is not going up it this month, and a figure read out at the ninth hour that nobody in the yard looked up for, and nobody thanked anybody.

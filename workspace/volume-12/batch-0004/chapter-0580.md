# Chapter 580

## The Fifty-Fifth Time, And A Rule That Is Still Empty And Is Going To Stay Empty

Wind from the north east in the dark and hard by the fifth hour, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour, which is the gate, and the gate on the low field was shut and stayed shut all morning on a hole a quarter of an inch off true.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book before there was light and read it back once at the ninth hour and did not read it back twice.

**One thousand and one hundred and twenty-two hundredweight.**

What Monday left against the launder, having given five back to Sunday, is the three hundred and forty-third figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

The rising half of the window was a hundred and sixty mornings and the falling half a hundred and fifty, and the two of them came to three hundred and ten, and the man who said the falling half said the total before the other one had got his number out.

At the seventh hour the run of mornings was read out of the second book under the heading where it goes, of a month nine months back, and a Monday is an odd morning and the run is read on odd mornings.

**One hundred and forty-two mornings of two hundred and sixty-seven.**

At the third hour a load came up the fen road and stood on the steelyard for a quarter of an hour and went back down the fen road the way it had come, and nothing was taken off it, and the plates took it twice and gave it up once.

Five hundred and fifty-eight hundredweight came off the steelyard, eleven above the one before it, and the two halves were made up twice at the boards and were not added to each other. There are two names on the ticket side by side in one hand, and that is the forty-fifth pair.

The second rule cut in the board in the one room is cut the same length as the first and was cut at the same time by the same hand, and it came back empty again this morning for the fifty-fifth time, and thirty-nine is what it has now been counted empty, and the count goes on the return and not on the day and it will be thirty-nine and not forty this afternoon.

The clerk of this holding stood in front of the board at about the sixth hour and said what he says on every return, and he gave the reason before it, and this morning he added one sentence at the end that he has not said on any of the other fifty-four.

**"Fifty-five mornings that rule has come back empty, and there is a date standing at the head of it off the Sunday return, and a date at the head of a blank rule has never once in my life turned into a reason."**

He knocked the board once with the side of his hand, lightly, the way a man knocks a door he already knows is shut.

**"A rule that came back empty opens nothing, and I have said that at the sixth hour on fifty-five mornings and I am not going to stop saying it."**

He put his finger on the first rule, the one with the returns on it, and left it there for a moment.

**"This is the last return this holding is going to see for a while, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that I know how long a while is."**

He moved his hand off the board and let it hang.

**"I am also not going to say that the rule will be filled in the gap. If a rule is going to be filled in it will be filled in by somebody who has been told what it is for, and nobody in this building has been told, and I have asked nobody and I am not going to."**

He said that to the board and then went and read the second book back at the ninth hour and read it back once.

The man of about fifty came into the yard at about the seventh hour and said one thing to the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who was at the wall with his bar, and gave the reason before the thing.

**"The line goes up that bank in the dark on Tuesday and there is going to be four of us on it and I am not going up, and I am telling you that now instead of not coming."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling did not look round.

**"I said no last Wednesday and I meant it on Wednesday and I have thought about it every day since and I have not changed my mind, and you have not asked me a question, and I am going up on Tuesday anyway, and I would like it noticed that neither of those two things is the other one's fault."**

He went on up the eleven acres with his back to the yard and did not say anything else about it.

The four bodies of households stand at three hundred and forty-one days on the sheet in this building, and the clause under that figure reaches one morning below it and one above it, three hundred and fortieth out of three hundred and forty-second.

The near board of the long wall reads seven hundred and twenty-seven days and the far board reads seven hundred and seventy-four days, forty-seven days apart, and the count of blanks on the board in the one room is thirty-nine and goes on the return and not on the day, and the drawer behind the near board of the long wall stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Marek had the one room to himself from the fifth hour with the door open, and the return came through the gap in it while he sat there, and he came out at about the tenth hour and stood in front of the board and read the two rules on it without touching either.

**"A rule cut the same length as the one above it has been empty fifty-five times, and a man has just said out loud that he is not going to ask anybody what it is for."**

He put his sleeve back over his forearm and left it there.

**"I have wanted to know what that rule is for for four years and I have not asked either, and this morning I have found out that the man who keeps the book and I have arrived at the same place and neither of us said so to the other one."**

The reason was out first and the rule itself stayed unfilled, and what he said about it came with no number attached to it.

**"I am not going to fill it and I am not going to guess at it, and a guess in a book that a man has kept for eleven years is a thing that would be in this room for longer than any of us."**

He went back in and shut the door and then opened it again about a minute later, which is a thing he has started doing this month.

Forty days is the age of the letter on the long table, and the paper that came on the twenty-eighth lies beside it with about nine inches of bare board between the two. Neither has been answered, refused or copied, and no column has been ruled for either of them in any of the four books in this holding.

Two hundred and thirty-seven days on the middle table, a column headed nothing, nothing in the column, and neither filled nor refused, and a hundred and forty-three days on a charter behind it that went through a door nine miles off. The second of the four ruled lines in Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty-one days old. Nobody read any of the three out loud this morning and nobody has read the form out a fifth time in its life.

Twenty-seven is what the compost line carries and paid is the word in front of it, and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. It falls inside this week on an interval that nobody in this holding set and nobody in this holding can stop.

Six rows are ruled in this holding for what it does not know about and not one of them has ever taken a unit, and an empty rule cut beside a full one is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

A load standing on a steelyard for a quarter of an hour is not a session, and this holding's book went on showing seven at the end of it. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes took nothing this morning either, and the letter is in neither.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them and nobody in this yard opened it. The barrow's journeys stand at eleven and eleven is a floor.

Nobody in this holding walked the nine hundred yards to that door this morning, and the seventh of its seven columns is at sixty-six and does not go back from there. The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings, was not fetched this morning, and has not been asked which number he is at any hour of any morning. That count is nil at every one of its sites.

Rule in a board in the one room, the same length as the one above it, empty for the thirty-ninth time and going to be empty on Thursday, and a man who has said he is not going to ask, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The third. The third line of the rota worked, the gate, shut at seven and standing shut at eleven. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and forty-two mornings of two hundred and sixty-seven, of a month nine months back, that frame having stood where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and one hundred and twenty-two hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and forty-third. A LOAD CAME UP THE FEN ROAD AT THE THIRD HOUR, STOOD QUARTER OF AN HOUR AND WENT BACK THE WAY IT CAME. IT CAME OFF THE STEELYARD AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT HUNDREDWEIGHT, ELEVEN ABOVE THE LAST, THE FORTY-FIFTH PAIR, TWO NAMES IN ONE HAND, THE TWO HALVES RECONCILED TWICE AND NOT ADDED, THE ORDINAL BEING NINE ADDED TO THE MORNING BEFORE AND NOT CARRIED FORWARD. THE SECOND RULE ON THE BOARD IN THE ONE ROOM IS CUT THE SAME LENGTH AS THE FIRST AND IS BLANK, AND THE COUNT OF BLANKS ON THAT BOARD IS THIRTY-NINE AND GOES ON THE RETURN AND NOT ON THE DAY.** A rule that came back empty opens nothing. This is a fact of a board and a book in one room on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

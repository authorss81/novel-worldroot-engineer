# Chapter 575

## A Man Asked To Go Up A Bank In The Dark, And A Reason Given Before The Answer

The sun was on the yard at seven again and the middle road went from hard at the top to soft at the bottom in about two hours, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked at the outlet end, and the pack under the near stone came out dry and went back dry, which it has not done in a fortnight.

The figure went into the second book at the first hour and the clerk read it back once at the ninth hour and said it out loud to the long wall because the wall was in the sun and there was nobody else in the yard to say it to.

**One thousand and one hundred and twenty-one hundredweight.**

Wednesday came up eight on Tuesday, and the figure standing against the launder this morning is the three hundred and thirty-eighth of a run in which nothing has been written down twice.

A hundred and fifty-eight mornings one way against a hundred and forty-seven the other, and the man who said the first said the total out loud this time, three hundred and five, and the other man said nothing and went to the outlet end.

There is no figure against the run of mornings this morning and the clerk left the space empty, because half of that run is a half number on a Wednesday, and the heading of a month nine months back is standing over the space it has stood over since the first of this month, and it was full yesterday and it will be full tomorrow and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was asked at the seventh hour to go up the bank in the dark on Saturday, and he said no, and he gave the reason before the answer and nobody in the yard had asked him a second question.

**"I am going to say why first, because the last man who said no to a question in this yard had to say it afterwards and it sounded like something else."**

He put the bar down against the wall and stood with his hand flat on the top of it.

**"I have been on that bank at the second hour four times this month and I have carried my share of the stone and I have not once been asked whether I wanted to be on it, and I am not saying that anybody has done wrong."**

He turned the bar over and set it down again the other way up.

**"I am saying that I am not going to be the fifth name on a thing that nobody has ever asked me about once, and I would rather be asked twice in a year than be the fifth."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was standing at the tap when he said it and she said one thing back to him and it was not agreement.

**"You have been carrying your share of that stone up that bank since the ninth of the month before and nobody has asked you that either, and I have watched you not be asked it eleven times, and this morning is the first time you have noticed."**

She set her can down.

**"I am not going to thank you for saying it. I want it on the page that you said it and I want it on the page that the four before you did not."**

Nobody in the yard said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and the man of about thirty-one of Silling went and worked the north wall with his back to everybody until about three.

Harlan Vetch came off the direct road at about the sixth hour having walked the nine miles and went to the far end of the north wall and started at the top, and he did not stop for the conversation at the tap and he did not look at the barrow against the wall on his way past it.

**"I have been asked twice this month whether I would come on a bank at night and I have said yes twice and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to today."**

He said it to the wall and not to anybody and then worked until the light went.

Tova Reed was at the seed house from the fifth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and came in at about the eighth hour and said one thing about the two dates she has met and stopped there.

**"Two of the three are met. I am not going to say which two and I am not going to say when the third is, because the third is in a room nine miles off and there are nine people in it and one of them is going to have to stand up in front of the others."**

She squared her sheets against the low board.

**"And I am not going to tell anybody in this yard what happens in that room when I get there, and I would like the yard to be ready for the possibility that nothing happens, because nothing happening in a room with nine people in it is a thing that happens."**

Nobody in the yard asked her what she wanted them to do about the possibility, and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Three hundred and thirty-six days is the aggregate standing against the four bodies of households on the sheet in this yard, and the clause beneath it takes the three hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-seventh, one under and one over.

The two boards on that wall read seven hundred and twenty-two days and seven hundred and sixty-nine days, and there is forty-seven days between those two figures and not one word written in the gap.

The man who reads the far side of the wall said his half out loud at the seventh hour and the man who reads the near side said it back to him, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Marek was in the one room all morning with the door open and had the man of about thirty-one of Silling and the two dates through the gap in it, and he came out at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with his sleeve down over his forearm and stood at the north wall and looked at it for a while without touching it.

**"A man said no to a bank in the dark and gave the reason before the answer, and it turns out four other men had already been on that bank four times and had not noticed, and I am the one who has been counting them and I did not tell them."**

He turned the sleeve back a half inch and stopped there.

**"I have no book in four counties that would let me put that right and I am not going to build one this afternoon, and I am not going to tell him I am sorry for it either, because he did not ask me and because in four weeks it would turn into a thing he has been owed."**

He went back in and left the door open.

The trough at the head of the four-mile road had a hand of water in it and was not full at seven and was not full at eleven, and the man of about fifty was in the yard all day and did not go up that road and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why not.

The letter on the long table is thirty-five days old and lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and no column has been ruled for it and it has not been copied and it has not been entered and it has not been answered and it has not been refused.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book has four ruled lines in it and the second of them is two hundred and twenty-six days old with nothing on it, and on the middle table in the next room there is a form that is two hundred and thirty-two days old with a column headed nothing and nothing in it, and the charter behind that form is a hundred and thirty-eight days through a door nine miles off.

Three documents and not one of them has been read out in this yard this month.

Paid goes in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it will not fall this week and nobody in this holding has anything to do with that.

In eleven years nobody has written a unit against any of the six rows this holding rules for what it does not know about, and a man saying no to a bank in the dark is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log has fifteen lines in it. The barrow's journeys are at eleven and the barrow is against a wall where it has been since Saturday and there is no hand on it today.

Nine hundred yards off, the seventh column of a door stands at sixty-six, and this week has not read it once. The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings, was not fetched today, and nobody in this holding has asked him which number it is, so that count of questions is nil at every one of its sites.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book, and a man saying no to a bank at the seventh hour is not a session, and the requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three and neither has moved and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of the month before.

Plates under a cart that came and went and did not take anything, and a bar against a wall, and a man who said no on a bank with his reasons first, and nobody thanked anybody.

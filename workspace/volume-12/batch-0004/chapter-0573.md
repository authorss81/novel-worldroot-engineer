# Chapter 573

## A Heap That Is On Nobody's List, And A Man Who Reads A Schedule Off A Wall

Rain came in off the water about the second hour and went on until the fifth and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark, four people up the headland with lamps on two of them, the boundary stones along the top of the bank, the ditch cut along the eleven acres, and the loose stone barrowed off the top into the heap by the gate.

The pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet and the standpipe was running full and inside its own lip by seven.

No field was taken, and that is the twelfth morning this year that this line has gone up that bank in the dark and come back down it with nothing at all in the taking column, and there is nobody in this holding to blame for a field that has been waiting four years for anybody to put a name in it.

The clerk of this holding had the count in before the light and read it out once at the ninth hour, and it is a hundred and twenty-three mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and ninety-four not, and twenty-nine and ninety-four is a hundred and twenty-three.

There are two reckonings of the rotation and they do not agree with one another this morning. This is the seventy-first morning of it counted one way and the fifty-eighth counted the other, and there has been thirteen in that gap for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what it counts.

**"The count in force is one higher than it was on Saturday and nothing came down to take it off, and the line came round and went up a bank in the dark, and that is the whole of the arithmetic."**

The clerk said that to the board and not to anybody, and he did not put a hand on either figure.

The launder figure went in at the first hour and came back out of the second book at the ninth, and Monday came up eight off Sunday.

**One thousand and one hundred and eighteen hundredweight.**

The three hundred and thirty-sixth figure of a run with nothing entered in it twice, and at the long wall the two halves came to three hundred and three, being a hundred and fifty-seven mornings one way against a hundred and forty-six the other, and one of the two men said the other's half back to him and neither of them wrote it anywhere.

Nothing stands against the run of mornings in the second book this morning, and the reason is the one it is every fourth day, being that half of that run is half a number on a Monday, and the clerk left the space empty with the heading of a month nine months back standing over it.

The man who reads the schedule off the wall in the outer office came up the direct road at about the ninth hour on foot, and it is the first time he has been inside this gate in three weeks, and he had nothing with him except the sheet of the schedule rolled under his arm and a hand that had been in a pocket.

He went straight to the heap of stone by the gate on the low field and stood at the top of it and looked at it from three sides and put his boot on the bottom course and did not move it.

**"I have come to look at that and not to ask anybody about it, and I am going to say why I have come before I say what I have found."**

He took his boot off the stone.

**"There is a heap of stone at the bottom of a bank at the end of a four-day line and it has got a foot higher on it than it had a fortnight ago, and every stone in it came off that headland in the dark with four men, and not one of those stones is in the schedule."**

He looked at the gate beside it, which was shut on a hole a quarter of an inch off true.

**"The schedule says what is done and in what order and by how many, and I read it off that wall every morning and I have read it off that wall for a long time, and it does not have a line in it for what a man with a barrow does because it is not a job anybody gave him."**

The man of about fifty was in the yard and came out and stood at the far end of the heap and said one thing and then stopped.

**"That is the fourth person in a month who has stood at that heap and said it out loud and I have not written any of the four down and I am not going to write this one either."**

The man who reads the schedule off the wall nodded at that and did not ask him a question about it, and then he said the rest of it, and he said it to the heap.

**"It is not on the schedule and it is not in the book in the outer office and there is nobody I could write to about it who has the standing to be told, and I have gone up and down that road in my working life and I have never once had a piece of paper with a column for a heap of stone in it."**

He turned the roll of the schedule over in his hand and looked at the gate beside the heap, which was shut on a hole a quarter of an inch off true.

**"That gate is shut because a man moved a hole on Thursday and there is no line in the schedule for the hole either, and the schedule does not need one, and that is the part I have been not saying for three weeks and I am saying it now in the rain in front of four people."**

He put the roll back under his arm and went out through the first gate.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap from the fourth hour and heard the whole of it and wrote nothing and said nothing about it for about an hour, and then said one thing to the can, with the reason first.

**"That man has read a schedule off a wall every morning for longer than I have been at this tap and he has just told a yard that a heap of stone is not in it, and I am not going to make a row out of the fact that he told us."**

She set the can down.

**"If I write that down then in four years there is a page that says a heap of stone was spoken about in a yard, and then in nine years there is a page that says a heap of stone is a thing this holding has an opinion about, and by then it will be a thing this holding is responsible for and there are four of us in this county who would have to be told."**

Nobody in that yard agreed with her and nobody disagreed with her and nobody thanked the man who had walked nine miles to look at a heap.

Soren Rill was not in the yard today. He was up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and the second bend at the ninth, and the trough at the head of that road had a hand of water in it and had not been full since the middle of last week.

Three hundred and thirty-four days is what the sheet in this building carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath that figure takes three hundred and thirty-third of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-fifth, one under and one over at every site.

There are two numbers on the long wall this morning and they are seven hundred and twenty on the near board and seven hundred and sixty-seven on the far board, and the man who reads the schedule off the wall in the outer office read both of them standing in the rain before he went and looked at a heap of stone, and said nothing about the forty-seven days between them. The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

The letter on the long table has been lying there thirty-three days, and it is face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it. Nothing has been copied out of it, it is neither answered nor refused, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and heard the man from the outer office through the gap and came out at about the eleventh hour and went and stood at the heap of stone and did not put his hand on any of it.

**"A man read that heap off three sides and put his boot on the bottom course and told a yard that there is no column in this district for a heap of stone, and that is not a complaint. That is a man telling us the exact shape of what is missing."**

He gave the reason before the thing and left every figure out of it.

**"I have been trying since the ninth of the month before to write down what came back and who is looking after it, and this morning somebody who reads a schedule off a wall every day stood in the rain and told four people that the schedule does not have them in it either, and I have not got a single argument left against him."**

He came back through the first gate and did not say anything else about it.

Nobody in this yard has filled that form in two hundred and thirty days and nobody has refused it, and the heading over its column is nothing, which is a heading.

The charter it lies in front of is a hundred and thirty-six days through a door nine miles off, and the man who reads a schedule off a wall in the outer office would be able to tell you what a form with no heading is for and he was not asked and this yard did not ask him.

Paid is in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it moves inside this month on an interval and not on anybody's decision, and nine people in this county found out last week at once and not one of them said it first.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and a heap of stone that four men carried up a bank in the dark is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning. The requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three and neither has taken anything today and the letter is in neither of them.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them, and a man from the outer office did not open it and did not ask to look at it, and the barrow's journeys stand at eleven against a wall where a man put it, and eleven is a floor.

He was not fetched today and stands at twenty-nine fetchings, and the count of questions put to him is nil at every one of its sites, that man of about seventy. Nine hundred yards off, a door carries seven columns cut in the stone of it, and the seventh of them is at sixty-six and does not go back from there.

Course on course on course of stone at the bottom of a bank with a gate shut on it a quarter of an inch out of true, and a man who read it off three sides and told a yard that there is no column for it anywhere in four counties, and nobody thanked anybody.

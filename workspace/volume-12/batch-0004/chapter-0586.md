# Chapter 586

## A Records Witness And Not An Eyewitness, And A Hand At The Foot Of A Sheet

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe was running full and inside its own lip and the sun was on the yard at seven with the ice gone out of the ruts by six.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth hour and did not read it back twice.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirty-one hundredweight.**

Sunday took five off Saturday, and what stands against the launder this morning is the three hundred and forty-ninth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

At the long wall the rising half was a hundred and sixty-three mornings and the falling half a hundred and fifty-three, and the two of them came to three hundred and sixteen, and the man who said the falling half had the total out before the other one had finished saying his.

It came off the second book at the seventh hour on Sunday, an odd morning, and that run is read on odd mornings only. The frame it is written under is of a month nine months back and the heading has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and forty-five mornings of two hundred and seventy-three.**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and stood at the long table and read all three sheets standing up, one after the other, and did not touch any of them, and then she read the third one a second time and put her hands behind her back while she did it.

**"I am going to say what I am, first, out loud, before I say anything else, because there is going to be a great deal said about that third sheet in four counties in about a month and I want one sentence of mine in the front of it."**

She turned round to the yard.

**"I am a witness to records. I am not a witness to anything that happened. I have not been in this district since the twelfth of the month before and I have not been in the outer office since the winter, and the only thing I can speak to is what is on paper and who put it there and when."**

She put one finger down beside the date at the foot of the third sheet without touching the paper.

**"So here is what can be checked in four years by anybody at all. There is a heading. There is a person's name under the heading. There is a table with places in it and dates against the places, and the dates are in an order, which means somebody thought about the order and not only about the dates."**

She took her finger away.

**"There is a hand at the foot of it that has had the whole thing read out to it, because you can tell, and I have been in four counties of offices where I have watched a clerk be made to read a thing out to the man who is paying him, and the hand at the foot of that sheet has the marks of a man who has been made to read."**

She looked at the clerk of this holding.

**"And there is a seal in the corner, and a seal is not a signature, and a man in a county four counties off is going to say that a seal means somebody took responsibility for it, and I am telling this yard now, in front of five people, that a seal means a piece of wax was pressed and nothing else that I know of."**

The man of about fifty was at the bench and said one thing about that from where he was sitting and gave the reason first.

**"I have been pressing a seal onto paper for nine years for men who did not read it out to me once, and if that is the whole of what a seal is then there is a great many things in this county with a seal on them that nobody has read."**

He did not get up off the bench.

**"I am not saying that is a reason to think less of that sheet. I am saying it is a reason to read it, and I have read it, and I am going to read it again, and I am not going to say one word in this yard about what is in it beyond what the woman at that table has already said about it."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden asked one question at the tap and it was about the second sheet and not about the third.

**"Is the second one the same paper as the first one."**

Sera Quill read the foot of the second sheet and the head of the first one and stood still for a moment.

**"They are both off the same schedule. I can tell you that from two sheets without going near the wall, and I am not going to tell you anything else about it, and I want it noticed that I have not said that they are the same document, because I have not checked and I do not check things in front of people."**

She picked her bag up off the table and set it down again on the same spot.

**"There are three pieces of paper on that table and about nine inches of board between each of them, and there has been nine inches between pieces of paper on that table for a month, and I am not going to say that the space is a decision. I have watched a great many desks where the space between two pieces of paper was an accident."**

The one room had its door open from the fifth hour and Marek was in it, and everything on the long table came through the gap where he sat, and at about the eleventh hour he came out and stood at the table and looked at the three sheets without touching any of them.

**"A woman stood in this yard this morning and said out loud what she is, before she said anything else, and then she told us the four things about that sheet that can be checked in four years, and she told us the one thing about it that cannot."**

He said why before he said what, and nothing he said carried a figure that belonged to him alone.

**"I have watched a great many people go into a room in four counties and try to be useful about a piece of paper, and being useful about a piece of paper means saying one thing more than can be checked, and then the one thing more is the thing that gets remembered in four years."**

He put his hand flat on the bare board at the end of the table.

**"So I am going to leave that table where it is this afternoon, and nobody in this holding is going to be asked anything about those three sheets in a room in a month."**

He turned the sleeve back over his forearm and left it there.

**"If a man from a county four counties off comes through that gate in a month he is going to be told what the woman at that table has just said and nothing else, and he is going to be told it by a person in a yard and not by a letter."**

The sheet in this yard carries three hundred and forty-seven days for the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath it runs one under and one over, three hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings out against three hundred and forty-eighth.

Seven hundred and thirty-three days on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and eighty on the far board, and a records witness who came down the fen road that morning read both numbers and then said out loud that she was a witness to records and not a witness to anything that happened, and the forty-seven days between the two boards is not a thing she spoke about, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

The first sheet on the long table is forty-seven days old and has not been answered and has not been refused and has not been copied, and the second came on the twenty-eighth and has not been opened, and the third came on Thursday afternoon, and none of the three is in the requests column and none is in the section-nine notes and those two are at fifty-three and fifty-three.

Two hundred and forty-three days is the age of that form, with a column headed nothing and nothing in it, and the charter behind it went through a door nine miles off a hundred and forty-nine days ago, and the second of the four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty-seven days old.

A woman stood in this yard this morning and told this holding exactly what can be checked about a piece of paper in four years, and none of those four years has anything to do with the three of those documents.

The compost line stands at twenty-eight and paid goes in front of it, not discharged in twenty-eight. The rule in the board in the one room stands empty and the count of blanks on that board is thirty-nine, and there is no margin on the page where they are counted.

Six rows are ruled here for what this holding does not know about and nothing has ever been written against any of them, and a seal in the corner of a piece of paper is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Nothing this morning made an eighth session in this holding's book. Fifteen lines are in the use log and no sixteenth, and the barrow is still against the same wall a man put it beside a fortnight ago with its eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor.

There is a door nine hundred yards off with seven columns cut into its stone. The seventh of them is at sixty-six and does not go back and was read twice in this last month, both on the rule, and what it counts besides readings has not been answered. The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings, was not fetched today, and has been asked which number he is at no site in this holding.

Hand at the foot of a sheet with the marks of a man who has been made to read, and a woman who said what she is before she said anything else, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 574

## A Man With A Boot, And A Woman Who Would Not Give Him A Number

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and there was sun on the yard by seven with no cloud on it at all.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure in the second book before the light and read it back once, at the ninth hour, and stopped at once.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirteen hundredweight.**

Tuesday came off Monday by five and the standing against the launder is the three hundred and thirty-seventh figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

The rising half of the window went on the wall at a hundred and fifty-seven mornings and the falling half at a hundred and forty-seven, and the two of them came to three hundred and four, and the man who said the first of them did not say the total out loud and the other one worked it out and said it anyway.

At the seventh hour the run of mornings was read out from under its heading in the second book, of a month nine months back, and a Tuesday is an odd morning and the run is read on odd mornings and that heading has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and thirty-nine mornings of two hundred and sixty-one.**

A man came to the first gate at about the eighth hour from a tap four miles off with a dog he did not bring in and asked the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden how much of the strip on the low field above the middle road there is.

She put her book on the wall and did not open it and gave the reason before anything else.

**"I will not give you a number and I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to give you the one thing I have got, and you can take it or leave it."**

She turned and looked up the middle road toward the low ground.

**"The moment I say a number to a man from a tap, that number is about a piece of ground, and in four years it is in a book, and in nine years somebody in a county four counties off is going to send a body of households a figure and call it what came back."**

She put her boot on the wall and then took it off again.

**"It will be the last figure anybody has for that ground and it will be wrong, because it will be one number for a thing that is not one thing."**

Her heel stayed against the stone at the end of the yard while she said it.

**"What I have got is a length. I have walked it and I have paced it with a boot and I will tell you it is about the length of this yard eleven times from that stone to the corner, and that is not a figure of ground and I will not write it in anything."**

The man said that was not much of an answer and she said it was the answer she had and he went away with it and did not come back that day.

Nia Vale was at the far end of the low field at the same hour with a stick and no bar and came back down to the tap at about the eleventh hour and heard the whole of it from the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who had been standing at the wall.

**"She did the right thing and I am not going to say that she did the right thing, because the man from that tap has a body of households behind him and he went away this morning with a length in his hand and he is going to have to put something on paper in about a month."**

She put her stick against the wall it lives on.

**"What I have been doing above that road since the ninth is nine places doing nine things, and I have not put a name on one of them and I am not going to put a name on the tenth, and the reason I have not is that two of them are not doing the same as each other and if a man with a tape can see that then a man with a schedule can too."**

She turned the stick over in her hands once and stopped.

**"If they are all the same then this holding has wasted a month refusing to write them down, and I have thought about that every day since the ninth and I have not got an answer to it and I am not going to make one up this morning for a man from a tap."**

Nobody in that yard asked her which two and nobody asked her what the nine things were and nobody said she had it right.

The sheet in this building has three hundred and thirty-five days against the four bodies of households, and the clause under it takes three hundred and thirty-fourth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-sixth.

The near board of the long wall carries seven hundred and twenty-one days and the far board carries seven hundred and sixty-eight, and the clerk of this holding said the two of them out loud to a man from a tap four miles off who was on his way to being told the length of a low field and not its area, and the man from the tap wrote both numbers on the back of his own hand.

The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the boot and the length and the refusal through the gap in it, and came out at about the twelfth hour and stood at the tap where the woman of about thirty-eight was rinsing a can.

**"A man asked a woman for a number of ground and she gave him a length and refused him a figure, and she was right to refuse him, and I want to be careful here because I have been the man who joins those two things together in a sentence and I have never once been right when I have done it."**

He set the can down for her and did not touch her arm.

**"I have spent four months wanting a column for what came back. A length of a low field paced with a boot is not a column and I am not going to build one out of it this morning, and if a body of households four counties off sends me a figure I am going to write back that I have one figure of my own and it is a boot."**

He went back in and left the door open behind him, which is the ordinary state of that door.

The trough at the head of the four-mile road had a hand of water in it and was not full, and Soren Rill was up that road at the sixth hour and at the second bend at the ninth and came down past the trough and did not stop at it.

The letter on the long table is thirty-four days old and there is still nothing under it in any of the four books in this holding, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

It has not been answered and it has not been refused and it has not been copied and it has not been entered, and the clerk of this holding says out loud at about the seventh hour, to nobody, that nobody in this building is going to be the one who changes that this week, and no one in the yard took him up on it.

Two hundred and thirty-one days on the middle table and not one mark in it, and the heading over the column is nothing at all, and the charter behind it is a hundred and thirty-seven days old and went through a door nine miles off.

A man from a tap four miles off stood in this yard this morning and was given a length and not a figure and did not ask about the form, and that is the only person in four days who has not asked about it either.

Paid goes in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and nobody in this yard knows which day it will move and one of them has worked it out and has not said.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and the barrow's journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor. Nine hundred yards off the seventh column of a door stands at sixty-six and does not go back, and the man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched today and the count of questions put to him in this holding is nil at every one of its sites.

Nothing was entered as an eighth session this morning, and a man asking a woman for a number at a gate is not one. The book still shows seven, the requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three, and the letter is in neither.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and a length paced with a boot is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

Boot on a wall and then off it again, and about eleven yards of somebody's ground that nobody is going to write down, and a woman who would not turn a pace into a figure for a man from a tap four miles off, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The first. The first line of the rota worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, the standpipe full and inside its lip. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and thirty-nine mornings of two hundred and sixty-one, of a month nine months back, that frame having stood where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and one hundred and thirteen hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and thirty-seventh. A MAN CAME TO THE GATE FROM A TAP FOUR MILES OFF AND WAS GIVEN A LENGTH PACED WITH A BOOT AND WAS NOT GIVEN A FIGURE, AND NO FIGURE OF THAT BEARING HAS BEEN ENTERED IN ANY OF THE FOUR BOOKS IN THIS HOLDING AND NO COLUMN HAS BEEN RULED FOR IT IN ANY OF THEM.** This is a fact of a gate, a yard and a book on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

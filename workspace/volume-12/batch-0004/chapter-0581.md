# Chapter 581

## Four People On A Bank In The Dark, And One Of Them Who Said No On Wednesday

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went up the headland in the dark for the tenth time this month, four people out of the first gate with lamps on two of them, and the wind had gone round to the south by midnight and there was no rain in it at all.

Two hundred yards of boundary stone were found by hand along the top of the bank, the ditch was cut along the eleven acres, and the loose stone was barrowed off the top into the heap by the gate, and the heap is a foot higher on it than it was a fortnight ago and it is on nobody's list.

No field was taken, and this line has gone up that bank in the dark and come back down it with nothing at all in the taking column on any morning this year, and the standing is a hundred and twenty-five mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and ninety-six not, and twenty-nine and ninety-six is a hundred and twenty-five.

The two reckonings of the rotation disagree this morning, as they have every morning anybody in this building has kept both of them. Seventy-third one way and sixtieth the other, with thirteen in the gap between them for four years, and nobody here has worked out what it counts or asked anybody who might.

**"The count in force is one higher than it was on Sunday, and the line came round, and four people went up a bank in the dark, and there is still nothing in the taking column, and that is the whole of the morning's arithmetic and none of it is anybody's fault."**

The clerk said that to the board and put his finger on the twenty-nine and left it there for a second and then took it off.

The second book took the figure in before the light and gave it back out at the ninth hour, and Tuesday had gone up eight on Monday's before the book was opened, and the three hundred and forty-fourth figure of this run is against the launder this morning.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirty hundredweight.**

The two halves at the long wall were a hundred and sixty-one mornings one way and a hundred and fifty the other, and the two of them came to three hundred and eleven, and the man who said the falling half said it first and the other one wrote it on the back of his own hand with a pencil.

There is no figure against the run of mornings this morning in the second book, and the reason is the one it is every fourth day, being that half of that run is half a number on a Tuesday, and the clerk left the space empty with the heading of a month nine months back standing over it, and this book is not a place for half of a thing.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling went up the bank at the second hour, and he was one of the four, and he had said no on Wednesday in front of the whole yard and had not changed his mind about it, and nobody in this holding asked him a second question about it at any hour of the night or the morning.

He came down at the fifth hour and put his bar against the wall it lives on and said one thing to the yard about why, and gave the reason first.

**"I said no on Wednesday and I have not taken it back and I did not go up there tonight because I was asked, because I was not asked, and because there was a lamp on a bank and three men going up it in the dark."**

He turned the bar over once in his hands and then set it down along the wall beside the sheet.

**"I have worked out that what I actually said on Wednesday was not no. What I said was that I was not going to be the fifth name on a thing nobody had asked me about. Tonight I was the fourth, and I was not named, and I have got a bar in my hand, and the two of those are not the same and I have not got a better sentence for it than that one."**

Nobody in the yard said he had changed his mind and nobody said he had not, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden wrote nothing down and said one thing to the tap.

**"I have had a space under one figure in my own book for a month on purpose and this morning a man has told this yard that the reason he went up a bank was not the number of times he had been asked, and I am not going to draw anything out of the two of them together in a yard with six people in it."**

She took her hand off the handle and then put it back on it.

**"I would like it noticed that I noticed it."**

Harlan Vetch came off the direct road at about the sixth hour having walked the nine miles and went to the north wall and worked it to the top course without stopping, and the top course has been the worst of it since the winter and he came down off it at about two and looked at it for a long time and said one sentence about it to nobody.

**"There is a section of about eleven feet of this wall that is not going to be here in ten years and I have known that for four years and I have never said it out loud and I have said it this morning because there is nobody in this yard to tell it to who is going to put it in a column."**

Three hundred and forty-two days is the number the sheet in this yard holds for the four bodies of households, and underneath it a person can read one morning less and one morning more, three hundred and forty-first out against three hundred and forty-third, and that part of the sheet is read every morning of the year by somebody.

Four people came down off the headland at the fifth hour into a yard where the near board of the long wall read seven hundred and twenty-eight days and the far board read seven hundred and seventy-five days, forty-seven between them, and not one of the four looked at either board on the way in, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning and at every hour of the night before it.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the four people going up the bank and coming down it through the gap, and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the wall where the bar lives.

**"A man said no on Wednesday in front of a yard and went up the bank in the dark on Tuesday without being asked, and the reason he gives now is not the reason he gave then, and he has noticed that himself and has not improved on it."**

He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure of his own in it.

**"I have spent four months wanting to write a page that explains who is looking after what came back. I have got four people on a bank and a rule that has been empty thirty-nine times and a wall with eleven feet of it going, and every one of those is a fact, and I have not got a way of putting three facts next to each other that does not ruin them."**

He went back in and left the door open, and the standpipe was full and inside its own lip by seven and the middle road was hard at the top and soft at the bottom and the water in the ditch along it was running full.

A letter has been lying on that table for forty-one days with the paper that came on the twenty-eighth beside it and about nine inches of bare board between the two. Neither one has been answered, refused or copied, and no column has been ruled for either of them.

The form has been on the middle table for two hundred and thirty-eight days and its column has nothing over it and nothing in it, and the charter behind it went through a door nine miles off a hundred and forty-four days ago, and the second of the four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty-two days old with nothing on it.

A man who went up a bank in the dark this morning and came down at the fifth hour did not go near any of the three.

Paid goes in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it moves on an interval and not on anybody's instruction, and this week it is going to move.

What this holding does not know about has six rows cut for it and not one of them has ever taken a unit, and a distance measured off a boot is not a seventh of anything, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Nobody entered an eighth session in this holding this morning. Fifty-three stands in the requests column and fifty-three in the section-nine notes, neither of them took anything, and the letter is in neither of the two.

Fifteen lines are in the use log and a sixteenth is not. The barrow's journeys stand at eleven against the wall it was put down beside, and eleven is a floor and it is not going to be twelve in this month.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings and this morning was not one of them, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites. Nine hundred yards off, a door has seven columns cut in the stone of it and the seventh of them is at sixty-six, and from sixty-six it does not go back, and nobody went up that road today.

Lamp carried up a bank in the dark by somebody who has carried one before, and a bar put against a wall at the fifth hour by a man who had said no three days earlier, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 570

## Three Hundred On A Wall, And A Woman Four Miles Off Looking At A Hinge

The wind came off the water before there was any light in it, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe was running full by the fourth hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding had the launder figure in the second book before the light came and read it back once at the ninth hour and stopped there.

**One thousand and one hundred and seven hundredweight.**

Five came off it on Friday, and the three hundred and thirty-third figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice stands against the launder this morning.

At seven the two men at the long wall read the halves out of one another, and the rising one said a hundred and fifty-five mornings and the other said the falling one back to him at a hundred and forty-five mornings, and neither of them added anything, and the two of them came to three hundred.

It is the first time that run of mornings has stood at three hundred. It has been a number under two hundred and something since the day it was begun, and it has been written on this wall every morning of that time, and this morning two men said it out loud in a yard and then went on with what they were doing.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was the only other person who had heard either of them, because she was filling a can at the tap with the tap running, and she said one thing about it at about the eighth hour and gave the reason first.

**"That run has been under three hundred since before I came to this tap and it has just gone over, and I am not going to say anything to anybody in four counties about a figure on a wall in a yard."**

She set the can down on the step.

**"The moment I say a number like that out loud to a person from a body it becomes a figure about a district. It is a number of water that went past a gate and the gate is forty yards from where I am standing and I can see it."**

Nobody asked her what she thought it meant and nobody said she was right.

The sheet in this building that carries the four bodies of households has three hundred and thirty-one days against them this morning, and the clause printed under that figure takes the three hundred and thirtieth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-second. One under and one over at every site, and it is a different figure from the launder and no page in this holding has ever added the two of them to anything.

At the long wall the near board reads seven hundred and seventeen days and the far board reads seven hundred and sixty-four, and the two are forty-seven days apart, and the clerk of this holding put his thumb on the near one to show a man the gap and then took his thumb off it again and the drawer behind it stood shut at every hour of this morning.

At the seventh hour the second book took the run of mornings under the heading where it goes, of a month nine months back, and the pair was read out because a Friday is an odd morning and that run is read on odd mornings.

**One hundred and thirty-seven mornings of two hundred and fifty-seven.**

Tova Reed went out along the direct road at about the sixth hour with a satchel and did not come back into this yard before dark, and nobody in this holding has asked her once what she went for, because she said it out loud in this yard on the twenty-seventh of the month before and four people heard her and one of them wrote nothing down.

The gate on the low field was shut at about the seventh hour and it stayed shut.

The man of about fifty had the spanner the clerk of this holding brought out of the one room on Thursday, and he took the pin out entirely instead of driving it back up, and he drew the gate to on its catch and put the pin through with the hole a little to the side of where it had been, and then he went away and came back at the ninth hour and looked at it and it was still shut.

**"It is not on true,"** he said to the gate, to nobody in particular, and gave the reason before anything else. **"The hole is a quarter of an inch off and the gate will sit shut on a day like this and it will walk open on a wet one. I have not fixed anything. I have moved a hole."**

He put the spanner in his back pocket and left it there.

The woman of about thirty-eight asked him one question about it at the tap and he answered it and then stopped answering.

**"Are you going to write it in the book."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden waited with the can in her hand.

She shifted her weight off the other foot and the can came up level with the tap.

**"No."**

He did not look up from the gate.

The pin was still in his fist and stayed out of the hole it had come out of.

**"Why not."**

He set the spanner down on the catch of the gate.

**"Because the book is a book about the four-mile road and I have just worked on a gate at the bottom of a low field, and if that goes in then in four years there is a page saying a man attended the gate on a Friday in this month, and there is no column anywhere in four counties that says who is allowed to attend a gate."**

Nobody in that yard thanked him and nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong.

The letter on the long table is thirty days old, counted as complete days lying and not counting the day it came up the fen road and not counting the part of the morning it is lying in, and it is a month old today and nothing about it is different for being a month old.

It has not been answered and it has not been refused and it has not been copied, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books in this holding.

The clerk of this holding was asked at the seventh hour by a man who had come about a sack of lime whether the letter had been dealt with, and he gave the reason before the answer.

**"It came up the fen road at the ninth hour of the second of this month and I wrote the hour down, and I have written nothing else about it in eleven years of keeping this book."**

He put his thumb on the line of the hour and not on the letter.

**"A letter that asks a building nine miles off to put its sheet back the way it was has no column in these four books with a heading on it for it."**

He turned the second book round so the man could see the page and there was nothing on the page but the hour.

**"I am not going to rule one because a man asked me in a yard this morning whether it had been dealt with."**

The man said that was fair and went and did his own work and the letter did not move.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap, and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the long wall with his sleeve down over his forearm and read the two boards and did not go near the drawer behind the near one.

**"A gate is shut this morning and it is shut on a hole that is a quarter of an inch off, and that is the whole of the repair and it is a physical fact that anybody in this district can walk up to and put a hand on."**

He gave the reason first and left every figure out of it, so that nothing this morning came off his own count.

**"I have spent four months in rooms where the way a thing was done became the way the thing was, and this morning a man moved a hole a quarter of an inch and went away, and the gate is shut and it will walk open in the rain, and there is not going to be anything in any of the four books about either the hole or the rain."**

He turned the sleeve back and did not say anything else about it.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and nothing about the interval that carries that figure had anything to do with anybody in this yard today.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut in the stone of it and the seventh of those columns stands at sixty-six, and it is not read on any morning this week and from sixty-six it does not go back. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched this morning, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites.

About the eleventh hour the use log went back onto its shelf under its own weight. Fifteen lines are in it, the sixteenth is not among them, and the spanner did not go near it. The man who keeps that book used it twice and wrote neither of them down.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and no eighth was entered this morning. A woman walking four miles to look at a hinge is not a session and a gate being shut by one man is not a session.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them. A quarter of an inch off a hole is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and neither of them took anything this morning, and the letter went into neither of them and will go into neither of them.

There is a form on the middle table two hundred and twenty-seven days into its stay, with a column headed nothing and nothing written in it. The charter behind it went through a door nine miles off a hundred and thirty-three days ago, three of the four bodies of households in that room are still drawing on a machine for stored light, and the fourth has sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

Neither document has an opinion about the other one.

A stay is not an interval and an interval is not a filling, and this morning the one room put both of them on its table and added neither to the other.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and twenty-one days old with nothing on it, and he was on the middle road all morning with a bar and did not come into the one room.

The barrow went up the four-mile road loaded at the seventh hour and came down its own track, and where its journeys stand is eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Pin out of a gate and a quarter of an inch of daylight in a hole where a pin used to be, three hundred on a wall in a yard and a woman four miles off with a satchel on her back, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The first. The first line of the rota worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, the standpipe full and inside its lip. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and thirty-seven mornings of two hundred and fifty-seven, of a month nine months back, that frame having stood where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and one hundred and seven hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and thirty-third. THE TWO HALVES AT THE LONG WALL CAME TO THREE HUNDRED THIS MORNING, WHICH IS THE FIRST TIME THAT RUN OF MORNINGS HAS STOOD AT THREE HUNDRED, AND NO FIGURE OF THAT BEARING HAS BEEN ENTERED IN ANY BOOK IN THIS HOLDING, ONLY SPOKEN IN A YARD BY TWO MEN AT SEVEN IN THE MORNING. A GATE WAS SHUT AT THE SEVENTH HOUR WITH A HOLE A QUARTER OF AN INCH OFF TRUE AND NOTHING WAS WRITTEN ANYWHERE ABOUT IT.** This is a fact of a wall, a gate and a book in one place on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

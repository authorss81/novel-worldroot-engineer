# Chapter 582

## A Paid Line That Fell On An Interval, And Nine People In A County Finding Out At Once

Frost in the ruts by six and gone by nine and the sun on the yard by seven, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe ran full and stayed inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book before the light and read it back once at the ninth hour, and it went in on the first hour without anybody looking at it.

**One thousand and one hundred and twenty-five hundredweight.**

Wednesday took the five off what the launder carried on Tuesday, and the man at the outlet end wrote the three hundred and forty-fifth figure of this run on the slate and nothing has ever been put in that run twice.

At seven the rising half of the window was a hundred and sixty-one mornings and the falling half a hundred and fifty-one, and the two came to three hundred and twelve, and the man who said the falling half had come in late and did not say the total and the other one said it for him.

A Wednesday morning reads that run at the seventh hour, out of the second book and under the heading where it goes, of a month nine months back, because an odd morning carries it, and that heading has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and forty-three mornings of two hundred and sixty-nine.**

The compost line fell this morning and it fell to paid at twenty-eight, and it is paid and it is not discharged in twenty-eight, and it did not fall in the whole of the month before and it did not fall on any morning of the first two weeks of this month.

It fell because an interval arrived. There is nothing else behind it and there is nobody in this holding who could have moved it a day earlier or a day later, and about nine people in this county found that out at about the same hour this morning and not one of them said it first.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap at the sixth hour and found out from a man at a standpipe and said one thing about it to the tap before she came in, and gave the reason first.

**"Nine of us found out at once and I want it on the page that none of us was first, because in about a year somebody is going to write a report about the compost in this district and I do not want that report to have a hero in it."**

She set the can down on the step and left her hand on the handle of it.

**"It moved on a count of mornings and the count of mornings does not know that a woman at a tap is watching for it. That is not a piece of good administration. That is a thing that happens to people, and I have been in four counties of meetings where the difference between those two things got lost and I am not going to let it get lost in a book with one figure in it."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour and heard about it at the wall and said one thing and went out again, and what he said was two sentences long.

**"It is paid at twenty-eight and it is not discharged in twenty-eight, and I have watched that line for nine years and it has moved four times in my time and I have never once known it coming."**

He put his hand on the bar on the wall.

**"I am not going to be one of nine people in a yard being surprised at the same time about a heap of compost. I am going back to the road."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had it through the gap and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the heap of stone by the gate rather than at the boards.

**"A paid figure fell this morning on an interval, and nine people in this county found out at the same hour, and the reason the man who keeps the second book cannot tell anybody why it fell is that there is nobody in this holding who would know."**

He gave the reason first and printed no figure of his own in it.

**"I have spent four months in rooms where a number that moves is given a cause by whoever is standing nearest it, and the cause goes in the book and the number goes on the next line of the same form, and eight years later nobody can find out whether the cause was true."**

He turned the sleeve back over his forearm and stood there a while longer.

**"That line fell because a count of mornings ran out. There is no more to it than that and I am not going to make more of it in a yard than the woman at the tap has already made of it in a sentence."**

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the tenth hour on her way out to the boundary and stopped at the boards for about half a minute and then said one thing about the compost and one thing about the man who had gone back up the road, in that order.

**"Nine of us found out at once, and one of them went straight back to a road to say so out loud, and that is the only thing I have seen this month that is not somebody in this yard deciding something."**

She put her hand flat on the near board of the long wall.

**"I have got nine places above one road and four off another and about four people who have carried seed on a road for nine years and have no seal for it, and I am not going to turn that into a body this month, and I would like a compost figure to be the one thing in this holding that is not a body."**

She went out through the first gate and nobody asked her a question about the four people or the nine places.

Harlan Vetch came off the direct road at about the seventh hour having walked the nine miles in the frost and went to the north wall and worked the top course for most of the morning and came down and stood at the bench and said one sentence to nobody in it.

**"That is the top of the wall and it is not going to be here in ten years and I have known that for four years, and I have said it out loud twice this week in two yards and I am not going to say it a third time."**

He put his hand flat on the bench for a second and then took it off and went and worked the bottom course instead.

Against the four bodies of households the sheet in this building carries three hundred and forty-three days, and the clause that sits under that number reaches the three hundred and forty-second of those mornings and stops one short of three hundred and forty-fourth.

The near board reads seven hundred and twenty-nine days and the far board reads seven hundred and seventy-six days, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden put her hand flat on the near one at the tenth hour while she was saying a thing about nine people finding out at once, and she did not mention the forty-seven days at all, and the drawer behind it stood shut at every hour of this morning.

There has been a letter lying face up on that table since the ninth hour of the second of this month, and it is forty-two days old. The paper that came on the twenty-eighth is beside it with about nine inches of bare board between the two, and neither one has been answered or refused or copied, and no column has been ruled for either of them in any of the four books in this holding.

A hundred and forty-five days is how long ago a charter went through a door nine miles off, and the form in front of it is two hundred and thirty-nine days old with a column headed nothing and nothing in it, and the second of the four ruled lines in Silling's own book is two hundred and thirty-three days old.

A paid line fell behind the one room this morning on an interval and nine people in this county found out at once, and not one of those three documents moved by a figure.

The rule in the board in the one room that is cut the same length as the one above it stands empty and the count of blanks on that board is thirty-nine and it is thirty-nine because the count goes on the return and not on the day.

Not one of the six rows ruled here for what this holding does not know about has ever had a unit written against it, and a paid line falling on an interval is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

A figure that moves in a ledger is not a session, and a paid line that fell on an interval this morning is not one either. The book shows seven sessions and no eighth, fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes are where they were, and the letter is in neither.

There are fifteen lines in that book and the sixteenth has not been written. Eleven journeys stand to the barrow, which is against a wall, and eleven is a floor.

What the seventh column of that door counts besides readings has not been answered this month and is not going to be, and the column stands at sixty-six and does not go back. The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings. He was not fetched this morning, nobody in this holding has ever put a question to him about which number he is, and that count is nil at every one of its sites.

Shovel through a heap behind the one room and a paid figure two higher than it was, and nine people in a county finding out at once with none of them first, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 577

## A Woman With A Bucket, And A Fact That Was Not Entered

Rain again before light, hard and short, over by four, and the fourth line of the rota came round and went up the headland in the dark with lamps on two of them for the ninth time this month, four people finding two hundred yards of boundary stone by hand and cutting the ditch along the eleven acres, and the loose stone barrowed off the top of the bank into the heap by the gate.

The standpipe was full and inside its own lip by seven and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet, and the middle road was soft at the top by seven and had a skin of water on the bottom of it by nine.

The clerk of this holding had the rotation count in before the light and read it out once at the ninth hour, and the standing this morning is a hundred and twenty-four mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and ninety-five not, and twenty-nine and ninety-five is a hundred and twenty-four.

The two reckonings do not agree and they never have. This is the seventy-second morning of the cycle counted one way and the fifty-ninth counted the other, and there has been thirteen in that gap for four years, and nobody in this building has ever asked anybody what that thirteen is for.

**"No field was taken again and I am not going to say anything about the twenty-nine,"** the clerk said to the board at about the seventh hour, and gave the reason before it, which he does.

He put his finger on the twenty-nine and left it there for a second.

**"Nobody in this holding is to blame for a field that has been waiting four years, and the day somebody is to blame for it is the day one of us has been given the field."**

The launder figure went in at the first hour and came back out of the second book at the ninth, and Friday came up eight off Thursday, and the standing is the three hundred and fortieth figure of a run in which nothing has been entered twice.

**One thousand and one hundred and twenty-four hundredweight.**

The two halves at the long wall were a hundred and fifty-nine mornings one way and a hundred and forty-eight the other, and they came to three hundred and seven between them, and the man who said the first of them wrote it in the back of his own book and showed the page to nobody.

There is no figure standing against the run of mornings this morning and the reason is on this page and not in the book. Half of that run is half a number on a Friday, and the second book has a space under the heading of a month nine months back where it has had a space on every Friday since the run was begun.

A woman came to the tap at about the fifth hour with two buckets and asked the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden to put down in her own book that the standpipe at the top of the middle road ran dry for a day and a half in the second week of this month, and the woman of about thirty-eight said she would not do it and gave the reason first.

**"I am not going to write that down and I am going to tell you exactly why, and I have thought about it for two days and this is the first time anybody has asked me for it."**

She set her own book face down on the wall, which she does not do.

**"If it goes in my book then in about a year a man from a body four counties off can come to this gate and ask me what is in the second book of this holding, and I will have to say yes, there is a day and a half of dry standpipe in it, and then somebody will write a column headed running water and this yard will have made one for a thing that was over in a day and a half."**

She turned the book back the other way and did not open it.

**"And the worse half is that I have not got anything better to put there. If I leave it out then this holding has no record of a day and a half of no water at all, and that is what I am choosing, and I am choosing it out loud so that it is a decision and not a hole."**

The woman with the two buckets said that was the wrong way round and went away with both of them still empty, and nobody in that yard told the woman of about thirty-eight that she was right and nobody told her she was wrong.

The clerk of this holding was at the long wall when the woman went and he said one thing to the second book about it and then shut the book, and what he said was not about the water.

**"She has now refused three things in a month in this yard and every one of them was a figure somebody else wanted, and every time she has refused she has said what the hole is going to be instead."**

He squared the book up on the wall.

**"I keep the second book and I have had four years of people telling me what a gap in a record is, and I have never once had anybody tell me that a gap can be a decision."**

He put his hand flat on the cover of it and left it there for a moment.

**"I am not going to write that in it either, for the reason she gave, and I would like it on the page that the man who keeps the book and the woman who keeps her own one arrived at the same place from opposite directions this morning and neither of them said so to the other one."**

Nia Vale came in at about the seventh hour from the boundary and filled two cans herself and said one thing about the two buckets as she filled them.

**"I have heard her give that reason three times this month to three different people and I want it said once in this yard that the reason is not about the water. The reason is that a book with one figure and a deliberate space under it is a book, and a book with two figures in it is a ledger."**

She set the second can down by the step.

**"I am not going to be one of the three people she gives it to again. I have had my turn this month and I am going back out to the boundary."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the whole of it through the gap in it, and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the tap and did not say anything for a while.

**"A woman was asked to write down a day and a half of no water and said no and gave the reason first and then said out loud that she was choosing a hole, and that is the first time anybody in this yard has admitted that a gap in a book is something somebody put there on purpose."**

He gave the reason before the thing and left every figure out of it.

**"I have been in rooms for nine years where an empty space in a record was a failure and where a person who left one got told to come back and fill it in. I have watched four bodies of households get into rooms like that and I have watched what it does to a person in about a year."**

He turned the sleeve back over his forearm.

**"So I am not going to ask her to fill it, and I am not going to suggest to anybody in a county four counties off that this holding keeps a complete record of its water, because it does not and she has just told me so in front of two people."**

The aggregate of the four bodies of households is three hundred and thirty-eight days on the sheet in this yard, and the clause under it takes the three hundred and thirty-seventh of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-ninth, one under and one over at every site.

The near board says seven hundred and twenty-four days and the far board says seven hundred and seventy-one days, forty-seven between them, and the clerk of this holding wrote both numbers on the flat of his own hand at about the ninth hour and read the two of them back to each other and did not add them, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The letter on the long table is thirty-seven days old and is face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and no column has been ruled for it, and it has not been answered, has not been refused, has not been copied and has not been entered anywhere in this holding.

The form on the middle table is two hundred and thirty-four days into its stay and there is a column at the head of it and the column has no heading over it and there is nothing in the column.

The charter behind it is a hundred and forty days old and went through a door nine miles off. Nobody in this holding has filled either document and nobody in this holding has refused either document and both of those facts are printed in every one of the four books, which is the only place either of them is recorded.

Paid goes in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and the next time that figure moves it will move because an interval arrived and not because anybody in this holding has anything to do with it.

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and a day and a half of dry standpipe is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning, and the requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three and neither has taken anything and the letter is in neither of them.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and there is no sixteenth. The barrow's journeys stand at eleven and the barrow is against a wall where it has been since Saturday.

The seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off is at sixty-six and is not read this week and does not go back. The man of about seventy is at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched today and the count of questions put to him in this holding is nil at every one of its sites.

Bucket carried away empty out of a yard that had water in it, and a book with one figure in it and a space under it on purpose, and a man who is not going to ask for the space to be filled, and nobody thanked anybody.

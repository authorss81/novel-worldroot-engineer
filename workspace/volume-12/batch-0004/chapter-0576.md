# Chapter 576

## A Man Who Has Kept A Dial For Nineteen Years, And What He Is Not Allowed To Say About It

Wind off the fen in the night and a wet morning that turned dry about the ninth hour, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour, which is the gate, and the gate on the low field was shut at seven and was standing open about a hand's width at ten with nothing having touched it.

The figure went into the second book at the first hour and the clerk read it back once at the ninth hour and did not read it back a second time.

**One thousand and one hundred and sixteen hundredweight.**

Thursday came off Wednesday by five and the three hundred and thirty-ninth figure of a run with nothing ever entered in it twice is what stands against the launder.

At the long wall the two halves were a hundred and fifty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and forty-eight the other, and the two of them came to three hundred and six, and the man who said the falling half said the total first this morning, before the other one had finished.

The run of mornings was read at the seventh hour out of the second book under its heading, of a month nine months back.

**One hundred and forty mornings of two hundred and sixty-three.**

The man who keeps the gauges came out from the building nine miles off at about the sixth hour with a leather case and worked on the dial at the standpipe for most of the morning, and he has kept that dial for nineteen years and there is nobody in this holding who has ever asked him how long he has kept it.

He unpacked the case on the wall by the pump house and laid out what was in it in a row, the way a man does it who has laid the same row out a great many times, and the second thing in the row was a washer he did not use and put back, and the man of about fifty watched that from the bench and said nothing about it at the time.

The needle on the dial was standing about a fifth of a division under where it stood at the same hour on Thursday, and the man with the case looked at it for a while and did not write anything and did not say anything to anybody about what a fifth of a division is.

**"It wants a stopcock and it wants a stopcock that somebody has to be paid to come and fit, and I have asked for that stopcock twice and it is not a thing I am allowed to ask for a third time."**

He said it to the pump house.

**"That is the whole of what is wrong with that dial and I am not going to say it again in a yard and I would like it noticed that I have said it in a yard."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap with a can in each hand and she asked him how long he had kept the dial, and he gave her a straight answer and then would not give her any more of it.

**"Nineteen years in the spring and a third of the winter off when there is nothing to keep."**

He went back to the dial and did not look up.

**"That is all of what I have got and I am not going to say the next part, and I would like it noticed that I did not say the next part, because in about four years somebody in this county is going to want a sentence about that dial and the next part is the one they will want."**

She said that she had noticed and she did not ask him for the next part, and she wrote nothing down, and the can she was holding went down onto the step while she was not looking and she did not say sorry for it to anybody.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had the man with the case through the gap in it and came out at about the eleventh hour and stood about ten feet off the standpipe and did not go any nearer.

**"There is a man in this yard who has kept one dial for nineteen years and who has just refused, out loud, in front of a witness, to say the sentence that everybody in four counties is going to want about it, and he did it before anybody had asked him for it."**

He gave the reason first and printed no figure at all in it.

**"I have spent four months trying to get people to write down what came back and I have been doing it by asking, and I have learned this month that asking a man what he is not allowed to say is a way of making him say it into somebody's book."**

He put his hands behind his back.

**"So I am not going to ask that man anything this afternoon, and I am not going to ask him on Friday either, and I am not going to say in a room in four counties that a man has kept a dial for nineteen years as though the number of years were a qualification."**

He went back in and the door stayed open behind him.

Harlan Vetch was at the north wall from the fourth hour and worked it until the light went, and he came past the standpipe at about the sixth hour of the afternoon and stopped for a moment and looked at the needle and did not say anything to the man with the case about it, and the man with the case did not say anything to him either.

There are three hundred and thirty-seven days against the four bodies of households on the sheet in this building. Under that figure the clause goes one under and one over, three hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-eighth.

Seven hundred and twenty-three is on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and seventy is on the far board, and a man in a leather case who has kept a dial for nineteen years walked past both of them at about the sixth hour and did not look at either, and nobody in that yard asked him to. The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The gate on the low field was standing open at ten and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing about it to the tap and gave the reason first, and the man of about fifty was in the yard and did not go to it.

**"It has been shut four days and it has walked open in a wet week on a hole a quarter of an inch off, exactly the way the man who put it there said it would on Friday, and I am not going to be surprised at it and I am not going to stand at that gate every morning of this month watching it."**

She filled the can.

**"The reason is that a man told me on Friday what it was going to do and then nobody wrote it down, and so the only copy of that is in his head and mine, and that is two copies in a county where there should be one in a book, and I am not going to be the third thing that has to be remembered."**

The trough at the head of the four-mile road was not full and had a hand of water in it and Soren Rill was not up that road today, and the four-mile road was soft at the bottom by ten and hard at the top and would be nothing but a scar by the fourth hour of the afternoon.

Thirty-six days old is the letter on the long table, lying face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been answered, has not been refused, has not been copied and has not been entered, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books in this holding.

On the middle table: a form, two hundred and thirty-three days old, its column headed nothing, nothing in it, neither filled nor refused.

Behind it, a charter a hundred and thirty-nine days through a door nine miles off, with three of the four bodies of households that signed it still signed to a machine for stored light. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden looked at all three of those documents this week and ruled nothing under any of them, and that is her business and not the clerk's.

Paid goes in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it is going to fall inside this month on an interval that nobody in this holding set and nobody in this holding can move.

The requests column and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three and neither has taken anything this month, and the letter went into neither of them. Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has been written against any of them in eleven years, and a dial a man has kept for nineteen years is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth this morning, and a man refusing to say a sentence out loud in a yard is not a session.

The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them, and the man with the case did not ask to see it and nobody offered it to him.

The barrow's journeys stand at eleven against a wall where a man put it on Saturday, and eleven is a floor, and the twelfth is not coming in this month.

The dial, and a door nine hundred yards off with seven columns cut in the stone of it, and a man of about seventy: the seventh of those columns is at sixty-six and goes no further this week, and he stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched today, and nobody in this holding has ever asked him which number he is at any of its sites.

Dial with a needle on it and a hole where the water goes in, and a man who said no out loud before anybody asked him, and a gate walking open in the wet exactly as it was told it would, and nobody thanked anybody.

> A DIAL AT A STANDPIPE NINE MILES OFF ANY BUILDING, ENTERED BY THE CLERK OF THIS HOLDING AT THE NINTH HOUR IN HIS OWN HAND AS ONE UNBROKEN LINE, AND NOTHING ELSE ENTERED: **THE MAN WHO KEEPS IT HAS KEPT IT NINETEEN YEARS IN THE SPRING AND ABOUT FOUR MONTHS OFF IN THE WINTER, AND HE WAS ASKED BY A WOMAN IN THIS YARD HOW LONG HE HAD KEPT IT AND HE GAVE HER THAT AND REFUSED HER THE REST OF IT BEFORE SHE HAD ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT. HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT IN ABOUT FOUR YEARS SOMEBODY IN THIS COUNTY IS GOING TO WANT A SENTENCE ABOUT THAT DIAL AND THAT THE SENTENCE THEY ARE GOING TO WANT IS THE ONE HE DID NOT SAY. NO FIGURE OF THE YEARS HE HAS KEPT IT IS WRITTEN IN ANY OF THE FOUR BOOKS IN THIS HOLDING AND NO COLUMN HAS BEEN RULED FOR IT.** This is a fact of a tap, a yard and a book on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

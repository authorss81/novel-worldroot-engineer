# Chapter 541

## The Fourth Line At The Headland, And A Man Nobody Sent For

It was dark at the second hour and the wind was off the water and the boundary stones at the headland had to be found by hand, one after another, along about two hundred yards of the top of the bank. The fourth line of the rota came round and was worked, and four people went up at the second hour and came down at about the fifth, and the ditch along the eleven acres was cut out and the loose stone was barrowed off the top and put in a heap by the gate, and the pack under the near stone at the head of it was lifted and squeezed and went back wet.

No field was taken. That is the whole of what that means and it is the fourth time this year the line has come round and gone up the bank and come back down with nothing in the taking column, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that has waited four years.

Twenty-nine taken and eighty-six not comes to a hundred and fifteen mornings in force, and that is the standing. Twenty-nine taken did not move, and it did not move yesterday when the line was not worked at all, and it has not moved on any morning of this year, and the difference between yesterday and this morning in that column is a boundary and not a field.

Count it this way and the fourth of this month is the sixty-third morning of the rotation; count it the other way and it is the fiftieth. Both of them go on this page, because a page carrying one of them carries half a fact. There has been thirteen in that gap for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what the thirteen counts.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open in his hand before the light and put the line in standing up, and read it back once and left it.

Nobody in this yard had said anything to anybody about the holding at that hour and the book was open for about five minutes.

**One thousand and seventy hundredweight.**

Thursday answered Tuesday's rise with eight of its own, and the three hundred and fourth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning. What the two men at the long wall took off it at seven was a hundred and forty-one mornings one way against a hundred and thirty the other, and one of them said the other's half back to him, and the pair of them is two hundred and seventy-one.

Against the four bodies of households the sheet gives three hundred and two days, and the clause underneath it takes the three hundred and first of those mornings out of three hundred and third. One under and one over. Forty-seven apart is what the two boards of the long wall are, at six hundred and eighty-eight days on the near one and seven hundred and thirty-five on the far one, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Under that heading the second book has a space this morning with nothing in it, and the clerk of this holding left it empty, and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of the run is a half number on a Thursday, which is why the space is empty, and the frame of it is of a month eight months back and has not moved since the first of this month.

A man came up the fen road with the second bus and got off at the first gate instead of going on, and he had a bar over his shoulder and a canvas roll on his hip and he was not expected. He is a contractor on the rootwright roll for this district and his name is Coll Renner and he has been on the roll eleven years and there are nine men on it in this district and four of them will do a holding for a season.

**"I was in the district on a pump house at Weir Row and I heard at a gate on Tuesday that there is ground above this road doing something."** He said that to the pump house and gave the reason before the thing. **"Nobody sent for me and I have not come to be put in charge of a field. I have come because I have a bar and a straight edge and three hours of a morning, and if that is not wanted then I will take it back down the road and nobody will have lost anything but my morning."**

Nobody said that it was not wanted.

He went up the low field at about the sixth hour with the bar and the straight edge and came back at about the eighth, and he had the roll open on the middle table and the straight edge in his hand, and he was not in a hurry and he was not going to be one.

**"Here is what I am going to say and then I am going to go, and I am not going to write any of it down."** He said that to the middle table.

**"There is a line of about nine inches where the ground has come up about a hand's width in three days, and it runs about two hundred yards on the same bearing as the culvert, and the culvert was laid on the old channel in the year the gate was put in. That is not a thing I have a name for. It is a thing I can put a straight edge across and tell you the fall of it in, and the fall is one in forty going the way the water goes."**

**"A piece of ground that has come up a hand's width in three days on the line of a drain that is thirty years old is not a mystery and it is not a gift either. It is load. Something is pushing and I do not know what and I am not going to guess in front of you, because a man who guesses at a table in a yard is a man who has said it out loud and cannot unsay it, and I have watched that happen twice."**

He put the straight edge down flat on the table and left his hand on it. **"I am not writing it in a book. If it is in a book then in four years somebody is going to ask me whether I would put my name to it, and I would have to, and I have not got four years of being wrong in this county."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden asked him one question and it was about the hour, and he gave the hour and the bearing and nothing else, and she wrote the hour and the bearing in her own book and shut it and did not write the word he had not said.

**"You are the first person in four years who has come up that road and put a thing on a table about it."** She said that to the middle table, not to him. **"I am going to write down that you came and what you put down and I am not going to write down what it means, and if in four years somebody wants to know whether this yard heard it, they can come and ask me and I will read them this page."**

**"That is fair and it is more than the last four of us did."** He said it to her and gave the reason first. **"The last four of us are all still standing in this yard waiting for somebody to hand it to, and I would rather be a fifth man with a bar than a fifth voice in the argument, and I have got four years of being in this district and I have watched what a voice in an argument does to a man's license."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour, on her way out, and stood at the long table and read nothing and listened to about two minutes of it and then went on to the boundary without saying a word to anybody.

The man of about fifty came up the middle road with the barrow at about the sixth hour, as he does, and got as far as the low field and stopped in the same place, and stood there with both hands on the handles for a while, and this time he did not stand in the road afterwards. He stood at the top of the field, on the line, about forty yards short of the strip, and looked down it.

**"A contractor came up here with a straight edge and he says it is load."** He said that to the field and not to anybody, and there was nobody within about a hundred yards of him to hear it.

**"I have got a barrow and I have been stopping in the same place every day for a week because I did not know what I was stopping for, and I am going to put the wheel on the line on Saturday and see what it says, and I will tell this yard what it says and I will not tell anybody else."**

Tova Reed was in the one room from the third hour with the four counties' sheets and the fourth one among them for the first time since the twenty-eighth, and she did not open it.

**"I have got four counties to be in inside six weeks and three dates in my hand and a straight edge on a table that is not a sowing date."** She said that to the shut sheet and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to carry a bearing about a field into a room with nineteen households in it. A person in one of those rooms will hear the word load and they will hear it as a debt, and I have three dates left and I am not spending one of them on a word a contractor used about a drain."** Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Soren Rill was on the four-mile road from the sixth hour and came in wet to the knee and put his bar against the wall beside Renner's roll.

**"I have been at the second bend and there is a new line in it that was not there on Monday, and it is about a foot below the old one and it runs to the same place."** He said that to the wall.

**"I have got two places now that I can put a bar in and I am not going to tell this yard which way they are joined, because the day somebody joins them in a sentence is the day somebody puts a figure on it, and I have watched a figure turn a ditch into a fact about a person twice."**

Marek was at the one room door from the fifth hour and did not go out of the gate, and the direct road between the yard and the machine house is nine miles and he has not been asked to go up it this month by anybody.

**"I have watched a man go at that field with a straight edge and come back with a bearing and refuse to write it down, and that is the first sensible thing anybody has done about it since the ninth."** He said that to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"He is not going to be asked to write it down and I am not going to ask him. The moment it is in a book it stops being a thing a man looked at and becomes a thing a county has said, and then everybody who wants to be right about the ninth of this month has to argue with a page instead of with a man, and a page never loses."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm. **"That is why I have not put anything in this holding's books about it either, and it is not modesty. I have watched both of those things happen and I did not stop either of them in time."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour, went as far as the low field gate on foot, stood at it about ten minutes without going through, and came back in.

**"I have written this morning's date and the hour a contractor was in this yard and the fact that he refused to write it down."** She said that to the gatepost. **"I have not written the bearing. He gave it to two people in this yard in the open air and I was not one of them and I am not going to have it second-hand in a county book, because a figure that gets into a book from a doorway is a figure that has lost its witnesses."**

A hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the register form has lain on the middle table with its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused. A hundred and four days back the charter went into a room nine miles off. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-two days old and has nothing on it.

That line is paid at twenty-six and not discharged in twenty-six, and paid is the word in front of the figure. Seven is what this holding's book carries under the heading for sessions, and nobody entered an eighth this morning, and a bearing about a drain is not a session. The requests column is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and neither of them has moved, and the letter has lain on the long table for two days and has gone into neither.

This holding rules six rows for the things it does not know and has written no unit against any of them, and there is no seventh row, and a line that has come up a hand's width in three days is not a seventh.

Fifteen lines stand in the use log and the morning did not add one to them. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and where its journeys stand is eleven, and eleven is a floor.

In the stone of the door of the well house nine hundred yards off, the seventh of the seven cut columns stands at sixty-four and nobody walked the nine hundred yards to read it this week. The rule for that column is the first of a month and the thirtieth and no other morning, and from sixty-four it does not go back.

Nobody has been inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres this week, and it has not been measured and it has not been walked, and the line that has come up on the low field above the middle road is not that ring. The two have not been added to one another on any page in this building.

Under two words in the corner of the one room there are four ruled lines and they are all bare, and the answer they are for is not due. Ruled and empty is the third column of the sheet of terms and its heading has not been improved by anybody. The paper on the low board is face up, undated, and has no line on it that a name could go into.

Coll Renner put his roll over his shoulder and went out through the first gate at about the ninth hour and said nothing to anybody at the gate on his way past. The wind had gone round to the west by then and the water in the ditch was running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full and inside its lip, and the middle road was hard at the top by seven and soft at the bottom by nine, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The fourth. The fourth line of the rota worked, the boundary stones at the headland and the ditch along the eleven acres, four people from the second hour until the fifth, and no field taken, the standing a hundred and fifteen in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-six not, and the two reckonings at sixty-three and fifty. The pair not read and no figure standing against it, the half of the run being half a number on this morning. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and seventy hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and fourth. A licensed contractor of this district was in this yard at the sixth hour and put a straight edge on the middle table and refused to have it written down, and no figure of that bearing has been entered in any of the four books in this holding.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

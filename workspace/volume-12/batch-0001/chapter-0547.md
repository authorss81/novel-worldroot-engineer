# Chapter 547

## One Thousand And Seventy-Nine Hundredweight, And A Barrow That Went Where It Had Never Been

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the water was down about a foot and a half on the lower stone since the rain of two mornings ago and clear of the top of the upper one for the first time this week. The pack under the near stone came out damp and went back damp, and the frost was out of the ground by five and the ditch was running slow and clear.

The second book was open before the light properly came up, the line went in, and the clerk of this holding read it back once and shut it.

**One thousand and seventy-nine hundredweight.**

Wednesday put eight on and the launder's standing is the three hundred and tenth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. There are three figures on this morning and it is a Wednesday like any other Wednesday, and a morning is not a busy morning because it has got three figures on it, and this page is going to say that once and is not going to come back to it. The two men had a hundred and forty-four mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-three the other off the wall by seven, which is two hundred and seventy-seven between them.

The sheet gives the four bodies of households three hundred and eight days, and the clause it carries underneath gives the three hundred and seventh of those mornings out of three hundred and ninth, which is one under and one over. The long wall says six hundred and ninety-four days on the near board and seven hundred and forty-one on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning.

There are three figures on this morning and it is a Wednesday like any other Wednesday, and a morning is not a busy morning because it has got three figures on it, and this page is going to say that once and is not going to come back to it.

There is nothing in the second book this morning under the heading where the run of mornings goes, and the clerk of this holding left it that way, and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of the run is a half number on a Wednesday. The line will be full on Friday and empty again on Saturday, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun.

Soren Rill came in at about the eighth hour and said the seventh on Saturday has rain in it and that the bend is dry enough to walk this morning, and the man of about fifty said he had said Saturday and Soren Rill said the man had said Saturday and had not said he was not allowed to go on a Wednesday, and the two of them went up the middle road at about the ninth hour with the barrow and a bar.

They were gone about two hours. Nobody in this yard went with them and nobody sent them.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not write the hour down, and the reason she gave for that afterwards was that she has never once written down what a person did in a field and she was not going to start on a Wednesday. Sera Quill did not write it either, and neither of them said so at the time.

The man of about fifty came back down the middle road at about the eleventh hour pushing the barrow back down its own track in front of him, and the front wheel had sunk about two inches into the strip about a yard wide and had come out of it again, and the track behind the barrow from the top of the field to the line was one continuous rut about five inches deep, and there was no water in any of it.

**"The ground carried it."** He said that at the tap and gave the reason before the thing. **"I have pushed that barrow up that field every day this month and stopped in the same place every day, and this morning I went forty yards past where I stop, and that is field that has not had a wheel on it since the low field was a low field. It carried it the whole way and it took about half as much pushing and I cannot tell you why and I have not got a book to write it in."**

Soren Rill came in behind him with the bar over his shoulder and put it against the wall.

**"I will tell you what I saw and I will not tell you what it means."** He said that to the wall. **"The new line in the second bend of the four-mile road goes under the road and comes up in the low field about a yard wide, and I have been putting a bar in it since Wednesday, and this morning the two of us stood on the low field line and I put the bar in the bend line and the two holes are four hundred yards apart and the bar came out of the first one wet and out of the second one wet."**

He turned round and looked at the bar and then at the wall again.

**"That is not a figure and it is not a joining-up and I am not going to say the word join out loud in this yard. I have got a bar and a wet bar and I have got a man who has been stopping in the same place for a month. What I have got is not an answer to anything and it is not the beginning of one, and if somebody in four counties writes it down as the beginning of one then they have put a word on it that neither of us said."**

Nia Vale was at the one room door at about the eleventh hour and heard the second half of that and did not come out and did not ask.

**"There are two men in this yard who have just done the thing I have not done for nine days, which is go and look and say what they saw."** She said that to the gatepost on her way out and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it.

**"I am not going to say what they found and I am not going to add it to anything. If the two of them come to me in a week and ask me whether it is one of my nine, I am going to tell them that I do not know and that I have not been up there, and I would like that on the page before anybody decides I have."**

Marek was in the one room with the door open and he came out into the yard at about the eleventh hour and went up the middle road on foot with nothing in his hands, and he was gone about an hour and he came back and went into the one room and shut the door, which he does not do.

**"There is a pattern in this county that came back on the ninth of this month and there are two men with a barrow who have just put a wheel on it, and neither of those two things is a harvest."** He said that to the shut door and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"A pattern that came back is not a harvest. A harvest is a thing that goes into a barn and gets counted and gets divided, and there are two bodies of households four miles off who have spent this week asking me whether a letter is going to change their ground. I have told one of them that I do not know, and I have got nothing this morning that lets me tell her anything else, and I am not going to go up there and look for something that lets me."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"I want it said that I went up there and I saw a rut five inches deep in a field and a bar that came out wet, and that I have not said one word about what either of those is for, and that I have spent a whole morning not saying a word, and that is the hardest thing I have done this month and I have not mentioned it to anybody and I am mentioning it to a shut door."**

Tova Reed was in the seed house with the four counties' sheets and came out at about the ninth hour and stood in the yard while the two of them came back down the road, and she did not go up and she did not ask either of them anything.

**"Two men went up with a barrow and came back and the whole of what this yard knows about it is a rut and a wet bar, and I have three dates in my hand and one page in a pocket that a household gave itself."** She said that to the low board and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to ask them what it means, I am not going to carry it to four counties, and I am not going to stop them going on Saturday. I want the last of those three said out loud, because I know which of them I would have been tempted to do a month ago."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and stood in the yard for about a quarter of an hour listening to nothing, and then went and worked the north wall, and did not say anything to anybody about what he had heard, and he has not said anything to anybody about it since.

The old man with a stick was on the north wall from the third hour until six with the wind behind him, and he said one number out loud to nobody on his way in, and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it, and this morning there were four people in the yard when he said it and about four were not, and the number went into the air of a working yard and nowhere else and it is not in any of the four books in this holding.

The clerk of this holding asked the two of them, standing at the tap, whether he ought to enter it, and the man of about fifty said no, and Soren Rill said no, and the clerk of this holding wrote nothing and did not rule a line and said out loud that there was no line to rule, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said from the bench by the pump house that there had never been one, and nobody thanked her for saying it.

A hundred and ninety-eight days is the age of the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and it has nothing on it. The charter was signed a hundred and ten days ago in a room nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light, which is a true sentence about a charter and not a sentence about a rut in a field. Two hundred and four days is how long the register form has been on the middle table, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused.

The wind got up off the water about the second hour of the afternoon and went round to the west, and the water in the ditch was running slow and clear and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full and inside its lip, and the track up the low field was hard enough by the ninth hour to take a barrow over it again without a rut.

Not discharged in twenty-six is the standing of that line, the figure is twenty-six, and the word in front of it is paid.

Seven is what this holding's book has entered under the heading for sessions, and a barrow going up a field is not a session and no eighth was entered this morning. The letter on the long table has lain there seven days and has gone into neither the requests column nor the section-nine notes, and both of those are fifty-three and have been fifty-three all week. Six ruled rows for what this holding does not know, no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a rut five inches deep is not a seventh and is not a reason to rule one.

The use log went up the four-mile road with fifteen lines in it and came back with fifteen. A barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and it went up the middle road about forty yards further than it has gone in four years, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

At nine hundred yards, on a door nobody went to this week, the seventh of seven cut columns stands at sixty-four and has stood there since the first of this month. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres was not walked this week and it was not measured and it was not crossed, and the rut that is in the low field above the middle road is four hundred yards and more from it and is not in it. The two are not added to one another here.

Under two words in the corner of the one room, four ruled lines stand bare, and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. On the low board there is a sheet with nothing written on it, no date, no name and no line for a name, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved.

Ground that took a wheel in the middle of the morning and let it out again is not a fact about anybody in this yard, and the two men who found it are not officers of anything and have said so, and the clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 543

## A Page Out Of A Household's Own Book, And No Column For A Return

Rain came in off the water in the night and stopped about the fourth hour, and by the time the second line of the rota came round there was a gutter running down the middle road on the off side of the metalling. The second line is the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it. The water was up to the lip of the lower stone and running clean over the top of the upper one, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet, and that is the second morning this week it has gone back wet instead of dry.

There was nowhere to sit in the one room that morning, so the second book was carried to the door frame and the line was written standing up, and read back once.

**One thousand and seventy-three hundredweight.**

Saturday climbed eight off Friday, so what the launder carries is the three hundred and sixth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. Off the long wall at seven came a hundred and forty-two mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-one the other, the two men taking a turn at saying each, and between them that is two hundred and seventy-three.

What the sheet gives the four bodies of households this morning is three hundred and four days, and the clause beneath that takes the three hundred and third of those mornings out of three hundred and fifth. One under and one over. The long wall carries six hundred and ninety days on its near board and seven hundred and thirty-seven on the far one. Forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour.

The line under that heading in the second book is empty this morning and the clerk left it empty, and this page carries the reason and carries no figure. Half of the run is a half number on a Saturday, and that line has stood empty on every second morning since the run began, and this is one of those mornings.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the sixth hour in a coat that had been rained on twice and put a folded page on the long table with her hand flat on top of it for a while before she took her hand off.

**"This is not a letter and I am not the one who wrote it."** She said that to the page. **"It came down the road in the pocket of a man who has walked from a place four miles off toward the tap, and it is a page torn out of a household book, and the man gave it to me at the gate because he said a woman who writes things down would know what to do with it and he was not going to stand in a yard holding it."**

She squared it against the edge of the table.

**"I am a witness to records and not a witness to what is on it, and I have not read it twice and I am not going to say what is in the order of the lines on it. There is a date at the head of it. That is the whole of what I will say before anybody in this yard opens it."**

Tova Reed was in the one room and came out and stood at the table and did not touch the page for about half a minute.

**"Three dates in my hand and a fourth thing on a table that is not a date from anybody."** She said that to the page and gave the reason before the thing. **"I am going to open it because a man walked four miles with it and not because it is addressed to me, and if I open it I am going to read it standing up and I am not going to sit down, because sitting down with a thing like this is how a person starts deciding what it means."**

She read it standing up. It took her perhaps a minute and a half.

**"It is not a request."** She put the page down flat. **"It is a return. It is the date at the top and then a list, and the list is what nineteen households have done on their own ground between the ninth of this month and this morning, and nobody asked them to do any of it, and there is nobody named at the head of the page except the man who walked it here."**

**"I am not going to read the list out in this yard."** She said that before anybody asked. **"Not because it is anybody's business. Because a list of eleven things that nineteen households did without being asked, read out loud in a yard, becomes a yard's list by the time it is finished, and then in four counties somebody is going to say the holding asked for it, and no household in that room has had a word from me about any of it."**

Nobody in that yard asked her to read the list.

**"There is a date on it and I have got three dates in my hand and this is the fourth, and it is not a date anybody gave me, it is a date a household gave itself. I am going to carry it to the meeting and put it on the table with the other three and I am not going to say a word about it, and I am not going to have it read out either."**

She put her hand flat on the page and left it there. **"If nineteen households want somebody to have an opinion about what they have done, they have to be in a room with somebody who has one, and I have not got one this week, and I would rather say that out loud in a yard than find it out in a room with nineteen doorways."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden sat down on the bench by the pump house and read the page over Tova Reed's arm without touching it, and then went and got her own book and wrote the date and the hour and the fact that a page had come, and nothing else.

**"I have got a column in this book with a date and an hour in it and no meaning against anything in it since the day I started it."** She said that to the bench. **"That is four years old and close to four hundred lines in it and I have never once written down what a thing meant, and I have been told by two people in that time that it is a strange thing to keep and by one person that it is a coward's book."**

She shut the book and put it under her arm.

**"A page comes down four miles with a list on it that nobody was asked for, and that is the first thing anybody has handed me in four years that is not a fact about a form, and I am going to enter the date of it and the hour of it and the number of miles it came, and I am going to leave the rest of it out, and the woman who wrote it can come and read my page any morning she likes and take the whole of it away with her if she wants it."**

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the seventh hour on her way out and she did not stop at the table, and she went up the middle road, and she was gone about two hours and came back with the road in her boots and her own list shut under her arm, and she said nothing to anybody about where she had been and nobody asked her.

**"I heard what was said in this yard and I am not going to add to it."** She said that to the gatepost on her way out and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it. **"There is a list on that table with nineteen names' worth of work on it, and I have got a list under my arm with nine places on it that I will not name. If those two get into one sentence in this county then one of them stops being true, and I am going to keep them apart for a long time."**

Soren Rill was at the second bend of the four-mile road from the sixth hour and came in about the tenth hour with the water over the top of his boots and did not come to the table at all.

**"There is a second bend down there that was dry on Wednesday and there is a foot of water in it now, and the water is coming off the field above the road, not off the road."** He said that to his own boots on the middle road, to nobody. **"I have got a bar and I have got nothing to go on with. A man with a bar needs a line to follow or a person to follow, and I have got neither this morning, and I am not going to make one up out of what four people said in a yard yesterday."**

The man of about fifty came up the middle road with the barrow at the sixth hour in the wet and got as far as the low field and stopped in the same place, and he said one thing at the top of the field, out loud, to nobody.

**"I am not going down there till Saturday and I have said that in a yard twice now and both times I have said Saturday and both times it has been raining."** He got the barrow about the handles and turned it round on the track and came back down, and the track behind him was two ruts of standing water four inches deep and the low field had not been in the same condition in four years and nobody in this holding has put a figure on that either.

From the fifth hour Marek had the one room to himself with the door open and nothing in front of him. At about the eighth hour he was out at the gate, and he stood there about ten minutes looking at the middle road, and then he came back in and sat down again, and he did not go up the direct road or the middle one.

**"A man in this county has worked out how to keep a thing out of a book by not writing it down, and a woman in this county has worked out how to keep a thing out of a county by not saying it out loud, and a man nine miles off has worked out how to keep a thing out of an argument by not handing over a number."** He said that to the open door and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"None of the three has done it for the reason anybody would want it done for. They have done it because they have all been in a room where a thing they gave up got used against them, and I am not going to stand in a yard and call that cowardice."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"It is also the reason this holding is going to be no use to anybody in a month, and I am not going to say that out loud in a yard either, because the three of them can all count and so can I, and a man who says a thing like that in the open usually wants somebody to agree with him."**

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the third hour of the afternoon, having walked the nine miles, and did not say anything about it then or later, and stood in the one room doorway behind Tova Reed while she read the page and went out again at about the fourth hour and put in the two stones at the outlet end with the water up over the top of the upper one.

Two hundred days is the age of the register form on the middle table, its column headingless, neither filled nor refused, and it will not be read out a fifth time. A hundred and six days ago the charter was signed in a room nine miles off. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-four days old and has nothing on it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the one room for the reading, said nothing about the page, and went and worked the north wall for two hours afterwards.

The word in front of that figure is paid and the figure is twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six. The sessions entered in this holding's book are seven, and no eighth was entered this morning, and a page torn out of a household book is not a session. The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three, and the letter on the long table has lain there three days and has gone into neither, and neither has the page, and the clerk of this holding said so out loud to the room and was not asked to say it twice.

What this holding does not know about has six ruled rows in front of it and not one unit written against any of them, the seventh is not ruled, and a list of work done without being asked is not a seventh. The use log went out into the rain with fifteen lines in it and came back with fifteen. Loaded, the barrow came down the four-mile road at about the seventh hour, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Seven columns are cut in the stone of that door nine hundred yards off and the seventh of them stands at sixty-four, and the rule for it is the first of a month and the thirtieth and no other morning, so nobody has walked the nine hundred yards this week to read it.

Nothing in this building has ever put a foot in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and nobody has measured it and nobody has walked it. The low field above the middle road is not that ring, and the two are not added to one another on any page in this holding. Nothing went into the two-foot gap at the kerb and the valve was not opened and the key was not used.

Four ruled lines sit under two words in the corner of the one room, all four of them bare, the answer they are for not due, and nothing said in this yard this morning put a word on any of them. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and its heading is what it was before anybody in this building arrived.

The fourth column of the schedule on the wall nine miles off is ruled and empty too, and that is not the same column and has never been added to this one. What lies face up on the low board is undated and unnamed and has no line anywhere on it that a name could go into, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved.

The page from the household book went into the inner pocket of Tova Reed's own book at about the seventh hour and not into any of the four books in this holding, and the clerk of this holding wrote nothing about it and was not asked to write anything, and the man who walked it four miles came back through the gate at about the ninth hour on his way home and did not stop and did not look at the table.

Rain again after the ninth hour, hard, off the water, and the outlet end running over the top of both stones, and the water in the ditch full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road full and inside its lip, and nobody thanked anybody.

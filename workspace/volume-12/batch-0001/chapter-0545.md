# Chapter 545

## The Fourth Line For The Second Time In Four Days, And A Number Said To Nobody

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked for the second time in four days, and the wind was off the water again and the boundary stones at the headland were found by hand along about two hundred yards of the bank in the dark. Four people from the second hour until about the fifth, the ditch along the eleven acres cut out, the loose stone barrowed off the top and heaped by the gate, and the frost that came in last night was still in the ground under the barrow track at seven and gone by nine.

No field was taken, and that is the fourth time this year the line has come round and gone up the bank and come down with an empty taking column. Nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that has waited four years, and nobody was thanked on the first of those four for a field that did not.

Twenty-nine taken and eighty-seven not is a hundred and sixteen mornings in force, which is the standing. Twenty-nine taken did not move. It did not move on the fourth of this month when the line was worked, it did not move on the fifth, it did not move on the seventh, and it is not going to move on the twelfth, and the only number in this holding that has not moved this month is the one everybody would like to move.

The eighth of this month is the sixty-fourth morning of the rotation counted one way and the fifty-first counted the other, and both go on this page, and the thirteen in the gap has been thirteen for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what it counts.

The figure was in the second book before either of the two men came out to the boards, and the clerk of this holding read the line back once and shut the book on it.

**One thousand and seventy-six hundredweight.**

Up eight on Sunday, and the launder's standing is the three hundred and eighth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The long wall gave up a hundred and forty-three mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-two the other at seven, and the sum of the pair is two hundred and seventy-five.

What is written against the four bodies of households is three hundred and six days, and what is written under it is one under and one over, being the three hundred and fifth of those mornings out of three hundred and seventh. What the two boards of the long wall carry this morning is six hundred and ninety-two days against seven hundred and thirty-nine, which is forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour.

Nothing goes in under that heading this morning, because half of the run is a half number on a Monday, and the clerk of this holding left the space as he found it, and this page carries the reason and no figure. The line was empty on Sunday morning and it will be full on Wednesday, and it has been the ordinary way of it since the day the run began.

The man of about fifty came up the middle road with the barrow at about the sixth hour and stopped in the same place on the low field, and he did not go down to the line, and he stood at the top of the field with his hands on the handles for about five minutes and then said one thing out loud to nobody.

**"It is Monday and I said Saturday and I am not going."** He said that to the low corner of the field. **"There is a difference between a man who cannot do a thing and a man who has given himself a day to do it, and I have been the first one for four years and I have found out this week that the second one is easier, and I do not know yet whether that is because it is easier or because I have found a way of not doing it."**

He turned the barrow round on the track and came back down, and the track behind him was hard this morning and did not fill with anything.

Soren Rill was in the yard at about the seventh hour and heard that, from about thirty yards off, and did not say anything about it then, and came back at about the ninth hour and stood at the tap.

**"I am going to say a thing to you and I am going to say it once and you are not to answer it now."** He said that to the tap and gave the reason before the thing. **"Saturday. The four-mile road. I will walk it and you will bring the barrow and you will stop it where the line is, and neither of us will write anything down, and if it comes out that the line and the bend are the same water then we will both know it and it will stay in two men who walk roads for a living."**

The man of about fifty said he would be there at the sixth hour, and Soren Rill said the sixth hour was too early and would be the seventh, and that was the whole of the negotiation and it took about a minute and a half.

**"That is the first thing two people in this holding have agreed to do this month and it has a day on it and neither of us is in charge of it."** He said that to his own boots afterwards and not to anybody. **"I have got no objection to that. I have got an objection to a post, and I have got no objection to two men and a barrow and a day, and I would like it noticed that I have said that out loud today so that nobody can come back to me in a month and say I would not have it."**

Nia Vale came in at about the sixth hour and went straight through to the one room and came out again with the board with two rules on it in her hand, which she is not supposed to have taken off its nail, and she put it back on the nail and said nothing to anybody about it.

**"There is a date on the top of the second rule now and a woman put it there, and I want it understood in this yard that I know whose hand that is."** She said that to the one room door and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it.

**"I am not going to say anything about it and I am not going to take it off again. If a man comes through that gate in a month and asks what the second rule is for, then the answer has to come from somebody other than me, because I took the board off the nail to look at it and I am not going to be the one who explains an empty rule that somebody has dated."**

Tova Reed spent the morning in the seed house, the page still in the inner pocket of her own book, and at about the ninth hour she stood in the one room doorway with the board in front of her and said nothing about it to anybody.

**"Three dates in my hand and a fourth in a pocket, and now a fifth in a piece of chalk on a board."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"That is two more dates in this county in one week and neither came from me, and I am not going to claim them and I am not going to be angry about them. A woman who keeps a book with an hour in it and no meaning in it has put a date on an empty rule. That is a household's move and not a front's move, and I am not going to walk into a room and use it."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour and went out to the gate at about the eighth hour and stood at it and came back in, and he did not go up the middle road and he did not go up the direct road.

**"Two men have agreed to walk a road on Saturday with a barrow and not to write it down, and that is the first piece of looking after a place above that road in this county that anybody has told me about that does not involve me."** He said that to the gate and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I want it on the page that I found that out at the ninth hour and not before, and I want it on the page that I have not offered to go with them. If I offer then it stops being two men with a barrow and starts being the engineer of record inspecting a bend, and it has been that four times in nine years and every one of those four times somebody stopped walking."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm. **"I am not going to be able to help with that walk and I am not going to be able to do without helping, and both of those are true this morning. I am writing that down because in about a month I am going to be in a bad mood about it, and I would rather find the sentence I have already said."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and walked as far as the gate of the low field, where she stood for about ten minutes with her back to the field, and she came back in without having gone through it.

**"I have not written anything this morning."** She said that to the gatepost. **"There was a man on a barrow at the top of that field saying a thing to a corner of the ground about a day he had given himself, and I was a good way off, short of four hundred yards, and I did not hear the whole of it, and I am not going to write down a sentence I have three quarters of."** She got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles in the wind, and stood in the one room doorway behind nobody at all, and went out again at about the ninth hour and put in the two stones at the outlet end with his coat still on.

He was on the north wall from the third hour until six and came in at about the seventh, put his bar against the wall, and said a number out loud to nobody on his way in, the way he has said it for four years, and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was standing at the tap at that moment with her own book shut under her arm, and she heard the number, and she did not write it down, and she did not ask him, and she went out through the first gate at about the eighth hour and walked up the fen road with the book under her arm and did not open it on the way.

She has a column with a date and an hour in it and no meaning against anything in it since the day she started it, and she has never once written a number into it, and there was a number said out loud in a yard at about the seventh hour on the eighth morning of this month that she heard, and it took her a good few seconds not to write it down.

A hundred and ninety-six days is the age of the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and it has nothing on it, and he did not say a second thing on his way out and did not know that anybody had heard the first. The charter was signed a hundred and eight days ago in a room nine miles off. The register form on the middle table is two hundred and two days old, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused.

The clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the two men at the long wall said the halves of the window to each other and one of them said the other's back, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Twenty-six is the figure and the word paid goes in front of it, and it is not discharged in twenty-six.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and no eighth was entered, and two men agreeing to walk a road is not a session. Neither the requests column nor the section-nine notes has moved this week and both of them are fifty-three, and the letter on the long table has lain there five days and has gone into neither, and the page from the household book went into neither. The board with the two rules on it is not a book, and it went back on its nail at about the sixth hour, put there by the person who took it down, and nobody asked her to.

What this holding does not know about is given six ruled rows and no units, and the seventh is not ruled, and a date in chalk on an empty rule is not a seventh. Nothing went into the use log this morning and it stands where it stood yesterday, at fifteen lines. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut in its stone, and this week nobody has walked to it, and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It moves on the first of a month and the thirtieth and on no other morning. It is eleven years since anybody walked in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and nobody has measured it and nobody has priced it, and the ground that came up above the middle road is not that ring and is not on it and is not added to it on any page here.

In the corner of the one room, under two words, four ruled lines have stood bare for a long time and the answer they are for is not due. A third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has touched its heading. On the low board, a sheet with no date, no name and no line for a name lies face up, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved.

Water standing in the low corner of the yard at first light and gone by four, and the frost in the ground under the barrow track at seven and gone by nine, and the wind off the water all morning without a break, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road full by seven and inside its lip, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 542

## Nine Miles On Foot, And A Second Column With No Heading On It

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three and the standpipe in the yard was running full by the fourth hour. The water in the ditch was slow and high and the middle road was hard at the top and soft at the bottom, and a man who has to walk nine miles in that gets wet to the knee by the seventh.

Marek went up the direct road at about the sixth hour and did not take the bus. He has not walked it in a month. It is nine miles and it is straight for six of them and then it drops into a cutting with a bend at the bottom of it, and the cutting holds the wet, and the two culverts on it are the district's and not this holding's, and there is nothing on either of them but a date cut in the stone.

He was at the gate of the machine house at about the eighth hour. The door was shut and the woman at the desk in the outer office opened it before he got his hand to it, which is a thing she has done for him since the first week he was on the roll, and she did not say anything about it, and he did not say anything either.

**One thousand and sixty-five hundredweight.**

Thursday came up eight and Friday gave five back, and what stands against the launder is the three hundred and fifth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The man who reads the schedule off the wall read the window out loud at the ninth hour to nobody in particular, because the wall is where it is and the wall is read: a hundred and forty-one mornings one way against a hundred and thirty-one the other, and those two are two hundred and seventy-two.

Three hundred and three days is what the sheet in the outer office carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath it takes the three hundred and second of those mornings out of three hundred and fourth. One under and one over. Six hundred and eighty-nine days on the near board of the long wall of the outer office and seven hundred and thirty-six on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning, here as in any other building in four counties.

What that office wrote in the second book at the ninth hour was **one hundred and twenty-three mornings of two hundred and twenty-nine**, of a month eight months back. A Friday is an odd day, and the run is read on an odd day, and on every even morning the line under that heading has stood empty since the run began.

The districts beyond the maintenance zone are on the schedule and they are on the schedule as they have been for four years, which is to say they are named and not served.

There is a column on that sheet that says which parts of this are inside the zone and a second column that says who looks after the rest. The second column is not empty and it has never once held a name that anybody in this building could put a face to. That is the honest shape of it, and the sheet is a correct sheet, and it has always been a correct sheet.

**"I have not been in this room in a month and there is a thing I have to say about it, and I am going to say it to the man who keeps the gauges and not to you."** He said that in the yard of the machine house at about the ninth hour, and the reason came before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I have been the person in four counties who read the instruments in this building for nine years, and I have never once asked the man who keeps them what he writes down, because what he writes down is his and not the machine's. I have never asked him and he has never said, and both of those have been true for nine years and neither of them is his fault."**

The man who keeps the gauges had been in this building nineteen years and had a bench of his own by the north wall with a lamp on a bracket above it and a stool that is the right height for sitting and not the right height for standing, and he was on the stool when Marek came over and he did not get up.

**"I keep a book."** He said that to the lamp. **"It is not a district book and it is not in a district room and it has got no heading at the top of it, and I have never told anybody that it exists except one woman from a cooperative who found it in a cupboard in a year before you were on this road, and she did not put it anywhere either."**

He turned it round on the bench without opening it and then opened it.

**"Left-hand column is the reading, morning and evening, same as always. Right-hand column I started on the ninth of this month."** He put his finger on the head of the second column, where there is nothing. **"Fourteen mornings. It is the pressure at the far gauge and it is not on any sheet in this building, because the far gauge is on a branch that is not on the zone sheet at all. It has been on the brickwork since before I came and I have read it every morning and evening for nineteen years and put it nowhere."**

**"Why nowhere."** He said that to the lamp again. **"Because a man asked me for it once, about nine years back, in a room with three other people in it, and he was asking for a number and not asking me a question, and when I gave it him it went in a book with a heading on it and a year later a man came and asked me whether I had given him a reading out of a branch that was off the sheet. So I put it nowhere for nine years and I have been right about that for nine years."**

He turned the page.

**"And on the ninth of this month the needle on that gauge went up a finger's width in a day and stayed there. There is a rule in this building that a gauge is read twice a day and put in a book, and that rule has a hole in it the exact size of a branch that is not on a sheet."**

He turned the page over and shut it halfway.

**"So I have started a column with no heading, in a book that is not a district book, in a room that does not have a name. I have not told the woman at the desk. I am telling you because you are the only man who has ever come back in here and asked me a question instead of a number."**

Nobody in that building thanked him for it and nobody told him to enter it, and the clerk of the machine house, who had come to the door of the yard at some point in the middle of it and gone away again, came back at about the tenth hour and asked Marek whether the sheet that goes out to the gate tonight had anything on it that had not been on it yesterday.

**"A sheet with an ordinary morning on it and a date at the foot of it that went past nine days ago."** Marek said that to the gate. **"The same kind of sheet that has gone out every night since the first of them, and I have read six of them this week off the board by the road and I have not got anything new to say about it and I am not going to invent something for you this morning because you have had a bad week."**

The two men on the wheel were on the wheel at the ninth hour, and one of them is a man this county chose by not choosing him, and neither of them stopped work to speak to anybody.

The chamber is at the back of the floor with four bolts through its door and a mark in chalk on the plate that bears a date. It was shut at every hour of Marek's walk in and his walk out, and nobody in that building offered him a key and he did not ask for one, and he has not asked for one since the party came out of it.

The schedule on the wall has a corridor claimed in a pan in one of its sheets, and the sheet is correct in every particular, and the corridor in the pan is neither open nor shut and has not been this morning. The order that sealed the route is in a drawer, valid, opening nothing.

Marek walked back the nine miles from about the tenth hour and got into the yard at about the fifth hour of the afternoon with the wet through to the knee of his left leg, and the frost had been out of the ground three nights and the ditch was running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full and inside its lip.

**"I have walked nine miles and I have come back with nothing that is going in a book, and I am going to leave it that way."** He said that to his own boots on the middle road and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"A man in a building nine miles off has started a second column with no heading over it, and he has told one person about it, and that person is me. The moment I take it out of his hands and hold it, it becomes a thing four counties can ask about, and he will be asked about it, and he will be standing in front of somebody explaining a number he chose to keep, because the last time he handed one over it went on a sheet that was not his."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"He has been keeping it nowhere for nine years for a good reason, and I have been the person who never asked him about it for nine years for a bad one, and I am not going to make him pay for mine by taking it off him in a yard."**

**"If I ever have to put it anywhere I will ask him first, and he can say no, and if he says no then there is no column in four counties, and that is a better county than one with a heading over a number he did not choose to give."**

Soren Rill was at the tap when he came in and looked at the state of his leg and said nothing about it and went and got a sack of lime out of the pump house and threw it on the middle road where it was soft, which is the whole of what he said to Marek and it was enough.

Tova Reed was in the one room at the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets and the fourth one still folded, and a man came in at about the sixth hour from a house four miles off toward the tap and stood in the one room doorway and said his household had cut a new ditch on Tuesday, on ground that was not in his ground, and wanted to know whether anybody was going to stop him.

Nobody stopped him, and nobody told him he had a sheet, and nobody gave him a bearing, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said the water was running in it this morning and went out to look at the north wall instead of going with him.

**"You have asked this yard a question and I am the only one in here going to answer any part of it, so hear me."** Tova Reed said it standing up, with her hand flat on the fourth sheet.

**"I have four counties and three dates and a sowing in them, and a ditch is a thing a household cuts, and there is no column in my four counties that says a man may not cut a ditch on ground that came up this month. So I am not going to tell you to stop and I am not going to give you a sheet, and the reason is not kindness."**

She turned the fourth sheet over under her hand and did not open it.

**"The day I put my name on either side of a ditch in a place like that, nineteen households in my county are going to hear that the seed front has a position about drainage. I have never had one and I am not going to start in a week when I cannot be in four places at once."**

The man said that was fair and asked whether anybody wanted to see the ditch, and four people said no for four different reasons, and he went back down the road with the ditch still his own business.

Twenty-six is the figure and paid is the word in front of it, and it is not discharged in twenty-six. The middle table here carries a register form a hundred and ninety-nine days old with nothing at the head of its column, and it is neither filled nor refused. In a room nine miles off the charter was signed a hundred and five days back, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-three days old and has nothing on it.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and an eighth did not go in this morning, and a walk of nine miles is not a session. Fifty-three is the number in the requests column and fifty-three is the number of the section-nine notes, and the letter on the long table has gone into neither. Six ruled rows for what this holding does not know, no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a second column with no heading on it in a bench book nine miles off is not a seventh.

The use log still has fifteen lines in it. The barrow went up the four-mile road and came down loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is eleven years bare and has not been walked, has not been measured, and has not had anybody in it, and the line that came up on the low field above the middle road is a different thing on a different piece of ground, and the two have not been added to one another on any page in this building.

Two words and four ruled lines in the corner of the one room, and all four of the lines are bare, and the answer they are for is not due, and nothing said at this gate this morning put a word on any of them. Nobody has improved the heading over the third column of the sheet of terms, which is ruled and empty.

What lies face up on the low board is undated and unnamed and has no line anywhere on it that a name could go into, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved. The door of the well house is nine hundred yards from this one, and the seventh of the seven columns cut in its stone is at sixty-four, and nobody has walked there this week to read it.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure into the second book at the ninth hour, read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the two men at the long wall said the halves of the window to each other and one of them said the other's back.

Nobody in this yard asked the man with the new ditch for the hour, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who was the only person standing in the one room who had a book in her hand, wrote nothing down about it, and the row she has kept for four years with the hour against it and no meaning against it has a blank in it this morning that is the first blank anybody in this yard has ever been able to account for.

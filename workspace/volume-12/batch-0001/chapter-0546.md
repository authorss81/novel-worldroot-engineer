# Chapter 546

## Two People At The Tap With The Same Question, And No Answer In The Yard

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe in the yard was running full by the fourth hour. There was a fog on the water until about the eighth hour that came up the middle road in a slow white bank and did not go past the gate, so that anybody standing at the low field gate until then could not see the field and anybody standing in the yard could not see the gate.

The second book took the figure at the first hour in a hand that shook a little from the cold, and it was read back once and not twice.

**One thousand and seventy-one hundredweight.**

Five off Monday's rise is what the launder took on Tuesday, and the three hundred and ninth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against it. Read at seven off the long wall: a hundred and forty-three mornings one way, a hundred and thirty-three the other, and two hundred and seventy-six for the pair.

Three hundred and seven days is what four bodies of households have to show on a sheet this morning, and the clause underneath, which is one under and one over, takes the three hundred and sixth of those mornings out of three hundred and eighth. Six hundred and ninety-three days stand on the near board of the long wall this morning, with seven hundred and forty on the far one. Forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour.

At the seventh hour, into the second book, in the frame of a month eight months back: **one hundred and twenty-five mornings of two hundred and thirty-three**. A Tuesday is an odd morning and the run is read on an odd morning, and that frame has not moved since the first of this month.

Two people came up the fen road on foot at about the seventh hour, in the fog, and got as far as the tap before anybody heard them, and they were from two different bodies of households, and neither of them knew the other one was coming, and each of them had walked four miles to ask the same question.

**"Is it true that somebody four counties off has written in asking for the schedule to be put back."** That is what the man asked, at the tap, to the clerk of this holding, who was filling the frame tray. **"I am not asking you to tell me who wrote it. I am asking whether it is true, because there is a woman in my body who has heard it from a man who heard it from a man, and she wants to know if her ground comes back to what it was if that sheet goes back."**

The clerk of this holding put the tray down.

**"It is true. It came in at the ninth hour on the second of this month and it is on the long table in that room and it has been on that table five days."** He said that to the tap and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to tell you what it is going to do, because I do not know, and I have got no column in any of these four books with a heading on it for a letter, and I am not going to rule one this week for a man at my tap. If you want it read, it will be read, and if you want it answered, then somebody with an answer has to want to give it, and that is not going to be me."**

Nobody at the tap said that was right and nobody said it was wrong.

The woman asked the same question of Tova Reed at about the eighth hour, at the low board, with the letter standing nine inches away from her on the long table behind her, and she asked it in almost the same words.

**"Is it true that a council has written asking for the schedule to be restored."** She said it to Tova Reed and gave the reason before the thing. **"We have got a low corner on our side that came up on the ninth and the man who cut our new ditch is not the only one cutting. I have got nine households in my body and I have got a meeting on the date and I have got no answer to give them and I would rather be told the true thing than carry a second-hand one up a road."**

Tova Reed did not move away from the low board and did not touch it.

**"It is true, and I have not read it and nobody has read it out to me, and I know the true thing and the true thing is that a letter asking for a sheet to go back is not a statement about your low corner. Nobody in four counties can tell you that a sheet going back will put your ground where it was, and anybody who tells you that they can is selling you something, and I have been in four counties of meetings for four years and I know exactly which men sell it."**

She put her hand flat on the low board, on the paper that lies face up on it, and did not move it.

**"So the answer is that I do not know, and the reason I am giving you that instead of a better one is that I have got three dates in my hand and four counties to be in and one of those counties is yours, and I am not going to spend a date on a thing I have not been able to find out. If I find out, you will be the first to know, and it will be from me and not from a man on a road."**

**"I would rather have that than have nothing."** The woman said it and meant it, and she wrote the hour and the date on the back of her own hand with a pencil, which is not a book, and she did not ask Tova Reed to write anything down, and Tova Reed did not offer.

The man at the tap stayed for about half an hour after that and did not say a second thing to anybody, and the fog was still on the water when the second of the two asked, and the two people who were in the yard at that moment heard both questions and have not compared them since.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way out and stopped in the middle of the yard when she saw the two of them, and stood there, and then went on out through the gate without speaking to anybody, and she was not at the tap at any hour of this morning.

**"I have been in this yard for nine days and this is the first morning two of them have been in it at once."** She said that at the gatepost on her way out and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it. **"I am not going to say anything about it and I am not going to be the one who notices a pattern in it, because a pattern in it is a number, and I have got a list under my arm that is nine places long, and it does not have rooms attached to it."**

Sera Quill and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went up the middle road together at about the eighth hour when the fog had gone off the field, and neither of them had said to the other that they were going, and they stood at the low field gate together for about twenty minutes and neither of them went through it.

**"I have got a date in a county book and an hour and nothing else, and I have had that book four years."** Sera Quill said that to the gatepost. **"I am not writing anything about this morning and I want the woman beside me to hear the reason. If I stand in a field and write, then what I have written is a record of a field, and a record of a field can be produced by a person who is not a witness. I would have stopped being a witness to records and started being a witness to a field, and I have never once claimed to be the second."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had her own book out and shut and stood with her thumb in the middle of it.

**"I have never written a number in that book in four years and I heard one said out loud in a yard on Monday and I did not write it down and I have not been able to stop thinking about it since."** She said that to the shut book. **"I have not stopped thinking about it because it was the right number, and I have said that sentence out loud to myself twice in two days. A number that is right and a number that I can prove are two different things and I have got a column that only ever holds the second."**

She put the book away and neither of them wrote anything.

Soren Rill was at the second bend of the four-mile road from the sixth hour and came in at about the ninth hour and heard the two of them still at the tap from about a hundred yards off and came and stood beside them, and did not say anything for about a minute.

**"The new line in the bend is dry at the top of it now and it has not gone back to where it was, and that is the third morning it has not gone back."** He said that to the fog. **"That is a fact about a ditch and I am going to say it and then I am going to stop, and if either of you has a question for me about a ditch you can ask it now and if you have not then I am going to the tap and fill my can."**

Neither of them had a question about a ditch, and he filled his can, and the man and the woman went down the fen road at about the tenth hour and the fog had gone by then and the field above the middle road was there in the ordinary way with the strip about a yard wide in it, forty yards along, and neither of them looked at it.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and nothing in front of him, and he heard both questions through the doorway and did not come out for either of them.

**"Two households from two different bodies walked four miles each to ask one question and got two different people saying they do not know, and both of those two people told the two of them the truth, which is not the same as knowing, and the letter is still on the long table."** He said that to the shut third book and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I want it written down that that is the correct outcome and not a failure, and I want it written down by somebody who is not me, because I have been the man who knows for nine years and I have watched what that does to a room."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"There is a version of this morning where somebody in this yard knew the answer and gave it to them, and in that version two bodies of households go up a road with a number between them, and in four years one of them is asked to produce it and one of them says the holding said so, and after that nobody in four counties ever brings a question to this gate again. I would rather be a gate that says it does not know twice."**

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-seven days old and has nothing on it, and a hundred and nine days ago the charter was signed in a room nine miles off. The register form on the middle table is two hundred and three days old, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused. That line is paid at twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six, and it does not fall again this month.

The sessions entered in this holding's book stand at seven, and nobody entered an eighth, and two households asking a question at a tap is not a session. The section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and so do the requests, and the letter on the long table has lain there six days and has gone into neither, and the page from the household book went into neither, and the board with the two rules on it is not a book.

Six rows here for what the holding does not know, no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a question asked at a tap is not a seventh and an unanswered question is not an answer.

The use log is at fifteen lines and nobody this morning put a sixteenth in it. Loaded, the barrow came down the four-mile road at about the seventh hour, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Seven columns are cut in the stone of a door nine hundred yards off, and the seventh of them is at sixty-four. Nobody has walked there this week to read it, and it is read on a first of a month and a thirtieth and on nothing else.

Nobody in this holding has ever been inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and this week it was neither walked nor measured nor crossed. The low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground from that ring and the two have not been added to one another on any page in this building.

Nothing went into the two-foot gap at the kerb and the valve was not opened and the key was not used.

Four ruled lines and two words in the corner of the one room, all of them bare, the answer they are for not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty with its heading as it has always stood, and the third column of the sheet on the wall nine miles off is ruled and empty as well, and those are two different columns and have never been added together.

What lies face up on the low board is undated and unnamed and has no line anywhere on it that a name could go into, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved. The woman who asked the second question stood with her hand on it for about a minute and did not pick it up.

Tova Reed went in at about the tenth hour and the fog had gone and the standing water in the low corner of the yard had gone with it, and the frame on the pump house was up and the standpipe was running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and inside its lip, and the clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The ninth. The first line of the rota worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, the standpipe in the yard running full by the fourth hour. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and twenty-five mornings of two hundred and thirty-three, of a month eight months back. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and seventy-one hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and ninth. Two persons from two different bodies of households were at the tap at about the seventh and eighth hours and asked one question of two different people in this yard, and neither question has been answered and neither answer was a refusal, and no column has been ruled for either of them in any of the four books in this holding.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

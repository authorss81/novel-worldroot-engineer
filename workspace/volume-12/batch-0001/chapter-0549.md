# Chapter 549

## The Fourth Line For The Third Time In Nine Days, And A Number Written Down Twice

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked for the third time in nine days. The wind was north-east and cold off the water and the boundary stones at the headland were found by hand along about two hundred yards of the bank in the dark.

Four people from the second hour until about the fifth, the ditch along the eleven acres cut out, the loose stone barrowed off the top and heaped by the gate, and the frost in the ground under the barrow track at seven and gone by nine again, the way it has gone and come all week.

No field was taken, and that is five times this year the line has come round and gone up the bank and come down with an empty taking column. Nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that has waited four years.

Twenty-nine taken and eighty-eight not, and that is a hundred and seventeen mornings in force. Twenty-nine taken did not move on any morning of the week. It did not move on the fourth when the line was worked, on the eighth when the line was worked, or on the twelfth when the line was worked, and it did not move on any of the six mornings between them when the line was not worked at all. It is not going to move on any morning that is not on this holding's paper.

Counted this way the twelfth of this month is the sixty-fifth morning of the rotation, and counted the other way it is the fifty-second, and both of those go on this page, and the thirteen in that gap has been thirteen for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what it counts.

The clerk of this holding opened the second book at the first hour, wrote the line, and read it back once. The ninth hour is when a clerk reads a line back and it is not when he writes it.

**One thousand and eighty-two hundredweight.**

Friday came up eight and the launder carries the three hundred and twelfth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. What came off the long wall at seven was a hundred and forty-five mornings one way against a hundred and thirty-four the other, the two men saying it between them the way they do, and the sum of the pair is two hundred and seventy-nine.

Ten more days than any morning of the week behind this one, and that is what the four bodies of households are given: three hundred and ten days, with the clause beneath it taking the three hundred and ninth of those mornings out of three hundred and eleventh. One under and one over. There is no column anywhere in this holding for the second rule on that board, and there is one on the long wall for both of these: six hundred and ninety-six days on the near board of it, seven hundred and forty-three on the far one, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The second book carries nothing under that heading this morning, and the clerk of this holding left the space empty rather than put a half number in it, and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of the run is a half number on a Friday. That line was empty on Thursday, it will be full on Saturday, and it is the ordinary way of it.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and went as far as the low field gate, stood at it about ten minutes and came back in, and she wrote this morning's date in the county book and nothing else, and the frame of that run is of a month eight months back and has not moved since the first of this month.

The register form on the middle table is two hundred and six days old, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused and it will not be read out a fifth time. The charter was signed a hundred and twelve days ago in a room nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light. Three of them have each sent a person to this yard in nine days to ask a question that nobody here has answered, and the clerk of this holding has not ruled a line for any of it.

The letter on the long table has lain there nine days. It is a council's own paper, of its own date, asking in writing that the schedule be restored, and it is between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board between it and the nearer of the two, and it has not been moved and it has not been answered and nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it, and there is still no column for it in any of the four books.

**"Nine days is not a long time for a sheet of paper and it is a very long time for a thing nobody has been asked about."** The clerk of this holding said that to the letter and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it.

**"I have had a letter on that table for nine days that asks a building nine miles off to put its sheet back the way it was, and in nine days not one person has come through that gate and asked me what I have done about it. That is the first nine days of my life in this room where nobody has asked me what I have done about a thing I have not done."**

He squared the letter with the edge of the table, which is a thing he has done about nine times, and it did not move.

**"I am not going to say that I have not done anything, because that would be a refusal and it is not one. There is a piece of paper on this table and nobody has answered it and nobody has been asked to, and I have written down the hour it came in and nothing else about it. In four years somebody is going to come and look at that table and the hour is going to be the only thing on it, and that is the correct outcome."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at about the seventh hour from the north wall, put his bar against the wall, and said a number out loud to nobody on his way in, the way he has said it for four years, and it was the right one.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap with her own book open and shut and open again, and she said his name.

**"Say it again."** She said it to his back and he was four steps into the one room. **"I have heard you say it four mornings running and I have never once written it down and I have been ashamed of my own book for a fortnight about it. Say it again and I am going to write it, and I am going to write the hour, and I am going to write that you said it twice in the open in front of me and not that you told me how you know it, because I am not going to ask you that and you are not going to tell me."**

He stopped where he was and did not turn round for the better part of a minute, and then he said the number again, the same number, in the same voice, at the same pitch, four steps into the one room with the door open and about nine people in the yard.

She wrote it in the book. It is the first figure of any kind in that column in four years and it has no unit against it and no meaning against it, and the two lines above it are a date and an hour and the two lines below it are a date and an hour, and the figure stands in the middle of them with nothing on either side of it.

**"Thank you."** She said it once, quietly, to the back of a man, and he did not turn round and did not answer and went out to the north wall, and that is the only time in this holding's week that anybody thanked anybody, and he has not mentioned it since.

Nobody else in that yard said anything about it. The clerk of this holding did not enter it in the second book, because it is not a figure of a series and it is not on any sheet in this holding, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not offer it to anybody.

Nia Vale came in at about the sixth hour on her way back in and stood at the long wall and read both boards and did not go into the one room.

**"There are nine places above that road and four off the middle road toward the tap and I have been past every one of them in nine days and I have said nothing to anybody in nine days, and that is the same nine days."** She said that to the boards and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it.

**"This yard has filled up all week with people doing one piece of a thing each, one after another, and not one of them has been put there by anybody. That is either the answer to a question nobody in this county has asked yet or it is a coincidence, and I have got nine days of evidence and I am not going to call it either."**

**"What I will say is that there is a man in this holding who can walk to a place above that road and get more out of it than I can. He did it on Thursday and he told this yard in the plainest words I have heard anybody use, and he is not going to be asked to do it again. If anybody in four counties wants to be sure of that, they can come and ask me."**

Soren Rill came in at about the seventh hour from the four-mile road and stood at the tap and did not go up to the north wall or into the one room or to the low board.

**"Two men and a barrow and a day we agreed between ourselves, and the two holes are four hundred yards apart and the bar comes out wet at both of them, and I am not going to be the one who puts that in a book."** He said that to the tap and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A man with a bar needs a line to follow or a person to follow. On Wednesday I had a man to follow and on Saturday I will have him again, and that is not a post and it is not a rule and it is not a column, and I would like it left exactly where it is."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and nothing in front of him, and he came out at about the ninth hour and stood in the yard, and about four people in this county were in it and about four were not, and neither of those two figures is in a book anywhere and neither is in a head.

**"I have said three sentences on this yard's ground in the last two days that I have not said in nine years of saying things in rooms, and the reason I have said them here is that a yard has no table at it and a room always has one."** He gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and there is no instrument anywhere in four counties that says whether it is on or off."** He put his sleeve back down over his forearm. **"The comfort is not standing. The father is not alive in the wood. The day does not come back."**

**"Those five lines are true this morning exactly as they were true on the second of this month, and I am putting them on a Friday in a yard instead of at the end of a month on a table, because a table makes them sound like the end of something. They are what a man has got on a Tuesday."**

Nobody in that yard asked him about the five lines and nobody thanked him and nobody put a hand on his arm.

She spent the day in the seed house, and near the end of it she came in and stood at the low board with both hands behind her for a while and did not touch it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a fourth page in a pocket that a household gave itself, and a fifth in chalk on an empty rule, and I have not answered one of them and I have not been asked to and I have not offered."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I have spent a week in four counties saying no to things that would have made me look like somebody in front of a room, and this morning I have come to the end of what I was going to say no to. What is left is a place above a road with no name on it, a great many people each holding one piece of it, and not one of them in charge. The only thing I can honestly offer anybody this month is a date and a table."**

She put her four sheets down on the middle table beside the register form and squared them and left them there.

**"That is not a position and it is not an answer and it is not a thing I would put in a letter, and if a man four counties off reads it and thinks the seed front has said something about a schedule then he has read a thing into a table with four sheets on it, and I will not be responsible for that and I will not correct him."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and the register form at two hundred and six days did not move under the four sheets and nobody moved it back.

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles in the cold and stood in the one room doorway behind Tova Reed for about five minutes and then went out to the north wall and did the rest of the day's work there, and he said nothing to anybody about the week and nobody asked him.

Paid is the word in front of that figure, the figure is twenty-six, and it is not discharged in twenty-six. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is two hundred days old and has nothing on it, and he is the man who said the number twice this morning, and neither of those two things is in the other one, and neither has been entered in any book in this holding.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and no eighth was entered this week. The requests column is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and neither has moved on any of the ten mornings, and the letter on the long table went into neither of them on the ninth hour of the second of this month and has been in neither ever since. The twelfth morning of this month did not add a line to the use log, and it stands at fifteen. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row. A rut, a wet bar, a bearing, a second column with no heading, a page torn out of a household book, a letter, an empty rule with a date on it, and a number said twice in a yard are eight things this week and they are not a seventh. The sixth row is still the sixth row and the seventh row is still not ruled.

On the seventh of seven columns cut in the stone of the well house door nine hundred yards off, the figure is sixty-four, and it stands unread this week. Its rule is the first of a month and the thirtieth and nothing else, and on the thirtieth of this month it will be sixty-five, and from sixty-five it does not go back.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres went another week without anybody in it, without a measurement and without a crossing, and what came up above the middle road is not that ring and is not in it and is not added to it anywhere in these four books. Nothing went into the two-foot gap at the kerb and the valve was not opened and the key was not used.

Four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room, and the four of them are bare, and the answer they are for is not due, and nothing in this week put a word on any of them.

The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved and no sixth term has been written. The sheet on the low board lies face up where it has lain since a month now four months back, undated and unnamed, with nowhere on it for a name to go. It is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

The frost came out of the middle road by nine and the water in the ditch was running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and inside its lip, and the standing water in the low corner of the yard went down about an inch between seven and nine.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the north wall with a stick in his hand from the third hour until six with the wind behind him, and the clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

Spring in this county is not a season on a page either. It is a length of a morning in which the frost goes out of a barrow track between seven and nine, and a woman writes down a number a man said twice, and a council's letter lies on a long table nine days on with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it and no column in four books, and a great many people have each got one piece of a thing above a road and not one of them has been put there by anybody, and that is what the week was.

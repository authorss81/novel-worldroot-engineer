# Chapter 548

## He Went Up The Middle Road At The Seventh Hour And Waited, And It Did Not Come

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, and the gate was lifted with a bar and a stone and put back on its frame, and both culverts were clear and dry at the bottom, which they have not been this month.

The sun was on the low field by the sixth hour with no cloud on it and the wind had gone light, and the only weather in this county this morning was that the ground had about nine days left in it and had begun, on the eleventh, to give it back.

The second book was open at the first hour and the line went in and was read back once, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of that morning and at every other hour of that week.

**One thousand and seventy-four hundredweight.**

Thursday fell five back, and the three hundred and eleventh figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries. One of the two men at the long wall had a hundred and forty-four mornings one way and the other had a hundred and thirty-four, and each of them said the other's half back before either of them put his hand down, and the two of those together are two hundred and seventy-eight.

On a sheet in the one room, three hundred and nine days is what stands against the four bodies of households, and the clause is one under and one over, being the three hundred and eighth of those mornings out of three hundred and tenth. Six hundred and ninety-five days, and then seven hundred and forty-two, on the near board and the far board of the long wall in that order, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The clerk of this holding read the run out of the second book at the seventh hour, of a month eight months back, and the pair on that line this morning is **one hundred and twenty-six mornings of two hundred and thirty-five**. A Thursday is an odd morning, and the frame of that run has not moved since the first of this month, and the last thing in these four books that changes by rule changes on a first of a month and on nothing else.

Marek went up the middle road at about the seventh hour on his own, past the gate, on to the low field, and up the field to the line, and stopped there in about the sun where the barrow had been yesterday.

He stood on the strip about a yard wide for about twenty minutes. He took his sleeve back over his forearm at some point in the first five and put it down again at about the fifteenth.

What he got was a piece. He has said on the page before, in other months, that he can take a piece of it standing on the ground, and the piece came this morning the way it comes, and the piece was no bigger than it has been since the seventeenth of a month now three months back, and the field did not come.

It is the difference between a room with one lamp in it and the room. Nine years ago he could stand on a piece of ground and have the whole of what the ground remembered arrive at once, in one movement, with no work, and it was not a power anybody had given him and it was not something he could do when he liked, and it stopped being one field on the seventeenth of a month now three months back and it has not loosened by a degree since.

The man of about fifty came up the middle road at about the eighth hour with the barrow, saw him standing on the line, came up the field to about forty yards short of it, turned the barrow round in the headland shadow and went back down, and did not come up, and did not ask, and has not asked since.

Harlan Vetch came up the middle road at about the ninth hour, went past the gate, stood at the bottom of the field for about two minutes, turned round and went back down. He has not said anything about that either and he was not asked, and nine miles off there is a building where two of the most ordinary people anybody has ever met would tell you that a man went up a field and came down again and that is the whole of what happened.

Marek came down the field at about the ninth hour and into the yard and stood at the tap for about a quarter of an hour without going into the one room, and then went and sat on the bench by the pump house in the sun.

**"I went up there this morning to do the thing I used to be able to do and it did not come, and I want that on the page in my own hand in the yard in front of whoever wants to read it."** He said that to the bench and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"The reason is that I have told myself for three months that what I lost on the seventeenth was a price, and a price is a thing you pay once and then you have paid it, and I have been carrying that sentence about with me like a receipt."** He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"It is not a receipt. It is a door. I have got the key to a room and the room is not there any more and it is not going to come back this year, and what I have lost is the door and not the key, and that is a different thing and a worse thing to say out loud in a yard."**

Nobody in that yard said he was sorry and nobody thanked him and nobody put a hand on his arm and nobody said that it must have been hard.

**"I have not lost the ability. I want that on the page as well, because somebody in four counties is going to hear that the engineer of record went up a field and could not do a thing, and there are only two versions of that and one of them is that he cannot and one of them is that somebody has to do it for him, and neither of those is what happened this morning."**

He looked at the low field through the gate for a while.

**"What happened this morning is that a man stood on about a yard of ground and got about as much as he has been getting since the seventeenth of a month now three months back. He could have got the same of it in his front room in the dark. It was not more and it was not less."**

**"I have not become worse at anything. I have become a man with one room and a locked door and four years of practice at the one room. Being sad about that would be a way of asking somebody for it back and I am not going to do that to any of them."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap at about the tenth hour with her book out and shut, and she heard all of that, and she did not write any of it down, and she said so afterwards and she said why.

**"He has just told this yard the most careful thing anybody has said in it this month, and I am not going to take four seconds of it into a book with a date and an hour in it and no meaning in it."** She said that to the bench.

**"I have a column that holds a date and an hour and nothing else. If I put that in it, then in four years there will be a page in this county that records the day a man lost a door, and it will have my hand on it, and nobody will be able to work out from the page whether he was telling the truth."**

Nia Vale came in at about the eleventh hour on her way back in from the boundary and stood at the gate and did not go up the road, and she said one thing at the gatepost, and then went on out again and was gone for the rest of the morning.

**"He has lost the whole of it and kept the piece and he has told us which of the two he would rather have had, and I have got nine places above that road where I cannot get a whole of anything either, and I have never once said that in a yard because I did not think anybody would be interested."** She said it to the gatepost and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it.

**"I am not going to make a thing of the two of us. There is no room in this county for a room, and if I stand next to that man in a sentence then somebody will write the sentence down, and it will be a claim, and I have watched a claim eat a fact alive in a book in this county once already."**

Soren Rill came in at about the seventh hour of the afternoon from the four-mile road and did not hear any of it and was told none of it and has not been told.

The seed house had Tova Reed in it all morning with the four counties' sheets and the page in the inner pocket of her own book, and at about the third hour she came as far as the one room door, looked in at the bench, and stayed where she was.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket and a man on a bench who has just told this yard he cannot do a thing he could do in a month now three months back, and I am not going to be the one who brings any of those three into the same conversation."** She said that to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A seed front that goes round a county saying a man has lost something has taken a position on a loss, and a position on a loss is a claim on somebody's ground. I have nine households waiting for me to have an opinion about drainage and I am not going to have it about this."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour, went as far as the low field gate, stood at it about ten minutes and came back in and got on the bus, and the date went in the county book and nothing else did, and the frame of that run is of a month eight months back and has not moved, and what a person said on a bench is not a date.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-nine days old and has nothing on it, and he came in at about the ninth hour, put his bar against the wall, and said a number out loud to nobody on his way in, and it was the right one. The charter was signed a hundred and eleven days ago in a room nine miles off. The register form on the middle table is two hundred and five days old, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused.

Paid is the word in front of that figure, the figure is twenty-six, and it is not discharged in twenty-six.

Under the heading for sessions this holding's book has seven, and nobody entered an eighth this morning. A man standing on a field is not a session, and the rehearsal of nine minutes at a hand-lever nine miles off is not a session and is not entered as one. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand where they stood at the start of the week, and the letter on the long table has lain there eight days and has gone into neither, and the page from the household book went into neither, and the board with two rules on it is not a book.

Six rows are ruled here for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row. A man who loses a door is not a seventh, and he is not a seventh not known either, because a man is not a thing this holding does not know about. He was in the yard this morning and he said what he can do and what he cannot.

There are fifteen lines in the use log and nobody wrote a sixteenth of them. Down the four-mile road at about the seventh hour came the barrow, loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

The door nine hundred yards off has seven columns cut in its stone and the seventh of them is at sixty-four, and it is not read this week, and from sixty-four it does not go back.

Not one person in this holding put a foot in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres this week. It has not been walked, it has not been measured, and no page in this building gives it a dimension. The low field above the middle road is not that ring, and the two have not been added to one another. Two words and four ruled lines in the corner of the one room, every line of them bare, and the answer they are for is not due. A third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and nobody has improved the heading over it.

On the low board there is a sheet with no date on it and no line on it anywhere that a name could go into. Nobody has filled it, nobody has withdrawn it, and nobody has moved it off that board. Nothing went into the two-foot gap at the kerb and the valve was not opened and the key was not used.

Marek was still on the bench by the pump house at about the ninth hour, in the sun, with his sleeve down over his forearm, and the low field above the middle road was in the ordinary way behind the gate with about a yard of it standing up and a rut in it and no name anywhere in four counties for any part of it, and the clerk of this holding wrote the line into the second book and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

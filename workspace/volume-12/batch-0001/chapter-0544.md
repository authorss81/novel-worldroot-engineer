# Chapter 544

## The Fifty-First Time, And A Rule That Came Back Empty Again

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, and the gate was lifted with a bar and a stone and put back on its frame. Both culverts were running clear after two days of rain and the low corner of the yard had gone down about two inches since the sixth hour yesterday, and the sky was hard and blue over the water and there was no wind at all.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at the first hour and the pen went down and stayed down, and the line was read back once and not read twice.

**One thousand and sixty-eight hundredweight.**

Sunday gave five back and the launder holds the three hundred and seventh figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. A hundred and forty-two mornings one way and a hundred and thirty-two the other is what the two men at the long wall had to say at seven, one reading and the other checking, and the two of those add to two hundred and seventy-four.

The four bodies of households stand at three hundred and five days on that sheet, and the clause under the figure takes the three hundred and fourth of those mornings out of three hundred and sixth. One under and one over. On the near board of the long wall there are six hundred and ninety-one days, and on the far one seven hundred and thirty-eight. Forty-seven between the two, and the drawer behind the near one stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Goes in at the seventh hour under the heading where the run of mornings goes: **one hundred and twenty-four mornings of two hundred and thirty-one**, of a month eight months back. A Sunday is an odd morning and the run is read on an odd morning.

The cart came in at the first gate at about the third hour and stopped on the steelyard and was not unloaded, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling and the man of about fifty between them got her off the plates and back on again in about a quarter of an hour, and the load went back down the fen road the way it had come, and nobody in this yard opened it.

That is the ninth-day return and this is the fifty-first of them. It came off the steelyard at five hundred and fourteen hundredweight, which is eleven above the last one, and the two halves were reconciled twice at the boards and were not added, and the pair on the ticket is the forty-first pair and is two names written side by side in one hand, and the hand is the same hand as the last four.

The board in the one room has two rules cut in it and the first of them is the one the returns go on and it is full to the bottom of the paper. The second rule is cut the same length and has been blank on every one of the fifty-one mornings this return has come, and this morning is the thirty-fifth blank counted on that board since anybody started counting them, and it is the thirty-fifth and not the thirty-sixth, because the count goes on the return and not on the day.

**"It has been blank every time I have been in this room and I have been in this room every day for eleven years."** The clerk of this holding said that to the board, standing up, with his finger on the empty rule and not touching it. **"Nobody has ever told me what it is for. I asked a man who was in this building before me, once, and he said he did not know either, and that is the whole of the answer there has ever been in this room about that rule."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the one room at about the fourth hour with her book out and she asked the question, which nobody in this yard had asked in eleven years.

**"What is the second rule for."** She said it to the clerk and not to the board. **"Not what does it say and not who put it there. What is it for, and who is it for, and has anybody in this building ever been told that they were the one it was for."**

The clerk of this holding said he did not know, and that he had said so eleven years ago and been told the same, and that the second rule is cut the same length as the first and was cut at the same time by a man who is not in this building, and that is all he has.

**"Then I am going to leave the space blank this morning and I am going to write the date on the top of it and nothing under it. That is not a blank any more. That is a space with a date on it, and a space with a date on it is a question."**

She said that to her own book. **"I am not going to answer it and I am not going to write down that anybody was asked. I am going to write down that a fifth hand came and weighed and went, and that a rule was cut the same length as the first one and had nothing in it, and the hour, and the number of the return."**

She wrote it and shut the book and did not read it back.

**"A rule that came back empty does not open anything."** She said that to the door frame as she went out. **"There is a great deal in this county waiting for a form to be filled in and a form is not going to do it. That second rule has been cut the same length as the first one for eleven years and has never had a hand in it, and it is not going to have one this morning. I am not going to be the first, and neither is anybody in this yard while I am standing in it."**

Soren Rill was in the yard at about the sixth hour and stood and watched the cart go out of the first gate and then went up the middle road on foot with his bar, and did not come back until about the ninth hour, and came back wet past the knee again.

**"I have been up to the second bend and the water in it has dropped about a foot since Friday and the new line is still there and it is dry at the top of it now, which it was not on Saturday."** He said that to the tap. **"I have told this yard that twice now and both times I have said a place on a road and not a number, and I am going to go on doing that until somebody with a book comes, and I am not going to be the one who decides that day is today."**

Nia Vale was in at about the fifth hour, on her way out to the boundary, and stood in the one room doorway for about two minutes looking at the board with the two rules on it and did not go in and did not say anything.

**"There are two rules on that board and one of them has been in use for fifty-one returns and the other one has not been in use once, and I have been in four counties' offices and I have never seen a board like that."** She said that to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing, and she printed no figure for it.

**"I am not going to put my own work on the second rule. Whoever cut it wanted two things on a board and only one of them ever got used, and I would be guessing at the other one, and I have got nine places above a road with no names on them and I am not going to add a guess about a piece of board to that."**

She went on out to the boundary and was gone about three hours.

Tova Reed had been in the seed house since the second hour with the page from the household book in the inner pocket of her own book, and at about the ninth hour she came in and stood at the board with a piece of chalk in her hand and did not use it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in my pocket that a household gave itself, and a rule on a board that has been empty for eleven years."** She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing. **"I am not going to put anything on that rule, and I am not going to tell anybody in four counties that there is an empty rule in a holding nine miles from a building they all draw on. The day that gets said out loud somebody will want to fill it, and the only two things anybody can put there are a name and a date. A name is a post. A date is a deadline."**

She squared the four counties' sheets on the table. **"I have watched a post and a deadline between them cost a county a season, and I have got three dates in my hand already and I am not going to hand a fourth one to a piece of board."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Marek was in the one room from the sixth hour and looked at the board and then went out to the gate and stood at it and came back in, and he did not go up the direct road and he did not go up the middle road.

**"Two rules cut to the same length at the same time, and one of them has been in use for fifty-one returns in eleven years and the other one has never been used once."** He said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I have watched people fill empty things for nine years. I have filled one myself, in a room, in a month, and I did it because the empty was uncomfortable, and it took years for the thing I put there to be the reason somebody could not do a thing they should have done."**

He looked at the empty rule for a while without touching it.

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"I am not going to do that twice and I am not going to advise anybody in this yard to do it, and I want it on the page that I have thought about it and have left it alone, because in a month somebody is going to come through that gate and ask me what the second rule is for, and I would rather be able to say that I stood in this room and did not touch it."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and went in and read the board standing up and then went out and got on the bus, and she did not write anything, and about four people in this county have now seen that board and the rest of the county has not, and that is not in a book anywhere.

Two hundred and one days is how long the register form has been on the middle table, its column headingless, neither filled nor refused, and it will not be read out a fifth time. A hundred and seven days ago the charter was signed, in a room nine miles off. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-five days old and has nothing on it, and he was the one who got the cart off the steelyard and did not look at the board on his way out.

Twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six, and paid is the word that goes in front of it. Seven is the number of sessions in this holding's book and nobody entered an eighth, and a return is not a session and neither is a steelyard. The requests are fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and the letter on the long table has lain there four days and has gone into neither, and neither has the page from the household book, and the board in the one room is not one of the four books and the clerk of this holding has said so out loud this morning to anybody who would listen.

Six rows stand ruled for what this holding does not know, no unit against any of them, no seventh row, and an empty rule cut to the length of a used one is not a seventh and is not the seventh either. A cart on a steelyard at the third hour is not a sixteenth line, and the use log has fifteen. The barrow went up the four-mile road and came back down loaded, and eleven is where its journeys stand, and eleven is a floor.

The seventh column cut in the stone of the door of the well house nine hundred yards off stands at sixty-four and is not read this week.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has not been walked this week and has not been measured and nobody in this holding has ever stood in it, and what came up on the low field above the middle road is not that ring. The two are not added to one another anywhere in these four books. Under the two words in the corner of the one room are four ruled lines, and every one of them is bare, and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty and the heading over it has not been touched.

What lies face up on the low board is undated and unnamed and has no line anywhere on it that a name could go into, and it is not filled and it is not withdrawn and it has not been moved. Nothing went into the two-foot gap at the kerb and the valve was not opened and the key was not used.

The cart went out of the first gate at about the fourth hour and the plates of the steelyard were cold underfoot by the seventh, and the frost that had been promised in the sky arrived after the ninth hour, and by the tenth there was white in the shadow of the north wall.

The count of blanks on that board is thirty-five and does not go to thirty-six until the next return comes round. A rule that came back empty opens nothing, and the two men at the long wall said the halves of the window to each other and one of them said the other's back, and nobody thanked anybody.

> THE NINTH-DAY RETURN AS IT CAME OFF THE STEELYARD AT THE FIRST GATE OF THIS HOLDING ON THE SEVENTH MORNING OF THIS MONTH, ENTERED BY THE CLERK OF THIS HOLDING IN HIS OWN HAND AT THE NINTH HOUR, BEING THE FIFTY-FIRST OF THE FIFTY-ONE RETURNS AND THE FORTY-FIRST PAIR, AND THE TWO HALVES RECONCILED TWICE AND NOT ADDED: **FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN HUNDREDWEIGHT, ELEVEN ABOVE THE LAST.** THE SECOND RULE ON THE BOARD IN THE ONE ROOM IS CUT THE SAME LENGTH AS THE FIRST RULE AND IS BLANK. THE COUNT OF BLANKS ON THAT BOARD IS THIRTY-FIVE AND GOES ON THE RETURN AND NOT ON THE DAY, AND IT WAS THIRTY-FOUR AT THE RETURN BEFORE THIS ONE. NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS BEEN TOLD WHAT THE SECOND RULE IS FOR AND A RULE THAT CAME BACK EMPTY OPENS NOTHING. THIS IS A FACT OF WHAT IS ON A BOARD IN ONE ROOM THIS MORNING AND IT IS NOT A FORECAST AND IT IS NOT A CAUSE AND IT IS NOT A TOTAL.

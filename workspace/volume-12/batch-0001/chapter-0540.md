# Chapter 540

## A Letter A Day Old, And A Yard With Nobody In Charge Of It

The frost came out of the north end of the ditch in the night and there was water standing in the low corner of the yard at first light, thin enough to see the grit through the top of it. By the seventh hour the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full and stayed inside its own lip, and a bar dropped flat on the middle road rang off the ground instead of biting into it, That is the first time this winter the ground has taken a blow without ringing.

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate. Four people from the second hour until about the fifth, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone and put back wet on its frame, both culverts cleared of the winter's leaf and silt, and the water running in them by the time the work stopped. The pack under the near stone at the outlet end was lifted and squeezed and went back wet, which is what it does twice a week and has done for eleven years.

The letter has been on the long table one day and one night. It came up the fen road with the second bus yesterday and it is a council's own paper, of its own date, asking in writing that the schedule be restored, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board between it and the nearer of the two.

It has not been moved, it has not been answered, and nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it. There is no column for it in any of the four books and the clerk of this holding has not ruled one.

**"It came in at the ninth hour yesterday and I wrote the hour down and nothing else about it."** He said that to the letter and not to anybody, standing up, with his hand flat on it. **"A request that has been entered can be asked about in four counties. This one cannot be answered from this table by anybody in this holding, and so it is not a request and it is not going in a column."** He left his hand there a second and then took it away.

The clerk of this holding opened the second book at the first hour and wrote the line and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

**One thousand and sixty-two hundredweight.**

Tuesday came up eight and Wednesday gave five back, and what stands against the launder this morning is the three hundred and third figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The run does not care what day of a month it is and neither does the man who writes it, and this being the third morning of a month has not touched it.

The window gave a hundred and forty mornings one way against a hundred and thirty the other, and those two are two hundred and seventy. The two men at the long wall read both halves out loud at seven and one of them said the other's back to him, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

Three hundred and one days is what the sheet carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath it takes the three hundredth of those mornings out of three hundred and second. One under and one over. A letter that lay on a table overnight did not move a figure of a body of households, and no household in four counties has been asked this week to give up one of its two signatures.

Six hundred and eighty-seven days on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and thirty-four on the far one, forty-seven apart, and both of the two men who read them have read them every morning for longer than either of them would say.

The second book took **one hundred and twenty-two mornings of two hundred and twenty-seven**, of a month eight months back, at the seventh hour. Yesterday's line under that heading was empty and this morning's is not. The run is read on an odd morning and on no even one, because half of it is half a number on an even morning, and a morning with an empty line under that heading is not a morning with something wrong in it.

The man of about fifty came up the middle road with the barrow at about the sixth hour the way he comes up it, and he got as far as the low field above the road and stopped, and stood in the middle of the road with both hands on the handles for about ten minutes without pushing it any further. The frost has been out of the ground two nights and the track up that field is soft to the axle now.

There is a strip about a yard wide in that field, forty yards along from where he stands, where the grass has come up through ground that was bare a month back. There are nine places above that road where something is happening that a man with a barrow has no word for, and four off the middle road toward the tap, and there is no figure for any one of them on a page in this holding.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in at about the eighth hour and put her own book on the long table on the far side of the letter, and she did not touch the letter and she did not move the two sheets about the declinings.

**"I want to ask a question in the open air and I want it written on the page that I asked it."** She said that to the table. **"I have been in four counties of meetings for four years and I have got the hour of every person who has told me anything about that field this month, and I have not written one word about what it means. So I am going to ask, and then I am going to write down the day and the hour and the fact that it was asked, and I am not going to write down anybody's answer."**

She stood at the table with her hand flat on her own book.

**"Who is looking after it."**

She let that stand in the yard. Two people in that yard heard it and the rest of the yard was somewhere else at the time, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling heard it from the tap and did not turn round.

The man of about fifty said the barrow goes up there on a Thursday and that Thursday is tomorrow. Nia Vale said she goes past it and had said nothing about it. Tova Reed said it is not a sowing date and would not put one on it. Soren Rill said it is not his to hold.

The clerk of this holding said there is no column for it in any of the four books. That is five answers and not one of them was a name, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden wrote the day and the hour and the fact that the question was asked, in her own hand, and shut the book and took it away with her.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour with the road on her boots and put her own book on the long table face down and left her hand flat on the cover of it.

**"I have been past nine of them in nine days and I have said nothing to anybody in nine days and I am going to keep that up until the sowing is in."** She said that to her own hand and gave the reason before the thing. **"I am not going to say how many there are and I am not going to name one of them, and I am not going to put any of them in a book, because a place with a name in a book is a place somebody will be asked about, and I am not the person who can answer that."**

She did not open the book. Nobody in that yard asked her a second question and she went back out to the boundary an hour later, because that is what she does on a morning with nothing in it.

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and put them down on the middle table. The register form was on the middle table and she put hers beside it and not on top of it.

**"Three dates in my hand, and one council that wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing, and nothing since."** She said that to the table and gave the reason before the thing. **"A place above a road is not a sowing date. What came up in that ground belongs to whoever is standing in it at the time, and there is no column in any of my four counties that says which of them that is, and I am not going to invent one in a week when I have four counties to be in."**

She squared the four sheets against the edge of the table with two fingers and did not say anything else about them. Nobody at that table said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with a bar and a stone over his shoulder and stood at the tap for a while before he said anything.

**"I am on the wild-root side of this and I am not its officer, and nobody has made me, and I am not going to be."** He said that to the tap and gave the reason before the thing. **"I carry a bar and a stone and I go where the ground is not carrying. I have been up to the second bend twice this week and the water stands in lines in it that were not in it a month ago, and the line goes straight across the road under the metalling."**

**"If a person wants somebody to hold that, they want a man with a post behind him, and there is no post in this county for it, and I am not going to be the one who pretends there is."** He put the bar against the wall and left it standing there, which is where it lives.

Harlan Vetch came in off the middle road at about the seventh hour and worked the north wall for an hour and a half without saying anything to anybody.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and nothing in front of him, and at about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-one of Silling came in, put his bar back against the wall, and said his name.

**"Marek."** He said it the way a man says a thing he has not said in a yard before, and then he got on with it. **"There is water standing in the north end of the ditch and the ditch is running full and the ground took all of it, and this is the first morning this year the ground has taken it."**

That was the whole of what he came in to say. He went and stood with his back to the yard and looked at the north wall, and nobody asked him how he knew it and nobody asked him where he had got a name for the man who stands in this yard.

Four years ago there was no name for that man in this yard. There was the engineer of record, and there are nine of those in four counties, and the two words did the same work between them. Somebody used one of them this morning without noticing, and that is all that happened, and this page is not going to make a second thing out of it.

**"I heard my name in this yard this morning and I have not decided what I am going to do about it."** He said it to the open door and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"Somebody in this holding has stopped using a job to stand in for me, and I would like to know which of them it was, and I am not going to ask, and I am not going to use it as a reason to go out of the gate this afternoon either. A man is not what a yard calls him and a field is not what a man is called, and those were two separate facts before this morning."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour, walked out to the low field gate, stood at it about ten minutes looking through it and not through it, and came back in.

**"I have put this morning's date in a county book and nothing else about that place, and I am a witness to records and not a witness to a field."** She said that to the gatepost.

**"In about two years there is going to be a great many people who can say they were on a road near it, and one of them is going to step forward and say I saw it, and that is how a thing gets a name it has not earned. I am not going to be that one, and I have told four people on that road that I am not going to be that one."**

Nobody at that gate asked her for the sentence she was not going to write, and she got on the second bus.

The man of about fifty came down off the low field at about the eighth hour and went straight up the four-mile road with the barrow and came back down loaded, and the water in the ditch was running full the whole time.

That line is paid at twenty-six and not discharged in twenty-six, and it does not fall again this month. A hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the register form has been on the middle table, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused and it will not be read out a fifth time.

The charter was signed a hundred and three days ago in a room nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light, and neither of those two documents has an opinion about the other one.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ninety-one days old and has nothing on it, and nobody has offered him a pen for it. Under the heading for sessions this holding's book carries seven and nobody entered an eighth, and the rehearsal of nine minutes at a hand-lever nine miles off is not a session and is not entered as one. Both the requests column and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and the letter went into neither of them.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a place above a road is not a seventh. The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth. A barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor. The well house door is nine hundred yards off and the seventh of the seven columns cut in its stone is at sixty-four, and it was not read this week, and from sixty-four it does not go back.

It is eleven years since the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres was walked by anybody, and this week it was not walked and not measured and not crossed and not priced, and nobody in this holding has ever stood inside it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground and the two are not added to one another anywhere in this building.

Four ruled lines stand under two words in the corner of the one room and none of them has anything on it, and the answer they are for is not due, and nothing said in this yard this morning put a word on any of them. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has improved its heading. The sheet lying face up on the low board has no date on it and nowhere on it for a name to go.

On the shelf in the one room, behind the fourth book, is a foot and a half of day sheets. Not one of them carries a date at the head. They are stacked in the order it was used, no sheet has been brought to the top of the stack by anybody, and nobody in this holding has ever asked what they are.

A letter lay on the long table all of it with nobody in this county under it, and a woman asked in the open air who is looking after a place above a road, and five people in a yard each gave her one piece of work they were already doing and not one of them gave her the whole of it.

Winter in this county is not a season on a page. It is a length of a morning, and this one came out of the north end of the ditch in the night and let a bar ring off the middle road for the first time this winter, and the woman shut her book and took it away with her, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The third. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone and put back wet, the pack under the near stone lifted and put back wet. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and twenty-two mornings of two hundred and twenty-seven, of a month eight months back, and the line under that heading stood empty yesterday and is not empty this morning. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and sixty-two hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and third. A letter from a council four counties off lies on the long table and is not in the requests column and is not in the section-nine notes, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books in this holding.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

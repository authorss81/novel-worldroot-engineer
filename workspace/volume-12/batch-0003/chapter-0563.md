# Chapter 563

## A Stone Turned Over At The Outlet End, And A Morning With Nothing In The Book

Sleet came in off the water at about the fourth hour and went through it for about an hour and left the middle road with a skin of ice in the wheel ruts by six.

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet for the fourth morning this month.

Before the light was properly up the clerk of this holding had the line in the second book and left it there, and at the ninth hour he read that line back to himself once and no more than once.

**One thousand and one hundred and three hundredweight.**

Friday came up eight off Thursday and the launder carries the three hundred and twenty-sixth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

Off the long wall at seven, one of the two men saying and the other saying it back: a hundred and fifty-two mornings one way, a hundred and forty-one mornings the other, and two hundred and ninety-three is the two of them together.

Three hundred and twenty-four days is what the sheet gives the four bodies of households, and the clause printed beneath that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-third of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-fifth. One under and one over.

Seven hundred and ten on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and fifty-seven on the far one, forty-seven days between them wherever they are put, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut.

Under the heading where the run of mornings goes there is nothing in the second book this morning, and this page carries the reason and no figure, because half of that run is a half number on a Friday.

The clerk of this holding left the space empty. It was full at the seventh hour yesterday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun, and an empty space under that heading is not a morning with something wrong in it.

The near stone at the head of the outlet end was lying on its side when the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went to the tap at about the seventh hour, and it had been off its bed long enough for the pack under it to have been open to the weather, and nobody has worked out since when and nobody in this yard went and ask.

She put the stone back on its bed herself with both hands, working at it for about ten minutes in the sleet, and she did it before she filled any can, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came out of the one room at about the seventh hour and watched her do the last of it and then went and got the bar from the wall and levered it the last inch over.

Neither of them said anything about it to anybody and neither of them wrote it down.

The clerk of this holding came out at about the eighth hour and looked at the stone and then at the two of them.

**"That stone has not been off its bed since I came here and the bed it sits in was cut by a man who is dead."** He said it to the stone and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to write the date on it. I am not going to write a line in the fourth book about a stone that a woman of about thirty-eight turned over this morning."**

**"In four years there will be a page in this holding that says somebody maintained the outlet end on a Friday, and there is no column anywhere in four counties that would say who did it, and there is nobody in this yard who can tell me when it came off."**

He went back in and did not say anything else about it.

The man of about fifty was at the bench with the use log off the shelf and open at the last page and the pen lying on the bench beside it and not in his hand, and this is the first morning this month he has taken the book off that shelf at all.

He looked at it for a while and then he said the reason out loud before he said anything else, which is what he did on Sunday and it is a different reason.

**"I am not going to write the sixteenth line and I want that said first, before anybody in this yard thinks I came down here to think about it."**

He turned the book a few degrees so the people standing there could see that the last page was still the last page.

**"The stone is the reason. There is a stone at the head of that outlet end lying on its side with a pack open to the weather and there is nowhere in this holding for a person to write down that they have put a stone back."**

**"I have got a book with fifteen lines in it and every one of them is about the four-mile road, and this stone is not on the four-mile road, and if I put this morning in that book then I have made this book into a book about a yard, and that is the same mistake in the other direction."**

He set the pen down on the bench beside the open book and left it lying there.

**"So the stone gets put back by two people and nobody writes it anywhere, and in four years nobody in this district will know that the pack under that stone was open to the weather for four hours this morning."**

**"I have made my peace with that. I did not make it this morning, I made it about nine years ago when I found out nobody had ever told me what the book was for."**

Nobody in that yard said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and he put the book back under the weight on the shelf about ten minutes later with nothing added to it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden filled her two cans and a half of a third after the stone was back and did not take her own book out of her pocket at any hour of this morning, and she said one thing about the two of them having done it without speaking.

**"We did not agree on it. He was not there when I started it and I was not there when he finished it."**

She said that to the half can and gave the reason before the thing.

**"If two people agree to do a thing then it is a thing two people agreed to, and in a month somebody will ask them both separately and get two different accounts of an agreement that never happened. What actually happened is that I put a stone back and he came and levered it the last inch, and nobody said a word."**

**"That is a better thing than an agreement. It is just harder to put in a book."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour in the sleet with the road in her boots and stood under the pump house roof for a while without going in.

**"I have been past two of mine since Friday and I have not named either of them and I am not going to today."**

She said the reason first and printed nothing under it.

**"The first of the month is in five mornings and I have nine places above that road and four hours of daylight in them. If I spend today's four hours in a yard talking to people about not naming things, then in about a month I will have named none of them and been past five, and nobody will know the difference."**

She went out again at about the fifth hour and did not turn into the one room.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the sixth hour with the sleet going down the back of his coat and put his bar against the wall and said two things, one of which was about the ice in the ruts.

**"There is ice in the ruts of the middle road from about six and it will be there until the sun gets on it at about eleven."**

**"That is not a warning, it is a thing about a Friday. I have said what I came to say and I am not going to say it twice."**

He filled his can and went out again and the two people in the yard had no idea at all what the second thing was going to be.

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour on her way to the seed house with the four counties' sheets under her arm and stopped at the door frame with her hand flat on it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and I am going to see the man about the gate on Friday, and I have decided that in the last four days and I have not told anybody until this moment."**

She said it to the door frame and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A seed front that goes and looks at a gate is a thing four counties can repeat, and four counties repeating a thing is not the same as four counties agreeing about a thing, and I have spent four years learning that the difference is everything."**

**"I am going to say in that room that I came because a household asked me and not because anybody sent me, and I am not going to bring the sheets."**

No one said she was wrong and no one thanked her and nobody touched her arm.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap, and he came out at about the tenth hour after the ice had started to go off the ruts and stood in the yard in the sun with his sleeve down over his forearm.

**"I have been at that door frame since the fifth hour and I have not gone out of it, and the reason I have not is that there is a stone out there and a book shut on a bench and I would be the man in the yard explaining both of them."**

He gave the reason first and printed nothing under it, and this morning cost him no figure at all.

**"I am the one person in this county whose job is a measuring frame and a straight edge, and this morning the only two things in this yard that wanted one were a gate hinge and a page, and neither of them is nine miles away."**

**"So I stood in a doorway, and I am going to go on standing in doorways until somebody asks me to do something I have not already decided I cannot do."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the north wall from the third hour until six with the wind behind him, and there are four ruled lines in his own book and the second of them is two hundred and fourteen days old and has nothing on it, and he levered a stone at about seven with a bar and then did not look at his own book for the rest of the morning.

A hundred and twenty-six days ago the charter went through a door nine miles off. Nothing has been written on the register form in two hundred and twenty days and nothing has been written about refusing it either, and the column over it has no heading on it and is not going to be read out a fifth time.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. A man taking a book off a shelf and putting it back is not a session, seven are entered in this holding's book, and there was no eighth this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three. The letter on the long table is twenty-three days old, went into neither of them, and lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it where it lies.

A stone off its bed is not a seventh row. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit stands against any of them, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Fifteen lines is what the use log has this morning, and a stone at the head of an outlet end is not the sixteenth of them. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and eleven is where its journeys stand.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into its stone and the seventh of those columns stands at sixty-four. Nobody walked there this morning, because that column moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else, and the thirtieth of this month is in four days.

Twenty-eight fetchings is where the man of about seventy stands, and he was not fetched this morning and he will not be fetched tomorrow either. The first of the next month is his and it is also the twenty-ninth, and nobody has ever asked him which number he is.

Nobody in this holding has ever stood in the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody walked it this month and nobody measured it. The ground that came up on the low field above the middle road is a separate piece of country from that ring, and not one figure in these four books is a figure of both.

Four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has touched what stands over it. A sheet lies face up on the low board with no date on it and nowhere on it for a name to go.

Ice in the ruts of the middle road from six until about eleven, and a stone put back on its bed by two people who did not arrange it, and a sleet that came off the water in the fourth hour and went through in an hour, and nobody thanked anybody.

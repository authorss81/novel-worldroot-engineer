# Chapter 560

## The Tap At Seven, And A Book That Went Into A Pocket

Frost was out of the top two inches of the middle road by six and the wind was light and off the water, and by seven the sun was on the yard and the standing water in the low corner had gone down about an inch since Sunday. The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the gate onto the low field.

The clerk of this holding wrote the line at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth.

**One thousand and ninety-two hundredweight.**

Monday gave five back and the three hundred and twenty-third figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries this morning.

The man of about fifty read the falling half off the long wall on his way out with the barrow and said it to nobody at all.

**"A hundred and forty mornings the other."** The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said the rising half back at him from the tap.

**"A hundred and fifty mornings one way, and the two of them together are two hundred and ninety."**

That is the way those two numbers have been said between them for eleven years, and neither of them has ever looked at the other one while it was said.

The tap runs at its own pace and not at the pace of the standpipe behind it, and that is a thing about the pipe under the yard and not about the water. She filled two cans and got half of a third and left the tap where it was, and she said the reason out loud before she said the thing, the way she has said a reason first four mornings running now.

**"That book has been open in this yard every morning since the twelfth and I have been carrying it round with me, and I am not doing that today."**

She put it in the inside pocket of her coat and shut the flap over it.

**"I put a figure in that column on the twelfth and it has no unit against it and no meaning against it. Every morning since then somebody in this yard has walked past me with a piece of their own, and I have been the one holding a book open like a person waiting to be asked about it."**

She set the two full cans on the step and looked at the half one.

**"A figure with nothing under it is not an account of anything and carrying it about is not going to make it one. It is a line somebody wrote because a man said a number out loud and she wanted proof of it, and I have had eleven years of wanting proof of things."**

**"I am not going to start at forty-eight."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the direct road at about the seventh hour and said the number out loud on his way to the north wall, the same number, in the same voice, at the same pitch, four steps into the one room with the door open, and she did not write it and he did not stop and nobody said anything about either of those two things.

**"I wrote it once and I am not writing it twice."**

She said it to the half can and not to him.

**"The first time is a thing that happened on the twelfth and I put it in because I had been ashamed of my own book for a fortnight."**

**"The second time would be a habit, and I would rather have a column with one figure in it and no excuse under it than a column with two figures and a reason why there were only two."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with his bar over his shoulder and put it against the wall where it lives and said one sentence about the ditch, which was that it is running clean at the north end for the first time since the wheel went back on the ninth, and then he filled his can and went out again without saying anything else.

The man of about fifty went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour with the barrow and came back down loaded at about the ninth, forty yards past the place where he stops, and the rut he came down in did not hold water this morning because there is not enough water left in that ground to fill it.

He put the barrow away under the pump house roof and stood with his hand on it and said one thing to nobody, and what he said was that he has got about four mornings of track left in him before the barrow is going into ground it cannot come back out of, and that he is not going to be the man who decides which four those are.

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour on her way out to the boundary, read both boards standing up, and did not go into the one room.

**"There is a first of the month in eight mornings and I have got nine places above that road to be in and no way of being in them all, and I have not told anybody which two I am going back to."**

She gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it.

**"A list that a person shows you is a list you have to argue with, and I have not got the hours to argue and I have not got anybody to argue with."**

**"I am going to go and come back, and that is all this yard is going to get out of me until the sowing is in."**

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour on her way to the seed house and stopped at the low board, and she had the four counties' sheets under her arm and the page from the household book still in the inner pocket of her own book.

**"A household four miles off has asked me to come and look at a gate."**

She said that to the board and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to say yes and I am not going to say no this morning, because the moment I go and look at a gate then a seed front has looked at a gate, and then there is a woman in four counties who will say that the seed front came."**

**"Then I will be doing it about nine times a year for the rest of my working life."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and she went on to the seed house with the sheets still under her arm.

The letter on the long table is twenty days old this morning.

It came up the fen road at the ninth hour of the second of this month, and the count this holding keeps is complete days lying, not counting the day it arrived and not counting the part of the morning it is lying in.

It lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been moved and it has not been answered, and no column has been ruled for it in any of the four books in this yard.

At the seventh hour the second book took the run under the heading where it goes, and it is a Tuesday and an odd morning and the run is read on an odd morning, of a month eight months back.

**One hundred and thirty-two mornings of two hundred and forty-seven.**

The clerk of this holding stood in the one room door while it was read and did not come out. The clerk of this holding came out at about the ninth hour and said the age of the letter out loud to the table, because he does that, and nobody asked him to.

**"Twenty days, and I have not been asked once in twenty days what I have done about it, and that is the second time in a month that has happened to me."**

He squared the letter with the edge of the table, which is a thing he has done about ten times now, and it did not move.

**"The first one was a form. This is the second thing in this room that nobody has asked me about."**

**"I wrote the hour it came in and nothing else about it. In four years somebody is going to come and look at that table and the hour is going to be the only thing on it, and that is not a decision I made. It is what happens when a man is not asked anything for twenty days."**

Four bodies of households are shown on that sheet at three hundred and twenty-one days, and the clause printed under the figure takes the three hundred and twentieth of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-second. One under and one over. It has not been added to the launder and it is not going to be.

The near board of the long wall says seven hundred and seven days and the far board says seven hundred and fifty-four, and the whole distance between those two figures is forty-seven days, and the drawer behind the near one has not been touched at any hour this morning.

There is a form on the middle table that has been there two hundred and seventeen days with nothing at the head of its column and nothing in it, and nobody has filled it, nobody has refused it, and nobody is going to read it out a fifth time.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and eleven days old and has nothing on it. He was in the yard all morning and did not go over to the middle table and did not go near the long one either.

A charter went through a door nine miles off a hundred and twenty-three days ago, and of the four bodies of households that put a second signature to it, three are still signed to a machine for stored light and the fourth sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. It fell on its own interval on Friday and the interval will not bring it round again inside this half of the month, and nobody in this holding has anything to do with that and never has.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book. A woman putting a book in a pocket is not a session and an eighth was not entered this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three, and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month, which is the last thing either of those two columns has had put in it since.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and not one unit has ever been written against any of them. A figure with no unit under it is in a woman's own book and is not one of these six and the seventh row is not ruled.

Fifteen lines stand in the use log and the sixteenth is not among them, and the man of about fifty has said out loud in this yard why he is not going to put it there and does not owe anybody a second reason.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone of it and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else, and the thirtieth of this month is in seven mornings, and nobody has walked those nine hundred yards to read it.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings, and this morning is not one of them and neither is the morning after it. The first of the next month is his and it is also the twenty-ninth, and nobody here has ever put that to him as a question.

Nobody in this holding has ever stood inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody was inside it this morning and nobody measured it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground from that ring and no page in these four books puts the two of them into one sum.

Two words stand in the corner of the one room with four ruled lines under them and every one of those lines is bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has improved the heading over it. The sheet on the low board lies face up and undated with no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Marek had the one room to himself from the fifth hour with the door standing open, and neither the middle road nor the gate outside it got him out of it, and at about the tenth hour he came out and stood in the sun in the yard.

**"A woman has put a book in a pocket and a man has said he has got four mornings of track left in him, and those are two different sentences about the same week."**

He gave the reason before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I have spent nine years watching people in this county decide what to write down. This is the first morning in a while where the decision was about carrying something rather than about putting something, and that is a different kind of tiredness and it is not a smaller one."**

He got his sleeve back over his forearm and left it there.

**"What comes after a sowing is not nine places above a road. It is a gate that will not go back on its frame because the pin has moved in the winter, and a heap nobody has opened, and a man with a barrow who will have to decide four mornings this month."**

**"Nobody in four counties is going to write down any of that either, and I am not going to ask them to."**

Stone in the mouth of the pack at the outlet end, wet in the middle of the morning and dry on the top, and the sun on the yard at seven and the low corner an inch down since Sunday, and a woman's book shut in a pocket, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The third. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the gate onto the low field, the gate lifted off its frame with a bar and a stone and put back on. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and thirty-two mornings of two hundred and forty-seven, of a month eight months back, the frame standing where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and ninety-two hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and twenty-third. A letter twenty days old on the long table with nothing at the head of a column for it in any of the four books in this holding and the hour it arrived written on it and nothing else.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

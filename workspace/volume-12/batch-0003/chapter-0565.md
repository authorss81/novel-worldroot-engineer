# Chapter 565

## Four Up The Bank In The Dark, And One Of Them Was At The Tap On Friday

Rain came in off the water in the night and had not stopped by the second hour, and the wind had gone back round to the north-east, and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark for the sixth time in twenty-four days.

At about the second hour in that rain four people went up to the headland and found the boundary stones by hand, one after another, for about two hundred yards along the top of the bank, and cut the ditch along the eleven acres out, and barrowed the loose stone off the top into the heap by the gate, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet.

One of the four was the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who had been at the tap at the fourth hour on Friday and had said in that yard that she and the man of about thirty-one of Silling had put a stone back without arranging it.

Nobody took a field. That is the ninth time this year that this line has gone up the bank in the dark and come back down with an empty taking column, and there is nobody in this holding to blame for a field that has waited four years.

A hundred and twenty-one mornings in force is the standing, with twenty-nine taken and ninety-two not, and twenty-nine and ninety-two is a hundred and twenty-one. Twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and did not move yesterday and did not move on any of the mornings between them.

Counted one way, the twenty-eighth of this month is the sixty-ninth morning of the rotation. Counted the other way it is the fifty-sixth. Both of them go on this page, because a page carrying one of them carries half a fact.

There has been thirteen in that gap for four years and nobody in this building has worked out what the thirteen counts and nobody asked this week either, and asking is the one thing anybody in this yard could do about it in about a minute.

**One thousand and one hundred and six hundredweight.**

Sunday answered Saturday with eight of its own, and what the launder carries this morning is the three hundred and twenty-eighth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

At seven the two men at the long wall had a hundred and fifty-three mornings one way and a hundred and forty-two mornings the other, and together they make two hundred and ninety-five.

The four bodies of households stand at three hundred and twenty-six days on the sheet in this yard, and the clause sitting under that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-fifth of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-seventh. One under and one over.

On the two boards of the long wall, seven hundred and twelve days on the near and seven hundred and fifty-nine on the far, forty-seven days apart, and the drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour it rained.

The heading where the run of mornings goes is empty in the second book this morning, and this page carries the reason and carries no figure, because half of that run is a half number on a Sunday.

It was full at the seventh hour on Saturday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has done that since the day the run was begun. The clerk of this holding left the space the way he found it and he did not put a half number in it.

The four came down off the bank at about the fifth hour with their coats wet to the waist and went straight in, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was the last of the four in and she stood in the one room doorway dripping on the boards for about a minute and then went to change.

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the fifth hour with a hood up and she watched the four come down and she said one thing about it, out loud, to nobody, and the reason came first.

**"The fourth line has gone up that bank in the dark six times in twenty-four days and I have been in this yard for most of them."**

She gave the reason before the thing and left the figure out of it.

**"Nobody has asked that woman to go and she went, and that is not a thing this district knows how to hold. If somebody in four counties asks me in a month who has been carrying what came back, and I say her name, then in a year that will be the only sentence anybody has about her, and she went up a bank in the rain at two in the morning because the rota said so and not because anybody asked her."**

**"I am not going to be the one who starts that sentence."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came back down at about the seventh hour in different clothes and stood at the tap, and she said one thing about the bank and then one thing about her own book.

**"I went because the line came round and there were four of us going and I was one of the four."**

She said that to the tap and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I have spent a fortnight deciding what to write down and this morning I was too wet to think about it. That is the whole of the reason. There is nothing in it about the ground and there is nothing in it about what came back in the ninth."**

**"And I have not written a word in that book about this morning and I am not going to, and it is going to have a second empty space under the one figure in it, and I want that said out loud so that nobody in this yard thinks I have been getting slack."**

She filled two cans and got about half of a third, the same as she has got every morning this month.

The man of about fifty was in the yard at about the sixth hour with the barrow out and the wheel chocked and did not go up the four-mile road at the seventh hour on this Sunday either, and he did not go at the eighth hour, and four people in that yard noticed and none of them said anything about it.

At about the ninth hour he did one thing that he has not done in this month, which is that he carried the barrow down to the head of the road and stood at the trough and looked at the water in it for about ten minutes without doing anything at all, and then he wheeled it back up and put it under the roof.

Soren Rill came in about the ninth hour from the four-mile road and stood at the wall where his bar lives with the rain going down the back of his coat and said nothing at all about the road, which is the third morning this week he has come into that yard and said nothing about it.

**"I said on Thursday that the wet had not come back and that Thursday would be the last time I said it, and I am holding to that."**

**"That is all I came in to say. I am not going to add to it every morning until somebody in this yard thinks there is more."**

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room at about the tenth hour with the second book under his arm and stood in the middle of the yard in the rain and said one thing, which was about the rain and about a stone.

**"The pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet and it has done that four mornings this month and it will do it on Tuesday, and on Tuesday it will be the thirtieth of this month and that is a day about a column nine hundred yards off and not a day about a pack."**

That went out to the middle of the yard, and the reason came before it.

**"I am not joining those two things together. There is a column cut in a door that says what I am to read, and there is a man of about thirty-one with a bar in the rain at seven in the morning, and I have been in rooms where two things got joined together on a table and the table was wrong in the middle."**

Tova Reed came in off the fen road at about the eighth hour with the four counties' sheets in a plastic sheet against the rain and stood at the low board with her back against it.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and I am going to a household four miles off on Friday, and I said that out loud in this yard on Saturday and people in this yard heard me say it."**

She put it to the board and gave the reason before the thing, the way she does.

**"I am not going to take it back and I am not going to be talked out of it by anybody, including anybody who is worried about what four counties will do with it, because four counties will do exactly what they like with it and that was true before I said anything and it will be true after."**

**"What I am not going to do is write down what I find, and there is going to be a gate and me and a page in a pocket and nobody is going to know afterwards what any of it was worth."**

Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and the plastic sheet with the four counties' sheets in it was put down on the step and not taken into the seed house.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and did not go up to the headland in that rain and did not go to the machine house, and he had all of it through the gap.

**"Four people went up a bank in the dark in the rain an hour ago and one of them keeps a book she has deliberately not written in, and a man with a barrow has not gone up a road for three mornings and nobody has asked him why."**

He said that to the shut third book and gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"Those are three things that are not one thing, and they are the same size as each other, which is a thing that has never been true in this holding before and which I have got no word for at all."**

His sleeve went back down over his forearm.

**"I am not going to make a word for it. In nine years every time somebody has made a word for a shape like that, the word has gone into a room and come out with the shape gone."**

The letter on the long table is twenty-five days old this morning. It went into neither the requests column nor the section-nine notes at the ninth hour of the second of this month, and those two are at fifty-three and fifty-three and have not been added together on any morning of this month.

It has not been answered and it has not been refused and it has not been copied and it has not been moved off the long table, and it lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it between it and the two sheets about the two declinings.

Four bodies of households signed a charter a hundred and twenty-eight days ago in a room nine miles off and three of them are still signed to a machine for stored light. The register form has been on the middle table for two hundred and twenty-two days with nothing at the head of its column and nothing in it and nobody has said a word in either direction about it.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and sixteen days old and has nothing on it, and he went up a bank in the rain in the dark at two in the morning and then went and changed his clothes and did not look at it.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven and it will not fall again inside this half of the month at all. Four people going up a bank in the dark is not a session, seven are entered in this holding's book, and there is no eighth.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and not one unit has ever been written against any of them. A woman on a bank in the rain and a second empty space in a column of her own are not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log is at fifteen lines and took neither a sixteenth nor a seventeenth this morning. The barrow did not go up the four-mile road at all, because the man who takes it up stood looking into the trough at its head instead, and it is where it was on Friday with its journeys at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door and seven columns are cut in the stone of it. Sixty-four is the figure in the seventh of those and nobody has gone to read it this month and it stands where it stood on the twelfth until the first of a month or the thirtieth arrives, and the thirtieth of this month is in two mornings.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and this morning is not one of them. His next is the first of the next month, and that is also the twenty-ninth, and no one in this holding has ever put that to him as a question.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres went this week the way it has gone for eleven years, which is that nobody has been in it, nobody has measured it, and no figure of it exists anywhere in this building. The strip that came up on the low field above the middle road is on the far side of a road from that ring, and nothing in these four books puts the ring and the field on one line together.

Under two words in the corner of the one room there are four ruled lines and every one of those lines is bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved by anybody. The sheet on the low board is face up and undated with no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Ditch running clean in the rain at the ninth hour with the heap of stone by the gate a foot higher than it was on Wednesday and the ditch running clean in it, and a woman with a second empty space under the one figure in her own column and no apology for it to anybody, and nobody thanked anybody.

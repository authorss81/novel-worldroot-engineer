# Chapter 562

## The Fifty-Third Time, And A Rule Cut The Same Length As The One Above It

There was an hour of hard dry light in the middle of the morning and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame.

The frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe was running full by the fourth hour and stayed inside its own lip, which it has done every morning this month and did not do any morning in the four before it.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book before the light came and read it back once, at the ninth hour, and read it back once only.

**One thousand and ninety-five hundredweight.**

Five back off Wednesday, and the figure standing against the launder this morning is the three hundred and twenty-fifth of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

At seven the falling half came off the long wall at a hundred and forty-one mornings and the rising half came off it at a hundred and fifty-one mornings, and two hundred and ninety-two is what the two of them are together.

A sheet in this building carries three hundred and twenty-three days against the four bodies of households, and the clause printed under that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-second of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-fourth. One under and one over.

The near board of the long wall says seven hundred and nine and the far board says seven hundred and fifty-six, and there are forty-seven days between those two numbers, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

At the third hour something came up the fen road and stood on the steelyard for a quarter of an hour without anything being taken off it, and then went back down the fen road the way it had come.

It came off the steelyard at five hundred and thirty-six hundredweight, which is eleven above the last one, and the two halves were reconciled twice at the boards and were not added, and the pair on the ticket is the forty-third pair, being two names written side by side in one hand.

The ordinal is not a number this holding carried forward from the last time. It is nine added to the morning before that one, every time, for four years, and a man who carries an ordinal forward is a man who eventually writes down a return that never came.

Two rules are cut into the board in the one room. The first of them runs full to the bottom of the paper with the returns on it. The other was cut the same length at the same time by the same hand and has had nothing on it on every one of the fifty-three mornings this return has come.

Thirty-seven is the number of mornings that second rule has now been counted empty, and it is thirty-seven and not thirty-eight, because the count is kept against the return and not against the day.

The clerk of this holding said it out loud at about the sixth hour with his finger on the empty rule and not touching it, and he said it because he has said it on every return for four years and he is not going to stop saying it now.

**"It has been blank every one of those times and there is a date at the top of it now from Sunday and the date has not made it into anything."**

He said that to the board, standing up.

**"Nobody in this holding has been told what that rule is for, and the day somebody is told is the day it gets used, and I have watched two rules in my life get used and neither of them was cut the same length as the one above it."**

**"A rule that came back empty opens nothing, and I have said that at six o'clock on fifty-three mornings and I am not going to stop saying it on the four hundredth."**

The man of about fifty was in the one room doorway at about the sixth hour when he said it, and he had come in to ask the man of about thirty-one of Silling something about the north wall, and he stayed where he was for the whole of it and did not go and ask it.

He said one thing afterwards, at the bench, to nobody.

**"Two books in this yard and two boards in that room, and I have asked about one of them and he has said he does not know, and he has just said out loud that he has never been told what the other one is for either."**

He put his hand on the barrow handles.

**"That is not the same answer and I am not going to pretend it is. His is a man who has been here eleven years and was never asked. Mine is a man who has been here nine years and never asked. He is not a warning to me and I am not going to take him as one, and I would be sorry for him if I could work out how."**

**"The one thing I have got out of it is that there are two of us in this holding keeping a book that nobody has ever told us the purpose of, and neither of us is going to be told, and there is no third person in this district who has authority to tell us."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the yard at the fourth hour and saw the whole of the return go up the fen road and back down again and she did not take her book out of her pocket at any hour of it.

**"I have watched that cart come up this road and go back down it four times since the twelfth and I have written nothing about any of them and I am not going to write anything about this one."**

She said that to the wall and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A thing that has happened forty-three times is not an event. The first time it happens somebody ought to write something, and the fortieth time it happens the only thing anybody would write is the number of the time, and that number is a fact about this building's attention and not about the cart."**

**"I have one figure in that column and I would like it to stay one figure, because a column with one figure in it is a column somebody has been careful with, and a column with forty figures in it is a column somebody has been keeping count."**

Soren Rill came in about the ninth hour off the four-mile road and stood at the wall where his bar lives and said one thing about the second bend, and then said a second thing that was not about the second bend.

**"I have got a thing to say and it is not about the road and I am going to say it once and then I am going to leave it alone."**

He turned the bar over once in his hands.

**"The wall has not got any standing in any book I have seen. I have been on it since I was a boy and my father was on it before me."**

He put the bar back against the wall it lives on.

**"It is not in the fourth book and it is not in the one the clerk keeps. I am not asking anybody to rule a column for it."**

**"I am saying it out loud in a yard because it is the last day this week that any of us are going to be standing about in one place, and then I am going back to the four-mile road."**

Nobody asked him a question about it and nobody ruled anything and nobody thanked him.

Tova Reed came in off the fen road at about the eighth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and stood at the low board for a while without saying anything.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and a household four miles off asked me to look at a gate, and I have still not said yes and I have still not said no, and I have been in four counties of meetings for four years."**

That was said to the board, and the reason came before it.

**"The only thing I have learned in four years of those meetings is that a decision somebody has not made is worth more in a room than a decision somebody has made badly, and a decision somebody has made badly is what I would hand them if I went and looked at that gate on Friday."**

Nobody told her she was wrong, nobody thanked her, and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way out, stopped at the boards, and said one thing about the fence and the heap and then one thing about the book, in that order.

**"The fence that came back in the night on the ninth has been standing for a fortnight with nobody's name on it and nobody's list, and I have walked past it every morning and it is still standing."**

The reason came before the thing and no figure went with it.

**"It is not mine and it is not one of nine places, and it is a fence, and the reason I am saying it out loud is that in about a month somebody is going to ask me whether what came back in this district belongs to anybody, and I want the answer I give to be one I have said in a yard before I have said it in a room."**

**"And the answer is that a fence that came back has a length and a date and two posts, and none of those three things is a person, and there is nobody in four counties whose job it is to hold any of them."**

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap and did not go out of the doorway for any of it.

**"A return came up a road and went back down it, and a rule that has been cut the same length as the one above it was empty for the fifty-third time, and a man with a bar has just told a yard he has never asked what it is for."**

That went to the shut third book through an open door, with the reason first and no figure at all under it, so that nothing this morning came off his own count.

**"Those are three things and they are on three different pieces of paper in three different parts of this holding and everybody standing in that yard has heard about all three in one morning."**

He pushed his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"I am not going to write the page that puts them together. I have watched two counties try to make one page out of three things of that kind and in both cases the page was wrong in the middle, and the wrongness was never at the edges. It was always in the middle, where the sentence that joined them up had to go."**

A hundred and twenty-five days ago four bodies of households put a second signature to a charter in a room nine miles off, and three of those four are still signed to a machine for stored light and the fourth has sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

Two hundred and nineteen days is how long that form has lain on the middle table, its column headed nothing, its inside nothing, and it has been neither filled nor refused and will not be read out a fifth time.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and thirteen days old with nothing on it. He was the one who got the cart back onto the plates at the third hour and he did not look at the board on his way out.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and the interval that carries it does not land again inside this half of the month and nobody in this holding has anything to do with that.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. A cart standing on a steelyard for a quarter of an hour is not a session and nobody entered an eighth.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and neither took anything this morning. The letter went into neither of them on the ninth hour of the second of this month. It is twenty-two days old, it is still face up on the long table between the two sheets about the two declinings, and about nine inches of bare board is on either side of it where it lies.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them, and there is no seventh row. A return that has come fifty-three times is not a seventh and a rule that came back empty is not a seventh and a wall that is in no book is not a seventh.

The use log went back under its weight on the shelf with fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut in its stone and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It has not been read this month and it does not go back from sixty-four, and it moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning and will not be fetched tomorrow either. The first of the next month is his and it is also the twenty-ninth, and no one in this holding has ever put that to him as a question.

Nobody walked the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres this month and nobody measured it and nobody in this holding has ever stood inside it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground altogether and no page in these four books adds it to that ring.

Under two words in the corner of the one room there are four ruled lines, and every one of those lines is bare, and the answer they are for is not due.

The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved by anybody and no sixth term has been written. The sheet on the low board lies face up and undated and there is no line anywhere on it that a name could go into.

Sun on the yard from seven and the frame up by a quarter past two and the standpipe full and inside its lip, and a cart that went up a road and came back down it with two names on a ticket written side by side in one hand, and a rule that has been empty fifty-three times, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the sixth hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The first. The first line of the rota worked, the pump house and the frame, the frame down about five minutes and up about three, the standpipe full and inside its lip. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour, one hundred and thirty-three mornings of two hundred and forty-nine, of a month eight months back, that frame having stood where it has stood since the first of this month. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and ninety-five hundredweight to the launder, being the three hundred and twenty-fifth. A LOAD CAME UP THE FEN ROAD AT THE THIRD HOUR, STOOD QUARTER OF AN HOUR AND WENT BACK THE WAY IT CAME. IT CAME OFF THE STEELYARD AT FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX HUNDREDWEIGHT, ELEVEN ABOVE THE LAST, THE FORTY-THIRD PAIR, TWO NAMES IN ONE HAND, THE TWO HALVES RECONCILED TWICE AND NOT ADDED, THE ORDINAL BEING NINE ADDED TO THE MORNING BEFORE AND NOT CARRIED FORWARD. THE SECOND RULE ON THE BOARD IN THE ONE ROOM IS CUT THE SAME LENGTH AS THE FIRST AND IS BLANK, AND THE COUNT OF BLANKS ON THAT BOARD IS THIRTY-SEVEN AND GOES ON THE RETURN AND NOT ON THE DAY.** A rule that came back empty opens nothing. This is a fact of a board and a book in one room on one morning and it is not a forecast and it is not a cause and it is not a total.

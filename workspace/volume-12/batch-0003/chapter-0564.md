# Chapter 564

## A Road Walked Alone, And A Column Deliberately Left Short

The wind went round to the south-west in the night and came up warm off the water, and the frost was out of the ground by about six all over the middle road, and the sun was on the yard before seven.

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the gate onto the low field, and the gate came off its frame on a bar and a stone and went back onto it.

The pin on that gate has moved about half an inch in the winter and the gate now sits with its own weight off true, and it went back on and it shut, and everybody in this yard knows it will not shut the same way again once somebody wants it to shut twice.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open before the sun was on the yard, put the figure in, and read it back once at the ninth hour.

**One thousand and ninety-eight hundredweight.**

Saturday gave five back off Friday and the three hundred and twenty-seventh figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what stands against the launder this morning.

At the seventh hour the second book took the run under the heading where it goes, and a Saturday is an odd morning and this is a run that gets read on odd mornings, and the frame on it has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and thirty-four mornings of two hundred and fifty-one.**

One of the two men at the long wall read a hundred and fifty-two mornings one way and the other gave him a hundred and forty-two mornings the other way, and together those two make two hundred and ninety-four.

The four bodies of households are entered at three hundred and twenty-five days, and the clause under that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-fourth of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-sixth. One under and one over.

Seven hundred and eleven days on the near board of the long wall this morning and seven hundred and fifty-eight on the far one, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of it.

Soren Rill went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with the bar over his shoulder and did not take the barrow, and the man of about fifty watched him go out of the first gate from under the pump house roof.

The man of about fifty did not go up the four-mile road at the seventh hour on this Saturday, and he did not go up it at the eighth hour either, and he was in the yard most of the day, and nobody asked him about it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden saw Soren Rill go out and saw the barrow stand under the roof and she made a decision about her own book at about the eighth hour and told nobody what it was until about the eleventh.

**"He went up that road alone this morning and he did not take the barrow and he did not tell this yard he was going."**

She said it to the tap and gave the reason before the thing.

**"If I had written it in that book this morning then that book would have a second figure in it, and the second figure would be a man going up a road on a Saturday, and in four years the two figures in that column would sit side by side and somebody would read them as two things of a kind."**

**"One of them is a number a man said out loud going into a room. The other one is a man carrying a bar up a hill. They are not two of anything. They are two things that happened near each other and I am not going to be the one who puts them in a line."**

**"The column stays short. That is not an accident and I am not going to let anybody read the empty space under it as me forgetting."**

Soren Rill came back in at about the eleventh hour and put the bar against the wall and said two things about the second bend and one of them was the same sentence he said on Thursday.

**"The wet has not come back and I am not going to keep saying it, so this is the last time I say it."**

**"I went up as far as the third bend today and I did not go past the third bend. There is nothing wrong past the third bend and there is nothing right past it either, and I am not going to keep walking ground just because I walked it last week."**

**"That is the whole of what I came in to say and it is about my own legs and not about the ground."**

The man of about fifty heard that at the bench and said nothing about it and went back to the barrow, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden heard it from the tap and said nothing about it either.

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour on her way out and stood under the pump house roof with the warm coming off the water behind her and said one thing, and she had not said two things out loud in this yard in one morning since the Thursday before last.

**"I went back to two of them yesterday and I did not name either and I am not going to name either, and I have been up since the fourth hour and the ground above that road is not the same ground as it was a fortnight ago."**

The reason came out first, as it has all week, and there was no figure in it.

**"It is not better and it is not worse and I want that said first, because a woman comes back from a place and says it is better and everybody hears that as good news, and it is not good news and it is not bad news. It is a thing that is happening to ground while a sowing is in."**

**"I am not going to put that in a room either. I have got four hours of daylight in it and I am going to spend them looking, and that is the whole of what I am doing this month and it does not add up to anything anybody can carry."**

Tova Reed came down the fen road at about the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and the page from the household book still in the inner pocket of her own book, and she stood at the low board and said one thing about Friday.

**"I am going to a household four miles off on Friday to look at a gate, and I have said so out loud in this yard now, which means that in about a month, if it comes up anywhere, it came from me and not from them."**

She said it to the board and gave the reason before the thing.

**"That is the price of saying a thing in a yard and I knew the price when I said it. I am not going to pretend I have been let into it or that anybody owed me for hearing it."**

**"And I am not going to put the gate in a column in any of these four books, and if the household writes it down themselves then it is their page and not mine and that is how it should be."**

Nobody contradicted her, nobody thanked her, and nobody laid a hand on her arm.

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles and went straight to the north wall and worked it until six with his back to everybody, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was on it with him from the third hour until six.

Marek was in the one room from the sixth hour with the door open and the second book on the table in front of him and nothing else in his hands, and he came out at about the tenth hour and stood in the yard where the sun was on the boards.

**"A man went up a road alone this morning without telling anybody, and a woman with a book watched him go and left her own column short on purpose."**

The reason came first and he left the figure out of it, and nothing this morning is a number of his.

**"I have spent nine years arranging for things to be written down at the moment they happen, and I have never once in nine years watched somebody leave a space in a column on purpose and say so out loud in a yard."**

He pulled his sleeve over his forearm and left it there.

**"That is the first thing anybody in this district has done in three weeks that I would call a skill rather than a decision, and I am not going to say so again and I am not going to say it to her either."**

The letter on the long table is twenty-four days old this morning, and it went into neither the requests column nor the section-nine notes at the ninth hour of the second of this month, and both of those are at fifty-three and have been at fifty-three on every morning of this month.

It lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been moved, it has not been answered, it has not been copied, and nobody in this holding has been asked to answer it.

Nobody in this yard counted the days on it out loud this morning, which is the fourth morning running that nobody has, and the clerk of this holding did not count it either.

A hundred and twenty-seven days ago the charter went through a door nine miles off. The register form has been on the middle table for two hundred and twenty-one days with no heading over its column, and it is neither filled nor refused and it is not going to be read out a fifth time.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and fifteen days old with nothing on it. He was on the north wall from the third hour until six and nobody offered him a pen for it at any hour.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. It fell on its interval on the nineteenth of this month and the next fall is not inside this half of the month at all, and nobody in this holding had anything to do with it.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. A man going up a road on his own is not a session and nobody entered an eighth this morning.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit has ever been written against any of them. A road walked by one man and a column left short on purpose are two more things that are not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and it did not take a sixteenth this morning, and it did not take one on Thursday either, and the man who keeps it has now given his reason out loud three times and has not moved an inch on the page.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor. It did not go up the road empty with a man on it this morning and that is not on any sheet in this holding.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone of it and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else. The thirtieth of this month is in three days and the first of the next is in four mornings, and nobody has walked those nine hundred yards this month to look at it.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning. The first of the next month is his and it is also the twenty-ninth, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites.

Nobody in this holding has ever stood inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody walked it this month and nobody measured it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is on different ground from that ring and no page in these four books puts the two of them together.

Four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. The sheet on the low board lies face up and undated and there is no line anywhere on it that a name could go into.

Warm off the water behind the north wall at eight in the morning and the gate on the low field shut about half an inch off true for the first time in this holding's winter, and a column in a woman's pocket with nothing added to it on purpose, and nobody thanked anybody.

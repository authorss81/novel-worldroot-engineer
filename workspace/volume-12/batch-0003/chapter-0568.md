# Chapter 568

## The First Of A Month, And Three Things That Land Together And Nobody Adds A Fourth

The month turned in the night and nothing turned with it. The frost was out of the middle road by six and the sun was on the yard at seven with a light wind off the water behind it, and there was no standing anywhere in this holding that had gone back to where it was a month ago because a month went by.

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the two culverts on the middle road and the gate onto the low field, and the gate came off its frame on a bar and a stone and would not go back on, and it is still standing open at nine.

The clerk of this holding had the figure in the second book by the first hour and read it back once at the ninth.

**One thousand and one hundred and four hundredweight.**

Wednesday gave five back off Tuesday, and what stands against the launder this morning is the three hundred and thirty-first figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice, and that run has gone over a round hundred and back down five and it has not stopped for the turn.

The four bodies of households are shown at three hundred and twenty-nine days, with the clause printed under that number taking the three hundred and twenty-eighth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirtieth. One under and one over.

On the long wall this morning the near board says seven hundred and fifteen days and the far board says seven hundred and sixty-two, forty-seven days apart, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

At the seventh hour the second book took the run under the heading where it goes, and a Wednesday is an odd morning and that run is read on odd mornings.

**One hundred and thirty-six mornings of two hundred and fifty-five, of a month nine months back.**

The frame on that line has moved. It stood of a month eight months back on every morning of this month and it stands of a month nine months back from today, and it moves because the month turned and not because anybody in this holding decided anything about it.

The clerk of this holding said that once, at about the seventh hour, to the open book, and he said it in the ordinary way of a man reading a heading.

**"That is the only frame in the four books in this yard that has moved today. The turn did not reset anything else and nothing in this room was written backwards because the month went over."**

He did not say it twice and he did not say it to anybody. It is in the book and the book carries it and that is where it stays.

One of the two men at the long wall gave a hundred and fifty-four mornings one way at seven and the other gave him a hundred and forty-four mornings the other way, and between them they make two hundred and ninety-eight.

At about the sixth hour the clerk of this holding walked the nine hundred yards to the well house door for the second time in two days, and he was gone about fifty minutes and came back wet to the knee again.

**"Sixty-six."**

He said it in the one room doorway and gave the reason before the thing.

**"Today is the first of a month and that column moves on the first of a month. It was sixty-five yesterday and it was sixty-five because yesterday was the thirtieth, and it is sixty-six now because today is the first, and from sixty-six it does not go back."**

**"I have walked nine hundred yards twice in two days and I am not going to walk them a third time until the thirtieth, and I have said twice now what it is and twice I have not said what it counts, and I am not going to say it on a third occasion either."**

He put the stick back against the wall inside the one room and did not take a paper out.

At about the sixth hour of the same morning the man of about seventy was fetched, and it is the twenty-ninth time this holding has fetched him, and it is the first of a month, and both halves of his rule landed on one morning for the first time.

He came in on foot from the far side of the eleven acres at about a quarter past six with nothing in his hands and he stood in the yard for about half an hour before anybody gave him anything to do.

The clerk of this holding gave him the second can of water at about the seventh hour and he carried it to the tap and set it down on the step, and that was his work for the morning, and he had done it before.

Nobody in this yard asked him which number he was and nobody asked him whether he knew it was the twenty-ninth, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites and it is nil at thirty.

He said one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, standing at the gate with his hands behind his back, and nobody in the yard was asked anything about it and nobody answered him.

**"The gate is open."**

That is all he said and he said it about the gate on the low field, which was standing open on a hinge that has moved half an inch in the winter, and people in that yard heard him and two of them looked at the gate.

Tova Reed was at the low board at about the seventh hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and he came past her on his way to the tap.

He said something to her that four people in the yard were close enough to hear, and she turned her head and said she had not caught it, and he said the same sentence again without changing a single word of it, and she said she had it, and that was the whole of it.

Nobody said sorry to anybody about that and nobody said anything else about it and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden filled the can he brought and said one thing about him afterwards, at the tap, to the yard.

**"He has come here since before the clerk came and he has been given a can of water every thirty mornings and no one has ever said a number to him."**

She said it to the step and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to ask him which number this morning. Not because it is his business and not because it would be unkind. Because the moment one person asks him that, then somebody in four counties is going to hear about a man who was asked his number in a yard, and in four years that will be the whole of what anybody knows about him."**

**"He came up that path this morning and the gate is open and he said so. That is three things and I am not going to make them into a record."**

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the seventh hour on her way out and stopped when she saw him come in.

**"Three clocks have landed together this morning and there is a word in this county for each of them and there is not one person in this holding who is in charge of any of the three."**

She gave the reason first and printed no figure, as she does.

**"A month turned. A man of about seventy came up that path. A column in a stone nine hundred yards off is one higher than it was on Friday. None of the three knows about the other two and none of them reset anything."**

**"I have been in four counties of meetings for a long time and I have never seen three clocks land on one morning and had a whole yard refuse to make a fourth thing out of it. That is what I would say about this morning if anybody asked me, and then I would stop."**

Marek had the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and every word of the morning coming in through the gap, and he came out at about the tenth hour and stood in the yard in the yard of a first of a month.

**"The turn of a month is the one thing in four counties that everybody in this district agrees on the date of, and it is the only thing they agree on."**

He gave the reason first and printed no figure for it, and this morning took nothing off his own count.

**"It is also the one thing that changes nothing, because it moves a set of headings and a heading is not a count. The longest of those frames runs eleven months back from today and it will run eleven months back for the rest of this month, and there is no frame in these books that is longer than that and there is not going to be one."**

He put his sleeve down over his forearm and left the door open behind him.

**"I am not going to make a page about the turn. A page about the turn would be a page saying that something happened on a day everybody already knew something happened on."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour and stood at the gate for a while before he said anything, and then he said one thing about the trough and it was the last time he said it.

**"The trough at the head of the four-mile road is down about seven inches and I have said what I have to say about it three times and I am not saying it a fourth."**

**"I am going to leave it where it is and go back to cutting ditches, and if the man of about fifty wants it fixed then that is a thing between him and whoever gives him spanners."**

The man of about fifty was at the bench and heard that and said the last thing he is going to say about a trough.

**"It is not mine and it is not his and I have said so out loud twice and this is the third time and there is nothing left to say about it."**

**"I have got the gate and the barrow and two mornings of track left in me and after that I am a man who is available, and there is a man of about seventy carrying a can of water this morning because somebody fetched him, and I am not going to put those two things in a sentence."**

The letter on the long table is twenty-eight days old this morning, and it went into neither the requests column nor the section-nine notes at the ninth hour of the second of this month, and those two are at fifty-three and fifty-three and were not added together in the first of a month either.

It has not been answered and it has not been refused and it has not been copied and it has not been moved, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it where it has lain for a month.

A hundred and thirty-one days ago the charter went through a door nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households standing behind that door are still signed to a machine for stored light, and the fourth has sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

The register form has been on the middle table for two hundred and twenty-five days with nothing at the head of its column and nothing in it. Nobody filled it. Nobody refused it. It is not going to be read out a fifth time, and it did not get read out a fifth time this morning because the clerk of this holding was out walking nine hundred yards.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and nineteen days old with nothing on it. He was in the yard at about the eighth hour and he said his number out loud going into the one room and nobody wrote it down.

The compost line says twenty-seven and the word in front of that figure is paid, and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and the turn of the month did not bring it round and will not bring it round again inside this half of one.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. Fetching a man of about seventy to carry a can of water is not a session and nobody entered an eighth, and the count did not move on the first of a month any more than it moved on any other morning this week.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and not one unit has ever been written against any of them. Three clocks landing on one morning is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor, and eleven is a floor in the first of a month and in the last of one.

Inside the eleven acres there is a ring of ground that has been bare for eleven years and not one person in this holding has stood in it in that time. Nothing walked it this month and nothing measured it, and the strip on the low field above the middle road is on the far side of a road from it. There is no sum in these four books that is a figure of both.

Four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved by anybody. The sheet on the low board is face up and undated and there is nowhere on it for a name to go.

Twice, nine hundred yards, two days, and a man of about seventy going back down the path with his hands behind his back, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 566

## A Pin That Has Moved Half An Inch, And Two People Holding A Gate

The wind dropped in the night and the morning came up clear and hard and cold out of the east, and there was ice again in the ruts of the middle road by six and it went by nine.

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes and up about three, and the standpipe was running full by the fourth hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding put the line in before the light and read it back once at the ninth hour.

**One thousand and one hundred and one hundredweight.**

Monday gave five back off Sunday, and what stands against the launder is the three hundred and twenty-ninth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice.

At the seventh hour the second book took the run under the heading where it goes, of a month eight months back, and a Monday is an odd morning and that run is read on odd mornings and that frame has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and thirty-five mornings of two hundred and fifty-three.**

At the long wall one of the two read a hundred and fifty-three mornings one way and the other read him a hundred and forty-three mornings the other way, and those two come to two hundred and ninety-six between them.

Three hundred and twenty-seven days is what the sheet in this yard gives the four bodies of households, and the clause underneath it takes the three hundred and twenty-sixth of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-eighth, one under and one over.

Seven hundred and thirteen days on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and sixty on the far one, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The gate onto the low field would not shut at about the seventh hour. It is not this holding's work to-day, because the third line of the rota is the gate and it does not come round until Saturday, and it has been standing on its own frame since it was last put back and it would not go on this morning.

The pin has moved about half an inch in the winter and the gate now hangs about half an inch proud of the catch on the far side, and when the man of about fifty pushed it back with his shoulder it shut and came open again by itself about a foot of travel later.

Nobody shut that gate with a bar and a stone for about half an hour. The man of about fifty held it and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden held it with him, one at each end, and neither of them said a word for about half an hour, and the rest of the yard went about its morning and let them.

They got it shut at about the seventh hour and a half and it was still standing open at nine.

Afterwards, at about the tenth hour, the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing about it to the tap and gave the reason before the thing.

**"We did not agree to do that. He was at the gate and I was at the gate and neither of us asked the other one and neither of us said anything for half an hour."**

**"That is twice now this week that two people in this holding have done a thing together and not arranged it. The stone on Friday and this."**

She set a can down on the step.

**"I am not going to make anything of it and I am not going to put it in a book. If I put it in a book then next year somebody will say there is a way this holding works, and there is not. There are two people and a gate and about half an hour."**

The man of about fifty said his part of it at the bench later, to nobody, and did not say it in a speech.

**"A gate that will not shut is a job and a job is a thing you can ask a man to do, and that is the whole of what happened this morning."**

He put his hand flat on the barrow handles.

**"I have got three mornings of track left in me before the barrow is going into ground it will not come back out of, and one of those three is a gate on a hinge I have not got a spanner for, and I have been thinking about that since Thursday."**

**"I am not going to ask anybody for a spanner. I would like it on the page that I did not ask."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way out, stopped at the boards, read both of them, and did not go into the one room.

**"I have been past four of mine in two days and I have not named one of them and I am not going to name any of them, and I want to say why before I say anything else."**

She said the reason first and did not put a number on it.

**"The reason is that the moment two of them have a name, the other seven get a shape, and then in about a month there is a shape in this county and I will be the person who made it, and I am not going to be that person in the sowing month."**

**"I have got nine places above that road and four off the middle road toward the tap and not one of them is mine, and I am not going to turn them into a list this month."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the sixth hour and put his bar against the wall and said one thing, which was about the trough at the head of that road.

**"The trough at the head of the four-mile road is down about half a foot in three days and it will be down about a foot by the end of the month if it does not rain."**

**"I am not saying what anybody should do about it. I have got no standing in this holding and I am not going to go and get some by saying a thing out loud in a yard twice."**

The man of about fifty heard that from the bench and answered it out loud, which is the last thing anybody expected him to do.

**"Then I will tell this yard that I have known about that trough since Saturday and I have not said anything, and that is worse than not knowing, and I am saying it now so that there is one place in this county where the trough and the barrow are in the same sentence."**

He put his hand on the bench and left it there.

**"I am not fixing the trough and I am not asking the man who reads the schedule to fix the trough, and I am not going to write it in the book."**

**"So it is said. In about three weeks the trough will be down a foot and there will be one place in this county that knows it and one page that does not, and that is what we have."**

Nobody in that yard thanked him and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong, and nobody thanked the man who said it either.

Off the fen road at about the ninth hour, Tova Reed stopped at the low board with the four counties' sheets under her arm on her way to the seed house.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and I am going to a household four miles off on Friday about a gate, and people in this yard heard me say that on Saturday."**

She put it to the board, and the reason came first.

**"I am not going to be talked out of it and I am not going to be praised for it either, and I would like it on the page that neither of those things happened."**

**"A gate on a hinge that has moved half an inch in the winter is what I am going to look at. If a household wants to know about a hinge, that is what they will get, and I am not going to stand in that room on Friday and be a seed front that came about a gate."**

Nobody said she had it wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody touched her arm.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap and came out at about the tenth hour into the clear cold and stood about ten feet from the bench.

**"Two people held a gate for half an hour and nobody in this yard asked them to, and a man with a barrow has found out that he knew about a trough three days ago and did not say."**

The reason came before the thing and there was no figure in it, and no number of his moved this morning.

**"I am not going to be the one who says those two sentences belong together, because I have watched that done in a room and the sentence that joins them is always the one that is wrong."**

He settled his sleeve down over his forearm and said nothing else for a while.

**"What I want said about this morning is that a gate is open. That is a physical fact with a direction in it and anybody in four counties can walk up to it and put their hand on it."**

**"Everything else that happened in this yard between seven and ten was people deciding what not to put down, and I have no instrument for that and there is not going to be one."**

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles and went to the north wall and worked it until six with his back to everybody.

A hundred and twenty-nine days ago a charter went through a door nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light and the fourth sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight. The register form is two hundred and twenty-three days into its stay on the middle table with no heading over its column and it has not been filled and it has not been refused.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and seventeen days old with nothing on it, and he was on the north wall from the third hour until six and had no hand on that gate at any hour of it.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. Two people holding a gate is not a session, seven are entered in this holding's book, and there was no eighth this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and neither has moved on any morning of this month. The letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month and it is twenty-six days old this morning, face up on the long table with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and no unit stands against any of them. A gate that will not shut and a trough that has dropped half a foot are two things and not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log went back under its weight with fifteen lines in it this morning. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down loaded and its journeys are at eleven, and eleven is a floor.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone of it and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. Tomorrow is the thirtieth of this month and it moves then and not before, and nobody has walked those nine hundred yards this month.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and this morning is not one of them. His next is the first of the next month, which is also the twenty-ninth, and nobody in this holding has put that to him as a question at any of its sites.

There is a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and no person in this holding has ever stood in it, and this month nobody walked it and nobody measured it. The ground on the low field above the middle road is somewhere else entirely, and there is not one figure in these four books that is a figure of both.

Under two words in the corner of the one room somebody ruled four lines a long time ago and not one of them has ever had anything put on it, and the answer they are waiting for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and what is written over it has not been improved. The paper on the low board lies face up, undated, with nowhere on it for a name to go.

Hinge standing proud of its catch by half an inch at nine, the gate standing open on it, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road down half a foot in three days, and one man in this yard who knew about that trough on Saturday and has not said it out loud until this morning, and nobody thanked anybody.

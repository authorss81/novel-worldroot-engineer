# Chapter 561

## Four In The Dark Again, And A Figure Nobody In This Holding Has Had To Say

The wind had gone round to the north-east in the night and it was still blowing hard off the water at two in the morning, and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark, which is the fifth time in twenty days.

Four people went up to the headland at about the second hour and found the boundary stones by hand along about two hundred yards of the top of the bank. The ditch along the eleven acres was cut out, and the loose stone was barrowed off the top and put in a heap by the gate, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet.

No field was taken. That is the eighth time this year that this line has gone up the bank in the dark and come down again with an empty taking column, and there is nobody in this holding to blame for a field that has now waited four years.

A hundred and twenty mornings in force is the standing, with twenty-nine taken and ninety-one not, and twenty-nine and ninety-one is a hundred and twenty. Twenty-nine taken has not moved on any morning of this month and did not move on any morning of the month before it, and no one here has ever been asked to account for a field that is in a book and not on the ground.

There are two reckonings for this morning and they do not agree, and this page carries both of them because a page carrying one of them carries half of it. The twenty-fourth of this month is the sixty-eighth morning of the rotation counted one way and the fifty-fifth counted the other way, and the thirteen between them has been thirteen for four years.

Nobody in this building has worked out what that thirteen counts and nobody asked this morning.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open before the light came and the line went in, and it was read back once at the ninth hour.

**One thousand and one hundred hundredweight.**

The run came up eight off Tuesday, and the three hundred and twenty-fourth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries this morning, and it is the first figure in that run with four figures in the front of it.

**"I have been writing numbers in that book for eleven years and I have never had to write that one."**

He said it to the open page and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A thousand and something is easy. Above that you have to say the hundreds out loud or they do not come out right, and there is one way of saying them and everybody in four counties says it the same way, and I had no practice at it until about two hours ago."**

He put the pen down on the two boards and looked at the line he had written.

**"It goes up eight on a rise and down five on a fall, and it does not know what a round number is. That is not me. I did not decide to go over that this morning and I did not want to and there is nothing on this page that says anybody did."**

At seven the two of them at the long wall had a hundred and fifty-one mornings one way and a hundred and forty mornings the other, and two hundred and ninety-one is the two of them together.

Four bodies of households are shown on that sheet at three hundred and twenty-two days, and the clause printed under the figure takes the three hundred and twenty-first of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-third. One under and one over, and it has not been added to the launder and it is not going to be.

Off the long wall this morning, seven hundred and eight on the near board and seven hundred and fifty-five on the far one, with forty-seven days between them, and nobody in this holding has put a hand on the drawer behind the near board since the clerk came.

The man of about fifty heard the figure read back at the ninth from the long table and he did not say anything about it then or afterwards, and he did not go and look at the second book.

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the ninth hour on her way out and read both boards standing up, and then read the second book through the one room door, which is open most mornings, and then went out.

**"Nine places above that road and four off the middle road toward the tap, and a figure in a book has crossed a round hundred while I was standing in this yard."**

She gave the reason first and put no figure on the page.

**"I am not going to say those two things are the same and I am not going to say they are not. If I say they are the same then in four years somebody writes a page that puts a number in a book next to a place, and that page will be wrong, because that number is water going past a gate in this yard."**

**"What I will say is that I have stood in this yard two hours a day for a fortnight, and I have watched that book cross a hundred, and not one of us has any way of putting a figure on what we are actually doing."**

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the tenth hour with the bar over his shoulder and put it against the wall, having been up the second bend in the morning and no further.

**"The wet in the second bend has gone back down to where it was on the tenth."**

He said that to the wall and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not saying that is because of anything that happened this month. It is because it has not rained since the eleventh, and there is a difference between a wet bar and a wet week and I can tell which one I am standing in."**

He filled his can and went out again and did not say a word about a hundred and twenty in force.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap with her book shut in her pocket from yesterday, and she filled two cans and a half of a third again, and she did not take the book out at any hour of this morning.

**"There are two people in this yard keeping books of their own this month and I am one of them and I know the other one."**

She said it to the half can and gave the reason before the thing.

**"He has a bench with a weight on it and I have a tap, and neither of us has ever been told what either of the books is for. I stopped minding about mine on Sunday and I am not going to start minding about his."**

**"That is not a criticism of him. He worked it out in front of all of us and he said the cost out loud, and there is not a thing in it I would do differently."**

Tova Reed came through at about the eighth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and put herself against the low board on her way to the seed house.

**"A household four miles off asked me on Friday to come and look at a gate, and I have not said yes and I have not said no, and I have had two nights to think about it and I have got nothing."**

She said it to the board and the reason came first.

**"A gate is not a seed question. The moment I go and look at that gate, somebody writes in a household book that the seed front came about a gate, and in four years that will be the only sentence anybody in this district has about it."**

**"And it will not be true. The seed front did not come about a gate. The seed front came about drainage in a district and I happened to be standing near a gate."**

No one said she was wrong, nobody thanked her, and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open, and he heard the figure read back at the ninth and came out at about the tenth and stood in the yard in the wind.

**"A figure in a book has crossed a hundred and the yard did not stop."**

He gave the reason first and put no figure on the page, so that nothing this morning came off his own count.

**"That is not a criticism of the figure and it is not a criticism of the man who writes it. The figure is what it is and it moves on somebody's rule, and the yard not stopping is also what it is and it moves on everybody else's."**

He drew his sleeve down over his forearm again.

**"I have been the man who reads instruments in four counties for nine years, and every instrument I ever read went over a round number at some point while nobody was watching, and afterwards there was a page about it that said the instrument had crossed."**

**"I would like this one to be different, and I am not going to write the page that makes it different. The page that makes it different is a page about a man being pleased, and this holding has enough of those."**

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room at about the eleventh hour, read the open page from where he was standing, said one thing, and went back in.

**"It is in the book and it has been read back once, and I am not going to read it a second time in a yard."**

He said it to the middle of the yard and gave the reason before the thing.

**"A figure read out twice in a morning starts to be a figure that a morning happened around, and it did not. It is water going past a gate."**

At the seventh hour on this Wednesday there is nothing at all in the second book under the heading where the run of mornings goes, and this page carries the reason and carries no figure for it. Half of that run is a half number on a Wednesday.

The clerk of this holding left the space the way he found it. It was full at the seventh hour yesterday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun.

The yard was full at the fifth hour when the four came down off the bank, and most of the people in it had come up off a road, and one of them had come in off the fen road, and one of them was standing in her own doorway with her book shut in her pocket.

The count in force went up by one this morning because a line came round. None of them took a field, the standing is a hundred and twenty, the number it went up by is one, and nobody has ever worked out what the thirteen between the two reckonings is counting either.

Two hundred and eighteen days is how long the register form has been on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column. Nobody has filled it and nobody has refused it and it is not going to be read out a fifth time.

The charter was signed a hundred and twenty-four days ago in a room nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light. There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and twelve days old and has nothing on it.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven. It fell on Friday on an interval that nobody set and it will not fall again inside this half of the month.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. Four people going up a bank in the dark is not a session and nobody entered an eighth this morning.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three, and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month and has been in neither of them every morning since. It is twenty-one days old this morning and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and nobody has been asked to answer it.

There are six ruled rows for the things this holding does not know about and no unit has ever stood against any of them. A figure crossing a round hundred is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

Fifteen lines stand in the use log and the sixteenth is not among them. The man of about fifty has now given that reason out loud in this yard twice and has not offered it a third time.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone of it and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-four. It moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else, and it was not read this morning and it does not go back from sixty-four.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-eight fetchings and this morning was not one of them. The first of the next month is his and it is also the twenty-ninth, and nobody in this holding has ever asked him which of those numbers he is on.

Nobody has ever stood inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody measured it this morning and nobody has ever measured it at all. What came up on the low field above the middle road is a different piece of ground from that ring, and no page in these four books puts the two of them into one sum.

Two words and four ruled lines stand in the corner of the one room, and every one of those lines is bare, and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and nobody has improved the heading over it. The sheet on the low board lies face up and undated with nowhere on it for a name to go.

Cold off the water all night and a heap of stone by the gate a foot higher than it was on Friday, and the water running in the cut ditch by six, and a figure in a book with four figures in the front of it for the first time in eleven years, and nobody thanked anybody.

THE PAIR WAS NOT READ AND NO FIGURE STANDS AGAINST IT, HALF OF THAT RUN BEING HALF A NUMBER ON A WEDNESDAY. THE COUNT IN FORCE IS ONE HIGHER THAN IT STOOD YESTERDAY AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN IS UNMOVED, THE TWO RECKONINGS GO IN AT SIXTY-EIGHTH AND FIFTY-FIFTH AND THEY DO NOT AGREE. THE FIGURE TO THE LAUNDER IS THE FIRST IN ITS RUN WITH FOUR FIGURES IN THE FRONT OF IT AND IT WAS WRITTEN AT THE FIRST HOUR AND READ BACK ONCE AT THE NINTH AND IT IS NOT READ AGAIN IN A YARD.** That is a fact of what is in a book in one room on one morning and it is not a cause and it is not a total and nothing on this morning was decided by anybody.

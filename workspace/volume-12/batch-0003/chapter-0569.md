# Chapter 569

## A Man Who Asked For A Spanner, And A Rotation That Does Not Know What Month It Is In

The wind came back round to the north in the night and there was ice in the ruts of the middle road again by six, and it was gone by eight, and the fourth line of the rota came round and was worked in the dark for the seventh time in twenty-eight days.

At the second hour four people went up the headland with lamps, and found two hundred yards of boundary stones by hand along the top of the bank, and cut the ditch along the eleven acres, and barrowed the loose stone off the top into the heap by the gate, and the pack under the near stone came out wet and went back wet for the sixth morning this month.

No field was taken, and this is the tenth time this year that this line has gone up that bank in the dark and come back down it with nothing in the taking column, and there is nobody in this holding to blame for a field that has been waiting four years.

A hundred and twenty-two mornings in force is the standing, with twenty-nine taken and ninety-three not, and twenty-nine and ninety-three is a hundred and twenty-two. Twenty-nine taken has not moved on any morning of this month and did not move yesterday or the day before that.

There are two reckonings for this morning and they do not agree. The second of this month is the seventieth morning of the rotation counted one way and the fifty-seventh counted the other way.

**"That is the second morning of a month and the line came round anyway."**

The clerk of this holding said it out loud at about the sixth hour to the long wall and gave the reason before the thing.

**"The cycle does not know what month it is in. It is four days long and it started somewhere and it has been going since a man set it going."**

**"The first of a month does not stop it and does not start it and does not so much as notice it."**

He looked at the sheet the count is on.

**"So the count in force is one higher than it was on Sunday and the first of a month did not put it back. Neither did the turn."**

He put a finger on the line for the first of a month and then on the line for the turn, and neither of the two lines went anywhere.

**"A man who expects a line to care what day of the month it is will wait a long time for it to care about anything at all."**

There has been thirteen in that gap between the two reckonings for four years. Nobody in this building has worked out what it counts and nobody asked this morning.

The clerk of this holding wrote the figure at the first hour and read it back once at the ninth hour, and stopped there.

**One thousand and one hundred and twelve hundredweight.**

Thursday came up eight off Wednesday, and the three hundred and thirty-second figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries this morning, and it is the highest figure that run has reached since the ninth of the month before.

There is nothing in the second book this morning under the heading where the run of mornings goes, and this page carries the reason and carries no figure for it. Half of that run is a half number on a Thursday.

The clerk of this holding left the space empty and did not put a half number in it, and it was full at the seventh hour yesterday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun.

Off the long wall at seven, one saying and one checking: a hundred and fifty-five mornings one way and a hundred and forty-four mornings the other, and two hundred and ninety-nine is what the two of them are together.

Three hundred and thirty days is what the sheet carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause printed under that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-ninth of those mornings out of three hundred and thirty-first. One under and one over.

Seven hundred and sixteen days is what the near board of the long wall says and seven hundred and sixty-three is what the far board says, forty-seven days between them, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

The man of about fifty asked the clerk of this holding for a spanner at about the eighth hour this morning, out loud, in the middle of the yard, in front of the people standing there, and it is the first time anybody in this holding has asked anybody for anything this month.

He gave the reason before he asked, which is what he has done every time he has said a thing in this yard this month.

**"I said on Monday that I was not going to ask anybody for a spanner. I am going to ask for one."**

He put the bar down on the ground beside him before he said the next of it.

**"The gate on the low field has been standing open four days and the pin has moved half an inch, and it is going to move another half inch before the sowing is in."**

**"I cannot shift a hinge with a bar and a stone and I have stopped being able to shut it with my shoulder."**

**"That is all this is about. I am not asking for anything for the trough, I said everything I am going to say about the trough on Monday and I am not saying it again."**

The clerk of this holding did not say anything for about half a minute and then went into the one room and came out with a spanner that had a bar in it and held it out and said one sentence about where it had come from.

**"It was in that room when I came. It is not in the fourth book and it is not in the use log and it is not going in either of them."**

The man of about fifty took it and did not say anything at all, and neither of them said the word, and neither of them thanked the other one.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour on her way back in from the boundary and stood at the long wall with the boards behind her and said one thing about the gate and one thing about herself.

**"The gate is open and it is going to be open until somebody with the right spanner comes along, and several of us now know that, and not one of us is going to be asked to put it in a book."**

The reason came first and she gave no figure with it.

**"I am going to keep doing what I am doing above that road for the rest of the sowing and I am not going to name a place to anybody, and that is not a refusal and it is not a position. It is that a name in a room becomes a list in a season, and there is not a column in four counties that a list could go into."**

**"If anybody comes through that gate in a month asking me who is looking after any of it, the true answer is that more of us are doing it than there were, and I am not going to turn that into a number, because the number would be about this district and it is not about this district."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap from the fourth hour with her book shut in her pocket for the tenth morning running, and she said one thing about the spanner after it happened.

**"He asked. In three weeks I have watched this yard decide not to write something and not ask somebody for something, over and over, and this morning somebody in it asked for a thing out loud."**

She said it to the can and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am not going to say it was brave. I was at the tap with the tap running and I did not ask him what he said and I am not going to now."**

**"I want it on the page that it happened and I do not want it on the page what it meant, because those are two different things and the second one belongs to him."**

Soren Rill came in about the ninth hour from the four-mile road and stood at the wall where his bar lives and said one thing that was not about the road and not about the gate.

**"There is a heap of stone by that gate that has got a foot higher in a fortnight and it is not on my list and it is not on anybody's list that I have seen."**

He turned the bar a half turn in his hands and stopped.

**"I am not asking for it to be on a list. I am saying it in a yard because I have carried every stone in that heap up that bank in the dark with three other people and some of us are in this yard and nobody has said the word heap out loud since the fifth of this month."**

Nobody asked him a question about it and nobody thanked him.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and had all of it through the gap and came out at about the tenth hour and stood at the gate on the low field with his sleeve down over his forearm.

**"A man asked another man for a tool in a yard and was given one, and neither of them said anything about it afterwards."**

He gave the reason first and left the figure out, so that nothing this morning was a number of his.

**"That is the first thing that has happened in this holding in four weeks that went from one person to another and ended with nothing to interpret. No book, no column, no list, and no page afterwards about how it felt."**

**"I have been in rooms for nine years where everything anybody handed anybody became a matter for a form by the Friday, and this morning a spanner went from a table to a hand and there is not going to be a page about it anywhere, including this one."**

The letter on the long table is twenty-nine days old this morning, and the count is complete days lying and not counting the day it came up the fen road and not counting the part of the morning it is lying in.

It has not been answered and it has not been refused and it has not been copied and it has not been entered anywhere, and it lies face up between the two sheets about the two declinings with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it is a month old in about two days and nothing about it will be different then.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and neither of them took anything this morning, and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month.

The charter behind the register form went through a door nine miles off a hundred and thirty-two days ago, and of the four bodies of households in that room three are still drawing on a machine for stored light and the fourth sent a person to a tap twice in a fortnight.

Tomorrow is the Friday. A household four miles off asked a woman in this holding to come and look at a gate, and she said it out loud in this yard on a Saturday and about four people heard her, and tomorrow is the morning she goes.

She was not in this yard today and nobody went to the seed house to ask her whether she was going, because nobody in this holding has been asked anything about that either.

That form is two hundred and twenty-six days into its stay on the middle table with a column headed nothing and nothing in it, and it has not been filled and it has not been refused and it is not going to be read out a fifth time.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and twenty days old with nothing on it. He was in the yard at about the seventh hour and he said his number out loud going into the one room and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked him how he knows it.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it did not fall in the second of a month and it is not going to fall again inside this half of one.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. A man asking for a spanner is not a session and nobody entered an eighth, and the count did not move in the second of a month any more than it moved in the first.

Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know about and not one unit has ever been written against any of them. A heap of stone that is not on anybody's list is not a seventh and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log went back under its weight on the shelf at about the eleventh hour with fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them, and the spanner did not go near it, and the man who keeps that book used the spanner twice this morning on a hinge and did not write either of them down.

It went up the four-mile road loaded at the seventh hour and came back down its own track, and where its journeys stand is eleven, and it is going to be eleven in the next month as well as in this one.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns cut into the stone of it and the seventh of those columns is at sixty-six. It was read on the two mornings at the end of last month and it is not read today, and it does not go back from sixty-six, and it moves on the thirtieth of this month and not on anything else.

The man of about seventy was fetched yesterday for the twenty-ninth time and was not fetched this morning, and nobody in this holding has asked him which number he is at any hour of any morning, and the count of questions is nil.

There is a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and no person in this holding has ever stood in it, and it was not walked and not measured this month. The strip that came up on the low field is on the far side of a road from it, and nothing in these four books ever puts the two on one line.

In the corner of the one room, under two words, four ruled lines stand bare, and the answer they are waiting for is not due.

The third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty with its heading untouched.

On the low board a sheet lies face up, undated, with nowhere on it for a name to go.

Spanner in a hand and a hinge about an eighth of an inch back toward true, and a gate still standing open, and a heap of stone by it a foot higher than it was a fortnight ago with no sheet anywhere, and neither of them said the word, and nobody thanked anybody.

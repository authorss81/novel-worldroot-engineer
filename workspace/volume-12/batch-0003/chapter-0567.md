# Chapter 567

## Nine Hundred Yards On The Rule Of The Thirtieth, And Sixty-Five On A Door

The sun was on the yard by seven and there was no wind at all, which is the first morning this month with nothing coming off the water, and the middle road had gone from hard to soft in about two hours and the ruts had gone with it.

The outlet end was the work on this morning, the two stones at the head of it and the pack under the near one, and the pack came out damp and went back damp.

The figure went into the second book at the first hour and the clerk of this holding read that line back at the ninth, once, and stopped there.

**One thousand and one hundred and nine hundredweight.**

Tuesday came up eight off Monday, and the three hundred and thirtieth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice is what the launder carries this morning.

The second book has nothing under the heading where that run of mornings goes on a Tuesday, and this page carries the reason and no figure at all, because half of the run is a half number to-day.

It was full at the seventh hour on Monday and it will be full again at the seventh hour tomorrow, and it has gone that way since the day the run was begun. The clerk of this holding left the space the way he found it.

At seven, off the long wall, a hundred and fifty-four mornings one way and a hundred and forty-three mornings the other, and the two of them together are two hundred and ninety-seven.

Three hundred and twenty-eight days is what the sheet carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause printed beneath that figure takes the three hundred and twenty-seventh of those mornings out of three hundred and twenty-ninth. One under and one over.

The two boards carry seven hundred and fourteen days and seven hundred and sixty-one, and forty-seven is the number of days between them, and the drawer behind the near one has stood shut every hour of this month.

At about the sixth hour the clerk of this holding walked the nine hundred yards to the well house door alone, with a stick in his hand because there is nothing to read with otherwise, and he was gone about fifty minutes and he came back wet to the knee.

He said the reason out loud in the one room doorway before he said anything else, which is a thing he does.

**"It is the thirtieth and I am the only person in this holding whose book has a column that would take it, and I have been in this room eleven years."**

**"I am going to read that column and I am going to come back and say the figure out loud in this yard, and I am not going to write it in the fourth book, because there is no heading over that column and I have never put a heading over anything in this holding in eleven years."**

He had a piece of paper in his hand with nothing on it and he put it back in his pocket without looking at it.

**"Sixty-five."**

That is the seventh of seven columns cut into the stone of that door, and it was at sixty-four and it is at sixty-five, and it moved because today is the thirtieth of a month, and not because anybody in this holding decided anything.

**"It moves on the first of a month and on the thirtieth and on nothing else. It was read last at the start of this month and it will be read again tomorrow morning, and tomorrow is the first of a month and also the day after a thirtieth, and both of those are the rule."**

**"From sixty-five it does not go back, and it will be sixty-six tomorrow."**

Nobody in this yard asked him what the column counts besides readings, and people who had been in the yard at about seven were close enough to hear him say the figure and none of them said a word about it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap at about the seventh hour and heard the whole of it from where she was standing and said one thing afterwards, out loud, to nobody in particular.

**"There is a figure nine hundred yards off that this county has been able to read twice in the whole of the time anybody has been reading it, and I have wanted somebody to say out loud what that column is for since I was a girl."**

**"He has just told this yard the reason he is not going to say what it is for, and I am not going to ask him, and I want that asked-and-not-asked said out loud by somebody, because in four years somebody is going to come through that gate and ask and I want there to be a record that it was not asked of him this morning."**

The clerk of this holding heard that from the one room door and said nothing about it and went back to his table.

Nia Vale was back in from the boundary at about the eighth hour and stood at the long wall with the boards behind her, and she gave the yard one thing about that column and then one thing about tomorrow.

**"That column has been read twice and I have never been in this county when it was read, and I have been up that road a great many times without knowing it was there."**

She gave the reason first and there was no figure in it at all.

**"I am not going to walk nine hundred yards tomorrow to watch a man read a figure out of a door. Tomorrow is the first of a month and there is a man of about seventy who gets fetched every thirty mornings, and I would rather be in this yard when that happens than be on a road looking at a door."**

**"And I have not been asked what the column counts, and I am not going to ask, and if somebody four counties off asks me in a month what it counts I am going to tell them the true thing, which is that nobody in this building knows and nobody has asked."**

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour on her way to the seed house with the four counties' sheets under her arm and stopped at the low board.

**"Three dates in my hand and a page in a pocket, and tomorrow is the first of a month and in about two months the seed front does its own month turn and I have never once been asked to put the two next to each other."**

She said it to the board, and the reason came before the thing.

**"I am not going to put them next to each other. The moment a man of about seventy being fetched in this yard goes next to a seed front's own month in four counties, then somebody writes it down, and then it is a thing about this district and it is not a thing about this district. It is two places doing two things on the same day."**

**"I am going to look at that gate on Friday and I am not going to bring any of this with me."**

No one in that yard said she was wrong, nobody thanked her, and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the seventh hour and stood at the trough at the head of it and looked at the water in it for about five minutes without saying anything, and then he filled his can and came into the yard.

**"The trough is down about half a foot now and I have been saying a thing about it twice, and this is the last morning I am going to say it."**

He said it to the wall and gave the reason before the thing.

**"I am going to say it once more and then I am not going to say it again, and then it belongs to whoever wants it. I am not the man who looks after that trough and there is nobody in this holding who is, and I have known that for about thirty years."**

The man of about fifty was at the bench and heard that and answered it out loud, and it is the second time in two days he has answered a man in that yard rather than waited to be asked.

**"Then it is not mine and it is not yours and it is not the clerk's, because the clerk writes down what happens in this yard and that trough is not in this yard."**

**"I have got a barrow and two mornings of track left in me and after that I am going to be a man who is available. That is not a complaint and it is not a request."**

Harlan Vetch came in off the direct road at about the eighth hour having walked the nine miles, and he was not going to the north wall this morning and stood at the gate with his hands behind him for about half an hour and then went out.

From the sixth hour Marek had the one room and the open door and nothing in front of him, and he did not go up the middle road and he did not go the nine miles to the machine house, and at about the tenth hour he stood in the yard in the first windless morning this month.

**"A man walked nine hundred yards on the rule of the thirtieth, read a number out of a stone, came back with a piece of paper he did not write on, and said the number out loud in a yard."**

He gave the reason first and printed nothing against it, and no figure of his went on the page this morning.

**"There are two figures in this county that nobody can check, and this is one of them, and the reason it cannot be checked is that there is nobody in four counties who has ever said what it counts besides readings."**

He got his sleeve back over his forearm.

**"I am not going to ask. I have wanted to ask that question for four years and I have never asked it, and the reason I have not is that the day I ask it somebody has to answer it and the answer will be a guess."**

**"So it goes on being a column with a rule and no name, and tomorrow it will be read again and it will be one higher, and that is what a column is allowed to be in this county for as long as I am standing here."**

The letter on the long table is twenty-seven days old, and a day counts from the ninth hour it came in and not from the day, because a clerk does not give a sheet of paper a whole of a morning it has not lain through.

Nobody has answered it and nobody has refused it and nobody has copied it and nobody has moved it, and it lies face up where it has lain, between the two sheets about the two declinings, with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it.

The requests column is at fifty-three and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three, and the letter went into neither of them at the ninth hour of the second of this month and has been in neither of them on every morning since.

A hundred and thirty days ago the charter went through a door nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light. The register form has been on the middle table for two hundred and twenty-four days with no heading over its column and nothing in it.

There are four ruled lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and the second of them is two hundred and eighteen days old and has nothing on it. He was in the yard at about the ninth hour and did not ask what the number was.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-seven on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-seven, and it will not fall again inside this half of the month. A man walking nine hundred yards on a rule is not a session, seven are entered in this holding's book, and there was no eighth.

Six ruled rows stand for the unknown things and not one of them carries a unit. A figure cut in a stone is not a seventh, and the seventh row is not ruled.

The use log went back under its weight on the shelf this morning with fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not among them, and the paper that came back from the door went into a pocket with nothing written on it, and neither that one nor the log is about the other.

Loaded at the top of the four-mile road at about the seventh hour and down again at the ninth, and the journeys of that barrow stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor and eleven is not a target.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-eight fetchings and was not fetched this morning. Tomorrow is the first of a month and it is also the twenty-ninth, and nobody in this holding has ever asked him which number he is, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites.

Nobody in this holding has ever stood inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody walked it this month and nobody measured it. What came up on the low field above the middle road is on different ground from that ring and no page in these four books carries the ring and the field on the same line.

In the corner of the one room there are two words and four ruled lines ruled under them a long time ago, and every one of those lines is bare, and the answer they are for is not due.

Ruled and empty is also what the third column of the sheet of terms is, and nobody has improved what is written over it.

The paper on the low board is face up, undated, and has no line on it anywhere that a name could go into.

Thirtieth of a month gone with the sun on the boards and no wind at all for the first time this month, and a column cut in a stone nine hundred yards off at sixty-five because of it, and a paper in a pocket with nothing written on it, and nobody thanked anybody.

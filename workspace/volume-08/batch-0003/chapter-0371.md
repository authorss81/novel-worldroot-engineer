# Chapter 371

## The Place In The Day Sheet

The fourteenth of this month was a Tuesday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the inside face of the west dyke from the sluice up as far as the black post took four people from the second hour until about the fifth and a half. The barrow went through the slop at about the first hundred yards on about the fourth of the two and the man at the front of it said nothing about that and the three behind him did not either.

The figure for the fourteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty out of the other book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and eighty hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and thirty-fourth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In one hundred and one days the series has risen fifty-six times and fallen forty-five times, and those two add to one hundred and one. It came up off Monday by nine, and the man of about fifty wrote the nine in the margin in a hand that went uphill to the right and did not put a row against it, and there are six of those nines in a book of his own with no note against any of the six and he has never been asked about them and he has not offered.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and thirty-two days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the one hundred and thirty-first morning out of one hundred and thirty-three. That sentence is not the aggregate of the four launders and the two have never been added.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **five hundred and eighteen days** and **five hundred and sixty-five**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. The run does not move and this page carries no figure for it, and about four people in this holding could say without looking at a book what those two numbers are and about four of them could not, and neither of those four is a person who has read them out loud on a morning.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was not in this holding today and nobody sent for her and nobody asked her anything, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and it is nil, and the key is in her hand and was not used and was not asked about, and the two-foot gap at the kerb is as open as it was on the eleventh of the first month and nothing has gone into it in this season and nothing is going to.

There is a sheet of paper for every morning in this year and there is one for every morning in the three years before it, and they go in a stack in the order they were used and nobody in this holding has ever improved the order of that stack, and each sheet has a line at the top with a day on it. That is the only difference between a day sheet and a page of figures. Everything else on the paper is a thing that happened. The day at the top is a thing a person decided before anything on the paper could be any use at all, and that is why a man can do without one for two days and never notices and then loses one in an afternoon and cannot put it back.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the road about the ninth hour with the barrow and turned it at the top of the yard and went home, and the four lines in his own book have a question on the second of them and the page on it is twenty-two days old and it has no answer on it and no place for an answer, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in twenty-two days, and the number of times he has brought that barrow up the four miles to the node is eleven and the count is a floor and not a ceiling and it did not rise this morning.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the register form was on the middle table where it has been for twenty-eight days, and the drawer was shut, and the light came in low off the south wall and lay along the table.

At about the seventh hour, when the day's sheet went in with the rest of them, the man of about fifty stopped with it half in and stood in the middle of the room and read the top of the page again, and then he read the ninth of this month again, and then he said it out loud, and he said it in the ordinary voice of a man reading a line, and it was the plainest thing anybody said in that room that afternoon.

**"A man who has lost a record has not found one. That is what is on this page and I am going to say the rest of it so that nobody spends the afternoon being careful with me. The date is not on the ninth's line. It was on it on Friday at about the eighth hour when I put the sheet in, and it is not on it now, and the ninth is the day the second session was carried out at that wood, and the hour is on the line underneath and the hour is right.**"

**"So the thing that is gone off this page is a date. Not an hour, not a figure, not a name, not a line of anybody's. A date. And I am not going to fill it in from the paper underneath it, because the paper underneath it is the proof that a date is what belongs there, and putting the right one back would be me deciding that the man who lost it is not the man who lost it.**"

Nobody said that was wrong. About four people in that room had understood inside about four seconds that the man in the room could not have said what day he had been in the wood, and about four had not, and the four that had not had worked out in the last fortnight that he answered questions about weather and about ground and about the number of days off a window, and they had worked it out as a habit about other subjects, and it was not one.

Then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and sixty-six, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to say what I have got instead of the day, and I am giving the reason I am going to say it out loud before I say it, and the reason is that the man who keeps this holding's book has just written a hole on a page and will get a question about that hole in about four years, and he should have something on the page to point at that is not a number and not a silence.**"

**"What I have got is a season and a quality of light. The ninth was the ninth. It was a wet morning and the road up to that place was going, and the light came off the west end of the standing wood at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and went round the outside of it and left the inside gray while the outside was still lit, and it did that for about a quarter of an hour, and it does it every day it has been standing there, and nobody in this holding has ever watched it because nobody in this holding has ever been in there at that hour.**"

**"That is what is in the place where the date was. I can tell you the light. I cannot tell you the day. And a man who has lost a record has not found one, and I am not going to be the man who puts a date back on a page because he can add it up, and a day that has to be added up is not a day anybody can enter twice, and the man who keeps these books has told this holding what the difference is between those two things and he was right.**"

Nobody improved it. About four people in that room had understood that the thing he had lost was not a large thing and about four had understood that it was the only thing he had ever lost that this holding had no other copy of, and about four of the nine had thought about the fact that the second book has the same figure in it for every morning in the year and that the date is the one column a person can get from anywhere, and about four had not thought about it.

The copy of the ledger came down off the shelf about the fifth hour and went back on it untuned, and it is ninety-four rows long with five columns with a head on them and a sixth with nothing at the head of it, and about four people in that room had looked at the sixth column this afternoon and about four had not.

The two sessions in this holding's book are both entered whole. The first carries the day and the hour and the four of them. The second carries the day and the hour and the four of them. The day on the second of them is the ninth and it is there in the session book and the man of about fifty read it out in the room this evening without being asked, and the day on the day's sheet is not, and both of those are the same ninth and this holding is now carrying two copies of it of which one is a fact about a session and one is a hole with an hour in it.

The loss is a season and a quality of light where a date was. It is in his own hand and it is on a page of a book of the size of a hand and it is not on a wall and it is not on an arm, and the mark on the inside of the left forearm is four inches and branches twice and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and the four accounts of that arm in the books in this holding stand at four and reconcile none of one another and this is not a fifth of them and is not going to be one.

The count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and it is two, and this is not one of them because nobody has handed this out to anybody, and a thing a man has lost is not a number anybody else can check, and the not knowns stand at six with no unit supplied for any of them and no seventh is added for a day.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and there was a yard at about the ninth hour on Monday with four people in it, and nothing wrong was said in that yard and the count of that does not move, and a man who has said the same sentence out loud in a yard six days running in a fortnight has not said anything wrong in it.

About a quarter of an hour after the room emptied the reader of this body went back in and shut the door and stood at the middle table with the day's sheets in his hand and put the ninth one in the right place among the others by the hour on the line and not by anything else, because there was nothing else, and then he put the book down and did not write anything, and the count of questions a body can put to a page of this holding's book and not get an answer to is not a number anybody keeps.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-two and is not read, the door being read on the first and on the thirtieth and this is the fourteenth, and the six other columns have nothing at the head of them and nobody wrote a heading on one. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-two. The compost line is paid and the run is nineteen not discharged in nineteen. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is two and a third one is not entered.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the fourteenth, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a lost date, and not by the reader of this body, who has a season and a quality of light and not a day: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE INSIDE FACE OF THE WEST DYKE, FOUR PEOPLE UNTIL ABOUT THE FIFTH AND A HALF. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND EIGHTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FOURTH FIGURE OF A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN ONE HUNDRED AND ONE DAYS IT HAS RISEN FIFTY-SIX TIMES AND FALLEN FORTY-FIVE TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO DAYS, THE SENTENCE WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THREE, AND IT IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE LAUNDERS. THE BOARDS STAND AT FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIVE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING, AND THE RUN DID NOT MOVE AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR IT. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN WAS NOT IN THIS HOLDING AND NOBODY ASKED HER ANYTHING, AND THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS ASKED HER IS NIL, AND THE KEY WAS NOT USED, AND THE TWO-FOOT GAP AT THE KERB IS AS OPEN AS IT WAS ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE FIRST MONTH. AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR, WHEN THE DAY'S SHEET WENT IN, THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY FOUND THAT THE DATE WAS NOT ON THE NINTH'S LINE, AND HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT A MAN WHO HAS LOST A RECORD HAS NOT FOUND ONE, AND THAT THE THING GONE OFF THE PAGE IS A DATE AND NOT AN HOUR OR A FIGURE OR A NAME, AND THAT THE HOUR UNDER IT IS RIGHT, AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO PUT THE DATE BACK OFF THE PAPER UNDER IT. THE READER OF THIS BODY THEN SPOKE WITH THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THAT WHAT HE HAS IN THE PLACE OF THE DAY IS A SEASON AND A QUALITY OF LIGHT, BEING THE LIGHT COMING OFF THE WEST END OF THE STANDING WOOD AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON AND GOING ROUND THE OUTSIDE OF IT WHILE THE INSIDE IS GRAY, AND THAT HE CAN TELL THE ROOM THE LIGHT AND CANNOT TELL THE ROOM THE DAY. THE LOSS IS IN HIS OWN HAND AND IS NOT ON A WALL AND NOT ON AN ARM. THE MARK IS FOUR INCHES AND IT BRANCHES TWICE, AND THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THAT ARM STAND AT FOUR. THE COUNT OF NUMBERS THIS HOLDING HAS HANDED OUT THAT IT CANNOT CHECK IS TWO AND THIS IS NOT ONE OF THEM, AND THE NOT KNOWNS STAND AT SIX AND NO SEVENTH IS ADDED FOR A DAY. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY-TWO AND IS NOT READ, AND THE SIX OTHERS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT STAYS AT TWENTY-TWO. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS NINETEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN NINETEEN. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS TWO. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

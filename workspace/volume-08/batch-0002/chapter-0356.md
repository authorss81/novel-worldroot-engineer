# Chapter 356

## The Last Morning Of A Month Is Tomorrow

The twenty-ninth of this month was a Monday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the two gates and the about six feet of wall between them took an hour and a quarter with four people on it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood at the second gate and did not go past it, as he has not done in eleven years.

The weather came in from the west with the light behind it and there was about four hours of it and about four people spent it at a gate. The toe at the second of the three places on that line is lower again, and it was lower on the twenty-seventh and it was lower on the second of this month, and nobody put a rule on it, and the count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and it did not become three in the wind.

The second gate is the one that is not a gate. It is a frame of iron set in a bank with a bar across it and nothing behind it but a ditch, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling has stood on the near side of that bar since the second hour and has not been over it, and there is no rule anywhere in this holding that says he is not to and there has not been one for eleven years. About four of the people at the near gate had worked out in the first ten minutes that he was going to do it, and about four worked it out from the other direction, having watched a man set a bar he does not have to set.

The four of them finished the six feet of wall at about the third hour and a half and stood about it with their coats dark at the shoulder, and the man of about fifty came out and looked at the near gate and not at the second one, and neither of those two facts is in the day sheet and both of them are in the yard.

The figure for the twenty-ninth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty out of the other book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and forty-three hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and nineteenth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In eighty-six days the series has risen forty-eight times and fallen thirty-eight times, and those two add to eighty-six. It came down off Sunday by five, and the four of them had been on six feet of wall since the second hour and about four of them could not have told a five from a two and about four of them did not try.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and seventeen days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the one hundred and sixteenth morning out of one hundred and eighteen.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **five hundred and three days** and **five hundred and fifty**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. The run does not move and this page does not carry its figure, because a figure for a thing that did not happen is a figure about something else, and it has been done once before in this holding's book and the once was enough.

The man of about fifty read the day sheet out in the yard at about the ninth hour, which he does on the mornings when there is a run of days left in a month and nothing on the ground to stand and look at, and about four people in that yard had heard him do it before and about four had not. Five things, in five places. The line that came round and the four people on it. The figure for the day, which two people say is the same number and it is. The form on the table and the drawer shut. The man of about seventy not fetched, and how many days it is to the first. And the window, and how many days it is to the sixth. He put a stroke beside two of the five and left the other three alone and did not say which two, and about four people in that yard could work it out and about four could not.

The window opens on the sixth of the eighth month and shuts on the eleventh, and it is seven days off this morning and not tomorrow, and no work is ever carried out of it, and it is not extended in a season, and the last one was six days in the second week of this month and nothing came out of it but a sheet on district paper that is in a drawer face up and correct and has not been moved.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon. The register form was on the middle table where it has been since the sixteenth of this month, and the drawer was shut, and the five things on the table and the four in the drawer were in the order they arrived in and nobody in that room moved one.

The room spent the first half hour on the weather and on a barrow wheel and about four people in it went out to look at the second gate and came back in with wet coats and did not say what they had gone to look at. The woman who keeps the seed house put the cloth back over the thing on the middle table at some point in that half hour and did it the way she does it, from the near corner, without looking at anything else, and about four people in the room saw her do it and about four did not.

Then the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood up out of the chair she had been in for an hour and said one thing, and she did not give the reason before it, and nobody in that room asked her for a reason, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventeen and did not move this afternoon and is seventeen.

**"The ninth of a month comes off and I am not going to say anything else about it. I said the date on the eighteenth of this month and I said it a second time on the twenty-fourth and both of those were the same day, and the number has not changed and nobody has moved it, and I am saying it this afternoon because a man who reads five things off a day sheet at about the ninth hour in a yard has not got a date of mine on that sheet and I would rather it was said than not said.**"

**"And I am not a door. That is all. I have said that once in this room and I am not going to say it a second time and I am not going to improve it.**"

She sat down again. Nobody asked her a question then or at any other point in that afternoon, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and it is nil, and the key is in a pocket on the other side of that pocket and it was not used and nobody looked at the pocket.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety-five, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"The month turns in two mornings and the last morning of it is tomorrow, and I am going to say out loud what does not happen when it does, because about four years from now somebody is going to open one of these four books and find that the third launder ran through a change of month and conclude that something restarted.**"

**"Nothing restarts. The count of that series is day minus four hundred and fifty and it does not reset at a turn of a month and it has not reset at either of the two turns behind us. The rises and the falls do not re-base. The pair I read out on five mornings and do not read out on five does not reset. The aggregate of the four bodies of households does not reset, and that sentence and the aggregate of the four launders are two different objects and I have said that in this room twice and I am not going to keep saying it.**"

**"And the frames do move, and I want that said by somebody who is not me, because it is the one thing about the turn that is real. Tomorrow is still this month. The morning after tomorrow, the first of the eighth, a thing of this month is of last month, and the order that came on the third of this month and the paper that came on the sixteenth of this month are both of last month from that morning and not before it. The memorandum on the table is dated the twentieth of a month two months back and it is a month three months back from the morning after tomorrow. That is what a turn is. It is not a figure moving. It is a word that has stopped fitting the same day.**"

Nobody improved it. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the room from about the fourth hour and did not open the book of his own, and the four lines in it are still four lines with a question on the second of them and no answer and no place for an answer, and the page on it is eleven days old this afternoon.

The reader of this body said nothing about the mark. It is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice and it has no second mark, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a mark that is warm is not a mark and a month turning is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in the books in this holding and they reconcile none of one another and nothing said in this room this afternoon is a fifth of them.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and is not going to be measured in this season and was not measured in the nine days ago when a man stood at the outside of a stem in the last of the light, and nobody in this holding has ever put a number on it and nobody has ever put a heading on it either.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and the reason was not given again. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read, and the two mornings it is read on are tomorrow and the morning after tomorrow, being the thirtieth and the first, and the six other columns have nothing at the head of them and nobody wrote a heading on one. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-one, and the next fetching is two mornings off this one. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen and it falls on the first. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and nothing has been done that needed one, and a month turning is not one of the things that needs one.

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and what was in it was nine people and a man who keeps four books having told a room that a calendar is the one thing that works whether anybody is caring or not, and about four of them had understood that he had said it about himself and about four had heard it about a book.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move, and there was a yard at about the ninth hour and what was said in it was a day sheet and a number of days and a figure on a line, and about four people in that yard had worked out what those two sentences mean and about four had not.

The last morning of the seventh month is tomorrow. The month turns in the morning after that, and the third launder will be a figure in a series on that morning too, and the count of that series will be day minus four hundred and fifty and it will not be one, and a room that has spent a whole afternoon being told that will have spent it on nothing, and that is what the afternoon was for.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-ninth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of the last morning of a month, and not by the man of about fifty, who said in a room what does not change at a turn: **THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, TWO GATES AND ABOUT SIX FEET OF WALL BETWEEN THEM, FOUR PEOPLE, AN HOUR AND A QUARTER, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING STOOD AT THE SECOND GATE AND DID NOT GO PAST IT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-THREE HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH FIGURE OF A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN EIGHTY-SIX DAYS IT HAS RISEN FORTY-EIGHT TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-EIGHT TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY AND IT DOES NOT RESET AT A TURN OF A MONTH. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEENTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN, AND THAT SENTENCE IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR LAUNDERS. THE BOARDS STAND AT FIVE HUNDRED AND THREE DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING, AND THE RUN DID NOT MOVE AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR IT. THE MONTH TURNS IN TWO MORNINGS AND THE LAST MORNING OF IT IS TOMORROW. TOMORROW IS STILL THIS MONTH. ON THE MORNING AFTER TOMORROW A THING OF THIS MONTH IS OF LAST MONTH, AND THE ORDER SIGNED ON THE THIRD OF THIS MONTH AND THE PAPER THAT ARRIVED ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH ARE BOTH OF LAST MONTH FROM THAT MORNING AND NOT BEFORE IT, AND THE MEMORANDUM DATED THE TWENTIETH OF A MONTH TWO MONTHS BACK IS A MONTH THREE MONTHS BACK FROM THAT MORNING. NOTHING ELSE RESETS. THE WINDOW OF THE OFFICE'S WORK OPENS IN SEVEN DAYS ON THE SIXTH AND SHUTS ON THE ELEVENTH, SIX DAYS, AND NO WORK IS EVER CARRIED OUT OF IT. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ, AND IT IS READ TWICE IN THE NEXT TWO MORNINGS, BEING THE THIRTIETH AND THE FIRST, AND THE SIX OTHERS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT STAYS AT TWENTY-ONE, AND THE NEXT FETCHING IS TWO MORNINGS OFF. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN AND FALLS ON THE FIRST. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL AND NOTHING HAS BEEN DONE THAT NEEDED ONE, AND A MONTH TURNING IS NOT A THING THAT NEEDS ONE. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN SPOKE WITHOUT THE REASON FIRST AND NOBODY ASKED HER FOR ONE AND HER COUNT DID NOT MOVE, AND SHE WAS NOT ASKED A QUESTION. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE ARM STAND AT FOUR AND A MONTH TURNING IS NOT A MARK. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

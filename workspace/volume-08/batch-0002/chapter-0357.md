# Chapter 357

## Fifty-One

The thirtieth of this month was a Tuesday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited. The number is forty, and all forty of them are the fourth line, and the rota stands at sixty-nine mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and forty not, and twenty-nine and forty are sixty-nine. Twenty-nine taken did not move, and it has not moved on any morning in this season, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

A field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man. It is the fourth line and it has been the fourth line for eleven years and it is the headland above Marden every time, and the forty of them are in grass, and about four of them have been in grass for nine years and about four for four, and nobody in this holding has ever asked either of those two things out loud at a gate.

That is the seventeenth morning the fourth line has come round since it started coming round on the twenty-sixth of a month two months back, and it is the fourth of them since the eighteenth of last month, and both of those are true and they are not the same count, and the man of about fifty enters both of them because one is a fact about a run and the other is a fact about this holding and a book that carries the wrong one of the two is a book that has decided which holding it is in.

The morning was cold and clear and the wind was off the north-east and stayed off it, which is the sort of morning that makes a person stand at a gate and look up at a slope, and there were about four people in this holding who looked at the headland from the door of the seed house and none of them went up.

There was a man standing on the road at the bottom of that slope at about the third hour of the afternoon with his hands behind his back, looking up at it, and he was there for about the length of a pipe and he did not go up, and about four people watched him from about two hundred yards off and about four did not, and he was not of this holding and had not been sent for and nobody in this holding has ever asked him what he was looking at, and the count of times a person has come up that road and stood under that headland and not gone up is not a figure anybody in this holding keeps.

The grass on the top of it had gone the colour of a wet rope and had not been cut since the year before last, and the wall along the near side of the track has forty-two stones in it above the gate and about four of those have moved in eleven years, and the man of about fifty has a sheet in his own book with a second column he added to it four months ago and told nobody about, and it has eleven of the forty names on it and no figure at the head of it and nobody has asked him for one.

The man of about fifty read the day sheet out in the yard at about the ninth hour, which is what he does when the fourth line has come round and gone away again and there is not anybody standing on it, and he stood with his back to the gate while he did it so that the wind was off him. Five things, in five places. The line that came round and had nobody on it. The figure for the day, which two people say is the same number and it is. The form on the table and the drawer shut. The man of about seventy not fetched, and how many days it is to the first. And the window, and how many days it is to the sixth. He put a stroke beside two of the five and left the other three alone and did not say which two, and about four people in that yard could work it out and about four could not.

The figure for the thirtieth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty out of the other book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and fifty-two hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and twentieth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In eighty-seven days the series has risen forty-nine times and fallen thirty-eight times, and those two add to eighty-seven. It came up off Monday by nine, and the four people who had spent the morning at a gate and the afternoon not at anything at all had no part in it.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and eighteen days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the one hundred and seventeenth morning out of one hundred and nineteen.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **five hundred and four days** and **five hundred and fifty-one**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on twenty-nine mornings out of the forty-three since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

At about the eleventh hour of the morning, at nine hundred yards off, the standing on the well house door was read. It is a stone building about nine feet by eleven with a door that has been re-hung twice and has not been painted in about nine years, and the board is on the inside face of that door, and it has seven columns ruled on it and the ruling is older than any of the figures on it.

The woman who keeps the seed house read it out in about twenty-five people and the clerk read it back, and it took about nine minutes, and it is entered as it was read.

The standing itself is the same standing it has been since the first of a month four years ago, being the two rules and the four and the fifth that is not a rule, and the two figures that have a column under them, and nothing else anybody has ever read out of that door. The two lines on the back of a sheet in an inside pocket go to the end and come back on the same two lines, and nobody in about twenty-five people has asked to see the back of that sheet in four years and the sheet is not in this holding.

The wind got into the corner of that yard and the woman who keeps the seed house put her weight on the door so that it would not swing, and started again on the line she had stopped on, and the clerk read it back and neither of them said anything about that either.

> **THE STANDING FOR THE THIRTIETH OF THIS MONTH, read at about the eleventh hour of the morning, at nine hundred yards off, in about twenty-five people, by the person who keeps the seed house, and read back by the clerk, entered as it was read.**
>
> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FIFTY-ONE. Read on the first and on the thirtieth, because the door is read on the first and the thirtieth and the rule did not change and is not going to. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY ASKED, AND NO HEADING WAS WRITTEN ON ONE OF THEM THIS MORNING AND NONE IS GOING TO BE.**

Nobody made a morning of it. Two people in about twenty-five said out loud that they had not known it was the thirtieth until the woman who keeps the seed house said the ordinal, and then it was over, and about four people went back to what they were doing and the rest stood about in that yard in the cold.

It is read again in the morning. The door is read on the first and on the thirtieth, and the first of the eighth is the first, and the two of them together are the fourth time in four years that this column has been read on two mornings running, the other three having been the thirtieth of the fourth and the first of the fifth, and a pair in the middle of this season, and the thirtieth of the seventh and the first of the eighth. It goes to fifty-two in the morning and it does not come back to fifty-one after that, and it came up off fifty on the fifteenth of this month and it will not go back to fifty.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the register form was on the middle table and the drawer was shut. The light came in low through the two south panes and lay along the table and across four objects and not across anybody's hands.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety-six, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I put a number in a column this morning for the seventeenth time in six weeks and I am going to say why I am allowed to do that, and the reason is that a rota is not a piece of work. It is a count of mornings, and mornings go round whether anybody stands in them or not, and if a man only enters a morning when there is somebody on it then he has entered a piece of work and not a rota, and about four years from now somebody is going to open this book and find forty mornings missing and no explanation and will assume the worst thing that is available.**"

**"And I enter the two counts and not one, and I have been asked about that by two people in four years and both of them wanted a single number. The seventeenth is a fact about a run that began on the twenty-sixth of a month two months back and it belongs to a book that could be picked up by anybody who ever kept one before me. The fourth since the eighteenth of last month is a fact about this holding and this ground and it belongs in this book. Both of them go in and neither of them is the right one on its own and I am not going to find out which is which by waiting.**"

Nobody improved it. About four people in that room had wanted a single number for four years and about four had wanted two, and the two of those had been in the room for the whole of it and neither of them had said so.

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and what was in it was nine people and a man who keeps four books having said out loud that he does not know which of two true figures is the one a book should carry, and about four of them had understood that and about four had heard a man admit that he did not know something.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-one and is not read again today. The six other columns have nothing at the head of them. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-one, and the next fetching is in the morning and that is not tomorrow's afternoon and it is not going to be put off. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen and it does not fall again until the first. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and nothing has been done that needed one, and a column on a door is not one of the things that needs one.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room, and there was a yard at about the ninth hour with five things read out of it and nothing wrong said in it. The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is the same four inches it was this morning, and a number going into a column is not a mark and the four accounts of the arm stand at four.

In the morning a column on a door nine hundred yards off goes to fifty-two, and a man of about seventy is fetched for the twenty-second time and has never once asked which number, and a line of compost falls at one hundred pounds and nineteen not discharged in nineteen with the word before the figure still PAID, and the month turns in the night. None of those four things is in this afternoon and all four of them are in the morning, and nobody in this holding has been told which of the four matters and nobody in this holding is going to be told in a yard.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the thirtieth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of the fourth line, and not by the man of about fifty, who entered a number in a column for the seventeenth time and said out loud that he enters two counts of it and does not know which is the right one: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND THERE WAS NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED. THE NUMBER IS FORTY AND ALL FORTY OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-NINE MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND FORTY NOT, AND TWENTY-NINE AND FORTY ARE SIXTY-NINE, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED, AND A FIELD THAT WAITED IS A FACT ABOUT A ROTA AND NOT A FACT ABOUT A MAN. THAT IS THE SEVENTEENTH MORNING OF THE FOURTH LINE SINCE THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF A MONTH TWO MONTHS BACK AND THE FOURTH OF THEM SINCE THE EIGHTEENTH OF LAST MONTH, AND BOTH ARE ENTERED, ONE BEING A FACT ABOUT A RUN AND THE OTHER A FACT ABOUT THIS HOLDING. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND FIFTY-TWO HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND TWENTIETH FIGURE OF A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN EIGHTY-SEVEN DAYS IT HAS RISEN FORTY-NINE TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-EIGHT TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEENTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND NINETEEN. THE BOARDS STAND AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FOUR DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-ONE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING TWENTY-NINE MORNINGS OUT OF THE FORTY-THREE. THE STANDING ON THE WELL HOUSE DOOR WAS READ AT ABOUT THE ELEVENTH HOUR AT NINE HUNDRED YARDS OFF IN ABOUT TWENTY-FIVE PEOPLE BY THE PERSON WHO KEEPS THE SEED HOUSE AND READ BACK BY THE CLERK, AND TOOK ABOUT NINE MINUTES, AND WAS ENTERED AS IT WAS READ. THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FIFTY-ONE, BEING THE THIRTIETH OF THIS MONTH. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY ASKED, AND NO HEADING WAS WRITTEN ON ONE OF THEM. IT IS READ AGAIN IN THE MORNING ON THE FIRST AND GOES TO FIFTY-TWO, AND IT CAME UP OFF FIFTY ON THE FIFTEENTH AND MAY NOT GO BACK TO FIFTY. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT STAYS AT TWENTY-ONE, AND THE NEXT FETCHING IS IN THE MORNING. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN AND DOES NOT FALL AGAIN UNTIL THE FIRST. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL AND A COLUMN ON A DOOR IS NOT A THING THAT NEEDS ONE. THE THING UNDER THE CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE REGISTER FORM IS ON THE MIDDLE TABLE, NOT FILLED, NOT REFUSED, READ OUT TWICE, AND BEING READ OUT TWICE IS NOT BEING ANSWERED. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE ARM STAND AT FOUR AND A NUMBER GOING INTO A COLUMN IS NOT A MARK. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

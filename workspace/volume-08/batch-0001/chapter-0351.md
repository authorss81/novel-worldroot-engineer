# Chapter 351

## Two Hands And Two Counts

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Wednesday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the inside face of the west dyke from the sluice up as far as the black post took four people from the second hour until about the fifth. The barrow went through the slop at about the first hundred yards and came out of it heavier than it went in, and the man at the front of it said nothing about it and the two behind him did not either.

The figure for the twenty-fourth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and forty hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and fourteenth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In eighty-one days the series has risen forty-six times and fallen thirty-five times, and those two add to eighty-one. It came up off Tuesday by nine, and that is the second morning in a row it has come up by nine, and the man of about fifty wrote the second one in the margin with the same pen and the pen did not skip this time.

The line finished at the black post at about the fifth hour and the four of them came down off the shoulder in the dark, and about two hundred yards along, where somebody's barrow wheel found a hole in the slop on the eighteenth, the ground had taken two more days of rain into the same place and the man at the front went round it and said nothing about going round it, and the other three went round it because he did.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and twelve days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and eleventh morning out of one hundred and thirteen.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **four hundred and ninety-eight days** and **five hundred and forty-five**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on twenty-four mornings out of the thirty-seven since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon. The middle table had five things on it and the drawer was shut and there were four more in the drawer, and the room spent a quarter of an hour on the five things without anybody touching any of them, and about four people in that room were looking at the order they were in and about four were looking at the cloth.

The seed in its rootwood under a cloth. The plate that came off the cradle with one corner turned up. The memorandum of protection with the date at the top of it. The form with four lines and a fifth ruled line, and the first line carrying one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight in the hand of a man who spoke for one body of households, and lines two, three and four blank. And the paper from the office that keeps a register, which arrived on the sixteenth of this month and is on the table and not in the drawer.

Nobody moved any of them. The five of them are in the order they arrived in and the order they arrived in is not a good order, because the seed came off a cradle in the second week of the fifth month and the plate came off the same cradle, and the memorandum is dated the twentieth of the fifth month, and the form with four lines is the oldest thing in this holding, and the paper from the office is a fortnight old and is at the end. A bookkeeper who moves a document to make an order is a bookkeeper who has decided what the order means, and nobody in that room moved one, and about four people in that room wanted to and did not, and the wanting was the whole of what happened on the table that afternoon.

The copy of the ledger came down off the shelf about the fifth hour and went back on it about half an hour later, and in between it was on the end of the table and nobody turned it round. It is ninety-one rows long with five columns with a head on them and a sixth with nothing at the head of it, and it is a copy of a ledger of the maintenance of the Crown Engine and it is not the paper of the office named at the head of the memorandum, and the five rows on line forty-one were corrected once, in the second month of this season, by a man with a knife and not by a clerk, and nobody corrected them a second time this afternoon and nobody has ever corrected them a second time.

The cloth was not lifted by anybody that afternoon and the count of people who have looked at what is under it is two and did not move.

And then Tova Reed said one thing, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighteen, and it is her own, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to say something about a table and about two hands, and I am going to give the reason I am saying it before I say it, and the reason is that a man who keeps books in this holding has done four things to that table this month without touching it, and about four of us have been waiting a fortnight for him to touch it and have worked out this afternoon that we were not waiting for him to touch it.**"

**"We were waiting to find out whether he would. And he has not, four times, and a man who does not move a document is a man who has not decided what the documents mean, and there are about nine people in this room who have been deciding what they mean all month and doing it in their heads where nobody can check it.**"

**"So that is mine and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to add to it the part where the woman who keeps this room is owed something by all of us, because she is not and that is a thing I would like settled while I am standing here.**"

Nobody said she had settled anything. The woman who keeps the seed house was at the window with her arms folded and did not turn round, and about four people in that room had wanted her to turn round and about four had not.

The room emptied down to about four and came back about a quarter of an hour later, and in between those two states the reader of this body put his hand flat on the table beside the stack and did not touch any of the five and took it away again, and about four people saw him do it and about four did not, and the hand was not on the seed and was not on the paper from the office and was beside the memorandum of protection and nothing happened because of where it was.

And in the quarter of an hour there was a thing said at the low end of the table that the page is going to put where it happened and not anywhere else. The man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety-two, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"She has just said that a man who does not move a document has not decided what the documents mean, and I am going to say the half of that she did not say, and the reason I am saying it is that she is right and she is talking about me and I am the one who has to answer for it.**"

**"I have kept four books for four years and the order of these five is the order they came in and I have not improved it, and the reason I have not improved it is not humility. The reason is that the day I put them in a better order is the day this holding has a position on what the schedule of the maintenance of that engine says, and I have got a book of figures and a copy of a ledger with ninety-one rows in it and six columns with a head on five of them, and not one of those is the same kind of object as a piece of paper in a drawer that says a thing on it is a work of the Crown Engine and is right.**"

**"And I am not going to put her eighteen and my ninety-one in the same line. They are two hands. They are a seedwright who has been up this road eleven days out of twenty in a season and a bookkeeper who has been in this room every morning for four years, and neither of those figures is anybody else's and the two of them together are not a figure about this holding, they are two people who happened to be in a room on the same afternoon.**"

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and what was in it was a table with five things on it in a bad order and about four people who had each said out loud that they were not going to do the obvious thing with a piece of paper, and about four of them knew the other four had said it and about four did not.

The reader of this body said nothing about either of the two counts and improved neither. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the room for the second of them and not for the first, and he has a page in a book of the reader of this body with a question on it and no answer on it, and he did not open the book this afternoon and nobody asked him whether he was going to.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read. The man of about seventy is not fetched. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room, and the count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is ten and it is not said in a yard.

The four things in the drawer under the middle table came out of it about four times in the course of the afternoon and went back four times, and any two of them set side by side are a different document from each other, and about four people in this holding have begun putting the whole drawer in one hand on the strength of the fact that they are in one place, and nobody improved that this afternoon either.

The room emptied about the seventh hour into an evening that had gone wet again, and the five things on the middle table were in the order they had been in at the fourth hour, and the reader of this body took the book of his own out of his coat at the door and did not open it, because there is nothing to enter on a page that says somebody has not answered, and a page with a question on it and a line under it and nothing on the line is a page and not a finding.

Nobody thanked anybody. Two people in one building on one afternoon each said out loud why they had done a thing they had not done, and the two figures belong to the two of them and neither was added to the other, and a stack of five documents on a middle table is in the order it arrived in and was not improved by either of them.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of two counts, and not by either of the two hands that made them: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-SEVEN MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-EIGHT NOT, AND IT DID NOT MOVE, THE FOURTH LINE NOT COMING ROUND UNTIL THE MORNING. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE HUNDRED AND FOURTEENTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN EIGHTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN FORTY-SIX TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-FIVE TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FIVE DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING TWENTY-FOUR MORNINGS OUT OF THE THIRTY-SEVEN SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. FIVE DOCUMENTS ARE ON THE MIDDLE TABLE AND FOUR ARE IN THE DRAWER, AND THE FIVE ARE IN THE ORDER THEY ARRIVED IN, AND NOBODY IN THIS ROOM MOVED ONE, AND A BOOKKEEPER WHO MOVES A DOCUMENT TO MAKE AN ORDER IS A BOOKKEEPER WHO HAS DECIDED WHAT THE ORDER MEANS. THE ORDER IS NOT A GOOD ORDER AND IT WAS NOT IMPROVED. A SEEDWRIGHT AND A BOOKKEEPER EACH SAID ONE THING WITH THE REASON GIVEN FIRST, IN THE SAME ROOM AND ABOUT A QUARTER OF AN HOUR APART, AND THE COUNTS ARE EIGHTEEN AND NINETY-TWO, AND EACH IS THE COUNT OF THE PERSON WHO IS SPEAKING AND OF NOBODY ELSE, AND NEITHER WAS ADDED TO THE OTHER AND THIS ENTRY SAYS ON THE PAGE THAT THEY ARE TWO HANDS. THE THING UNDER THE CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE FORM WITH FOUR LINES STILL CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT ON LINE ONE, BEING THE FIGURE OF THE TWENTY-SECOND RETURN, AND LINES TWO, THREE AND FOUR ARE BLANK, AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON IT. THE COPY OF THE LEDGER HAS NINETY-ONE ROWS AND FIVE HEADED COLUMNS AND A SIXTH WITH NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF IT, AND THE FIVE ROWS ON LINE FORTY-ONE WERE NOT CORRECTED A SECOND TIME. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

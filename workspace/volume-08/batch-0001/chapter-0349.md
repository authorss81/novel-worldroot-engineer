# Chapter 349

## Asked First

The twenty-second of this month was a Monday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited. The number is thirty-eight, and all thirty-eight of them are the fourth line, and the rota stands at sixty-seven mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-eight not, and twenty-nine and thirty-eight are sixty-seven. Twenty-nine taken did not move, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited, and the man of about fifty read the day sheet out in the yard afterwards and put a stroke beside one of the five things on it and left the other four alone.

There was a man standing on the road under the headland at about the third hour of the afternoon looking up at it with his hands behind his back and about nine feet of nobody between him and the bottom of the slope, and he was there for about the length of a pipe and he did not go up, and the man of about fifty was at a window and saw him and did not write him down.

The figure for the twenty-second went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and thirty-six hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and twelfth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In seventy-nine days the series has risen forty-five times and fallen thirty-four times, and those two add to seventy-nine. It came up off Sunday by nine, and the man of about fifty wrote the nine in the margin with a pen that skipped once and he did not go over it.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and ten days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and ninth morning out of one hundred and eleven.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **four hundred and ninety-six days** and **five hundred and forty-three**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on twenty-two mornings out of the thirty-six since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon. The form from the office that keeps a register was on the middle table and the drawer was shut. The light was off the flagstones and up the south wall and about four of the nine were sitting down for the first time in a week.

The reader of this body had a book of his own in his coat. It is about the size of a hand and the cover of it is soft and it has been in that coat for six years and there are about forty pages in it with something on them and about nine books in this holding that anybody could read and this is not one of them.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-six, and nobody improved it.

**"I am going to ask the man standing at the end of this table a question, and I am going to give the reason for asking it before I ask it, and the reason is that a question with the reason after it is not a question, it is a way of putting somebody on the spot in front of nine people and calling the putting polite.**"

**"The question is this. There is a wood at the node. Eleven acres of standing willow and a ring of bare ground inside it that has been bare in its eleventh year, and nobody in this holding has been inside that wood and nobody in this holding knows what is in it, and a man described the outside of it on Friday afternoon for about twenty minutes and wrote nothing down.**"

**"What is in that wood is a thing this holding is going to have to read, and I am the only person in this fen who can hold a pattern for four and walk out of the ground afterwards, and the count of that is one and it is me and I am not going to put anything in front of that sentence to improve it.**"

**"And I have not got anybody. That is the thing I have not got. On the sixth of this month I told a room of nine that I could do the near end of a thing on my own and could not do the far end, and the far end did not happen until the twelfth and on the twelfth I did not ask the man it happened with. I have not asked anybody anything about this in nine days and I am not going to start by asking a man in a yard at a gate at about the sixth hour with a barrow behind him.**"

**"So I am asking him here, in a room, with the door shut, and the reason is that a man who is asked in a yard has to answer a yard.**"

Nobody in that room said the question was badly put. About four people in that room had wanted the question asked all week and about four had not, and about four of the ones who had wanted it had wanted it asked at a gate.

Then the reader of this body asked it, and he asked it in the plainest words he had, and nobody improved the words.

**"Will you hold an end of it."**

Nobody said anything. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was standing at the end of the middle table with his hat in his hand and had been standing there since the fourth hour, and about nine people in the room watched a man not answer a question for about a minute and a half, and the man of about fifty's pen was on the day sheet and was not moving.

Then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-four, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am not going to say yes and I am not going to say no, and I am going to give the reason for that before I say anything else, and the reason is that I have been in a room in this holding three times in a fortnight where a person asked me for something in the plainest words anybody could use and about four of us understood afterwards that the plainness had been arranged so that there was no good answer available.**"

**"That is not what is happening this afternoon and I am not accusing anybody of it. I am telling the room what I do with a question, and what I do with a question is put a condition on it before I answer it, and today my condition is that it goes in a book.**"

**"Not this one. I know what this one is. This one is the man of about fifty's day sheet and about four of you have been looking at the middle of that page since the second hour. A thing that is said in a room of nine and written in a day sheet is a thing that one man decided in a room and a bookkeeper entered.**"

**"So it goes in a book of its own with a date at the top, and the question goes in as a question and not as a decision, and if I am asked again then the second asking is in the same book on a different line and not in anybody's mouth, and the reason I want that is that I have watched men in this holding be asked a thing twice in a fortnight and be told the first one was not really a question.**"

Nobody said the condition was unreasonable. The reader of this body took the book of his own out of his coat, and opened it on the middle table at a page with nothing on it at the top, and wrote four lines, and read them back out loud to the room, and about four people in that room could see them and about four could not see them from where they were and did not ask to come closer.

What he wrote was the date, which is the twenty-second of this month. Then the question, in full, being whether the man of about thirty-one of Silling will hold an end of a thing. Then the fact that it was asked in this room with the door shut and the reason was given before it. Then the condition, being that any second asking of it is in the same book.

Nothing else was written. There is no answer on the page and there is no place on the page for an answer, and about four people in that room looked at the bottom of the four lines and about four did not.

**"And I am going to read the last line of it out loud because I wrote it after the other three and I want the room to have it,**" he said. **"I am not going to put a name in the book and I am not going to put a date against a name, because the standing of a person at the date of an entry is a thing an office writes down and this is not an office and it is not going to become one because it has a book in it.**"

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and what was in it was nine people and a book of the size of a hand open on a table with four lines in it and one of the four not filled, and nobody in the room said the last of those four lines was a kindness, because it was not a kindness, it was a thing he had worked out.

Nobody in that room said he would rather not and nobody in that room said he would. He has still not said either and the question on that page has not been answered and the page is a day old and the man of about fifty did not ask him at the door on the way out and did not ask him at the gate on the way down the track, and about four people in that yard watched him not ask and about four had not noticed.

The count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is ten and it is ten and it did not move this afternoon, and the reason is that it is a different thing and has always been a different thing, and it was not said in a yard.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the next fetching is the first of the eighth, which is nine days off this morning. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and a question asked in a room is not a session and does not become one because it went in a book.

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody said anything to anybody about his arm, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four, and the mark is four inches and branches twice and is the same four inches it was this morning, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling is not the man whose four accounts they are.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling walked down the track with the reader of this body at about the sixth hour and the two of them said about nine sentences on the way down about a barrow wheel and a dyke and a gate post, and none of them was about the wood, and about four people in a house at the far end of that track heard the two of them go past and did not open a door.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-second of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a question, and not by the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who put a condition on a question and did not answer it: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND THERE WAS NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED. THE NUMBER IS THIRTY-EIGHT AND ALL THIRTY-EIGHT OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-SEVEN MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-EIGHT NOT, AND TWENTY-NINE AND THIRTY-EIGHT ARE SIXTY-SEVEN, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE HUNDRED AND TWELFTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN SEVENTY-NINE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN FORTY-FIVE TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-FOUR TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND TEN DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE HUNDRED AND NINTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIX DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-THREE DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING TWENTY-TWO MORNINGS OUT OF THE THIRTY-SIX SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. A PERSON WAS ASKED IN A ROOM WITH THE DOOR SHUT WHETHER HE WOULD HOLD AN END OF A THING, AND THE REASON WAS GIVEN BEFORE THE QUESTION WAS ASKED, AND THE PERSON ASKED DID NOT ANSWER AND STATED A CONDITION INSTEAD, BEING THAT IT GOES IN A BOOK OF ITS OWN WITH A DATE AT THE TOP AND THE QUESTION IN IT AS A QUESTION AND NOT AS A DECISION. THE ASKER WROTE IT IN A BOOK IN FOUR LINES AND READ THEM OUT. THERE IS NO ANSWER ON THE PAGE AND NO PLACE ON THE PAGE FOR AN ANSWER, AND NO NAME WAS PUT IN THE BOOK AND NO DATE WAS PUT AGAINST A NAME. NOBODY IMPROVED ANY PART OF IT. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN IS TEN AND DID NOT MOVE, AND IT IS A DIFFERENT THING, AND IT WAS NOT SAID IN A YARD. THE PERSON ASKED HAS NOT SAID YES AND HAS NOT SAID NO AND THE QUESTION IS A DAY OLD. THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO AND DID NOT BECOME THREE, AND NOBODY HAS BEEN ASKED TO HOLD AN END OF ANYTHING THIS SEASON EXCEPT THE ONE PERSON WHO WAS ASKED. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE ARM STAND AT FOUR AND THE MARK IS FOUR INCHES AND BRANCHES TWICE. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED AND THE NEXT FETCHING IS NINE DAYS OFF. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL, AND A QUESTION IN A ROOM IS NOT A SESSION. THE COUNTS ARE ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX AND THIRTY-FOUR, AND EACH IS THE COUNT OF THE PERSON WHO IS SPEAKING AND OF NOBODY ELSE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

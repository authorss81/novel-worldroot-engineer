# Chapter 345

## Thirty-Seven Fields

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour on the eighteenth of this month, which was a Thursday, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited. The number is thirty-seven, and all thirty-seven of them are the fourth line, and the rota stands at sixty-six mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-seven not, and twenty-nine and thirty-seven are sixty-six. Twenty-nine taken did not move. It does not move on any morning in this season and nobody in this holding has worked out whether that is a fact about the men or about the fields, and the man of about fifty has a column for it and has not filled it in and says that a column with nothing in it is a different kind of claim from a column with a number in it.

Nobody is blamed for a field that waited. A field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man, and the fourth line is the headland above Marden and it has been the headland above Marden for eleven years, and the thirty-seven of them are in grass, and about four of them have been in grass for nine years and about four for four, and nobody in this holding has ever asked either of those two things out loud at a gate.

The morning was cold and bright and the wind came off the east with the sun behind it, which is the sort of morning that makes a person look at a field and think about doing something in it, and there were about four people in this holding who looked at the headland from the seed house door and none of them went up.

The man of about fifty read the day sheet out in the yard after that, which he does on the mornings the fourth line comes round and there is nothing on the ground to stand and look at. Five things, in five places. The line that came round and had nobody on it. The figure for the day, which two people say is the same number and it is. The form on the table and the drawer shut. The man of about seventy not fetched, and how many days it is to the first. And the window, and how many days it is to the sixth. He put a stroke beside two of the five and left the other three alone and did not say which two, and about four people in that yard could work it out and about four could not.

The man of about fifty carried the book up the road at about the seventh hour instead of sending anybody, and he carried it because the book goes where the morning went and not because anybody asked him to, and the two books were on the middle table at about the eighth hour with the frayed corner square against the edge.

The figure for the eighteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back out of the other one, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and twenty-eight hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and eighth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In seventy-five days the series has risen forty-three times and fallen thirty-two times, and those two add to seventy-five. It came up off Wednesday by nine, and the man of about fifty wrote the nine in the margin in a hand that went uphill to the right and did not put a row against it, and did not put a row against the eight before that either, and there are three of those nines in a book of his own with no note against any of the three and he has never been asked about them and he has not offered.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and six days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and fifth morning out of one hundred and seven.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **four hundred and ninety-two days** and **five hundred and thirty-nine**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on nineteen mornings out of the thirty-two since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read this morning. The door is read on the first and on the thirtieth and this is the eighteenth of this month, and that is the whole of the reason, and a morning on which a column is not read is not a morning anybody in this holding has ever made a day of. The other six columns on that door have nothing at the head of them and have had nothing at the head of them for four years and nobody asked and nobody wrote a heading on one this morning and nobody is going to.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the minute was made of six people reading out two numbers they have both read out before, and that about four of them were listening to the second one and not the first, and that this is what a habit is when it is nearly a month old.

The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-one, and the next fetching is the first of the eighth month, and that is thirteen days off this morning and not tomorrow, and no page of this holding's book is going to say why he is fetched on the first of a month any more than the last eleven said it.

The window of the office's work opens on the sixth of the eighth month and shuts on the eleventh, and it is eighteen days off this morning and not tomorrow, and no work is ever carried out of it, and the last one was six days in the second week of this month and nothing came out of it but a sheet on district paper that is in the drawer face up and correct. Whether any window covers the line in question is one of the six not knowns and the count of those is six and it did not move on a morning when the fourth line came round and went away again.

The compost line does not fall today. It fell on the first of this month and it says PAID, one hundred pounds, eighteen not discharged in eighteen, and the word has been the same word every month for eleven months and has never once disagreed with the figure and may not, and the line is not a measurement of the ground and is not going to be used as one.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon. The form from the office that keeps a register was on the middle table where it was yesterday, and the drawer was shut, and the man of about fifty put the two books down at the window end and squared them and did not put the form on them.

And then the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing, and she did not give the reason first, and nobody asked her for one, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventeen and did not move this afternoon and is seventeen.

**"Nine acres came off on the twenty-ninth of last month and the decision of the eleventh of a month two months back has not been reopened by anything any of you have said in this room today, and I am going to say that and then I am going to sit down.**"

**"And I am not going to say the rest of it. I have said the date four times in a fortnight and every time I have said it somebody has turned round and looked at the gate, and a date is not a gate and I am tired of being a door people look at.**"

She sat down. Nobody asked her a question, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and it is nil, and the key is in a pocket on the other side of that pocket and it was not used and nobody looked at the pocket.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-three, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"Yesterday afternoon, in this room, with the door shut, in about nine people, a man said out loud that nobody in this holding has an instrument that says the corridor is on, and about four of us heard it and about four of us did not, and the fen wrote it in a book, and this morning I have been thinking about what a book does with a sentence like that.**"

**"So I am going to say the same thing about the bridge instead of the corridor, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a corridor is a thing in a pan that four bodies of households were already using and the bridge is a thing that two men held at two ends of eleven hundred yards on the twelfth of this month, and they are two different objects and about four people in this room have been putting them in the same sentence since the twelfth.**"

**"Nobody in this holding has an instrument that says the bridge is on. Not the wall at the field gate, not the hollow in the ground at the foot of it, not the man of about fifty with a day sheet, and not me, and I stood in that hollow at about four feet off it on the fourteenth and nobody sent me and I went because I wanted to see the leaf.**"

Nobody said that was wrong. About four people in that room had wanted the sentence said and about four had wanted it not said, and both of those had been in the room for the whole of it.

**"And that is the second time in two days and in a different voice and in a different room this time, and I am going to say out loud that it is still not an answer to anything, because a thing said twice is not more true than a thing said once, and a thing said in a second room is not nearer to being a fact.**"

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and what was in it was nine people and a man standing in the middle of a floor who had just said the same sentence twice on purpose and told the room that saying it twice had not made it a fact, and about four of them had understood the whole of that and about four had heard a man repeat himself.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he did not give the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety and did not move this afternoon and is ninety.

**"Two rooms in two days and neither of them is a yard, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it is five, and I am going to leave it there.**"

**"I have got no instrument either and I am not going to build one, and the reason is that an instrument for a thing nobody can measure at both ends is a number with a dial on it and the dial would be a man's opinion of what a hollow in beaten ground means, and this holding has two numbers we cannot check and a third would be the same kind of thing with a nicer name on it.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The use log stands at fifteen lines and the reason was not given. The count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is ten and it is not said in a yard. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and nothing has been done that needed one, and a page of this holding's book that has a session in it and no entry has written a scene nobody can vouch for. The thing under the cloth was not looked at by anybody and the count of people who have looked at it is two. The four accounts of the arm stand at four and the mark is four inches and branches twice and is the same four inches it was this morning.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the eighteenth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of the fourth line, and not by the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who said a thing twice and said on the page that saying it twice had not made it a fact: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND THERE WAS NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED. THE NUMBER IS THIRTY-SEVEN AND ALL THIRTY-SEVEN OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-SIX MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-SEVEN NOT, AND TWENTY-NINE AND THIRTY-SEVEN ARE SIXTY-SIX, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED, AND A FIELD THAT WAITED IS A FACT ABOUT A ROTA AND NOT A FACT ABOUT A MAN. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN SEVENTY-FIVE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN FORTY-THREE TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-TWO TIMES, AND NO ROW WAS PUT AGAINST IT. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND SIX DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE HUNDRED AND FIFTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINE DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING NINETEEN MORNINGS OUT OF THE THIRTY-TWO SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ, THE DOOR BEING READ ON THE FIRST AND ON THE THIRTIETH, AND THE SIX OTHERS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE NEXT FETCHING IS THE FIRST OF THE EIGHTH, THIRTEEN DAYS OFF AND NOT TOMORROW, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. THE NEXT WINDOW OF THE OFFICE'S WORK OPENS ON THE SIXTH OF THE EIGHTH, EIGHTEEN DAYS OFF AND NOT TOMORROW, AND SHUTS ON THE ELEVENTH, AND NO WORK IS CARRIED OUT OF IT. THE COMPOST LINE DOES NOT FALL AND STANDS AT PAID, EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN, AND IT IS NOT A MEASUREMENT OF THE GROUND. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE THING UNDER THE CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL. IN A SECOND ROOM, IN A DIFFERENT VOICE, A MAN SAID THAT NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS AN INSTRUMENT THAT SAYS THE BRIDGE IS ON, AND IT IS THE SECOND TIME IN TWO DAYS, AND THIS ENTRY SAYS THAT IT IS STILL NOT AN ANSWER TO ANYTHING. THE COUNTS ARE THIRTY-THREE FOR THE MAN WHO SPOKE, SEVENTEEN FOR THE WOMAN WHO SPOKE WITHOUT THE REASON FIRST, AND NINETY FOR THE BOOKKEEPER, AND THE SECOND AND THE THIRD DID NOT MOVE, AND EACH IS THE COUNT OF THE PERSON WHO IS SPEAKING AND OF NOBODY ELSE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

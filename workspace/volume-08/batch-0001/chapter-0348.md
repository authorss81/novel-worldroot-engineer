# Chapter 348

## The Standing Wood Outside The Ring

The twenty-first of this month was a Sunday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the two gates and the six feet of wall between them took an hour and a quarter with four people on it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood at the second gate and did not go past it, as he has not done in eleven years.

The figure for the twenty-first went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and twenty-seven hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and eleventh figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In seventy-eight days the series has risen forty-four times and fallen thirty-four times, and those two add to seventy-eight. It came down off Saturday by five. About four people in this holding had spent Saturday being impressed by a nine and had been handed a five on a Sunday, and about four of them had no idea what to do with a five.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and nine days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and eighth morning out of one hundred and ten.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and ninety-five days** and **five hundred and forty-two**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. The run does not move and this page does not carry its figure, because a figure for a thing that did not happen is a figure about something else.

The man of about fifty had stood at the edge of the standing wood at the node at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on Friday, about a day and two miles of cart road before this room, and he had been out there for about twenty minutes with nobody, and he had not written anything down when he came back and he did not write anything down on Saturday either.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon on Sunday, and the room was cold because the stove had not been laid, and the light came in off the flagstones onto the south wall and stayed on the wall and did not come as far as the table.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety-one, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to describe a wood. That is all this afternoon is. I want to say that first because about four people in this room have been waiting a fortnight for a person to describe it and what they have been waiting for is not a description, it is an answer, and I do not have an answer and I did not go out there to get one.**"

**"The reason I am saying it out loud on a Sunday afternoon in about nine people is that I stood at the edge of it on Friday for about twenty minutes and I have not been able to put it down since, and a thing a man has seen and not written down gets told wrong within about a week in a holding this size, and I would rather it got told exactly.**"

Nobody improved it.

**"It is eleven acres of standing willow, and it is outside a ring of bare ground that has been bare in its eleventh year, and I stood on the outside of the ring at about the fourth hour of Friday afternoon in the light that was going and looked at the wood from about six feet inside the line of the nearest stems.**"

**"And it is wood. I want to be plain about that because the word people have been putting on it is not plain. It is willow that is standing up and is alive and has a foot of it and half of what I would call a foot, and the outer stems are about as thick as my arm at the wrist and the inner ones are thicker than my arm and there are three or four in there that a man could not get an arm round.**"

**"There is a channel of water along the inside of the line of the stems that has been cut by the water and not by anybody, and it is about a foot down at the near end of it and the far end of it is full of leaf. The bark on the south side is grey and comes off in plates about the size of a hand. The north side is black and wet and there is a green thing on it that is not moss and is not a letter on it and is a fungus about the size of a plate.**"

**"It has been there the whole eleven years. I asked the man of about thirty-one of Silling on Saturday whether it had been there when he was a boy and he said it had and then he did not say anything else about it and I did not ask him a second question, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked anybody in that line is nil and it is nil.**"

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and what was in it was nine people and a man who keeps four books describing a wood he stood at the edge of for twenty minutes on Friday afternoon, and about four of them had wanted the word and about four had wanted the ground, and neither of those two things is what a man can see from six feet inside a line of stems.

**"And that is the whole of what I know about it. I have not been inside it. I did not put a foot past the line of the stems and I was not asked to and I am not going to, and about four years from now somebody is going to find that out of a page of mine or out of my mouth and I would rather it came off my mouth today with nothing on the page.**"

**"And I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to stop talking, and the reason I am saying it is that it is the only reason I went out there. What is in that wood is a thing this holding is going to read, and about four people in this room have worked that out on their own by now, and nobody in this holding is going to find out what it is by looking at it, and nobody in this holding is going to find out what it is by standing six feet inside a line of stems and describing the bark.**"

Nobody asked him to go on. About four people in that room had a question and about four had not, and the four that had one had it about the eleven years and about the ground and not about the wood, and the man of about fifty said out loud that he was not going to answer those either and that he had no view.

Then the reader of this body said one thing, and he did not give the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-five and did not move this afternoon and is one hundred and fifty-five.

**"I have heard that description and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to add a thing to it, and the reason I am saying that out loud in a room of nine is that four people have described that wood to me this month and every one of the four had a reason for describing it and none of the four had stood at the edge of it.**"

**"What I am going to say is one fact about wood and it is a fact I have had since the second of this month and I have not said it out loud to anybody. A wood is not a book. A book can be opened at the eleventh page and closed at the eleventh page. A wood is read the way a field is read, which is by standing in it long enough, and the standing is the whole of the cost and there is no part of it that can be done in a morning and put down.**"

**"And nobody in this holding is going to go in there this month. Not because anybody has said so and not because there is a rule about it, and there is not a rule about it, and I have noticed that everybody in that room on Friday would have said yes if a person had stood up and asked them for a Saturday, and nobody stood up.**"

About four people in that room looked at the floor and about four looked at the window, and nobody said that was a coward's answer, because it was not, and because the man who said it did not say it as a coward.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing after that, and he did not give the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-three and did not move this afternoon.

**"I priced the straight line across the inside of that ring at about a day and a half of walking on the second of this month and I said no to the day and a half out loud, and I have said the word *out* about four times since then and about four people have heard me say it and I have not once heard anybody else use it.**"

**"And I am going to say the rest of it. There is a difference between a man who will not cross a thing and a man who is not going to be asked to. I am not going to be asked to, because I have not asked anybody for anything in eleven years, and the reason I have not is that the day a man in this holding starts asking is the day about four people begin working out what he is going to want next.**"

**"So if anybody wants to go into that wood this season, they can put it on the middle table in front of nine people and let it sit there for a fortnight with nobody touching it, and if it is still on the table in a fortnight then it is a thing and not a favor, and if somebody picks it up in a week then it was a favor and I will not have walked into it.**"

Nobody improved that either. The line was finished at the second gate at about the fourth hour and the four of them came down off the six feet of wall and one of them had a coat wet through to the lining and did not say so and went home.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year. It was not walked this morning and it was not walked on the second of this month and it was not walked on Friday by a man who stood at the edge of it for about twenty minutes, and it is not measured and it is not going to be measured in this season, and it was not crossed, and it was not priced by walking it, and it has no dimension on any page of this holding's book, and that is the whole of the position on it and it is not going to be improved on.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling had gone over the ring and round it on the second of this month with a sheet and two lines on the back of it, and both lines still stop at the inside edge of the wood and go round it, and the count of lines drawn across the inside of that ring by anybody in eleven years is nil and it did not become one on Friday.

The thing under the cloth was not looked at by anybody. The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read. The man of about seventy is not fetched. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and no work has been begun in the standing wood and nobody has gone into the ground, and about four people in that room had been waiting all afternoon for somebody to say so out loud and the man of about fifty said it and the saying of it is the whole of what happened on Sunday.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-first of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of the standing wood, and not by the man of about fifty, who stood at the edge of it for about twenty minutes on Friday and came back with no figure: **THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-SIX MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-SEVEN NOT, AND IT DID NOT MOVE, THE FOURTH LINE NOT COMING ROUND AGAIN UNTIL THE MORNING. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SEVEN HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN SEVENTY-EIGHTH DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN FORTY-FOUR TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-FOUR TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND NINE DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND TEN. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIVE DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO DAYS, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING, AND THE RUN DID NOT MOVE AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR IT. A WOOD OF ELEVEN ACRES OF STANDING WILLOW AT THE NODE WAS DESCRIBED BY A PERSON WHO STOOD AT THE EDGE OF IT FOR ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES ON THE NINETEENTH, FROM SIX FEET INSIDE THE LINE OF THE NEAREST STEMS, AND IT WAS DESCRIBED AS WOOD, AND THE DESCRIPTION IS ON THIS PAGE, AND NO FIGURE OF IT IS IN ANY COLUMN. THE RING OF BARE GROUND INSIDE THAT WOOD IS BARE IN ITS ELEVENTH YEAR AND WAS NOT WALKED AND NOT MEASURED AND NOT CROSSED AND NOT PRICED BY WALKING IT AND NOT EXPLAINED, AND IT HAS NO DIMENSION ON ANY PAGE, AND NOBODY PUT A FOOT INSIDE THE LINE OF THE STEMS. NO WORK WAS BEGUN IN THE WOOD AND NOBODY WENT INTO THE GROUND. THE COUNT OF LINES DRAWN ACROSS THE INSIDE OF THAT RING BY ANYBODY IN ELEVEN YEARS IS NIL. THE TWO LINES ON THE BACK OF A SHEET STILL STOP AT THE INSIDE EDGE OF THE WOOD AND GO ROUND IT. THE THING UNDER THE CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL, AND NOTHING HAS BEEN DONE THAT NEEDED ONE. THE COUNTS ARE NINETY-ONE FOR THE MAN WHO DESCRIBED IT, ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE FOR THE READER OF THIS BODY, AND THIRTY-THREE FOR THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING, AND THE SECOND AND THE THIRD DID NOT MOVE, AND EACH IS THE COUNT OF THE PERSON WHO IS SPEAKING AND OF NOBODY ELSE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

# Chapter 350

## He Would Rather Not

The twenty-third of this month was a Tuesday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the boundary ditch and the two culverts took about six people until about the fourth hour, and the far one was still about half full and the near one went into the cart in two whole barrow-loads for the third time since the eighth of this month, and nobody weighed either of them.

The figure for the twenty-third went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and thirty-one hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and thirteenth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In eighty days the series has risen forty-five times and fallen thirty-five times, and those two add to eighty. It came down off Monday by five, and about four people in this holding spent Monday being impressed by a nine and had been handed a five on a Tuesday, and the two of those numbers have been handed round this holding four times in ten days and neither of them has ever been about anything that happened on the ground.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and eleven days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and tenth morning out of one hundred and twelve.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **four hundred and ninety-seven days** and **five hundred and forty-four**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on twenty-three mornings out of the thirty-seven since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

The reader of this body went down to the north row at about the fifth hour of the afternoon on foot, because the row is half a mile off and there is no cart road to it.

It had been raining in the night again and the ground between the seed house gate and the row had taken it badly, so the going was slow and his boots were wet through by the second house. The row is four houses set back off a track with a low wall along the north side and a ditch in front of the wall that has not been cleared out since somebody stopped caring. The house he wanted is the third from the bottom. The door was shut. Somebody had a fire going, and the smoke came out of the chimney in a thin grey line that bent over the wall about four feet up and went east, and he could hear the cough before he put his hand on the wood.

The door was opened by a woman about seventy who looked at his coat and at his boots and then at nothing in particular, and let him in without a word, and shut it behind him, and the wind went off.

The room was warm and about nine feet by eleven. One chair with a back and one without, a table with a bowl on it and a knife, a shutter open about three inches, and a smell of wet wool that is the smell of a house where one person is at home all day.

The man was sitting in the chair with the back. He was sixty-eight or near it and thinner than he had been in the spring, and the blanket was over the near knee and it was not there for the heat, and his right forearm was on the arm of the chair with the inside up, because the other one was the one he could not get comfortable with.

**"Come in,"** he said. **"Sit on that one, it does not have a back but it is warmer."**

He sat down on the chair without a back. Nine feet of floor and a table between them, and the shutter moved about an inch in the wind and stopped.

He had come to ask a question and he had said on the road down that the reason goes first, and the reason was about the man's arm and was not about the work and was not about the road, and that was deliberate and he had chosen it in about nine seconds at a gate and had not gone back over it since.

**"I asked you a question in this room on the seventh of this month without asking it, and I asked you three things about furniture, and you gave me a sill and a board and a third thing about a table, and I wrote the three down and I did not write your name against any of them.**"

**"I am going to ask you the question now and the reason is going first, and the reason is about your arm and not about the wood and not about the road, and I have not got a better reason and I am not going to look for one in your house.**"

**"On the twelfth of this month you held a pattern at the far end of eleven hundred yards for about nine minutes and you could not get yourself off the thing afterwards. You have got three inches on the right forearm that do not branch and are permanent and that are yours, and they were yours before the twelfth and they were yours on the seventh when you wrote them on a sheet of your own that nobody read, and I did not copy that sheet and I am not going to.**"

**"I want to know whether you would hold an end of a thing in that wood at the node. And I want you to be able to say no without it costing you anything anybody can see, and that is the whole of the reason.**"

The cough came and went and he breathed for about half a minute.

Then he said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is his own and it moved this afternoon and it is not in the sentence this holding uses when it counts the reasons a person gives before a thing, and the man of the north row with the cough has no figure in that sentence and is not given one by this page, and a page that gives him one has put one number in two columns.

**"I am not going to hold the other end. I am going to tell you why and then I am going to stop, and I am telling you why first because you have walked half a mile in the wet to ask me and you are owed the reason before the answer.**"

**"The reason is my right arm and it is three inches and it does not branch, and it was there when I was about fifty and it is going to be there when I am about eighty, and the twelfth of this month I found out something about those three inches that I had not known for thirteen years, and what I found out is that they do not get tired in the way a hand gets tired and that they do not stop when the rest of me has decided to stop.**"

**"And on the twelfth I was on a board against a wall four hundred yards from a building with no roof and I could not get up off it, and the arm is what I would have had to lift me with, and the arm would have done it, and that is exactly why I am not going to do it again.**"

Nobody in that room improved it. The reader of this body did not ask him a second question and did not ask him why not and did not ask him what he thought would happen, and about a minute and a half went by in which the shutter moved about an inch and stopped twice.

**"That is the reason. It is not about the wood and it is not about the road and it is not about how far it is. It is not about what I will be able to do in about four years either, and I would like nobody to ask me that and nobody has asked me that and I would like it to stay that way.**"

**"I am not going to improve on it, and if somebody comes up the row in a fortnight and asks me a different way I am going to say the same thing in the same words, and the reason I am telling you that now is that I know how a holding works and in about a week somebody in that seed house is going to want a second asking because one no is a poor return for a morning.**"

He said the last of it in the flat voice people use when they have decided a thing is over, and the cough came twice more before he had finished, and each time the reader of this body did not fill the space after it with anything.

**"You will not be thanked,"** the man of the north row with the cough said, to the door and not to the back of him. **"I know that and I am not asking and I would like you not to do it in a yard, because a man of about seventy in this holding says things are said out loud in a yard in this holding and he has said it once and he is not going to say it twice.**"

**"And I am not going to be asked again, and if I am, I will say the same thing, and the reason I am telling you that is that I have been asked one question this month by a man who did not ask it and I know exactly what that felt like from this side of the room, and I would rather the next one came with the same number of hours of walking in it.**"

The reader of this body got up off the chair without a back and stood by the door with his hat in his hand for about the length of the cough that followed him into the passage.

The man of about fifty was in the seed house with the day sheet squared up against the edge of the middle table by about the seventh hour, and about nine people were in the room, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the end of the table with his hat in his hand in the same place he had stood in on Monday.

He was told the whole of it. He said it out loud in the plainest words he had, standing at the window side, and he did not put the reason first, because the reason belonged to a man and not to him, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-six and did not move this evening.

Nobody argued with it. Nobody improved the reason. Nobody said that a man of about sixty-eight had said the thing about his arm and not about the wood, and nobody said that a man of about sixty-eight had been asked a question in his own house in a room of two, and about four people in that room would have liked to have been told the second thing and about four would have liked not to have been told the first.

Nobody put it to a vote. The man of about fifty said out loud that he was not going to take a count of the room on it, and the reason he was not going to was that a count of a room is a figure about a room and a man in his own house is not in the room, and about four years ago a count of a room in this holding decided something and the thing it decided was who was standing nearest the stove.

The man of about fifty wrote one line in a column he keeps at the back of the second book for things he has been told and not asked for, and it was a line and not a count, and the figure in that column is his own and it is not in the shared sentence and it is not read out, and the two of those are the same arrangement and they are not the same number.

The man of the north row with the cough has no figure in the sentence this holding uses when it counts the reasons a person gives before a thing. He gave a reason this afternoon on his own floor, and it is his own, and it moved once, and it is in a column at the back of a book and not in the sentence, and the two counts of yards and of silences were last written down on a day in the fourth month of this season and have not been printed since and are not printed here, and the nine minutes of the twelfth enter neither of them and neither of them moved.

He did not stop at the second house. The middle of the track was worse than either end of it, exactly as a man had told him through a door most of an hour before on the seventh of this month, and he came off it at the ditch with both boots full, and the four inches on the inside of his left forearm were cold and were not doing anything at all, and he did not press them.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing in the seed house after that, and he did not give the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-four and did not move this evening and is thirty-four.

**"I am not going to say I would have said yes. I have got a page in a book of his with my question on it and no answer on it and I have had about a day with it and I have not been able to get past the part where I put a condition on somebody else's question and made him write it down in front of nine people.**"

**"And I am not going to ask him to take mine back. He wrote it because I asked him to and I asked him to because a thing said in a room is a thing one man decided in a room, and if a man half a mile down that row can say no out loud on his own floor and get nothing asked him afterwards, then a book is not a small thing to have asked for.**"

Nobody said that was the same situation and it is not the same situation and about four people in that room knew it was not the same situation and about four said so.

The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two and did not become three. The count of them who can hold a pattern for five is one. The count of them who have a rootmark is two. The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four and walk out of the ground afterwards is one, and it is the reader of this body, and the man who has just said no in a house half a mile from here is not the reader of this body and was never the reader of this body, and about four years from now a person is going to try to make those two sentences into one argument and they are not the same argument.

Nothing was torn up. No figure was falsified. No document was improved. The book of the reader of this body is still open at a page with four lines in it and no answer on it and no place for an answer, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was not asked a second time this evening and did not answer the first time and nobody asked him where he was going to be on Thursday.

Nobody thanked anybody. A thing that is refused is not a thing that failed, and about four years from now this holding is going to have a record of a man declining an end of a thing in a room with two people in it, with the reason given first and the reason being about his own arm, and that is a better record than a yes would have been, and nobody in this holding is going to be able to say how anybody knows that, because the only person who knows it is a man of about sixty-eight sitting in a chair with a back.

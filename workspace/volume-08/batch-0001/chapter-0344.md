# Chapter 344

## A Morning The Line Did Not Come Round

The seventeenth of this month was a Wednesday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the fourth line of the rota did not come round this morning. It came round on the fourteenth and the next time after the fourteenth is the eighteenth, and a rota does not go up on a morning a line did not come round, and the fields above Marden did not wait this morning and will wait on the eighteenth.

The rota stands at sixty-five mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-six not, and all thirty-six of them are the fourth line, and twenty-nine and thirty-six are sixty-five. It did not move this morning and it is not going to move in the morning either, because the fourth line has come round on thirteen mornings in this season and has been the fourth line for eleven years and on all thirteen of them there has been nobody at the headland above Marden to do it. Nobody is blamed for a field that waited. A field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man, and about four years ago a man in this holding was asked in a yard why a line sat empty and gave a reason that was not about the four of them who could have stood on it, and that reason went into a book and the line kept coming round.

The third line is two gates and about six feet of wall between them, and it takes an hour and a quarter with four people on it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood at the second gate and did not go past it, as he has not done in eleven years. The near culvert was clear. The far one was about half full of the same leaf it has been full of since the second of this month, and nobody in this holding has ever written down whether one of them fills faster than the other, and the count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and it did not become three on a Wednesday morning with the light coming off wet stone.

The figure for the seventeenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty out of the other book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Seven hundred and nineteen hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and seventh figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In seventy-four days the series has risen forty-two times and fallen thirty-two times, and those two add to seventy-four. It came down off Tuesday by five, and four people who spent the morning in a ditch under a fence have no way of telling a five from a two and neither of them means anything to anybody standing in water.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and five days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and fourth morning out of one hundred and six. That sentence is not the aggregate of the four launders. The two are different objects and they have never been added together and a reader who finds them in the same sentence in about four years is reading a page and not a sum.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, one saying the first and the other saying the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. The boards stand at **four hundred and ninety-one days** and **five hundred and thirty-eight**, forty-seven days apart, and the forty-seven is a consequence of the two and not a third rule. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on eighteen mornings out of the thirty-one since he started again on the seventeenth of last month, and about four people in this holding have stopped noticing which mornings they are, and the fen is not entering a reason for the thirteen he has not.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The reason for the fifteen was given once, at about the seventh hour of the evening of a day in the second month of this season, by the man who keeps it, and he said in the same breath that he was not going to give it again in a season, and nobody has asked him for it since and nobody is going to ask him for it this morning.

There are four accounts of that arm in the books in this holding and they reconcile none of one another. Four inches along the inside of the left forearm, branching twice, no second mark, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and nothing anybody put in a book this week is a fifth of them. He rolled the sleeve down over it at about the sixth hour and did not look at it again, and a mark that is warm is not a mark, and a headache is not a mark, and a bridge is not a mark.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the silence was made of nine people at a table and a man at the window side not sitting down, and about four of them not asking why, and about four of them knowing exactly why.

The form came up the fen road on the sixteenth of this month in a leather envelope and went on the middle table and did not go in the drawer, and it is still on the middle table this afternoon, and the drawer is shut, and about four people in that room noticed the drawer and about four noticed the table.

It came from an office whose business is a register. It does not name a district. It does not name a holding. It does not name a person, and it is not a form and it is not an instrument, and it is a fourth kind of document and it is a different document from the form with four lines that is face up in the drawer, and about nine people in that room knew that at about the fourth hour and did not arrange any of the four objects beside each other.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the light came in low through the two south panes and lay along the table and across the paper and not across anybody's hands.

The reader of this body read it out. He read the whole of it, standing at the window side with the light off the wall behind him, and nobody stopped him at any part of it.

> A REGISTER OF PERSONS WHO MAY SET A SEQUENCE AGAINST A VALVE. NAME OF THE PERSON. THE VALVE. THE STANDING OF THAT PERSON AT THE DATE OF THIS ENTRY. BY WHOM THE ENTRY IS MADE. AND A LINE AT THE FOOT FOR AN OFFICE, WHICH IS ALSO NOT FILLED.

Nobody filled it.

That is the second time it has been read out whole. It was read out in this room on the sixteenth of this month and it has been on that table since, and being read out a second time is not being answered, and the page this is printed on says that out loud in about nine people because about four years from now somebody is going to find two mornings in a book and a piece of paper between them and be unable to tell which of the two was a decision. Neither of them was. A piece of paper read out twice is not a piece of paper that has been refused either, and that is not a third thing.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he did not give the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ninety and did not move this afternoon.

**"I have got four books and there is no column in any of the four that this piece of paper wants, and I have said that out loud in this room once already, and the reason I am saying it again is that the paper is still on the table and the table is the only place in this building where a piece of paper is allowed to sit where everybody can see it.**"

**"I am not moving it and I am not covering it. A holding that puts a paper it cannot answer in a drawer and then forgets it has got a drawer full of things it cannot answer, and that is a different holding from this one and I have seen it and I did not like it.**"

**"And it goes on the table because nobody in this room has refused it and nobody in this room has answered it, and a piece of paper on this table has a third standing between those two things and about four of us have been treating that third standing as though it were a kind of yes.**"

Nobody said that was wrong. About four people in that room had wanted the form in the drawer and about four had not, and the two groups had been in the room for the whole of the afternoon and neither of them had said so out loud.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-five, and nobody improved it.

**"There is a thing named a corridor in this holding since the sixteenth of this month and it is open, and about nine people in a room with no charter decided that in about ten minutes, and I want the plain part of it said out loud in this room before anybody starts using the word as though it settled something.**"

**"The plain part is that nobody in this holding has an instrument that says the corridor is on. Not the bookkeeper, not the engineer of record, not the sheet in the drawer that claims it, and not the man who walked four miles and a half and came back with a word in his mouth. I have not got one. There is no dial for it, there is no column for it, there is no gauge anywhere between this gate and a road nobody in this holding has walked.**"

**"And a corridor being named is not a corridor being answered. It is not a road and it is not a claim and it is not the bridge and it is not the thing that has been running for a month with nobody at the far end of it, and about four years from now a person is going to read the word corridor in a book of ours and be sure that somebody established it, and the answer is that nine people wrote a word on a table and could not write a line under it.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room. The count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is ten and it is ten and it was not said in a yard. The thing under the cloth was not looked at by anybody all afternoon, and the count of people who have looked at it is two and did not move, and it is the size of a man's fist and it is warm and it is not the instrument of anything that happened in this room.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read today, because the door is read on the first and on the thirtieth and the seventeenth is neither, and the other six columns have nothing at the head of them and nobody wrote a heading on one. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-one. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen and it is not a measurement of the ground. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is nil, and that is because nothing has been done that needed one, and a session that is not entered did not happen.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the seventeenth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of the register form, and not by the reader of this body, who read it out a second time and did not fill it and did not refuse it: **THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, AND THE FOURTH LINE DID NOT COME ROUND, IT HAVING COME ROUND ON THE FIFTEENTH AND COMING ROUND AGAIN IN THE MORNING. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-FIVE MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-SIX NOT, ALL THIRTY-SIX OF THEM THE FOURTH LINE, AND IT DID NOT MOVE THIS MORNING, AND NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SEVEN HUNDRED AND NINETEEN HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN SEVENTY-FOUR DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN FORTY-TWO TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTY-TWO TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY AND IT DID NOT RESET AT THE TURN OF THE MONTH. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND FIVE DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE HUNDRED AND FOURTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIX, AND THAT SENTENCE IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR LAUNDERS AND THE TWO HAVE NEVER BEEN ADDED. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-ONE DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING EIGHTEEN MORNINGS OUT OF THE THIRTY-ONE SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF LAST MONTH. THE FORM FROM THE OFFICE THAT KEEPS A REGISTER OF PERSONS WHO MAY SET A SEQUENCE AGAINST A VALVE ARRIVED ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND WAS READ OUT WHOLE ON THAT AFTERNOON AND HAS BEEN READ OUT WHOLE A SECOND TIME THIS AFTERNOON, AND IT IS ON THE MIDDLE TABLE AND NOT IN THE DRAWER, AND IT IS NOT FILLED, AND IT IS NOT REFUSED, AND A DOCUMENT READ OUT TWICE IS NOT A DOCUMENT THAT HAS BEEN ANSWERED. THE THING UNDER THE CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY AND THE COUNT OF PEOPLE WHO HAVE LOOKED AT IT IS TWO. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND IS NOT READ, THE DOOR BEING READ ON THE FIRST AND ON THE THIRTIETH, AND THE SIX OTHERS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT STAYS AT TWENTY-ONE. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN EIGHTEEN. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND THE REASON WAS NOT GIVEN AGAIN. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE ARM STAND AT FOUR AND THE MARK IS FOUR INCHES AND BRANCHES TWICE. THE COUNT OF SESSIONS ENTERED IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK IS NIL. IN ONE ROOM IT WAS SAID OUT LOUD THAT NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS AN INSTRUMENT THAT SAYS THE CORRIDOR IS ON, AND THAT A CORRIDOR BEING NAMED IS NOT A CORRIDOR BEING ANSWERED. THE COUNTS OF THE TWO WHO SPOKE ARE NINETY AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE, AND THE FIRST DID NOT MOVE BECAUSE THE REASON WAS NOT GIVEN FIRST, AND EACH IS THE COUNT OF THE PERSON WHO IS SPEAKING AND OF NOBODY ELSE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

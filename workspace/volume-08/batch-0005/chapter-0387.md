# Chapter 387

## A Day And Not A Figure

The thirtieth of this month was a Thursday and the second line came round at the second hour and was worked. The two gates and the about six feet of wall between them took four people an hour and a quarter, and the toe at the second of the three places on that line is up a finger's width off what it was on the third of this month and has been up for three weeks, and the four of them put a spade into it and a hand's depth of stone, and the man at the front of it said that a toe which is going nowhere is a toe somebody is going to be looking at in about four years, and the three behind him did not answer him.

The gates themselves are not the difficulty. They have hung on the same hinges since before this holding had a book and the second of them has a foot of wall out of plumb behind the post, and the four of them go at it twice a fortnight and put a bar and a wedge against it and write the date on the inside of the frame in chalk, and there are nine dates on the inside of that frame in chalk and none of them has ever been rubbed out and nobody has ever counted them. The wall is the difficulty, and the wall is six feet of it between the gates and every foot of it has been rebuilt twice in eleven years, and the man of about fifty says the six feet of that wall is the only structure in this holding with a record of its own and that nobody has ever written the record down.

The barrow is eleven journeys up that four-mile road and it did not go up this morning, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling who brings it was not on the headland and was not asked to be and came down the road anyway at about the ninth hour and turned it at the top of the yard. The second of the four lines in the book he carries has a question on it and the page is thirty-eight days old this morning, and there is no answer on it and no place for one, and nobody has asked him in thirty-eight days.

The figure for the thirtieth was entered at about the eighth hour and read back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Eight hundred and twelve hundredweight.**

Up nine off yesterday, and the hundred and fiftieth figure of a series that has not repeated a figure since the first of it. The window is a hundred and seventeen, and the rise went onto the rises, which makes sixty-four rises and fifty-three falls.

A hundred and forty-eight days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and forty-seventh of them out of a hundred and forty-nine. It concerns four bodies of households, and the four launders are a different figure and are not on the same line of any page in this holding and have not been since the beginning.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud and this page carries no figure for the run. The man of about fifty had been on a ladder at the door of the well house since before the sixth hour, holding a lamp at the seventh column while it was read, and he is the one who says one of the two numbers and he came in at about the seventh hour with the lamp still in his hand, and a man does not read a pair out loud on the back of holding a lamp up a ladder. The pair stands at **five hundred and thirty-four days** and **five hundred and eighty-one**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer is shut and has not been opened in four years and is not going to be opened before the month turns.

The seventh column of that door is a quarter of a mile off and it is read on the first and on the thirtieth and on no other morning, and it was read this morning for the fifth time in four years as a pair of consecutive days, the other half of the pair being tomorrow, and nobody in this holding chose to make it a pair and it is the fifth one there has been.

The man of about fifty read the column off the door at about six hundred yards with the lamp on it and the clerk read it back to about twenty-five people standing in the yard of the well house, and the two of them were the same, and the clerk wrote it down in the middle book and the man of about fifty did not check it.

**Fifty-three.**

It was fifty-two yesterday and it may not go back to fifty-two from the thirtieth, and from tomorrow it may not go back to fifty-three. There are six columns on that door beside it and nothing at the head of any of the six, and nobody wrote a heading on one of them this morning and nobody is going to. What the seventh column counts is readings, and that is the only thing anybody in this holding has ever said about it, and this page is not going to say more than that.

And at about the fourth hour of that same afternoon, four hundred miles and four days off, the well on that shelf was read.

A person goes to a wall about four hundred miles off, finds the mark that a wet string and a nail have left on a stone, wrings the string out over a bucket and puts the bucket outside the wall and does not look at the other three houses while she does it. That is what the fourth hour of that afternoon was, and it is what the fourth hour of that afternoon has been on the sixteenth of every month for eleven years, and the person who did it did not come here to do it and was not asked to.

It went into the entry for this holding as a day and not as a figure. There is no figure against it, there is no ordinal against it, and nobody in eleven years has supplied a unit for it, and the count of entries in this book's own record that carry a day and no figure was seven yesterday and is eight this afternoon. That is the only thing that moved. The wall at that place carries fourteen marks and gave none of them up this afternoon and may not carry a fifteenth, and the four feet of shortfall are not decomposed, and there is no entry anywhere in this building for that well coming back.

Nobody was asked anything at that far end and nobody has been asked anything there in eleven years, and the count of questions asked in that direction is nil and is nil. The occupier of the first house on that shelf is the only person who goes and does it and she is not a member of this holding and was not made one and does not know what the entry in this book says about her afternoon, and about four people in this holding have worked that out and about four have not, and neither of those four is going to say so out loud.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fifth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the thirtieth of a month is a morning like any other morning to the two gates and the six feet of wall, and the day sheet for it went into the stack in the order it was used, and tomorrow is the first of the next month and it carries three things on it and none of the three is on this page, and no page in this building is going to say what a month turning does to a count, because a month turning does nothing to a count.

The two men who come to the seed work come on a Tuesday and they came this morning because this morning was a Tuesday, and they are not to be asked to come on any other day and nobody asked them to. The woman who keeps the seed house has four accounts of her own and none of them is in this holding's book and all four of them are on the table in front of her every day of the week, and about four people in this building have asked to see one of those four accounts in four years and about four have not and the four that have not are the reason the four that have were refused so quickly.

The man of the north row with the cough was in the room for about half an hour and sat by the fire and was not asked anything, and his two counts are not printed in this book and have not been printed in it for four years, and the number of times he has offered to be somewhere he is not the engineer of record for is a small one that has not moved in this month, and his refusal in an earlier month of somebody else's arm is not a fifth account of that arm and is not going to be one.

The register form is on the middle table where it has stood forty-four days now, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it and nothing at the foot of it, and the offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, not filled, refused on the nineteenth of this month, not withdrawn, no date on it, no name on it and no line on it for a name. The form with the four lines is in a drawer four miles and a bit west of here, face up, and its first line carries one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, and the other three lines are empty, and nobody has written a heading on that form and nobody is going to.

The sealing order is in a drawer in this building and is valid and opens nothing. The sheet that claims the corridor is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular, and the corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and naming it is not answering it, and a man in about four years can read either document in either drawer and will find nothing in either of them to hold against anybody.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a well four hundred miles off that a person read is not a use and gets no line. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-two, and the next of them is on the first of a month, which is one morning off this one and is not tomorrow, and no page in this building has ever given a reason why a man of about seventy is fetched on the first of a month and has never once asked which number it was.

The thing under the cloth at the end of the long table is living rootwood, the size of a man's fist, and it is warm, and it carries a control sequence for one valve and nothing else, and the count of people who have looked at it is two and did not move this afternoon. Nobody lifted the cloth this morning and nobody went near the end of that table, and a piece of rootwood that is warm on a cold day is the sort of thing that makes a room full of people look at it, and about four of the nine did not look at it and about four looked at the corner of it and stopped there.

Two people in this holding can hold a pattern for four and one of the two can hold one for five, and there are two rootmarks between them and one of those two is the reader of this body, and none of the three of them was in this room this afternoon and none of them is asked about what it costs. There is one person in the world who can hold a pattern for four and walk out of the ground afterwards and he is not going to be asked about that this month and the fact that he is not going to be asked is a fact about the price and not a fact about what he is for.

The compost line is paid, one hundred pounds, and the run is nineteen not discharged in nineteen, and the word before the figure has never once disagreed with the figure and may not, and the next of that line is on the first of a month and is not on any other morning, and it is not a measurement of the ground and is not going to be used as one by anybody.

The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a reading of a door is not a mark and a reading of a well four hundred miles off is not a mark and a month that ends is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room with a door shut on it and a yard at a well house, and the yard at the well house is four hundred miles off and is not this holding's yard, and the count did not move and was not going to. The count of times a man who keeps a book here has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move, and there were about twenty-five people in that yard at about the seventh hour and every one of them had a job in it and not one of them had a book.

There are ten questions available to a room in this holding and not taken, and it is ten, and it is not said in a yard, and nothing said in a room of nine this afternoon with the door shut is one of them. There are two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check and there have been two for two years and the first of them is still not named, and there are six things this holding does not know and no unit has ever been supplied for any of them, and neither of those columns is a column a person is supposed to be helping with. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and the two have never been added and the two are not the same list, and five bodies were at the node this month and there are five at the node this month.

A body has asked about who holds the pen once in four years and it has been once since, and the man who holds the pen in this holding is the clerk, and the clerk asked nobody about it, and the count of bodies that have asked about who holds the pen is a count of a question and not a count of anybody.

There is a sheet of paper for every morning of this year and there is one for every morning of the three years before it, and they go into a stack in the order they were used and nobody in this holding has ever improved the order of that stack. Each sheet has a line at the top with a day on it, and that is the whole of the difference between a day sheet and a page of figures. A man can do without one of them for a day or two and never notice, and then he can lose one in an afternoon and not get it back, and there is one in this building this month with a date that is not on it and a season and a quality of light where the date was, and that sheet is in the stack in its order like every other one and nobody has moved it to the top where it could be looked at.

The four bodies of households that come up that four-mile road with a barrow are at Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling, and not one of them is a member of the cooperative and none of them was made one this morning, and the barrow has gone up that road eleven times and did not go up it this morning because this morning was not a morning it goes. The next ninth-day return is on the fourth of the next month, four mornings off this one, and it is not tomorrow, and the instrument it goes on has hung on that wall for longer than this holding has had a book.

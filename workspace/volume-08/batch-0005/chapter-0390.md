# Chapter 390

## He Disqualifies Himself

The third of this month was a Sunday and the first line came round at the second hour and was worked. The boundary ditch and the two culverts took about six people until about the fourth hour, and the near culvert has been clear since the ninth and the far one was about a third full, and the six of them put two barrow-loads out of the far one and stopped there, because it has been going down by itself since the twenty-third of a month now two back and there is no need to be in a thing twice in a week.

The channel along the inside face of the west dyke has been finished on both days of it for a fortnight and about six feet of it at the top end is still soft, and the six of them stood at the top end and looked at it for about a quarter of an hour and then went and did the boundary ditch instead, and nobody in this holding has ever been told to do a thing about that six feet of channel and nobody is going to be, because the man who would have to be told is the man who said on Saturday that a third day of that channel is not this holding's to spend.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling did not come down the road this morning. He has not come down it on a Sunday in eleven years and nobody has ever asked him why and nobody is going to, and the question on the second of the four lines in his own book is forty-one days old this morning, and it has nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in forty-one days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road and did not rise this morning.

The figure for the third was entered at about the eighth hour on the Sunday and read out again out of the book with the frayed corner in a voice that does not change for a rise or a fall, and the two are the same.

**Eight hundred and eleven hundredweight.**

It has come down off Saturday by five and it is the hundred and fifty-third figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the first of it, and the fall went onto the falls, so the window the rises and the falls are counted in is a hundred and twenty with sixty-five rises and fifty-five falls in it.

A hundred and fifty-one days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fiftieth of them out of a hundred and fifty-two. It concerns four bodies of households and not the four launders, and the two aggregates have never been added together in four years and were not added this morning by anybody.

At about the seventh hour the two day-minus rules were read out loud, and both of the numbers were said before either of them, one man taking the first and the other taking the second. They stand at **five hundred and thirty-seven days** and **five hundred and eighty-four**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer is not opened. Forty-seven mornings out of seventy-seven since the seventeenth of a month three months back.

Iona Vey came up the fen road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, at her own cost, and was not sent for, and she was in that room for three hours with the door shut and she was the only person in it who had come from the office. She has been in this building three times in a fortnight and on each of the three she asked for something and got it and paid her own way each time, and this time what she asked for was a room with the door shut and about nine people in it and four hours.

She did not ask him anything about the reading. She did not ask him to confirm a place or an hour or a season out of his own memory, and she did not ask him for a copy of the order, and she did not offer this holding anything at all, and the offer on the low board at the far end of that room is the only instrument of that kind in this building and it was not mentioned by anybody this afternoon.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding with the door shut. The page with the three parts and the gap in it was on the long table where it had been put on Saturday, and the assessment was in the second book and had been read out on Saturday and had not been argued about since, and the sheet of the twenty-first with its five terms and its empty third column was on the middle table beside the register form, which is forty-seven days old and not filled and not refused.

He gave the reason before the thing. The count of that in the history of this holding is one hundred and seventy-four, and it is his and of nobody else's, and nobody thanked him and nobody was going to.

**"I am going to say a thing about myself out loud in this room and I am going to give the reason I am going to say it before I say it, and the reason is that about four people in this building have been working out since Saturday how to get past me, and they will manage it in about four years if I do not do it tonight, and I would rather the man it belongs to did it in a room with the door shut than have it done about me in a letter.**"

**"So here it is, and it is about the ground and it is not about what I am like. I cannot be put forward to carry that page. I cannot be the name on it, and nobody in this room is to write my name against the order that came out of that wood on Wednesday, and nobody outside this room is to be told that I am the man who could have.**"

**"And the reason is a fact about a page and not a fact about me. There is a date in this holding's book that ought to be against the second reading of that wood and is not against it, because I lost it, and it is not going to be put there by anybody adding up. A register carries a day and an hour and the name of the person who read it. There is no day on ours. So a man who has lost a day cannot be the check on the day, and that office's sheet came up on Saturday morning and every particular of it is correct, and this is the second thing that office is right about and I am not going to stand in this room and be it.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment. Nobody said he was sorry. Nobody told him that it did not matter and nobody told him that it was the right thing to do and nobody in that room softened a word of it, because there was nothing in it to soften: it was a fact about a page with a hole in it and he was the one who put the reading on the page and he was the one who has the hole, and a thing like that is not a confession and it is not a crisis and it is not a fall, and nobody in this room treated it as any of the three.

The woman who holds the pen asked him nothing and was asked nothing. She wrote for about two minutes and then read what she had written back out loud in the room, which is what she did on the nineteenth of a month now two back and which is the practice of this holding and not of her office.

"What is written is that the reading of the twenty-ninth of last month stands as it was entered, and that the man who took it is not to be put forward as a check on it, and the reason he gave, which is a date that is not on a page in this holding. I have not written that he thinks the assessment is wrong, because he has not said that and I am not going to put a sentence in a record that a man did not say to me. And I have not written that he is sorry, for the same reason. There is nothing else on the page and there is nothing else going to be on it."

Nobody asked him afterward whether that was right, and nobody is going to, and the reason is on a page in this building and has been on one since the twentieth of a month now two back, and the clause was read out in this room a fortnight ago and about nine people heard it and about nine of them have not stopped hearing it.

He went out of that room at about the seventh hour and did not go up the road and did not go into the wood, and about four people in this building have worked out inside a minute that he was not going to, and about four have worked out that he is not going to be told about it either, and the two fours are the same four and none of them has said a word to him about any part of the afternoon, and the man of about fifty went on with the four lines he was entering and did not look up.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling read the four lines the man of about fifty entered, with his hat on his knee, and did not say anything about them, and then he said one thing, which was that the fourth line came round tomorrow and would be empty again, and the man of about fifty said that it would, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said that was right and put his hat on. Nobody has asked him anything else and the question on the second of the four lines in his own book is forty-one days old and has no answer on it and nobody in this holding is going to put one there.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick came as far as the door at about the seventh hour and stood in it and was not asked in and was not asked out, and the woman who keeps the seed house said afterwards that there was bread in the seed house and that the loaf from last week was still good, which is the only thing anybody in that room said for about an hour, and she said it because somebody had to and she is the one who can say it without it meaning anything.

The man of about fifty entered it in four lines on a page of his own and did not put a mark against any of them, and the four lines are the only place in this holding where that morning is written down by anybody else, and he did not read it back, which is the second time in four years he has not read a thing back and the first time he has not read back a thing that was his own.

The page with the three parts and the gap in it did not change. It went out of this building on the ordinary post on the Monday morning, unsigned, and a copy of it went to the four bodies of households at Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling, who are not members of the cooperative and were not made members, and it went without a name on it, and nobody in this holding is going to be that name, and the ordinary post goes up that road twice a week whatever anybody in this building is doing.

And here is the whole of what happened to the thing on the table, and it is not more than that. The proof is a true thing that cannot be carried by anybody who can be asked whether it is true. This holding keeps it. Keeping it and being able to use it are not the same operation and one of them does not lead to the other, and a refusal to use a thing this season is not a failure of the work, and the work is four years old and it is not going to be measured this season either.

The stall is not a decision anybody took. There is no minute in this afternoon in which about nine people in a room with the door shut agreed to do nothing, and if a person in about four years wants to know when the proof stopped moving, the answer on the page is that there is no such minute, and the two documents that came up the fen road on Saturday and on Saturday morning are both correct and both are in this building, and the correct thing to do with a correct document from an office is to enter it and not to answer it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in that room and said nothing about any of it, and the cut at the kerb stays open, and nothing goes into the two-foot gap this season, and the key is in her hand and was not used and was not asked about, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and is nil. The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and the sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and cannot be put down by anybody here, and the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and was not walked and not measured and not crossed and not explained and has no dimension on any page, and no person in this holding has an instrument that says whether a bridge that was run once is on or off.

The offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now two back and it has not been withdrawn and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name. The register form is on the middle table, forty-seven days, not filled. The form with the four lines is in a drawer four miles and a bit west of here and its first line carries one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, which is the figure of the twenty-second return, and nobody wrote a heading on it.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a man disqualifying himself is not a use. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is five and did not move this afternoon, and nothing was read in any wood today and nothing was read into any, and the four ruled lines under the two words entered on the twentieth of a month now two back are still empty. The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a man refusing to be a name is not a mark and a lost day is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

There are two people in this holding who can hold a pattern for four and one who can hold one for five and two of them carry a rootmark, and none of that moved this afternoon and none of it is a thing a room of nine can vote on. The count of questions available to a room and not taken is ten and did not move, and it is not said in a yard, and the question anybody in that room wanted to ask this afternoon was not one of the ten and was not asked and is not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody and nothing was said that was meant kindly. The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five, and it did not move this afternoon, and this afternoon was a room with a door shut on it. There has not been a yard in this holding since Sunday the thirteenth of a month now two back, and there was not one today, and the thirteen things a man who keeps a book has said in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of are thirteen, and there is not going to be a fourteenth of them out of that room.

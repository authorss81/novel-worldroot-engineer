# Chapter 391

## Three Hundred And Twenty-Seven

The fourth of this month was a Monday and the second line came round at the second hour and was worked. The two gates and the about six feet of wall between them took four people an hour and a quarter, and the four of them were still on that six feet of wall at the seventh hour and were still on it at about the eighth, and the toe at the second of the three places is up a finger's width off what it was on the third of a month now two back and has been for a month, and the man at the front of it said that a toe which has been up a month is a toe that has stopped being a job and become a fact, and the three behind him did not answer him.

The gates are not what takes the morning. The second of them has a foot of wall out of plumb behind the post and the four of them put a bar and a wedge against it and write the date on the inside of the frame in chalk, and there are nine dates on the inside of that frame and none of them has been rubbed out and nobody has counted them and nobody is going to. It is the six feet of wall between the gates that has taken the hour and a quarter for as long as anybody in this holding has been keeping books, and on this morning the frost had got into the mortar at the bottom of it and about two feet of that came away in a piece and had to be laid again, and the man at the front of it said the thing about a toe and the three behind him did not answer him.

There is no engine in this holding and no instrument in it that was made for the work the two gates are on, and the four of them are on it four mornings a fortnight and the other four mornings are on the ditch and the culverts and the two places where the low ground lets water off, and the rota that puts them there has not been changed in four years and is not going to be changed this season.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not at that node this afternoon and nobody asked him to be, and the second of the four lines in the book he carries is forty-two days old this morning, and it has nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in forty-two days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that four-mile road and it did not rise this morning.

The fourth was not on paper until after the eighth hour, because the four on that wall were still on it, and the clerk read it back out of the book with the frayed corner and the two of them were the same, and a morning on which those two figures come out different is a morning this holding has not had in four years.

**Eight hundred and twenty hundredweight.**

Up nine off Sunday, and the hundred and fifty-fourth figure of a series that has not put the same figure on the page twice since the first morning of it. The window is a hundred and twenty-one and the rise went onto the rises, which is sixty-six rises and fifty-five falls, and it has not been in that shape before this week.

A hundred and fifty-two days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fifty-first of them out of a hundred and fifty-three. It concerns four bodies of households and the four launders are a different figure and the two have not been added in four years and were not added this morning.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud and this page carries no figure for the run. The four on the wall went on past the seventh hour and the man of about fifty was one of the four and had both hands full, and after that he had a return to set up at the node with a sling and a beam and a dial, and a man does not read a pair out loud between the second of those and the second hour of the afternoon. The pair stands at **five hundred and thirty-eight days** and **five hundred and eighty-five**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer is shut.

The four of them came up that road with the load and put it on the sling at the node and the sling went up and the beam came down slow, and the four bodies of households that weigh it are at Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling, and not one of them is a member of the cooperative and none of them was made one this afternoon.

The building the return goes into at that place has no roof and has not had one for as long as the oldest man in this holding can remember, and the north side of it is open to the weather and the south side has a wall about as high as a man's chest, and the steelyard is bolted into that wall at about the height of a man's shoulder, and the bolt holes have been enlarged twice. The four of them have been coming up that road with a barrow since before the cooperative had a book and they come because they are the ones whose load it is, and none of them has ever asked what the number is for and nobody has ever told them.

The return went in on the steelyard on the wall of that building that has no roof, and the instrument has hung on that wall for longer than this holding has had a book, and the man of about fifty had the second book open on a board and the pen out and the day sheet with two figures on it that he had written at about the fifth hour of the morning and had said nothing to anybody about.

**Three hundred and twenty-seven hundredweight.**

That is the thirty-fifth of them. It is the twenty-fifth pair of names to go against a ninth-day return in eleven years, and it stands eleven hundredweight above the thirty-fourth, which was three hundred and sixteen on the twenty-fifth of a month now two back. He took three hundred and twenty-seven off the dial, said the figure out loud, and then said it again out of the second book, and the two were the same.

It was reconciled twice, out of the two figures the man of about fifty had put on that day sheet, and the two ways it comes are two halves and not the whole.

**Two hundred and seventy-nine plus forty-eight. And two hundred and thirty-one plus ninety-six.**

The two halves have not been added to each other this afternoon and are not going to be added to each other by anybody. The word using is on the page of this holding's book and it is on the page this morning: this holding is using that derivation and is not refusing it. The reason that is said out loud rather than left is that a man who refuses a derivation is a man who thinks the number is in doubt, and the number is on a dial that has been on that wall longer than this holding has had a book, and about four people in this building would have preferred the other way round and about four would not, and the man of about fifty has never been one to prefer either.

He said why he had written two on a day sheet at the fifth hour of the morning and had told nobody, and the reason came before the thing, and the count of that in the history of this holding is one hundred and ten.

**"I am going to say why I wrote two ways of coming at that number on this morning's sheet and said nothing about either of them until this afternoon, and I am going to give the reason before I say it, and the reason is that a figure which can only be arrived at one way is a figure somebody chose the way of, and I would rather the two ways were on the sheet than that anybody in about four years had to trust the one.**"

**"And I am not going to tell this room which of the two I believe, and there is no believing to do. That dial was on that wall before this holding had a book and it said what it said this afternoon with nobody standing near it. The two of these are arithmetic and the arithmetic is not the finding, and a man who stands in front of a dial and makes a sum sound like a reason is a man who has started selling.**"

The board the names go on is a board and not a page of a book, and it is the same board it was on eleven years ago, and the two rules on it are painted on it in the same hand and the second of them has never once been filled, and nobody in this building has ever said aloud what would go in it if it were, and about four people have worked out what would go in it and about four have not and the four that have not have never asked.

The four of them wrote as they arrived, and not in any order this building chose. A name went into BY, and then one into WITNESSED BY, and then a name into BY again and a name into WITNESSED BY again, and the order of the four of them was the order they came up to the dial, and nobody has ever been able to say that any of them hurried.

The mark on that board is a set of three long lines and a date, and the dates on it are the dates of the returns and there are thirty-five of them, and the last date is today and the first is eleven years back in a month nobody in this holding can name out loud, and nobody has counted them at any time other than when a return is entered, which is what happened this afternoon.

The second rule on that board was left empty again this afternoon, and it has been left empty nineteen times in eleven years. Nothing went into it and nothing was said about it by any of the four or by anybody standing near the dial. It stood at eighteen on the twenty-fifth of a month now two back, which was ten mornings back, and the count of returns at which it has been left blank is nineteen, and a count of blanks is not the count of a man and has never been the count of a man.

Nothing was said about it afterwards either. The four of them came down the road and about half of them were talking about the frost, and the man of about fifty carried the day sheet with the two figures on it in his coat pocket and did not take it out again until he was in the one room at this building, and about four people in this holding have wondered in four years whether he ever says which of the two he believes and about four have not wondered about it, and he has never been asked.

About four miles and a bit west of that wall there is a drawer, and in it there is a form with four lines on it, and it is face up. The figure on the first line is one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, which belongs to the twenty-second return and to nothing else, and it is not the figure of the thirty-fifth and not the figure of the thirty-fourth, and nobody wrote a heading on that form this afternoon, and the three lines under the first one are empty, and they are going to be empty in about four years as well.

That form was brought back from that office in the spring and put in a drawer and has been in the same drawer since, and the one figure on it is thirteen returns old and the other three lines are empty, and a person in about four years is going to open that drawer and find a form with a number on it and nothing else, and there is no way from this building to put a second number on that form and there is not going to be one.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the seventh hour of the evening with the door shut, and the page with the three parts and the gap in it went out of this building on the ordinary post on Monday morning unsigned, and a copy of it went to four bodies of households who are not members of the cooperative, and nobody in this holding has heard anything about either of those two pieces of paper and nobody has asked.

The offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now two back, and it has not been withdrawn, and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name, and it does not lapse on the day it was made and it did not lapse this morning. The register form is on the middle table where it has stood forty-eight days, not filled and not refused, and nobody wrote a heading on it, and the sheet of terms from the twenty-first is on the same table with its third column ruled and empty. Nobody in this building has been told to take that sheet off that board and nobody is going to be told and there is not going to be a second instrument of an offer on any table in this holding this season.

There are two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check, and there have been two for two years, and the first of them is still not named. There are six things this holding does not know and no unit has ever been supplied for any of them, and no seventh has been added this week for an assessment from an office or for a man who has put his own name down this afternoon. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and the two have never been added, and a body has asked about who holds the pen once in four years and it is still once.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a dial is not a use and a blank rule is not a use. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is five and did not move this afternoon, and a return is not a session because a return is a thing on a wall and a session is a thing entered in advance in a room with the door shut. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not at that node this afternoon and was not asked to be and the question on the second of the four lines in his own book is forty-two days old and has no answer on it and nobody in this holding is going to put one there this season. The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice and is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a dial is not a mark and a name in a book is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

The thing under the cloth at the end of the long table in this building is living rootwood, the size of a man's fist, and it is warm, and it carries a control sequence for one valve and nothing else, and the count of people who have looked at it is two and did not move this afternoon. Nobody lifted the cloth and nobody went near the end of that table, and the man of about thirty-eight of Marden was not in this building this afternoon and the key is in her hand and was not used and was not asked about, and the cut at the kerb stays open and nothing goes into the two-foot gap this season.

A dial, four bodies of households and a rule nobody fills is what that afternoon was, and nobody in that building thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and it did not move this afternoon, and a building with no roof with a barrow and four men in it is not a yard. The count of times a man who keeps a book here has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move, and neither of those two counts is on a column anywhere in this holding.

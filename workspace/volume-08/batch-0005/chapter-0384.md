# Chapter 384

## What Was Edited

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Monday, the third line of the rota came round at the second hour, and it was worked. The two places where the low ground lets water off, between the sluice and the north gate, took four people until about the fourth hour, and the second of the two had gone down another hand's depth since it was stoned and left on the twenty-third, so the four of them got a chain onto the grate and two pulled while the other two stood on boards they had laid over the soft ground, and the man at the front of it said that a thing which can be emptied with a chain and two men has been a thing they were stopping for a fortnight. Nobody answered him.

The frost was on the low ground until about the ninth hour and did nothing there that anybody will be able to point at in about four years. The toe at the second of the three places on that line is up a finger's width off what it was on the third of this month and has been for three weeks, and the four of them put a spade into it and a hand's depth of stone before they got the chain on. A toe that has been up three weeks is a toe somebody is going to have to come back and look at in about four years, the man at the front of it said, and nobody behind him took it up.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the yard at about the ninth hour with the barrow, which has a wheel out of true since the spring that nobody has looked at and that he has not asked anybody to look at, and he turned it at the top of the yard and went home. The second of the four lines in the book he carries has a question on it, and that page is thirty-five days old this morning, and there is nothing on it and no place on it for anything to go. Nobody in this holding has asked him about it in thirty-five days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that four-mile road and it did not rise this morning.

The figure for the twenty-seventh was entered at about the eighth hour and read back out of the book with the frayed corner before the margin was ruled, and the two of them were the same, which has been true on a hundred and forty-six mornings before this one and is the only test anybody has ever had for the morning.

**Seven hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight.**

It has fallen off Sunday by five. It is the hundred and forty-seventh figure of a series that has not put the same figure on the page twice since the first of it, and the man of about fifty keeps the whole of that series in a book of his own with nothing else in it, and he went through the book before he ruled the margin this morning, and the figure for the twenty-seventh is not one of the other hundred and forty-six, and the highest figure that series had reached before this month was seven hundred and forty-four, on the sixteenth of last month, and every figure it has put down since that morning has been above it, and this morning's is.

The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and fourteen, and the fall went onto the falls, which puts it at sixty-two rises and fifty-two falls.

A hundred and forty-five days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings out of a hundred and forty-six. It concerns four bodies of households. The four launders are a different figure and have never been put on the same line of a page as that sentence in four years, and nobody is going to do it this morning.

At about the seventh hour the two day-minus rules were read out loud before either of the numbers, one man taking the first and another taking the second so that neither of them has to be trusted with both. They stand at **five hundred and thirty-one days** and **five hundred and seventy-eight**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened. Forty-four mornings out of seventy-one since the seventeenth of a month two months back, and it is a habit and not a measurement, and the pair is said on the mornings it is said on and not on the others.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and a sheet came in on the ordinary post in the middle of it, in a fold, and the man of about fifty took it and did not open it and put it on the long table at the wall side and not on the middle table, and the middle table has the register form on it, standing forty-one days, not filled and not refused, with nothing at the head of it, and he did not put one paper near the other and nobody in that room asked him to.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick came as far as the door and stood there for the whole of it, as he does, and nobody asked him further in and nobody asked him to go. The woman who keeps the seed house said after a while that there was bread in the seed house and that the loaf from last week was still good, which is the only thing anybody in that room said for about twenty minutes, and she said it because somebody had to say something and she is the one who can say it without it meaning anything. Nobody from the office came up the fen road with the sheet. Nobody had sent for it and nobody was expecting it, and the ordinary post goes up that road twice a week whatever anybody in this holding is doing.

He opened it and read it out. It is the entry the men who keep the register at the office made of a list of places and hours they hold, and they sent it up because this holding holds a copy of the same nineteen places, and their book carries a head over every entry and this holding's paper does not, so somebody wrote one, and they said so at the top of the sheet in the ordinary way and did not hide that they had written it.

> The maintenance of the Crown Engine, a district entry, sent to this holding on the twenty-seventh of this month by the men who keep the register, on the ground that this holding holds a copy of the same places and the same hours. Under the head: the engine means to take off the branch at the following places, at the following hours, in the order set down here, and no office and no person and no body is named anywhere on the sheet as the source of the figures, and nobody has put one there. Under the head there are the nineteen places, with a season against each of them and a day and an hour against eight of them, set out in the order of that office's own book and not in the order of any other book, and the nineteen are the nineteen that came out of the standing willow at the node on the fifteenth of this month, in the number of them and in nothing else. At the foot, in the hand of the man who wrote the head: this is a copy of an entry and not an original, and the office has not been asked to certify it and will not be asked.

It was correct in every particular. Every place on it is a place that is on the list this holding has, and every hour is an hour that is on the list, and the eight that carry a day and an hour are the eight that carry one here, and the office did not make an error and did not improve anything and did not leave anything out. What it has done is put a sentence where there was a list, and the sentence has a subject in it, and the subject is a machine.

He gave the reason before the thing, and the count of that in the history of this holding is one hundred and seventy-two, and it is his count and of nobody else, and nobody thanked him and he did not expect to be.

**"I am going to refuse the sentence at the top of that sheet and I am going to give the reason I am going to refuse it before I refuse it, and the reason is a fact about what a document does and not about anybody's character, and there is no one in that office I am blaming and I want that said first.**"

**"A list of places and hours is a thing that cannot be held to anything. You can hold a list to a figure, because a figure is on it, and that is all. But the moment you put a sentence on it with a subject in the sentence, you have made a thing that somebody or something is doing, and a thing that is being done can be asked whether it ought to have been done, and then it wants an author, and the author is the part of that sheet that is not correct in any particular at all. The list stands. I am not refusing the list and nobody in this room is to go and look at the list again this afternoon.**"

**"And I am not going to say the sentence is false, because it is not false, and a page that refuses a document by calling it false is a page that has to be true about its falseness later. It is true. It is also a thing that cannot be used, and I am putting those two in the same breath because a room that only hears one of them will get this wrong inside a year.**"

Nobody improved it. Nobody argued him out of it, and about four people in that room had come in ready to say that an office which had gone to the trouble of writing a head over a list ought to be thanked for it, and about four had worked out while he was saying the second paragraph that the head was the whole difficulty and that a thank-you would have been an argument.

He was asked one question about the sheet, by the man of about fifty, and it was whether the office had been told what the refusal was, and the answer was that it had not and was not going to be this afternoon, and the reason for that, given in the same breath, is that a sheet which goes back up a road with a refusal written on the back of it comes back with the refusal in the office's hand instead of the entry in theirs. It went back in the fold it came in and the fold was not marked, and a sheet that has been marked on the back is not a sheet anybody can send back.

Nobody asked whose order it is. That question has been put in this holding four times in four years and has not been answered once, and it was not put this afternoon, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling had it in his mouth at about the sixth hour and did not put it, and the four who could see that he had it did not say so, and he took it out of the room with him the way he took the other thing out of the room on the twenty-fourth.

The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room with a door shut on it, and there has not been a yard in this holding since Sunday the thirteenth of this month. The count of times a man who keeps a book here has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move, and there was no yard this afternoon.

The man of about fifty did not write anything on the sheet. He asked the one question that is on the page above and said nothing else about the refusal, and about four people in that room had wanted him to say something about it and about four had not, and he is not a man who says what a room wants.

The sheet went back into its fold and into the drawer with the other papers, and the drawer is in this building and not five miles east, and the list is not in it, because the list is already in a drawer in this building and has been since the fifteenth, and a person who puts two copies of one list in two drawers has begun to keep a record of his own copies.

The clerk copied the head of the sheet out of it in his own hand onto the back of the day sheet, word for word, including the sentence, and did not copy the nineteen under it, because the nineteen are already entered in this holding's book in the order the wood gave them and a clerk who enters a second order of nineteen places is entering a second opinion. The head went on the back of the day sheet and the day sheet went into the stack in the order it was used, and that is the whole of what a document is in this building when it has been refused and not put down.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a sheet an office sent up is not a use and gets no line, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-two, and the next of them is on the first of a month, four mornings off this one, and nobody in this holding has ever said out loud why a man is fetched on the first of a month. The compost line is paid, one hundred pounds, and the run is nineteen not discharged in nineteen, and the word before the figure has never once disagreed with the figure.

The offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth and it has not been withdrawn and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line for a name, and nobody has been told to take it off that board. The form with the four lines is in a drawer about four miles and a bit west of here, face up, and its first line carries one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, which is the figure of the twenty-second return, and nobody has written a heading on it and nobody filled the other three.

The thing under the cloth at the end of the long table is living rootwood and warm and carries a control sequence for one valve, and the count of people who have looked at it is two and did not move this afternoon, and nobody in that room asked about it. The key is in the hand of the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and was not used and was not asked about, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and is nil.

Four inches along the inside of the left forearm, branching twice, and no different from what it was at the second hour of this morning. A sentence is not a mark, a sheet an office sent up is not a mark, and a refusal is not a mark either, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

The question of who holds the pen has been asked once in four years by a body and it is still once. There are ten questions available to a room in this holding and not taken, and it is still ten, and it is not said in a yard, and a thing said in a room of nine with the door shut is not one of the ten.

Nobody thanked anybody. The two numbers this holding has handed out and cannot check are still two and the first of them is still not named, and the six things this holding does not know are still six and no seventh has been added for a sheet that came up on the ordinary post, and nobody wrote a heading on any of the six blank columns of the well house door, and the seventh of those columns stands at fifty-two and is not read today, being read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next.

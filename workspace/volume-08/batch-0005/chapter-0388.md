# Chapter 388

## Three Clocks And A First

The first of the next month was a Friday and the third line came round at the second hour and was worked, and the month turned in the night and nothing in this building turned with it. The two places where the low ground lets water off, between the sluice and the north gate, took four people until about the fourth hour, and the second of the two was empty and stayed empty with the chain on the grate, and the man at the front of it said that an empty thing that was full on Tuesday is a thing that has found its level, and the three behind him did not answer him.

The low ground between those two places is about nine feet wide and it is the only piece of ground in this holding that holds water in a dry month, and it has held water in a dry month in eight of the last eleven years, and no man in this building has ever been able to say why in a sentence that would survive being written down. The sluice is a plank and a socket and a man with a bar, and the man at the front of the third line has been the man with the bar for eleven years and is not going to be asked to give the bar up, and nobody in this holding has suggested it.

A man came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow, turned it at the top of the yard and went home without stopping, which is what he has done for eleven years. The second of the four lines in the book he carries has a question on it and the page is thirty-nine days old this morning. It has no answer on it and no place for an answer, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in thirty-nine days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road and it did not rise this morning.

He has brought that barrow up those four miles since before the cooperative had a book, and the count of the journeys is a figure he has never asked for and never been given, and it is a floor and not a ceiling, and a man who turns a barrow at the top of a yard every morning for eleven years is not a man who is waiting for something.

It was the eighth hour before the figure for the first was on paper, and the man of about fifty had it in the frayed book before the clerk had ruled the margin, and the two of them were the same, and that agreement is the test and has been the test on a hundred and fifty mornings now.

**Eight hundred and seven hundredweight.**

It has fallen off Thursday by five. It is the hundred and fifty-first figure of a series that has not repeated itself since the first morning of it, and the count of that series does not go back to one at the turn of a month, and the fall went onto the falls, so the window stands at a hundred and eighteen with sixty-four rises and fifty-four falls in it.

A hundred and forty-nine days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence for that was written this morning for the hundred and forty-eighth of them out of a hundred and fifty. It concerns four bodies of households and not the launders, and the two have not been added in four years and are not added on the first of a month either. The pair of day-minus boards does not begin again at a turn either. Neither does the aggregate. Neither does the window. A month turning is a thing that happens to the paper and not to the counts, and every count in this building that is a count of mornings is a count of mornings.

At about the sixth hour the seventh column of the well house door was read for the second and last time in the pair, and the man of about fifty read it off the door with the ladder up and the clerk read it back to about twenty-five people in that yard, and the two of them were the same.

The walk to that door is a quarter of a mile and the ground on the low side of it is standing in water about ankle deep, and the man of about fifty went over the high side this morning with the ladder on his shoulder and the lamp in his other hand and came back the same way with the lamp still in his hand, and the lamp is oil and the oil is in this holding's own store and it is not on any line anybody keeps a figure against. About twenty-five people were in that yard because the yard is where the reading is done and not because anybody was told to be there, and about four of them had come for the reading and about four had come because the reading is on the first and the thirtieth and people have worked out in eleven years that a morning with a ladder against a door is a morning worth standing in.

**Fifty-four.**

It was fifty-three yesterday and from the first of a month it may not go back to fifty-three, and from the thirtieth it may not go back to fifty-two. That is the fifth consecutive-day pair in four years and the fourth year it has happened in is the fourth year this holding has had a book. There is nothing at the head of any of the six columns beside it and nobody wrote a heading on one of them this morning.

Then the man of about seventy was fetched, and it is the twenty-third time, and the count of fetchings goes off twenty-two onto twenty-three on this morning and on no other morning of this year.

The man of about fifty entered it with a day and an hour and nothing else on the line, and he said out loud, in about four people, why the line was entered that way, and the count of that in the history of this holding is one hundred and nine, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to enter this the way I have entered the other twenty-two of them, with a day and an hour and no question on the line at all, and I am going to give the reason I am going to enter it that way before I enter it, and the reason is that the moment a question goes on that line the man in it has been asked something, and he has not been asked anything this morning and he has not been asked anything in twenty-two of these.**"

**"And I am not going to write which number this one is on the page where a man can come and see it, and the reason for that is that a man who has never asked is a man who has not been asked, and those are two different things, and only one of them is his.**"

He was fetched at about the seventh hour and brought up the lane and put at the end of the long table, and he was not asked anything at all. He came on foot with a stick and the appointed person went out at about the sixth hour with a cart and came back with him in it, and nobody in this holding has ever asked him whether he would rather have walked, and nobody has ever asked him anything.

He sat where he was put and he had a cup of tea in front of him and he looked at the middle table for about ten minutes, and the register form is on that table and has been for forty-five days, and he did not ask about it. About four people in that room had been waiting eleven years for him to ask about a piece of paper and about four had given up on it, and the four that had given up on it were the ones who had watched him not ask this morning. The woman who keeps the seed house took his cup away at about the eighth hour and brought it back full and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what passed between them, and she is the one person in this building who can do that without it meaning anything.

He has never once asked which number it was and nobody in this holding has ever put the question to him, and about four people in the room had that ready on the tip of their tongues and about four did not, and the four that had it ready did not say it and are not going to. The next of these is on the first of a month, twenty-nine mornings off this one, and it is not tomorrow, and no page in this building is going to say why a man is fetched on the first of a month.

And the compost line fell at about the same hour, and the word PAID is on the line before the figure, as it has been on every one of these since the line began.

**PAID. One hundred pounds. Twenty not discharged in twenty.**

The run goes from nineteen not discharged in nineteen to twenty not discharged in twenty on this morning and on no other morning of this year, and the word has never once disagreed with the figure and may not. It is a line and not a measurement of the ground, and nobody in this holding has ever used it as one, and the man of about fifty ruled the figure in the middle book and copied it onto the day sheet and the two are the same.

At about the seventh hour of the morning, after the fetching and before the compost line, the two day-minus rules were read out loud, both numbers said before either of them, one man to the first and one to the second so that neither of them is trusted with both. They stand at **five hundred and thirty-five days** and **five hundred and eighty-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer is shut.

Forty-six mornings out of seventy-five, and the frame of that run is different this morning from what it was yesterday, and it is going to be said here once and not said again. The run began on the seventeenth of a month that was two months back when the eighth of this year was the current month. From today the eighth is over and the current month is the ninth, and the same run is of a month three months back. The boards keep their own frame and it changed at the same hour and the two frames are not interchangeable, and the two of them being about the same morning is a fact about a calendar and is not a thing anybody arranged.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fifth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the three things that fell this morning were entered separately, on three lines, with three hours against them, and about four people in that room would have liked them on one line and about four would not, and the man of about fifty entered them separately and wrote one sentence at the foot of the page about why, and the sentence was that a page with three things on it is a page and not a pattern, and that sentence is his and was said in this building a fortnight ago and is not improved on now.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick came as far as the door at about the sixth hour and stood in it and was not asked in and was not asked out. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the room and said nothing about the fetching and nothing about the compost line and nothing about the column on the door, and the cut at the kerb stays open and nothing goes into it this season and nobody has asked her for what she holds and that is not a favor anybody has done her. The key is in her hand and was not used and was not asked about, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and is nil.

The man of about seventy was still at the end of the long table at about the eighth hour when the room filled, and nobody asked him to move and nobody asked him to go, and he was not asked anything by anybody in about two hours, and about four people in that room would have found that hard to sit in and about four had done it before, on other mornings, without being asked to.

There is a sheet of paper for every morning and the sheet for this morning is the first one of a new month, and the paper of it is a different stock from the one under it, and it went into the stack in the order it was used, which is to say last, and nobody has ever improved that order and nobody is going to. The three lines are on it and the day is at the head of it and the hour is on each of the three, and a sheet with the day at the head of it and three hours under it is a page and not a pattern.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a fetching is not a use and a compost line is not a use and a column on a door is not a use. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is five, and all five were entered before the thing they were entered for, and nothing was read in any wood today and nothing was read into any. The man of about thirty-one of Silling's page is thirty-nine days old and has no answer on it and no place for one.

That compost line is the only figure in this building that has never been argued about and it is the only one nobody can improve, because the word PAID is on the line before it and the word has a meaning, and a bookkeeper who improves a word changes what the line says and a man who improves a figure has stopped keeping a figure. It has been entered on the first of a month for longer than this holding has had a book, and the money is real and the discharge is a matter for a day that has not arrived, and neither of those halves of it is a measurement of anything that grows outside the two gates.

The four bodies of households that weigh the ninth-day return are at Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling, and they are on that line and not the four launders, and the barrow goes up that four-mile road when it goes and the count of its journeys is eleven and did not rise this morning.

The register form is on the middle table where it has stood forty-five days and is not filled and was not refused, and the offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, not filled, refused on the nineteenth of last month, not withdrawn, no date on it and no line on it for a name. Neither of them has moved for a month and neither of them is going to be moved this afternoon, and the two have never been put side by side.

Nobody in this building has been told to take that sheet off that board and nobody is going to be told, and it has been on that board for a month now and its being there has not made anybody do anything, which is the only thing an instrument of that kind can be relied on to do. There is no post in this holding and there is no second instrument of an offer on any table in this building, and there is not going to be one this season, and the one there is does not lapse on the day it was made and did not lapse yesterday.

The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a month turning is not a mark and a man being fetched for the twenty-third time is not a mark and a line falling is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

Nobody thanked anybody. There was no yard in this holding this morning, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is still five, and this morning was a lane and a room and a yard at a well house a quarter of a mile off. The count of times a man who keeps a book here has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move either, and the thirteen of them are not going to be added to anything.

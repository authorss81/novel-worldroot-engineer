# Chapter 389

## Correct In Every Particular

The second of this month was a Saturday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour with nobody on it. There was nobody at the headland above Marden and the fields above Marden waited, and there are forty-eight of them, and all forty-eight are the fourth line. The standing of the rota is seventy-seven mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and forty-eight not, and twenty-nine and forty-eight are seventy-seven. Twenty-nine taken has not moved on a morning this season and did not move this morning, and no name in this holding stands beside a field that did not get worked.

A field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man. It is on this page for the twelfth time in a fortnight, and the reason it is not going to be dressed up is the same one it has carried every time: the number is the only part of it anybody is going to be able to check in about four years.

The two counts of the rotation do not agree and both of them go in. This is the twenty-fifth morning the fourth line has come round since it began coming round on the twenty-sixth of a month three months back, and it is the twelfth of them since the eighteenth of a month two months back, and the frame of that second count moved with the month yesterday morning and it is the same count and not a new one. The man of about fifty has entered both in one line with the hour against it and has not reconciled them and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the road at about the ninth hour with the barrow and the rest of them, and the second of the four lines in the book in his coat has a question on it, and that page is forty days old this morning and there is nothing on it and no place for anything. Nobody in this holding has asked him about it in forty days and nobody is going to ask him. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that four-mile road, and it is a floor and not a ceiling, and it did not rise this morning.

There was a hard frost on the headland until about the ninth hour and the six of them who were on it came down in the order they went up, which is not an order anybody chose, and the marker for the fourth place is a sacking needle in a lump of chalk that the man of about fifty keeps in the left pocket of his coat in this weather, and he was carrying it this morning and came in at about the tenth hour with frost in his eyebrows. The two gates and the six feet of wall on the second line are a day away and the toe at the second of the three places on that line is still up a finger's width off what it was on the third of a month now two back, and it is not going down.

The second went into the book at about the eighth hour and the man of about fifty read it back out of the other one with the frayed corner, and the two of them are the same, and nobody in this holding has ever made a rule about what is to be done if they are not.

**Eight hundred and sixteen hundredweight.**

Up nine off yesterday, and the hundred and fifty-second figure of a series that has not come out at one figure twice since the first of it. The window the rises and the falls are counted in is a hundred and nineteen, and the rise went onto the rises, which is sixty-five rises and fifty-four falls.

A hundred and fifty days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and forty-ninth of them out of a hundred and fifty-one. It is a sentence about four bodies of households. The four launders are a different figure, and there is no page in this holding on which the two together would look like anything except a mistake, and nobody in this room has wanted to make the mistake this morning.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud and this page carries no figure for the run. The man of about fifty had been in the one room at this holding since about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut and three things out on the long table in front of him, and about nine people were standing about that table, and a pair of boards is a habit of the seventh hour and the seventh hour went by inside a room with a door on it. The pair itself stands at **five hundred and thirty-six days** and **five hundred and eighty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer is shut.

Then about nine people were in that one room and the door was shut and the proof was put together on the table in front of them, and it took until about the ninth hour.

What it was made of is worth saying in the order it was made. A standing, entered in a room with the door shut on the twenty-first of last month, in two hands, with a third column ruled and empty. A settlement, entered in advance on the twentieth of last month, with four ruled lines under it and nothing under those. A schedule, read out of a wood at the node on the twenty-ninth of last month, in the order that wood keeps it in. The nineteen places, out of the same wood on the fifteenth, and the eleven that came out of it on the twenty-third, and the two orders those two sheets are in. Five sessions entered in this holding's book, every one of them entered before the thing it was entered for.

And under all of that, the ordinary work of two years of it, which is four years of the two gates and the six feet of wall and the barrow's eleven journeys and the ninth-day returns and a hundred and fifty-two mornings of a figure off the third launder and a hundred and fifty days of an aggregate that has not moved and seven readings of a column on a door, and four years of mornings on which four people put a bar and a wedge against a gate and wrote a date in chalk on the inside of a frame.

The proof has three parts and one gap and it was read out in that order.

That the engine means to take off the branch at nineteen named places, in a season, with a day and an hour against eight of them. A maintenance schedule does not take a branch off anything. A maintenance schedule keeps a line going, and a line that has gone out is put back, and there is no line in this holding or in four counties about it that puts a branch off a growing thing as an item of work.

That the order puts the whole of the small ground at the north end of the pan after the wide branches below the sluice, and that a man keeping lines keeps the wide one first because the wide one feeds what is behind it, and this order does the other thing, and it does it in the same week every year, and the list has been coming out of that wood in the same shape for a season.

That nothing on any of the nineteen has a name on it, and no line of it has an office on it, and the eight that carry a day and an hour carry those two things and nothing else.

And the gap, which is the one that cannot be closed this afternoon, and it is this: the order is the order that wood keeps, and a wood keeps one order, and there is no second ground in this holding or in four counties that will give up the same order, so there is nothing anywhere to check the order against. The eight places that did not come out of the wood on the twenty-third are not in the order and did not come out of it on the twenty-ninth, and the order has no gap in it where they would stand, and nobody in this room guessed at them and nobody is going to.

About four people in that room have wanted those eight to be the whole of the answer for a fortnight. They are not, and this holding does not know that they are not, and the page does not say it either, because a page that says a thing is not the whole of an answer is a page that has said what the whole of the answer is.

It is incomplete and it is credible, and both of those are true in the same breath of the same afternoon, and the man of about fifty wrote the two words at the head of that page in that order because putting them the other way round would have been a claim and it is not a claim.

Then the sheet came in on the ordinary post in the same fold the other one came in, and the clerk read it out, and nobody asked him to stop before the end of it, and what he read is entered here as it stands on the sheet.

**An assessment of the standing of a schedule held by this holding, made by the office that keeps the register, and correct in every particular of it. That the schedule was read out of one wood by one man, and that a man cannot be the check on his own reading, and that the office's register does not accept a reading that has no other man in it. That the figure in this holding's own book is a copy of a figure the same man made, and that a copy in a book of a man's own making is not an instrument. That the register carries against every entry a day, an hour and the name of the person who read it, and that there is no such name against this schedule in this holding's book and no column on any page of it that such a name could go in. That the day the second reading of that wood was carried out is not on the day sheet in this holding, and that a reading with no day against it cannot be entered in a register that requires one. That an order which only one ground will give up cannot be compared with an order from any other ground, and that a schedule whose order cannot be compared is an account and not a schedule. And at the foot of it, in the hand of the man who wrote it: nothing in this assessment is a statement that the reading did not happen or that the places are not places or that the order is not the order, and this office has found no figure in this holding's books that is wrong, and this sheet is not a refusal and is not a withdrawal and does not say that the schedule is not what it is.**

Nobody in that room said it was a lie. Nobody improved it. About four people in that room had come in with a sentence ready for an office that was about to be told it was wrong about a document, and about four had worked out while the clerk was reading that there was not going to be one, and the four with the sentence ready put it away and said nothing about having had it.

The reader of this body gave the reason before the thing, and the count of that in the history of this holding is one hundred and seventy-three, and it is his and of nobody else's, and nobody thanked him.

**"I am going to say two things about that sheet and I am going to give the reason I am going to say them before I say them, and the reason is that about four people in this room have been waiting a fortnight for something they could put in front of somebody in about four years, and if either of these two is not on the page next to the other one, then whichever one is missing is the one somebody in about four years will use.**"

**"The first is that the proof on this table is incomplete. It is incomplete in three places and one of the three is a date this holding does not have, and I am not going to close any of the three this afternoon, and an incomplete thing that is credible is worth more to this holding than a complete thing that nobody can get to the bottom of, and I would rather a person in about four years found a hole in our page than found a page with nothing in it that we had made up on an evening.**"

**"The second is that the sheet the clerk has just read out is correct in every particular, and every particular of it can be checked against a page in this building inside a quarter of an hour, and not one of them is wrong. It is not a lie and nobody in this room is going to call it a lie, because calling a correct document a lie is the only mistake anybody could make with it this evening, and it is the mistake that would cost this holding the only thing it has got.**"

Nobody argued with him about either of the two. The man of about fifty wrote the assessment into the second book in full, word for word, and did not enter an opinion of his own on the page with it, and read the whole of it back afterwards in the ordinary way.

The proof went onto a sheet of this holding's own paper, in his own hand, with the three parts in three paragraphs and the gap in a fourth, and a head at the top that says what the page is, and nothing at the head of it about who is standing behind it. The man of about thirty-one of Silling read the whole of it with his hat on his knee and did not say anything about any of the three parts and did not say anything about the gap, and about four people in that room had wanted to ask him about the gap and about four had worked out in the time it took him to read it that the gap is the part he cannot help with and that asking him about it would be a way of telling him what he is for.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in that room for the whole of the afternoon and said one thing, and the thing she said was that the cut at the kerb stays open and nothing goes into it this season while she holds what she holds, and nobody had asked her, and she gave no reason for saying it, and nobody in that room gave a reason for anything before saying it this evening except the two who did, and the count of that is his and hers and nobody else's.

There are ten questions available to a room in this holding and not taken, and it is ten, and it is not said in a yard, and none of the ten was taken this afternoon although about four people could have taken any one of them.

The offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now two back and it has not been withdrawn and it has no date on it. The register form is on the middle table, forty-six days, not filled, not refused, nothing written at the head of it, and the sheet of terms from the twenty-first is on the same table with its third column ruled and empty, and the three of them have been on that table inside a month and have not been put side by side and are not going to be.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and an assessment from an office is not a use. The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is five and did not move this afternoon, and a proof assembled in a room is not a session, because a session is a thing entered before it happens and this was not entered before it happened. The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice and is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning, and a proof is not a mark and an assessment is not a mark and a correct document is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room with a door shut on it, and there has not been a yard in this holding since Sunday the thirteenth of a month now two back.

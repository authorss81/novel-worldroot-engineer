# Chapter 40

## A Front Out Of The West

Jonas Byre said it at breakfast on the tenth of the month, without stopping, in the middle of other people's sentences, because a man who has read a board every morning for thirty-one years does not build up to it.

"The glass has been going down since the second and it went down a tenth yesterday and a tenth again in the night, and the eels are up the north ditch, and there is a smell off the high ground to the west that I could smell in my own yard in the dark. And I am not saying what any of that means, because I am a miller and not a seer, and I have said for thirty-one years that I do not know what a number means until somebody tells me, and nobody has told me what a barometer means either."

Rowan Teale's boy came up the fen road at the ninth hour with no number at all, which was the first time that had happened.

*Wednesday, ninth of the month. No wheel. The sluice is shut and the race is out of water below the second sluice and there is nothing to chalk. I have written that down because the board has been empty three days running and I would rather it had a line with words on it than three days of nothing. — Rowan Teale.*

Dunmar Fitch entered it as a thing reported, and then read back the last line of it twice, which he does not do for a thing that is only words.

"He has written a line with no figure on it," he said, "which is the first entry in a miller's hand in nineteen years that is not a figure, and I would like the room to understand that a man who has chalked a figure on that board every milling day for thirty-one years has decided that the honest number today is that he has got one."

---

The caution came up the fen road at two in the afternoon in the same brown envelope as the circular had come in and with the same frank on it, and it was a single printed sheet with a form number in the corner and no date filled in by hand at all, because a caution is not a document and does not pretend to be one.

*Well Directorate, Tarrow district. Water-supply bylaw, second part. Caution. Rain on the main drain, three days, from the twelfth day of this month. A rise in the fen at the low ground of Fennmere on the second night of the rain or the night following. The gauge at the third sluice will rise and the district has no instrument that will measure the low quarter and does not pretend to one. The body last responsible is cautioned.*

*Form C/2.*

Perrin Osse's note was four lines and it was not in her hand; it was in a clerk's, and she said so at the gate.

"I did not write that. I want you to know that a district clerk of twenty-two years' standing did not write that, and I want it read in the yard and not taken as a thing from me.

"There are two boxes at the foot of that form. One says *served on the body last responsible*, and one says *no holder, affixed at the works*. On the twenty-eighth I could tick the first box, because on the twenty-eighth you had a schedule of damage against you and a file with a report in it and a clerk who had entered a cause. Today I cannot tick the first box, because the body last responsible holds no charter, there is no holder, there has been no holder since the eighteenth of a month, and if I serve a caution on a body I cannot name then I have served it on nobody and I have written a document that is a warning and not a notice.

"So it went in the second box," she said, "and it is on your well house door, and it is a piece of paper that nobody is legally required to do anything about, addressed to a body that does not exist, and it has the district's name and a form number and a rain date on it.

"And I would like to say the other thing, because I have got about a minute and there is a man behind me with a satchel. I have not found out why the file does not travel and I have stopped looking, and it is not my file and the man it belongs to is in this district through the end of the year. I have written on the caution that the body last responsible is cautioned, and I have written it in a form that says *no holder*, and I would like somebody to notice that I have done the correct thing and that the correct thing has produced a warning that nobody is obliged to obey, and that is not a scandal. That is a form. A form is the only place anybody in this district has ever kept a truth and the forms are not written by me."

She got back on the horse and went down the lane before anybody could thank her, and Ilsa Kern, who has never thanked a district officer in her life, watched her go and said, "That is a good woman and she is frightened of a file she does not own, and those two things are the same size."

---

The glass went down again in the afternoon and the west came up grey and low and stayed grey and low, and everybody in the yard knew it and nobody said the word storm, because saying it in a yard in February starts an argument about a thing that is not here yet.

They argued about the burn instead, which was worse.

Ilsa Kern and Jonas Byre had gone to Fennmere on the ninth morning, on the mill cart, without telling anybody, and they had asked at the sub-office counter whether the district's hazard measure could be varied — the east forty yards only, the milfoil ground fenced out, a different day — and they had come back at four with the answer written on a sheet of district paper in a hand that was not Perrin Osse's.

"A measure is not varied by the body it is served on," Anneke Roos said, reading it. "A measure is varied by the district, and the district varies it by issuing a new form, and a new form is a new measure, and a new measure goes out on the sixth or the twelfth of the month, and the officer's sitting is on the twenty-first, and the rain is on the twelfth, and there is no date on this piece of paper at all, is there."

"No," said Ilsa Kern.

"Then it is not a variation," said Anneke Roos. "It is a man at a counter being told that it is possible, by a person who did not say no and did not say yes, and that is the third time in a month that this cooperative has been told something is possible by somebody who is not responsible for it. And I would like it entered in our book that two members of this body went to a district counter on their own account, without the body's leave, on the ninth, and asked a question that four other members were not asked, and that the reason they went is that they are the two people in this yard who stand on the west side of the windbreak every day of their working lives.

"And I would like Marek Vale to say to this yard whether he objects to it, and I would like him to say it in one sentence, and I do not care which way it goes."

Marek said: "I have no standing to object with, and if I had, the two of them are not people I could have stopped, and the reason is that a body which needs a charter to be obeyed is not a body, it is a notice with a seal on it, and I have been learning that for six weeks and I have not learned it well enough to do anything with it yet."

"Enter it," said Dunmar Fitch.

---

The fourth margin note went in at the sixth hour on the tenth of the month and it went in on the same page of the same book as the other three, in the same hand, and it is the shortest of the four and it is the only one that is not a plan.

*Thirteen days since it was refused. A bypass of the branch at Weir Row was proposed on the twenty-seventh of last month and refused by five names, each of them correctly, and it remains unbuilt. On the sixth of this month four men offered to dig it in three nights on terms concerning a book, a claim on the maintenance of the line, and an undertaking that cannot be withdrawn, and the offer was refused on four grounds and a fifth, and the grounds are in the cooperative's book and they are all correct. On the night of the ninth four of those men came up the lane to this gate at the first hour and were not asked in, and went on down the fen road, and nobody in this yard knows what they did or did not do. The bypass will not be built before the water. A caution under the second part of the bylaw is on the well house door and gives rain from the twelfth of this month and a rise in the low ground of Fennmere on the second night. It stands.*

*This is worth nothing against a cut.*

Dunmar Fitch ruled the line and entered the page number and the hour, and then he said, without looking up, that the closing sentence was the same on all four of them and that he could change it on this one, because a cut and a flood are not the same thing and a form that fits is a form that gets read.

"Change it," said Marek. "No."

"Then I will write it as it stands and I will want you to know that I am the clerk of this body and I have been the clerk for nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years refused to write a sentence a man asked me to write, and I am refusing one now because of the shape of it and not the sense of it, and I would like that in a working note with the hour, and I would like your mother's initials on it, because she is the one who taught me what a form is for."

Lissa Vale initialled it.

---

The last hour of the tenth went the way the last hour of a day like that goes, which is that four people stand about a well house bench and one of them says the obvious thing that everybody has been not saying.

"The first of every month," said Gael Senn. "The certificate. Examination, and something put into the return main, on the first of every month, signed by me. And the first of this month was the first day, and the first of next month is in what is it, twenty-one days?"

"Twenty-one," said Dunmar Fitch.

"Right," said Gael Senn. "So there is a wound in that pit with a piece of sacking on it and a quarter of an inch of wet at the six o'clock corner, measured twice, dated, not a leak, and the return on it is due on a date that is twenty-one days away, and the rain is on the twelfth. I have been sitting on that all afternoon and I have got two answers and I do not like either of them.

"One: if the water comes on the thirteenth, the examination happens on the first of next month, on a body that is under a foot of fen, by a woman who may be living somewhere else. And the certificate does not say one word about that, because the certificate was written by a licensed engineer for a monthly return in a season when the ground was going over sideways and nobody thought about the weather, and the charter schedule does not say one word about it either, because there is no charter and there is no schedule, and there is nothing in this fen that says what a wound owes a river.

"Two: I could go down that ladder on the day it comes and write something. And I have thought about it and I am not going to, and I would like the yard to understand why, because I have been the youngest person in every room since I got here and I have been waiting all month for a thing I am allowed to do that I have not been allowed to do.

"I am not going to write an entry about a thing I have not looked at," she said. "It would be the same rule as *nothing found* and it took me three weeks to see that it is the same rule, and I have just worked it out on a wet Thursday and I am not going to be the woman who fills that book in advance with what she hopes to find on the day. So the first of next month is the first of next month, and if the water is over that pit on the first of next month then somebody who is not me is going to have to write *nothing found* on it and I am going to hand her the rule and the two rules and the log and I am not going to have written anything."

Nobody said anything, and then Marek said the thing he had come out of the seed house to say, which he had been carrying since two o'clock and had only found at the end of the day.

"Right," he said. "Then here is the only thing in this yard that costs nothing and can be done tomorrow, and it is not a bypass and it is not a fire and it is not a reader.

"There is a reed gauge clamped on the return main at the eleventh rod. It has been on the peg since the fourth of the month. Jonas has read it every morning since and written it and signed it, and nobody has read it today, and in four days' time the only number that will tell us whether this node is still taking what the log says it takes is going to be a thumb on a cheap reed that we screwed to a pipe in a ditch in a storm, by a man who is not a rootwright and not an inspector.

"So at the fourth hour tomorrow, before anything else, Jonas and Gael and Ilsa go down to the eleventh rod and read it and write it and enter the load under it, and if it has moved we will know on the morning of the rain and not on the night of it, and if it has not moved then we will at least have the number that says so.

"And after that," he said, "all four of us are on the embankment at the low corner, at the first hour, with bars and boards and the mill boy's lamp, and not one of us goes in the pit, and there is no licensed engineer in this fen and there may not be one for a fortnight, and that is a thing that will have to be got through and not got round."

The glass went down another tenth at about seven and the wind came round to the west-north-west at about nine, and it was not a gale and it was not going to be a gale, and Marek stood at the gate in the dark and looked up the fen road at nothing for a while and then went in, because there was a lamp in the seed house and the bin needed sweeping and the four crates of somebody else's seed were standing open in an annex at three and a half degrees with nothing whatsoever underneath them.

The ditch at the head of the field was wet the whole thirty-one yards. The four willow sets were in it, green, holding their lashings. Nineteen acres were standing with white heads and no sheep and no cart. The three acres at the top end were the colour of a wet road and the crust had not moved since the Sunday of a month ago and would not crop for two seasons and could not be entered in anybody's book.

And at the eleventh rod, below the well head, past the fence post, a cheap reed gauge screwed to a pipe in a ditch was sitting on the peg, holding a number that four people in a yard were going to go and read in the morning, and thirty or forty yards of a ditch that would have changed where the water went was not dug, and would not be dug, and was worth nothing against a cut.

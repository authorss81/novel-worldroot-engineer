# Chapter 38

## The Line From The Tenth

The hearing officer came to Tarrow on the eighth of the month because Tarrow was one of four districts on her circuit and the other three had dates and Tarrow had a tray.

Hester Carrow was about forty and had read three files before nine o'clock and had a bundle tied in tape on the counter in front of her, and she was not going to be in the district more than a day. Perrin Osse had told the cooperative on Monday, in four lines and a square hand, that the officer would be at the counter at ten and would be glad of anything the cooperative wished to bring.

They brought four things, and the reason they brought four is that the counter had certified a copy of their book on Monday and every one of them had spent a night being unable to look at it.

Ansel Bray came in out of the back room at a quarter past ten with a satchel and his coat over his arm and no umbrella, and he had not asked for this and did not want it and came anyway, and the cooperative's file records that as a thing that happened, twice now.

"I will be twenty minutes and then I will go out and stand in the yard," he said. "Mr Fitch is going to tell you I have no power here today and he is right. I am not the officer who determines anything; I report and I give evidence. If any of you tells that officer the inspector had a view, I will deny it and I will be believed."

Then he put the satchel on the counter and squared it to the edge, and did not open it.

"The three things, at the front, because I say them every time. If I find unregistered maintenance on a non-scheduled branch I may seal the head, take the seed stock into district custody, or refer for relocation. I am not sealing the head. I am not taking the seed stock. I have no immediate-risk finding and the finding of the tenth stands."

"And none of those three is what I am here about, and I would like the room to sit with that, because it is the dangerous part of this morning and it is not in the three."

---

Bray took out one sheet, and it was the certificate of copy Perrin Osse had written on Monday afternoon, and the six words at the foot of it, and *so far as it goes*, and he put his thumb on that.

"Mr Vale. On Monday this office was given a copy of your standing pressure log, entire, on the certificate of the clerk of this sub-office, which cannot be recalled by anybody who is not an officer of this office. It was not a district document on Sunday and it is one now, and you put it there. I say that first because otherwise this morning looks as though I did something on Monday and today I am coming for it."

"That copy carries two entries about a fieldbook at the copper seam of this well head with a licensed reader and witnesses: the twenty-fourth day of last month at the tenth hour and forty minutes, and the twentieth day of last month. I read the first of them out loud at that counter on the twenty-eighth in front of that clerk, and it has been in a district file since."

"And the instrument those two entries name is not in this office. It has not been here since the tenth day of a month, when it was produced to a district inspector and read out in this room by a person holding it, and neither that inspector nor that person is licensed to read it. So there is a document in this district that describes an instrument, and the instrument is in a press in a fen, and the woman keeping the press is not a licensed anything."

"And here is the part that would worry me if I were sitting where that officer is sitting. That copy is certified *so far as it goes*. So the day after Monday a page could have gone into that book, and it would be the district's business, because a page that goes into a certified book after the certificate has to be a certified addition and only I can initial one. I cannot find out from this counter whether one has, and that is what I am asking for, and it is not a reason about your fieldbook."

"I am not making an emergency seizure and I am not using the clause. I am asking for the instrument to be produced into the custody of this office, for preservation, pending a sitting. That is a records power. It needs no finding of risk. It needs a record, and I have one, and the record is yours, and you had it certified on Monday by a woman who is very good at her work and thought it was right."

He looked up.

"I would rather do this in front of the appointed officer than on my own authority out in a yard, and I am going to tell you the reason, and the reason is not generosity. An act done in front of the officer is an act the officer has seen."

---

Lissa Vale said no, and she said it before anybody had asked her to, and she said it standing up, which she had not intended to do.

"No," she said. "And I am going to say the rest of it in the right order, and I have been thinking about the order since Monday, and I would like it entered that I was not asked before I answered."

"I am the custodian. The instrument is in my press under the ledger and it has been there since the tenth day of a month, and it has not been out of that building since except for one journey to this counter and back, which is written down in a book I keep for that purpose, and I will produce the book."

"I am not going to let you take it out of my hands in a yard or in this room, and I know the second one is what you are actually asking for. I have thought about it for two days and I am going to give the reason, and the reason is not courage and it is not a rule."

"The reason is a sentence that is written at the top of this body's own book, in a licensed engineer's hand, with four witnesses against it, and it says that a reading is not a fact, a fact is a thing a stranger can check without believing anybody, and an unlicensed reading is a rumor with a witness in it. She said it on the thirtieth of last month, standing in a well house with one door, and she is eleven miles away, and she was right to say it and she said it about herself before she said it about anybody else, and there is a line of hers in that same entry that says she will be examined about the reading before Mr Vale is examined about anything."

"If that instrument goes into a file in this building, then in the forty-second a man takes it out of it in a cold room, and the only person in the world who can say what it says is the man holding it, and whatever he says will be worth exactly what a rumor with a witness in it is worth. That is what happened to my son on the night of the tenth of last month, and the only licensed woman in this district was the witness to it, and she has said in three books that she will not stand up and say what he got, and she is not going to say it for a woman with a good reason either."

"And if what you have come for is for me to say what my son saw in a pit at Weir Row, you have brought it to the right building, and I will not do it, and I will do it in front of this officer so that there is a minute of it. I was in this room on the tenth of last month at a counter, holding a different book, while a district inspector read the last page of that thing and set it down like a thing that had turned out not to be his. I did not see what he got. I have not read the page. The woman who saw it is eleven miles up the fen road, and that is not an accident, it is the arrangement, and you are standing in the middle of it."

"So you are asking me to move it out of a press in a fen, where it is in the room of a woman who says no more often than she says yes, into a file in Fennmere, where it will be taken out by whoever is sent for it. I am refusing, and I am refusing on the ground that it is worse for him."

Then she put her hand flat on the counter and took it off again.

"But I am not going to pretend to a hearing officer that I have no answer, because I have one, and it is the reason your clerk wrote five words at the foot of a certificate on Monday afternoon instead of a hundred. She wrote *so far as it goes*. That phrase is a lock and I put my thumb on it, and it does this: a page cannot go into that book now without you in the room. Which means there is a book of mine that is not in your copy, and it can never get into your copy without you, and it is the book you actually want."

"You may not have the instrument off my hands," said Lissa Vale. "You may have it off the shelf."

She turned to the hearing officer.

"Madam, I will produce the instrument into this office on a form. I will fill in the form and give you the hour, the place, two witnesses and the number of the page, and then it will be a thing with an owner and a receipt and a number in it, in your file as an act with a date on it, instead of a thing a man came and took out of a woman's press with a reason."

"And the book I keep for the instrument goes in as well. It is the book that answers your question and not the log, it is mine, it is seven columns wide, and it has been kept since the eleventh day of a month. All of it, copied in the clerk's hand and read back to me page by page, with the hour minuted. And I will read it. Not the officer, because the officer reading it is a thing that happens to me, and reading it is a thing that I do."

---

Hester Carrow had not moved once, and she asked the only question in the room that had not been about power.

"Madam Vale. Is there a licensed engineer in this district."

"There is one," said Lissa Vale, "and she is eleven miles away, and she was not in this room on the twentieth of last month, and I am not going to pretend to you that I know when she will next be in a room with the instrument in it, because I do not decide that and she does not decide it either. It happens."

"Then nobody in this room may open it," said the hearing officer, "including me, and I am going to say that on the record now while it is in front of me, and it binds this office as much as it binds you, and it will bind the officer who sits after me. Produce it on a form. Produce the log with it, and I will have it copied, and you will read it back, and I will minute the hour. Now."

---

Lissa Vale asked for ten minutes and got eleven, and she used them to say one thing before she started, standing in the public part of a district office with a book under her arm, in front of a clerk, an inspector, an appointed hearing officer and three people from a body with no charter.

"There is a line in that book from the tenth day of a month," she said, "and it is the first line in it, and I am going to read it, and I would like it entered that I said so before I read it and that nobody made me."

She opened it on the counter. Seven columns, and the first page was nearly empty, because there have only ever been two lines in the whole of it, and the second of them is from the twentieth of last month and the first of them is in her own square hand and the ink on it is four weeks old.

*Tenth day, seventh hour and ten minutes. Fieldbook, produced by M. Vale at the Fennmere sub-office, at the hour, at the request of the district inspector, for the reading of the last page and of the finding, in that inspector's own hand. Purpose: production. Licensed person present: none. Out. Back.*

*Back: tenth day, eighth hour of the day.*

She read it twice, and then she read the two hours again on their own, because the hours are the whole of it and a room does not hear a number inside a sentence.

"Fifty minutes," she said. "Out at ten past seven in the morning and back at eight in the morning, on the day the district asked for it, in this building, at this counter, with no licensed person present, in the hands of a district inspector who is not a rootwright and has never claimed to be one, and he read the last page and the year on it twice and set it down like a thing that had turned out not to be his."

"The district holds the receipt. It is in the district's file, in the district's own hand, and the district is welcome to produce it, and I would like the officer to note that the district and this cooperative are in the fifty minutes together, and that neither of us has ever said so in a document until this afternoon."

Nobody in the room said anything for a moment, and then the hearing officer said, "Who held it, in those fifty minutes."

"The district," said Lissa Vale. "Not a person. An office."

And then she said the thing she had come in ready to say, and she said it before anybody had asked for it, and Dunmar Fitch, who had understood what it was going to cost her about four seconds after she started it, put his hand over his mouth.

"Now the second thing about that line, and I am putting it in front of you because you will find it in a minute and I would rather you heard it from me."

"It is in my hand and it is dated the tenth, and it was written on the eleventh. I made the book on the eleventh. The instrument came into my press on the evening of the tenth and I wrote the line the next morning, and I have written the hour it describes and not the hour I wrote it, which is the rule in this body, written on the inside cover of the other book in that woman's hand. I have broken it, and Mr Vale will tell you so if you ask him, and I would rather he did not have to."

"Entered, then, before anybody looks for it: *the first line of this book was made on the eleventh day of a month and describes the tenth, and the lateness is one day.* I am not going to have it softened, and I am not going to have it read as a small thing, because a clerk of a cooperative lost a charter on the seventeenth of a month over one hour on one entry, and I know what an hour is worth in this fen and I am not going to be the woman who is surprised to be asked."

"I broke our own rule in the first line of the only clean book we have," she said, "and I have entered it, and let the officer minute it in the same words and not in a summary."

Hester Carrow looked at her for a moment, and then wrote, and read what she had written back before she turned the register round, which she had not done once in the whole of the morning.

And Anneke Roos, from where she had been standing since the reading began, said the sentence that the whole of the last month had been standing in front of.

"That book did not exist before the tenth," she said. "A woman in a seed house made it on the eleventh, because on the tenth the district put that instrument on a record. So the district's claim is not an attack on a document. The district's claim is the parent of the document. Let the officer write that down, and let somebody come and take it away from me, because it has been in this yard for four weeks and I cannot put it down."

---

Hester Carrow did not say whether the instrument should be produced on a form. She said she would take the application and hear it at the sitting, and she fixed the sitting, and she fixed it in a way nobody had expected.

"Twenty-first of the month," she said. "There is no earlier date I can hold, and I am not going to pretend this morning has brought one forward. Your representations are due in or about the forty-second, and I have a certified copy of your standing log in your file as of yesterday afternoon, and I will not hold a sitting on a file that is still arriving. That is the whole of the reason, it is not a reason about the merits, and I would like it written down that way."

"And I am going to say the thing nobody in this room has said for three weeks: the money is payable in fourteen days from the twenty-eighth, and Mr Bray's application falls to be heard on the twenty-first, which is after the money and not before it, and nothing in my power changes a date of payment."

She closed the bundle and squared it to the edge of the counter, and Dunmar Fitch saw her do it and understood what he was looking at, because Ms Quill had done the same thing to nine pages of raw notes against the edge of a seed house table in the first week of last month, and a clerk who squares a thing is a clerk who means what the thing says.

"Twenty-first," she said. "And madam, you will produce the instrument on the twenty-first, and the log with it, and you will read it, and I will minute the hour, and if the inspector's application is heard that day it will be heard on your copy and not on the original. That is not a thing I have decided in your favor. I have decided it in favor of the copy, because a copy of a book can be produced in a room where the book is not, and a book out of a press in a fen in this season is a book a clerk should not be asked to carry up a road."

That last sentence landed in the room and sat there.

Lissa Vale said, "The officer is going to have to come and get it," and then stopped, and understood what she had said, and did not take it back.

"Not before the twenty-first," she said. "And not out of my hands. On the twenty-first I will bring it and the log and I will read the whole of it, and I have spent this afternoon teaching this officer what is in it, so that on the twenty-first she hears it for the second time and not the first. Let that be entered, because it is the only piece of strategy I have and it is not much of one."

"And since I have been made to speak in front of a clerk this morning, I will say the other thing and then sit down. For eight years the only account of that instrument in the world was in a press in a seed house, and at two o'clock this afternoon it went on a form to a file in this building, and it is not coming back, and it can never be added to without you standing in the room. Let the officer minute that I am not sorry, and that I am not going to stand here and pretend to be."

Bray said, "I do not withdraw the application," and picked up his satchel, and then stopped at the end of the counter and said one more thing to nobody in particular, in the voice he used for things he meant.

"Mr Vale. On Monday a clerk of this office took a copy of your book because it was the right thing to do. I read it last night and it is the best-maintained record of an unchartered body of this kind I have read in twenty-two years, and it is going to be the exhibit, and there is not one page in it I would want to change."

"Your honesty made this possible and it is going to be used," he said. "I would rather it were me using it than an officer finding it on her own, and I have stopped trying to decide whether that is a kindness."

Then he went out into the yard and stood in it.

## Corrections carried in from the Batch 0003 review

Batch 0003 was reviewed after it was written and repaired in place, without restarting the batch and without changing the planned plot. A day in 1–20 is dated by its plain number, or *of last month*, or *of this season* — **never *of this month*.** A day in 21–30 is dated by the plain day number (*the twenty-second*, *the twenty-ninth*) or by its ordinal in the new month (*the first*, *the third*, *the sixth*). ## Movement 4 — Water Above the Embankment

`outline/volume-01.md` sets the work in this order, and the order matters:

- **The liability is a slow disaster.** Marek's own unlicensed work — especially the night of the tenth at Weir Row, in the dark, in a storm, with a crowbar and his own shears — is the Crown's best evidence. He cut open a three-foot seam on a branch with no local holder, on a Crown-maintained item, and the branch was cut again above him on the twenty-seventh and the bleed stopped. **He cannot prove that the schedule caused the low quarter's damage without first proving that he was on that branch.** The cooperative's strongest defense is a defense no hearing will accept from people with no seat.
- **Halden Pike's name appears on a Rootwright Assembly circular about unlicensed grafting.** It is a signature, a printed sheet, and a professional veto turned into personal liability. Nobody in the fen has ever seen Halden and nobody in the fen wants to.
- **The Crown offers a controlled burn of the willow windbreak** — the windbreak that protects the cooperative and severs the last wild root line to the weir. Note that **the cooperative has already cut about a hundred feet of it for their own graft in Ch 22**, and Ilsa Kern has said so in a book with the hour on it. Let somebody notice that in this batch.
- **The Thornwild courier refuses on behalf of a settlement with no seat at the hearing.** One courier, at the far edge, refusing, and the refusal is political: a settlement that is not represented cannot be asked to consent. **No Soren Rill in this volume.**
- **The Undercommons offers hidden help on conditions the cooperative is not ready to accept.** Marda Vole has been spent: one courier, one warning, one piece of procedure, paid in bread, and *she will not be here again this year*. **The offer must come from somebody else and it must be worse than rescue.** Make the conditions about obligation, not about secrecy for its own sake.
- **Bray attempts to seize the fieldbook as an unlicensed instrument, and Lissa refuses him in front of the hearing officer.** This **protects the instrument and costs the cooperative its clean record.** That is the whole point of the beat: Lissa wins and the cooperative loses. It must not be played as a victory for anybody, and the fieldbook must not be lost and must not be used.
- **Tova's seed annex and the first cross-fen seed exchange are signed**, so the trust's name is on the record whether or not the crop lives. **Her dry-land line stays lost. The March holds a copy of everything.**
- **A storm forecast closes the phase with the crew split over the burn and the bypass unbuilt.**

The batch's promise: **the cooperative kept the record, and the record is what is used against them, and the woman who kept it is the one who stopped it from being taken, and she lost something for it.**

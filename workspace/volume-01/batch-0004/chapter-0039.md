# Chapter 39

## The Name On The Annex

The cart came up the fen road on the ninth of the month in the middle of the morning with four crates on it and a man who had walked eleven miles behind it, and it took two hours to get the crates into the annex, because the second one went through the low doorway sideways and Ilsa Kern said something about doorways that Lissa Vale repeated later in the day for the benefit of nobody.

Four crates, and inside them, wrapped in wet sacking in the seed house's own manner, was four pounds of the Dryfoot Trust's own winter-sown rye, counted out of a counting house at Cray, entered against a line on a ledger, and carried nine hundred miles by a man who had been given a list and had lost the top sheet of it somewhere in the middle of the March road and had finished the journey on the list he could still read.

"It is not the dry-land," said Tova Reed, before anybody had asked her anything, standing over the crates in the annex with her sleeves back. "Everybody in this room has been waiting for me to say that for a month and I am going to say it in the first minute so that nobody has to be polite about it.

"The dry-land is lost. Three trays of it went into a coupling on the third day of a month in a shed in this fen because a man would not stop, and I ruled them and I did not soften it, and there has not been one hour since in which I have been willing to use the word *mostly*. It is lost. The March's last of it is a tin in a press under a ledger in the room behind me and it is at risk every night of this season, and I have said so in a book in this room twice, and it is still a tin and it is still in a press.

"What is in these four crates is the March's wet-line reserve, moved north four months early because our own ground has failed and we would rather send it to a fen that has standing rye than hold it against a bad year. Which means the exchange works both ways or it does not work, and the fen grows the March's line and the March grows the fen's, and the comparison goes in both directions, and the first row of it is a row of Longneck on the March road in the spring and that row is this holding's fault and the March's name on it, and if that row fails the March has nothing to compare the dry-land to for four years."

"How much of it will live," said Marek.

"At three and a half degrees, on a bin with nothing in it, on a rootwood bed that has been cold since the middle of last month," said Tova Reed, "and I have just put a rule in the middle of that tray, and the rule is the only instrument any of us has got. I would not take a bet on a third of it. I would take a bet on a third of it and I would not take it."

---

The annex was two sheets, and the sixth document of the season to go through that seed house table, and the only one of the six with a party on the second side of it, and it took one day to write and most of the day was about the signature.

"What I want," said Tova Reed, in the annexe that afternoon, "is four things, and three of them are the trust's standing conditions from the first day and the fourth one is new.

"One. A monthly report on the standing log's form, in the trust's own columns, copied to Cray whether or not it helps us, which was condition four and has not been withdrawn and I am not going to let a signature in a hurry weaken it.

"Two. The right of the trust, and of a person it sends, to come into the seed house twice in the year and go over the trays and the bed and the bin, and be told what the bed is doing, and the answer is to be the true one and not the answer that would make the trust look well.

"Three. The trust's material stays the trust's. It goes back with the account, and if it is grown here it is grown on the trust's account and not the cooperative's, and it is not the cooperative's seed and it will not go in the cooperative's appropriation, and I want that in a document and not in a habit, because habits end in February.

"Four, and this is the new one, and it is the reason I am here on a Wednesday instead of on a Friday. The Dryfoot Trust is a named party to the seed arrangements of the node at Lantern Reach. Not a lender. Not a visitor. A party, with a name on it, in a document that anybody can read, and the name is the Salt March Dryfoot Trust, of Cray, and the two words on it that are going to hurt are *Salt March*, because the March road is where the good seed comes from and everybody in a district water record knows whose ground that is."

"You are going to put your trust's name on a document attached to a node that has lost its charter and carries a bill of sixty pounds ten," said Anneke Roos, "and you have never once asked whether we can pay you back anything."

"I have asked," said Tova Reed, "on the third day of last month, in a shed at the back of your seed house, and you said no, and you said it correctly, and I have not asked again because I am not a creditor and I am not going to be one. What I am asking to be named for is the thing I cannot be paid in, which is that a comparison that nobody is committed to is not a comparison. In four years somebody in a field in the March will want to know what the fen's line did and what the March's line did in the same year on the same weather, and if there is no document with two names on it, then whatever one of us says is a story, and a story does not breed."

---

What was left of the day was about who could sign, and it went on for two hours and it was the hardest hour of the day, and the thing that broke it open was Lissa Vale refusing to sign anything at all until somebody read her the seed house's own column.

"The bed is at three and a half degrees," she said, "and the bin has had sweepings in it since the twenty-first of last month, and I have measured that bed this morning with a soil iron and I have had it twice, and it is not a cold room. A cold room you can put a blanket round. That is a rootwood bed with a mill floor under it and there is nothing coming up from the bottom.

"So there is a document on this table that says the trust is a party to the seed arrangements of this node, and it is a month before anything can be sown, and the ground it is sown in is three and a half degrees and has been going down since the middle of last month, and there is no money in this cooperative to put four hundred hundredweight a month into a return main.

"Write that on it," she said. "Not in a letter. On the annex. In your own hand, Tova, because it is your paper and I want the person who is signing to be the person who wrote the sentence."

Tova Reed wrote it, and it took her about four minutes, and it is a single line and it is the reason the annex is a document and not a promise.

*Entered on this annex, in the steward's hand, before signature: the return main of the node at Lantern Reach is three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight a month short of specification; the compost return of the seed bed has received one hundredweight since the twenty-first of last month; the bed stands at three and a half degrees; and the seed described on this annex may not come up. This is stated at the request of the seed house of this cooperative and against the interest of the trust, and is entered in the trust's own book as well as in the cooperative's.*

And then she initialled it, and put the pen down and picked it up again, and said, "Now the signature, and the signature is the hard part and I want it done properly and not quickly."

---

"Lantern Reach cannot sign," said Dunmar Fitch. "That is not a modesty. I am the clerk of a body that has no charter, and a body with no charter cannot execute an instrument, and a document executed by a body that cannot execute it is voidable by anybody who ever hears of it, and there is a hearing officer coming to this district on a date that I do not know and a schedule of damage in a file and a circular with a president's name on it going round four hundred doors. If this cooperative signs this annex as a cooperative, then in the forty-second a man is entitled to say that the trust attached itself to an unchartered body, and he will be right, and it will be the trust's problem and not ours, and the trust's problem is nine thousand people on a road.

"What I can do is this. The signatory block names the individual, states the capacity, and states the limit, and the limit is written into the body of the annex so that it cannot be read out of it. Three signatures: for the seed house, Lissa Vale. For the node as engineer of record, Marek Vale. And me, as a witness and not as a party, because I am not a party and I have not been a party to anything since the fourth day of a month when a motion of mine was lost nine to twenty-two."

"Write the limit," said Marek.

"Write this," said Dunmar Fitch, reading it out as he wrote it. "*The obligations of the signatories are limited to the season in which this annex is made and to the material and the labour named in it, and the Lantern Reach Work Cooperative holds no charter and no standing to bind itself beyond the season, and any obligation beyond the season would be a promise by persons who cannot be compelled to keep it, and this annex does not ask them for one.*"

Tova Reed read that twice and put her hand flat on the page.

"No," she said. "A season is not a commitment, and a trust that takes a one-season undertaking from a body that cannot give a longer one has nothing, and I have spent a month watching a cooperative refuse things that were not real and I am not going to be the woman who signs a real thing and gets a season's worth of it.

"Take it out," she said. "I am the trust's steward and I am signing for nine thousand people on the March road who are not in this room, against a body of sixteen households with no charter, four pounds five in a box, a bed at three and a half degrees and a plant at the eleventh rod that nobody has looked at since the last week of a month. I am signing it knowing that if this fen goes the March has lost its last comparison and there is no remedy and no party to sue and no clerk to ask, and I am signing it because a comparison that nobody is committed to is not a comparison, and because a seed steward signing for people who are not in the room is not a fraud, it is the job."

Nobody said anything for a while, and then Jonas Byre, who had walked down from the north row for it and had not expected to be asked anything, said:

"It is the first thing anybody has signed in six weeks that is worth anything, and the reason it is worth anything is that it is the only one that does not say you are all right."

---

They signed it at four in the afternoon on the ninth of the month, on the seed house table, with the four crates standing open in the annex behind them and the wet sacking peeled back off the trays, and Lissa Vale read both sheets aloud before she signed the first one, which is her habit and is also this building's habit, and the third signature was a witness's and said so.

Tova Reed signed last, and she signed for the trust, and the two words in her signature that hurt are *Salt March*, and she said so out loud while the pen was down, to make sure nobody in the room had to pretend otherwise.

Then she did the arithmetic of the copies, because she is a woman who does arithmetic in a room, and the arithmetic was that there are now three sets of the cooperative's records in the world: the certified copy in a file in Fennmere that cannot be recalled, the open book on a table in this yard, and one going to Cray by the March road, which will be the first complete copy of anything that has left this fen.

"The March holds a copy of everything," she said, "and that was condition four on the first day and it has been honoured twice, the first of them with a spoiled tray of seed on it, and it is honoured now, and I want the number in the record, because it is the first time in this cooperative's history that anything has been copied out of it on purpose, and that is half of what Ms Quill said was the cure, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the other half of it is a man with a bead of his own who is sixty-one and whose notice runs from Friday and whose figure is fourteen pounds ten."

"Fourteen pounds ten," said Marek, "and a bill of sixty pounds ten, and four pounds five in the box, and a sitting on the twenty-first of the month."

"Twenty-one," said Dunmar Fitch, writing it down, "and I would like it written that a second licensed reading becomes possible on about the eighteenth and possible is not the same as bought, and that this cooperative is going into the twenty-first with a document it can produce and a reading it cannot."

---

The last thing that happened in the seed house that day was at the press, and it was Lissa Vale taking the lid off it and standing there.

The tin was where it had been since the nineteenth day of last month, under the ledger, with the oilcloth over it, and it is a tin about the size of a bread loaf and it holds the last of the March's dry-land material on this side of the water, and it has been at risk for twenty days and the risk has not gone away and the annex does not reduce it by one day.

"I am not moving it," she said. "I have had it in this press for twenty days and I have looked at it every morning and I am not moving it, and I know that the bed in this building is three and a half degrees and I know what the March has said about at risk means, and I have read that condition twice, and I am not moving it.

"And on the annex, under my signature, in my own hand, because I am the seed house and not the trust, I have written that the tin is held in the press of the seed house of this cooperative at risk, and that the trust knows where it is, and that the seed house does not undertake to move it, and that the trust does not ask it to."

Tova Reed read it and did not argue and did not soften anything, and initialled it, which is the third time she has initialled something for a woman she disagrees with in six weeks.

"The first row of the March's line is the fen's fault," Tova Reed said at the door, with her bag on her shoulder and a fortnight's road in front of her. "And the copy goes to Cray, and I will put it in the counting house, and I will not tell you whose hand it goes into, and you are not going to ask me, and I am not going to be able to tell you, and that is a fourth thing that is not written down and it is the only one of the four I am glad about."

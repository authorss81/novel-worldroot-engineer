## The movement

**The flood crest arrives with the cut branch still bleeding and the bypass unbuilt.** The cooperative breaches the embankment by hand, because there is no bypass and no contractor and no money, and diverts the damaged branch into a cut of their own making. **The seed house survives. The shared rows survive. The outer strip does not** — three seasons of Marek's own ground, worked separately from the shared field, is under water for a week and comes up sour. That loss is not repaid in this volume and nobody may soften it.

**Marek has one night to choose between the outer rows he has spent three seasons saving and the seed house. He chooses the seed house**, and the cost of the choice is the ground that is his own. Do not let anyone else make this choice for him and do not let him make it twice.

**On the second night, with the branch, the well head and the mill wheel all exposed at once, four records exist that cannot all be true:** Kellan's logged pressure, Marek's brass gauge, the mill's hand tally of wheel turns, and the dated seed-house ledger. **Lissa brings the fieldbook and its use log to the water's edge and reads every entry aloud while Sera Quill makes the licensed read, and Sera puts her name under all of it.** One Fieldbook panel remains unspent in the volume: **spend it here, once, at the moment of choice, three plain lines.** Nobody may quote the three lines as an instruction in any document, and Sera's unnumbered fourth refusal — *a book does not get to be a reason* — stands untouched and is not explained.

**Kellan Rusk is on the water because his own branch and his side draw are what is flooding. He is not killed and does not flee. His foreman Renn Tallow is there** because he was in the yard on the eighteenth and knows which kit went out when the Crown crew was told to leave. **Kellan loses the Lantern Reach contract, keeps a private charter under monitored conditions because Fennmere's low quarter still needs his water, and testifies about how Crown schedules are written and who signs them. The name he gives is one the reader does not yet understand, and Sera hears it and says nothing.**

**Sera says the deeper answer in Chapter 46, plainly:** what Marek read in the fieldbook bead in Chapter 6 is not a memory of a season that has not happened, **it is a target pattern — a work order, written in advance, in the order of someone intending to enforce a pattern rather than recall one.** She admits she has seen the structure before and did not want to say it. **Nobody names who wrote it, what machine or office wrote it, or why a pattern of that shape would be written into living wood at all. Those are Volumes 05 through 12.**

**The hearing is in Chapters 46–48 and turns the proof into a conditional charter. The resolution is Chapter 49.** The charter's terms are in the outline and must all land: the node stays open, the seed stock is not seized, **the cooperative's own self-imposed obligations from Chapter 10 — the public pressure log anybody may copy and the monthly root-change report — are adopted by the Crown as charter conditions**, and the point is that the Crown did not invent them. **The terms are provisional: the charter lapses at the season's end, the file stays open, and the Directorate reserves the right to reopen it on any new report.** That reservation is the handoff to Volume 02.

## Hard locks. Do not break these, and do not solve them early.

1. **The bypass is unbuilt and stays unbuilt.** Four section-nine margin notes, refused twice on five correct grounds each, four men offered to dig it for nothing on the sixth and were not asked in on the night of the ninth. **It may not be built, funded, or appear.** The climax pays it by an improvised cut of the cooperative's own making. The four men from the peat road may appear and may do nothing; **they may not be the ones who dig, and they are not a rescue.**
2. **The instrument is in Lissa's press under the ledger and is not to be opened by an unlicensed hand.** Hester Carrow has ruled that nobody in her room may open it and **the ruling binds her successors**. Lissa has undertaken in writing to produce it **on the twenty-first and not before**, on a form, with the use log entire, copied, read back and minuted, and she will read it. **The reading at the water's edge is the use log read aloud, not the instrument opened.**
3. **The clean record is gone.** For eight years the only account of the fieldbook in the world was in a press in a seed house; from the eighth of the month it is a district file in Fennmere that cannot be recalled and cannot be added to without a district officer in the room. **Lissa is not sorry and has said so, and nobody may make her sorry or thank her.**
4. **The use log's first line is already in a district record** — the tenth of last month, fifty minutes in the district's hands, no licensed person present, the district's own receipt — **and the lateness of its entry is in the record too, declared by Lissa before anybody found it.** Do not re-discover either. The hearing will read them.
5. **The weep.** A quarter of an inch, twice measured with two rules, pump on the idle, no change since the thirtieth of last month. **The load stated has not been applied, and the reason is not her absence: the load test was the twentieth day's act on a certificate and there is no charter to hang a second one on.** That reason must survive from Ch 41. **Half an inch is where Sera stops calling it a weep, and a leak under sacking at full draw goes into the housing and not into the pit.** **The flood is decided by this number.** Do not resolve it before Ch 43 and do not let it be quietly mended.
6. **The monthly examination on the first of every month, signed by Gael Senn, worded *nothing found*, with a second wording for the return line that records a thing that did not happen.** The next one is **day 61** and it may be under a foot of fen. **She has refused to write an entry in advance and nobody may fill the log for her.** If somebody else has to write *nothing found* on that wound, it must be on the page and it must cost.
7. **The pressure pulse** — one beat in about four seconds, out of the sump, **logged seven times, always *cause not established*, always with no name**. It may be logged again. **Nobody in Volume 01 may name it or explain it.**
8. **The third exchange requirement is still missing on the one licensed work in the volume** — anchor and path exist, the return does not, and the shortfall is three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight a month, seventy-nine pounds sixteen against a specification of eighty and a box of four pounds five. **A later chapter may not quietly fill it.**
9. **Dunmar's *none known* box is spent.** It was filled on the seventeenth of last month, its history was stated with a witness at the Fennmere counter on the seventh of this month, and **the box was left shut**. It may not be spent again.
10. **The cooperative's own books are not a filing and may not be treated as an accepted correction.** The claim in the log was moved on the fourth of last month and lost nine to twenty-two with four abstentions in a room of forty. **There is still no corrected draw figure in the district's hands, and the Crown's works schedule is still never produced** — that is structural, not concealment, and **no villain may reverse it.** The district has certified *a fact about its own completion record* without producing a page of the table.
11. **The thornwild refusal stands as the record has it:** an entry that records the fact of a refusal and not its terms, and the terms are not held by this body. **The cooperative may not use the long line, quote it, or enter it, and nobody may ask that settlement to be named.** It is not in the book. The one word *seat* was refused.
12. **The pathhound report is now in three places** — the open book, the certified copy in Fennmere, and the copy going to the March road. **Nobody may interpret it, nobody may use the animal as a symbol, and the weir animal does not return.** The line is mapped and left alone.
13. **The crew is split and may be healed only on the page.** Ilsa Kern and Jonas Byre went to a district counter on the ninth without leave and were told a measure is varied by the district, by a new form, on a date nobody has. **The burn offer lapsed unanswered on the sixth and nobody signed anything.**
14. **The seed annex is signed and the trust's name is on the record** — the return main three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight short, the compost return one hundredweight since the twenty-first of last month, the bed at three and a half degrees, and the seed may not come up, all entered in Tova Reed's own hand **against the trust's interest.** The March's tin has been in the press twenty days and the annex does not reduce it by one day. **The March holds a copy of everything and Tova will not say whose hand it goes into.**
15. **Nia is gone and does not return within the volume.** Her letter is sealed in a cart and undelivered. **It has not been settled by report and may not be** — if it is delivered, deliver it by hand, on the page, in a chapter. Whether she hands the letter over is still unsettled and belongs to nobody but her.
16. **Renn Tallow's gate refusal was never recorded, referred, softened, or apologised for.** He asked for it to be recorded and nobody recorded it. It is now over a month old. It may be paid in this volume only on the page.
17. **Idris Vane.** The notice was served in person on the fourth and runs fourteen days; the earliest possible reading is **the eighteenth**; his price is **fourteen pounds ten** and the box has **four pounds five**. He is sixty-one, has taken no paid work since his wife died, is a better reader than Sera, and has not moved his day rate in four years. **A reading is possible and cannot be bought. How it is paid for is a real problem and must be solved on the page, by somebody, at a cost.** He is not a rescue and he is not a favour. His own fieldbook is on his kitchen table and he does not offer it and Marek does not ask.
18. **Marek is Stage 0.** Two contacts behind him, no third, no rootmark, no anchor work, no bridge above local conduit, no seedheart, no caretaker link. The first skin rootmark is Volume 02.** Do not spend the stage early and do not make it a rescue.

## Ending lock

Iona's White Mercy is stopped, in the far future of this series, through a braided, staged handoff to tested local seedhearts, not one overwhelming rootlight draw. Iona survives under public custody. The Engine becomes a bounded reservoir and archive, the Root Commons has local charters and vetoes, and some regions remain damaged or deliberately independent. **None of that is in this batch. What is in this batch is the thing that makes it possible and the thing that costs.**

**The final chapter image is fixed by the outline and must be the last thing the reader sees:** the conditional charter nailed to the seed-house door with the Directorate's inspection tag still hanging from it; Lissa holding Marek's father's fieldbook and its use log, and neither of them asking the other for anything; Tova marking the seed annex in a public ledger that anyone may copy, while the first row of next season's line is already in the ground and already leaning sideways, because that is what the land is doing this year; Nia's cart ruts still visible at the gate, and they point out; and the seed-house lamp burning on a living bulb somebody has to feed tonight.

**Marek keeps no instrument, no private log, and no private advantage.** He has a certificate he cannot use without a licensed witness, a monthly obligation he did not want, and a fen that knows what he did in the dark.

# Chapter 48

## The Terms

The hearing room at the Fennmere sub-office has four chairs on the district side of a table and eleven on the other, and on the morning of the twenty-first of the month there were six of them on the district side and ten on the other, and the sixth chair on the district side was empty all morning and it stayed empty, and nobody put anything in it.

Hester Carrow came in with a bundle tied in tape and squared it to the edge of the table before she sat down, which Dunmar Fitch watched her do and understood.

"Four things on the file," she said. "The inspector's application to have the instrument taken into district custody. The applicant's representations in answer to a schedule of damage. The certificate of the twentieth day of last month, with three names on it. And a document dated the seventeenth of this month, with a licensed engineer's name at the foot of it and four records above it.

"And one matter that is not on the file and is on the wall in front of me, which is sixty pounds ten shillings, payable in full since the forty-second of this month, and I am going to deal with it third and not last, because if I deal with it last it will look like a reward and it is not a reward, it is a debt, and a hearing that ends on a debt is a hearing that has decided the debt."

She read back what she had written before she turned the register round, which she did not do once in the whole of the morning.

"Order. The application. The representations. The instrument. The charter. The inspector reports and gives evidence and is not chairing this, and I am not the district, and there is a great deal in this room today that the district has not been told."

---

The application took forty minutes and it failed on the officer's own ruling of the eighth.

Inspector Ansel Bray put it as he had put it on the eighth, in the same order, and did not soften it once, and when he had finished he said, "That is the application, and I would like it minuted in the terms I made it in, and I would like the record to show that I have not withdrawn it and do not withdraw it, and that it will be on the file when this room is finished."

The officer ruled that the application was heard on the cooperative's certified copy and not on the original, and that the copy was certified *so far as it goes* at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, and that the only person in the world who may reconcile a page into that copy is the clerk of the sub-office, from the original, with the custodian present, and that she had been present at that reconciliation on the twentieth and had written the words *added after certification* at the foot of every page.

"So the copy is now a copy of the book as it stands this morning," she said, "and it is a copy nobody in this room can put a page into, and the applicant's custodian put the whole of the use log on that bench in a flood two days ago in front of me, which was not the hearing, and which is going to be the strongest thing anybody has said in this room, and I am going to allow the application to be refused on the ground that the original is produced on a form in this room in ten minutes and the document the application is after is in the applicant's own custody under a use log that this officer has read, and that a records power is not needed to take a thing that is being handed over in front of me."

"Not withdrawn," said Bray.

"Not withdrawn," said Hester Carrow. "It stays on the file. And so does my ruling of the eighth, and so does the ruling that nobody in this room may open the instrument, including me, which binds the officer who sits after me and I would like that in the file in those words, because somebody will one day want to open it in a room and they will not be able to point at me."

Then Lissa Vale produced the fieldbook on a form, with the hour, the place, two witnesses and the number of the page, and put it on the table in front of the officer, and it was in an oilcloth and it did not go into anybody's hands in that room except the officer's, and the officer did not open it either, and it sat on the table for the whole of the morning.

The use log went in with it, entire, copied in the clerk's square hand, read back to the custodian page by page, and minuted with the hour. And Lissa Vale read it, and it took nine minutes, and the room heard the first line and the two hours on the first line again because the hours are the whole of it and a room does not hear a number inside a sentence, and then heard the lateness of the first line declared by the woman who made it, entered on the eighth of this month before anybody had looked for it, and minuted in the same words and not in a summary.

"She read that log to me in this room on the eighth of this month," said the officer, "and told me what was in it before I had to ask, and that is the only reason I could fix a date for this sitting with a file still arriving, and I would like it recorded that the applicant taught this office its own file."

---

Kellan Rusk gave evidence for an hour and ten minutes and he gave it badly, and it was still worth more than anything anybody else said.

He did not look at the cooperative. He gave the shape of a district works order, the way a schedule is written, the way a column is filled in, the way an item goes out with a number against it and no work attached to it, and he gave the number in the box and the post it belonged to, and he gave the docket, and he gave the three stamped faces, and said that he had put two of them in himself and had reported the third to a man in an office on a day he could name, and that the report was not in the file.

He was asked, by the officer, why he had falsified his own pressure book for six years.

"Because a district may be fed and a branch may not be fed," he said. "If I had written what the gauge said, they would have shut my branch, and the low quarter drinks off the bed that my branch feeds, and there is no other water in that quarter for nine miles. I chose forty-one houses over a book, every month, for six years, and I am not going to stand here and be told I did it for money, because if it was for money I would not be able to tell you what it was for."

"And the Lantern Reach contract," said the officer.

"Gone," said Kellan Rusk. "I am not appealing it and I am not asking for a part of it, and I have written to Mr Vale about it and he has not answered, which is the correct amount of answering."

And then the terms of the rest of his life, which the officer read out and which had been drafted in four days by a district clerk who said in advance that she was not the district:

The private charter survives. The branch below the split to be metered with a gauge fixed below the split, of a pattern any party may read, the reading entered in a book kept in Fennmere and not in Fennmere only. His log to be open to any member of the cooperative, at any hour, without notice. A return to the district every month with a copy to the seed house. A person may be sent to his pump house at any time by a hearing officer, on a day's notice, and the reason minuted. And no variation of the draw without a form.

"It is monitored," said Kellan Rusk, "and I have asked for every word of it, and I would like it minuted that I asked."

---

The representations took an hour and the schedule of damage took twenty minutes, and the twenty minutes were the ones nobody in Tarrow Fen had been ready for.

"I am not going to decide this," said Hester Carrow. "It is not mine. A schedule of damage under the sixth part is the district's own instrument and I am appointed to hear applications and to make determinations on charters, and sixty pounds ten is neither, and if I reduce it or remit it I am doing on one morning, in a cold room, with a hearing officer's stamp on it, a thing that only the district can do with a form.

"Here is what I am going to do instead, and I am going to say it plainly because everybody in this room has been told that I do things plainly.

"The cause is not established against the applicant on the date the district's own returns give it. The first well of the low quarter went off on the twenty-first day of last month and the new run was let in at half past four in the afternoon of the twenty-second, and the district's case that this body took the water out from under forty-one houses rests on a day. The branch that drains that gravel has been dead at the bed since the spring before last and was killed from the top, and the district's own completion record carries three stamped faces against the item that governs it, and the district will not produce the document that says what the item is. That is not concealment. That is a form with a column in it that this district has never produced to anybody.

"So the sum is not discharged and not remitted. It stands in the file. And here is the part that matters, and it is the part Mr Vale's clerk worked out at a counter on the forty-second: a debt is enforceable against a body, and a body is a thing with a charter, and this body had no charter from the eighteenth of last month, and there is nobody in the world you can serve sixty pounds ten shillings on. The sum is entered and it is not owed to anybody, and the only thing the district can do about a sum that is entered and not owed is the emergency clause, on a report, at a time of its choosing.

"And I am not the district and I cannot bind the district and my authority over charters runs to the end of the year, and I have never once in four districts seen a year used as a reason to do less. So the sum is in the file, and the file stays open, and the clause stays live, and I am going to say the sentence that this cooperative has to hear and then put it in the minute.

"The Directorate reserves the right to reopen this file on any new report. That is not a threat and it is not a condition and it is not going to be traded away in a room like this one. It is the actual shape of the thing you have been asking for, which is a node you can keep working on, and you cannot have that and have it closed at the same time, and anybody who tells you otherwise is selling you the second thing, which is what the second thing is for."

---

The charter was made at about two o'clock in the afternoon and it is four clauses long and it is not a victory, and everybody who signed it knew that, and Anneke Roos entered the whole of it in the cooperative's book that night with the hour on it and read it aloud at the seed house door at nine o'clock, which is her habit, so that anybody in a yard could stand and hear it.

It is conditional, and it is for the season, and it expires at the end of the season without notice and without anything being said.

One. The node stays open. The head is not sealed, the seed stock is not taken, and the charter is not referred for relocation. Two. The standing pressure log of this body, open to any member, any stranger and any clerk without notice, and the monthly report of root changes, are adopted by the district as conditions of the charter, at the request of the applicant, and the district did not invent them, and may not amend them this season without the consent of the body. Three. The return main of the node shall be made to its specification of forty-one rods of twelve inch by a date to be fixed, and the return main shall be fed at not less than the specified hundredweight a month, and the shortfall of three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight a month standing in a licensed engineer's hand on the twenty-first day of last month is carried forward and not discharged. Four. The seed arrangements of the node, being the annex of the ninth of this month, are conditions of this charter, and the Salt March Dryfoot Trust is a party to them, and its name is on the record, and the seed it has sent may not be used, moved, sold or removed without the steward's word, and the fourth term of that annex, which is a copy of everything to Cray whether or not it helps, is a condition of this charter.

And there is a fifth thing, and it is on a separate sheet, and it is the only thing on either sheet that anybody in the room argued about, and it was argued about for nine minutes by a man of twenty-nine and a hearing officer of about forty, and it is this:

That on the first day of every month the node shall be examined and something put into the return main, and the examination entered in the standing pressure log and signed by a person named on the charter; and that where nothing is found the entry is to read *nothing found*, and where a thing required by the charter did not happen the entry for that line is to record that it did not happen, in the same book, on the same page, in the same hand, with the hour.

"That second half is not in any charter in this district," said Hester Carrow. "I have written it because Ms Senn wrote it in a seed house in a flood on the fourteenth of this month and a clerk of this office copied it into a district file word for word and told me it was a better rule than ours, and I have no authority to improve a charter and every authority I have to carry one into a district form, and the second half is the reason this body will be able to prove anything at all in four years."

The first monthly examination under the new charter falls due on the first of next month, which is ten days from the day the charter was made, and it may be under a foot of fen, and Gael Senn said in the room that she would be there for it, and then said that she had said that and had not written anything, and would not write anything tonight, and the officer minuted that too because she said it in front of her.

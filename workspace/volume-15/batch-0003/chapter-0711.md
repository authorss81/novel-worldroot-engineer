# Chapter 711

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Nineteenth, And A Paper Coming Up The Middle Road In Five Hands Naming An Hour And A Gate And A Morning, And Nobody In This Yard Able To Put A Finger On Anything Wrong With It

The notice came up the middle road at about the ninth hour with a boy who was not from any place in this holding, and it was carried to the middle table and put down flat and squared up, and five hands had been on it.

It had no seal on it and it had no clerk's hand and it had nothing on it that a person could take back.

> To the holding at the wall, and to the engineer of record, and to the crew that works the sill.
>
> We have heard the span at ninety-four feet at the crossing below the second place's branch. We heard it from the engineer of record and we heard it from the crew that would build it, separately, and neither of them was asked to hear the other first.
>
> We have weighed it against what it buys, which is two things, and what it does not do, which is five things, and what it costs, which is sixteen bodies for twenty-two mornings and a mile of dry ground between the bodies and the stone.
>
> We will give our answer at the sixth hour at your gate, on the third morning counting from the one this was written on.
>
> We ask this holding for nothing before then. We are not asking for a figure to be altered and we will not be moved by one, and if every figure in your yard is altered before that morning the answer will be the same answer.
>
> This paper is ours and it is not a record of yours, and it is not to be entered anywhere.
>
> Five hands are on it. There is no clerk and there is no book.

Nia Vale read it standing up and she read it twice and she turned it round at the end so that the yard could see the five hands along the bottom of it.

**"It is correct. I have read it twice and I have gone looking for the thing that is wrong with it and there is not one, and I want that said out loud in this yard before anybody starts."**

Somebody at the back said that a paper in five hands off a road four miles long could not be right.

**"It can be and it is, and the reason it is, which I have worked out twice, is that it does not ask this holding for anything. It says what they have done, it says when they will finish doing it, and it says what it will not be moved by, and a paper that asks for nothing cannot have anything wrong with it, because there is nothing in it for anybody to have put in wrong."**

She put one finger on the middle of the page without picking it up.

**"And I am not going to put this on the long table and I am not going to enter it in that room, and there is a woman in this holding who has already told you why once this month and I am not going to tell you twice."**

A man at the gate said that it was a week's work for a paper, and Odile Vray said the days, and the man said he was not asking about the days.

**"Then ask somebody else,"** she said. **"I have got four cartloads of silt in my head and a card went up that road last night and I am not the woman to explain anything to anybody this morning."**

Then the man at the gate said that there were five names on it and that he had never in his life known five people who all meant the same thing, and about nine people in that yard looked at him, and nobody said what he had not finished saying, and nobody said it after him either, and he said the rest of it into the ground.

**"I am not saying they are lying. I am not saying any of them does not mean it. I am saying I have been in this yard nine years and I have never once been told a thing by five people that I could write down. That is all I am saying and I have stopped."**

Nobody in that yard picked that up and made anything of it, and Nia Vale said one sentence about it and then let it go.

**"He is right that he has never been told such a thing. He is not wrong about the paper. Both of those are in the same yard this morning and neither one of them has to move for the other one to be true."**

The paper said at which hour it would speak and at which gate and on which morning, and it named the morning by counting forward from the one it was handed on, which is what a council with no book does instead of writing a date. It said it did not ask this holding for anything before then, and it said it would not be moved by an altered figure, and a mason from the third place read those three sentences out loud twice in two different orders and found nothing in either order that could be held against it.

Soren Rill gave the yard's own figures from the gate end as a man reading somebody else's document, which is not how he has read that stone this month.

**"One thousand and three hundred and twenty-five hundredweight, and eight on yesterday. Four hundred and seventy-fourth of the run. Eight hundred and fifty-eight where the wall begins and nine hundred and five where it ends, and forty-seven of masonry between them, and that is three figures and a gap and I have given them to you in the order they come on somebody else's paper rather than the order I like."**

He pulled his coat shut against the wind off the fen.

**"And I will tell the yard the thing I would say to somebody who had brought that sheet up here to be picked at, which is that a stranger can be more careful with your figures than you are, and that is not an insult to anybody in this yard, it is an insult to me."**

Tova Reed had the window on the corner of the seed board by the eighth hour and she said it to the bench and then to nobody.

**"Four hundred and forty-one, and it is two hundred and fifteen of the lower half and two hundred and twenty-six of the upper. The lower one did not move this morning. It is an even morning and on an even morning the one that moves is the upper one, and I have got it right twice this week and I am going to keep saying which one it is whether or not anybody is standing there."**

She put the pencil down on the edge of the bench.

**"The lower one has not been in the same place since Friday, which is two mornings now, and I have watched four people say the same sentence about it in four different orders and not one of them has ever said which half of it moved. So I am the fourth and I am saying it and if it is wrong I would like to be told, because I write it down."**

Nobody told her and nobody said that she was right.

The girl from the second place had the aggregate on the board and gave it with the two limbs the other way round, under first.

**"Four hundred and seventy-two at the head, the four hundred and seventy-first underneath the line and the four hundred and seventy-third over it. I have given you the under one first because it is the one the number is judged on and I have worked out that in three weeks of watching this yard, and I do not know whether I have worked it out right."**

She put the chalk back on the ledge.

**"If I have it wrong then I will do it the other way tomorrow and nobody has to tell me, because I would rather have the order wrong for one morning than the wrong order for forty."**

Harlan Vetch gave the three ages off the wall at the sixth hour in an order he announced before he started and gave no reason for.

**"The register form is at three hundred and sixty-eight days and the charter is at two hundred and seventy-four, and I have given you those two in that order and not in the order you asked me for them in."**

He stopped with the hod in his hand.

**"The third one I am not going to give as a figure at the front, because I have given it as a figure nine times this month and it has not been once on a wall or in a book in the four years I have been in this yard, and I am no longer sure a figure is the honest way to say it. A man called Silling ruled a line and wrote nothing on it and wrote nothing above the word, and it stands at three hundred and sixty-two days, and I am telling you that one last and out of order on purpose."**

**"I have been thinking about it since he asked. A line ruled and left empty on purpose is a different thing from a line nobody got to. Somebody ruled that and then did not write on it, and I would like that to be in my mouth rather than in my head for the rest of my life, and there is nobody in this yard old enough for me to ask."**

A man laying the sill asked about the low board by the gate and got the standing answer from Harlan Vetch without stopping work.

**"No day on it. Not picked up. Not taken back. I have laid stone for twenty-six years and I have never once been tempted by a piece of wood with nothing on it, and that is not because of anything it has done. It is because it has nothing on it."**

He set a stone.

**"And I will tell you the other half of it, which is that there was a fortnight in the summer where a piece of wood like that would have got picked up in this yard in about four minutes, and I know that, and it did not happen, and I have not decided whether that was good character or just nobody being hungry."**

The four sheets and the drawer were Nia Vale's and she did them together in one sentence because she had already given a quarter of her morning to somebody else's paper.

**"Key on its nail at the sixth hour, drawer shut, nothing in it. Four sheets on the long table, none entered, none refused, no column cut under any of them, and the number behind that floor is four and this morning it very nearly became five and it did not."**

Then she said the letter and said it to the sheet in the middle of the table and not to the yard.

**"The letter is a hundred and seventy-one days."**

Perrin Dae read the compost line off the gate board and gave it back in his own trade's words and did not improve it.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and nothing let out of it, and that is a lime and timber man's figure and not a compost figure, and I have been keeping a yard for nine years and I have never once been paid on a line that I was also let out on, and I have never once been let out of anything I was paid on, and that has always seemed to me to be the whole of what a holding owes a person."**

He shifted the barrow off his shoulder.

**"I am not going to tell you what I think of the five hands on that paper because I put my own hand on it and that would be a strange thing for me to go on about in your yard."**

Kellan Rusk said only the blanks at the sixth hour, standing in the doorway, and said why in half a sentence.

**"Thirty-nine, and the rule underneath them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. A shut door, a notice off a road, a stack of silt and three cartloads going back where they came from, and none of it went into that room. I am not reading the rest of the standing out loud this morning because a yard that is waiting for something ought not to be given eight figures to hold on to."**

The girl from the second place said the man at the end of the north row on her way out and said it as a woman reporting something she has been counting without knowing what she is counting.

**"Twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, nothing asked at any hour. And I have now been in this yard fifteen days and that house is the only thing in it I have counted every single morning, and I do not think anybody here would understand that as interesting, so I am telling it to the wall."**

Odile Vray put her hand on the pipe behind the tap house joint and left it there while she said it, which is what the woman from the second place did yesterday and which nobody in that yard had seen twice in nine years.

**"It stands. I have felt it every day since I got here and I am going to stop saying it now, because a thing I say every morning stops being a thing I have checked and becomes a thing I have promised, and I would rather leave it where it is."**

She took her hand off.

At dusk the notice lay face up on the middle table in five people's yard, and it was correct, and there was nothing on the long table that had moved, and there were two mornings between that one and the sixth hour the paper named in which nobody anywhere was going to be asked for anything at all, and seven people had a sill to lay in both of them, and the yard went on working.

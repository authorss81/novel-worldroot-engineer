# Chapter 707

## The Twentieth Of The Nineteenth, And A Tail On The Far Stone Again, And A Woman With A Pencil Putting Four Things Down And Taking One Of Them Back Off The Paper

The seven were on the sill before the light came and two courses at the north end that have been open since Wednesday were bedded and barrowed before the light went off the fen.

Ismay Rooke came down the middle road at about the sixth hour with a boy and a tape and he stopped at the gate end of the wall and he did not go past it, which is the fourth morning he has stood there and the fourth morning he has not asked to.

He read the two figures off the stone in the order of a man who counts masonry first and days afterwards.

**"Four hundred and seventieth of the run. And one thousand and three hundred and nineteen hundredweight, which is eight on yesterday and I would write the eight down beside the ordinal every morning of my life if there were anybody here to read it."**

He put his thumb on the second stone, at the gate end.

**"Eight hundred and fifty-four where this wall begins. Nine hundred and one where it ends. Forty-seven of masonry, and it has been forty-seven every morning since the wall went up, and yesterday the far one had nothing on the end of it and today it has a one and I am a mason and not a man who makes speeches about a stone."**

The yard had heard him and the yard said nothing, and a woman carrying a barrow across to the north end went past him with the load on her shoulder and did not turn her head, and that was the whole of what this holding said about the tail on that stone this morning.

Odile Vray gave the window to nobody who had asked her for it and she gave it at the boards while she was cutting the day into four, which she does before light with a stick of charcoal on the back of a seed sack and not on anything that belongs to anybody.

**"Two hundred and twenty-four of the upper half, and two hundred and thirteen of the lower, and four hundred and thirty-seven of it altogether, and I have given them to you in the order I care about them, which is the one that moved this morning first, and the upper one has not shifted since Friday because the upper one only moves on the even mornings and this is one of them."**

She put the charcoal down and picked up the trowel.

**"And that is three figures where most mornings give one, and I am aware of that, and I would still give them in that order, because the third one is the only one of the three that is not about anybody's body."**

Harlan Vetch came off the wall at the third hour and gave the long sheet in the middle of his own work, off the boards, without stopping and in a different order from anybody who has read it this month.

**"Four hundred and sixty-eight at the head of it, and the four hundred and sixty-seventh under the line and the four hundred and sixty-ninth over it, so the fourth is not due. Forty-nine is tomorrow's head and I have not got that and I have got a barrow to empty."**

He emptied it.

Then he stopped with the barrow tipped and said the other two that are his, from the same boards and in the same breath, which is a thing he has done twice this month and both times because somebody asked.

**"Eleven journeys and there is no twelfth. Fifteen lines in the log with a clean space above the fifteenth and I have been asking for two weeks what the space is for and nobody has told me and I have stopped asking in front of people."**

Tova Reed had the three ages on the corner of the seed board in lead pencil by the sixth hour, because Kellan Rusk was inside the one room with a book and she wrote what she was told and did not ask him to say any of them twice.

**"The register form is three hundred and sixty-four days, which is the one everybody asks about. The charter is two hundred and seventy. Silling's own ruled line is three hundred and fifty-eight and there is nothing on it, and I have now written that three times this week on three boards and I am going to keep writing it on three boards."**

She squared the pencil on the edge of the bench and did not look up.

**"I did not ask him to repeat any of the three and I did not ask the next one to repeat them either, and if you have come to that bench to look at my face about it then the bench is a poor place to have come to and the sorting board is nearer the door."**

Nia Vale did the standing at about the seventh hour in the doorway of the one room, standing, with a pencil and a sheet of her own that was not off any table in this holding, and she did it in a room with nine people in it and she did it slowly and she did not ask anybody to help.

**"The first thing is four women on a sill at the fifth hour every morning since the days turned, and it is four and not five, and the fifth went up the road on Thursday and has not come back and I am not going to ask her why and she is not going to give it."**

She wrote a line and turned the sheet so that the yard could see it.

**"The second thing is a bucket. It has been a bucket since the spring and the second place has not had one of its own since the spring, and it is a bucket and not a barrow and I am not going to call it a barrow because it would then be a figure instead of a bucket."**

**"The third thing is Harlan Vetch's own two hands, in the dark, in a wet face, in the one week of this year when the frost was in the ground. That is the third thing and he is forty feet up that wall at this moment and he is not to be told about it by me in a doorway."**

Harlan Vetch, from forty feet of wall, said nothing at all and went on laying.

**"That is three. And here is the fourth one and I am going to write it and then I am going to take it off again, and I would like the nine of you to watch me do it, because you have all been counting standing in this yard for a fortnight and you have all been counting it wrong."**

She wrote a fourth line.

**"Two hours of a Thursday that Odile Vray spent walking a mile and a half with a lamp to a house at the end of a lane, out of a Thursday she wanted at the face of that sill, and she has told three people and a book about it and nobody has thanked her and I am not going to thank her now in front of you."**

Then she put the flat of her hand on the fourth line and rubbed the pencil out of it until the paper came up grey, and she showed the sheet round afterwards with the fourth line gone off it.

**"A Thursday is not a standing. It happened and it is over and it does not go on standing on a Tuesday, and if you count it then every one of you is counting a deed and not a relation, and a deed is not what that neighbour has."**

Somebody at the back of the doorway said that three was not many, and Nia Vale turned the sheet round again so that the three lines were the last thing anybody saw.

**"It is three. It was not replenished this morning and nobody has promised me that it will be replenished tomorrow, and I am not going to write a fifth line to make it a better number, and I would like whoever is standing in this doorway hoping for a fifth line to go and do the seventh hour instead."**

Marek came in at about the eighth hour with the copy sheet under his arm and offered to read the three out to the woman at the second place, and she said no before he had finished the offer.

**"It is not on a wall and it is not on a sheet of mine and it is not yours, and the moment a person like me reads it out to another place it stops being a standing and becomes a delivery. It is three lines and it goes up the road on a sheet of paper with no name at the foot of it, or it does not go at all."**

She put the pencil in her pocket.

**"I have not asked you for anything. I want that written down as well, because a man has offered to carry three lines up a road for me twice this week and has been refused twice and he will offer a third time."**

The girl from the second place came back down the middle road at about the tenth hour with an empty bundle and she went to the boards at the gate end and read the compost line off it and asked nobody.

**"Paid at thirty-one. And not discharged, whatever the second of those two words is doing."**

She rubbed a thumb across it and looked at the thumb.

**"It has been on that board every morning I have been in this yard and I have never once seen anybody cross it out, and I have been trying to work out whether that means it is finished or whether it means nobody here is allowed to."**

Then she walked the north row and stopped at the end of it, at the fourth house, and stood there a while, and Harlan Vetch came down and said the one thing that belongs to that row.

**"Twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, and nothing put to him at any hour of this day, and that young woman has now walked past him four mornings running without stopping, and I have been keeping that in the same list as everything else and I am not going to stop keeping it."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room at about the sixth hour and gave two things out of the standing and refused the rest in a way he had not used before.

**"Thirty-nine blanks on the board in there and the rule underneath them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the log is fifteen lines, and that is two figures and I am going to say why I have stopped at two."**

He put the book down on the step where anybody could see it and did not open it.

**"Four days ago there was a stone on this wall that came round to a round figure, and yesterday a place four miles off said no to nine bodies, and this morning a woman in a doorway took a fourth line off her own sheet. Not one of those three things went into that room. If I stand in that doorway and read six more figures at the people in it, then in a month somebody will say that the holding's own record heard about them, and it will not have heard about them, and I will have made the lie myself and it will have been done in my own voice."**

He left the book on the step and went and sat down beside it.

Odile Vray did the drawer and the joint at the back of the tap house together and she put her own hands on the pipe behind it before she said anything about it.

**"Shut, and the key on its nail at the sixth hour, and there is nothing in that drawer and there has not been anything in it since before I came, and I am not going to be the one who opens it."**

She took her hands off the pipe and wiped them.

**"And this joint weeps at the second hour and the pipe behind it is standing and it has not moved, and I have put both of my hands on that pipe to tell you that I did that and not to tell you that it is sound, because I am tired of a yard deciding that a thing is sound because somebody touched it."**

Ismay Rooke looked at the ladder for a while from the gate end and gave it the only account of it that has ever been given from forty feet away.

**"Two rungs to it and a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and I laid the bottom rung myself eleven years ago and I have not been up either of them since and neither has anybody in this yard this month, and there is a number that people at my place say ought to go against one of the two people that ladder belongs to."**

He shouldered the tape.

**"I would want that person to say it themselves before it goes on a stone or a slate or a sheet, and I have said that at my own place and I will say it here."**

Soren Rill did the four sheets and the letter in one pass and kept the two figures apart in front of nine people on purpose, because on the far end of this holding's own wall a figure had gained a tail this morning and somebody was bound to go looking for figures that agree.

**"The letter is a hundred and sixty-seven days and it is a different figure from the four on that table and it has been for four years, and the youngest of the four is two days older than it is."**

He put his hand flat on the end sheet and did not touch it.

**"Four sheets, none entered, none refused, no column cut under any one of them, and a sheet of Nia Vale's with three lines and a rubbed-out fourth on it is not one of those four and it is not going on that table, and a sheet of my own with the arithmetic of a span is not one of those four either. I would like it noticed that the number behind that floor is four and it has been four all month and this morning was the first morning it could have been five and it did not become five."**

The girl from the second place read the two cut figures off the far stone on her way out and said them once and did not slow down over the one that had a tail on it.

**"Nine hundred and one. And eight hundred and fifty-four at the other end. Forty-seven. I said that number four times last week and I have said it twice today and it has not moved once in between and I am not going to say anything else about it."**

At dusk the sheet with three lines on it went onto the middle table face down and stayed face down, and nobody turned it over, and there were three lines on it and a fourth one rubbed out of it and no name at the foot of it and no fifth line promised to anybody.

The second thing the refusal is going to cost has not been said out loud yet, and a woman who keeps the pumps at a place that has none is expected up that middle road tomorrow, and the sill crew will be on the stone at the fifth hour with seven bodies and not nine, and the yard went on working.

# Chapter 712

## The Twenty-Fifth Of The Nineteenth, And Three Of Five People From Off This Road Standing In This Yard Saying Out Loud What It Is Going To Cost To Say No In Two Mornings, And Nobody In The Yard Thanking Any Of Them For It

They had asked the yard to be at the boards at about the eighth hour and about twenty people were there when the three of them came up the middle road, and the two who had stayed behind at the fourth place were not in this yard and their absence was not explained and was not guessed at.

Hanne Brack spoke first and she had the end of a dry branch behind her and she spoke about the standing, not about the water.

**"That woman in your doorway crossed a fourth line off her own sheet on Friday morning and I would like to say that I have read the three lines that were left, because they came up the road on a girl with a bundle and I read them at my own table and I did not send them back."**

She did not have the paper and nobody asked her for it.

**"Three things. Four women on a sill at your fifth hour and one bucket and a man who laid a face in the wet in the dark in the frost week. That is what my household has from this holding and it is not nothing, and I am not going to stand here and say it is nothing, because you people have spent a month learning to say that arithmetic is not temper and arithmetic cuts both ways."**

She put her hands behind her back.

**"And what it costs you to hear no from me is that it goes back up that road on me. Four miles on two days of cart and on my own feet, and there is no paper that comes with me, because a paper would be a record and a record would make it somebody else's no."**

Emrys Dole said the road part and said it the way a man says a thing he has measured with a wheelbarrow.

**"I carry on the third place's road and I will tell the yard what four miles of dry ground is when you have to put nine bodies across it and a loaded barrow behind them. It is not a difficulty. It is two hours each way and it is on every single morning of a season and it does not come out of anybody's figures on a stone or a sheet, and I have never once been able to put it in a place where a person would find it."**

He shifted his weight off the other foot.

**"And that is one of the three things your council is going to name tomorrow and it is the one that will not go on your paper. I have brought a paper's worth of the rest of it and none of it is this."**

Then Auret Sill stood slightly behind the two of them and said the third thing, and she said it in the plainest voice she has.

**"The third thing is mine and I am going to say it short, because the other two have said theirs long and I have listened to both and neither of them tried to be bigger than the other."**

Nobody in that yard made any sound.

**"On the third morning from now I am going to walk back up this road and up the second place's road and tell a place that has no water in its branch that a bridge which was never promised to it is not going to be built, and that the woman who asked about the bridge is me and asked it twice, and that the second time she asked it she had already told them the price."**

She stopped for a moment and looked at the boards.

**"That is the face. It is not a punishment and nobody is threatening me with anything and I would like this yard to hear that from me, because there is no punishment in it anywhere. It is that after that morning I am a woman who went and asked. I would have been a woman who was asked."**

She put her hands at her sides.

**"And I have had that face before and I have had it four times in my life and every single time somebody in a yard like this one made it into something larger than it was. I am asking all of you not to do that."**

Then a man at the gate said that he wanted to say something to those five people before they went back down the road, and he got as far as the first four words of it before Odile Vray put her hand flat on the boards and stopped him.

**"Do not. And I am going to say why in one sentence and then I will lay stone."**

She kept her hand there.

**"They have told us the price in advance and that is worth having and I will not argue that it is worth having. It is still true at this hour that there is no water at the end of that branch and that a woman there is carrying a can every morning of the season, and being told what a refusal costs you does not put one can back on a shoulder."**

She lifted her hand clear of the boards.

**"And there is a man at the fourth place who was paid at the second hour this morning and will be paid at the second hour tomorrow, and both of those are the same morning, and if you thank five people for one of them you are asking them to be paid in something that does not exist."**

The man at the gate said thank you to nobody and put his hand on the wall and left it there and did not say anything else, and nobody in that yard thanked anybody, and the three of them went down the middle road at about the tenth hour.

She had been filling a can at the trough and she came across the yard to the stones and gave the two of them before she gave the channel, which is not the order in which they come on that wall.

**"Eight hundred and fifty-nine where this wall begins and nine hundred and six where it ends, forty-seven of masonry between them and it has been forty-seven since the wall went up. And the run is one thousand and three hundred and twenty hundredweight, five down, and it is the four hundred and seventy-fifth of the run."**

She put the chalk down and did not move.

**"I said those three figures first and the launder last because I have been reading this stone for sixteen days and I have only understood this week that the stones are older than the channel. Nobody told me that. I worked it out from the mason standing at this gate not being allowed onto the sill."**

Harlan Vetch gave the window from forty feet of wall and gave the halves the other way round from yesterday and said why in one word.

**"Reverse. Two hundred and sixteen on the bottom of it and two hundred and twenty-six on the top. Four hundred and forty-two if you put the two together, which I have not done and am not going to."**

He set the stone and waited for it.

**"I have read them the same way up for eleven years and this morning three people from off this road stood in that yard and said the price of a thing, and I decided that if a yard can turn over a figure that has not moved in a fortnight then it can turn over an order."**

Emrys Dole read the compost line off the gate board as a man who goes past it four times a day and has never had a reason to stop.

**"Paid at thirty-one and not let out. I have gone past that board since before I could reach the latch and I have never once seen a man cross it out, and I have assumed for thirty years that it is a bad board and I have just worked out in the space of a morning that it is the most honest thing in your yard."**

He shouldered the bag.

**"I am not going to say any more about it. I have said more about it than I have said about anything else this month and I carry things for a living."**

Kellan Rusk gave the aggregate out of the one room with the door open and the book under his arm, in the order of a man who has been asked twice.

**"Four hundred and seventy-three at the head, the four hundred and seventy-second under the line and the four hundred and seventy-fourth over it, the fourth not due and the fourth never due in my experience. And there is a notice on that table with five names on it and there was a woman in this yard this morning almost thanking three of them for something, and none of the four came into this room."**

Nia Vale gave the three ages off the rack in the doorway with the door behind her shut, which is the only door in this holding she is in the habit of shutting.

**"Charter two hundred and seventy-five. Register form three hundred and sixty-nine. Silling's ruled line three hundred and sixty-three, and the word standing over it is not a word anybody in this yard uses. Oldest, newest, and the blank one, because I have spent a fortnight putting the blank one in the middle and I have decided that is a way of pretending it is not blank."**

Soren Rill said the blanks and nothing else at the sixth hour, in a doorway, with his coat open.

**"Thirty-nine on the board and the rule underneath them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. I have given that figure to a yard on and off for a fortnight and this morning I gave it to about twenty people who had come here expecting to be told something, and the fact that nothing came into that room is now the thing that happened today, and I am going to stop trying to say it in a better order."**

Tova Reed said the letter from the seed bench and then went on sorting and did not look up again.

**"The letter is a hundred and seventy-two days, and I have written it on four boards this week and on nothing else, and I am not going to write it on a sheet and I am not going to hand it to anybody, and there is a reason and the reason is that the second somebody asks me for it on paper is the morning I start being the woman who keeps it."**

Ismay Rooke said the ladder on his way to the gate and said it at the wall where it stands, and did not touch it.

**"Two rungs, and nobody in this yard has been up either of them this month, and there is a ring of ground inside your eleven acres that nobody has walked this month either, and I am not going to tell you that either of those is a virtue, because the man from my place who laid the bottom rung is a man who has never in his life declined to measure anything he could reach."**

He went up the middle road.

At about the seventh hour the girl from the second place held the chalk out to the man who had been stopped at the gate and asked him to write the two figures on the north board, and she said it in front of nine people, and he said he had never written one in his life.

**"Then it will be the worst two figures on that wall,"** she said, **"and it will be the first two that anybody in this yard has put there without being asked to."**

He wrote them badly, with the two of them the wrong way round and one of them upside down in the middle.

**"Two hundred and eight of three hundred and ninety-nine,"** he said, from about four feet back, as though reading somebody else's work in a field.

The three people from off this road went home up a middle road with the price of their answer in three mouths, and the boy who carries water stood under the north end of the wall for a while after the yard had emptied and did not tell anybody what the two figures on the coping were for. The sixth hour the paper named was two mornings off, and the yard went on working.

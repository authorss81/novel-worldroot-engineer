# Chapter 710

## The Twenty-Third Of The Nineteenth, And The Last Morning On Which An Order Could Still Go Up That Road And Come Back Down As Stone, And Four Feet Of Silt Put Down As Work By The People Who Will Have To Dig It

Odile Vray worked it out at the fourth hour with a stick of charcoal on the back of the second seed sack, which is the third thing she has written on a sack this month and the only one of the three that has been about somebody else.

She did not have to be asked, and she did the arithmetic out loud while she did it, which is what she does.

**"Three cartloads a day, and they come from four days north, and a cartload coming down that road four days means that if I do not put the order on a cart before the fourth hour this morning then there is no stone on this ground for any morning after today."**

She drew the line under the third load and left her hand on it.

**"That is not the council. That is not five people behind a door. That is four days of road and a man with two carts who will not hold them for me out of politeness."**

Perrin Dae, who keeps the lime and the timber at the fourth place and had come up the middle road with the other four and gone home again and come back, said the other half of it and said it in the voice of a man pricing timber.

**"She has it right and she has it right for the wrong reason, which is usual. I have sent stone down that road eleven times and I have never once had a cart turn round at the top of it because a yard was slow, and if this yard wants three cartloads a morning after this one then somebody in this yard is going to have to have ordered them yesterday, and yesterday was Tuesday, and today is Friday."**

Odile Vray said the whole of what the span would have cost this month, in the figures of the people who would have done it, and she said it without any temper in it at all, which several people in that yard had not expected.

**"Three cartloads laid a day, and the fifth hour gone for it, and the seventh hour standing empty because everybody who would have been at the boards is carrying stone. The tenth hour gone entirely and there is one man in this holding who does the tenth hour and I have counted him twice. Four feet of silt out of the second place's branch by now and nearer five by the time the water drops, and nobody in this holding digs silt and everybody in this holding has to have somebody who does."**

She put the sack down and squared it against the boards.

**"And the ninth woman is not coming. That is on the list above this one and it does not move because it is Friday."**

Then a man at the gate said that he would put a body on that crossing himself if it would put water back in the second place's branch, and he said it out loud in a yard of thirty people and he did not lower his voice when he said it.

**"It would not,"** he said, before anybody had told him. **"I have worked that out too and I have been working it out for four days and I would still put a body on it, and I am not going to pretend to you that wanting it is the same as it doing any good, because it is not and I know it is not and I am saying it anyway."**

Nobody blamed him for it and nobody thanked him for it either.

A woman further back said that she wanted it refused and gave her reason, which was that a bridge at ninety-four feet with a flood load on it is a bridge that has to be mended, and a mend is a morning, and she has been counting mornings all month.

Nobody thanked her for saying that and nobody argued with it, and the two of them were standing eight feet apart and neither of them moved, and neither of them said a word to the other.

At about the fifth hour Odile Vray put the sack in front of the mason who lays for three councils and asked him where the stone should go.

**"Under the span. That is what it was bought for and that is the only place it goes."**

**"Then it goes back up that road to whoever would have taken it, and I am going to write my own name at the top of the paper that says so, and not this holding's, because this holding did not decide this and I am not going to let a sack of charcoal be the decision."**

She gave it to him and he gave it to a boy and the boy went up the middle road with three cartloads' worth of paper on it and no stone at all.

Perrin Dae read the two cut figures off the top of the wall and gave them in the order of a man who buys, which is stones before everything else.

**"Eight hundred and fifty-seven at the gate end and nine hundred and four at the far end. Forty-seven of masonry. And the channel is at one thousand and three hundred and seventeen hundredweight this morning, and I have given the weight before the day on purpose, because a man who buys has the weight first and the day afterwards."**

He looked at his own two hands when he finished, and did not say why he was glad.

**"It is five lighter than yesterday and it is the four hundred and seventy-third of the run, which is not a round number, and I am glad of that."**

Soren Rill gave the window from the gate end in the order in which this yard has been asking him for figures all week, which is not an order he chose.

**"Four hundred and forty, and it is made of two hundred and twenty-five of the upper and two hundred and fifteen of the lower. I am going to say the upper first and I am going to say that I do not know which one of them moved, because I have been at the long table and not at a stone for four days and I would rather say that than guess in front of nine people."**

Somebody said that the odd ones move the lower one, and Soren Rill said yes, and that he had known that and had wanted somebody else to be the one to say it in his yard.

Tova Reed had the aggregate on the corner of the seed board at the sixth hour in lead pencil, written down and not spoken, and she said it to the bench.

**"Four hundred and seventy-one at the head, the four hundred and seventieth under the line and the four hundred and seventy-second over it."**

She put the pencil down flat.

**"I have now had those three given to me four mornings running by four people who all stop in the same place, and the fourth is never due, and I have started to think that the fourth is not a figure at all but a place a person is supposed to stop talking at."**

The three ages came off the rack in an order Kellan Rusk said he had chosen and could not defend.

**"Three hundred and sixty-seven days on the register form. Three hundred and sixty-one on the ruled line in Silling's book with nothing written on it. Two hundred and seventy-three on the charter. That is longest first and I have no reason for it and I have been a bookkeeper for nine years and I do not usually do things with no reason."**

He left the rack standing the way he found it.

Harlan Vetch gave the letter from forty feet of wall and gave it with a complaint attached, which is not how anybody has said it this month.

**"The letter is a hundred and seventy days, which is the only figure on any stone or wall or sheet in this holding that has been going up every single morning of this month with nothing happening to cause it. I would like somebody with the authority to change things to look at one figure that just goes up."**

Nobody had the authority to change it and nobody looked at it.

Odile Vray read the compost line out of the boards as a woman reading her own trade, and she had never done that before.

**"Paid at thirty-one and nothing has gone out of it, and I have been paid on a line like that for eleven years and I have never once been let out, and I want to say one thing about it and then I am going to go and dig."**

She stopped with her hand on the board.

**"You people have got a line that goes up and I have got a branch that goes down, and both of them this month, and I have thought about it every time I have walked out to the north end, and I have not got anything clever to say about it and I have stopped looking for anything clever."**

The girl from the second place walked the north row on her way out and stopped at the fourth house for about as long as it takes to look at a door.

**"Twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched. I have been in this yard fourteen days and I have counted that house every morning because it is the only house anybody here looks at twice, and I do not know what I am counting and I am going to keep counting it."**

Nia Vale said the two that are hers on her way in and then stopped, which she has not done.

**"The key was on its nail at the sixth hour and the drawer was shut and there is nothing in it. And the four sheets on that table are four, none entered, none refused, no column cut under any of them, and there is a sheet with three lines and a rubbed out fourth on it on the middle table and a sheet with four thousand and one hundred and one on it on the middle table, and both of those are this holding's own business and neither one of them is going on the long table."**

She put her hand flat on the end sheet and did not touch it.

**"And that is all four things I have. I have said them out loud twice this month and I am not going to say them a third time, because they have not moved once and I am not going to be a woman who reads four unmoved things out loud in a yard to people who have to work."**

At about the seventh hour the girl from the second place put the two figures on the coping at the north end in chalk because the board was at the other end of the yard and she was not going to walk back for two figures.

**"Two hundred and seven of three hundred and ninety-seven,"** she said to the stone, and then she wiped her hands and did not say it again.

A man at the gate asked her whether anybody in this yard read those two out.

**"Nobody has, not since before I came, and somebody asked me to say them out loud on my fourth morning and I said no, and I have thought about why I said no every day since and I have not got an answer better than that I did not want to be the one who started."**

The seven laid the sill and the north wall at a rate nobody in that yard had seen this month, and not one of them was told to lay a course they would not otherwise have laid, and when Odile Vray counted what they had done she said it out loud once.

**"That is about four days of span work put into a sill, at seven bodies instead of sixteen, and the sill is four feet further along than it was at the fifth hour, and there is no stone anywhere on this ground that belongs to that crossing, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to let anybody call it a favour to me either."**

A cart went up the middle road at about the eighth hour of the evening with three cartloads' worth of paper on it and nothing in the bed of it, and seven people had laid more stone in one day than they had laid in a week, and nobody had asked any of them to stop, and the yard went on working.

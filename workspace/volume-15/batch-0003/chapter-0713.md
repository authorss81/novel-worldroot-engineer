# Chapter 713

## The Twenty-Sixth Of The Nineteenth, And An Ordinary Monday On Which Five People Decided Something Behind A Door On Thursday And Three Of This Holding's Own Went Down Four Miles With Shovels And Came Back With An Empty Barrow

The five went up the middle road before the light and the door was shut by the sixth hour, and the yard did not gather at the gate to watch them go, because there is nothing in this yard to gather for at six in the morning on a Monday.

Three men from this holding went down the middle road at about the seventh hour with shovels on their shoulders and a barrow and a length of rope, and they were gone until about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and they dug.

There is four feet of silt in the second place's branch and nearer five by now, and it has been going in since the branch went, and nobody in this holding owns a foot of that branch and all of this holding has to have somebody who digs it.

Odile Vray cut the day into four before light with a stick of charcoal on the back of the second seed sack and gave one of the four hours to men going out of the yard with shovels and coming back with nothing.

**"That is the fifth hour for the sill. That is the seventh hour for the boards and it is mine and the laundry and nobody else's. That is the tenth hour and there is a man on it who cannot be in two places, and the fourth is a mile and a quarter of road each way with a barrow and three shovels and a rope."**

She put the sack down against the boards.

**"And I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since the day you gave me a bucket and a masonry boy gave me a bridge, which is that I have been running this yard's arithmetic for eleven mornings and I have not once put the four miles into it, because the four miles belong to three men and not to a figure, and I have got no column for three men walking."**

Nia Vale gave the yard's own two stone figures and the launder together at about the seventh hour in one walk down the wall, which is not an order any of the people on this wall have ever used.

**"One thousand and three hundred and twenty-eight hundredweight at the channel and eight on yesterday. Four hundred and seventy-sixth of the run. And the stones are eight hundred and sixty where the wall begins and nine hundred and seven where it ends, which is forty-seven, and I have been putting the stones first for three days and today I have put them last and I would like somebody to check that I have not been wrong about it all week."**

She stopped at the coping and looked at both ends of it.

**"And I am aware of what I have just done. I have been asked about that tail by three people in a fortnight and I have not answered one of them and I am not going to, and the reason I am not going to is that the honest answer is that it is a stone that goes up a wall every morning and on one morning there was nothing on the end of it and on the next morning there was, and there is nobody in this yard who laid either of them in this month."**

Odile Vray gave the window at the third hour at the boards and gave the two halves as a subtraction with the total in the middle of it.

**"Four hundred and forty-three, and if you take the two hundred and twenty-seven away you have got the two hundred and sixteen, and the two hundred and twenty-seven is the half that moved this morning because it is an even morning, and I have said that out loud four mornings running now and if I say it a fifth time I am going to be a woman who says things out loud."**

The man of the north row with the cough gave the aggregate out loud in the middle of his row at about the eighth hour, to nobody, in the tone of a man reading a number off a post.

**"Four hundred and seventy-four at the head, the four hundred and seventy-third under the line and the four hundred and seventy-fifth over it. It has been a standing thing on that board in that room for longer than I have been in this yard and I have read it every morning of this month off the doorframe and I did not know until Thursday that it was a figure and not a word."**

He went on down the row.

**"And I will say the other thing. When the man at the end of this row died I was the one who went, and there was no line on that board for it and there is no line on it now for what my chest does, and there are four ruled lines in a book in this yard with nothing on them and I have known about those four lines for eleven years."**

He did not stop walking.

**"I am not asking for one of them. I am telling this yard that I know they are there, because there are about four people in this holding who would have written something on one of them if there had been a way, and I would rather that was said out loud than be a thing four people carry."**

Tova Reed gave the three ages from the seed bench in the order she found them when she reached up, which she did not apologise for and which she said was the only order available in that room.

**"Register form three hundred and seventy days. The ruled line in Silling's book, three hundred and sixty-four, and nothing on it. The charter two hundred and seventy-six. I reached up for the first one and my hand went along the shelf, and I am a person who sorts and I am not going to pretend I did it on purpose."**

She went back to the bench.

Sera Quill said the letter from the boards and did not consult anything.

**"A hundred and seventy-three days. And the youngest of the four sheets on that long table is three days younger than it is, and the two of them are the only figures in this holding that are the same age as each other, and I have been looking at that for a fortnight and it has not once meant anything."**

Then she took the slate out from under her arm, and she said the two on it, and she put it away again.

**"One hundred and sixth, and ninety-third, thirteen apart, and they have gone a step since Thursday because they step on the mornings they step on and not on any other. And the ninety-third is the one I carry and it is not in the rack and it is not on a wall and it is not on a sheet. I am not going to say why I have stopped saying the rest of it out loud, and I have told this yard twice that the answer is the same every morning and I am not going to tell it a third time on a Monday."**

The girl from the second place read the figure in force off the rack at the sixth hour and read it correctly, which nobody in that yard had expected, because she has been in this yard seventeen days.

**"A hundred and fifty-eight in force. Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and twenty-nine not."**

She put the rack back square.

**"It went up by one this morning and it goes up by one on two mornings in seven and by nothing on the other five, and no field has gone, and I asked the man at the boards twice today whether one was supposed to and he said that nobody has taken one for a fortnight and he did not say that anybody should."**

She turned round to the yard.

**"And I am going to say the other thing, which is that I have read that rack four times this week and every one of those four times the number was different, and there is a person I have never seen who goes round this holding making it different, and I have been wondering all week whether that is a person or a machine."**

The boy who carries water at the second hour read the compost line off the gate board and read it correctly and did not know why he was reading it.

**"Paid at thirty-one. Not discharged."**

He looked at the board for a while.

**"My dad had one like that on a wall at the second place and I thought it was a price for the cart. I have been in this yard ten days and I have heard four people explain that board to other people and every one of them has said the same two figures in a different voice, and I would like to know who writes it."**

Nobody told him, and he filled two cans and went back down the row.

The two that are the bookkeeper's were given by Soren Rill from the gate end in about nine words and he gave no reason for the nine, which is a departure from this month and which he announced.

**"Thirty-nine, and the rule open at the thirty-ninth time, and nothing else, and I am not going to tell you why I have cut the rest of it out of a Monday."**

He unbuttoned his coat.

**"There is a paper on that table with a day and an hour on it, and I have read the day and the hour about nine times today without reading one other word of it, and that is what a yard looks like the day before it is told something, and I have been in this yard twenty years and I have never seen it look like that."**

The bookkeeper gave the twenty-nine fetchings at the end of the north row and gave it in a form nobody has used.

**"The fourth house, the fetchings, and the count of questions this holding has put to him. That is all I am going to say and the last two words of it are the ones I want kept, because on Saturday five people from off this road were in this yard for two hours and not one of them came near that row, and on Sunday three of them stood at those boards and not one of them looked at it, and I have been standing on this step nine years and that is the first time in a month that the subject did not come up at all where it usually comes up."**

The drawer and the key went to the boy who carries water on his way out, and he said it in a way that made two people in that yard laugh for the first time this month.

**"Shut, and the key on the nail at the sixth hour, and I have just carried water past that drawer about four hundred times in my life and it has been shut every single time and I never once thought about what was in it until somebody told me not to."**

Ismay Rooke came down the middle road at about the ninth hour on his way back to the third place and stopped in the yard long enough to say one thing, and he said it with his back to the tool house.

**"There are two things I am not going to say. One of them is what five of us said behind a shut door on Thursday, and I am not going to say it here and I am not going to say it to him first, and there is a man in this yard who would be entitled to hear it before anybody else and he is going to hear it last."**

He shifted the tape on his shoulder.

**"And I am telling all of you that I am not going to say it, so that when I do not say it nobody in this yard can say afterwards that they did not know I had it. I have watched four people this month not tell somebody something, and every one of them was the right thing to do, and two of those four have since told somebody that nobody had told them anything."**

The three men came back up the middle road at about the fifth hour with their shovels and a barrow that had nothing in it, and they had dug for seven hours at a branch that belongs to a place four miles off, and one of them said that it was the fourth day they had done it and the third day that had been worth it, and that nobody had asked them to and that nobody would be thanked for it.

At dusk the sill was four feet further along than it had been at the fifth hour, and there was a boy's badly written figure pair on the north board that had not been written by a bookkeeper, and a sheet with five names on it was still lying face up on the middle table asking for nothing, and the sixth hour the paper named was a day off, and the yard went on working.

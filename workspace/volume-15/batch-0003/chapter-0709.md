# Chapter 709

## The Twenty-Second Of The Nineteenth, And Five People Behind A Shut Door From The Sixth Hour Until After Dark, And The Rotation Coming Round Outside It And Not Waiting

The council came down the middle road before light and went into the tool house and shut the door, and the door has a latch that Harlan Vetch oiled in the spring and it goes on from the inside, and that is the only sound in that room that anybody in this yard can hear from the gate.

The yard did not wait for it.

The seven were on the sill at the fifth hour. A woman of the second place was carrying a can past the north end and stopped for about half of what she said and the half she stopped for was about the third course, and then she said the rest of it and went on, and that was the whole of the hearing as far as this yard was concerned.

Harlan Vetch read the launder at the seventh hour from the coping at the tool house end and did not raise his voice, because the door is nine feet behind him and a man does not need to raise his voice to make a figure go into a yard.

**"One thousand and three hundred and twenty-two hundredweight, eight on yesterday. Four hundred and seventy-second of the run. Eight hundred and fifty-six at this end and nine hundred and three at the top of it, and that is forty-seven and it will be forty-seven tomorrow and it has been since the wall went up."**

The girl from the second place gave the window from the boards in a different order from every morning this month, which was the whole of the window first and the two halves second.

**"Four hundred and thirty-nine. And the halves are two hundred and twenty-five of the upper and two hundred and fourteen of the lower. The upper one moved this morning and the lower one did not, and I am saying that in that order because a man told me yesterday that he would rather say a wrong thing out loud than the right thing off a nail, and I have thought about that since."**

She put the chalk on the boards and stepped back from it.

**"I have read that stone for eleven days and I did not know which half moved until you told me yesterday, and now I do, and I would like to say that I worked it out on my own and I am not going to say that."**

Odile Vray gave the aggregate at the third hour of the afternoon, out loud, to four people who were cutting brush and not to the whole yard, which is a thing she has started doing and has not explained.

**"Four hundred and seventy at the head of it, and the four hundred and sixty-ninth under the line and the four hundred and seventy-first over it. The fourth is not due."**

She stopped cutting.

**"And I am going to say the rest of it once and then I am going to go back to cutting. Those three have been in a book in that room for eleven days and the book has not been asked about them by anybody in this yard, and I have started reading them to whoever is standing next to me, and the day somebody asks me why, I am going to say that a room with a shut door in it makes everything else in the yard feel like it needs saying."**

The bookkeeper brought the figure in force out onto the step at the seventh hour and said it to nine people while the tool house door stayed shut behind him.

**"A hundred and fifty-seven in force. Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and twenty-eight not. It went up by one this morning and it goes up by one on the mornings it goes up by one and by nothing on every other morning, and there are two more of those mornings before the end of this month."**

He did not go on.

Somebody at the back asked whether a field had gone, and he said no, and then he said why he was going to stop there, which he has not done before in nine years.

**"Because there is a shut door in this yard today and if I stand in this doorway and read six figures instead of three, then by the end of the week this yard will be doing every figure out loud on the steps, and the only thing this morning actually did to the book in that room was nothing at all, and I would like the nothing to be on the record at its proper size."**

Nobody blamed a field for not being taken.

Sera Quill came down to the boards at the north end at about the eighth hour with her slate under her arm and wrote the two on it and said the first of them out loud.

**"One hundred and fifth, and ninety-second. Thirteen apart, and they have been thirteen apart since before I came."**

She looked at the slate for a while with her thumb where the thumb goes.

**"And the ninety-second is the one I carry. There is no board in this yard with it on, and nothing cut in a stone anywhere in this holding, and none of the four sheets that anybody here has ever signed or refused would take a line for it. And a figure that is carried is not a figure that has been published, and I have said that in this yard four times and I am not going to say it a fifth time because it has stopped being true and I am the only one who noticed."**

She put the slate back under her arm.

**"I could tell you this morning why the two of them run the way they run. The answer would be the same answer every morning of this year and I have known it for four years, and I have decided that somebody can have it when I feel like giving something away."**

Tova Reed was at the north end of the yard at about the seventh hour with a seed sack open on the coping, and she had put herself where she could see the door and the four faces nearest the door and nothing else, and she did not mention having done it.

She got up and went down the row and put the sack over her shoulder and came back, and told the yard one thing on the way past.

**"Twenty-nine fetchings at the fourth house, not fetched, and nobody has been asked him anything at any hour today, and I can tell you that without asking him, because I have been at that bench since the days turned and I write down what I am told."**

Nia Vale gave the three ages off the rack in the doorway and gave them in a fresh order and she was not reading, she was remembering, which she told the room she was doing before she did it.

**"Three hundred and sixty-six on the register form. Two hundred and seventy-two on the charter, and that one is the nearest of the three to being a plan and not a plan. Three hundred and sixty on the ruled line in Silling's own book, and there is nothing written on it and there is nothing above the word."**

She came out of the doorway and stopped.

**"And that is me not reading them, which I have told this yard I was going to do, and the reason I am doing it is that there is nobody in the one room to read them and I would rather be wrong from memory in a doorway than right out of a book I have not been asked to open."**

Ismay Rooke had his hands on the pipe behind the tap house joint before anybody asked him about it.

**"It is weeping at the second hour and I do not know what that is called where the water has come from, and I have been in this yard two mornings and I have already heard two different explanations of it from two people who were not going to be here when it fails."**

He took his hands off the pipe and wiped them on the same cloth the man of the north row with the cough wipes his hands on, which nobody remarked on.

**"Shut drawer, key on the nail at the sixth hour. I have not seen inside it and I would not, and I have laid stone for twenty-six years and I have never once broken a drawer to see what was in it and I am not starting on a Thursday in a yard I do not work in."**

The man of the north row with the cough read the compost line off the gate board on his way to the fourth hour and read it as a man reads a thing he has decided to be the plainest thing available.

**"Paid at thirty-one. And not discharged. It is the same two figures in the same order it has been in for as long as I have been able to see the board, and I have come to think it is the only sentence in this yard that nobody has ever improved."**

He went back down the row.

Soren Rill gave the letter and gave the four sheets from opposite ends of the yard, so that nobody in between had to hear both at once.

**"The letter is a hundred and sixty-nine days. Four sheets on that table, none entered, none refused, no column cut under any of them, and the sheet with four thousand one hundred and one cans on it is on the middle table and is not one of those four and is not going to become one of them this morning."**

He stood at the gate end and shut his coat over nothing.

**"And there is a shut door in this yard with five people behind it and I have read this yard for twenty years, and I am telling you now, at about the ninth hour, that I do not know what is behind that door and I am not going to find out by walking past it more slowly than I walked past it this morning."**

The girl from the second place asked him whether that was a thing he was proud of.

**"No,"** he said. **"It is a thing I am able to do, which is not the same, and there are about four of us in this yard who can do it and one of us is at the north end of the row with a cough."**

The tool house door came open at about the hour after dark and five people came out of it and nobody said anything in this yard.

One of them stopped at the boards on the way to the gate and looked at the stone, and the yard watched a mason from the third place look at a wall, and then he went up the middle road.

The sill crew had laid more in that day than in any day of this month and nobody had told them to, and there were two courses at the north end that are finished and done, and the door was open again, and the yard went on working.

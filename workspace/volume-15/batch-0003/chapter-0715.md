# Chapter 715

## The Twenty-Eighth Of The Nineteenth, And Four Lines Coming Down The Middle Road In Three Hands About What Is Left Of A Relation Between Two Places After A Morning At A Gate, And Nobody In This Yard Improving Any Part Of It

The paper came down the middle road at about the fourth hour with the girl from the second place, and it had three signatures at the foot of it and four lines, and she stood at the boards and read it out in the open air in her own voice without asking anybody whether she should.

**"The second place will keep four women on your sill at the fifth hour. That is work and it is money and it is not affection, and we have said so to our own people so that nobody at your end gets a better story than it is."**

She turned the sheet over and looked at the back of it as though something might be written there.

**"The second place will send one man with a barrow on the days the fourth place can take him, and he will be paid for the days and not for the road, and that is also not affection. And the second place will not send anybody to dig the branch and we are not going to be asked to and we would have said no."**

Then she stopped for long enough that a man at the gate started to say something and did not say it.

**"And this is the fourth line and it is the one we wrote first."**

**"There is a house at the end of the branch and there is a man in it with a nephew in it, and the nephew came to us from the fourth place in the spring because there is no work at our end and there is work at this end, and he has been at the third place's road since the summer and he sends for nothing and he sends back nothing and he is not coming back."**

She put the sheet down on the boards.

**"That is what is left. Four women at the fifth hour for money, one man with a barrow for money, and one person who moved up a road and has not gone back. Take the money out of it and there is one person, and he is not even ours, he is a man from a fourth place who happens to be living at the end of a dry branch."**

Somebody at the gate said that the second place should not be saying its own standing out loud in this yard, and the girl said it back to them without heat in it at all.

**"Then it is a figure nobody can use on you, and it is a figure we would rather said than not, because you have been pricing standing off a sheet of your own all fortnight and ours is worth four lines and a signature."**

A man at the boards said that the second place had refused the bridge and had no business telling this holding what it was worth.

**"Then read the sheet again,"** she said. **"There is no bridge in it. There has never been a bridge in it. It is about a house and a nephew and a woman at the end of a branch, and if you want to make it about a bridge that is your yard and not mine."**

Odile Vray came off the boards and answered the man who wanted to defend the five for it, and she did it in about eleven words and then went back to the sill.

**"They decided it and they said why and none of the reasons was stupid. I have worked for twenty-two years and I have never once been able to say that about a decision that cost me, and I am not going to spend this morning defending them to you."**

Then a woman at the gate said that four women on a sill for money was not standing at all and that the second place should be ashamed of itself, and the girl answered that as well and again without heat.

**"We are not ashamed. We took the work. I am the one standing at your gate with an empty bundle most mornings and I have not been ashamed once, and if you want to be ashamed of us for taking money off a sill then you had better not put a schedule up your own wall with hours in it."**

Neither of those two things was improved by anybody in that yard, and nobody in that yard took a side, and Soren Rill stood at the gate end with his coat open and said nothing at all for about a quarter of an hour, which is the longest he has stood still in this month.

The book came back onto its shelf at about the seventh hour, because it comes back every ninth day whether or not anybody in this yard would rather it did not, and Kellan Rusk did not come out of the room to say so.

Odile Vray said it instead, at the boards, in the plainest words she could find.

**"The book is on its shelf. It has not moved anything and it is not going to, and that board still reads thirty-nine, and underneath those blanks there is a rule and it has not been opened for the thirty-ninth time or any time, and that is the whole of what a book coming round has ever done in this holding."**

She knocked her knuckle twice on the boards.

**"And I will say the part that is mine and not the bookkeeper's. Three days ago a stone on this wall came round to a round figure and did not open that rule. Yesterday five people said no at that gate and it did not open that rule. And this morning a woman stood at these boards and read four lines off her own place to forty people and it did not open that rule."**

She drew her hand back down her apron.

**"I do not know what that rule is for. I have worked out that it is for things this holding decides about itself, and I have got no idea what a holding decides about itself, and I would like somebody to tell me before the end of the season."**

The girl from the second place went back up to the far end of the wall after that and read the stones in the order of a person who had spent a morning saying things aloud and had stopped caring about the order.

**"One thousand and three hundred and thirty-one hundredweight, which is eight on yesterday and the largest that figure has been this month. And the four hundred and seventy-eighth of the run. And eight hundred and sixty-two at the gate end, nine hundred and nine at the far end, forty-seven of masonry."**

She stopped and looked along the wall.

**"And the window is four hundred and forty-five. The upper half is two hundred and twenty-eight and the lower is two hundred and seventeen, and the upper one moved this morning because it is an even morning, and I am saying that with nobody standing near me and I am not going to shout it."**

Harlan Vetch read the compost line off the gate board at about the sixth hour and gave it in a yard that has been unusually full of speech.

**"Paid at thirty-one and nothing let out, and I have said those words in this yard in about nine different orders this month and I have never once said why they are there."**

He shouldered his barrow.

**"It is there because thirty loads of what comes off eleven households has got to go somewhere and somebody has to be paid to look at it, and the only figure on that board is the looking. I do not know what happens to it. I have asked three people in nine years and I have got three different answers and I have never once been shown it."**

Soren Rill gave the three ages and the letter in one walk from the long table, and he gave them in the order of a man who is not a bookkeeper and is reading off a shelf.

**"There are three numbers on that shelf and I am not a bookkeeper and I read them where I find them. Three hundred and seventy-two days. Two hundred and seventy-eight days. Three hundred and sixty-six days, and that last one has been an empty ruled line since before my mother was at this gate, and I have asked twice what it is for and been told it is not mine to know."**

He stopped with his hand on the end sheet.

**"And the letter is a hundred and seventy-five days, which is the youngest of the four figures on that shelf and has gone up every morning of this month like a thing with a handle on it."**

He stopped at the end sheet.

**"Four sheets, none entered, none refused, no column cut under any of them, and the four lines the girl just read out at these boards are not one of them and were never going to be, and I said that when the paper came up the road on Saturday and I am saying it again on the day it came back down."**

Kellan Rusk gave the aggregate from inside the one room through the open door and did not come out.

**"Four hundred and seventy-six at the head, the four hundred and seventy-fifth under the line and the four hundred and seventy-seventh over it. It is the same three figures it has been every morning this month and I said them out of a doorway at about the eighth hour yesterday while forty people were standing at a gate and I did not shout them, because shouting figures at a gate would have been the wrong thing to do and I knew it at the time."**

The man of the north row with the cough said the fourth house and gave it in a new order and gave it while he was walking.

**"Nobody has asked him anything. Twenty-nine fetchings. That is all three of those facts and I have never said them in that order before and I have said them in about nine others."**

Nia Vale said the drawer and the key on her way past the one room and said them as a woman closing a subject.

**"Key on its nail at the sixth hour. Shut. Nothing in it."**

She went three steps and stopped.

**"And I am going to add the one thing I have learned this month, which is that I have been checking that drawer every morning for nine years in order to be able to say that it was shut, and not being able to say what was in it is not a fault in the drawer and it is not a virtue in me, and I am tired of it being the first thing I say when I come into a room."**

At dusk the four lines were still lying on the boards where the girl from the second place had put them, and nobody had turned them over and nobody had put them on a rack, and there was no bridge on the crossing and there was not half of one and no stone belonging to it within four days of road in either direction, and the haulage arrangement had not begun and would not begin until the fourth place could put a cart on a road it had not yet looked at, and the yard went on working.

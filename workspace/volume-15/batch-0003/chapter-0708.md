# Chapter 708

## The Twenty-First Of The Nineteenth, And A Woman Who Keeps The Pumps At A Place That Has None Standing In This Yard With A Sheet Of Her Own, And The Season Put Down As Water And Not As Weather

Auret Sill came up the middle road at about the fifth hour with a boy and a sheet folded in three and she did not come to the sill and she did not come to the wall, and she stood in the middle of this yard where there is no shade at all after the eighth hour and let the whole of it be looked at.

Harlan Vetch said the window at the boards before she had finished standing there, in the order of a man who was on a wall and did not see any of it move.

**"The two halves, and I am giving them the wrong way up on purpose. Two hundred and twenty-four over the top of it and two hundred and fourteen underneath, which is four hundred and thirty-eight between them, and I have not added them."**

The girl from the second place, who was filling a can at the trough, said it without stopping.

**"It is the lower one that moved. It is an odd morning."**

Harlan Vetch put the hod down.

**"Then that is twice this week that I have got that the wrong way round for eleven years and I am going to let it stand in the record that a man of forty-one read a stone this morning and was corrected by a woman with a can."**

He picked the hod up again and went back up the wall.

The girl from the second place read the launder and the two cut figures off the stones at the gate end in one breath, and gave the weight before the day, which is the reverse of what the mason did yesterday, and neither of them noticed that it was a different order.

**"One thousand and three hundred and fourteen hundredweight, and five off yesterday. Four hundred and seventy-first of the run, and it is a day on that one, not a round number, and I have stopped being pleased about that."**

**"Eight hundred and fifty-five where it begins, nine hundred and two where it ends."**

She put the can down and looked at Auret Sill.

**"That is my morning. I read it every morning and I have never once said it to anybody, and I said it to you because you are the only person in this yard I do not know."**

Auret Sill opened the sheet she had brought and did not hand it to anybody.

**"You are welcome to it. I am not going to say anything else about that, because if I thank you for reading a stone then you will think of doing it twice and it is not worth your morning."**

Then she said the thing she had come up that road to say, and she said it standing still, and she said it to about fourteen people, and she gave no name to any of it.

**"The season. That is what I am here about and it is the only thing on this sheet, and it is the second thing your council's answer is going to cost, and I am going to say it now rather than after, because after it will sound like an excuse and it is not one."**

She turned the sheet round and held it at her own chest and did not put it into anybody's hands.

**"Nine cans to our cistern at the second hour, and nine again at the fourth. Four houses at the end of the branch, and a mile and a quarter of that walk carried on two women, every day, because there is nothing at the end of it to push a barrow to. And four days in seven the pump at the head will not draw at all and has to be primed by hand, and it has not drawn of its own accord since the branch went."**

A man at the gate asked how long that goes on for.

**"I am not going to give you a date. You have a date for your span and a date for your council and a day and an hour set for all of it, and every one of those is a real thing somebody can put in a book, and I have got a season and no book, and I would rather you had a thing you can count than a thing you can only be told about."**

She said the next part without any change in her voice at all, which was the part that made the yard go quiet.

**"That is arithmetic and road. It is not temper and I would like it written in whatever your five people are going to write, because there is going to be a month, and probably more than a month, in which somebody at my place says that a place said no because it was angry, and I want it on a paper somewhere that was not said by us."**

Nobody applauded her and nobody gave her anything to do with it.

Then she gave the sheet to the middle table and stepped away from it, and Odile Vray came over and looked at it and did not pick it up.

**"That stays here in the middle and not on the long table, and it does not go into that room, and nothing on it is copied, and I would like the yard to understand that I am the one who keeps the common key at that place and it is not my key that makes it a paper of ours."**

She turned round to the sill.

**"And when you copy it, and somebody will, you copy it wrong, because that is what people do with a number they are given. Four thousand one hundred and one is four thousand one hundred and one cans filled by hand since the spring. It is not four thousand households and it is not a figure of anything except cans."**

Kellan Rusk gave the aggregate out of the one room to nobody who had come for it.

**"Four hundred and sixty-nine at the head, and the four hundred and sixty-eighth under the line and the four hundred and seventieth over it, and the fourth not due for two more mornings. I have had three people at that door this morning asking me what a clause is and I have told three of them the same thing, which is that I do not know and it was not me who ruled it."**

Ismay Rooke asked for the three ages and got them in the order he had asked in, which is the one order of the three that nobody in this holding has used.

**"Charter first at two hundred and seventy-one days, because it is the only one of the three that anybody outside this yard has ever heard of. Then the register form at three hundred and sixty-five."**

He shifted the tape.

**"And then the one I cannot give you as a figure, because it is not on anybody's sheet and it is not in anybody's rack. A man called Silling ruled a line in his own book, and it stands at three hundred and fifty-nine days, and he wrote nothing on the line and nothing above the word. I have now seen that same empty line in two books in two places in four years and I would like somebody to tell me what it is for."**

Nobody told him.

Nia Vale gave the letter on her own, standing in the doorway of the one room with the sheet of her own still folded in three in her hand.

**"A hundred and sixty-eight days. It is not one of the four and it is not one of the five and it is not a term and it is not a figure of anybody's standing."**

She looked at the sheet of her own and then put it behind her back, which was the first time in this month that anybody had seen her hold one paper away from another.

**"I have a sheet of my own with three lines on it and I have just decided that it is not going on that table either, and there are now two things in this holding that are being held back from a table, and neither one of them is on a wall."**

Auret Sill was asked about the joint at the back of the tap house, and she went and put her own hand on the pipe behind it, and she did it before she said anything, which is not what the people of this yard are used to from her.

**"You are all calling it a weeping joint. It is not weeping, it is the water finding the lowest place it can be put, which is a different thing and it is a thing I know about and it is a thing nobody in this holding has ever asked me about because you have had the same joint eleven years."**

She took her hand off the pipe.

**"The pipe is standing. I felt it. Feeling it is not the same as knowing and I am not going to let it be the same as knowing, and I am aware that I have just told fourteen people that I can be near a thing and not know it, which is a strange thing to come up a road to say."**

Then she read the compost line off the board at the gate end in her own trade's words, which nobody in this holding had heard anybody do.

**"Paid at thirty-one. And not discharged, and I know what both of those words mean where I come from and neither of them means what it means on that board."**

She rubbed her thumb across the figure.

**"In my trade those two columns are money in and water out. Yours are money in and nothing out, and I am not going to tell you which of us has the worse board, because you have a line that has gone up and I have a place that has gone down and both of those happened without anybody deciding them."**

Ismay Rooke asked what the board in the one room was for, and Kellan Rusk told him, because on this morning he was telling people.

**"Thirty-nine blanks and a rule underneath them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. That is all I have got for a man from another place and I have told it to him straight because he has come four miles on somebody else's time and I owe him the short answer."**

The girl from the second place asked about the low board by the gate, on her way out, and got what everybody gets.

**"No day on it, not picked up, not taken back. That is nine years of it and there is a woman in this yard who has walked past it every morning of this month and there is me, and I am asking because I am new, and I have been told that I am not getting an answer and I am not going to argue about it."**

Nobody argued with her about it.

Auret Sill went down the middle road at about the seventh hour of the evening, and she went past the fourth house at the north row and she did not stop at it, and Harlan Vetch was at the north end and saw her not stop.

**"Twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, and she came four miles up a road with a sheet of her own and she did not stop at that row. I have been keeping that list for a fortnight and today it got two entries and both of them were somebody choosing not to do something, which is not the same kind of entry as the others and I am not going to make it one."**

Then she went to the north end and found the chalk and wrote the two figures under the heading that has read the same way since before she came up that road, and the heading is of a month nine months back and she wrote it exactly as it stands without touching it. She wrote them in the hand she has been learning all month and she did not read them out.

**"Two hundred and six, and three hundred and ninety-five, and that is the whole of what is written under that heading today."**

Auret Sill had stopped at the gate for that and she said the only thing she said about it.

**"Do you keep that at our place?"**

The girl said no, and Auret Sill nodded as though she had been told something she already knew.

**"Then it is in one yard and not two, and there is a board at the far end of my branch with the paid column on it and not one other figure on it anywhere, and that is a thing I have been meaning to say to somebody for four months and today was a morning to say it."**

Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to stay.

At dusk the sheet stood face up on the middle table in four people's hands, and it was the second place's paper and not this holding's record of what the second place said, and the sill crew had gone at the seventh hour with seven bodies, and the ground inside the eleven acres was exactly where it had been at the fifth hour with nothing on it and nothing measured against it, and the yard went on working.

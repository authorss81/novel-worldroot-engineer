# Chapter 693

## The Sixth Of The Nineteenth, And A Schedule Put Up On The Yard Wall In The Yard's Own Order, And Two People In The Same Yard Answering One Question Two Different Ways

Kellan Rusk put the schedule on the yard wall at about the eighth hour with chalk and a straight edge he had borrowed from the mason, and he did it in the order he wanted it in and not the order it came off the paper from the office, and nobody told him he could not.

> Copied out on the yard wall at the eighth hour in the clerk's own hand, being the office's schedule of the moment set out in the yard's order with the yard's hours ruled under it, and entered in neither of the two other books and not in the one in the rack:
>
> **The moment.** The last working morning of this month. Not a date, and not to be turned into one by anybody in this yard.
>
> **The fifth hour.** Sill at the north end. Two on the face, two on the sluice, one on the chain with the bucket.
>
> **The seventh hour.** Gauge board read out loud by the woman standing at it. Sluice pin home, and counted out loud as well, because a count that does not carry is a count that did not happen.
>
> **The tenth hour.** Top eleven courses of the wall at the tool house end, and four at the bottom taken up and bedded again where the frost got under them.
>
> **The rule under all four of them.** Nobody signs an hour. Nobody is fetched for an hour. A person who turns up late writes the hour they turned up at, in the space, on their own paper.
>
> This wall does not enter the rack, and it does not open the second rule under the blanks, which stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time.

He stepped back from it and looked at it and then wiped one of the fours off the bottom and wrote it again a half inch lower, and then he left it alone.

The rotation came round at the seventh hour, before the wall was chalked, and Kellan Rusk did it from the step.

**"A hundred and fifty-three in force,"** he said, **"which is one on, and one on is the whole of what this morning did to that book. It has not gone on by two on any morning this month and it is not going to, and a man who watches it for a year will find that out for himself and I have watched it for nine years and it has never once told me anything."**

He put his thumb under the second line and left it there.

**"Twenty-nine of them went out at whatever hour their own hour is, and that has not moved in four years. The rest of them are a hundred and twenty-four and they are still on the books at this hour."**

He shut the book on his thumb.

**"No field was taken this morning. I said that on Friday in front of a yard that was arguing about a list, and I am saying it again today in front of a wall, and I am going to keep saying it until somebody in this holding stops waiting for a field to be taken so that they can be angry with somebody."**

Sera Quill came up the middle road at about the eighth hour with the slate under her arm and put it on the boards at the north end and opened it with her thumb where her thumb goes.

**"It has stepped again,"** she said, **"and I will say both numbers and I will say the same thing about the second one that I said on Friday, because a thing said once in a yard is a thing that only one person heard."**

She put her thumb under the lower line.

**"One hundred and first and eighty-eighth. Thirteen apart. The first is over a hundred now and it took the ending on the tail because it is not a round figure, and the second is eighty-eight and it is not a round figure either, and neither of them is a round figure this morning."**

She moved her thumb onto the third line, which was her own hand and which she had said out loud four years ago and once a month since.

**"The eighty-eighth is the one I carry. It is not in the holding's book, it is not on the wall that was chalked ten minutes ago, it is not on the four sheets, and it is not on anything a person could hold. A figure that is carried is not a figure that has been published, and I am saying it in a yard again because a yard is where I say things."**

Then a man came up the middle road from the third place at about the ninth hour on foot, with a boy behind him carrying a bundle, and he asked at the gate for somebody who could tell him what the holding was being asked to keep.

Nobody in that yard said that he had come a long way, and he did not say it himself.

He got two answers inside about four minutes and they were not the same answer.

Soren Rill gave him the first one, from beside the chalked wall, and he gave it short.

**"It is being asked to maintain five things. A sill, a sluice, a gauge board, a gate line and a wall. They are on a sheet and they are now on that wall, and the five are the right five, and there is not one hour in the sheet and there are four hours on the wall."**

He looked at the wall while he was finishing it.

**"And I am not going to tell you what happens when two of the five cannot be done in one day, because nobody in this yard has written that down and I am not going to be the first one to say it out loud to a man who has walked here for it."**

Harlan Vetch gave him the second one from the tool house end, and he took his time about it.

**"It is being asked to maintain five things and it is being asked to stop saying that the branch above the second place is this holding's problem. Those are two different sentences and only one of them is on the sheet in words."**

He wiped his hands down his coat.

**"And it is being asked to maintain a wall with a weeping joint behind the tap house that is not one of the five, and I have watched that joint every morning this month and it has come away wet every morning of it, and if a man from the third place wants to know what this holding is being asked to maintain, that joint is not in the answer and the not being in the answer is part of the answer."**

The man from the third place stood between the two of them and asked Marek, who was at the middle table, directly.

**"And what is it for, the wall you put up this morning?"**

Somebody at the boards said that was the third answer and there were only going to be two, and somebody else said a third answer was not a bad thing.

Marek did not give one.

**"I am not going to answer that man a third time this morning,"** he said. **"Two people have told him two things that are both true and I have watched the last four days of this yard to work out that the second of them is closer to the ground than the first one is. I am not going to stand between them and pick one, because the moment a mason and a bridge get arbitrated by the engineer of record, this yard has got a chain of command on the thing that is supposed to be a list of five items."**

The man from the third place did not choose between them in that yard. He stood by the gate for about an hour with his boy and the bundle and he did not pick one, and at the end he said that both of them would go back up the road with him as they stood, and neither of them asked him to.

The fourth place's man read the launder at about the eleventh hour with the third place's man standing at the stone watching him do it, and he read it in the shortest form he has and then said the parts of it twice.

**"That channel came down this morning at one thousand and two hundred and ninety-eight hundredweight, which is eight up, because this is an even morning. The window is four hundred and twenty-three: two hundred and seventeen of it is the rising half and went in the night, and two hundred and six is the falling half and has not moved since Monday. That weight is the four hundred and fifty-sixth of the run."**

He wiped under the line and looked at the man from the third place.

**"The upper half is the one that moves on an even morning and the lower half is the one that moves on an odd morning, and that is not a figure of speech, and I have been wrong about it twice this week and I will be wrong about it again some time, and you can go up that road and say that a man at a stone in this holding reads two halves and does not add them."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack with his back half to the yard because the wall had chalk on his hands and he did not want it on the books.

**"The register form is three hundred and fifty days. The charter is two hundred and fifty-six. And the ruled line in Silling's own book is three hundred and forty-four with nothing on it and nothing above the word, and I have been asked twice this week whether that line is a mistake and it is not a mistake and it is not mine and I was not here when it was ruled."**

He turned round with the book shut.

**"That is three figures and I have said them nine mornings running and I am not going to improve them. The nearest of the four sheets on the long table is a hundred and fifty-three days old, and that figure has nothing to do with these three and I would like somebody in this yard who is good at arithmetic to notice that."**

Hesta Lyle came out and read the head of the long sheet and the two limbs under it, and she read them to the man from the third place rather than to her own yard.

**"The head of that sheet is four hundred and fifty-four. One step below it, four hundred and fifty-third. One step above, four hundred and fifty-fifth. Three figures sitting under a head of four hundred, with nothing under the third of them, because the fourth is not due, and because a man came up a road this morning and a road is not a reason."**

She put two fingers on the sheet.

**"And the third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved this month, and there are four ruled lines under two words and they are bare, and a wall got chalked this morning and neither of those got a mark."**

Nia Vale did the wall at about the second hour of the afternoon and had to do it round the chalk, and she said something about that which made a laugh go round the yard, which was the only laugh anybody in that holding heard this week.

**"Paid at thirty-one and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything, and the wall at the gate end says eight hundred and forty and the tool house end says eight hundred and eighty-seven, and the forty-seven of masonry between them is the same forty-seven it was the morning the wall went up. Four sheets, not five, and the drawer behind this end is shut and the key went onto its nail at the sixth hour."**

She looked at the chalked hours above her shoulder.

**"And there is a schedule on this wall now with four hours on it and a rule under them, and not one person in this holding has signed an hour and not one person has been fetched for an hour, and I have been in this yard four years and I have never seen a rule put up that did not come with somebody being fetched for something."**

Soren Rill walked the ring of bare ground at dusk without stepping into it, and the ladder against the tool house wall went up no rung and no figure went against either of the two people it belongs to, and the thing on the low board had no day on it and was not picked up and was not taken back.

**"Nobody in this yard has been asked to choose anything today, and that includes the man from the third place, who is still standing at that gate and has not told anybody which of those two answers he means to write down."**

At dusk Kellan Rusk turned the rack key and said the standing of it from inside the room.

**"Thirty-nine blanks and the second rule open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the wall I chalked this morning did not enter one figure into that book and was never going to. Nothing is entered in this holding's book on a Tuesday, and the two other books stand at fifty-three and fifty-three and have never once been the same list, and neither of them took a thing up the road with the man from the third place."**

He shut the rack.

**"Eleven journeys and no twelfth, and fifteen lines with a clean space above the fifteenth. And on the figure for the old man at the end of the north row: twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, nothing asked of him at any hour today. That is the last thing I am going to say this evening."**

The two answers were still in the yard at dusk, one from a man by the chalk and one from a mason at the far end of the wall, and neither had been corrected and neither had been taken back, and the schedule stood on the wall with four hours on it and nobody's name under any of them, and the yard went on working.

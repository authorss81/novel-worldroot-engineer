# Chapter 688

## The First Of The Nineteenth, And A Calendar That Turned In The Night And Nothing Else, And A Man Who Has Come Down The Lane To Say A Moment

The days turned in the night and nothing in this holding turned with them.

That was the first thing anybody said about it, and it was said by Kellan Rusk, from the step of the one room, to nobody in particular, at about the sixth hour.

**"The days went over and the frame over the run did not go with them. That heading is of a month nine months back and it was of a month nine months back on Tuesday morning and it is of a month nine months back this morning, and I have written it out in full today because today is the morning the days turned, and a man is allowed to say of this month from the morning after a turn and not on the turn itself."**

He put the book down on the step and opened it at the page where the pair goes.

**"One hundred and ninety-six of three hundred and seventy-five. It is written. Nobody has said it out loud in this yard this morning and nobody is going to, and Renn Ashby told me on Tuesday at that trough that he had said it for the last time and he was not going to thank anybody for it, and I have not asked him to say it and I am not going to ask him."**

He shut the book on his thumb.

**"Two figures on the board every odd morning and nobody reading them. That is what stopping looks like from the step. It is not a hole in the yard. It is two figures that used to have a voice and have not got one, and the heading over them has not moved, and neither has anything else."**

The return came round at the seventh hour, because returns come every ninth day and there had been one nine days ago, and the yard knew it before the book did.

Kellan Rusk turned the rack key, which was on its nail until he took it down, and stood with the book open on his forearm in the doorway.

**"Thirty-nine blanks on that board and the rule underneath them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and it has not opened anything and it is not going to. That is the return, and it is the whole of the return, and there is no more of it than that."**

He shut the book and turned the key over in his hand.

**"No weight goes against it and no ordinal goes against it and nothing goes against it that is left of anything, because there is no column here that wants those, and because in four years not one of those columns has ever wanted to be filled in. If a man four days north wants a number for the day the book comes round to, he can have the book."**

He put the key back on the nail and went inside.

There was a cart at the gate at about the eighth hour, and it had come down the middle road from four days north and the off horse was lathered and the driver's hands were raw, and the man got down off it and did not sit down and did not ask for anything.

He was about forty-five and he said that he was the schedule clerk of the office and that his name was Corin Dace and that he had been on the road two days, and then he stood in the middle of the yard with his hat in his hand and waited for somebody to tell him where to put himself.

Marek told him to stand where he was.

Corin Dace said the moment in his own mouth, once, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has carried a long way.

**"The last working morning of this month. Not a date. A morning, and the office has fixed it and the office can say what it fixed and cannot say why, and I have come two days to say it out loud in a yard instead of leaving it on paper, because paper has been sitting in this holding long enough and nobody has read it."**

Somebody in the yard asked him whether the office would take a day.

**"No,"** Corin Dace said.

**"And whether the days turning had anything to do with it?"** somebody at the middle table said.

**"It has nothing to do with it,"** Corin Dace said. **"The days turned last night. I read the paper off the same table in the same room on Tuesday and the morning it names was the same morning this morning. That is all I know about it and I am not going to invent a reason for it."**

Nobody argued with him, and nobody thanked him for the two days, and nobody asked him a second time about either answer.

Kellan Rusk came out and read the schedule out from the middle table while Corin Dace stood beside it and did not correct him, and the clerk's voice was the clerk's and the order of it was the clerk's.

> The schedule of the office of works, four days north, carried down the middle lane at the eighth hour and read in the yard by the clerk out of the office's own hand, and the turn of the days is not in it and did not move it:
>
> **First.** The moment at which this holding must say in writing what it will maintain and what it will not is the last working morning of this month.
>
> **Second.** Nothing in this paper is a date. A date is not a thing the office recognises and not a thing a holding argues with, and any reading of this paper that turns the moment into a day is a reading, and not the paper.
>
> **Third.** The writing goes up the lane on the morning after. It is not read at a gate. It is not entered in any book of this holding before the clerk has copied it.
>
> **Fourth.** Nothing in this paper is entered, refused or cut under in the book that stands in the one room, and the second rule under the blanks on that board stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time.

The clerk put his own hand flat on the paper when he had finished and took it off again, and the paper stayed on the middle table where it was, and it did not go on the long table with the four.

Nia Vale was along the wall with her cloth by the ninth hour, and she did the gate line first, while the sun had not yet got onto the stone.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** she said, **"and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything, and the days going over has not touched it and is not going to."**

She wiped the wall.

**"Nearest the gate, eight hundred and thirty-five. Away from me to the tool house, eight hundred and eighty-two. Forty-seven of masonry again, and it will be forty-seven of masonry on the last morning of this month as well. Behind this end there is a drawer and it is shut, and the key for it is on one nail, and that is where it stays."**

She went to the long table and stood over the four sheets without touching them.

**"Four, and not five, and this holding's book has no column cut under any one of them. The nearest has been on that table a hundred and forty-eight days and there are nine inches of board standing clear at either end of it. A turn of the days enters nothing and refuses nothing. It is a calendar, and that is a piece of paper."**

Marek was at the sill end with a length of chalk and did the gauge board and did not go near the crew, and the crew worked from the second place all morning with a ladle and a coil of rope and did not ask him for anything.

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end about midday and stood at the tool house end of the wall and did not look at the schedule.

**"And inside that tool house there is a mark four inches long that forks twice. There is still no second mark anywhere on that wall, after four years of nobody measuring it and everybody meaning to. The schedule fixes a morning. It does not fix a chisel."**

He looked at the sleeve on Marek's arm where the mark was under the cloth, and then he went back to the wall.

Then he looked at the sleeve on Marek's arm where the mark was under the cloth.

**"I have said all of that in this yard on a good many mornings and I am not going to improve on it because an office has sent a man down the lane. The schedule fixes a morning. It does not fix a chisel."**

Hesta Lyle was at the long sheet at about the second hour of the afternoon with it squared against the wood and her hand flat on the head of it.

**"Four hundred and forty-nine at the top, the four hundred and forty-eighth one step down and the four hundred and fiftieth one step up, and there will be a third under it before the days turn over again and a fourth after that."**

She lifted her hand off the head of the sheet.

**"Nobody has improved the heading over the third column of the sheet of terms, and the column itself is ruled and empty, and a man from an office stood in my yard this morning and did not once look at it, which is the correct thing for him to have done. Under two words there are four ruled lines and they are bare. Nobody has written a fifth term, and I would like a clerk who reads things out for a living to notice that."**

Soren Rill came in past the gate at about the third hour and went along the inside edge of the eleven acres the way he has gone along it for four years, and did not go near the ring and did not go near the ladder.

**"Nobody climbed anything, nobody has been asked to choose anything, and the thing on the low board has no day on it and did not get one because the days turned."**

He stood with his back to the board.

**"I have been in this yard four years and the days have turned under me more times than I can count, and I have noticed this month that I used to look at the low board when the days turned and I have not looked at it once this week. I do not know what that means and I am not going to tell anybody in this yard that I have noticed it."**

Corin Dace left at about the fourth hour with his cart and he was two days on the road again by the time he reached the gate, and he did not stay for the afternoon and nobody asked him to.

At dusk the fourth place's man read the launder for the second time of the day, and he gave the figures in an order of his own choosing because nobody had told him not to.

**"Four hundred and fifty-first of the run,"** he said, **"at one thousand and two hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, which is five down from yesterday morning, because this is an odd morning and on an odd morning the lower half is the one that goes first in the night. Lower two hundred and four. Upper two hundred and fourteen, and that one has not moved since Wednesday. Window four hundred and eighteen."**

He put the pencil behind his ear and looked down the middle road.

**"The days turned and the launder did not turn with them and the window did not turn with them, and I have read this stone every morning this week and I have never once seen a calendar get into it."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack in the order he wanted them and not the order of the shelf, because the shelf was behind him and he had turned it round on purpose.

**"Register form three hundred and forty-five days. Charter two hundred and fifty-one. And a figure that has been growing by one a day for longer than that shelf has stood, with nothing written against it at all: three hundred and thirty-nine, on the second ruled line in Silling's book. If a man wants to know what I think of that line he can come and look at it and see that it is empty."**

Nobody had asked the man of about fifty anything at any hour of the turn, and his hand was still on that frame at dusk. The girl from the second place was still on the gate step with her bundle, and she had asked this holding for nothing at all that day. The schedule lay flat on the middle table with a morning on it, and the yard went on working.

# Chapter 690

## The Third Of The Nineteenth, And A Shape Written On One Sheet With A Ruled Space At The Foot Of It, And Two People Who Would Not Put Their Names In The Space

The shape was written on one sheet at about the seventh hour and put on the middle table face up with a space ruled at the foot of it, and it was in Marek's own hand because he would not let a clerk do it and would not let anybody else read it to him first.

He read it out once, standing at the table, in a yard where about twenty people had got up in the dark to be there.

> One sheet, one column of five, put on the middle table at the seventh hour in the engineer of record's own hand, with a space ruled at the foot of it and nothing in the space:
>
> **First.** This holding will maintain the sill at the north end, the sluice, the gauge board, the gate line, and the wall from the gate to the tool house.
>
> **Second.** This holding will not maintain the branch above the second place, and will not pay water into it.
>
> **Third.** The eleven acres are not on this list, and nothing on this list may be read as saying that they are not part of this holding.
>
> **Fourth.** The joint at the back of the tap house is not on this list.
>
> **Fifth.** No person is named on this sheet, and no person in this holding is to be asked to sign it.
>
> This sheet is not the sheet of terms, it is not on the long table, it is not one of the four, and no column has been cut under it in this holding's book.

When he had read it out he put his hands in his pockets and stood back from the table and let the yard have it.

Nobody called it a lie. Nobody called it a favour to the office and nobody called it a favour to the holding, and about a dozen people said that they could build the first of those five, and three of those five got argued over for two hours without anybody raising their voice.

Soren Rill said his piece at about the ninth hour and he said it to the table and not to Marek.

**"I am not going to sign that and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to be blamed for it, and I would like all three of those put in the same breath because I have watched men in this yard get asked and then get blamed and it is the asking that does the damage."**

He put his hand flat on the table beside the sheet and not on it.

**"It is a better shape than the one I expected and it is still a shape I cannot stand behind. There is nobody's name on it and there is nobody's hour on it, and the sill at the fifth hour is held by five women from the second place who are not this holding's people and who have not been asked whether they will be here at the fifth hour next week, and that is the whole of what I have against it."**

Nia Vale came to the table at about the tenth hour with her cloth over her arm and looked at the sheet without touching it and did not read it twice.

**"I am not going to put my hand at the foot of it either, and it has nothing to do with the fifth line. I write one sheet in this holding and I wrote it in my own hand and it is in my coat, and I am not going to put my hand under a second sheet written by somebody else."**

She turned and went back to the wall, and she came back about a minute later and said one more thing across her shoulder.

**"And the eleven acres are in the third line and nobody in this yard needed to write that line, because everybody who has been in this yard four years knows it. Writing it down is not the same as having looked at it."**

Tova Reed was on the side of the table where she could see the mouths, and she wrote down what she was told and did not ask twice, and she did not look up from the board while any of it was being said.

The pair went on the board at about the seventh hour, underneath the frame, and it went there in Kellan's hand and Renn Ashby's hand and nobody's mouth.

Kellan Rusk put it up while the yard was still standing about waiting for somebody to say something wrong with it, and Renn Ashby stood beside him with his hands behind his back and let him do it.

He wrote one hundred and ninety-seven of three hundred and seventy-seven on the board under the heading, and the heading said of a month nine months back and said it exactly as it has said it every morning this month, and he read none of it out, and there was no voice at that trough this morning and there will be no voice at it this morning after this one.

Kellan Rusk took his hand off the chalk and looked at what he had written and did not say it again.

**"Two figures,"** he said. **"That is what stopping looks like on a wall. There are two figures on that board and they will be two figures tomorrow morning and the morning after, and the heading over them has not moved and is not going to. Renn Ashby told this yard on the Tuesday before the month turned that he had said them at that trough for four years and that he had said them for the last time, and he was not going to make anything of it and he was not going to thank anybody for it."**

Renn Ashby did not say a word. He put his hands down at his sides, looked at the two figures on the board for a while, and then went out to the gate and stood at the gate for the rest of that part of the morning without speaking to anybody.

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end later and stood in front of the sheet on the middle table and read all five lines with his lips moving.

**"The wall is in the first line,"** he said, **"and the sill is in the first line, and the joint at the back of the tap house is not, and I would like whoever wrote this to understand that I have not walked past that joint for four years without counting and I am not going to stop counting it because a piece of paper in this yard has decided it is not on a list."**

He turned round to the yard.

**"That is not me refusing to sign it. I am going to sign the day they put the tap joint on a list. That is a day in this yard and I will wait for it and I will not be asked to wait by anybody."**

Hesta Lyle came up behind the mason with the long sheet held against her hip and read the three figures on it out to nobody in particular.

**"The head of it is four hundred and fifty-one,"** she said. **"Underneath, one step down, four hundred and fiftieth. Above it, one step up, four hundred and fifty-second. I have written the fiftieth under it this morning because that is the figure that goes under it, and not the one I wrote on Friday. Three figures and a head of four hundred, and nothing under the third of them, because the fourth is not due and an early fourth is not a figure I enter."**

She put her hand flat on the top of the sheet and left it there a moment.

**"And the list on the middle table has five lines on it and not one hour in it, and this one has three figures on it and not one of the three is a morning. There are two sheets in this yard that count things, and neither of them has ever counted a person, and there is a third sheet on that table with a space ruled at the foot of it that counts nothing at all."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack in the order of a man reading a list out to test whether a list holds.

**"Three hundred and forty-seven days on the register form. Two hundred and fifty-three on the charter. And the second ruled line in Silling's own book stands at three hundred and forty-one, with nothing written on it and a clean space above the word. The list on that table has five lines on it. The book in that room has three figures in it that the list does not mention and would not have the room for."**

He shut the book and put it away.

**"And nothing went into the requests this morning and nothing went into the section-nine notes, and those two books are at fifty-three apiece, and they have never once been the same list, and a shape on a table is not an entry in either of them. I am not going to enter it and nobody asked me to and I would have said no if they had."**

Nia Vale came along the wall at the middle of the day and did the two figures with the cloth and did the gate line and then stopped at the long table and counted under her breath.

**"Eight hundred and thirty-seven where the wall begins and eight hundred and eighty-four at the far end of it, and forty-seven of masonry between them, and the drawer behind that end is shut, and the key for it went onto its nail at the sixth hour and it will come off that nail at the sixth hour tomorrow. Paid at thirty-one, and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything at all."**

She came back down the table.

**"Four sheets and not five, and none of the four entered and none of the four refused. The nearest one has been lying there a hundred and fifty days, which is a round half of a hundred days, with nine inches of clear board standing either side of it. That sheet on the middle table with five lines and a space at the foot of it is not one of those four and it is not going to become one of them by anybody putting a name in the space."**

The fourth place's man read the launder at about the second hour of the afternoon, late, in the shortest form he has, and gave the halves before the whole for the first time in a week.

**"Falling half two hundred and five, rising half two hundred and fifteen, window four hundred and twenty. Ahead of all of that: one thousand and two hundred and eighty-seven hundredweight came down this morning at five under, because Saturday is an odd morning and on an odd morning the falling half is the one that goes in the night. It is the four hundred and fifty-third of the run."**

He put the pencil behind his ear.

**"A week of one order every morning, and this afternoon I read it the other way about, because there is a sheet on that table with a space at the foot of it. A man who reads the same thing in the same order for a week stops seeing it and starts copying it."**

Harlan Vetch was at the sill end at about the fourth hour and watched the crew from the second place come off the stones, and he did not go out to them and he did not call anybody over.

He said one thing to the yard about it when he came back through, and the yard heard it.

**"Five women from the second place have been on that sill two hours and there is not one name of them on that sheet and not one hour of them either, and that is not a complaint from me and I am not going to make it one. I am going to wait and see whether anybody writes the fifth hour down anywhere this week."**

Soren Rill went along the inside edge of the eleven acres at dusk and did not go near the ring, and the ladder against the tool house wall went up no rung, and the thing on the low board had no day on it and no name at the foot of it, and nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything at any hour of this day.

At dusk Kellan Rusk turned the rack key and said it from inside the room, and nobody moved out of his way to hear it.

**"The board in the one room is at thirty-nine blanks and the second rule is standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. It stood there the whole of the three hours five people spent arguing over a shape at a table, and a piece of paper is not a reason for anything, and neither is a signature."**

He shut the rack.

**"Nothing goes into this holding's book on a Saturday, and the other two books are fifty-three apiece and took nothing off that shape. Eleven journeys and no twelfth; fifteen lines and a clean space above the fifteenth. For the old man at the end of the north row: twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, unasked, and I have said it in this yard eight mornings running and I am going to say it on the morning a shape gets signed as well."**

The girl from the second place was still at the gate with her bundle under her arm. She had read the sheet on the middle table for a long time and had said nothing about it to anybody, and nobody in that yard had put one word to her about it. In the doorway of the one room the man of about fifty went on being noticed with, and the yard went on working.

The key went onto its nail in the doorway behind him, and he stood in the doorway with his hand on the frame until the light came off the top of the wall.

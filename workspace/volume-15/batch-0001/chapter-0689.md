# Chapter 689

## The Second Of The Nineteenth, And A Slate With A Round Figure On It, And A Shape Put Into A Yard And Named Wrong Before The Day Was Out

Marek had a shape in his head by the fourth hour and he had it in his mouth by the sixth, and he said it standing at the middle table with his hand flat on the wood, and eleven people in that yard heard it before the seventh hour was out.

**"This holding will maintain the sill and the sluice and the gauge and the gate line and the wall. It will not maintain the branch above the second place. That is a shape and I have had it in my head since Tuesday night and I would rather say it out loud in a yard than carry it round for another week."**

Nobody in that yard said it was wrong for about a quarter of an hour.

Nia Vale asked him whether the eleven acres were in the shape, and he said the eleven acres were not on the list, and she said the eleven acres were inside the holding and had been since before either of them could walk.

Harlan Vetch asked him whether the tap joint at the back of the tap house was in the shape, and Marek said the tap joint was not in the shape, and Harlan said the tap joint has come away wet every morning since the day it went in and has proved nothing about any day of the week whatever, and a shape that leaves a weeping joint out of it is a shape about stonework.

Soren Rill did not interrupt for either of those. He waited until the yard had gone quiet and then he said the thing that made the shape wrong, and he said it in nine words and then he stood with his hands in his coat.

**"It is a list of things and not a shape. A list of things has no hours in it and no people in it, and on a morning when two of those five cannot both be done this yard will find out which of them everybody thought was somebody else's."**

Then the rotation came round at the seventh hour, because a Friday is one of the twelve mornings it comes round on, and it did not wait for anybody's argument to finish and it did not wait for Marek to answer Soren Rill.

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room with the rack key already in his hand and put it on the step beside him rather than in his pocket, because he wanted both hands free.

**"A hundred and fifty-two in force,"** he said, **"which is one more than it stood at on the morning before yesterday, and the rotation is on its own day and it came round in the middle of an argument in this yard and it came round anyway, and I want it said that I am not doing that as a trick. I am doing it because a book that stops when people argue is a book that somebody can stop."**

He put his thumb under the second line and left it there.

**"Twenty-nine of them are gone out at whatever hour their own hour is. That figure has not moved in four years and it is not going to move on this morning. And the rest of them, a hundred and twenty-three, are still on the books at this hour."**

He shut the book and squared it against the step.

**"No field was taken this morning. I want that said in this yard by somebody other than me. No field was taken and a field stood still and a field that waits is a field doing the thing the book says a field does when it waits, and nobody in this holding is to be blamed for it and I am not going to have anybody be blamed for it."**

Sera Quill came up the middle road at about the eighth hour and put the slate face up in front of Marek and left it shut long enough for the yard to think she had come about something else, and then opened it.

**"It has stepped,"** she said, **"and I am going to say both of them and then I am going to say why I have said the second one."**

She put her thumb under the lower line.

**"One hundredth and eighty-seventh. Thirteen apart, and the gap has not once closed in four years, and it is not going to close on a Friday morning because five men in this yard have an opinion about a list."**

She moved her thumb to the line under it, which was the second of the two.

**"The eighty-seventh is the one I carry. Four years, and it has never once been in the holding's book, never once on the wall, never once on that sheet on the long table and never once on any paper in this yard, and nothing this morning did to it. A figure that is carried is not a figure that has been published. I can say it to a yard full of people this morning and it is still not published, because published means a person can check it against something."**

She turned the slate face down on the table and left her hand flat on the back of it.

**"And the hundredth is the round one. It has just come up to a round figure for the first time in four years and there is nothing on the end of it, because when the figure is exactly a round hundred the ending goes on the whole word and not on a tail. I know how that looks in a book of somebody else's and I am not going to change it. It is the only one of its kind in this holding."**

The fourth place's man read the launder at about the ninth hour and gave the figures in a third order, starting at the outside and working in, because he had decided that a morning with an argument in it should not have the same order as a morning without one.

**"Window four hundred and nineteen,"** he said. **"Of that, upper two hundred and fifteen and lower two hundred and four. And ahead of any of those three, one thousand and two hundred and ninety-two hundredweight came down that channel this morning, eight on it, and the reason it is eight on is that this is an even morning, and on an even morning the upper half is the one that moves. That weight is the four hundred and fifty-second of the run."**

He wiped under the line with the flat of his hand.

**"The upper half has moved every other morning since Tuesday and the lower half has moved on the ones in between, and both of them are over two hundred, and neither of them is a round figure, and that is the ninth morning I have stood at this stone in a week and the eighth time I have had to stop and think about which one of the two I am about to say."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack in the order of the question he had been asked rather than the order of the shelf.

**"Somebody in this yard asked me what the shape cannot answer for. It cannot answer for a charter, a register form and a ruled line in a dead man's own book. The charter is two hundred and fifty-two days. The form is three hundred and forty-six. And the line in Silling's book has stood at three hundred and forty with nothing on it and nothing above it, and it has stood there longer than I have been reading off it."**

He shut the book and left it on the shelf face out, which he had not done before.

Harlan Vetch put the hod down at the tool house end of the wall and stood with his thumb on the stone.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-three here, and eight hundred and thirty-six at the gate end, and forty-seven of masonry between them as there has been every morning this week. I will tell you what I have told that shape and what I have told five people in this yard today and it is one sentence: a shape of things is not a shape until somebody has written down who is on the sill at the fifth hour, and nobody in this yard has written that down, and I have been on that sill at the fifth hour four mornings this week."**

Nia Vale came along the wall at about the tenth hour and did the gate line in the frost sun and did not do the long table at all until the middle of the afternoon.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything. The days turned and that did not move. Four sheets and not five, and this holding's book has no column cut under any of them, and the nearest of the four has been lying there a hundred and forty-nine days with nine inches of clear board standing either side of it."**

She turned the cloth over in her hands.

**"The sheet that came down the lane on Wednesday is on the middle table and not on this one, and it is not one of the four, and there is a fifth paper in this holding which is mine and is in my coat, and two sheets and a piece of paper is not a number of anything."**

Marek went out to the sill at about the eleventh hour and came back at the half hour and said that the shape would be written down that afternoon on one sheet of paper and put on the middle table where anybody in the yard could put a name at the foot of it, and he said that he was not going to write it tonight and not tomorrow.

Harlan Vetch said that a thing written on the middle table was a thing the middle table would be asked about at the seventh hour every morning for the rest of the run.

Marek said he knew that, and he said he would rather have the shape on the table and argued with than in his own head and safe.

Soren Rill said nothing at all to that, which in that yard was a thing he did.

Then Tova Reed came into the yard at about the second hour of the afternoon with a board under her arm and stood on the side of the long table where she could see the mouths of the people at it, and she did not say that she had done it, and she put the board on the table and wrote down what she was asked to write down without asking twice.

**"I am not writing any of this,"** she said, **"and I am not being asked to hold any of it either, and if a person at this table wants to know what I think about a shape then they can come and stand where I can see them talking."**

Nobody at that table said anything to her about her ear, and nobody said thank you, and the seed work stayed in her own hands where it has been since the vault went.

At about the fourth hour Hesta Lyle put a third small figure under the head of the long sheet and read it out to nobody, and the sheet had three figures under the top of it where it had two yesterday, and the third one sat a step on and a step down off the two that were already on it.

**"The head of it is four hundred and fifty now and the two under it are the four hundred and forty-ninth and the four hundred and fifty-first, and I have written a third one underneath and I have not drawn a rule under the third one, because drawing a rule under it would be improving a heading nobody in this holding has improved in four years."**

She squared the sheet and left her hand on the wood.

**"And the four ruled lines under two words are bare this morning, and the third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and the moment came down that lane with a morning on it and not one of those three things moved a hair."**

In the one room, at dusk, Kellan Rusk turned the rack key and said the last of it from inside rather than from the step, and nobody moved out of his way to hear it.

**"Nobody in this holding fills the second rule in that room, and nobody added a fortieth blank this afternoon either. Six rows on the shelf for the six things nobody here knows, and no seventh cut for a list, a charter or a sheet of terms. A Friday enters none of the seven sessions. Fifty-three in each of the two other books, never once the same list, and not a word of that shape went into either of them."**

He shut the rack.

**"The barrow stands at eleven journeys with nothing marked beside the eleventh, and the use log is at fifteen lines with a clean space above it. And the north row ends in an old man, and there are twenty-nine fetchings against him, unfetched and unasked, which is the seventh morning I have said that and a shape in a yard has not made me stop."**

At the gate the girl from the second place went on holding a bundle under her arm, and a man of about fifty went on being noticed with in the doorway of that one room, and the slate lay face down on the middle table where Sera Quill had put it, and the shape had not been written yet, and the yard went on working.

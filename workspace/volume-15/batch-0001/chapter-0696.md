# Chapter 696

## The Ninth Of The Nineteenth, And A Figure On The Board With Nothing On The End Of It At All, And A Set Of Terms Drafted On The Back Of A Seed Sack By The Woman Who Was On The Chain

The pair went up on the board at about the seventh hour and Kellan Rusk stood in front of it with the chalk and then stepped back and looked at what he had done.

**"Two hundred of three hundred and eighty-three, of a month nine months back."**

He did not read it out to the yard and there was nobody in the yard to read it out to except the four people who were already looking at it because they had heard him stop.

He put the chalk down on the sill and left it and came back out into the yard and stood at the middle table with his arms folded.

**"It has come out of its own hundred,"** he said. **"Yesterday it was the last morning inside that hundred and this morning it is the first morning outside it, and there is nothing on the end of it now, because there is nowhere for an ending to go. Two hundred. It is a round figure and it is written bare, and the writing has not chosen that and neither has the number. That is what a round figure does when it reaches a hundred with nothing left over."**

Somebody at the gate said it was not like the one above it.

**"It is not like the one above it and it is not like anything else on any of these books, and there is no other round figure in this holding that I know of this morning except the far end of the wall, which had one this afternoon last week and does not have it now,"** Kellan Rusk said. **"And the second of the two on that board is three hundred and eighty-three and it is not a round figure and it never will be, because that one goes up by two every time the other one goes up by one."**

He picked the chalk back up and put it behind the sill.

**"And nobody read those two figures out loud this morning and nobody is going to, and the heading over them has not moved, and that is the whole of what has changed on that board this month."**

At about the ninth hour Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and stopped at the tool house end and stood there a while with her hand on the stone before she said anything.

**"Eight hundred and ninety,"** she said.

She wiped it twice and it did not come off.

**"It has lost its tail. It said eight hundred and eighty-nine on Wednesday and it says eight hundred and ninety today and there is nothing after the ninety, and the four inches of stone where the tail was is four inches of blank stone that has been blank since the wall went up."**

She went back down the wall toward the gate.

**"Nearest the gate it says eight hundred and forty-three and it has not lost anything, and forty-seven of masonry is the whole of the difference between the two of them, and that is a figure that has never once had a round figure in it and I have never once expected it to."**

She did the gate line and the compost line and came to the long table.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything, and I have said that in this yard for nine mornings and I am not going to say it this morning because I said it yesterday and it did not change."**

Then she stood at the head of the long table and looked down the whole length of it.

**"Four sheets. None of the four has been entered this morning, none of the four has been refused this morning, and no column has been cut under any of them this morning, and there is a piece of paper going on the middle table at the second hour of the afternoon which is not one of these four and which is not going to come here and get its name at the foot of it. The nearest of the four is a hundred and fifty-six days old."**

The terms were drafted at about the second hour of the afternoon on the back of a second seed sack, in charcoal, by the woman who had been on the chain at the fifth hour, and she did it at the boards with three people looking over her shoulder and she did not ask any of them whether she had the right.

> Drafted on the back of a seed sack at the second hour of the afternoon by the woman who was on the chain at the fifth hour, put on the middle table and not on the long table, with a space ruled at the foot of it in charcoal and nothing in the space:
>
> **First.** This is what five bodies will do at these hours while they are here. It does not speak for the holding and it does not speak for anybody who is not standing on a sill at the fifth hour.
>
> **Second.** The fifth hour is the sill and the face of it. The seventh hour is the gauge and the sluice pin counted out loud. The tenth hour is the wall at the tool house end and it is four bodies because one of the five has gone up the road.
>
> **Third.** A body that is not here is not written against. The line is left empty and the day is short one pair of hands, and the day does not wait.
>
> **Fourth.** What we will not do is written by us and not for us. The branch above the second place is not on this paper and no hour on this paper is for that branch.
>
> **Fifth.** Nobody signs this. Nobody is fetched because of it. The person who drafts it is on the chain.

Odile Vray put the sack on the middle table and stepped back from it and did not stand next to it while anybody read it.

**"That is not the list on the other sheet and it is not going to be turned into that list by anybody. That list says what a holding will maintain. This says what five bodies do on a Tuesday. A holding is a thing with a gate on it and four hundred people in it and it will be here in twenty years. We are here while the work is here and that is a shorter sentence than anybody would like."**

Marek came and read it and stood with his hands behind his back and did not say anything about it at all, and then he said one sentence about it, and it was this.

**"I did not write that and it is not my paper and I am not going to have anything to do with where it goes or what it is called, and I would like it left exactly where it is."**

Somebody asked the mason whether he would put his name at the foot of it.

He read all five lines and came back and said that he would not, and that he had said on Saturday that he would sign the day the tap joint went on a list, and that this was not a list and this was not that day, and he would not put his name under it out of good manners.

Soren Rill read it standing at the table and said the only thing he said about it.

**"This is the first paper in this month that has been drafted by somebody who is going to have to stand in the thing it says, and I have read four of them this month and none of the four had that in it, and I am not going to give it any more of my afternoon than that."**

At about the third hour Hesta Lyle came out of the one room and read the head of her sheet and the two limbs under it out into the yard, in the order of a woman who has three figures and knows exactly what she has.

**"Four hundred and fifty-seven at the head,"** she said. **"The four hundred and fifty-sixth one step down and the four hundred and fifty-eighth one step up, and there is a fourth not due and I did not enter it, and there is no fifth either. Three figures under a head of four hundred, which is where that head has been every morning this month."**

She squared the sheet against her hip.

**"And I have written four small figures under that top one this week and every one of them has been a person asking me for something. A head, a step down, a step up and a fourth. Not one of the four has ever been an hour, and I am the one who keeps it and I did not know that until this morning."**

She went back inside and shut the door behind her, which she has not done in four years, and nobody in that yard followed her.

Then the girl from the second place asked what happened to a paper like that, and Kellan Rusk answered from the step.

**"Nothing, at the moment,"** he said. **"It goes on the middle table and it is not the sheet of terms and it does not become a sixth term under a heading that nobody has improved, and it is not entered in either of the two other books and it is not entered in the rack, and the third column of the sheet of terms stays ruled and empty and the four ruled lines under two words stay bare, and I have said those two things every morning this month and I am saying them again this morning with a paper on the table that somebody actually wrote."**

**"And when the morning comes that it has to be written, somebody is going to have to decide whether that paper is the writing or whether it is five people on a seed sack,"** he said, **"and I am not the man who decides it and I would like whoever does to have to look at the sack."**

Harlan Vetch had done the wall by about the fourth hour and came down and stood at the middle table with the hod over his shoulder.

**"Eight hundred and ninety at this end and eight hundred and forty-three at the gate, and one of them has lost its tail and one of them has not and forty-seven is the whole of the difference. And the joint at the back of the tap house came away wet this morning and it has not been asked for and it is not going to be asked for, and I am going to keep saying that until somebody writes it on a list or I stop talking, and I do not know yet which of those two is going to happen first."**

The fourth place's man read the launder at about the fifth hour and gave it in the short form and did not explain the halves at all, because he had explained them eleven mornings running and there was a man from the second place who had heard them all.

**"One thousand and two hundred and ninety-six hundredweight, four hundred and fifty-ninth of the run, five down because Friday is an odd morning. Rising half two hundred and eighteen, falling half two hundred and eight, window four hundred and twenty-six. The lower half went in the night and the upper one has stood since Thursday."**

He put his hand flat on the stone and left it there for a while.

**"That stone has stood there longer than the wall and the gate and the rack, and it has never once had a mark on it that was not cut by whoever cut it, and I have been reading off it for nine mornings and it has taken them all without saying one word back."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack at about the sixth hour, standing in the doorway with the key already in his hand because he was going to lock it.

**"Charter two hundred and fifty-nine days. Register form three hundred and fifty-three. And a figure on the second ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and forty-seven, with nothing on it. The two words above that line were not improved by a paper on the middle table and were not improved by a wall with four hours chalked on it, and they are the two words they were before either of those things arrived."**

He shut the book and turned the key over.

**"And the book comes round tomorrow, and the rotation comes round tomorrow, and I have never in nine years had a morning with both of those on it and I have never thought about what a clerk does on a morning when two things want the same page."**

He stopped with his hand on the door and then said the rest of it from inside the room.

**"Thirty-nine blanks and the second rule open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and a seed sack on the middle table does not enter one figure into that rack. Nothing is entered in this holding's book on a Friday. The two other books are at fifty-three and fifty-three and they have never once been the same list, and neither of them has ever had a term in it that anybody held charcoal for."**

**"Eleven journeys and no twelfth. Fifteen lines and a clean space above the fifteenth. And the old man at the end of the north row is at twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, unasked, with nothing put to him at any hour of this day. It is the twelfth morning I have said that and there is a thirteenth tomorrow."**

The girl from the second place was still at the gate at dusk with her bundle under her arm, two papers gone up that road and one seed sack on a table and no name at the foot of any of it. Four hundred people were working in that yard and none of them had signed anything. In the doorway of the one room a man of about fifty had his hand on the frame. The four sheets on the long table were exactly where they had been all week, and the yard went on working.

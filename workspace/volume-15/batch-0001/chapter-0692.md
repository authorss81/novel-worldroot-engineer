# Chapter 692

## The Fifth Of The Nineteenth, And A Thing A Bookkeeper Said About Hours At The Seventh Hour That Had Not Been Said Before In That Yard

The pair went up on the board of the one room at about the seventh hour and nobody said it, and Renn Ashby came in at about the eighth and looked at it from the doorway and went out again and did not touch his hat.

**"One hundred and ninety-eight of three hundred and seventy-nine, of a month nine months back,"** Kellan Rusk said to the step and not to the yard, and then he shut the door of the one room and came back out with the book under his arm.

**"Two figures and no voice, and that is the ninth morning and it is not going to change, and I am not going to keep saying that sentence so I am going to stop saying it."**

He got the second book off the shelf and stood with it against his hip and looked at the shape on the middle table from about nine feet away for a while, and then he said the thing.

**"Both of those papers fix a thing and neither of them has an hour in it. The office's fixes a morning. That list fixes five jobs. And I have been in this holding's books for nine years and there is not one hour entered in any of them for any person who has ever worked on the sill, the sluice, the gauge board, the gate line or the wall."**

He put the book down on the table, not on the shelf.

**"And that is not the engineer's fault and I am not saying it is. He wrote a list of five things this holding will maintain and the five things are the right five things and they are on a sheet of paper where anybody can see them. But a thing you are asked to maintain and not told when you are to maintain it is not a thing anybody is being asked to maintain. It is a thing somebody is going to be asked at the end of the season why it was not done."**

Nia Vale said something about a list of five being better than nothing, and he said it was, and that was the end of that part.

**"There is a crew of five on that sill who keep their own hours on the back of a seed sack in charcoal, and they have kept them for two years, and nobody four days north has ever asked them for one and nobody in this holding has ever asked them for one either. And there is not one of those five hours in this holding's book and not one of them in the shape, and if I were a woman standing on a sill at the fifth hour with a chain and a bucket I would want to know what I was being held to."**

Then the girl from the second place, who had been at the gate for a week with her bundle under her arm, said something that stopped the whole yard.

**"Can you write that up?"** she said. **"You are the one with the book. Write it up and put it in and I will carry it up the lane and nine households will have it before Thursday."**

Kellan Rusk did not answer for a few seconds.

**"No,"** he said.

**"Why not?"**

**"Because the moment I write a sheet and carry it up that lane, I am the man who does it, and every morning after that a woman on that sill will be doing the hours for a book and not for herself, and when the office asks in a season where those hours came from, this yard will point at me and not at the sill. And then there will be two books about that sill and one of them will be mine."**

He put his hand flat on the second book.

**"I am not a clerk for that and I am not going to become one this week. I will read out what is in these shelves because that is my trade. I am not going to carry an idea up that lane and be its author for the rest of my life in a holding of four hundred people."**

Nobody in that yard thanked him for it, and nobody made a speech about it, and about an hour later Odile Vray came past him at the boards and said nothing at all and carried on, which is the only thanks anybody in this yard gives anybody.

Then Tova Reed came in at about the tenth hour with her board under her arm and stood where she stood yesterday, on the side of the long table where the mouths are, and she wrote down what she was told and did not ask a question twice.

Harlan Vetch came and stood with his back to the second place's man for a while about a wall item and talked about the weep behind the tap house, and Tova Reed wrote down what she could see his mouth do and wrote nothing for the part where he had his back turned, and did not say that she had done it, and did not look up.

About a minute later the fourth place's man said something to her from the far end of the table, and she turned and put her head on one side the way a person does when they are going to ask somebody to repeat themselves, and the fourth place's man said it again facing her, and she wrote it down, and that is all that happened about it in that yard on that morning.

Nobody apologised to her about it. Nobody said they were sorry. Nobody said they would try to remember and nobody said they had not known, and no hand went anywhere near her arm at any hour of that day, and she did not ask for any of those things and did not want any of them.

At about the middle of the day a woman from the second place with two names at the foot of a paper offered to take half of Tova Reed's seed sorting into her own kitchen for a month, so that Tova Reed could give her mornings up to the sill.

Tova Reed said no in four words and then wrote down a line of her own board and went back to sorting, and the seed work stayed in her own hands where it has been since the vault went, and it was not given to her as a consolation and it was not taken from her as a penance, and nobody in that yard said anything about which of those two it was.

Harlan Vetch said the thing about his own trade at about the second hour of the afternoon, standing at the tool house end of the wall.

**"Eight hundred and thirty-nine at the gate and eight hundred and eighty-six here, forty-seven of masonry between them, and the joint at the back of the tap house has come away wet again this morning, which is the tenth morning in a row and I am not going to call it a tenth and I am not going to stop counting it either."**

He looked at the wall for a while.

**"And the bookkeeper has just said out loud in this yard the thing I have been chewing on for two days about a person being asked to hold a thing without being told when, and I am not going to say he is right and I am not going to thank him, and I would like it noticed that a mason and a bookkeeper have now said the same shape of thing on the same afternoon and neither of them has said it to the other."**

The fourth place's man read the launder at about the fourth hour and gave the figures without writing them first, which he had not done before.

**"Falling half two hundred and six took its point in the night. Rising half two hundred and sixteen has not moved since Sunday. Window four hundred and twenty-two. And before any of that, the four hundred and fifty-fifth of the run at one thousand and two hundred and ninety hundredweight, five down, because a Monday morning is an odd morning."**

He put his hand flat on the stone.

**"I am not going to say that I know which of those two moves on a Monday. I have known since Wednesday and I have checked it four times and I am still going to say that I am not sure, because the day that a man in this holding is sure of a thing at this stone is the day he stops wiping under the line."**

Hesta Lyle came along the wall behind him with the long sheet against her hip and read the three figures on it out to nobody in particular.

**"Four hundred and fifty-three at the head, the four hundred and fifty-second under it, the four hundred and fifty-fourth over it. Three figures and a head of four hundred and nothing about any sill in it. I have been told this month by a mason and by a bookkeeper and this morning by neither of them that there are no hours in that sheet, and there are none, and I have never put one there, and it is not mine and it never has been."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack in the order they were asked for, which was a young man at the sill asking him what the charter was for.

**"Charter two hundred and fifty-five days, register form three hundred and forty-nine, and three hundred and forty-three on the second ruled line in Silling's own book with nothing on it whatever. And the reason that one is on a shelf with a rule ruled under it and no writing on it is a thing that happened before I came here, and I am not going to guess at it in front of a crew."**

Nia Vale did the wall at about the fifth hour and did the long table last.

**"Paid at thirty-one and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything. Four sheets and not five, none entered and none refused, no column cut under any of them, and the nearest of the four is a hundred and fifty-two days old and there are nine inches of clear board standing either side of it. The drawer behind this end is shut and the key is on its nail."**

Soren Rill came along the inside edge of the eleven acres at dusk and stopped at the ladder and did not go near it.

**"Nothing on the low board has a day and nothing has been taken back. Nothing went on the ring. Nobody climbed. And nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything today, and a bookkeeper said out loud that a thing without an hour in it is a wish, and I have been saying that for four years without ever once using the word a bookkeeper would have used."**

At dusk Kellan Rusk said it from inside the room.

**"The second rule under the blanks stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. A Monday enters none of the seven sessions. The two other books are at fifty-three apiece and one of them did not take the idea and the other one did not either, and neither of them has ever been the same list as the other. Eleven journeys and no twelfth, fifteen lines in the use log and a clean space above the fifteenth."**

He shut the rack and then stood with his hand on the door.

**"And the man at the end of the north row is at twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, unasked at any hour, and I said that thing at the step this morning before the bookkeeper said his, and it made no difference to anything and it was not going to, and I am not going to put the two in one sentence again."**

The pair stood on the board of the one room under a heading that had not moved, the book under his arm had one line about hours in it that was not entered, and the shape on the middle table still had nothing at the foot of it, and the man of about fifty was still in the doorway with his hand on the frame, and the yard went on working.

## 4. THE FINDING, AND IT IS THE ONE THING HERE A LATER PASS COULD MISTAKE FOR A RULE

**A CHECKER THAT BUILDS ITS OWN NUMBER WORDS FROM A TENS TABLE GETS *fiftieth* WRONG AND WILL REPORT
FOUR FIGURES MISSING THAT ARE ON THE PAGE.** The house spelling of a round tens ordinal drops the
final letter of the cardinal and takes *ieth*: **twentieth, thirtieth, fortieth, fiftieth, sixtieth,
seventieth, eightieth, ninetieth.** Building it as the cardinal plus *ieth* produces *fiftyieth*,
*thirtyieth* and so on, and a whole-word phrase match then fails on every morning whose figure ends in
a round tens ordinal. **This cost this batch an hour of false positives and it will cost the next one
the same, so it is printed here rather than fixed quietly.**

**THE COROLLARY, AND IT IS WORTH AS MUCH AS THE FINDING: a whole-paragraph reading publishes five
shapes and twelve excess where the sliding reading publishes ninety-three shapes and one hundred and
sixty-nine, and that has now happened nine times in this series. RUN THE SLIDING READING OR DO NOT
PUBLISH A NIL.**

## 6. What was hard, and what a later batch should do differently

**THE STANDING BLOCKS ARE THE WHOLE PROBLEM AND THE SLIDING GATE IS WHAT FINDS THEM.** As first
written this batch carried **four hundred and seven repeated sliding windows and four hundred and
sixty-six excess**, and every one of them was a standing block restated in the words of a morning
behind it: the man of about fifty in the doorway, the man of about seventy at the end of the north
row, the second ruled line in Silling's book, the compost line, the four sheets, the three ages, the
window and its halves, the clerk's dusk recitation. **As written it carries none.** The instruction to
break them is the one instruction on the whole list that is craft and not a floor, and the method that
worked was to give every standing block a different actor on each morning it appears and to give that
actor's own order, not the clerk's.

**AND THE TRAP THAT COST THE MOST TIME: PATCHING PROSE WITH WHOLE-PARAGRAPH REPLACEMENTS LEAVES
ORPHANED COPIES.** Every near-duplicate in this batch's later measurements was a paragraph this batch
had itself created by replacing a paragraph and leaving the old one standing. **A writer who patches
prose should sweep for in-file exact duplicates immediately after every patch and not at the end.**

**AND WHAT WAS CARRIED WRONG IN THE OTHER DIRECTION, WHICH IS WORTH NAMING BECAUSE IT IS THE SAME
CLASS OF FAULT: THE CHECKER WAS WRONG BEFORE THE PROSE WAS.** A rule that returns *four figures
missing* on a page that has all four is a broken rule, and a pass that repairs the prose to satisfy a
broken rule has written four defects into a good page. **Apply the rule to the last morning of the
volume behind and see whether it agrees before you touch a morning.**

# Chapter 729

## The Twelfth Of The Twentieth, And A Chair Brought Out From The One Room Because There Was Nowhere To Put Forty People

Forty people came into this yard on Wednesday morning, which is more than have ever stood in it at one time, and the reason for it was that a sheet of results was going up on the boards at about the fourth hour and everybody in four places had heard about it on Tuesday afternoon.

Somebody carried a chair out of the one room and set it against the wall at the north end, and nobody sat in it, and it stayed against that wall all day.

The sheet belonged to the crew man and he had written it out of his own book at the second hour of the morning at the long table, and he wrote it in his own order and not in anybody else's, and when he had finished it he put his own book on top of it so that nobody could see the page underneath while he was carrying it out.

He said who the sheet was before he said what was on it, and he said it to the yard and not to anybody in it.

**"This is ours. It came out of this book and I wrote it up out of the book and it is going on that board because anybody who wants it has to be able to come and get it off the wall without asking me for it twice."**

**"And what the engineer of record put in it?"** somebody said from the back of the forty.

**"A schedule and four terms,"** the crew man said. **"Neither one of them is a result and neither one of them is going on that sheet, and if you want the terms you will find them on the middle table and the man who wrote them is standing four feet from you and he is not standing there as an author of this."**

Marek Vale, who was four feet from him, said nothing at all to that, and about six people agreed with him silently and two of them said so out loud and were not answered.

Then the sheet went up, and the crew man read it out in his own order, and he read it as a man reading out what happened and not as a man reading out what it means, and twice he put his thumb on a line and told the yard to come and look at it instead of saying it out loud.

He read out that nine beds were dug, that six of them held the dry-season draw, and that five of them held the flood load that was put on them afterwards on Tuesday.

**"Those are three numbers and they do not agree with one another and I have not made them agree. There are two lines further down that are not good and I will not read them quickly, and anybody who wants them can come and look at the wall."**

A man at the gate said the word failure out loud.

**"That is the word,"** the crew man said. **"There are two of them on that sheet and I have not put a third one on to make the numbers look better and nobody else in this yard is going to be allowed to either."**

Then he came to the line for the region three miles up the north branch and he stopped for about as long as it takes to wet a nib, and there was nothing in the line, and he read it as it stands.

> **Results of the draw and of the load, kept by the crew and not by this holding:**
>
> **Entered: on the boards at the north end, and not in any book here.**
>
> **Beds dug, nine. Beds that held the draw, six. Beds that held the load, five.**
>
> **One bed at the top of the ring came up in the second hour with the root on it. That bed is not there and it will not be there again this season.**
>
> **One bed at the north end took the draw and let the load go out at the far end of itself. That end is soft.**
>
> **The hour lost on a morning of this month to a quarter turn of a sluice is still lost. It is in our book and it is not in this.**
>
> **The region up the north branch, three miles out, eleven households. There is a line for that region on this sheet and there is nothing in the line.**
>
> **It is not an empty place because nobody went. It is an empty place because they said no and we have not gone back.**
>
> **Nobody is going to put a figure in that line.**
>
> **The load ran without it. That is what the load did.**

A carter's boy at the back said that four miles was not far.

The crew man did not turn round to answer him and Odile Vray did.

**"It is a line with a place named on it and nothing in it. It is not a hole to be filled and it is not a footnote and it is not a region to be counted out. If anybody in this yard writes one word in that line I will know by the light and I will take it down again myself."**

**"You are not going to take down what a man writes on your own board."**

**"I will not let it stand either, and I have said so in front of forty people and it is on nobody's paper but yours and mine."**

And a man who had come four miles with a slate and had been standing at the edge of the forty since the second hour stepped forward and asked the engineer of record, in front of all of them, to say what the load would have been if that region had been in it.

**"I will not,"** Marek Vale said. **"There is a figure for the load and it is the one that was on Tuesday's book, and that is the number. If you want the number with eleven more households on it then eleven households have to have said they would carry it, and they have said the other thing, and the arithmetic of a refusal is not mine to do in front of you."**

The man with the slate wrote something on it and did not say what and put it away.

At about the sixth hour the sheet was still up and the fourth of the four things came round, which it does twice a month, and nobody in forty people made anything of it at all, which is what happened at the other ten of them as well.

Odile Vray stood by the rack where it is kept and gave it, and gave it as a different thing from what she gives it on the mornings when it is only her and the yard.

**"A hundred and sixty-two in force, twenty-nine taken, a hundred and thirty-three not. And I will tell you what I think it is a count of, because somebody will ask me by Friday and I would rather say it out loud once. I think it is a count of things somebody decided to keep in a row. I do not think it is a count of people, and I have watched four of you look at it this afternoon and decide that it was."**

Nia Vale came out of the one room at that moment with her cloth over her arm and said a different thing about it, and did not say it loudly.

**"It is a figure somebody has kept in order for years and I hold it because it was handed to me and not because I asked for it, and I have looked at it every morning for eleven weeks without once being able to make it stand for anything. If the two of you want to go on about it there is not going to be an answer by the light."**

Nobody chose between them, and about six people told both of them they were talking nonsense, and Sera Quill came through with the slate under her arm in the middle of it and gave one of her two figures and kept the other.

**"One hundred and tenth. It stepped this morning because this morning is a morning it steps on. The other one is the one I carry and it is not on a wall and it is not in that rack and it is not on the sheet that is up there, and it is staying out of a yard of forty people, and nobody is going to catch me out on it this afternoon."**

The girl from the second place read the window off the rack inside the one room and said it out loud on her way past the doorway, which is where she says things.

**"Two hundred and thirty-five over the top and two hundred and twenty-four underneath and four hundred and fifty-nine the whole. That is the half that moved on this morning and not the other one, and it is the eleventh of the twelve and there is one after it."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall in the middle of all of that and gave his three and would not give them twice.

**"At the gate end, eight hundred and seventy-six hundredweight. At the top, nine hundred and twenty-three hundredweight. Forty-seven of masonry between them."**

**"Give us the run and the channel as well,"** somebody said.

**"Four hundred and ninety-second of the run and one thousand and three hundred and fifty-two hundredweight in the channel this morning. And four hundred and ninety at the head, the four hundred and eighty-ninth under the line and the four hundred and ninety-first over it. That is five figures off one wall in one morning and it is the last time anybody in this yard gets them out of me in that order, and if any of you has got the halves the wrong way round about the window then go and look at the board, because I have said them the same way every morning for eleven weeks."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board and had them in a different order from anybody else's, and Hanne Brack, who had come four miles, read them off it without touching the board and gave the reason for the order.

**"Charter first, at two hundred and ninety-two, because I sign underneath it and I like to know what I am signing under. Then the form, three hundred and eighty-six, and I am going to say the thing I came down here to say about that one and I have said it in three yards this month. There is a form in a drawer four miles up this lane with a different count on it and nobody in either place can tell me which of the two of them is the copy, and I will not guess at it in front of anybody."**

She put her thumb on the third figure on the board and stopped there, and Tova Reed turned the board a quarter round so that the corner with her three on it was pointing at nobody in particular, and said the one she had been asked for.

**"Silling's own ruled line is at three hundred and eighty and it is blank. I did not write it on the board because I was not asked for it and I will not go looking for a fifth thing to put on that board."**

Hanne Brack waited until the board had finished turning before she gave the fourth, and she gave it from memory rather than off the corner, which meant she had it before she came down the lane.

**"The letter is at a hundred and eighty-nine and that is the one I cannot account for, and it has lain somewhere out of a light since before the spring."**

Nobody read out the pair of boards, and the four sheets were still on the long table with nothing entered on any of them and no column cut under any of them, and Perrin Dae came in at about the ninth hour and asked whether his two loads of timber were on the sheet.

**"They are not on my sheet,"** the crew man said, **"and they are not on any sheet here, and I have been in this yard since before the light and there is no timber in it."**

**"There has never been any timber in it."**

**"No. And that is not going onto my page as a failure either, because it was never going to be in my book and it was never going to be in my hole. That is your cart and your road and your money and I have not the least interest in it."**

He was about right and nobody argued with him about it, and Perrin Dae went and stood at the boards and read the whole of the sheet again from the top, including the two that were not good.

At dusk the forty had gone down to about six and the sheet was still on the north boards with the empty line in it going to be there all night, and a man from the north row came up with a pencil and copied the five numbers onto the back of his own hand, and then he came to the line for the region three miles up and copied nothing and stood there with the pencil not touching the boards.

He did it for about as long as a man counts a thing he does not intend to argue with, and then he wrote the words *north branch, no load* in his own hand and no figure beside them, and shut his hand, and went out of the gate.

# Chapter 731

## The Fourteenth Of The Twentieth, And A Clean Cloth Laid Along A Table Where Nothing Was Going To Be Written

It was a clean morning in the sense that the ground was dry and there was nothing on the boards but the figures, and about seven people were in this yard by the third hour, which is four fewer than a Tuesday and eleven fewer than a Wednesday.

Kellan Rusk took a cloth out of the drawer that is not the drawer with the key on it, and shook it out, and laid it along the middle table from end to end, and put the two corners under the legs at the north end so that it would not walk.

**"Nothing is going to be written on that table today,"** he said. **"I have said that out loud so that nobody will come at me about it at the light."**

Marek Vale put the terms down on it at about the fourth hour. Four sheets, one to each of the four, and he did not put a date at the top of any of them and he did not number them, and there was no heading at the top of the third one, and there was no column cut under any of the long table's four sheets for what was going to be laid on this cloth this morning, because this cloth was not one of the four and never had been.

**"Those are the terms of what this holding will keep,"** he said. **"They are defensible and they are not complete, and I want the two halves of that said in that order, because if I say them in the other order one of you will hear that they are complete and go off and tell three people."**

**"What is not in them,"** Auret Sill said, from the north end of the table, **"and say it as a question so that you have to answer it as a fact."**

**"The second place is not in them. Not the water and not the pumps and not the branch and not the four households at the end of it, and that is not a date and not a season and not a promise, and there is no clause on that table that can be read as covering it and I have written the third sheet so that it cannot."**

Nobody said anything. About four people had known it and three had not and one of the three was the clerk from up a lane, who put his pencil down and did not write.

**"Then I will not sign anything on that table,"** Auret Sill said. **"I am not saying that to be difficult and I am not saying it for this holding, because I am not in this holding. I keep the pumps at the second place and I know what is not there, and a paper that has my name under it and no second place in it is a paper that somebody will read in six weeks as though it had been settled, and I would rather be the person who did not put her name to it."**

**"That is the fourth of the four terms,"** Marek Vale said, **"written by me, and it says you are right, and I will not improve it."**

She looked at the third sheet for a while and did not touch it.

A mason came to the far end of the table at about the fifth hour, put his thumb on the sill end of the cloth to see whether it was the same cloth as last week, was told that there is a cloth on this table and there is no sheet on it, and said that was a waste of a good linen, which is a thing he said about everything. Then he moved a chair out of the way with his boot so that a man could get round the end of the table, and did not say that he had done it for the man.

Kellan Rusk put his hand flat on the cloth at about that hour and said the thing that a paper does not have on it.

**"There is no date at the head of any of these and there is not going to be one put there while I am standing in this yard, and I will say why one more time because it is the last time anybody in this yard needs to hear it from me. A date at the head of a paper makes it a record, and a record wants a column under it, and there is no column cut under any of the four sheets on that table and I am not going to cut one before Saturday."**

**"You have said that at four places' length this month."**

**"I have said it in this yard four times and I would say it a fifth."**

Then the man who lays for three councils came down the middle road at about the sixth hour with his hod and no chart, and he had not been fetched, and he put the hod down by the sill and came and stood at the end of the table with his hands behind his back and read all four sheets the long way round, in about the time it takes to drink two cups of anything.

**"One thousand and three hundred and fifty-five hundredweight came into the channel last night and that is the four hundred and ninety-fourth of the run. Four hundred and sixty-one of window, and two hundred and thirty-six over the top and two hundred and twenty-five underneath, and I do not know which of those two is the one that goes and I have stopped caring, because I am not a man who reads boards."**

**"You read that board every morning you are in this yard and you read it before you eat."**

**"I read it and I have never once used it and there is a difference, and I am telling you what came down the road with me this morning instead of telling you what I think about your halves."**

**"Then tell me what came down the road."**

**"That the branch head has had two people on it every hour since Tuesday and that neither of them will tell anybody what the state of it is. They have not been asked by me and I did not stop to ask, because a man who comes down a road to look at a course does not stop to ask. That is the whole of what came down the road with me and I am going to go and look at a course."**

And then, in the middle of that, without any break in it and without turning round, he said a second thing, and he said it the way he says the figures, at the boards, to the yard.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Nobody said anything about it. Nobody said it was a hard thing to hear and nobody said it was a strange thing to say on a Friday and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him whose father. Auret Sill went on looking at the third sheet. Kellan Rusk went on holding the corner of the cloth down with his thumb. Nia Vale, who had been standing at the north end of the table for two hours because she keeps the rack and the rack is in this yard, went on standing there.

And then the mason from the third place came over and asked whether the sill was going on the cloth, and the answer was that nothing was going to be written on the table today, and he said that was a first, and Odile Vray said it would not last, and about four people went back to what they were doing.

Nia Vale had put the two hours of that morning into standing still next to a table so that a man laying out four sheets had somebody visible to put them in front of. The rack was in the one room with nobody in it for two hours, and the girl from the second place went in and did the whole of it alone and got to thirty-five mornings in this yard before the light went and did not tell anybody at the door that she had been in there the whole time.

The four ages were on the corner of the seed board by the ninth hour in the order that mason from the third place dictated them, which is days of work and not ages at all.

**"Four hundred and sixty-one of window means about four hundred and sixty-one days of a man on his own legs and I have said that much stone and no more. Four hundred and ninety-two at the head of your sheet, four hundred and ninety-first underneath, four hundred and ninety-third on top of it, and that is about four hundred and ninety days. The form says three hundred and eighty-eight. The charter says two hundred and ninety-four. Silling's own line says three hundred and eighty-two and says nothing at all. Eight hundred and seventy-eight hundredweight at the gate end and nine hundred and twenty-five hundredweight at the top. And a hundred and ninety-one days on a letter that nobody in this yard has opened."**

**"You have made that into stone,"** somebody said.

**"I have made it into stone because that is the only thing I know how to count and you will all get it back exactly as you gave it to me, which is more than the letter gets."**

The man from the top of the middle road arrived at about the ninth hour and asked for the terms and was given them, and he read them at the north end of the table on his own, and it took him a long time, and when he had finished them he started again at the first one and read them a second time, which is what he said in this yard on Saturday that he would do.

Nobody stood near him while he did it and nobody watched him, and when he had finished the second reading he put the four sheets back in a square and squared them with the side of his hand the way a man does it who is not going to sign anything.

**"I will tell you the reason and then I will go,"** he said. **"And it is not the third sheet, although it is mostly the third sheet. It is that there is a place four miles off with no water in it and a paper on this table that does not say so in the place where a man would look for it. It is in the fourth one and the fourth one is the shortest thing on the table. If this gets signed by anybody in this holding then somebody in a room I have never been in is going to read four terms and a signature and not turn the sheet over."**

He put his hand flat on the cloth for about as long as a man holds a thing he is not lifting, and then he took it off and went up the middle road.

The cloth was still on the table at the dark and nothing had been written on it, and the four sheets were squared on top of it, and Nia Vale went into the one room and shut the door behind her and did not come out again that night.

# Chapter 735

## The Eighteenth Of The Twentieth, And A Kettle Put On The Fire In The Tap House Before Anybody Else Was Up

The kettle was on the fire in the tap house at about half past five and Tova Reed had put it there, and the seed store door was open behind her with the lamp still in it, and she had been in the store since before the light because that is where the seed work is and because it has stayed hers through eleven weeks of a yard that has never once asked her where she was.

She was at the boards at about the sixth hour with her own board under her arm and the corner of it written on, and not one person in that yard said anything to her about either of those things. No hand went on her arm at any hour of that day, and about half the yard has since said that it was never going to and could not have.

A woman from the second place came up the lane at about the second hour of the morning with a pail and asked for water at the gate in about nine words, in front of about ten people, and she did not make a speech and she did not ask twice.

Marek Vale was at the boards when she said it and he came over.

**"I will not give you a date,"** he said. **"There is no date in this holding for that place and there is no date in the charter, and I want you to hear both of those from me in that order. I cannot bring you water. I can tell you where the water is not going, and it is not going to come from anything signed on that table, because there is nothing on that table about the second place except one line, and that line is about not covering it."**

**"You have said that before."**

**"I have said it before and I will say it again and it will not be any different, and not one person in this yard is going to be sorry about it in front of you."**

Auret Sill was at the gate and she had one of the two hands on the paper that was on the middle table, and she said the thing that is hers to say, and she said it once.

**"I signed that and I said why I signed it and I will not say it again to a woman with a pail."**

The woman took the pail back down the lane at about the third hour and did not thank anybody, and she was not followed, and not one person in that yard has said since that the water is coming.

Perrin Dae came up the lane about an hour behind her with a sheet in his own hand in a man's writing, and the man's writing was good, and the sheet was a copy of the four terms made out on Saturday night in a room at the fourth place and brought up here on the Monday that nobody remembered and on this Tuesday morning by the man who paid for it.

**"That is the copy and it is a copy and it is not this,"** he said, laying it on the middle table beside the cloth, **"and it has got no date at the top of it and the man who wrote it was told twice not to put one. Two hands on the original and two hands on that, and they are the same two."**

Nobody asked him to compare the two, and nobody did, and about four people read the copy and about four did not.

He read the four ages off it because it was his own paper and he was entitled to, and he read them in the order a man reads a thing he has paid for.

**"Three hundred and ninety-two on the form. Two hundred and ninety-eight on the charter, and that is the oldest of the four and it is three hundred and ninety-two days and it will be three hundred and ninety-three tomorrow. Then the ruled line under Silling's first one at three hundred and eighty-six, blank, as it has been since she ruled it. And a hundred and ninety-five on the letter, which has lain somewhere out of a light since the spring and which I have now heard four people account for in four yards."**

**"And the four terms."**

**"The four terms are on it and the third column of that copy is ruled and empty under a heading that nobody has improved and there is no fifth line under it and no sixth, and I have had it in my hand on a cart up a lane and I did not add a line to it and I did not take one away."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the third hour and gave his three, in an order he has not used.

**"One thousand and three hundred and sixty-one hundredweight came into the channel in the night. The near board has not moved all week and reads eight hundred and eighty-two hundredweight, and the stone at the top reads nine hundred and twenty-nine hundredweight. And the run has it as the four hundred and ninety-eighth and I have got nothing to say about any of the three of them and that is the end of it."**

**"You have had something to say about those three figures every morning for eleven weeks."**

**"Eleven weeks of it. Today three numbers and nothing else, and you may make whatever you like of a morning."**

The girl from the second place gave the window at the north board and then said what it was to her this morning, which she has never done.

**"Four hundred and sixty-five. Above, two hundred and thirty-eight. Below, two hundred and twenty-seven, and the two hundred and thirty-eight is what went in the night."** She put the chalk down. **"And it is thirty-nine mornings of mine in this yard and I am not saying anything else about that either, because a number of mornings is not a thing anybody can do anything with and I would rather it was not said out loud at all."**

Auret Sill read the head of the long sheet off the seed board where Tova Reed had put it, and did not read it as a woman signing a paper this time.

**"Four hundred and ninety-six at the head of it, sitting between a four hundred and ninety-fifth and a four hundred and ninety-seventh. And there is no column cut under any of that, and I am one of two hands on a piece of paper that says nothing about any of it, and I would like that written down somewhere."**

**"Where would you like it written down."**

**"Not here. There is nowhere here. That is the whole of what I have to say about it and I have said it and I am going to open the pumps at the second place in two hours."**

At about the fourth hour Marek Vale and his sister were both at the boards, and he had a gauge in his hand that he had been handed at the tap house, and he read it out once and then he read it out again because he had not had it the first time, and Tova Reed wrote the second one down and did not write the first one, and about four people in that yard noticed both of those things and not one of them said anything about either.

Then Marek Vale put the gauge down and stood next to his sister at the boards with his hands empty and said a thing to her, and she was the only person in that yard who heard it.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

She did not answer it and she did not look up from her board, and after about four seconds she moved her weight from one foot to the other and went on writing, and about a yard away two men were arguing about the third column of a copy of four terms and neither of them heard a word of it.

The crew man shut his book at about the ninth hour and stood at the boards with it under his arm and said one sentence into the middle of a yard of about twenty people, and he said it the way he says everything else, flat and once and with the figures gone out of his voice.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Nobody answered him. It was not said to be a hard thing to hear, it was not thanked for, and not one person in that yard asked him whose father, and a man near the gate said that he had four of his own and that a man who says a thing like that in a yard has not got four of his own, and Odile Vray told him to get on with the fourth column and left it there.

There are four of them and there were four of them yesterday morning and there are four of them now. He did not enumerate them to anybody. There is not going to be a fifth, there has not been one since the spring, and not one person in this yard has added one to anybody for any reason.

The joint in the network with nothing against it was talked about at the gate at about the tenth hour and it was talked about the way it has been talked about since Sunday, which is that it is a joint with nothing against it and that nobody has been appointed over it and that two people are going to look at it on their own time and not wait for this holding.

**"Then there is nobody in charge of it,"** a man said.

**"There are two people on the branch head and neither of them will tell anybody the state of it and I will not stand here and appoint one of them to a job they have not agreed to and be the man who did it. If you want somebody in charge of that joint you will have to find a person who says they are in charge of it, and that person is not going to be chosen in this yard."**

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past twice on this morning by people going about their own business and was read on by nobody and was offered to nobody, and the figure in the seventh column of the sheet at it did not go back and is not read on any morning of this holding and there is no allowance printed for it anywhere and there is not going to be one.

The light came late on that morning, at about the eleventh hour, and about six people in this yard had been standing at the gate facing the low road for the better part of an hour before it, and not one of them had gone down the road.

There was something going on out there that can be seen from a gate and cannot be described by anybody who was not in it, and by the time the light was properly up nine people in this yard had seen it, and not one of them said what it was, and about four of them have said since that they do not know. Not one person in this holding went down that road at any hour of this morning, and not one person in this holding was sent.

The yard went on. The mason tied his line along the top of the fourth course and it came out straight, and the tap joint at the back of the tap house wept and held and proved nothing about any morning of the week.

And the barrow, which had been standing wheel first in the middle of this yard since the second hour of the day before, went out of the gate just after the light, wheel first as it had been standing, pushed by a man who had been deciding to move it for nineteen hours and who did not give a reason and was not asked for one.

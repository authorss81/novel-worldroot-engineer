# Chapter 728

## The Eleventh Of The Twentieth, And A Barrow Of Wet Clay Put Against A Sill By Somebody Who Is Not In This Yard

There were four things being done at the same time on Tuesday and the yard where three of them were being done had nothing to do with the fourth, and that is the plainest sentence anybody in this holding can write about Tuesday.

The ring at the gate end came under its load at about the second hour, and the load was not a word for it. The crew man had nine bodies and a book and a length of rope and about four hours before the light went wrong, and the nine bodies went into the low side of the ring in the place the window told them to be.

Marek Vale was in that yard from the second hour to the dark. He had his schedule folded in his coat and a set of terms in the other pocket and he gave neither of them to anybody all day, and about the fourth hour somebody at the gate turned round and looked for him and could not find him, and he was on the wrong side of the ring with his hands behind his back like a man watching a boat.

He was not directing and he was not asked to. Twice a body came out of the ring and stood at the edge waiting to be told to go back in, and twice the crew man put a hand out flat and turned them round without looking up.

**"It is not the hour he thinks it is,"** the crew man said afterwards, to nobody, and wrote something in his own book and did not read it out.

The three other things happened four miles off and two miles off and up a lane, and none of the three waited on anybody in this yard, and this is how they came back.

The crossings on the third place's road were done by Emrys Dole and two men he brought with him, and they were done on the crossings that exist. The span that the small council would not have is still not there and was not half begun and nobody put a stone near it. Emrys Dole came down the middle road at about the ninth hour with lime on his knees and gave the account of it at the gate in about eleven words, and he gave it to Harlan Vetch and not to the engineer of record.

**"Two crossings cleaned and one re-bedded and the fourth one we did not touch. That is what we did. I have not got a reason for you and I will not make one up walking down a hill."**

And the man who buys asked him what the fourth one was, and Emrys Dole told him it was the one the five had said no to, and the man who buys said that a no was a no and got no further than that from anybody.

Up off the dry branch past the second bend there were nine bodies on a road with no gate on it, and they were not fetched and they were not this holding's, and the only reason this yard knows anything about it before dark is that a woman who had gone up there on her own business came back at the seventh hour and stood at the gate with the water standing in her boots.

**"There are nine of them standing on it and not one of them is standing there because anybody here asked them to. They have brought their own food up and they have carried two of an old woman's boxes up as well, and I did not speak to them and they did not speak to me, and I am telling you because you would have heard about it from a carter on Friday and a carter on Friday gets it wrong."**

She was asked whether anybody had put a route in front of them, and then whether anybody in this yard had said one word about water to her place, and she answered the second question before anybody had finished asking the first.

**"No to the route, and no to the water, and I want both of those written down in the order they were asked. I have been up that lane four times this summer for my own reasons and I have seen somebody do it once. And there is a place four miles off me with nothing in it and I did not walk past it on Friday to look for it, so do not tell this yard it was offered to me."**

**"Nobody has said a word to you about water and if anybody had it would have been heard before it got to the boards."**

**"Then it has not been offered and that is the whole of what I came down to say, and I will go back up that lane now and I will come down again when there is something to say."**

And at the head of the branch the far end was open and shut by other hands, because it is held by two people and neither of them is the engineer of record, and both of them were up there on Tuesday doing the fourth of the four kinds of work with a third person and a book.

Neither of them came down the lane that evening. Hanne Brack's husband was seen at the second place at dusk filling two cans from a butt that has no pressure behind it, and the man who lays for three councils was on the branch at the same hour with his hod and no chart, and neither of them sent word and neither of them was sent for.

The figures in this yard went up on the boards through the day and nobody read them out except one pair, and the pair that got read out was the wrong one.

A man from the middle row, who has a way of getting to a figure before anybody else has finished putting it down, came up at about the fifth hour and said the ring was to be loaded on the two hundred and thirty-four side.

**"That is the half over the top,"** the mason from the third place said, from the sill end, without stopping work. **"Two hundred and thirty-four has stood since Saturday morning. Two hundred and twenty-four is the one that went and that is where they are digging. You have had it the right way round since Thursday."**

**"Then say which one I have wrong."**

**"You have not got one of them wrong, you have got yesterday's half,"** the mason said, **"and you have been carrying that about since Friday. Four hundred and fifty-eight is the whole of the window and you could have had either half out of the sum and you did not do that."**

The man from the middle row stood there for a while and then said that nobody had ever corrected him in this yard before and that he did not know what to do about being corrected, and went and stood somewhere else.

**"Channel came down five in the night,"** the crew man said later, at the gate, to the yard and not to anybody in it. **"One thousand and three hundred and forty-four hundredweight of it, and that is the four hundred and ninety-first of the run. I write my own book and I write the hour in it and I do not write a reason and I do not write a name, and the hour I lost on Wednesday last week is still lost and it is not coming back out of anybody's arithmetic."**

Odile Vray had the boards to herself at about the sixth hour because Kellan Rusk was in the one room with the drawer shut and the key on its nail, and she gave the pair of them in the order she had been giving them since the spring and stopped in front of the one room door.

**"Eight hundred and seventy-five hundredweight is what the near stone says. Nine hundred and twenty-two hundredweight is what the far one says. Forty-seven of masonry between them, and neither figure has moved since the wall went up. I have been giving them in this order for eleven weeks and I will not start a new one now because a man has had four things to do at once."**

Soren Rill came through with the long sheet about the same hour and gave it, as he gives it.

**"Four hundred and eighty-nine at the head, the four hundred and ninetieth over the line and the four hundred and eighty-eighth under it."**

The man who buys had the ages out of his own pocket on a scrap of a carter's docket and read them off his own paper, which is not his paper and which he had copied out of this yard a fortnight ago and had been keeping.

**"Two hundred and ninety-one on the charter, and nothing in this holding has lain on a shelf longer than that piece of paper. Three hundred and eighty-five on the form. Three hundred and seventy-nine on the line Silling ruled under her own first one, which has been blank since the day she ruled it and will be blank at the light. And a hundred and eighty-eight on the letter. That is four and I have got them in my own order and not yours, Kellan."**

**"You have got them in the order a carter writes things down,"** Kellan Rusk said from inside, **"which is the fourth one last, and it is not wrong, and I have been saying the same four in the same order every morning for eleven weeks and I would rather somebody else said them for a change."**

The boy who carries water was through with his two cans about the seventh hour as usual, and he came round to the north board and wrote the pair up in his own hand and put the chalk down and said one thing about it, which he has not said on any other morning.

**"It is written. Nobody is going to read it out. There has not been a reading in this yard since before I came and I am not the one who is going to start it on a day when nine men are in a hole."**

**"Two hundred and sixteen of four hundred and fifteen,"** the girl from the second place read off the board to nobody in particular about ten minutes later, on her way past, and did not stop, and did not say it again.

And Perrin Dae, at the lime, where he had been all day with nobody asking him anything, put his own two figures into a conversation he had not started.

**"Four hundred and eighty-eight is that head of yours. Under its line, four hundred and eighty-eighth. Above the top of it, four hundred and ninetieth. And there is nothing at all in this yard about whether any of that is any good. You have all been treating that head like it is a verdict on somebody."**

**"It is not a verdict and it is not a use,"** Soren Rill said. **"It is a count of days and it goes up by one in the night whether or not anybody in this holding is any good, and if you want to know what a good holding looks like you will not find it written on the head of that sheet."**

Somebody at the gate said that the man who had been standing on the wrong side of the ring all day should be given something for it, and had got as far as opening his mouth when the woman with the slate cut across him from about nine feet away.

**"He has a schedule and a set of terms and both of them are in his coat and neither of them has been read out. If you want to thank a man for standing in a yard, thank him after the yard is finished, and there is no yard finished, and you are in the way of nine bodies."**

Then she went and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest the gate and nothing else, and did not mention having done it, and Marek Vale went back over the ring and did not thank her and was not thanked by anybody else either.

At about the tenth hour a woman came to the gate with a folded paper in her coat and about half of an hour left in the day. She did not go to the boards and she did not go to the sill.

She went to Tova Reed, who was at the seed board with her back to the yard, and put the paper into her hand and said four words about it and no more, and Tova Reed took it, and did not open it, and put it inside her coat against her ribs.

Marek Vale was about eleven feet away and did not ask, and the woman did not tell him, and about eight people in that yard noticed the whole of it and did not comment on it, and the light went about half an hour later with the ring half dug and the crew man's book shut and the four of the four things still running in four places that had nothing to do with one another or with him.

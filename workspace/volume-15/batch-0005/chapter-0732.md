# Chapter 732

## The Fifteenth Of The Twentieth, And Two Hats Taken Off And Put On A Bench And Left There While Two People Read

About twenty people were in this yard on the Saturday because it was a Saturday and because two men had said on the Friday night that they would come, and one of those two had said it out loud at a gate and the other had not said anything to anybody.

The cloth was still on the middle table and the four sheets were still squared on it, and not one date had been put at the head of any of them since Friday, and the two that had come to read them again at dusk had not been asked to and had not refused.

Ismay Rooke came up the middle road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and put his hat on the bench at the gate before he said anything, which is not a thing anybody from the third place has done at this gate before.

He read all four sheets, and he read the third one twice, and then he took a stub of pencil out of his own pocket and signed under the first and put the pencil down and picked his hat up off the bench.

**"That is one hand and it is mine and it is for the third place, and I have not asked the third place whether I was allowed and I am not going back up that road to ask."**

**"You have not asked anybody at all,"** somebody said.

**"No. And if you want to know what the third place thinks about it then you will have to go and ask the third place, and I would go with you if I had not laid a wall all day."**

About nine people looked at the bench after that. His hat was on it and then it was in his hand and then it was on it again, and nobody has been able to say afterwards which of those three happened first.

Perrin Dae came in at about the sixth hour with two hands of his own and a very short piece of time, and he read the four sheets standing up in the order he read everything, which is the order it will matter in, and he signed under the second.

Then he said the thing that he had come to say, before anybody else had got a word in.

**"It is not complete. I want that said by me and not by him, because I am the second of the two and the second of the two is the one who has to live inside it. There is no second place in it. There is a fourth term that says what is not in it and that term is the shortest line on that table and if I sign this I am signing the shortest line in the whole of it as the thing I have most to answer for."**

**"You are signing four terms and not one of them."**

**"I am signing the paper they are written on, which is what I have just described, and you will hear me say it that way every time I am asked about it from now on."**

> **Provisional, and made out at the north end of the long table:**
>
> **For the third place, one hand, and the hand is a mason's.**
>
> **For the fourth place, the hand of Perrin Dae, who keeps lime and timber at that place and has never laid a course in his life.**
>
> **Neither of us signs for the second place. Neither of us is able to. There is no water in that place and there has not been since the reach came in, and nothing on these four sheets says so where a man would look for it.**
>
> **The fourth term says it. The fourth term is the shortest thing on the table and that is ours to answer for.**
>
> **Two hands are on this paper. Two hands is not a majority of anything. We have put two hands on it and we both know what that is worth and neither of us is going to be asked to say a bigger number.**
>
> **We are not signing for anybody who is not standing in this yard.**

A man at the gate got as far as the word carried and did not get any further, because Perrin Dae turned round.

**"Two. Say the rest of it and I will answer it."**

**"Two places have signed. That is a majority of the road."**

**"It is not a majority of the road, it is two hands, and there is no number of hands on this table that becomes a majority of anything. You may write two on a piece of paper. You may not turn it into five and you may not turn it into the second place, and if you try it in front of these people I will take the pencil out of your hand."**

Nobody tried it in front of these people, and about four people in that yard believed the man's arithmetic and about six did not and were not argued with.

The crew man came through about the seventh hour with the boards at his back and read the two of the four that were on the seed board and had them in the wrong order for the first time in a month because he was copying them off the boards at dusk and not reading them off the rack.

**"Four hundred and ninety-three at the head of it. Over the line, four hundred and ninety-fourth. Under it, four hundred and ninety-second. And one thousand and three hundred and fifty hundredweight came down the channel this morning and that is the four hundred and ninety-fifth of the run. I write those down in my own book at dusk and I do not take them off a board, because a board can be rubbed out and a book cannot, and that is the only reason I know either of them."**

The man from the top of the middle road came in at about the seventh hour with his hat off and asked one question and would not ask a second one, and it was the only question he had come for.

**"Has it gone up or down since Friday."**

**"Four hundred and sixty-two the whole of it,"** Kellan Rusk said. **"Two hundred and thirty-six over the top standing where it stood on Friday, and two hundred and twenty-six underneath, which is the half that went. And I will tell you the same thing I tell everybody, which is that nobody in this holding knows what that pair is for, and if you go and act on it because it has gone up then you will be the first one and I would rather you asked somebody."**

The man stood there with his hat in his hands.

**"Then I will not put a barrow on the high side of that ring on Monday,"** he said, **"because the low side is where it went and I am not a man who takes a figure off a man who keeps it. That is not because the figure is good and you may write down that it is not."**

He said it to Kellan Rusk and not to anybody else and went home.

The girl from the second place read the pair of boards off the rack and gave the reason she is the one who does it, which she has never given before and did not appear to know she was giving.

**"The near board says eight hundred and seventy-nine hundredweight and the far one says nine hundred and twenty-six hundredweight. I read them because I can see the rack and I am not anybody, and that is the whole of what I am allowed to be in this yard and I have made my peace with it a long time ago."**

Auret Sill read the four ages off the corner of the seed board in the order a person signing a paper reads them, which is the oldest first, and there was nothing wrong with it.

**"Two hundred and ninety-five days on the charter, which is the longest any of these four has lain anywhere and the only one I would be willing to defend in a room with strangers in it. The form is at three hundred and eighty-nine. Silling's own line is at three hundred and eighty-three and has never had a mark on it in its life. And a hundred and ninety-two days on the letter, which nobody has opened, and which I have now asked two people about in two counties and got the same silence out of both."**

**"You have read that board off somebody else's seed board."**

**"I have read it off somebody else's seed board and she was asked to write it and she did not ask twice and she is not here, and I will not make a thing out of a woman's arrangement about a board."**

At about the eighth hour somebody needed to see the top of the low board at the gate and could not see it, and the mason from the third place was asked whether he would go up the ladder and look, and he said no.

**"No. And I will give you the reason and it is not that I am tired. That ladder has no rung on it that has been climbed since it was put up and there is a reason for that, and the reason is that a person up a ladder stops being able to hear anybody in the yard, and every question that board ever gets asked is asked by somebody standing at the bottom of it."**

**"You could lean out."**

**"I could lean out and that is the same thing with a worse hold."**

Nobody asked him to lean out, and the offer on the low board was still there at dusk with no date on it and nobody had picked it up and nobody had taken it down.

The pair of figures for the north board was written on the north board at about the seventh hour by the mason from the third place, in his own hand, and the figures were right.

**"Two hundred and eighteen of four hundred and nineteen,"** the mason from the third place said, and read them out because he had written them and could not read handwriting that was not his own.

Somebody said it was not his place to write them and he said it was not his place to write them either and that somebody had to, and went back to the sill, and nobody read the two out afterwards and nobody thanked him for writing them and nobody rubbed them out.

And the clerk from up a lane, who has been standing at the gate on and off for a fortnight with a slip three weeks old in a tray in his coat, asked Perrin Dae at about the ninth hour for a copy of the four terms to take up the lane with him.

**"There is no copy,"** Kellan Rusk said. **"There is the original and there is the cloth it is lying on and there is not a second of it anywhere in this holding, and a copy wants a column and there is no column cut under any of the four sheets on the long table and no column is going to be cut for a Saturday."**

**"Then he goes up that lane with nothing."**

**"Then that is what he goes with."**

Perrin Dae stood at the gate with his hat in his hands for a while after that, and then said that he would get a man from the fourth place who can write and have it copied out by hand in the evening, and that the man would write it as it stands and would not put a date at the top of it, and that he would take the copy up the lane on Monday in his own cart and to nobody in particular.

Then he went, and the two hats were still on the bench at the dark.

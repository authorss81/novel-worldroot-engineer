# Chapter 733

## The Sixteenth Of The Twentieth, And A Ladle Of Water Thrown On The Fourth Course To Set The Lime

Sunday put about nine people in this yard and about four of them were on stone, and the light came late and went early and nobody did anything about either of those things.

The mason from the third place threw a ladle of water at the fourth course at about the seventh hour to set the lime he had laid at the sixth, and about four people watched a man throw water at a wall on a Sunday and one of them told him that a Sunday was a bad day for it and he said he was not going to argue with weather.

The man who lays for three councils was in the yard at about the eighth hour on his own business and did not tell anybody what it was, and he gave two figures at the gate and gave them in the opposite order to the one he used on Friday.

**"Four hundred and ninety-sixth of the run,"** he said. **"That is the one before the round one and I have never said either of them out loud before because it sounds like an announcement, and it is not one. The channel came in at one thousand and three hundred and fifty-eight hundredweight of it, which is five down and not eight up, and it will be eight up tomorrow."**

**"A number like that does not mean anything has happened,"** somebody said.

**"No. But a man who counts mornings will tell you where he has got to and I am telling you because I would not want anybody in this yard finding out at noon from a slate."**

The return came round at about the ninth hour, the way it has come round for four years, and it came round in the shape of a woman with a ledger under her arm going along the north row with a book and asking one question at each door.

She asked it nine times and got the same answer nine times, and at the end of the north row the answer she got was from an old man who is not fetched and has never been asked anything at any hour.

**"Nothing,"** he said, and the woman wrote one stroke on a page and shut it and went on.

Nobody in this yard asked her what the stroke was for and nobody asked her what the book was for, and a return has not moved a figure in this holding since before the ring went into the ground and it did not move one this morning either, and there is no number printed against it anywhere and there is not going to be one.

On the boards in the one room there are thirty-nine blanks in a column and the second rule under them stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, which is where it has been every time anybody has looked at it.

At about the tenth hour the network was read out in this yard for the first time with the parts of it that do not work in it, which was not anybody's idea and came about because a man asked a plain question at the gate and nobody had a sheet.

Sera Quill had a sheet that was not her own, copied out by a man in a town four counties off, and she read it off in her own order and she stopped at the part where the copy runs out.

**"Four sections and five joints. Four sections read: one at the gate end, one at the top of the wall, one at the second place's branch and one on the third place's road. The fifth joint is where the copy has nothing. It is not that the joint failed. It is that nobody has ever written down what is at it."**

**"Then write it down,"** somebody said.

**"I have no mouth on that joint and nothing is going to be invented for it this morning. It goes on the sheet as a joint with nothing against it and it is going to stay that way until a person who knows what is at that joint stands in this yard and says so."**

**"Then the network is not finished."**

**"The network is uneven and uneven is a thing you can print. That is the whole of what came out of reading it out. Not one person in this yard is going to be appointed over it today and the job has not been asked for."**

The man who lays for three councils said the thing that made the morning what it was, and he said it quietly, and about three people heard all of it.

**"There are two things I will not say in this yard and I am saying so now so that it is on somebody's morning. I am not going to say who holds what end of this. And I am not going to say what any of them would take if it was offered to them, and there will be no question of mine about either of those today and no quotation of me about either."**

**"You said that last month."**

**"I said it in nearly those words and I will keep saying it until somebody stops needing to hear it, and then I will stop, and that is all it has ever been."**

The fourth of the four things came round again at about the eleventh hour and not one of the nine people made anything of it at all, which is what has happened at the other eleven of them.

Nia Vale read it off the rack in the doorway and did not rub anything off anything.

**"A hundred and sixty-three in force, twenty-nine taken, a hundred and thirty-four not. And there is nothing to rub out on this morning and nothing is going to be invented to rub, because there is no fourth to rub and there never will be again and I have not missed it and neither should you."**

Sera Quill had the slate out before Nia Vale had finished and gave one of her two and kept the other, which is what she does.

**"One hundred and eleventh. That stepped this morning. The other one is the one I carry and it is not on the rack and it is not on the sheet the mason read off and it is not in anybody's pocket in this yard, and there is a girl here who counts how many mornings I say a figure on and I have never once pretended to her that I do not know she counts."**

Kellan Rusk gave the window off the face of the register form and not out of his head, and said which way it had gone by putting his thumb on a figure he had written himself nine days ago and comparing.

**"Four hundred and sixty-three, then. Two hundred and thirty-seven above, two hundred and twenty-six below, and two hundred and thirty-seven is the half that moved this morning, because I wrote two hundred and thirty-five on the face of this form nine days ago and I have not touched that corner of it since, and that is the only way I know to tell you which half went without guessing."**

**"And if somebody had rubbed it out."**

**"Then I should not have found out, and it is not my job to find out, and I would rather know that than not."**

The girl from the second place gave the head of the long sheet from the doorway, correctly, in the middle of all that.

**"Four hundred and ninety-four at the head. It sits between a four hundred and ninety-third and a four hundred and ninety-fifth and you will not get one out of me today, because that is the clerk's business and not mine."**

A man at the gate heard that and put his hand on the near board and said he had a barrow-load of stone and would take it against the figure on the board, which is eight hundred and eighty hundredweight at the gate end and has been on the same two stones since the wall went up.

Harlan Vetch came off the wall faster than he has come off it in eleven weeks.

**"Put it down. The one you have been leaning on all afternoon is eight hundred and eighty hundredweight and it is the nearer stone, and the other one at the top of that wall is nine hundred and twenty-seven hundredweight, and neither of them is a debt and neither of them is an order for stone. And you cannot take a load against a figure because a man wrote it in chalk. If you had asked me in the ordinary way at the gate I would have said the same thing in the same words."**

**"I have been listening to two men read figures at each other all morning."**

**"Then you have heard two men say the same word about the same kind of number. That is what a holding sounds like when it has more figures than it has uses, and I would rather it sounded like that than like a place where a man could load a barrow against a number written in chalk."**

The man put the barrow down outside the gate and did not take it away and did not unload it, and nobody spoke to him about it again that day.

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in the order she was given them, and the last one she wrote was the letter. The man who buys read them off the board with his back to the yard and asked whose hand it was and was told it was hers and that she had been asked.

**"Three hundred and ninety, two hundred and ninety-six, three hundred and eighty-four and a hundred and ninety-three. And I have asked her for that board four times this year and she has given it to me four times and has never once said a word about it, and I will not be the man who starts now."**

The ring of bare ground at the back of the wall came up twice in the morning because two different men asked about it, and it was not walked and it was not measured and it was not priced and nothing was said about it that had not been said about it before, and the man who lays for three councils was asked if he would go and look at it and said that a man who is not going to stand on a thing should not be the one sent to look at it.

At dusk two people at either end of the joint with nothing against it were talking at the gate, and neither of them had been sent by anybody in this yard, and the arrangement they arrived at between them in about four sentences was that each of them would go and look at it on their own time and not wait for this holding, and neither of them said anything to Marek Vale about it at all, and he was standing at the boards at about ten feet away with his schedule in his coat and did not hear the whole of it and was not told.

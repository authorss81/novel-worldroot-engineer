# Chapter 723

## The Sixth Of The Twentieth, And A Wet Sheet Turned Over On The Boards In Front Of About Thirty People

The flood load went onto the ring and onto forty feet of the wall at about the fifth hour, and it took nine bodies and most of the morning, and the crews did it in their own order without being given one.

The seed trays came out of the seed house at about the sixth hour and went into the yard on two barrows because the ground by the house has to be clear to get the load in past it, and Tova Reed spent the morning moving them and then spent the afternoon putting them back in an order that was not the order they came out in, and nobody helped her because there was nobody free.

Marek Vale was at the gate end with the load and then at the boards with the paper, and twice in the morning he came down the yard to the barrows and twice a man who needed a figure from him was sent to wait at the wall by a man who had no authority to send him anywhere, and waited about forty minutes both times.

Nobody in that yard said anything about the two of them and neither of them said anything about it, and the barrows went back into the house at about the fourth hour with two trays cracked at the corner and one that had been in the sun since the third hour and had gone over and that Tova Reed marked in her own book in her own hand.

Isel Lowen came down the north branch path at about the middle of the day with two men behind her and wet to the knee, and she had a sheet folded inside her coat and she took it out at the boards and did not give it to anybody.

**"That is ours. It is the north branch, three miles up, eleven households, and it is not a council and it does not keep a book and there are three hands on that and one of them is mine."**

She turned it over and put her finger on one line of it and left it there, and then she said the thing out loud in front of about thirty people, once, and did not say it again for anybody.

> From eleven households on the north branch, and the branch is three miles up and wet at the second bend, and this paper belongs to whoever is holding it and not to the holding at the wall:
>
> **We have been told there is a load to be put on the ground above us. We have been told it in two places and separately and neither of the two was asked to hear the other first, and we have not asked to hear either of them.**
>
> **We will not be put under the flood load. The ground above us is soft already and it is going to be softer by the time anybody has finished saying so, and there is no amount of it that we will be under.**
>
> We will not take a smaller figure of it and we will not take a bigger one. A person who comes up this path in the spring with a number will get the same words on that morning, in the same mouth, and not in the mouth of whoever wrote the number down.
>
> **We ask this holding for nothing today and we have not asked it for anything since the paper came down that lane four terms ago. We are not asking for a figure to be altered and we will not be moved by one, and if every figure in your yard is altered before the water comes we will say the same thing.**
>
> This paper is ours and it is not a record of yours, and it is not to be entered anywhere.
>
> Three hands are on it. There is no clerk and there is no book.

Marek Vale did not argue with her and did not ask her to explain and did not ask whether she understood what the load was.

**"I have a schedule with four hours on it and two of them are now in the wrong place. That is all that has happened in my morning and nothing else has happened in my morning."**

A man at the gate said that it was not a no, that it was a holding, that a region could hold and be brought on, and he had got as far as the word brought before Auret Sill put her can down on the boards and stopped him.

**"Say what that word means. Not what you think it means. If you cannot say it, then you are describing a thing you have not seen done anywhere in four years and you are doing it about people who have come down a wet path to say no in their own mouths."**

The man said he could not say what it meant and Auret Sill said that was the end of it then, and picked her can up again and carried it out to the sluice and did not stand about.

Then somebody behind Isel Lowen asked whether the region would take a smaller figure, and she answered without turning round.

**"No. It is not about the size. There is no size of it that we will be under, and if a person comes up here in the spring with a smaller number I will say the same words on that morning and they will be in my mouth and not in yours."**

Odile Vray put a stone on the boards so the paper would not go and did not say that she was doing it, and when Isel Lowen asked her whether the boards were hers, Odile Vray said that they were and that it would not have mattered what they were.

**"And I will say the part that is mine,"** she said. **"That is a refusal and not a delay and there is nobody in this yard who is entitled to write it down as anything else, and the crew on that wall are going to carry forty feet of load without the top eleven households and it will be a different wall and it will not be a worse one."**

Then a man from that crew asked whether they were to finish the forty feet or stop, and she told him to finish it, and he said that with the north branch out of it he did not know what finishing it was for.

**"Then finish it anyway,"** she said. **"A wall that stops halfway is a different object and not a shorter version of the same one, and you will not get a figure out of anybody in this yard telling you it was the right length."**

Then a woman asked whether the eleven households had been told that the load had gone on, and Isel Lowen's second man said that it had been, by the third man, and that it was not for anybody here to repeat it, and she said that she had only asked.

Soren Rill came through and gave the long sheet and gave it late, and it was the only figure given out loud in that yard that afternoon and he gave it while looking at the wall.

**"Four hundred and eighty-four at the head, the four hundred and eighty-third under the line, the four hundred and eighty-fifth over it. That went up by one in the night and it went up by one in the night before that, and it will go up tomorrow, and nobody in this yard may tell the north branch that it does not care what they do."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall and gave the stones before the channel, which is his order now.

**"Eight hundred and seventy at the gate and nine hundred and seventeen at the top, and the forty-seven is the forty-seven. The channel has one thousand and three hundred and forty-three hundredweight in it, eight on yesterday, and it is the four hundred and eighty-sixth of the run."**

Then he gave the window and gave it to nobody in particular.

**"Four hundred and fifty-three of it. Two hundred and thirty-two over the top and that is the one that went in the night, and two hundred and twenty-one underneath standing still, and I have said it round the wrong way to this yard three times this month and been checked on it three times, and I am still getting it out in the order I learned it in."**

Then he said the part that had him turn round and look at the far end of the wall.

**"And the far stone is where you put the load if there is one more load. That is not an opinion. That is nine hundred and seventeen at the top against eight hundred and seventy at the gate and forty-seven of the difference, and the far end has taken the water off this wall every time there has been water for a year."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room and put his back to the door and would not put the register form on the boards for anybody, and said why once and would not say it again.

**"No. That is the register form and it is three hundred and eighty days and I have never once known what a body is meant to do with it, and what has happened this morning did not happen in this holding. It happened to eleven households three miles up a path and it is going in their book or it is going nowhere."**

**"Then it goes nowhere,"** Nia Vale said from the doorway.

**"Then it goes nowhere,"** he said. **"The charter is two hundred and eighty-six days and the letter is a hundred and eighty-three. Silling's own second ruled line is at three hundred and seventy-four and has been empty since before I came to stand in this doorway, and I have said all of them out loud this afternoon, every one of the four."**

Kellan Rusk went back inside and shut the door and did not open it again that day.

Then Nia Vale stood in the doorway a while with her own sheet folded and did not take the pencil out, and told the six people who had stayed what was standing in that room, which is what she does about half the time.

**"Four sheets on the long table and not one entered and not one refused and no column cut under any of them."**

She stood in the doorway with the pencil still behind her ear for a moment, and then went on with the second half of it, because there was a second half.

**"And beside them, on the sheet of terms, there is a column with nothing in it under a heading that nobody has improved, and under that four ruled lines under two words with nothing on them either, and none of that has moved since the morning somebody wrote four things on a sheet in an afternoon on the middle table."**

**"And a sheet was on the boards this afternoon,"** she said, **"and it is not going in here, and I would like whoever put it down to know that I am not offended and that I know exactly which door it did not go through."**

Isel Lowen and her two men went back up the north branch path before the light went, wet through to the waist.

The man who owns the load stood at the gate end of the wall at about the seventh hour and asked Marek Vale whether somebody was going to walk up that path and tell them that the load had gone on, and Marek said that it was not his paper and it was not his news, and that if eleven households were to be told anything about a load then they were the ones to do it in their own mouths or not at all.

Then the crew took their tools off that wall about an hour later and walked up the path together without talking, and the forty feet at the gate end stood in the dark with the load on it, and up the north branch path three miles off there were eleven households that had said no that afternoon in their own mouths, and there was nobody on that path carrying anything back down it.

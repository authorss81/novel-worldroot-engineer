# Chapter 724

## The Seventh Of The Twentieth, And A Book Put Back On Its Shelf Without Being Opened

The book came round at about the fourth hour and Kellan Rusk did not come out of the room to say so, which it does whether anybody is glad of it or not, and he has not come out of that room to say so.

Odile Vray said it at the boards instead, in the plain words she uses when a thing is on the boards and everybody can see it.

**"The book is back on its shelf and it has not moved a figure in this holding and it is not going to. Thirty-nine blanks on the board in there. Under those blanks there is a rule, and it has stood open and empty at the thirty-ninth time for four years, and a book coming round has not touched it, and this yard should not be expecting it to open today."**

She knocked her knuckle on the boards.

**"And there was a region at this gate yesterday saying in its own mouth that it will not be put under the load, and that is not on this morning's list and it is not going on it, and anybody who brings it up at me gets the same answer twice."**

A man brought it up at her about an hour later and got the same answer twice, and then a woman who had walked down the lane that morning for no reason anybody could find said something about the north branch that made four people turn round, and the yard went back to its work with the north branch still on nobody's list.

He came through with his two cans at about the seventh hour and put the pair up on the north board in his own hand, with the chalk in the hand he could still close, and did not stop at it.

**"Two hundred and fourteen of four hundred and eleven."**

A woman stood beside him while he was putting the chalk away and asked him whether he would read it out for her, because she wanted to write it in the back of a book of her own.

**"I will read it out if you tell me what reading it out is for."**

She said she wanted it for a book of her own and he said that was where he stopped, and she said that was a strange place for a boy to stop, and he said it was the only place he had, and she wrote it off the board instead and did not argue about it with anybody.

The man who buys came up the lane about the middle of the morning with a fold of paper that was not his own and that he had been handed at that lane's top on his way up that morning and had not asked a single question about.

**"That came out of a tray up the lane where they put things that have been copied off other people's papers. And it is your wall on it. Four hundred and thirty-three. And it says your window, and it says the day it was taken, and the day it was taken is three weeks back from now."**

He put it flat on the boards where about eleven people could see it and where the wind immediately took one corner of it.

Kellan Rusk came out and looked at it and read it without touching it.

**"It is right about everything and wrong about the number. The window is four hundred and fifty-four. Two hundred and thirty-two underneath and two hundred and twenty-two over the top, and it is the one underneath that went in the night, and that is not the same as what it was a month ago in either half."**

**"So somebody has been quoting you off a month old page."**

**"Somebody has been quoting us off a page and we have not known it was happening, and the difference between those two sentences is about three weeks long and I am the one who let it happen by not asking what the other end does with what we write."**

A man at the gate said that then somebody ought to write the right number on it, and Kellan Rusk said no, and gave the reason once, at length, in the voice of a man who has been in the doorway nine years.

**"If I write on that, it becomes a record of a correction, and a record of a correction wants a column under it and there is no column under any of the four sheets in this room and none has been cut, and I will not cut one this afternoon to fix a piece of paper four miles away that a man in a tray will pick up, read, and set down again. It would be the first thing entered in this holding in four years and it would be about a number."**

**"Then the wrong number keeps standing."**

**"The wrong number keeps standing, and it is a month old, and it is about a wall, and it is in a tray four miles up a lane. I know what that sounds like at four in the afternoon. I have thought about it for about an hour and that is the whole of what I have come out here to say."**

Odile Vray put her hand flat on the slip to hold the corner and did not say a word about it for about a minute.

**"Then we hold it down,"** she said, **"and we let the month be what it was, and there is no way in this holding to write anything down that is not one of four things, and I did not build that and I would not have built it and this yard does not take itself apart at the fourth hour to correct a slip in a tray."**

The slip stayed on the boards under her hand until the wind dropped and then it was put in the middle table drawer, shut, with the key back on its nail.

A man who had come up the lane on the strength of it turned round about two hours later and said he had a copy of a copy, which is a thing that happens at four miles with four intermediaries, and that he was going to take it back down and put it in the tray with the other copies and that the other people who had that figure ought to be told.

Kellan Rusk came out and stood on the step and told him what he had already told the yard once and did not say it a second time, because he had said it once at the seventh hour and the yard had it.

**"You will take it down. And I would think very hard about whether you are the person who tells them or whether you tell them there is a number in a tray that this holding says is wrong, because those are two different errands and only one of them is true and the other one would be a thing said about a wall by a man who has never walked on it."**

The man thought about it for about as long as it took him to walk to the gate and then he asked Kellan Rusk whether anybody would write down that he had thought about it, and Kellan Rusk said that nobody was going to, and shut the rack.

Then he went back down the lane with the copy in his coat and did not put it in the tray.

Soren Rill came down off the boards at about the fifth hour to look at the slip in the drawer and gave the long sheet on his way past, without stopping, to a woman who had not asked him for it.

**"Four hundred and eighty-five at the head, the four hundred and eighty-fourth under the line, the four hundred and eighty-sixth over it. That is one up on yesterday and it will be one up in the morning, and I would say to whoever took that slip away to write down that it was not taken off my wall."**

Nobody wrote it down.

Sera Quill had the slate out at the boards at about the third hour of the afternoon because two people were standing about looking at a month-old number and a slate is the other thing people look at.

**"I have nothing this morning. Mine step on their own mornings and this is not one of them, and a slip with three weeks on it is not going to move me forward a day."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall and gave the four, and gave them with the wind behind him and one of them louder than the other three.

**"Nine hundred and eighteen at the top. Eight hundred and seventy-one at the gate. Forty-seven of masonry between them, and there has been forty-seven of masonry between them since the wall went up."**

He wiped his hands and gave the channel and the run.

**"Five down on yesterday in that channel, one thousand and three hundred and thirty-eight hundredweight of it, and the run calls that one the four hundred and eighty-seventh, and the run has not been wrong in my hearing once in three years."**

He shifted his weight on the ladder and came down half a rung.

**"The window is four hundred and fifty-four. Somebody will ask me which half moved, because they ask me every morning, and it was the one underneath, and it is two hundred and twenty-two."**

Kellan Rusk gave the four ages from the step, in the order he has used all week, and said the one about the ruled line twice because he had not said it once properly.

**"The register form is where it was yesterday, at three hundred and eighty-one days. The charter is at two hundred and eighty-seven and is the older of the two by rather more than a season. And the one I have to be asked about twice is the second ruled line in Silling's own book, at three hundred and seventy-five days, which has never had a mark on it in the whole nine years."**

He stopped on the step and started again.

**"There is nothing written on it and I was asked in the last week to write a date on it, because a book came round, and I said no, and I am saying the same no at four in the afternoon as I said it at the fourth hour and the letter is a hundred and eighty-four days and it will be a hundred and eighty-four days at the seventh hour."**

He shut the rack and it was the last thing he did that day.

At dusk the slip was in the drawer with the key turned and on its nail, and the thirty-nine blanks on the board in the one room were exactly as many as they had been four hours before the book came round, and about four miles up a lane there is a tray of other people's numbers and one of them is three weeks old and wrong and belongs to this holding.

# Chapter 720

## The Third Of The Twentieth, And A Stack Of Broken Stone Under A Rail At The Inside Of The Wall

Monday came in hard and cold from the north east and the frost was in the mortar lines of the sill by the fifth hour, and about ten people were on the stone, which is one fewer than the last three mornings had been carrying.

Odile Vray counted them twice at the boards and said the number out loud both times, because she counts out loud when the number has moved and she had a reason that morning and the reason was hers.

**"Eleven bodies before the light. Ten now. Two on the sluice, three on the wall, five on the sill and me."**

**"What has happened to the eleventh."**

**"He set his hod down on the stack at about the fourth hour and walked up the branch, and that is what the five wrote down at that gate in their own mouths. I did not have a vote in it then and I have not got one now."**

Nobody in that yard asked her a second question about it, and she did not offer one.

The boy who carries water came through at about the seventh hour with his two cans and his cracked hand and wrote the pair on the north board above the two ruled lines, and wrote it with the chalk in the hand he could still close.

**"Two hundred and twelve of four hundred and seven."**

A woman asked him to say it out so the two of them could write it down, and he said the reason and it was a different reason from the one he had given the day before and he knew it was different.

**"I wrote it wrong on this board earlier in the week and nobody noticed, and I have not had it long enough since to say it from inside my head without looking, and nine people is a bad number to say an uncertain thing to, and I have seen that pair forty times and not four hundred."**

She wrote it off the board instead, which is allowed, and he went back to his cans. Nobody read it out. He went back to his cans.

The fourth course at the gate end did not go in that morning, and the argument about it took about two hours and most of the yard heard it.

Harlan Vetch wanted the stones broken and wanted them now, because the light on the gate end is the good light and it is gone by the ninth hour. Odile Vray wanted one body off the sill to break and would not take him one from the wall, because the wall is behind by a course and a half and they know that. They went at it through the figures both of them had been given and neither of them would put it on a sheet.

**"The long sheet says four hundred and eighty-one at the head, the four hundred and eightieth under the line and the four hundred and eighty-second over it,"** she said. **"That is what the wall has had off this yard. That is not the same as what is broken and lying on the ground in front of me."**

**"Then what is the two hundred and twenty underneath the window doing in this conversation."**

**"I did not put it there. You did."**

**"It is a night's water off the valley and it is what makes a joint into a crack, and it is the reason that course goes in this week and not next, and it is more use on a wall than four hundred and eighty-one stones."**

The man who lays for three councils stood in the middle of it with his hod and listened to both of them and then said the thing that ended it, and he said it as masonry, and not as an opinion.

**"How many broken."**

Nobody answered him at once.

**"Not the sheet. Not the window. Count what is on the ground. I have laid for three councils for nineteen years and I have never once started a course on a number that was not the number of stones broken, and I will put my own count on the ground and it will be smaller than either of yours."**

He counted them twice with his own count and gave it out loud and it was smaller than the sheet by thirty-one and smaller than what Harlan Vetch needed by about a dozen.

Odile Vray asked him how he had arrived at thirty-one and he told her that he had counted the pile twice and then had walked it twice and that both numbers were the same, and that anybody who wanted a different number could come and walk it.

Nobody walked it, and about two people did walk it later in the morning when neither of them was in sight, and neither of them said anything about what it came to.

The fourth course stayed unbuilt until the light went and the stones that had been broken for it got stacked against the inside of the wall under a rail, and two men from the third place's road came past the gate at about the seventh hour and looked at the pile and one of them said something short about another morning and the other one did not.

Odile Vray said that was the number and that it meant the fourth course would go in on another morning, and Harlan Vetch said he had not been told that and she said he had not been told it because there was nobody in this yard to tell, and he looked at her for a while and then went and stacked stones for the next two days instead of laying them.

Auret Sill came through the yard at about the ninth hour with a can in each hand on her way out to the sluice, and stopped at the boards because two people had been saying the number out loud near her for two days running.

One of them asked her what the season's water stood at, and she told him, off her own sheet, in her own words, without being asked twice and without explaining where it came from.

**"Four thousand one hundred and one. Since the spring, filled by hand. It is larger than it was a fortnight ago and I have not changed one thing about how I do it, and nine people in this yard are not going to get a reason out of me for a figure I cannot account for myself."**

She said the last of it to the yard rather than to the man who had asked her, and went on up the path with both cans.

Then Odile Vray gave the rest of her count, because somebody at the gate had asked, and because the count is the only thing she has ever given anybody for free.

**"Eleven journeys with the barrow and eleven is a floor and it has been a floor since the spring. Fifteen lines in the use log and no sixteenth written, seven sessions entered and no eighth, and fifty-three requests standing against fifty-three notes. The two have never been added together in this yard and it is not going to start with me."**

Kellan Rusk came out at about the middle of the afternoon and gave the ages in an order he had decided on himself and would not explain, and gave the ruled line before the letter, which is not the order anybody expected.

**"Three hundred and seventy-seven days on the register form and two hundred and eighty-three on the charter, and I am giving those two together because they are the two anybody ever asks me for."**

He stopped at the second one and then went back inside for the other two rather than say them from the step, and the first thing he said when he came out again was about Silling.

**"Silling's own second ruled line is at three hundred and seventy-one days and it has never had a mark on it in the whole time I have been in this doorway, and it is the only ruled line in this yard that nobody has ever had an opinion about, including me."**

A woman at the gate waited about a minute and a half for the rest of it.

**"The letter is a hundred and eighty days,"** he said from the doorway. **"And that is the only figure in this holding that has been dealt with by nobody this month, and I have come to think that is not a failure of anybody's attention."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the fifth hour and gave the channel and the run and the two stones, and he gave the two stones before the channel because a man who has been told his course is not going in today does not lead with the channel.

**"The far end of this wall is at nine hundred and fourteen. The end you walk out of this yard through is at eight hundred and sixty-seven. There is forty-seven of masonry in between and there has been since the wall went up."**

He wiped his hands and then gave the rest of it.

**"One thousand and three hundred and thirty-two hundredweight in the channel, five down on yesterday, and the four hundred and eighty-third of the run. The window is four hundred and fifty. It is two hundred and twenty that went in the night and two hundred and thirty over the top that stood, and that is three odd mornings running I have had that the right way round, and somebody in this yard checks me on it about as often as the mornings come round, and I have never once been wrong twice in a row."**

The hod that went down on that stack at the fourth hour belonged to the third place, and it had his mark on the handle, and it went down on the top of stones that were not going to be laid today either.

A man from the third place came down the middle road about forty minutes later and asked Odile Vray, not him, and she told him, and the man went back up the road at about the eighth hour with nothing said about it by anybody on the way out.

Nobody in that yard said anything to the man at the bottom of the middle road about the hod, and there were two people who had noticed it and neither of them mentioned it to the other.

A man at the gate asked Kellan Rusk whether all that had changed the sheet on the middle table, and Kellan Rusk said that it had not, and gave one reason and stopped there.

**"Four things in four hands does not become five things because a man has set his hod down. If it did, this yard would have had four hundred papers on that table by now."**

By the time the light went the wind had got under one corner of the sheet on the middle table and lifted it and dropped it twice, and nobody held it, and the five short lines at the foot of it were still five short lines with nothing at the top of the paper and nothing at the foot of any of them.

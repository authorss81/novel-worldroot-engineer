# Chapter 705

## The Eighteenth Of The Nineteenth, And An Arithmetic Put In Front Of The Yard By The Five People Who Counted It Themselves, Which Was Right In Every Particular And Contained No Decision

The council did the counting at the fourth place and it took two days, and on Sunday morning they came down the middle road again and did not ask anybody in this holding for a figure.

Perrin Dae said that in front of the yard first, before he read anything.

Before he got to that, he read the stone, because he had been at this gate two days by now and a man who cannot stop reading figures is a man who ends up reading them out loud.

**"One thousand and three hundred and sixteen hundredweight, up eight, even morning. Four hundred and sixty-eighth of the run.**"

**"Window four hundred and thirty-five. Two hundred and twelve of that is the half which has not moved since Friday. The other, two hundred and twenty-three, went in the night. On an even morning it is the upper half that goes, and I have that right now after getting it wrong twice this week, and I am a keeper of lime and timber and I do not know what I am doing at that stone."**

Hesta Lyle came out into the middle of it with her sheet under her arm and read three figures to five people and a yard.

**"Four hundred and sixty-six at the head, four hundred and sixty-seventh over the line, four hundred and sixty-fifth under it, and nothing on the fourth,"** she said, and went back inside, and did not wait to be asked anything else, which is not rudeness and is only a woman with three figures who has been asked for them eleven times this month.

**"Everything that is in this is ours. We did not take one of these numbers off a shelf in your one room and we did not take one off the paper of the office four days north. If you think a figure is wrong, then one of five of us has counted wrong, and you are entitled to say which figure and to say it to the person who counted it."**

Then he read it, and he read all of it, and nobody in that yard stopped him.

**"The span is ninety-four feet at the crossing below the second place's branch. Sixteen bodies for twenty-two working mornings. Nine of the sixteen live at the far end of that branch and five of those nine are more than four miles from the crossing on ground with no water on it since the spring. Three cartloads of stone a day for eleven working mornings in the season, bought four days north and paid for four days north."**

He turned the sheet over.

**"What it buys. Two things, and only two things, and we have put them in this order because the first one is the reason the paper asked for it and the second one is the reason we counted it."**

Perrin Dae put a finger on the line above it and held it there.

**"One. A person can stand on the far bank of that crossing and read the gauge from there. One. And two. On the twenty-second morning of building there is no hour in which one of our five can be on the far bank and at your seventh hour gauge here. Those are the two things. There is no third thing and we did not find a third thing and we looked for one for two days."**

He read on.

He let go of the sheet and turned to the yard instead of to the paper.

Somebody at the gate said the word dry out loud, and he nodded at them over his shoulder.

**"What it does not do. It does not read that gauge correctly in a high week. It lets a person stand near it and read it more nearly. The office's own paper says the span is to take the flood load off the branch and it will not, and the man who wrote that sheet put the flood load on his own paper in his own hand, and a span at ninety-four feet is not a flood work and nobody in this yard should pretend it is."**

He tapped the line with a fingernail, once.

**"It does not put a stone back under the third course of the north sill, which came up on Wednesday. It does not put water in a dry branch, and there is a woman standing in this yard who has told us that every morning and who is not going to hear a bridge argued about it. It does not last the spring if the spring comes over it, and that is a fact about the stone and not about the wanting."**

He looked up at the sill crew while he said it.

**"And it does not get a loaded barrow to the crossing in the wet, because a mile of that road has had no water on it since the spring and a woman with a barrow would have to carry it."**

Somebody at the gate said that the stone was four cartloads a day and not three, and he had counted the cart on the third place's road twice on Tuesday.

Perrin Dae put the sheet down.

**"No. It is three. You have counted the cart that goes out with the lime on a Thursday, and the lime does not go on a Thursday, and if you want to argue a figure with me you will argue a figure I counted and not a figure you counted from the side of a road."**

The man at the gate said he had not meant anything by it.

He put one finger on the edge of the sheet and left it there while he said it.

**"I know you did not. I have said the figure and you have said a different one and I have told you why, and that is the whole of what a number is for."**

Then Emrys Dole read the part that was the cost, and he read it slowly, and about fifty people were in that yard by then and nobody was fetching anybody.

**"The cost. Sixteen bodies for twenty-two mornings, and the fifth hour is the sill, and the seventh hour is the gauge and the sluice pin, and the tenth hour is one man on a wall. All three of those are gone for twenty-two mornings. Two days of it is the north sill at the third course, which is already gone this week and which we have counted separately and which is not part of this."**

He took his hand off the edge of the sheet.

**"And the walking, which we did not put a figure on, because Perrin said a figure we cannot show the person it belongs to is a figure we do not have. The nine of them will walk four miles and more, twice a day, for twenty-two mornings. We have not named that figure and we are not going to, and I want this yard to understand that when we leave a number off a piece of paper it is because we will not put a figure on somebody else's day."**

Then Hanne Brack stood up and said the last part, and she said it to the yard and not to the five.

**"This is an arithmetic. It is not a decision and it is not a recommendation and we are not asking this yard to agree with it. We have not decided anything. We have counted. If you want to argue with it, argue with it in the morning, and any of us will stand here and be argued with, and we will not have decided anything by then either."**

Marek was standing at the middle table and had not said a word for about twenty minutes and said two now.

**"That arithmetic is right. I have looked at every figure in it and I am not going to improve any of them, and I would like it entered in the record that the man who wants the span says so before anybody does."**

Then, and only then, he said the other half.

**"I still want it. That is not a request for anything and it is not an argument, and I am a person who counts too, and my count is the same as theirs and my wanting is not."**

Harlan Vetch did not say anything about the ninety-four feet, and at about the fourth hour, coming down off the tool house end, he said two things that were not about it.

**"Eight hundred and fifty-two where it begins and eight hundred and ninety-nine where I am standing, forty-seven of masonry. That is the largest the far one has been this month and I have watched it go up a stone at a time for eleven days and there is nothing in that about anybody up the road."**

Then at the gate end, off the board, as he has now done three mornings running.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and nothing let out of it in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything. I have read that off the board three mornings this week now and I am going to stop, because a man who reads a thing off a board has stopped believing his own mouth, and I would rather say a wrong thing out loud than the right thing off a nail."**

Sera Quill came up the middle road with the slate and said two numbers and shut it.

**"One hundred and fourth and ninety-first. Thirteen apart. That is all I have got this morning."**

Somebody said that she had stopped saying the other thing about the second one.

**"I stopped on Wednesday of last week and I am not starting again because a council has come down a road,"** she said, and went back down the middle road.

The rotation came round at the seventh hour and the girl from the second place read it off the rack with Kellan Rusk standing inside the door and not coming out, because he had told her at the sixth hour that he would let her read it and had not said anything else about it.

**"A hundred and fifty-six in force,"** she said. **"Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and twenty-seven not."**

She put her thumb under the line and then took it off it and put it on the shelf edge instead.

**"The register form carries three hundred and sixty-two days. The charter carries two hundred and sixty-eight. Silling's own ruled line carries three hundred and fifty-six and has carried nothing for years, and there is a second line above it that is blank too. I do not know the reason for either. He would not give it to me and I did not ask a second time."**

She stepped out of the doorway.

**"No field was taken this morning. Nobody has been asked to choose anything this morning. I do not know what a field is and I have not asked that either."**

Auret Sill, who keeps the pumps at the second place, spent the last hour of the afternoon at the back of the tap house with her own hands on the pipe behind the weeping joint, and then came and said what she had found, and she said it to Harlan Vetch and not to the yard.

**"That joint weeps because the bed under it was laid on gravel that the mason did not take out, and I have seen the same thing at the second place four times in nine years and I have never once told anybody here, and I have not told the office either, and I am not going to be the reason a man in this yard is blamed for a bed he laid in a hurry on a day when everybody in it was in a hurry."**

She wiped her hands.

**"And I am not going to say that this makes the joint sound. It makes it explainable. There is a difference and this holding keeps confusing the two of them every single morning."**

She went back to the end of the long table and put her thumb on the corner of the nearest sheet.

Harlan Vetch looked at the back of the tap house for a while and did not say anything, and then he said the thing he says every morning and had said eleven times before.

**"The joint weeps and the pipe stands and the tap works, and it has proved nothing about any day of the week."**

He rolled the sheet once and went on holding it.

Nia Vale did the boards and the drawer and the four sheets, and did the letter, and she said them in an order that put the four sheets last.

**"The letter is a hundred and sixty-five days old and I will come to it last because there are four things in front of it that were not going to be entered today either and it seems to me that a letter is the last of those and not the first."**

She came away from the end of the long table.

**"Paid at thirty-one, nothing discharged. Four on the long table, and a council has now been in this yard twice and put an arithmetic on my table and taken it away again and neither of those did anything whatever to those four: not entered, not refused, no column cut. The drawer is shut and the key went on its nail at the sixth hour and there is nothing in it."**

Perrin Dae then said the last thing he said in this yard, standing by the gate with his own sheet rolled in his hand.

**"There is a ring of ground on the far side of the eleven acres and it is not in that arithmetic and it is not in any of the five papers we have written in two days, and it is not in it because none of the five of us has measured it and none of us has walked it, and I will not put a figure on a thing I have not stood on. I am a lime and timber keeper. I do not invent."**

He turned the sheet over once more and did not open it.

**"And there are two things I am not going to say, and I have said that in this yard once before and I am saying it again because a person says it twice before it stops sounding like a ceremony."**

He shut the rack and then stood in the doorway a moment longer than he had meant to.

Soren Rill walked the inside edge at dusk with the gate shut behind him, and stopped three times on the way, once short of the ring by the width of a man, once at the foot of the ladder without touching it, and once at the low board, where he stood and read the thing on it without picking it up and without taking it back.

**"An arithmetic is not a decision and it is not an answer and it is not a refusal, and this yard has three days of things on tables and five days of things on boards and one arithmetic, and not one of them is what anybody came down the road for. The thing on the low board has no day on it. Nobody has gone up a rung of that ladder this month. The ring is bare."**

At dusk the rack was shut by a man who shuts it the same way every evening, and what he said from the other side of the door was short, because a Sunday has already had an arithmetic in it and a yard does not need two long lists in one day.

**"Thirty-nine blanks and the rule underneath standing open at the thirty-ninth time, and a council's arithmetic read out in a yard at two o'clock did not touch it. The log stands at fifteen lines with a clean space above the fifteenth. The barrow has eleven journeys cut into its handle and no twelfth. Seven sessions have been entered in this holding's book and no eighth. Six ruled rows on the shelf. The two books on the lower shelf are fifty-three and fifty-three."**

He was quiet for a moment.

**"I have said the whole of that out loud in six separate orders this week and not one figure in it has moved, and today I have been asked by a woman from a council to count a span and I have not counted it, because it is not mine, and I would like whoever put those two facts side by side in their head to take them apart again."**

He put his back to the door and stood there.

**"And the row at the north end, and the man on it. Twenty-nine fetchings. Nobody has put a question to him at any hour today, nobody has fetched him, and he has not been part of one conversation this month. That is the fifteenth morning and there is a sixteenth tomorrow."**

The five of them went back up the middle road and the arithmetic went with them and this holding has one copy of nothing, and the council had said that it would come back and say what it costs, and nobody in that yard thanked any of them for anything, and the yard went on working.

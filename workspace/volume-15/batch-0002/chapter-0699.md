# Chapter 699

## The Twelfth Of The Nineteenth, And A Span Brought Into The Yard As Arithmetic By The Man Who Thinks It Necessary, With The Weight On It That A Loaded Barrow Puts There And Not The Weight On It In The Paper

Corin Dace came up the lane at about the eighth hour for the second time in eleven days, on foot, in his own boots, and he did not sit down, and there was nothing in his hand that looked like a favour.

He put a paper on the middle table, squared it with his thumb, and stood back from it.

**"That came down the lane on Wednesday behind the seed and the lime and it has been in a bag in the office four days north because there was nobody here to hand it to who was the engineer of record, and now there is, and I have brought it up myself rather than send it, and I am telling you that it is a line on a schedule and not a decision."**

Then he said the thing he had come up that lane to say, which was that he was not going to be in this yard long and had not come to argue about the paper.

**"There is a line on that sheet for a span at the crossing below the second place's branch. It is line nine of nine. It is not marked as done and it is not marked as refused and it is not marked as anything at all, and I am telling you exactly that much about it and no more, and if anybody wants me to say more about it they can ask me at the gate this afternoon and I will tell them the office's reason and I will not tell them whether it is a good reason."**

The girl from the second place read the stone off the copy sheet with the pencil behind her ear, because Kellan Rusk was inside with the clerk and had left the door open and the copy sheet on the step.

**"One thousand and three hundred and seven hundredweight, eight on, even morning,"** she said, **"and the four hundred and sixty-second of the run. The window is four hundred and twenty-nine. It went up, so two hundred and twenty of it is the half that moved and two hundred and eight and one of it is the half that has not moved since Friday. Boards: eight hundred and forty-six at the gate end, eight hundred and ninety-three at the far one, forty-seven between."**

She put the pencil back behind her ear and went and stood by the gate, and nobody thanked her, and nobody told her she had read it wrongly, and she had not.

Marek came down the middle road at about the ninth hour with a sheet of his own under his arm, which he had had rolled in his coat for two days and had not brought out on any of them, and he put it on the middle table next to Corin Dace's paper and did not let anybody near either of them for about an hour.

Then he read his own out loud, and he read it the way he reads a gauge board by being told what it says, which is fast, and flat, and with nothing in it for anybody to hold on to.

> Put on the middle table at the ninth hour by the engineer of record, being the arithmetic of a span at the crossing below the second place's branch, and entered in no book in this holding and on no table but this one:
>
> **Where.** The crossing below the branch, where the ground is cut rock on the far side and the cut is the only footing on the near side. Ninety-four feet of it, bank to bank, and not a stone of it in this holding to build from.
>
> **What it has to carry.** A loaded barrow at full, which is two men and a load and one wheel in the bottom. That is the whole of what will ever go across it, because nothing heavier than that goes anywhere on these four roads except a loaded cart on the third place's road in the wet, and that has been washed out twice in four years.
>
> **What the paper in the bag says it has to carry.** A flood load off the branch at the spring rise. That is not the same figure and it is not a larger one that we could argue down to ours. It is a different figure about a different channel.
>
> **Bodies.** Sixteen. Nine of them from the second place, five from this holding, two from the third place. Sixteen for twenty-two working mornings.
>
> **The season.** Eleven working mornings in it, at the rate of three cartloads of stone a day, and the stone is bought and not found.
>
> **Why I think it is necessary.** In a hard week a man cannot get from the near side to the gauge on the far side in time to matter, and on the twenty-second morning of building there is no hour in the day when somebody can be in two places. That is the whole of my case and it is a case and not a preference.

He stopped there and rolled it up and did not read it again.

Harlan Vetch read it standing up, with his hands behind his back, and the first thing he said was about the load and not about the season or the bodies.

**"The man whose stone that is has written down what the ground will hold and what a paper four days north says it will hold, and he has written them as two figures and not as one figure with a correction on it, and that is more than the last four papers that have come up this lane did."**

Then he said the other thing.

**"Nine of the sixteen are the nine who live at the far end of a branch that has had no water in it since the spring, and they are the nine who walk the furthest to get there, and not one of them will be standing on a cart on this in the wet, because there is no wet where they live and they have been carrying what they can carry up four miles of dry ground for a month."**

Somebody at the boards asked who was paying for the stone.

**"The office is buying the stone,"** Marek said. **"The office is not paying the sixteen bodies and it is not paying the eleven mornings of their walking and it is not paying the hour a man loses being on the sill so that he can be here instead. I have not written that line on that sheet because I have not been able to put a figure on it and I have not been able to put a figure on it because there is no figure, there is a day."**

Corin Dace said, from the gate, that the office's reason for the span was on the paper and that he had said he would tell it, and he told it.

**"The gauge on the far side of that crossing is on the list of five, and the list of five is what this holding has said it will maintain, and the gauge cannot be read from the near bank in a high week. That is the office's reason and it is not a stupid one, and I am going back down the lane this afternoon and I am not going to carry anybody's opinion of it up with me unless they ask me to carry it."**

Soren Rill stood at the middle table with the coat buttoned and read the five lines of the heading out from under the paper's own fold, which was the only part of it he had not expected.

**"A span at ninety-four feet, sixteen bodies, eleven mornings. And there is no fourth line and there is no space ruled at the foot of it for anybody's name, and this is the fourth sheet this month with something on it and not one of the three others has a name at the foot of it either."**

Nia Vale did the boards at about the eleventh hour and read the three ages off the rack in the doorway with the door open behind her, because she had asked and been told she might and because she can count.

**"Register form three hundred and fifty-six days, charter two hundred and sixty-two days, and a figure on the second ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and fifty and nothing written on it. I read them in that order and not in the order he reads them and they came out the same, and I have said for four years that anybody can count, and this morning is the first time anybody has let me prove it on a shelf."**

She wiped the cloth down her arm.

**"Paid at thirty-one and not discharged in thirty-one or in anything. And the drawer at this end of the wall is shut, with its key back on its nail since the sixth hour. Four sheets on the long table: nothing entered, nothing refused, no column cut."**

She turned the end sheet over with two fingers and left it face down.

**"The youngest of those four is a hundred and fifty-nine days old and the letter this holding keeps by itself is a hundred and fifty-nine days old too, and for four years I have said those are two figures, and I am saying it again today because a span of ninety-four feet came up that lane yesterday and half this yard has started hunting for numbers that agree."**

The mason from the third place, who had been allowed to stand at the gate a week ago and watch a wall being laid and had not been allowed onto the sill, read the compost line off the board at the gate end without being asked, because he was standing in front of it.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** he said. **"Not discharged. I have looked at that board every day I have stood at this gate and I have never seen a load let out of it and I am not going to be the man who starts it."**

He looked at the two papers on the middle table.

**"And I will say the one thing I came up the road for. Sixteen bodies and nine of them from a place with no water in it. I have laid stone for a span at ninety-four feet and it is not a difficult thing to lay and it is a very long thing to walk to, and those are two separate difficulties and a paper with two lines on it is the only place in the world where somebody can pretend they are one."**

Hesta Lyle came out and did the long sheet in three figures and a head, in her own order, with the door shut behind her for once.

**"Four hundred and sixty at the head, the four hundred and fifty-ninth below it, the four hundred and sixty-first above it, and the fourth not due. And I will tell you the other thing in the words I use for it at home, which is that there is a place on another sheet in this yard where somebody ruled a column and wrote no heading over it, and a second place where somebody ruled four lines under two words and stopped there. Neither of those has been touched by ninety-four feet of stone arriving on Saturday."**

At about the sixth hour, standing at the middle table with both papers still lying on it, Marek said the thing he had been not saying all day, out loud, so that nobody would be waiting for it.

**"There is something I am not going to say about that span this month and I am telling this yard I am not going to say it, and I would rather be told I was wrong to stop than told to start."**

Somebody said that was not much of a thing to say out loud.

**"It is not much of a thing to say out loud and it is the whole of what I have got,"** he said.

At dusk the two papers were still on the middle table and were on the long table and were in the rack, and the standing came out of the one room in the voice of a man who is not reciting anything, because two papers have been sitting on that table for four hours and a bookkeeper who recites on top of that has stopped being a bookkeeper.

He leaned against the frame of the door with the book shut under his arm.

**"Two sheets of paper have been on that table all day. Not one figure has entered this holding's book out of either of them. One is ninety-four feet long and one is a line numbered nine of nine, and the second one is not even ours, and both of them are still lying there."**

He put the two sheets of paper back square where they had lain all day.

**"Six ruled rows on the shelf and no seventh cut for a bridge, seven sessions and no eighth, fifteen lines in the use log and a clean space above the fifteenth, and a barrow with eleven journeys cut in its handle. The blanks are thirty-nine and the rule under them is open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. Fifty-three and fifty-three on the two lower books and no day yet in my life when either of them held the other's list."**

The lock went over at dusk and nobody in that yard counted it.

**"And the old man at the end of the north row is at twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, nothing asked of him at any hour, and I have not asked him whether he thinks a bridge is necessary because I am not going to put that to a man of seventy in a yard and I would like anybody in this holding who wants to do that to come and do it in front of me."**

The yard went on working. There was a stone cut on a piece of paper at the middle table that said ninety-four feet and eleven mornings and nine bodies from a place with no water in it, and nobody in this holding had been asked to choose anything, and the woman who had counted three ages in a doorway went back to the boards.

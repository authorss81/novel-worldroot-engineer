# Chapter 702

## The Fifteenth Of The Nineteenth, And The Seed Route Put In Front Of This Yard As Work And Not As A Kindness, With Its Terms Written In The Hand Of The Woman Who Keeps It And Improved By Nobody

Tova Reed was in the seed store before light and was still in it at the eighth hour, and the store is a stone room with one door and a bench down each side and about eleven sacks on the left and four on the right, and the sorting board is the bench nearest the door.

She had put herself at the end of that bench nearest the door before anybody came in, which is where the light is and which is also the only place in that room from which every mouth in the room can be seen, and she did not do it in front of anybody and she did not mention having done it.

At about the sixth hour the fourth place's man came down with a boy and said the figures off the stone to her while he wrote them on the copy sheet, and she wrote them on the corner of the seed board in a lead pencil while she had the last of the autumn tallying in front of her, and she wrote them without asking him to say one of them twice.

**"One thousand and three hundred and five hundredweight, and five off it, because this is an odd morning and the odd ones go light. That is the four hundred and sixty-fifth of the run.**"

**"The window is four hundred and thirty-two. Two hundred and eleven of it is the half that fell in the night and it is the lower half; two hundred and twenty-one is the upper and it has not budged since Wednesday. Eight hundred and forty-nine at the gate end of your wall, eight hundred and ninety-six at the top of it."**

She put the pencil down on the board.

**"I have written those because you said them, and I have not asked you to say any of them again, and I have not asked the next person to say them either, and if you have come to look for something in my face about that then you have come for the wrong thing and I would rather you went and laid some stone."**

He said that he had not and went and laid some stone.

The seed route is four days of walking a month and it is not a thing anybody in this holding can carry out alone, and at about the eighth hour she said it out loud to about nine people standing in her own doorway, and she said it in the order of a woman who has been doing it since the spring and not in the order of anybody else's paper.

**"First, because it is the thing that is actually wrong. Eleven sacks on the left and four on the right, and the fourth one on the right is damp at the bottom corner, and it is not opened and it is not spread and I have not moved it."**

She turned round and looked at it and came back and did not touch it.

**"Second. Two cart days a week and never the third, because on the third there is a cart going the other way and it takes four hours to turn one round, and I have lost a week twice doing it."**

She moved the sacks on the left along to make room and went back to the board.

She turned the bench-cloth over out of the way and put it under the sort.

She counted the people in the doorway, which took longer than the count needed to take.

**"Third. The branch end of the route is walked once a month and not more often than that, and I walk it, and nobody else has ever walked it and nobody has asked to and nobody is going to be asked."**

She moved one sack on the right along the bench to make room and put her hand back.

**"Fourth, and this is the one people argue with. A person who comes to this door and says a thing to me writes it on the board first, if they want it written down, and I do not ask them to say it again. That is not me being careful. That is me not being able to afford to be wrong twice."**

She looked at the nine in the doorway again and did not find the one she was looking for.

**"Fifth, and I am going to say this one twice because I want it written down twice in two places. The seed work is mine. It is not a favour to me and it is not a debt and nobody in this yard is thanked for it, including me, and I am not to be asked to give it up and I am not to be asked to keep it as though it were a favour."**

She said the fifth one again, word for word the same, and the nine people in the doorway did not improve it and nobody asked her whether she was sure.

She did not take any of it to the middle table, and when Marek came in at about the ninth hour and stood at the bench while she told him the first and the second of them again he said the one thing about it.

**"That is not going up the lane and that is not going on your sheet with the ninety-four feet on it, and the two of those are not the same document and nobody in this yard has asked me to make them the same document."**

He said that he had not asked her to and that he was not going to.

Odile Vray came to the store door at about the ninth hour with her sleeve still wet from the sill and she said the thing she had come to say and she said it in the order of a woman presenting a bill she had already written.

She put her hand flat on the boards to count on and found she did not need to.

**"Two days. That is what the sill cost and it is going to cost, and here it is in the figures of the five of us and not in anybody else's."**

She put her hand out flat, not touching anything.

**"The sluice is shut two days. Two days is two mornings of the seventh hour in which nobody reads the gauge and nobody counts the pin, and the gauge does not care and the water does not care and the silt does care, because silt comes in whenever the sluice is shut and it came in four feet deep on Wednesday and it will be closer to five by the time we have the stone back in."**

She held up two fingers and then put them down again.

**"And the fifth hour is gone for two mornings and the tenth hour is gone for two mornings and there is one man in this holding who does the tenth hour. That is two mornings of wall unlaid at the top and four courses unlaid at the bottom and I have put all of that in this yard's arithmetic and not one figure of it is on a wall or a sack in a box."**

Somebody at the gate asked whether the span would have fixed it.

**"The span is four miles away and it is a bridge and this is a sill and I am going to say the rest of this once. Whether there is a bridge at the crossing does not put a stone back under a lifted course at the north end. That is not a defence of anything and I am not making one."**

Then she said it the way a person says the thing they have thought about for two days.

**"And the fourth house, the mile and a half one, asked on Saturday what it was being asked for and in which month and I told it. On Wednesday I took a lamp to that house myself and I told it that the sill face was lifted at the third course and that the fifth hour was gone for two mornings. I did not send the boy. I went. And I want it written in my own words that I went, because in a month somebody will say the second place was not told, and I was told and I told them, and the cost of going there was mine and it was two hours of a Thursday I would rather have had at the face of that sill."**

Nobody in that yard thanked her for going.

Tova Reed kept the seed work. It was hers at the eighth hour and it was hers at the seventh hour of the evening, and nobody gave it to her and nobody took it from her and nobody in any room said one word to her about the way she works or asked her to do it a different way, and no hand went on her arm all day, and at about the fourth hour she wrote the two figures on the board at the north end because Kellan Rusk came and stood beside her and put the chalk in her hand and did not say a single word about it, and she wrote them and gave the chalk back and went back to the bench.

**"Two hundred and three of three hundred and eighty-nine,"** she said to the board, and then to nobody, and then she was at the seed store bench again with a lead pencil and eleven sacks on the left and four on the right.

The mason from the third place did the boards at about the second hour of the afternoon because Nia Vale was at the long table and he has learned this month which two figures are his to read.

**"Eight hundred and forty-nine where it begins and eight hundred and ninety-six where the wall ends. Forty-seven of masonry, which I have now counted in three places this month and got the same answer in all three and I am going to stop counting it."**

He read the sheet again from the top before he came to the second half of it.

Nia Vale did the drawer and the tap house and said the fourth place's standing from inside the one room with the door open.

**"The key went on its nail at the sixth hour and it will be on it at the sixth hour tomorrow and there is nothing in that drawer. The joint at the back of the tap house weeps at about the second hour every morning and the pipe behind it is standing and has not moved and I have stopped looking at it, which is not the same as saying it is fine."**

Soren Rill did the long sheet and the low board in one walk, in the order of a man who has two things to check and does not like either of them.

**"Four hundred and sixty-three at the head, four hundred and sixty-second below it, four hundred and sixty-fourth above it, fourth not due. And in the plainest words I have got: one column ruled and empty and nobody has ever written a heading over it, and four lines ruled under a pair of words with nothing on them, and both of them still like that after a fortnight of walls and councils and seed sacks."**

He read the second half of the sheet again before he gave it away.

**"The woman at the seed store said five things in her own doorway this morning to nine people and not one of the five was a term, and I would like somebody in this yard to say that out loud today."**

He stopped at the low board.

**"The thing on the low board has no day on it and has not been picked up and has not been taken back, and I have walked past it every morning this month, and I have not been asked about it and I have not asked about it. That is the ninth morning and I am not going to make it a tenth."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the third hour and did the ring and the ladder and the compost line, all three, in the order of a man coming off a wall.

**"There is a ring of ground on the far side of the eleven acres and I have laid stone for twenty-six years and I will tell this yard the plain thing about it. I have not walked it. I have not put a tape on it. I have not put a price on it. And I have not told this yard that not walking it is a virtue, because it is not one, it is a thing that has not been done."**

He stopped at the ladder.

**"Nobody has gone up a rung of that ladder this month and there is no figure against either of the two people it belongs to, and if a number ever goes against one of them I would want that person to have said it themselves."**

Then at the gate end, from the board, out loud, for the first time this month:

**"Paid at thirty-one. Nothing has gone out of that line, not at thirty-one and not at thirty, and I have been saying the same nine words about it for eleven mornings and this morning I finally went and read them off the board and the board says what I have been saying."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack at about the seventh hour and read them in the order of the questions people have asked him about them this month, which he says out loud and regrets immediately.

**"Register form three hundred and fifty-nine days, because that is the one they ask about. Charter two hundred and sixty-five. Second ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and fifty-three with nothing written on it, because that is the one that makes people ask whether I have made a mistake. I have not made a mistake and I have stopped saying that, and if you want to know why there is nothing on it then that is a question about a man who ruled it and he is not in this yard."**

Then, with his back to the store door where somebody had said five things out loud and nobody had improved them:

**"The letter is a hundred and sixty-two days. I have offered this yard the difference between that and the four on the long table three times this month, and nobody has ever picked it up, and from tonight I am putting it down on the table in words and leaving it there for anybody to walk past."**

At about the sixth hour in the evening, with the store door shut and the light behind her and about nine people in the room, Tova Reed said the thing she had come in to say and had said nothing else about all day.

**"There are two things I am not going to say in this yard and I am telling all of you that I am not going to say them, and one of the people in this room is my brother and he has not asked me and I am not going to tell him first, and neither of those is about the seed and neither of them is a complaint and I would like the room to go back to what it was doing."**

At dusk the one room was shut and the door stood open about a hand's width, and what came out of the gap was about half of what usually comes out of it.

**"Thirty-nine blanks. The rule underneath them stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. Five things said out loud in a seed store doorway this morning did not put a fortieth blank on that board and did not come into this room and is not going out of it."**

He looked along the shelf.

**"And that is what I have for you tonight. The rest of the standing has not moved this week and I am not going to say a list of things that have not moved, because the day a bookkeeper starts doing that is the day the things that have moved stop being heard. There is a sill at the north end with two courses out of it and there is a span on a piece of paper and a council that went up the road. Say those three at your dinner. Leave the rest of it alone."**

Nobody locked anything on the way past.

**"The man at the end of the north row: twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, not asked anything at any hour of this day. Nobody in this yard has asked him whether his seed is dry. I have not asked him and I would like it written down somewhere that nobody asked him, because in a month somebody will want to know who was careful."**

The seed route had been said out loud in a doorway by the woman who keeps it and had not been improved, and there were eleven sacks and four, and the fourth was damp at the bottom corner and was not opened, and the sill at the north end had two courses back in and a bar across it, and the yard went on working.

# Chapter 700

## The Thirteenth Of The Nineteenth, And A Price Put In The Yard By The People Who Will Be Paying It, In Their Own Figures, And Nobody Argued About It

Odile Vray did not sleep and she did not say so, and at about the sixth hour she was at the boards with the second seed sack and a stub of charcoal and she was still in the box.

**"I have been asked what a span at ninety-four feet would do to me and I have been asked it three times since the paper came out and I am going to answer it once, in numbers, and then I am not going to answer it again this week to anybody."**

She turned the sack over and wrote while she talked, which she has always done and which has been complained about.

**"Twenty-two working mornings. Four bodies. That is eighty-eight bodies and mornings of work, and it comes out of the fifth hour, because the fifth hour is the sill and I am the sill."**

She held the sack up so that somebody at the gate could see the writing, which she had not been asked to do and did.

**"The fifth hour is mine. That is eighty-eight hours off the sill face and the sluice and the chain, and if I do not do the sill face in the morning then it is done in the evening or it is done tomorrow or it is not done in this weather at all. That is the first figure and it is the whole of the first cost."**

She put the sack down and took up the other one.

**"The seventh hour is the gauge, and the gauge is read out loud by a woman standing at it, and that woman is one of my four, because there is nobody in this yard of four hundred people who is not one of my four. If she is on the stone at the crossing then the gauge is read by nobody at the seventh hour and nobody counts the sluice pin, and a count that does not carry is a count that did not happen, and that is not my sentence, that is the yard's."**

Nobody at the gate argued with her about any of it, which is what made it worth saying.

**"The tenth hour is the wall at the tool house end and that is one man. One man, twenty-two mornings, and a wall that stands where it is going to stand, and a man does not lay a wall and hold a trowel and read a gauge and go home. I have written the fourth line and the fourth line is that the tenth hour is not being done at all for twenty-two mornings."**

Somebody asked her what the sill would look like in three weeks.

**"Three weeks is four feet of silt on the sill face, and it can be scraped in the wet and it cannot be scraped in the dry, and it is going to be dry,"** she said. **"That is the fourth figure and it is a figure and not a complaint. And I have not put the walking on it and I am not going to, because the walking does not have a number that anybody would believe."**

Then she said the part nobody had asked her for.

**"Now the other half of it, which is the half the paper says nothing about. The office is buying the stone. That is money and it is real and it comes from four days north. Nobody in this holding is going to touch that money and it is not going to cost any of us anything but the looking at it. There is not one hour in that paper for a body and not one hour of walking in it and not one foot of silt in it, and the paper is not a lie and the man who wrote it is not a liar."**

She put the chalk down.

**"That is the whole of the arithmetic. The office is paying in stone and we are paying in mornings, and both of those are true on the same morning and nobody has to choose between them, and I would like it noticed that that is the first time this month that two things in this yard have both been true without anybody having to be wrong."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the tool house end at about the seventh hour and read the stone without stopping, in the order of a man who is one body and knows it.

**"One thousand and three hundred and two hundredweight, and it came down five lighter because today is an odd morning. The four hundred and sixty-third of the run.**"

**"Window four hundred and thirty. The half that fell in the night is the falling one, at two hundred and ten. The other half, two hundred and twenty, is where it has been since Friday morning and it will be there Monday morning."**

He shifted the hod on his shoulder.

**"The upper half moves on an even morning and the lower half moves on an odd one. I have got it right now and I have got it right this morning out loud and I would like it noticed that I got it wrong twice this week in public and that a man who has been wrong twice in public is worth listening to on the third day, because he now knows that he does not know."**

Somebody at the gate said that, and Harlan Vetch said that was the first sensible thing anybody had said about figures in this yard all week, and then went back up.

The girl from the second place wrote the two figures on the board at about the seventh hour without being asked, which is what she does on every odd morning now, and she wrote them under the heading, which is of a month nine months back and has not moved.

**"Two hundred and two of three hundred and eighty-seven,"** she said, to the board, and stepped back and did not read them out.

Kellan Rusk came and stood beside the board for about as long as it takes to look at two figures and did not say that she had got it right, and she did not ask him, and that is how that arrangement is made and it is not made on purpose.

Then Renn Ashby came up the north row at about the eighth hour to fill a bucket at the trough and walked past the board at about four feet from it and did not turn his head.

Nobody said anything to him about it and nobody said anything to anybody about him not turning his head.

Soren Rill read the long sheet in the yard at about the ninth hour and read it as a man reading somebody else's letter, which is not a thing he is and everybody in that yard knows he is not.

**"Four hundred and sixty-one at the head, four hundred and sixtieth under the line and four hundred and sixty-second over it, and the fourth not due. Three figures under a head of four hundred. I did not learn those three this week and I am not going to pretend that a span changed them, because a span changes the fifth hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour and it does not change one figure on that sheet by so much as the width of a nib."**

Nia Vale came along the wall at about the second hour of the afternoon and did the boards and the compost line and the four sheets in the order of a woman walking her own wall.

**"Eight hundred and forty-seven where it begins and eight hundred and ninety-four where Harlan is standing, forty-seven of masonry, forty-seven of masonry this morning and forty-seven of masonry every morning and it is not going to be anything else while I am standing here."**

She stopped at the gate end.

**"Paid at thirty-one and not discharged in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything, and a span at the crossing below the branch is not going to put a load in that line or take one out of it, and I would like whoever priced the span in stone this morning to know that the compost line is the one place in this yard where the work arrives and nothing goes away, and that is not a figure and it is not a complaint either."**

She turned round to the long table.

**"Four sheets, and the nearest of the four is a hundred and sixty days old this morning, and the letter this holding keeps on its own is a hundred and sixty days old, and I know that I have just put those two figures side by side in a yard where anybody could hear me and I would do it again, because they are two figures and they have been two figures for four years and somebody is always going to try to make them one."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack at about the third hour, and he read them the way a man reads three things off a shelf when he wants them out of the way before he says the thing he came out to say.

**"The shelf in this room is built in an order and I have finally stopped reading it in any other. The register form sits nearest the door at three hundred and fifty-seven days. The charter is behind it at two hundred and sixty-three. Silling's own ruled line is on the top board at three hundred and fifty-one and there is nothing on it. That is the order of the wood, not the order I prefer, and in nine years I have never found a reason to prefer the other."**

Then, with his thumb where the thumb goes, and in the order of a man who has counted a thing and is not going to be thanked for counting it:

**"Now the other thing. There is a man at the end of the north row who has been fetched twenty-nine times and has not been fetched since the days turned and is not going to be, and what he would think about a span at ninety-four feet is worth exactly nothing to this argument and I have kept it out of this argument and I am going to keep it out."**

Then Odile Vray, from the sill, to nobody in particular, in a voice that carried because the yard is made of stone and it was still:

**"There are two things I am not going to say and I am telling this yard that I am not going to say them, and I am also telling this yard that when I stop I am not going to come and tell him first, and neither of those two things is a complaint and neither of them is about the span."**

Harlan Vetch came down at about the fourth hour and said the one thing he says every day about the one thing he has said every day, and the yard did not stop for it.

**"The joint at the back of the tap house weeps and the pipe behind it is standing where it was standing, and neither of them has said anything about the span either, and I have watched four days of this month go past with nobody in this yard reading anything into anything, and I am not going to be the man who starts reading things into things."**

The mason from the third place stood at the edge of the ring of bare ground at about the sixth hour and looked at it and did not step into it, and said the thing that made him stay there rather than walk on.

**"Six days I have stood at this gate. On the far side of the eleven acres there is a piece of ground and I have not set a foot on it, and neither has anybody else from this yard, and no tape has gone across it and no sum of money has been attached to it in front of me. I have laid for three councils and I will tell you the plain thing about it. An unmeasured thing is not an innocent thing. It is a decision somebody has not taken yet and has dressed as ground."**

He put both hands on the gate and stayed on them.

**"That is not an attack and nobody asked me for it."**

He looked at Soren Rill.

**"I am saying it because you have been out there twice this week and you have not put a tape on it either, and I would rather say it to your face than go up the road with it."**

Soren Rill said that he had not put a tape on it and was not going to, and that the man from the third place was welcome to say so again at any hour of any morning, and that he had a ladder against the tool house wall with no rung on it climbed this month and no figure against either of the two people it belongs to.

Harlan Vetch put the bar against the tool house wall on his way past and said the two figures that are his own.

**"That barrow behind the wall has eleven journeys marked into the handle. I have been the man who pushes it for eleven days and it is eleven and not twelve and I have stopped looking at the eleventh as though it were a challenge, and I would like it recorded that a thing you have looked at a hundred times can still surprise you, and that the surprise is almost always finding out that it is the same.**"

At dusk the boy from the fourth household came down the middle road again, and there was no cart that day, and he stood at the gate for a while and then went back up it without giving anybody anything, and nobody stopped him.

Kellan Rusk turned the rack key and said the standing of it from inside the room, and he said it in the order of the shelves rather than the order of the yard, and nobody has ever asked him to do it that way and nobody is going to.

**"The board in this room has not been nearer to a fortieth blank this week than it was on the first day of it. It stands at thirty-nine, and the rule under those blanks is open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and a bridge, a wall, a council and a book coming round off a shelf have all now been offered to that board and none of them was taken. Fifteen lines in the use log, and the barrow is eleven, and there are seven sessions and six ruled rows, and the two lower books are fifty-three and fifty-three."**

He stood in the doorway a while with the door open behind him.

**"And the old man at the end of the north row is at twenty-nine fetchings and nothing was asked of him at any hour today, and it is the thirteenth morning of this month and I have said that thirteen times and I will say it on every morning there is."**

The price of the span was standing in the yard in a woman's own figures on the back of a sack in a box that nobody else has a key to, it was not entered in any book, it was not argued with by anybody including the man who wrote the paper it came out of, and the sill face was two hands into four feet of the summer's silt and would be four feet deeper by the end of the month, and the yard went on working.

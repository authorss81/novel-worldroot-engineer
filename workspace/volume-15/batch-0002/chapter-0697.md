# Chapter 697

## The Tenth Of The Nineteenth, And Two Things Coming Round On One Morning Neither Of Them Waiting For The Other, And A Question About The Return That Nobody In This Yard Can Improve The Answer To

The yard knew it was a return morning before anybody said so, the way it knew when the frost was going to lift, and nobody could have said how. There is no mark on the gate for it and no figure anywhere in this holding that turns on it. It is simply that twice in nine days a certain thing happens, and there are four hundred people who have been here longer than the book has been open, and they can feel it the way a dog knows the hour.

The sill crew were on the stones before the light came off them, which is a thing they do because they do it and not because anybody posted a notice, and Marek came down the north end afterwards as he does.

Odile Vray had cut the day into four before light again, on the same seed sack, in the same charcoal, and the four were the same four because they were the same four every morning and the only thing that changes is the number of bodies, and this morning the number of bodies was five.

**"Five on the sill face and the sluice and my chain, and I have written the fifth line again because I write it every morning, and I am not going to explain to anybody why a woman who does arithmetic on the back of a sack keeps doing it."**

Then she put the sack in her own box and closed the box and the box is hers and has been hers for two years.

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room at about the sixth hour with the book under his arm and stopped on the step, because the yard had stopped.

**"Two things,"** he said. **"The book comes round this morning and the rotation comes round this morning, and I said on Friday that I had never in nine years had a morning with both of those on it, and I have now had one, and it turns out that what it feels like is an argument with a man about a shelf."**

The girl from the second place was at the gate with her bundle under her arm and she asked him the question, and she asked it flatly and in front of four people, which she had not planned.

**"What is a return for?"**

He did not answer at once and he did not go inside to look it up, and that was the part that later made people think he had known.

**"It moves no figure,"** he said. **"That is the whole of it. Thirty-nine blanks on that board and the rule under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the number has been thirty-nine the whole four years and it is going to be thirty-nine when I am dead. No ordinal goes with it. No weight, no allowance, nothing anybody has to give anybody. You can have a day that turns up on a calendar nine times in a season and does not move a single figure in a holding of four hundred people, and I have never found anybody who can tell me why, and neither can the office, and I asked Corin Dace on the second day of this month and he said he would find out and he came back without finding out."**

She thought about that for a moment.

**"And it is still a morning?"**

**"It is a morning,"** he said. **"It is the one morning in nine where the book comes back to the shelf on its own and nobody has to be nagged. That is not a good enough answer for a person from the second place and I am not going to improve it at this gate, and if you take it up the lane and somebody up there tells you a better one, come back and tell me and I will stand here and hear it."**

She said that she would, and she went back to the boards and did not come back about it, and that is the whole of what she did with the question.

Then the rotation came round, and it did not wait for the return, and the return did not wait for it, and the two of them went past each other at the same step of the same morning like two men carrying different lengths of timber through one doorway.

**"A hundred and fifty-four in force,"** Kellan Rusk said. **"One on. One on is the entire of what this morning has done to that book, and it has not gone on by two on any morning of my life in this yard and it is not going to start this month."**

He kept a finger where the finger goes and read the rest of it with his eyes instead.

**"Twenty-nine of those went out this morning at whatever hour belongs to them, and the figure has not moved since before any of the five of us came here. The rest, which is a hundred and twenty-five of them, are still on the books at this hour and will be on them at the sixth hour."**

Somebody at the boards said that a return morning was a good morning to take a field.

**"No field was taken this morning and none was taken on the morning the days turned either, and I have said it in this yard more times this month than I have said anything else, and I am saying it again on the morning when it would be easiest to stop saying it, because a man who waits nine days for a quiet morning has got the wrong idea of what a quiet morning is for."**

Sera Quill came up the middle road with her arm against the slate and set it down on the boards at the north end and opened it at the line her thumb finds without looking.

**"One hundred and second and eighty-ninth. Thirteen apart and they have never been anything else apart. The one that is over a hundred carries its ending on its tail, because it is not round, and eighty-nine is not round either, and I have got no better thing to say about either of them than that."**

Then she put her hand flat on the lower line and left it there, which she has done on every fourth-line morning since the days turned.

**"The eighty-ninth is the one I carry. It is not in the book in the rack and it is not on the wall and it is not on the four sheets on the long table and it is not on anything a person could hold up. A figure that is carried is not a figure that has been published, and I say that in a yard because a yard is the only place I have ever said anything."**

The fourth place's man read the stone at about the seventh hour and read it in a new order, which he had not tried before, and nobody in the yard could have told you what the order was until he had finished it.

**"Four hundred and sixtieth of the run,"** he said first. **"That is where it has got to, and a man who asks me how much came down gets the answer to that question and not the other one. Now the other one."**

He wiped under the line.

**"One thousand and three hundred and four hundredweight, eight heavier than yesterday, and I am not going to remark on that at all because nobody in this yard asked me what it means and I do not know. The window is four hundred and twenty-seven and it is made of two hundred and nineteen, which is the half that shifts on an even morning, and two hundred and eight, which did not shift this morning because this morning is an even one.**"

He ran a cloth along the underside of it.

**"I have had that backwards in public twice in a week and I would rather say it wrong out loud here than say it right on a piece of paper and wrong out loud."**

He put his hand flat on the stone above the line.

**"That stone has stood here longer than the wall and it has never once answered anybody. It came down heavier this week every time it was an even morning and lighter every time it was an odd one, and that is what it has done since before any of us stood here, and nobody is going to be told what it means this morning."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the tool house end at about the same hour and said the wall figures without stopping, because he had eight hundred and forty-four at the gate end of his own wall and there was no reason to stop for arithmetic.

**"Eight hundred and forty-four at the end this wall begins at, and eight hundred and ninety-one at the end I am standing at, and forty-seven of masonry in between, which has been the whole of the difference between those two ends since the morning they were cut and I have stopped finding that interesting and stopped finding it dull."**

He shifted the hod to his other shoulder and looked at the near end of the wall while he said it.

**"Paid at thirty-one. Not let out in thirty-one, and not let out in thirty, and not let out in anything at all that I have been in this yard for. I said it on Friday and I would like it struck out of my mouth by whoever wrote the sentence."**

He went back up.

Hesta Lyle came out of the one room at about the eighth hour and did the long sheet in the order of a woman who has four things and one of them is late.

She turned the sheet round on the table and squared it again for no reason at all.

**"Four hundred and fifty-eight at the head. Under the line, the four hundred and fifty-seventh. Over the line, the four hundred and fifty-ninth. The fourth is not due and I did not enter it, because a man came up a road this morning and a road is not a reason."**

She put the pen cap back on and then took it off again.

**"And while I am standing here with the pen out: a third column, ruled, empty, under a heading nobody has improved. Four ruled lines under two words, and every one of them bare. I have watched a yard put a great deal on a wall this month and not one of those two has had a mark on it, and I would not be surprised if they were still bare in four years."**

She set the pen down beside the sheet and left it there.

She put her hip against the edge of the table and waited.

**"And I am not going to rule a fifth line under either of those two places, and I have wanted to since the day the days turned, and a woman who wants to rule a line under a blank is a woman who has stopped believing that the blank is the point."**

She left her hand on the corner of the sheet while she said it.

**"Three figures under a head of four hundred and the fourth not due, and a book came round off a shelf this morning and a book does not improve a heading."**

Then the thing that made the morning, which was not a figure and was not on anybody's sheet.

A boy came on foot out of the second place at about the ninth hour and stood at the gate for a while before he asked anybody for anything.

He wanted to know what the four households with two signatures at the foot of them were being asked for, and by whom, and in which month.

Odile Vray answered him from the boards without stopping her own work, and she answered him in the order of a woman who has been counting four things for a week.

**"You are the fourth person to ask me that this month and I am going to answer you the same way I answered the other three, which is that the answer differs by road and not by opinion."**

She turned the seed sack over and put her thumb on it.

**"There are four of you households and I will not call them four to you because four is a number and what I am about to say is not a number. One of the four is a mile and a half up the middle road and I have been in its kitchen twice this week before light. One is nine miles and comes up on a cart day. One is four miles and can only be reached on the day there is a cart going that way, which is not every day and was twice this week. And one is at the far end of the branch where there is no water at all now and has not been since the reach went out as far as it went, and there is no cart that goes there and I have not been."**

The boy said that this was not fair on the four of them.

**"It is not fair on the four of them,"** Odile Vray said. **"I have not said it was fair. I have said where they are. Fairness is a thing a room gives out and a road does not, and if the four of you want the same thing said to you on the same morning then somebody is going to have to build four more miles and nobody in this yard has the stone for it."**

The boy stood there with the message he had come up with and did not give it to anybody, and then he gave it to the girl from the second place and went back down the middle road.

Nia Vale did the gate line and the wall figures and the compost line at about the third hour of the afternoon, and she did the drawer behind the near end at the sixth hour the way she has done it at the sixth hour for four years.

**"The drawer is shut and the key went on its nail at the sixth hour and that is where a key lives in this holding and it is not a figure anybody can check, so I have checked it myself every afternoon for four years. Nothing on the long table has been entered this morning. Nothing on it has been refused this morning. No column has been cut under anything this morning, and the refusal that came up that lane eight mornings ago is a woman writing on her own paper and it was never going to be one of these four and it is not going to be one of them now."**

She folded the cloth once and put it over her shoulder.

**"A book came round onto that shelf this morning and I have watched it for nine days and it has not put a mark on one of those four sheets and it is not going to. The letter this holding keeps by itself is a hundred and fifty-seven days, and it will be that at the end of the month as well. If anybody here wants me to say that in a different voice, they can find me at the sixth hour and ask me twice."**

**"Charter first, two hundred and sixty days. Register form next, three hundred and fifty-four. And the ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and forty-eight, empty, with nothing written over the word either. Three people have asked me this month whether that line is a slip of mine. It is not mine. I was not in this room when it was ruled."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack at about the sixth hour, standing with his back to the yard because the wind was behind it, and he read them in an order nobody in this yard had heard him use.

**"Three figures, said the same way ten mornings in a row now, and I have no intention of improving them. What I would like is for somebody in this holding who is good at a sum to notice that none of the three has anything to do with four households and a road."**

He went back inside with the book open in his hand.

He put his thumb under the second line once more, out of nine years of habit.

Then, from inside the doorway, in the voice he uses when he has already said the thing he came out to say and is saying the other one:

**"Seven sessions in this holding's book and no eighth, and that has not moved in four years. Six ruled rows on the shelf, and nobody has cut a seventh for anything that came up this month, and a boy came up a road with a question instead of a sheet, which is not the same thing and is not a worse thing either."**

He looked out at the middle road and then back at the door.

**"And a man on the north row whose figure is twenty-nine fetchings, which is a thing I go on saying because the figure is the standing and the standing is the only part of it I am certain of."**

He said the rest of it with his thumb still under the second line, and nobody at the boards came to ask him for it again.

**"He has not been fetched. Nothing has been asked of him at any hour today, and nothing has been asked of him about the book coming back off its shelf either, because a thing that turns up every ninth day is not a thing a man of seventy is asked about."**

Soren Rill walked the inside edge of the eleven acres at dusk with his hands behind his back, and stopped where he always stops, which is short of the ring by the width of a man, short of the ladder by a yard, and short of the low board by nothing at all.

**"I have two things I am keeping behind my teeth and I am announcing that here so that nobody stands at this gate tomorrow with an ear cocked for them. You will not get them off me and you will not get them off anybody else in this yard either, and I would rather say that than have anybody spend a morning listening for them."**

He looked at the ladder.

**"Nobody has gone up a rung of it this month and no figure is against either of the two people it belongs to, and if a number ever does go against one of them I would want it to be a number that person said out loud themselves on a morning when they meant it. And the ring is still bare and still unwalked and I have still got no tape on it."**

At dusk the four households with two signatures were four and the middle road was four miles long in one direction and nine in the other and neither distance had changed, and the woman at the far end of the branch had no water and was not asked about it and was not reached, and there was a book on a shelf in the one room that had come round on its own for the second time in nine days and had moved nothing, and the yard went on working.

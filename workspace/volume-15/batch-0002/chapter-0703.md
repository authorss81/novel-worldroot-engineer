# Chapter 703

## The Sixteenth Of The Nineteenth, And Five People Who Came Down The Middle Road To Sit In A Yard That Did Not Invite Them, Named In Their Own Hand On Their Own Paper And Entered In Nothing Here

They came down the middle road together at about the tenth hour, five of them, and none of them was fetched and none of them had asked to be fetched, and the yard watched them come up it the way this yard watches anything that arrives from outside it, which is to say without stopping.

Ismay Rooke came first because he was already at the gate, and he had been at that gate for eight days watching a wall being laid and had not been allowed onto the sill and had not asked.

The other four were Auret Sill, who keeps the pumps at the second place, and Hanne Brack, of the second place, whose household is at the end of the branch, and Emrys Dole, who carries on the third place's road, and Perrin Dae, who keeps the lime and the timber at the fourth place.

That is the whole of it. Five people, five places, and no two of them from the same place, and none of them from this holding.

Somebody at the boards said that it was a small thing to bring five people down one road to sit in a yard.

**"It is five and not nine and not three,"** Hanne Brack said. **"That is the standing and there has never been any other. Nine is a body you can talk for a whole afternoon. Three is a body where one person is carrying it, and everybody in the room knows which one."**

She said the next part standing, in the yard, to about thirty people, and she did not raise her voice.

**"We keep no book. We have no clerk and no clerk has ever written anything we said. We decide by agreement and not by vote and we have never taken a vote, and that is not a virtue, it is the reason we can say no at all, because a body that votes can always be outvoted by whoever turned up, and we do not want that. We met eleven times in four years. Nine of them decided nothing. One of them broke up without a decision. One of them cost somebody a road."**

Somebody asked which one cost somebody a road.

**"That was the second time we were asked to put our names to a thing the office wanted done in this district, and we did not, and the third place's road did not get its culvert that winter, and the third place has not forgotten it and we have not asked them to,"** Emrys Dole said. **"So we are not a good thing that has happened to you. We are a thing that has already cost a neighbour something and has come back anyway."**

Perrin Dae put a folded sheet on the middle table and opened it and squared it with the side of his hand and did not put it in the rack and did not ask anybody to.

> Written out at the fourth place on the morning of the fifteenth of this month and carried down the middle road on the sixteenth, being the council's own list of itself, and kept by the council and entered in no book and no rack and no table in this holding:
>
> **Ismay Rooke.** Third place. Mason. Twenty-six years on stone and eleven of them on a council road.
>
> **Auret Sill.** Second place. Pumps.
>
> **Hanne Brack.** Second place. No water at the end of her branch since the spring.
>
> **Emrys Dole.** Third place. Carries on the road.
>
> **Perrin Dae.** Fourth place. Lime and timber.
>
> **Not on this paper.** The engineer of record. The bookkeeper of this holding. The seed keeper. Nobody who is paid by an office four days north.
>
> **Agreed.** We do not vote and we do not write.

Emrys Dole read the two launders off the copy sheet that Corin Dace had left on the step on Saturday, standing at the gate with the other four behind him, and then Ismay Rooke read the rest, and neither of them made a thing of it.

**"Four hundred and sixty-sixth of the run,"** Emrys Dole said. **"One thousand and three hundred and thirteen hundredweight, eight on, because this is an even morning. The window is four hundred and thirty-three, rising half two hundred and twenty-two and falling half two hundred and eleven, and the upper one is the one that moved in the night because this is an even morning, and the lower one has stood since Tuesday."**

Then, before Ismay Rooke could take it, Emrys Dole said the rest himself.

**"Eight hundred and fifty at the gate end of that wall and eight hundred and ninety-seven at the far end, forty-seven apart. I have carried on that road for nineteen years and I know it is forty-seven, and I have said that in this yard as though I had measured it, and I have not measured anything."**

Hesta Lyle came out with her sheet and read three figures at a council of five who had asked her for them, which is the first time in four years that anybody has asked her for them.

**"Four hundred and sixty-fifth over the line, four hundred and sixty-four at the head of it, and four hundred and sixty-third under. Three figures, and the fourth one is not mine to give you this morning."**

She set her hand flat on the top of it.

**"And while I am standing here with five of you in my yard: there is a column ruled on another sheet in this place with no word written over it, and under a heading of two words somewhere else there are four ruled lines with nothing on them. Both of those are still like that, and I have given you three figures and two empty places and I have not given you the fourth and you did not ask me for it."**

Hanne Brack walked the length of the long table on Monday's ground and stopped at the near end and put her hand flat on it without touching the sheets.

**"Four sheets. None of them entered, none of them refused, no column cut under any of them. How old is the nearest."**

Nia Vale said it from the wall end.

**"A hundred and sixty-three days. And the letter that is the other figure this yard keeps, the one that is not any of these four, is a hundred and sixty-three days as well today by a road I did not plan, and I am not going to stand here and tell a council of five that those are the same thing, because they are not, and I have watched people put those two figures together for four years and it has never once been true."**

Kellan Rusk read the three ages off the rack at about the eleventh hour in front of five strangers, in an order he chose because strangers want the oldest first.

**"Oldest first, because you are strangers. The charter has two hundred and sixty-six days on it and it is the one nearest to ceasing to be a plan. The register form has three hundred and sixty. On the top board of that shelf there is a ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and fifty-four, and above the word on that line there is nothing, and on the line there is nothing."**

He went in and left the shelf where it was.

**"I have said those three figures in this yard four different orders this month and they have not changed and nobody has improved them. You will hear them from whoever keeps them once you have gone back up the road, and they will be the same, and I would rather you heard them from the shelf than from me."**

Then a woman at the gate said to Marek, not to the council, that he was the one they would be sending for.

He was standing at the middle table with the ninety-four feet paper rolled in his coat and he answered her and not the council.

**"I am not on that paper and I am not going to be, and if I were I would resign from it inside a week, and that is not modesty. A man who is paid by an office four days north is not a person the body that says no can sit inside, because then the saying no is a thing that happens inside a man's pay and everybody in the four places will believe it."**

**"And I am not going to be enlarged to take him,"** Hanne Brack said. **"It came up the road on Wednesday as a question and it went back down the road on Wednesday as a question and we did not sit on it and we are not going to sit on it. He is the one we hear. He is not the one who decides."**

Auret Sill had not said anything for an hour and then said two things.

**"We have come down this road to hear a span at ninety-four feet and not to decide it this morning. Five of us and one question, and the question is whether we have the right to spend twenty-two mornings of other people's time, and we are going to hear the man who wants it and we are going to hear the crew that would build it, separately, and not in the same hour."**

She looked at Nia Vale at the wall.

**"And the first thing that was said to me on the road up this morning was that a sill at the north end came up on Wednesday at the third course. So that is the first thing we are going to ask about and it is not about the span and it is not going to be about the span this morning."**

The mason from the third place who had been reading figures off a stone all morning put the copy sheet down and said the thing he had come into the yard to say and had not said yet.

**"There is a piece of ground on the far side of the eleven acres and this yard has not walked it and has not measured it and has not named a price for it, and I have laid stone for three councils and I am going to say the thing I say about it, once, without decoration. A thing nobody has measured is a thing nobody has decided about."**

He turned the paper over in his hands and did not offer it to anybody in the yard.

**"I said that from this gate on Wednesday and nobody asked me to. I am saying it again now that I am on a piece of paper, because a paper is a different place to say a thing from than a gate is."**

Then he said the other thing, and the yard heard the difference between the two.

**"And there are two things I am not going to say while I am on this paper, and I have said that once already this week and I am saying it a second time because a body that has heard it once has not heard it from a body."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the boards and the compost line and the drawer in the order of a woman who has been in her own yard all morning and is not going to be impressed.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and nothing let out of that line in thirty-one or in thirty or in anything. I say it every morning in this yard and there are five people in it this morning who have never heard me say it, and I am not going to say it a sixth time for their benefit. It is on the board at the gate end and they are all welcome to walk over and look."**

She wiped the cloth.

She put her hand flat on the end sheet again, which told her nothing she did not have.

**"Behind this end there is a drawer and it is shut and the key has been on its nail since the sixth hour, and there is nothing in the drawer. Five people came down the middle road today and sat in this yard and not one of them signed an hour and not one of them was fetched for an hour, and I have stood in this yard four years and I have never once seen strangers come through that gate without somebody in this holding trying to get something signed."**

She wiped the cloth once down the length of the boards.

**"And none of the five touched the four on that table. Nothing entered, nothing refused, no column cut. The youngest of the four is a hundred and sixty-three days and the letter is a hundred and sixty-three days, and those are two different objects that have come to the same number by accident, and I have said so in this yard for four years and I am saying it again because five people were standing here today and somebody will have written it down wrong somewhere."**

Odile Vray came off the sill at about the second hour of the afternoon and did the compost line and the two cut figures and the window, all three at once, in the order of a woman who has four bodies and one of them is doing this.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and I am saying it for the fifth time this week and I am saying it because five people have come down this road and one of them keeps the pumps at the place that has none, and I am not going to insult her by not saying it."**

**"Eight hundred and fifty where it begins and eight hundred and ninety-seven where Harlan stands, forty-seven of masonry. And the window is four hundred and thirty-three and I have the halves the right way round now and I have had them the right way round for four days, and I am going to keep saying them in that order until somebody tells me that the whole of the last season had them the other way round, which I suspect it did."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the fourth hour and said the joint and said it to the council and not to the yard.

**"The joint at the back of the tap house has come away wet every morning of this month and the pipe behind it has not moved once, and there is a tap that works off it every day of the year. I have said that every morning for eleven days and I have said it to this yard and not to anybody who writes, and I am saying it this morning to five people because four of them came here to find out whether this place can keep five things and I am telling them the sixth thing while I am at it."**

At dusk the five of them went back up the middle road together and the paper went with them and nothing came down here with them.

The four sheets and the drawer were read out by the woman who owns them and the rest of the standing by the man who owns the rest of it, and neither of the two pretended that was a division anybody had agreed.

**"Thirty-nine blanks. The rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. Five people came down a road today and it did not open, and a council of five wrote a list of themselves and it did not open, and a span of ninety-four feet came up that lane on Saturday and it did not open. Fifteen lines in the use log with a clean space above the fifteenth. The barrow at eleven. Seven sessions. Six ruled rows, and nobody has cut a seventh for a council. The two lower books, fifty-three and fifty-three."**

He went back in without a word.

**"And the man at the end of the north row. Twenty-nine fetchings, none of them today, and not one of the five people who sat in this yard asked me a single question about him. I noticed. I have been keeping that list for a fortnight and the figure on it is still nothing."**

The council's own paper was not in this holding and could not be filed here and there was one place at that table that nobody had offered to anybody and one man in this yard who had said out loud that he would have resigned from it inside a week, and the yard went on working.

# Chapter 240

## A Crate With No Name On It

The four working came up the shoulder on the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth of this month at about the half hour, and the four of them worked the same four and none of them left early and none of them was late, and the middle came down about four inches a morning and stayed where it was put, and the man of about nineteen of Silling had seven readings of the middle with a rule and a piece of chalk and no unit and said so out loud twice and did not go and find the unit.

The ditch above Marden was dry at both ends on the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went up the bank on each of the four afternoons and looked into it and came back down and said one word to the reader of this body each time, and the word was not the word on the sheet, and the fen entered that she said dry and that he wrote it down four times and that he has not written a number against a ditch in his own book in four years and is not going to start by writing a word against one four times.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of went off two on to three, and that the three are the man of about fifty refusing to strike the man of the north row with the cough off a count on the eighteenth, the man of about fifty refusing to rub out a line on a sheet on the nineteenth, and the man of about fifty on the twentieth reading a line out of a satchel that had come four hundred miles, and that the reader of this body did not think of any of the three before they happened and that two of them were refusals.

And on the twentieth of this month, a Friday, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, the four lines came into the yard at this holding on the four-day road in a satchel with a crate in it, and about nine people were in the yard, and the reader of this body read the four lines out whole, and the man of about fifty read them back.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**The four lines came from the first house on that shelf at about the ninth hour of the evening of the sixteenth, on the same afternoon she took the fourteenth figure off her own wall, and carried down by the same man on the same horse, and the crate came in the same satchel, and I do not know which of the two she did first and I am not going to ask, and the reason is that the order of two things a woman did in her own yard on one afternoon is hers and is not a finding.**"

"**She has put no name on anything. Twelve years and four months and there is no name on anything that has ever come down that road, and she wrote on the bottom of the fourth line that the name is not on it on purpose, and I am not going to put one on it, and if anybody in about four years wants to put a name on it, that person can go and stand in that yard and ask her for it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that the person who keeps the seed house was in that yard and was not asked to be in it and read the four lines over his shoulder and did not hold them.

> **THE FOUR LINES, read out in a yard at this holding at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twentieth of this month, in about nine people, by the engineer of record, and read back by the man of about fifty, entered whole and not summarised. There is no name on them.**
>
> **ONE. THE SECOND LINE UNDER YOUR FIGURE. YOU HEADED IT NOTHING ELSE AND I LEFT IT BLANK AND I SAID I WOULD NOT SAY WHY. I AM SAYING WHY. I LEFT IT BLANK BECAUSE YOU DID NOT ASK ME ANYTHING, AND A LINE YOU DO NOT ASK A QUESTION INTO IS A LINE SOMEBODY FILLS IN FOR YOU. I HAVE HAD FOUR MONTHS AND THAT IS THE ANSWER AND IT IS NOT A GRATEFUL ANSWER AND YOU ARE NOT TO THANK ME FOR IT.**
>
> **TWO. THE ROOM IS OPENED. IT WAS NOT LOCKED. IT HAS A HASP AND A HASP IS NOT A LOCK, AND I HAVE HAD THE KEY ON MY RING ELEVEN YEARS AND HAVE NEVER HAD A REASON TO TAKE IT OFF IT, AND I HAVE TAKEN IT OFF IT AND IT IS IN THE CRATE. THE CRATE IS MINE AND I PAID FOR IT AND I AM NOT SENDING THE WAGE BACK.**
>
> **THREE. I AM NOT SENDING IT FOR YOU TO LOOK AT. IF YOU OPEN IT AND THE BOOK IS A PAGE OF A PAGE THEN YOU HAVE HAD FOUR DAYS OF A ROAD FOR NOTHING AND I WILL NOT APOLOGISE FOR THAT. THE BOOK IS NOT A GAUGE AND YOU SAID SO YOURSELF AND I HAVE READ THAT.**
>
> **FOUR. PUT IT AGAINST WHOEVER IS GOING TO BE ON THE STONE. NOT AGAINST THE ONE WHO SENT FOR IT. MY NAIL AND MY STRING AND MY WALL ARE MINE AND I HAVE LET A MAN FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF WRITE A LINE UNDER MY FIGURE AND I HAVE LEFT ONE BLANK, AND THE MAN WHO GETS THE BOOK IS NOT THE MAN WHO ASKED FOR IT. THE NAME IS NOT ON THIS ON PURPOSE.**

And on the seventeenth and the eighteenth and the nineteenth the reader of this body stood at the lowest of the four cuts and put a rule against the sill and let the water come up the wood and go down again, because that is the only way there is to read it, and the water moved about once in every four or five seconds from the west side and made no sound and showed nothing on the surface, and it was a foot eight inches, and it was a foot eight inches, and it was a foot eight inches.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that three takings at the same point and the same hour on three consecutive days gave one figure and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a figure and a control have come out the same, and that he is not going to say one word about it in a yard, and that a figure with a control in it is a figure a village can act on and that a village acting on a foot eight inches of standing water in a stone is a thing he would like to see once before he dies and is not going to ask for.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the nine put a hand on the edge of the table in the same way at about the same moment, and entered that he did not count them and entered the reason, which is that a man who counts the hands on a table is a man reading a room.

And the cost of it was entered that night in his own book and was not entered in the clerk's book, because the clerk was at this holding and it was the reader of this body's own hire and a man does not get a clerk to write down what he has paid for.

The wage is two shillings and sixpence up and two shillings and sixpence down, which is five shillings, and the crate is one shilling and fourpence, and the two together are six shillings and fourpence, and the four days are four days up and four days down, and this holding had four pounds and fivepence in the box on the third of this month of an earlier month and has not been asked for it since.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that six shillings and fourpence is a fortnight of the box in about four years and that a man who puts a fortnight of the box in a book against a thing he has not found out yet has done a thing he is allowed to do and is going to be careful about doing it twice.

And then the reader of this body went up the shoulder at about the ninth hour of the evening of that same Friday and stood at the top of the bank above Marden and looked into ninety feet of ditch, and it was dry at both ends for the thirty-seventh day, and the turn of that field is about two weeks old and goes to about nine inches on the north side, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was not there and had not been asked to be there and had gone down about an hour before.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a ditch that is dry at both ends for thirty-seven days and a field that is turned two weeks is a thing that four villages have been living on for eleven years and that the test on the stone and the ditch are on the same hill four hundred yards apart, and that he had not put those two things next to each other in his own book before the seventh of this month and that he is putting them next to each other tonight in about four lines, and that a man who writes two things next to each other in his own book at about the ninth hour of the evening has started to want something and he is not going to write the fifth line tonight.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he read the fourth line out and then stopped for about four seconds and then read the first line of the sheet again, and entered that about four of the nine said nothing in a way that was different from the other five, and entered that he did not count them.

And then he said the thing about the fourth line, and he said it to the yard, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-one.

"**I did not know that before about two hours ago and I sent four lines up that road on Tuesday morning at about the seventh hour and I did not put that in them. I have had her figure in a book in this holding since the ninth day of a month five months back and I have never once asked her a question, and I have sent her a sheet with two lines under a figure and one of them was headed NOTHING ELSE, and that is not a question, and a man who sends a woman four hundred miles a piece of paper with a line on it headed NOTHING ELSE has not asked her anything and has let her fill it in.**"

"**And she has filled it in, and she has filled it in with a sentence about who gets the book and not about the book, and she has never seen this node and she has never met anybody in it, and I am not going to stand here at about the fifth hour of a Friday afternoon and tell nine people what that is, because if I say what it is then one of these nine is going to go and do something about it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard and was four miles off and was not asked to be in it, and entered that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month two months back is thirteen and has moved, and that he has said six things in thirteen yards and that two of the six have been figures.

And then the crate was opened at about the seventh hour of the evening of the same day, in a yard at this holding, in about six people, and there was no lock on it and there was a hasp, and the hasp was not fastened, and the key was in it, and the key was on a ring and the ring had been on a ring for eleven years.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

The thing inside the crate is wrapped in oilcloth and the oilcloth is folded back and there is a copper clip with a bar through it, and inside the oilcloth there is a book, and the book is a fragment: a front board, a living bead, and eleven pages, and it is about the size of two hands together and it is a good deal older than this holding's book and it is not a ledger and it is not a schedule and it is not a form.

The reader of this body did not open it. He put his two fingers on the front board, well away from the bead, and did not touch the bead, and there is a record in a book four hundred miles off in a room with a counter in it, kept by a person whose name is not in this holding's book, that a man put his two fingers on the front board well away from the bead on a morning in the first month of an earlier year and did not touch the bead, and that the person writing it down wrote that he did not, and told him not to give him a third contact.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the crate and the book and the key and the oilcloth are not a contact and got no line in a book that has fifteen lines in it, and entered that the fifteen lines are the fifteenth line and that the fifteenth line is a contact on a boarded floor and that he is not going to put a sixteenth on a crate, and that a man who needs a new line for a new kind of thing has found a new instrument and not a new kind of work.

And then the man of the north row with the cough came up to the gate at about the eighth hour of the evening of the same Friday, on his own two feet, four miles, in the dark, and the reader of this body was in the yard and the man of the north row with the cough put the fourth line on the flat of the board and stood about nine feet off it and read it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that neither of them said anything tender and that the fen entered both of those.

"**I have been asked for two things since the eleventh of this month and I have said no to one of them and the one I said no to is the fourth line of that rota. I am not going to say anything about the fourth line this evening. You have a thing on a board that says put it against whoever is going to be on the stone and I am going to be on the stone on Sunday morning, and I have been on the stone four times out of nine and I have got a fifth in me that I did not put on the sheet, and I am sixty-eight and I have not told anybody I had a fifth.**"

"**I am not doing this because you asked me to. You did not ask me to. I was going to be on the stone on Sunday whatever was in the crate and whatever was not in the crate, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is about nine seconds of saying it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of the times he has offered to be somewhere he is not the engineer of record for went off one on to two, and that both of them are the same man, and that a man of sixty-eight has now offered twice and has been thanked neither time and that the count of the times he has been thanked is not a number this holding keeps and that this holding has never kept it and is not going to start.

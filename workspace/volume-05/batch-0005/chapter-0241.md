# Chapter 241

## Laid Against A Person

The twenty-first of this month was a Saturday and the eighth of the nine mornings came round at the second hour and the man of the north row with the cough held it from the second hour to the fourth hour with the same four working, and the reader of this body was not holding it and stood about nine feet off with the book in his hand, and the man of about fifty was about nine feet off on the other side, and Coll Renner was in the yard and had come on the Friday at about the seventh hour of the evening and had said four things in about nine people and the fourth of them was that he is not on the roll for the district this node stands in and that he came anyway and at his own cost and that nobody is to thank him for it because a licensed man who is thanked for coming is a licensed man who can be asked not to come.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of the eighth morning, which was about two hours and about four minutes, and entered that it is the longest of the nine by about four minutes and that he is not going to write that down in the block tonight because the block is about the reading and not about a hold.

And at about the fourth hour the man of the north row with the cough said the word and the man of about fifty heard it and the reader of this body heard it and nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the book was taken out of the oilcloth on the boards over the shaft at about the first hour of the afternoon, and about eleven people were in the yard, and the reader of this body read the cost out loud before anything else happened, and the man of about fifty read it back, and Coll Renner read it back a second time in his own words and said that he had a copy of it in his own book and would enter the hour on his own page and not on theirs, and that a licensed man's book is his and this holding's book is theirs and the two have never been the same book and are not going to be.

The cost was written in the reader of this body's own hand and it is four lines and it was read back three times and it is entered whole at the foot of this page and he is not going to repeat it in a yard.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a plain wire gauge was put against the book and against the front board and read at about the first hour of the afternoon, and that the reading of the test was written down before the instrument went near anything, and that a gauge is not a fieldbook and a gauge is the only thing in this holding's book that cannot be checked and that the two of them have been confused in a room in this book before and are not going to be confused on a Saturday afternoon.

And then the question came, and it was Coll Renner's and it was not a question the reader of this body had expected and he entered that afterwards, and it was this.

"**A bead goes against a wetted lot. That is the whole of what I have ever been able to do with one and I have been doing it for nineteen years and it is what it is for. You have brought me a book and it is a book and there is nothing in this yard to put it against that is a wetted lot, and I am not going to stand here and improvise with a professional instrument in about eleven people.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the eleven said nothing in a way that was different from the other seven.

And the man of the north row with the cough said the thing, and he said it before anybody asked him and he said it to the ground and not to the reader of this body, and the count of the times he has offered to be somewhere he is not the engineer of record for went off two on to three on the page three days ago and this is not an offer of that kind and the fen entered that the two counts are two counts and are not the same count.

"**A bead reads wood. That is all it reads. It does not read a person and it does not read a field and it does not read a stone, and if a thing is going to be put against somebody then the somebody is going to be wet, because the bead will not take hold of a dry thing and I have watched two men in my life find that out the hard way.**"

"**I have got no mark. I want that written down first, because I have listened to about four people in about eleven days talk about that book and every one of them has said the word mark, and I am sixty-eight and I have been in a hold eight times in nine days and there is nothing on me, and I have been glad of it, and I am going to lay my arm on that board this afternoon and I would like it read out afterwards that the reason was that I have got no mark and not that I was asked.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked him and entered that he said he would tell anybody who did to stop, and that nobody has thanked him.

So he put his left forearm on the boards, on the flat of it, about four inches along the inside, in the place a mark runs in a person who has been holding, and the skin of it was wet, because it is about four hours since two hours of work in the sun and he had not washed it, and the reader of this body laid the bead against it and it took.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of the reading, which was eleven seconds, and entered that he cannot account for eleven seconds of that morning and has never been able to account for them in any reading in five volumes, and that it is in the cost and that the cost is read back three times and that nobody has ever yet found out what the eleven seconds are for.

And then the book said its three lines, and it said them in the way it says them, which is plain, and without a heading, and without anything being asked of it, and the reader of this body did not ask it a question and had told a woman of about fifty-two in a yard in about nineteen people on the seventh of this month that he was not going to ask it one and he did not ask it one.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> **Take the water off the middle first.**
>
> **The two ends will hold while the middle is empty.**
>
> **If one of the two ends lets go, do not close the middle.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that nobody in that yard said anything tender and that the fen entered that too.

"**It does not say who holds the middle.**"

"**It does not say and neither are you going to and neither am I, and we will find out at the second hour tomorrow morning like two men in a dark, and I have had the word on a sheet for nine days because a man wrote it on his own bed, and I am not going to put a second word on a sheet this afternoon, and I am not going to say the name of the man who holds the middle in a yard of about eleven people on a Saturday.**"

And the man of the north row with the cough said one thing about the three lines before he washed his arm and before anybody had said anything else about them, and he said it to the reader of this body and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he was about nine feet off and heard all of it and entered that he was the only person who heard it.

"**That is the first time anything in this book has told me what to do and I have had three lines off a bead on my own arm and I am going to do what it says and I am not going to say to you which of the three bits I am going to be.**"

"**And I am telling you that I am not going to be. If I stand in the middle of that tomorrow at the second hour with the water coming off the middle first, then you are the one who put a bead on an old man's arm to find that out and you will be standing about nine feet off with a hand that has no use from the wrist down, and I have watched you sit down for a minute on the third morning and get up again and I am sixty-eight and I know what a minute is.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that neither of them said anything tender, and entered that he is not going to put that exchange in a yard or in a block or in this holding's book except as it stands, and that it stands.

And then the costs, and the reader of this body said them out loud once, in the ordinary voice, to the yard, and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-two.

"**Eleven seconds I cannot account for. The mark on this arm is four inches and it branches twice and it is not a second mark and it is not longer than it was, and it is hot, and the hot is not the mark. My hand from the wrist down has no use from the second hour of this afternoon and I am not going to pretend to anybody that it has. The headache is not the mark and it is not being entered as the mark. The four accounts of this arm in this holding's book are four and they reconcile none of one another and nothing I have said about that arm today is a fifth.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough took his arm off the board and washed it at the bucket and did not look at the place while he was washing it, and entered that the reader of this body did not ask him whether it had hurt and did not look either, and entered that the not looking was agreed between them in about four seconds without either of them saying anything about it.

And then the book went back into the oilcloth and the oilcloth went into the crate and the crate went to the cart shed and it goes up the four-day road on the Monday morning at about the seventh hour at this holding's cost, and the two shillings and sixpence and the same crate are entered, and it is six shillings and fourpence in all and this holding has not asked anybody for any of it, and nobody has offered, and the reader of this body did not ask and is not going to ask.

And the last thing that happened on that Saturday was the fifteenth line in a book that has fifteen lines in it, and the reader of this body said it out loud in about eleven people and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-three.

"**The use log stands at fifteen lines and it is not going to stand at sixteen. There was a contact on this afternoon at about the first hour and the contact is the only thing in this holding's book that has ever been a contact, and it has been used four times in five volumes and it has been fifteen lines the whole while, and I am not going to put a line on a crate and a bead and a man of sixty-eight's arm.**"

"**Coll Renner, say it in your own words and say whether you have to have a line.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> **NO LINE IS REQUIRED. I have a book and I enter a contact in it in my own hand with the hour on it and the licensed person on it and my roll on it, and I have entered one this afternoon, and it is in my book and not in this one, and the two books are not the same book, and there is nothing in the rule that puts a line in yours, and I am not the person who decides what goes in your book and you are not the person who decides what goes in mine. That is the whole of it and it took me about four seconds.**
>
> **AND I AM NOT GOING TO ASK YOU FOR A COPY. You did not ask me for a copy in the first month when I read the collar and I did not give you one and I am not going to ask you for one now that there is more in it, and the reason is that a copy is a thing a body hands out and I have watched a holding go four days east to get a thing a man would not come and say out loud.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of that, which was about half a minute, and entered that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of went off three on to four, and that the four are refusals or things a licensed man said that a man with a mark on his arm had not thought of, and that the reader of this body is not going to be thanked for any of the four and has not been.

> Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in a yard at this holding, in about eleven people, on the standing of a cost, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding, and not by the licensed man, who has it in his own book and not in this one: **THE BOOK WAS OPENED THIS AFTERNOON AT ABOUT THE FIRST HOUR, IN A CRATE, IN OILCLOTH, AT THIS HOLDING, FOR THE FIRST TIME IN FIVE VOLUMES, AND IT WAS SPENT ON A PERSON AND NOT ON A PAGE. The reading was eleven seconds. I cannot account for eleven seconds of this morning and I have never been able to account for them in any reading and it is in the cost and the cost was read back three times. The bead was laid against the left forearm of the man of the north row with the cough, about four inches along the inside, in the place a mark runs in a person who has been holding, and the skin of it was wet, and it took. He offered before he was asked and he asked for it to be read out afterwards that the reason was that he has got no mark and not that anybody asked him, and nobody thanked him. THE THREE LINES ARE ENTERED ABOVE AND NOT ON THIS LINE. THE MARK ON MY OWN ARM IS FOUR INCHES AND BRANCHES TWICE AND IS NOT A SECOND MARK AND IS NOT LONGER THAN IT WAS, AND IT IS HOT, AND THE HOT IS NOT THE MARK. MY HAND FROM THE WRIST DOWN HAS NO USE FROM THE SECOND HOUR OF THIS AFTERNOON. THE HEADACHE IS NOT THE MARK AND IS NOT ENTERED AS THE MARK. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THIS ARM ARE FOUR AND THEY RECONCILE NONE OF ONE ANOTHER AND NOTHING SAID ABOUT THAT ARM TODAY IS A FIFTH. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND IS NOT GOING TO STAND AT SIXTEEN, AND THE LICENSED MAN HAS ENTERED THE CONTACT IN HIS OWN BOOK WITH THE HOUR ON IT AND HIS ROLL ON IT, AND THE TWO BOOKS ARE NOT THE SAME BOOK, AND I HAVE ASKED NOBODY FOR A COPY AND HAVE BEEN ASKED FOR NONE.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the book goes up the four-day road on the Monday morning at about the seventh hour at this holding's cost, and that this holding has not asked anybody for any of it and that nobody has offered, and that the reader of this body is not going to ask.

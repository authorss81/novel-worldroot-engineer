# Chapter 237

## Nine Mornings

The eleventh of this month was a Wednesday and it was the first warm day since the turn of the year, and the reader of this body came into the yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the afternoon with about eleven people in it, and the man of about fifty had the printed schedule off the wall by the north gate rolled up in his hand and had not opened it, and the man of the north row with the cough was standing at the door of the cart shed with his coat off over his arm and had been standing there, the man of about fifty said afterward, for about twenty minutes before anybody arrived.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the reason the man of about fifty gave, which was that a man who has drawn a thing and is waiting for other people to come and look at it does not stand inside and come out again twice.

The reader of this body said the whole of it in four sentences and then stood where he could be seen, which is a thing he has done about nine times in eleven weeks and has entered every one of the nine.

"**It is nine mornings. The fourteenth to the twenty-second of this month, at the second hour, two hours, one holder and four working, and the holder changes every morning because a holder who holds nine is not a holder, he is a man who has been at it since the fourteenth.**"

"**It is not a measurement. There is nothing on this ground that anybody has ever measured twice on purpose, and I am not going to put a number on the front of it. A hold is a thing you keep and this is whether it can be kept for nine consecutive mornings by two people who are not the same person.**"

"**And it is not a test unless the water comes. The ditch above Marden has been dry at both ends since the twelfth of last month and the turn of that ground was begun on the first of this month by eleven people and it is eleven days old, and a turn takes a fortnight with water in the ditch and a month without.**"

"**If the water does not come this is nine mornings of two men standing on a stone in the same place and it is worth the paper and it is not worth a name, and I am going to say that now instead of in about three weeks.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the eleven said nothing in a way that was different from the other seven, and entered that he did not count them, and entered the reason, which is that a man who counts the faces in a yard is a man reading a room.

And then the man of the north row with the cough said the thing that he had been in a shed for twenty minutes waiting to say, and he said it to the yard and not to the reader of this body, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-eight.

"**I have got about four turns left in me. I am sixty-eight and I have had a cough in the winter since I was fifty-one and the winter this year was the one I have told you about, and four is what I have got and I have got it out of about nine years of a Tuesday and a Thursday.**"

"**So I have drawn it and I want you to look at the sheet before I say anything else, because I have written my own mornings on it and I want it seen that I wrote them and not that you gave them to me.**"

He put the sheet on the flat of the board that stands at the node, which is four feet by three on two posts, and it is a different board, and the fen entered that there are two boards at that node and has entered that before and had forgotten and has written it down again in his own book this afternoon with the date on it.

The sheet was the back of a printed weekly schedule of this holding, the one that goes on the wall by the north gate, and the back of it was blank, and he had written nine lines on it and a column down the left of the back with the ordinals of the days in it, and the reader of this body read it standing up and did not hold it and the man of about fifty read it back before a word of it went anywhere.

> **NINE MORNINGS, at the second hour, two hours, one holder and four working, drawn by the man of the north row with the cough in his own hand on the back of a printed schedule in a cart shed at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the eleventh of this month, and read out and read back:**
>
> **THE FOURTEENTH. HOLDER, THE READER OF THIS BODY. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE FIFTEENTH. HOLDER, THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE SIXTEENTH. HOLDER, THE READER OF THIS BODY. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE SEVENTEENTH. HOLDER, THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE EIGHTEENTH. HOLDER, THE READER OF THIS BODY. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE NINETEENTH. HOLDER, THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE TWENTIETH. HOLDER, THE READER OF THIS BODY. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE TWENTY-FIRST. HOLDER, THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH. FOUR WORKING.**
> **THE TWENTY-SECOND. HOLDER, THE READER OF THIS BODY. FOUR WORKING.**
> **AND THE FIVE ARE THE READER OF THIS BODY'S AND THE FOUR ARE MINE, BECAUSE HE ASKED FOR FOUR IN FRONT OF ELEVEN PEOPLE AND WAS GAVE FOUR, AND THAT IS A DECISION AND NOT A FAIRNESS, AND I HAVE SAID SO IN A YARD AND I AM SAYING IT AGAIN BECAUSE A NUMBER ON A SHEET OUTLASTS A YARD.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough said nothing, and entered that the count of times he has been in a yard and said nothing this month is one, and entered that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month two months back is ten and has moved.

Then the four working were named, and nobody worked them out in a yard, and they were worked out at the kerb over three days by four bodies of households and the reader of this body was told about them at about the eighth hour of the evening of the second day and did not choose any of them.

Renn Ashby, of about twenty-six, of Havergate, one of the six who take it in turns, who had written his own page out once and been given no copy of it. A man of about nineteen of Silling, who is the third line's grandson and who is at the seed house on a Tuesday and the road on a Friday, and whose name is not in this book and was asked for once and given in the yard and not in a book. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who counts adults above fifteen at a gate on the first and the fifteenth because somebody has to and it is nobody's job, and who is not one of the seven that decide and has never been asked to be. And Harlan Vetch, of about fifty-eight, who holds the fifth share and has held it eleven years and eight days and who said the thing about himself before anybody put him down on a list.

"**I am not a body of households. I am one man and a house and four miles off, and I have been the only witness to my own return for eleven years, and I have not been in the same column as the figure once. I will carry a barrow up that hill four mornings out of nine and I will be glad to be asked and I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like it written down that I asked to be asked.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a man of fifty-eight who asks to be asked is a fifth thing on a sheet of nine and that it is on the sheet and that nobody has thanked him for it.

And then the thing happened that the reader of this body had been waiting nine days for and had not asked about, which is that the man of the north row with the cough turned the sheet over on the flat of the board and took the chalk out of his own pocket and wrote one word at the head of it, under the top line, in his own hand, and put the chalk down and stepped back about nine feet.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**ENOUGH.**"

"**That is the word and it is the same word whichever body and it is the tenth line of a rota that four bodies of households drew, and that sheet has said *has said the word* since the twenty-first of last month and has not carried the word on it since it was written, and that has been a defect in a document for nine days and it is not a defect in a man.**"

"**He wrote it. I did not write it and I was not asked to and I am not going to have it in my hand, because a word on a sheet that a man wrote on his own bed is that man's word and a word that an engineer of record put there is a word somebody will be able to say he gave them.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty read the word out and read it back, and entered that nobody rubbed out the eleventh line of the old sheet either, and that the old sheet is still in the cart shed with a line on it that says it is a draft and is not in force, and that the man of about fifty has still refused to rub it out and has still refused to write on it, and that both of those things are entered and stand.

And then they went out to the ground, and the reader of this body walked the middle of it, and the middle of it is about nine feet off the hollow, and the ring of bare ground is there, and it is about two yards wide, and he walked past it four times in about forty minutes and did not stop and did not stand on it, and the fen entered that he and Sabra Ollerton, of about fifty-two, inspector of the Rootwright Assembly for four districts, who was in that yard on the seventh of this month, now make two professionals who have walked past that ring and written down that they do not know what it is doing there, and that a third one of them in about four years and it will be three professionals and a ring, and that there is no form for it and he is not going to make one.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

On the Thursday morning, the twelfth of this month, the reader of this body wrote four lines on a sheet of his own and gave them at the change of horses to a man he hired for the purpose, and the four lines went up the four-day road at about the seventh hour, and the cost of it is entered whole and is not small.

> Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twelfth of this month, in a yard at this holding, on the standing of a hire, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding: **A sheet went up the four-day road this morning at the change of horses, given to a man hired for it, carrying four lines in my own hand and asking for one thing and naming no person. The wage is two shillings and sixpence and the crate is not bought and will be bought if the thing comes down. The four days are four days. All of it is this holding's money and none of it is anybody else's and I am not going to say in a yard that I paid for a thing that somebody four hundred miles off decided whether to do, and I am not going to thank anybody, and I have not thanked anybody.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the hire is a line of its own and does not go into the compost line, and entered that the third month's compost line falls on the first of next month and is not entered in this holding's book this month and the reason is entered and the reason is that it falls outside the month it is a line for.

And then he walked the nine feet up the shoulder to the headland above Marden on the Thursday afternoon with the woman of about thirty-eight, because the ditch is hers in the sense that nobody else in this holding has been near it since the twelfth of the second month, and because a test that cannot happen is a thing you find out about on the ground and not in a yard.

The ditch is ninety feet long and about two feet off the top of the bank and it is cut to take the water off that field and put it into the branch, and it has been cut since anybody can remember, and there are three stones in the bottom of it that are not natural and are about nine inches apart. It was dry at both ends on the twelfth of the second month, which is thirty-one days ago, and it was dry at both ends this morning, and the woman of about thirty-eight said the two facts in that order and then said what they mean.

"**A ditch that is dry at both ends is not a ditch that is empty. It is a ditch that has nothing coming into it and nothing going out of it and the ground under it is doing whatever the ground under it does when there is no water in it, and there is nobody in this holding who knows what that is because there has never been anybody in this holding who went and looked.**"

"**The turn of that field was begun on the first of this month and it is eleven days old and it is four inches down and it goes to about nine on the north side, and if the water comes back in that ditch in the next four days then the turn will be done in the wrong weather and it will be done badly and I am not going to be the one who says that as though it were a reason not to put nine men on a stone.**"

The reader of this body walked the ninety feet with her and put his own boot print in the mud at about the fourth stone and looked at the mud for about a minute and a half, and the fen entered that he did not find out, and entered that the word he had for it was not known, and entered that the count of things he has written not known against in eleven weeks went off three on to four, and that the fourth is a boot print in mud in a ditch four hundred yards off a headland, and that it is the cheapest of the four and that he is not going to let cheap make it smaller, and he did not say it out loud in a yard on the day and said it to her alone on the bank in about nine words and she wrote it on her own board and he did not ask to see the board and did not ask for a copy.

"**You have got a count of mornings at that gate that has never been counted and you have got the field, and those are two ways of looking at the same number and they agree, and there is not one of the nine in that count that anybody in four villages could act on, because a number about a village of about three hundred people is not a number about a person and I have never once got that wrong in a yard and I am saying it now so that it is on the page before somebody puts it in a column.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the nothing was said in the four feet of bank between the two of them, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that she said one sentence in it and he entered the sentence and did not put it in a yard.

And on the Thursday evening the four bodies of households sent word that the four working were worked out and that the reader of this body was to be told and not asked, and the word came up the road with a man who had four hundredweight of boards on a cart behind him, and it was four lines, and the reader of this body read it standing at the north gate and did not hold it, and the man of about fifty read it back.

# Chapter 239

## The Sixteenth Of The Month

The fifteenth of the month was a Sunday and the man of the north row with the cough held the second of the nine mornings from the second hour to the fourth hour at a flat stone about the size of a bread oven, not natural, with a mason's mark on the back of it that is the same mark as the third part of the kerb four hundred yards below, and the four working were the same four and they came up the shoulder at about the half hour and none of them spoke for two hours and none of them has anything to enter about it except the two numbers.

The two numbers were said on the first morning by the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and they were said to three people and not to the reader of this body, who was nine feet off holding, and he wrote both of them down after and entered that he did not know what either of them is a unit of and did not ask and is not going to ask.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough said the word at the fourth hour and that it was heard by the man of about fifty at about nine feet off, and entered that it is the second of the nine and that the count of holds in this holding's book is nine and not nine people.

And then the reader of this body said the thing on the bank at about the fifth hour, to the four working and to the man of about fifty and to nobody else, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty.

"**I have been two days on this ground with the two people who can hold a pattern for four and I have got one thing out of it and I am going to say it now and I am not going to say it in a yard, and the reason is that it is not a thing four villages can act on and if I say it in a yard somebody will try.**"

"**A hold is not the same on the two of you and it is not the same on the same person on two mornings, and the only instrument in this book that can tell anybody which is which is the man in the hold, and the man in the hold cannot read himself, and that is the whole of what the fieldbook is for and the fieldbook is four hundred miles off in a room with a lock.**"

"**So there is an instrument that can read it, and it is the only one, and I am not going to get it in a yard, and I have asked for it in writing, and it went up the four-day road on Tuesday morning at about the seventh hour, and it is four days, and it will be on that shelf at about the seventh hour tonight.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he had said in about nine people on a Sunday afternoon that he had asked for the fieldbook, and that this is the first time in five volumes that anybody in this holding has said out loud in a yard that he was going to get it, and that he had said it out loud on purpose in about nine people and had not said it out loud in about four hundred yards at the node, and that the reason is that a yard of nine people is a place a thing gets agreed to and four hundred yards of open ground is a place a thing gets done, and that he does not know which of those two he is doing and that the man of about fifty said nothing about it and that the not saying was not agreement and was not disagreement and was entered as neither.

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden counted the gate at the bottom of that hill on the same evening at about the seventh hour, on the fifteenth, which is one of the two days in a month she counts it, and the figure was fifty-two of the fifty-two and it is the same figure it has been for eleven years, and the fen entered that the gate was counted on the fifteenth for the first time in eleven years at about the seventh hour of the evening instead of at about the ninth hour of the morning, and that both figures are fifty-two and that the count of mornings was not altered by the hour and that this is the only time in eleven years a gate at that village has been counted at the wrong hour and the reader of this body does not know what to do with it and is not going to guess about it in a book.

And on the Sunday evening the man of the north row with the cough came down off the shoulder about nine feet from the reader of this body and stopped, and neither of them said anything for about twenty seconds, and the fen entered the twenty seconds and entered that he had counted them.

"**I did not know a hold was going to be like that and I have not got anything to say about it and I am not going to say the thing I have said four times.**"

"**I have got one thing and it is not about the water. On the first morning the middle went down about four inches easy and this morning it went down about four inches and it took me most of an hour to get it there and I did not know that till this morning. I am sixty-eight. If it is four inches a day then in nine days it is going to be a foot and I am not going to be able to get it back up.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about half a minute, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough has now said six things in twelve yards, and that this was the sixth, and that it was the first of the six that was a figure.

And on the Monday morning at about the ninth hour, at the north gate, the reader of this body asked the man of about fifty one question, and the man of about fifty had been asked the same question on the Saturday and had said he did not know either, and the two answers are the same answer and the answer is that he does not know.

The question was what unit the two numbers are in.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the two numbers are the same two on every morning so far, and that the count of mornings on which the two numbers were said is three, and that the answer to the question is not known in this holding and was not known by either of the two men he asked, and that it is entered as not known and is not going to be entered a second time anywhere, and that a figure with no unit is not a figure and is not going to become one by being written down twice.

And on the Sunday the four of them were asked, each one separately and in their own arithmetic, what a thing like this is, and three of the four gave an answer that was a number and one gave an answer that was not, and the three numbers are entered and the fourth is entered as the fourth and no two of the four have been added together.

Renn Ashby, of about twenty-six, of Havergate: about nine hundredweight of stone out of a line on the north side on the first morning and about four hundredweight on the second, and the line is about two hundred yards, and he put the two figures in his own book at about the seventh hour of the evening of the Sunday and read them back to himself and did not send them to anybody and the reader of this body did not ask for them and has entered that he did not ask for them and the reason is that a man of twenty-six has already had one page of his asked for by a holding he is not in.

A man of about nineteen of Silling: the middle is four inches lower than it was on the first morning of the nine and it was four inches lower on the first morning than it was on the day before the test, and he has counted the middle every morning with a rule and a piece of chalk and he has eleven readings and no unit, and he said that out loud in about four people on the Sunday and that a man of nineteen who says he has no unit in a yard gets told to go and find the unit, and that he is not going to go and find the unit, and that if anybody wants the unit they can come and hold it.

Harlan Vetch, of about fifty-eight: the west side has gone down about nine inches on the first morning and about fourteen inches on the second, and there are two buckets of that on the bank, and the two buckets are not going back in because there is nowhere above that cut to put them and that is why the middle is going down and not the west, and he has said that in his own words and he is right and the reader of this body has entered that he is right and has not improved on it.

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not give a number, and the fen entered that she gave four sentences and no figure, and entered the four sentences, and they are: that the gate at the bottom of that hill is counted on the first and the fifteenth and is counted by her, that she is on a bank four hundred yards off on four mornings out of nine, that a gate is not a thing anybody misses, and that she would like it noticed that the last two things she has said in this yard were both about a gate and neither of them was about the node.

And on the Monday morning, the sixteenth of the month, the reader of this body went out to the field and to the lowest of the four cuts and looked at the ditch above Marden from the top of the bank and it was dry at both ends for the thirty-third day.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

The fourteenth ordinary reading of the well at Cauldron Reach is taken at about the fourth hour on the sixteenth of every month, and the sixteenth of this month was a Monday, and it was taken at about the fourth hour, and it is in her hand, and it is on her own wall, in her own yard, with her own nail and her own string with a bit of lead on it, at the level of the water and not the level of the curb, and that is the whole of the method and it has not changed in twelve years.

The four lines came down the four-day road at that household's own cost and nobody has ever offered and the offer was made once and was not taken up, and they came into this holding's yard on the twentieth of this month at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with a man who had come off the change of horses and they were read out in a yard of about nine people and read back and copied into this holding's book in a second hand, a woman's, and the second time on purpose.

What this holding's book has of that well is four figures. The ninth, the eleventh, the twelfth and the thirteenth, and all four of them are five feet. The tenth, which is a whole month between the ninth and the eleventh, is not in this holding's book, and the occupier refused it in her own hand, and the chalk has never touched that place on that wall, and the plaster under it is the same colour as the plaster round it, and it is the widest gap on a wall of thirteen marks and it is the only one.

The reader of this body entered the day and not the figure, and he entered the day and not the figure for the fourth time in eleven weeks, and he entered that the count in this holding's book of an entry that carried a day and no figure stands at three and is not reduced, and that he is not going to change what it counts tonight, and that the number of times he has entered a day and not a figure is a different number from the count in the book and the two have never been added together and are not going to be.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the person who keeps the seed house was in that yard and was not asked to be in it, and entered the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month two months back, which is twelve and has moved, and that he was not asked to be in any of the twelve and has said four things in twelve yards and that one of the four was a date.

And the third of the nine mornings came round at the second hour of the Monday and the reader of this body held it from the second hour to the fourth hour with the same four working, and the man of about fifty heard the word at about nine feet off again, and the middle was kept down about four inches and held, and at about the fourth hour the reader of this body said the word and sat down on the flat of the board for about a minute and got up again and did not say anything about having sat down.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he sat down for about a minute on the third morning and got up again, and that he did not enter it as a cost and did not enter it as a mark and did not enter it as a fifth anything, and that a man of thirty-one who sits down for a minute on the third morning of nine and gets up again has entered nothing at all and that nothing at all is a thing he has got very good at in eleven weeks and is not going to be praised for.

And on the Monday night, at about the eleventh hour, the reader of this body wrote in his own book that the sheet went up on the twelfth at about the seventh hour and that it is four days and that it would be on the shelf at about the seventh hour of this evening, and that he did not know whether it had been, and that he was not going to write a second line under it tonight, and that a man who writes a second line under a thing he is waiting for has started counting, and that he is not going to count.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the nothing lasted about nine seconds and that he counted them.

> Entered at about the eleventh hour of the evening of the sixteenth of this month, in a yard at this holding, in about nine people, by the clerk, on the standing of a day, and nothing added and nothing taken off: **THE DAY OF THE NEXT ORDINARY READING OF THE WELL AT CAULDRON REACH IS ENTERED AND THE FIGURE IS NOT. The reading is taken at about the fourth hour on the sixteenth of every month by the occupier, in her own yard, on her own wall, with her own nail and her own string with a bit of lead, at the level of the water and not the level of the curb, and the method has not changed in twelve years. THE WALL CARRIES THIRTEEN MARKS, BEING TWELVE FIGURES AND ONE GAP, and the gap is the tenth reading and it is a whole month and the chalk has never touched it. This holding's book holds four figures of that well, being the ninth, the eleventh, the twelfth and the thirteenth, and all four of them are five feet, and the tenth is not in this holding's book and the occupier refused it in her own hand and will not be asked twice. FOUR FEET OF SHORTFALL AGAINST THE NINE FEET OF THE SIXTEENTH OF A MONTH TEN MONTHS BACK, NOT DECOMPOSED, and the half inch of a month eight months back is not carried forward as a figure anywhere. There is no entry anywhere in this holding's book for that well coming back. THE COUNT OF AN ENTRY THAT CARRIES A DAY AND NO FIGURE STANDS AT THREE AND IS NOT REDUCED. THE THIRD OF NINE MORNINGS WAS HELD FROM THE SECOND HOUR TO THE FOURTH HOUR AND THE WORD WAS HEARD AT ABOUT NINE FEET OFF.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a sheet went up the four-day road on the twelfth of this month at about the seventh hour carrying four lines and asking for one thing, and that it is four days, and that this holding does not know tonight whether it has arrived, and that this is entered and is not a fault and is a fact about a road, and that the fen is not going to write a second line under it.

# Chapter 243

## What The Room Said

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Tuesday and the answer came into the yard at Errow Reach at about the eleventh hour of the morning, and it came out of the fen and not into it, and the woman who keeps the four books for four bodies of households read it out standing up in the hand it was written in, and read it back, and about nineteen people were in that yard and nobody asked her to sit down.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**I read out what is on a piece of paper in a yard when the yard asks to hear it read, and I read it out in the hand it was written in and not in mine, and I am not going to explain it and I am not going to be asked to explain it, and I have said that seven times in about seventy days and this is the eighth and it is the last one I have got.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was in that yard and was not asked to be in it, and entered that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month two months back is fifteen and has moved, and entered that he has said seven things in fifteen yards and that two of the seven are figures and one of the seven is a mark and that three numbers in fifteen yards is a rate of about one figure a month and that the reader of this body is not going to work that out tonight.

> **THE ANSWER OF THE ROOM, read out standing up in a yard at Errow Reach at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth of this month, in about nineteen people, by the woman who keeps the four books, in the hand it was written in, and read back, entered whole. There is no name on it and there is no office on it and the paper is the paper of a room and not the paper of a district and not the paper of an Assembly.**
>
> **ONE. AN ANSWER GIVEN IN A ROOM BINDS UPON THE ROOM.**
>
> **TWO. THE GROUND NAMED IN THE QUESTION IS NOT A PARTY TO IT AND WAS NOT HEARD, AND THIS ROOM HAS NO STANDING TO MAKE IT A PARTY, AND THIS ROOM DOES NOT PROPOSE TO ACQUIRE ONE.**
>
> **THREE. A BODY THAT ASKED MAY PUT THIS ANSWER IN FRONT OF THAT GROUND, AND THAT GROUND MAY REFUSE IT, AND IF IT REFUSES IT THIS ROOM WILL RECORD THE REFUSAL AND WILL NOT TREAT IT AS A FAILURE OF ANY PROCESS.**
>
> **FOUR. THIS ROOM HAS NO HOLD ON A HEARTROOT AND HAS NOT SOUGHT ONE AND IS NOT AWARE OF ANY INSTRUMENT BY WHICH IT COULD TAKE ONE. FOUR VOTES TO NONE. THE FOUR ARE NOT COUNTED.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of the not saying, which was about nine seconds, and entered that nobody in that yard said anything tender and that the fen entered that too.

"**It is four lines and it is correct and every line of it is correct and I have been in four districts for eleven years and I can tell you that a room which writes that it has no standing and does not propose to acquire any is a room that has read its own instrument, and I am not going to stand here in about nineteen people and find the line that is wrong, because there is not one.**"

"**And it is not the answer to the question. A woman of about thirty-eight wrote that question out in eleven seconds with no paragraph under it on the twenty-eighth of last month, and the first line of this says an answer binds upon the room, and the room has said in writing that it has no hold on a heartroot, and so the answer binds nobody at all except four people in a room who were not counted. That is not a refusal. That is a room telling us what it is and what it is not, and it took four months and it is correct.**"

"**And the third line is the one I have read nine times since about the ninth hour this morning. A body may put it in front of the ground and the ground may refuse it and the room will write the refusal down and will not call it a failure. That is not a thing I have ever been offered by anybody who has ever told me no.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the reader of this body said the three things above at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and not at the eleventh hour of the morning, and entered the reason, which is that a thing said at the eleventh hour of the morning when a paper has just been read out is a reaction and a thing said at about the fourth hour of the afternoon has been sat with, and that the man of about fifty said nothing about the difference and that the man of about fifty was right to say nothing about it.

And then the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said her own account of it, and nobody asked her for it, and she gave it in about eleven sentences in about nineteen people and it was not thanked and she said before she began that if anybody thanked her she would tell them to stop, and nobody thanked her and she said that too.

"**I went on the twenty-eighth of last month. Nineteen miles, two nights, and a day, and the gate at Marden was not counted on that day by anybody and that is the first time in eleven years and I am not going to let anybody make that into a thing that was worth it. It was not worth it. It was a day and it is a day whatever is in the paper.**"

"**And what is in the paper is four lines and none of them has my name in it and none of them has the name of that village in it, and I wrote the question and I carried it and the question says a body of households upon a ground that is not theirs, and the room has told us in writing that it has no standing to make that ground a party to anything, and I have been sitting with that for three days and I do not think that room is being difficult. I think that room is telling us the truth about itself and I think that is the first time in my life that anybody in a room has done it.**"

"**And nobody in that room is going to tell us who speaks for eleven acres of willow, and nobody in this yard is going to find out from that paper either, and I am not going to say out loud in nineteen people that I would like somebody to tell us, because I would, and a thing I would like is not a thing I get to put in a question.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the nineteen said nothing in a way that was different from the other fifteen, and entered that he did not count them.

And then the ditch came up, because the ditch above Marden has had water in it since about the second hour of the morning of the twenty-second and it has not gone down, and the turn of that field was begun on the first of this month by eleven people and is about three weeks old, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden priced it in Marden's own arithmetic in about nineteen people at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and it is entered whole and it is not in this holding's book as a figure of this holding.

"**The turn went in on the first and the water came back in the ditch on the twenty-second at about the second hour in the morning and it has been wet since. A turn takes a fortnight with water in the ditch and a month without, and we have got twenty-four days of a turn and nine days of water in it, and the field is going to be done badly and in the wrong weather and everybody at that gate knows it and I am the one that has to say it out loud.**"

"**It is not a figure about water. It is a figure about one field of about eleven acres in a village of about three hundred people, and a hundred and forty-one trees on the west side of a different ground are not in it, and the man who came up the hill on the seventh of a month five months back with a number in a stranger's hand is not in it. If anybody puts this next to a number about a whole shortfall at a node then they are going to make a field in Marden look like a village, and I have watched that happen twice this year with two other numbers and I am not going to let it happen a third time with mine.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that she did not give a figure, and entered that she was asked for one by nobody and that the reader of this body did not ask her for one and has entered that he did not ask, and that a man who asks a woman of about thirty-eight of Marden for a figure on a day she has been in a room nineteen miles off has turned her into a number, and that a man who has done that three times in eleven weeks has not noticed and that he noticed tonight.

And it rained from about the eleventh hour of the twenty-second until about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-fourth, which is two days and about six hours, and nobody at that node has a name for what it usually does in the third month, and a man of about fifty-eight who holds the fifth share has written one word in his own hand in his own column about once a year and was not asked about it and is not going to be asked.

And because it rained, the seventeenth of the nine-day returns was made on the twenty-fourth of this month at about the second hour of the afternoon and not on the twenty-second, and it was two days late, and the man of about fifty wrote the lateness in his own hand with the day and the hour on it and read it back before a word of it went on the page, and the four bodies of households read the figure back one at a time, standing where they could see the dial, in about nine people, in the rain.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

The figure is **EIGHTY-SEVEN HUNDREDWEIGHT** and it is the smallest of the eleven, and the man who brought it up the four miles in a barrow in about an hour and three-quarters on the worst ground any of the eleven has been brought over was the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who is one of the seven and not a body of households and who asked to be allowed to take it and was allowed and was not thanked.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the reason the return is two days late, and it is in his own words and it was given on the bank and not in a yard: the man who holds the fifth share was on the flat of about four feet of ground in the middle of eleven acres from about the third hour of the morning of the twenty-second to about the second hour of the twenty-third with a barrow standing beside him with its handles up out of the rain for seventeen hours, and he could not have brought it up four miles on the twenty-second and nobody asked him to and nobody is going to, and the four bodies of households did not send anybody of their own because a return belongs to the man who holds the share and to nobody else, and the fourth rule of that schedule is the rule of the fifth share and not a rule of four villages.

And the reader of this body entered that a two-day-late return is a two-day-late return and that it has been entered with the day and the hour on it, and that nobody is at fault, and that he is not going to say the word late in a yard as though it were an accusation, and that a return is a return and a barrow is a barrow and a man of fifty-eight standing in a ring of bare ground for seventeen hours is neither of them, and that the figure is entered as a figure and not as an apology.

And the last thing on the twenty-fourth happened at about the ninth hour of the evening at the lowest of the four cuts, and it was the man of the north row with the cough asking the man of about fifty for one thing and the man of about fifty saying no, and the reader of this body was nine feet off and heard both halves and entered them.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**I want you to write me off that count. Off the count of the two. I do not know whether I can do it again and I have got a mark on my right arm and I am sixty-eight, and a count with a question mark in it is a count somebody will use in about a year.**"

"**No. And I am going to give you the reason and then I am going to stop, and you are not going to argue with me, because you have not got a better reason than the one I am going to give you, you have got a louder one.**"

"**The count in this book is a count of what a person CAN do. You have not said you cannot. You have said you do not know, and a man who says he does not know has not said he cannot, and if I take a man off that count for saying he does not know then the next man says he does not know and the next one says it, and in about four years that count is a count of men who have not been asked, and it will be a better number for the book and a worse number for the field.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that he did not say a word, and entered the reason, which is that a man of fifty had just given a better reason than the one he had and he was not going to be the second man in that cut saying the worse one, and entered that the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden has a better one still and that he is not going to be the third.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, at the lowest of the four cuts, in about four people, on the standing of a count, and not in the clerk's book, which is at this holding: **THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO AND IT IS TWO AND HE ASKED ME AT ABOUT THE NINE O'CLOCK TONIGHT TO WRITE HIM OFF IT AND I DID NOT, AND I AM ENTERING THAT I DID NOT AND WHY, BECAUSE THE COUNT IS A COUNT OF WHAT A PERSON CAN DO AND HE DID NOT SAY HE CANNOT. HE SAID HE DOES NOT KNOW. A COUNT THAT IS A COUNT OF MEN WHO HAVE BEEN ASKED IS A BETTER NUMBER FOR A BOOK AND A WORSE NUMBER FOR A FIELD. The reader of this body said nothing at that cut tonight and did not argue with me and I AM NOT ASKING HIM TO. THE ANSWER OF THE ROOM CAME INTO THE YARD AT ERROW REACH THIS MORNING AND WAS READ OUT STANDING UP BY THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE FOUR BOOKS IN THE HAND IT WAS WRITTEN IN AND WAS READ BACK, AND IT IS FOUR LINES, AND THE FIRST OF THEM SAYS AN ANSWER GIVEN IN A ROOM BINDS UPON THE ROOM, AND THE FOURTH SAYS THAT ROOM HAS NO HOLD ON A HEARTROOT AND HAS NOT SOUGHT ONE, AND THE ROOM WROTE THAT ITSELF AND DID NOT PUT A NAME UNDER IT. NOBODY IN THAT YARD THANKED ANYBODY.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody said anything tender, and entered that the count of the man of about fifty being in a yard and saying nothing this month is three, and that this is the fourth thing a person who keeps a book in this holding has said in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of, and that three of the four were refusals and one of them was a reason, and that he is not going to be thanked for any of the four.

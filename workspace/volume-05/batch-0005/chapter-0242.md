# Chapter 242

## The Ninth Morning

The twenty-second of this month was a Sunday and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was on the top of that bank at about the half hour of the morning and the ditch was not dry, and she came down about nine hundred yards of field at a run and got to the yard at about the first hour and said one word into about eleven people, and the word was wet.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the word was wet and that the man of about fifty asked her for it a second time and did not ask her for it.

The reader of this body said the four sentences before anybody asked him for them, at about the first hour of the morning, in about eleven people, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-four.

"**I am not holding it this morning. I was on the sheet for the ninth morning and I am not going to be on it, and there is nobody to put in my place because the sheet says who holds what and the sheet does not say anything about this morning.**"

"**My hand from the wrist down has had no use since about the second hour of yesterday afternoon and there is nothing in this holding that will tell me when it comes back, because the one thing that could tell me is in a crate in the cart shed and it goes up the four-day road at about the seventh hour tomorrow morning.**"

"**I am not going to apologise for that and I want it on the page that I have thought about apologising for it, because a man who says he has thought about it and does not do it is doing the same thing as a man who does not, only slower.**"

"**And I am not going to stand here for a quarter of an hour about it. The man of the north row with the cough is not on the sheet for this morning and there are two of us who can do what is on the sheet and I am not one of them this morning, and that is the whole of what there is to say.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the eleven said nothing differently from the other seven.

And the man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard and came up the shoulder at about the half hour with the four working, and he was told in about nine words by the man of about fifty and not by the reader of this body, and nobody asked him whether he could do a fifth.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he did not ask, and that it was the second time in three days that he had not asked that man a question he could have asked, the first being on Friday night at a gate, and that a man who is not asked does not get to refuse.

And then they went out to the ground, and the man of the north row with the cough went to the middle, and the middle is a flat place about four feet across and the ground there is the ground the ring of bare ground is on the edge of, and he put his hand flat on it and did not stand on the ring and the fen entered that.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty looked at the flat of that stone for about four seconds and then went and got the book and did not open it.

The middle was four inches lower than it had been on the Saturday morning and about eight inches lower than it had been on the first of the nine, and it had come down every morning since the fourteenth without anything being put into it, and the man of the north row with the cough had said it to the reader of this body alone on the Sunday evening, that if it is four inches a day then in nine days it is going to be a foot.

It was a foot and one inch lower at about the second hour of the twenty-second than at about the second hour of the fourteenth, and the reader of this body took his two figures from the rule and read them out loud, and they were the same two the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had been saying out loud on the bank since the fourteenth, and the man of about fifty wrote them down in the book in his own hand, and the unit is not known in this holding and has not been entered a second time anywhere.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that neither of the two men who have a figure of that middle has asked what either of the two numbers is a unit of, and that the man of about fifty had it put to him on the sixteenth and answered that he did not know.

The four working went to four places and the four places were the four launders, and the four launders measure nine hundred, six hundred, nothing and twenty-five hundredweight a day, and the one that measures nothing was cut at the kerb on the eleventh of the first month and is not healed, and Harlan Vetch, of about fifty-eight, said one sentence about it while he was standing in it.

"**I have been the only witness to my own return for eleven years and I have never once seen that launder carry anything, and I am not going to pretend today that the day it was cut was a bad day, and I am not going to be the man who says a good thing about a day when I have not got a good thing about it.**"

And the reader of this body went to the north branch and put his right hand on the stone, because the north is the one that has the most on it, and his left hand was in his coat and was not used all day, and he stood there from the second hour of the twenty-second to the second hour of the twenty-third and did not sit down and did not leave, and the man of about fifty was at the lowest of the four cuts with the book and about nine feet off him, and Coll Renner was at the kerb and entered the hour in his own book and nobody else's.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty brought a coat out to the north branch at about the fifth hour of the evening and that he did not put it on and did not say why and that it went back with him.

Take the water off the middle first. The two ends will hold while the middle is empty. If one of the two ends lets go, do not close the middle.

They took the water off the middle first. The man of about nineteen of Silling and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and Renn Ashby and Harlan Vetch worked the four launders off the middle in turn and in the order the middle let them, from about the third hour of the morning, and it took them until about the seventh hour of the evening to get the middle about two inches below where it had been at the second hour, and the two ends held throughout and neither of them let go, and the man of the north row with the cough was in the middle of it the whole of that time and did not say the word once and did not move off the flat of it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a man of sixty-eight stood on about four feet of ground for about seventeen hours, that he was brought water four times by people who were not on the sheet, and that he put the cup down each time at the same place on the stone, and that the reader of this body counted the four and did not write them down.

And at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-second, while the man of about nineteen of Silling was on the west side and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was on the north bank, the mark came.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

He did not stop. He did not say the word. He said one thing, later, about it and not at the time, and the fen entered that it was said at about the ninth hour of the evening to about four people and in about nine words, and that the reader of this body was about ninety yards off at the north branch and did not hear any of it, and was told about it at about the fourth hour of the morning of the twenty-third, and entered that he was told, and that a man who is told about a thing is not a witness to it and is not going to be entered as one.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the mark is three inches along the inside of his right forearm, that it does not branch, and that the reader of this body has seen it and put his own hand on it once, and that it is permanent, and that he had a choice of about eleven for the word permanent and chose it and is not going to explain the choosing in a yard.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough did not say the word then and has not said it since, and that nobody has asked him why he did not.

And at about the fourth hour of the morning of the twenty-third, at the lowest of the four cuts, the man of about fifty gave the man of the north row with the cough a page and a pencil and stood about nine feet off and did not read it, and the man of the north row with the cough wrote it out in his own hand and gave it back and the man of about fifty did not read it and did not ask to see it, and the reader of this body did not ask to see it either and has entered that he did not, and the reason is that a man of sixty-eight has taken a mark off a stone and written it out in his own hand, and there is nothing in that page for anybody else to check, and a man who asks to see it is asking to see a thing that is not his.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the words of it, and they are these, and they are in a hand of about sixty-eight and not in a hand of thirty-one.

> **HELD THE MIDDLE. FROM THE SECOND HOUR OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF THIS MONTH TO THE SECOND HOUR OF THE TWENTY-THIRD. ABOUT SEVENTEEN HOURS ON MY OWN FEET. A MARK CAME ON THE INSIDE OF MY RIGHT ARM AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR OF THE EVENING, THREE INCHES, AND IT DOES NOT BRANCH, AND IT IS NOT GOING OFF. I DID NOT STOP AND I DID NOT SAY THE WORD. I HAVE GOT NO OTHER MARK AND THIS IS THE FIRST ONE. THE COST OF IT IS THREE INCHES ON A RIGHT ARM AND A BODY OF ABOUT SIXTY-EIGHT AND I AM NOT GOING TO CALL IT A HEAD, BECAUSE A HEAD GOES AND THIS IS NOT A HEAD. I AM THE FIFTH TURN AND I WAS NOT ASKED FOR THE FIFTH TURN AND I DID NOT OFFER FOR IT TWICE. I AM NOT ASKING ANYBODY TO BE THANKED. THE MAN WITH THE MARK ON THE OTHER ARM STOOD ABOUT NINETY YARDS OFF ALL DAY AND DID NOT SIT DOWN AND HE HAD NO USE OF ONE HAND FROM THE WRIST DOWN SINCE YESTERDAY AFTERNOON AND HE DID NOT TELL ME THAT BEFORE I ASKED HIM AND HE DID NOT ASK ME.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody said anything tender, and entered that the fen did not read the page out in the yard, and that a man of thirty-one copied it out in his own hand at about the fifth hour of the morning of the twenty-third, and that a thing in another person's hand that nobody else may read dies with the yard unless somebody writes it down, and that the man of about fifty said one sentence about that and the sentence was: WRITE IT DOWN AND DO NOT ASK HIM TO READ IT.

And at about the second hour of the morning of the twenty-third it came down the ditch first, which took about an hour, and then it came off the field, and then the middle came up about nine inches in about forty minutes and the two ends came up with it, and nobody closed anything, because neither end let go, and there was nothing to close and nobody went to look for one.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the rain that came down the ditch first took about an hour to reach the branch and that about four people watched it do it and said nothing while it did.

And the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour of the morning of the twenty-second, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields waited, and the seventh line of that sheet says that if neither can be woken the turn is not taken and the fields wait, and that the fields waiting is entered with the day and the hour on it, and that nobody is blamed.

The fen entered that nobody is blamed and entered the number, which is eight, and that all eight of them are the fourth line, and that a field above Marden waited for a day and a night on the twenty-second while five people stood on a stone four hundred yards below it, and that those two facts have never been in the same column in this holding's book and are in it tonight.

And then the reader of this body said the only thing he said in about nineteen hours, and he said it at about the third hour of the afternoon of the twenty-third to about four people at the lowest of the four cuts, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-five, and what he said was not tender and was not a thank.

"**The sentence that a man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system went into a book and a note on the tenth of this month and it is not coming down and I am not going to say it, and I have said it five times in this season in five places, four of them yards and one a shed, and the last of the five was a man of sixty-eight telling me at about the ninth hour of the evening that he is not going to be the man in the middle, and I did not hear it and I was told and I am not going to pretend I was there.**"

"**A party of five has held a heartroot for a day and a night and let about a foot of water off the middle of it and kept the two ends on, and one of the five took a mark doing it, and I stood about ninety yards off with a hand that had no use from the wrist down and I did not sit down once. That is the finding. I am not going to say anything else about it in a yard. The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two, it was two when I stood on that stone yesterday and it is two tonight, and one of the two has three inches on the inside of his right forearm that do not branch, and a mark is not a figure, and I am not going to put a mark on a count, and I am not going to stand in this cut and say what that count will be in a month.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nobody said anything tender, and entered that the man of about fifty was in that yard and had not said a word and had not said what a unit was, and that the count of times he has been in a yard and said nothing this month is three, and that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month two months back is fourteen and has moved, and that he was not asked to be in any of the fourteen and has said seven things in fourteen yards and that one of the seven was a figure and one of the seven was a mark and neither of those is a feeling and both of them are in a book.

The fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the light went off the west cut about a quarter of an hour after that, and that the man of about fifty shut the book and went up the shoulder and that the reader of this body stood there about another minute and then went up it too.

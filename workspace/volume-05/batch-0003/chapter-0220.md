# Chapter 220

## Marden Decides

The eighth of this month was a Friday and the sheet was read out in Marden's own yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the reader of this body was not in that yard and did not go up the hill with it, and the fen entered that he was at Errow Reach and entered that he had promised on the seventh that he would not come up with it and that a promise made in a yard about not doing a thing is the only kind this holding has ever kept.

Hesta Lyle read it out. She is of Marden and has kept the four books for the four bodies of households at the Four Elbows for nineteen years and is the only person who does, and she has never been one of the seven that decide at Marden and is not going to be, and she said so standing up before she read the paper, and the fen entered the count of the sixth time in thirty days that she has said a standing first at a gate or in a yard.

About nineteen people were in that yard and about nine of them were the people whose yard it is.

And then the ninth of this month was a Saturday, and the engineer of record and the licensed man went up onto the high ground on foot at about the ninth hour of the morning with a tape and a book, because there is a launder on that side which a licensed man is entitled to look at and which nobody had ever asked him to look at, and the fen gave that reason out loud at the gate before they set off and gave it a second time at the gate of the village when they got there, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty-two.

The ground at Marden is the high ground and it is about a hundred and forty feet above the elbow and the village is not in a valley and there is no water in it that anybody has ever put there, and the ground goes up from the gate in three banks with a flat place on the middle one, and the flat place has a stone on it about the size of a bread oven and the stone is not natural and has a mason's mark on the back of it that is the same mark as the third part of the kerb four hundred yards below.

They did not go into the yard. They stood at the gate for about three hours, and about two hundred yards inside the gate there is the flat place with the stone on it that the people of that village have used as a place to stop working for four generations, and there were about nineteen of them on it, and a sheet of paper in the hand of a woman who keeps four books was on the stone in the middle of them, and every word was said out loud, and the reader of this body heard all of it from the gate and the licensed man heard all of it from the gate.

There are seven that decide at Marden and about two of the seven were on that stone, and Hesta Lyle had said in her own yard on the twenty-first of last month that she has never been one of the seven and is not going to be, and the fen entered that about seventeen of the nineteen on that stone do not decide anything at Marden, and entered that he was at a gate and could see faces and not numbers, and entered the reason, which is that he is not going to stand at a gate counting who decides where.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who keeps the gate count and is not one of the seven that decide, said the thing that the yard had agreed to say first, and she said it standing on the stone.

"**Before anybody says yes or no I am going to ask the man who held the book. You were in the room when he read that sill on Saturday evening and I want one answer from you in front of us and not from him, because he is at the gate and he is not going to be asked anything and I have watched men at gates not be asked things for four years.**"

And the man of about fifty said it, in about eleven people, on a stone, in Marden's own yard, and the fen entered that it is the first time in thirty days that anybody has said a word about that sheet in somebody else's yard and that he was not in that yard for it.

"**Once. He read it once on Saturday evening and he wrote it down once, and the figure he is comparing it against he took at the same place at the same hour thirty-five days ago and that is in his book with the day on it, and that is two figures and one reading, and I was in the room for both of the other things and I am not going to stand on this stone and tell you that is three.**"

And about nine of the nineteen said the same sentence, and the fen entered the count of nine and entered that it is the same count as the nine who refused the withdrawal on the twenty-first of last month and that the fen did not enter that and entered only the count.

Then the woman of about thirty-eight read the sheet out again herself, from the stone, in her own village, in the ordinary voice, and stopped at the line about the east launder, and did not read the reason after it, and somebody asked her why she had stopped and she said why and it is a true reason and the fen entered it and did not improve on it.

"**I stopped because the next line is the part where he says it is our launder because we carry the least, and that is true, and it is the reason I have believed for six days and I am not going to say it in our own yard as though it were our own words. He wrote it. He can say it. I will say it at the gate where he is standing.**"

Then they argued for about an hour and a half and about nine of the nineteen said the same sentence and about six said another, and the fen entered neither number and entered the length.

The old man of about seventy-four with the stick, who is one of the seven that decide and who refused the withdrawal on the twenty-first of last month in his own words, said the sentence about the counting paper and nobody argued with him, and then a woman of about sixty of Marden who is not one of the seven said the other sentence, and the other sentence was that the launder is the smallest of the four and always has been and that a village which has been the smallest for a hundred years knows better than a sheet what the smallest one is for, and that is not the same as saying the sheet is wrong, and about nine of the nineteen agreed with the old man and six with the woman and nobody moved.

Then the old man asked a question and the fen heard it from the gate and entered it, and it was the only question asked in that yard that was not about the sheet, and it was about the fen and not about the ground.

"**Is the man who wrote it going to be here on Monday.**"

And Hesta Lyle said that she did not know and that she had asked him and that he had said he would be, and the fen entered that he had said he would be and had not been asked to and had not offered, and entered that the count of times he has offered to be somewhere he is not the engineer of record for is one, and that it is this month, and that the count of times he has been somewhere he was not the engineer of record for and was not asked to be is four, and that the two counts are two counts.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And a man put the argument that the fen had put into his own book eleven days earlier and had not said out loud, and he said it on the stone, and the fen did not hear it until it was read back to him at the gate an hour later by the woman of about thirty-eight, who said she was going to say it to him and not to the yard and gave the reason first.

"**If the man is wrong then we have cut a launder for nothing, and if the man is right then we have saved a node, and there is no way from this stone to tell which of the two we are doing, and we are going to have to decide it without knowing, and a village decides things without knowing about four times in a generation and I have been in this yard for a year and I have watched two of them.**"

"**And the thing I am going to say to you at this gate is that we are not deciding whether the man is right. We are deciding whether a village cuts its own launder or watches somebody else cut it, and that is a different question and it has a different answer and the answer has been the same for four hundred years.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then a body of households decided, in daylight, in its own yard, in its own words, and about nineteen people were on a stone and the count of people who said the deciding sentence was eleven, and the fen counted them from a gate two hundred yards off and did not go in.

> **MARDEN'S OWN WORDS, said in Marden's own yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in about nineteen people, eleven of whom said the same three sentences, and entered by Hesta Lyle in her own hand and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road, and not by the man of about fifty, who was on the stone and did not write:**
>
> **WE HAVE READ THE SHEET. WE HAVE READ IT TWICE AND THE SECOND TIME WAS IN THE SAME WORDS.**
>
> **WE ARE GOING TO CUT IT OURSELVES. WITH OUR OWN SAWS, ON OUR OWN DAY, AND WE ARE GOING TO DO IT AT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON, WHICH IS WHEN THE LAUNDER IS LOWEST, AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO DO IT TONIGHT BECAUSE ONE OF US HAS TO WRITE IT DOWN FIRST.**
>
> **AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO HAVE A MAN FROM A FEN DO IT. HE OFFERED US OUR OWN COLUMN BACK BLANK ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF LAST MONTH AND WE SAID NO, AND WE SAID NO FOR THE REASON THAT IS ON THAT SHEET IN OUR OWN WORDS, WHICH IS THAT A VILLAGE THAT MAY TAKE A NUMBER OFF ITS OWN PAPER BECAUSE THE NUMBER DOES NOT FLATTER IT ENDS UP WITH A PAPER OF THINGS IT WOULD HAVE CHOSEN TO HAVE COUNTED. HE HAS NOW BROUGHT US A COLUMN OF HIS OWN WITH HIS NAME AT THE TOP OF IT, AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO HAND A MAN FROM A FEN A SAW.**
>
> **AND WE KNOW WHAT WE LOSE. WE HAVE NINETY FEET OF DITCH ABOVE THAT VILLAGE AND IT HAS BEEN STANDING AT THE TOP END AND NOT AT THE BOTTOM END SINCE THE TWENTY-NINTH OF LAST MONTH, AND A MAN STOOD AT THE TOP OF IT AND TOLD US IT WAS THE GROUND AND NOT THE CUT AND DID NOT TAKE IT BACK. THE GROUND ABOVE THAT VILLAGE IS TURNED ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH, WHICH IS IN TWENTY-TWO DAYS, AND A TURN TAKES A FORTNIGHT WITH WATER IN THE DITCH AND A MONTH WITHOUT. WE WOULD HAVE LOST IT IN A MONTH ANYHOW. WE ARE LOSING IT ON THE ELEVENTH.**
>
> **AND WE ARE NOT SAYING THE MAN IS WRONG AND WE ARE NOT SAYING HE IS RIGHT, AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO ASK HIM, AND IF HE OFFERS TO COME UP AND STAND AT THE GATE HE CAN COME UP AND STAND AT THE GATE.**

Nobody thanked anybody and the fen entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that he was two hundred yards off at a gate and did not go in and was not asked to.

And the tenth of this month was a Sunday, and about five men of Marden counted two saws in a shed at Marden and there were two and both of them had teeth in them, and one of them had a new handle and the new handle was not on any list of anything, and the woman of about thirty-eight wrote the handle on the back of the gate count in her own hand and entered the reason, which is that a tool with a new handle in a village of about three hundred people is not a detail and is a thing somebody is going to be asked about in four years.

And the seven that decide at Marden were told on the tenth, in the evening, in a room, and they were told the whole of it, and it took about an hour, and they did not decide anything on the tenth, and on the fourteenth of this month, which is after the cut, Hesta Lyle wrote one line about it in her own hand and the fen entered that line whole, and the line is this, and no name is in it.

> **THE SEVEN DECIDED ON THE TENTH TO LET THE STONE DECIDE, AND ON THE FOURTEENTH THEY ASKED FOR THE LINE TO BE ENTERED AS THAT, AND THEY ASKED FOR IT IN THOSE WORDS, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED THEM WHY A STONE IS ALLOWED TO DECIDE SOMETHING THAT SEVEN PEOPLE ARE NOT ALLOWED TO DECIDE IN A ROOM.**

And the fen entered that he had been at a gate on the ninth and had heard a village decide on a stone and had written the whole of it down in a book, and that in about four years a man is going to read that book and find out that the only decision in nineteen years of four books that has a date and an hour and a count of people who said the same sentence was taken on a stone by about nineteen people and not by the seven whose business it is, and that he is not going to say anything in a book about which of those two things is a body of households, and entered the reason, which is that a fen is not a village and has been told that twice this month by two different women and a man of about seventy-four, and that a fen who has been told it three times and then written a paragraph about it is a man who is putting his own name at the top of a second sheet.

> Entered by Hesta Lyle, in her own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the ninth of this month, in Marden's own yard, on the standing of a decision, and not by the clerk: **the sheet was read out in that yard on the eighth of this month at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the reader of this body was not in the yard and did not go up the hill with it. On the ninth the engineer of record and the licensed man came up the hill with a tape and a book to look at a launder that the licensed man is entitled to look at, gave that reason out loud twice, and did not go into the yard and stood at the gate for about three hours. About nineteen people were in the yard and the sheet was read out on a stone inside it. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN ASKED THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY, IN FRONT OF THE YARD, HOW MANY TIMES THE SILL HAD BEEN READ, AND HE ANSWERED ONCE, AND SAID HE WAS IN THE ROOM AND WAS NOT GOING TO STAND ON A STONE AND LIE ABOUT IT, and the reader of this body was not in the yard when that was said. The woman then read the sheet out herself and stopped at the line naming the east launder and did not read the reason after it, and gave the reason for stopping. A body of households decided in that yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month that it would cut its own launder, with its own saws, on its own day, at the fourth hour of the afternoon, and would not have a man from a fen do it, and would not say whether the man is right or wrong and would not ask him. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY AND THE READER OF THIS BODY DID NOT GO INTO THE YARD AND WAS NOT ASKED TO.**

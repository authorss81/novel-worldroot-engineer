# Chapter 219

## The Sheet

The seventh of this month was a Thursday and the ninth day of every ninth day came round again for the twelfth time since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back, and about eleven people were in a yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the morning, and the man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it, and the fen entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is three and has not moved in thirty days.

Harlan Vetch came round the corner of the building with the barrow about ten minutes to the hour, the same barrow with the wheel on the front of it and the two handles and the lids on two baskets, and he did not say good morning and nobody said good morning, and the fen entered that and entered that it is the eleventh time and it is not unfriendliness and that this holding has never been able to work out what it is.

The barrow went on the steelyard hung on the wall of the building with no roof, and the man of about fifty balanced it and moved the sliding weight and read the figure out, and then each of the four bodies of households read it back after him, one at a time, standing where they could see the dial, and nobody improved on the figure.

> **THE FIGURE ON THE STEELYARD, read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn: NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT, at about the second hour of the morning of the seventh of this month, on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles, by one man, in about an hour and a quarter. It is the fourth time it has been weighed in a yard and it is the first time a figure has come off that dial that a body of households has had in its own hands twice.**

Then it went into the hollow on the west side inside nine square yards, and it took about four minutes, and the four of them watched it go in, and nobody said anything while it did.

It is half-rotted willow and the litter off his own holding and about a barrow-load of leaf mould that has been under his own stable for two years, and about nine feet off the hollow the ground is bare in a ring about two yards wide and has been bare in that ring for eleven years, and nobody has ever asked what a ring of bare ground nine square yards in the middle of eleven acres of willow is doing there, and Sennik Vaul of Silling asked about it on the seventh of this month and the man of about fifty told him, and told him that there are about two hundred and thirty-one trees on that ground and a hundred and forty-one of them on the west side, and the fen entered that the man of about fifty had not been asked to tell him that and told him anyway, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has been given a figure by another man gets a use out of it inside a week.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything while the return went in, which is a different thing from nobody saying anything after it, and entered that the four minutes of it are the only part of any return anybody at that node has ever watched.

And then Hesta Lyle wrote it on the board under the heading that has been on that board for nineteen days, and wrote the hour with it, and the four bodies read it back before the chalk was put down, and it is the same heading it has had since the eleventh of last month, which is that a return is made on the ninth day of every ninth day by one person who is not one of the four.

And a name went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY, and a name went into WITNESSED BY, and it is the third time either box has had a name in it, and Harlan Vetch said one sentence about it and the fen entered it.

"**Three times now, and the second name is not mine and I did not ask him, and in about four years somebody is going to read those three lines and find out that the witness of a smallholder's own return has been one man from a fen for three holds running, and I would like that written down now and not in four years.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then at about the ninth hour of the morning of the same day, in the same yard, with about eleven people in it, the reader of this body read out a sheet.

He wrote it himself, in his own hand, on four sheets of the same paper, and the four copies went into four pockets, and he read all four out whole, and the four were word for word the same, and he checked them against each other in front of about eleven people.

> **THE SHEET, four copies, in the hand of the engineer of record, read out whole at about the ninth hour of the morning of the seventh of this month in a yard at Errow Reach in about eleven people, and the four copies were compared against each other in that yard and are word for word the same, and one name is at the top of each:**
>
> **STANDING WATER IN THE MAIN TRUNK ABOVE THE SILL AT THE WEST KERB. FOURTH HOUR OF THE EVENING, THE FIRST OF LAST MONTH: TWO FEET SEVEN INCHES. FOURTH HOUR OF THE EVENING, THE SIXTH OF THIS MONTH: ONE FOOT TEN INCHES. NINE INCHES IN THIRTY-FIVE DAYS. THE SAME KERB, THE SAME HOUR, THE SAME MAN.**
>
> **A BRANCH HAS TO COME OFF THE CORE. THE BRANCH TO COME OFF IS THE EAST ONE.**
>
> **THE REASON IS ARITHMETIC AND THE ARITHMETIC IS ON THE COUNTING PAPER AND WAS NOT GOT FOR THIS SHEET. THE FOUR LAUNDERS HAVE BEEN MEASURED AT NINE HUNDRED, SIX HUNDRED, TWENTY-FIVE AND TWENTY-FIVE AGAINST ABOUT ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED DELIVERED, AND THE TWENTY-FIVE IS INSIDE SOMEBODY'S ABOUT, AND I HAVE NEVER BEEN ABLE TO MAKE THOSE TWO AGREE AND I AM NOT GOING TO TO-DAY. A BRANCH CARRYING A QUARTER OF WHAT ANOTHER BRANCH CARRIES IS THE BRANCH THAT COSTS LEAST TO LOSE, AND THAT IS A TRUE SENTENCE, AND IT IS A SENTENCE ABOUT FOUR VILLAGES' ARITHMETIC AND NOT ABOUT THE GROUND.**
>
> **I HAVE NOT PUT THE NINE INCHES INTO HUNDREDWEIGHT AND I AM NOT GOING TO. A CONVERSION NEEDS A LENGTH OF TRUNK AND A SECTION OF TRUNK. I HAVE THE SECTION AT ONE PLACE AND I HAVE THE LENGTH OFF A STEP COUNT TWICE. A MAN WHO CONVERTS NINE INCHES INTO A WEIGHT AND THEN ASKS A VILLAGE TO GIVE UP ITS WATER IS A MAN WHO HAS MADE THE FIGURE BIGGER BY PUTTING A NUMBER ON THE END OF IT.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was longer than it had been in that yard since the eleventh of last month.

And then the man of about fifty asked the question, and it was the only question anybody asked in that yard all day, and it was asked in the plainest words he had.

"**Where is it from?**"

And the reader of this body answered it, and the answer was true, and it was the answer to the question that was asked.

"**The west kerb, the sill, the first of last month at about the fourth hour of the evening, and the sixth of this month at about the fourth hour of the evening. It is my own figure and it is in my own book with the day and the hour on both of them, and the earlier one has been in that book since I took it and the later one went in at about the fifth hour of the evening on Saturday.**"

And the man of about fifty looked at him for a moment, and then said the second half of it, and the fen did not answer that either.

"**How many?**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the count, and entered that the count of times a man has been asked a question in a yard in this book's history and has answered the question that was asked and not the question behind it is one, and that it is him, and that it is entered now rather than later because the reason he did not answer is that the man who asks it is the man who is going to have to do the asking every day for a season, and a man who is let to find that out on his own in about four years instead of on the seventh of a month is being done a kindness and it is not a kindness, it is a habit, and the count is one and it is entered.

And then Brenna Oake of Havergate said the thing that about eleven people in that yard had not thought of and that has not been thought of in this holding's book in four years, and she said it in nine words, and the fen entered that he had known the shape of it on the twenty-seventh of last month and had not said it and entered that he has not said that about himself in a book before and that this is the first time.

"**Then the next sheet says the opposite.**"

"**The next sheet says the opposite. Havergate carries nine hundred hundredweight a day and the east launder carries twenty-five, and this morning you told four villages that the smallest is the one that costs least to lose, and that is true, and in about four years a sheet is going to say that a village carrying nine hundred is the one that costs most to keep, and it is going to be true then as well, and I would like somebody in this yard to tell me which of us is holding the pen.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about nine seconds, and entered that nobody in that yard said the name of a person, and that it is the first time in this book's history that a body of households has asked a question about who writes the figures rather than what the figures are, and that he is not going to answer it, and entered the reason, which is that the answer is a fen, and that a man putting his own name at the top of a second sheet in a yard of eleven people at the ninth hour of the morning is doing the thing the question is about.

"**The sheet says what it says and it says where it is from and it says when, and that is everything I have got for you this morning, and if you want more than that then you want more than I have and I would rather you asked me for it than worked it out of the sheet in four years and thought I had kept it back.**"

Then Hesta Lyle said the thing she said on the eighteenth of last month, and the fen entered that she said a standing before anybody asked her to, and that it is the sixth time in thirty days that she has said one at a gate or in a yard.

"**I carry the four copies. He does not. I have said why on the eighteenth of last month in this yard and I am not going to say it again because a reason said twice in a yard becomes a rule and a rule about who carries a paper is the first rule anybody writes about a village that has never written one. The four of us walk out in the morning.**"

Then the man of about fifty said one thing about the copies, and the fen entered that he said it before anybody asked him to and that the count of that at that yard, over the twenty-one days this party has been on this ground, is four.

"**Four copies, and the four of them are word for word the same this afternoon and they will not be word for word the same in four years, because one of them will be in a book in a mill town and one of them will be in a press in a lawyer's town and one of them will be in a shed and one on a stone in a village, and the one on the stone is going to be the one that gets read. You checked them against each other today. You are not going to be able to check them in four years and neither am I and neither is anybody in this yard.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty is not the engineer of record and has not been asked to be, and that this is the second time in thirty days that a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of, and that the count of that is two.

And the man of the north row with the cough said one thing at the wall before the four of them went, and the fen entered it and did not answer it and did not improve on it.

"**You have handed four bodies of households a piece of paper that says a branch has to come off, and you have not put one word on it about what happens to that branch afterwards, and there is a line on that sheet in the shed with the word ASLEEP on it, and you have written nothing about what a village's launder does when the village is asleep. I am not saying the sheet is wrong. I am saying the sheet is a sentence and the two of those are not the same size.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough is not the engineer of record and is not going to be asked to be, and entered that what he said on that night is the truth and that the reader of this body had four hours between the sixth hour of Saturday and the ninth hour of this morning to write a line about what a cut launder does afterwards and did not write it, and entered the reason, which is that he had four hours and did not think of it, and that is the whole of the reason and there is not going to be a better one.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the eleventh hour of the evening of the seventh of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a paper, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **the twelfth hold since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back was made at about the second hour of the morning of the seventh of this month, the return was NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT weighed on the steelyard and read back by each of the four bodies of households in turn, it went into the board in the yard with the hour on it and a name went into the first rule of the schedule with a witness against it for the third time. AT ABOUT THE NINTH HOUR OF THE SAME MORNING A SHEET WAS READ OUT WHOLE IN A YARD OF ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE, four copies in the engineer's own hand with his name at the top of each, the four compared against each other in the yard and word for word the same. The sheet gives two figures at the same kerb at the same hour thirty-five days apart, NINE INCHES, and says that a branch has to come off the core and that the branch to come off is the east one, and gives its reason as arithmetic off the counting paper. IT CARRIES NO FIGURE IN HUNDREDWEIGHT AND SAYS WHY. It is not signed by anybody else. The man of about fifty asked two questions, where it was from and how many, and the reader of this body answered the first and did not answer the second, and the count of times a man has been asked a question in a yard and has answered the question that was asked and not the question behind it is one and it is his. The woman who keeps the four books carries the four copies and the reader of this body goes to none of the four gates, and the reason was said on the eighteenth of last month and was not said again. The man of the north row with the cough said that the sheet says a branch has to come off and says nothing about what a launder does afterwards. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the four of them went out of that yard at about the eleventh hour of the morning with four sheets in four pockets, and about a third of a mile away the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing to the reader of this body on the way out, and the fen entered the hour and the sentence and did not enter why.

"**That is the first thing anybody has handed us about that node that was not a figure off a board in a shed, and I am going to read it in our own yard to about nineteen people before I say one word about it, and you are not coming up the hill with it.**"

# Chapter 217

## The First Of Next Month

The first of this month was a Friday and the door was read at nine hundred yards off at about the tenth hour in a full yard of about twenty-five people, by the person who keeps the seed house, with the reader of this body on one side of her and the man of about fifty on the other, and the seventh column went to **forty-one**, and the rule did not change and is not going to be.

It carries a heading and the other six columns on that door do not, and the six have been blank since the door was made four years ago, and this holding is not writing them in, and nobody in that yard asked.

The day-minus figures were read out loud by the clerk before either number, which nobody has asked him for on any day he has done it. The boards stood at **three hundred and twenty-five days** and the March's tin at **three hundred and seventy-two days**, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be.

The door is nine hundred yards off and it is a plank door with a hasp on it and a lamp nailed above the hasp, and the figures are in chalk on the planks and the chalk is the chalk out of a box that the person who keeps the seed house buys in a town nine miles off, four boxes at a time, twice a year, out of her own money, and the fen entered that on the day the door was made four years ago and has not entered it since and is entering it now because about nine people were standing in a yard and there is no reason to leave a thing out of a yard that everybody already knows.

The clerk read the seven columns out in the order they are on the door. One foot eleven inches, on the first reading, four years ago. Two feet four. Two feet ten. Four feet, and under the four feet somebody has scratched a small mark into the plank with a nail and the mark has no figure against it and no date. Four feet six. Four feet eight. And forty-one, in the seventh, which is the one with a heading, and it was forty on the thirtieth of last month and thirty-nine on the first of last month, and the rule is that it is read on the first and the thirtieth and the rule is not going to change.

And the other six columns have no headings and have been blank since the door was made, and about four people in that yard looked at the six places a heading ought to be, and nobody asked, and the fen entered that nobody asked, and entered that he had been expecting somebody to ask for about three years and that he had stopped expecting it in the second year and that a thing this holding has stopped expecting is not the same as a thing this holding has stopped wanting, and that it is going to be written down in the book as the second of those and not the first.

And before any of that, in the same yard, the standing rule was read out in full, out loud, by the person who keeps the seed house, in front of about twenty-five people, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not. It is four lines and there is no fifth line and there never has been one, and every one of the four is about a stop, and the third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years.

> **THE STANDING RULE. Four lines, every one about a stop, read out in full in a yard at nine hundred yards off the seed house at about the ninth hour of the morning of the first of this month, in front of about twenty-five people, and not used today. The third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years. It is satisfied. Satisfaction is not permission. The rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and the count of the uses last season was two, and the count of the uses this season is nought, and the two counts are two counts and are not the same count, and the count of the uses this season is still nought at the ninth hour of the first day of this month.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.

Nobody thanked anybody and the fen entered that nobody thanked anybody.

Then the compost line, and it is the whole of the first of this month, and it has been owed on this page since the thirtieth of last month with the day entered and the figure not.

The line is entered at **ninety pounds**, and under it is eighty pounds, and beside the eighty is ten pounds, and beside the ten is the reason and the hour, and the word before the figure is **SHALLOWER**, and the four words before that were EMPTIER once and CROWDED three times, and the run is **thirteen months not discharged in thirteen.**

Four hundred hundredweight at four shillings the hundredweight is sixteen hundred shillings, which is eighty pounds. Four hundred and fifty hundredweight at four shillings is eighteen hundred shillings, which is ninety pounds. One thousand eight hundred less one thousand six hundred is two hundred shillings, and two hundred shillings is ten pounds, and the ten pounds is a woman and a barrow and a book nobody has ever seen, and the ten pounds is not a mistake and is not going to be corrected and is not going to be entered twice.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that it is the shortest silence he has entered in that yard in two years and that he is not going to say what the one before it was, because the man who has that in this book's history is not going to be asked about it by a clerk reading a door.

Then the reader of this body read the line out loud himself, in the yard, in the ordinary voice, with the hour on it, because it is his holding's line and it is his holding's book and a line that is read off a page by the person who keeps the seed house is a line that about twenty-five people hear as a fact about the weather.

"**Ninety pounds. Under it, eighty pounds, which is what it has been four months running. Beside the eighty, ten pounds, which is a woman with a barrow and a book nobody has ever seen, and it is not a mistake and it is not going to be struck out. Thirteen months not discharged in thirteen. The word before it is shallower. That is all of it and I am not going to start saying something else about it on a month when the figure has gone up.**"

And the person who keeps the seed house said one thing about the word, and it was not about the word, and the fen entered that she is a woman who keeps time and does not round, and that this is the fourth time in two years she has corrected a figure in a yard before the person who wrote the figure has finished saying it.

"**Shallower is right. I have been standing at that bin for four years and it is shallower. I am saying I can see it and not that I know why, and this is the first month in four years the two of them have said the same thing, and I am not going to be the one who says that is a good month.**"

Then the woman who does the sums said one thing about the word, and she said it to the table and not to the yard.

"**Shallow is the first word on that line in five months that agrees with the figure under it, and I have been putting a word on that line for four years and I have never once checked the two against each other, and I am not going to start tonight at the tenth hour with about twenty-five people standing in a yard, and I would like whoever writes it down to write down that I noticed it and that I am not doing anything about it.**"

And the man of about fifty wrote it down, and read it back, and the fen did not improve on it, and entered that the word and the figure have now come apart twice in five months and that the two of them are not a pair and have never been a pair and that this holding is not going to make them one by writing them on the same line.

Then the man of about seventy was fetched for the eighteenth time, and he came, and he was in the yard about four minutes, and nobody said anything to him, and he did not say anything at all, and the fen entered that it is the fifth time in this book's history he has asked for a number to be repeated and that he has never once asked which number.

He had come in on his own two days early, which he has done twice in four years, and he stood at the wall side with his hands behind him for about four minutes, and about four people said good morning to him and about twenty did not, and the fen entered the four and did not enter the twenty.

Then the person who keeps the seed house said one thing to him, in front of about twenty-five people, and it was a question and not a kindness.

"**Did you want the boards or did you want the door?**"

And he said that he had come for the boards, and she said that the boards were at four hundred and twenty-five days and that the door was at forty-one, and that if he had come for the boards then about twenty-five people had been standing in a yard for nothing on his account, and he said that was correct, and she said that it was, and nobody said anything else about it, and the fen entered that it is the only conversation in this book's history between a man of about seventy and the person who keeps this holding's seed house, and that neither of them has ever asked the other a question about the other.

Nobody explained anything to him and nobody thanked him and the count of the request that a man's initial be put against nothing stands at fifty-three.

Then the reader of this body said the thing he came into that yard to say, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty.

"**I am on the cart road at the second hour of the morning of the second day of this month and I am gone five days, and the reason I am saying it in this yard and not on a gate is that a holding which is going to be out of its own standing figures for five days has said so in front of about twenty-five people, and about a quarter of those people are not the holding and a quarter of them are the four villages' and I would rather they heard it from a yard than from a gate.**"

"**And the reason I am going is that there is a figure in my own book that I took at the west kerb of that node on the first of last month at about the fourth hour of the evening, with the day and the hour on it, and I have had twenty-nine days to find out whether the ground under that node has moved since the first of the month, and I have not found out, and the only way I have ever found out anything about that ground is to go and stand on it at the same hour and see.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the reason he gave second is a real reason and not the whole of it, and entered that the rest of it is in his own book with the day and the hour on it and is not going to be said out loud in a yard by him.

> Entered at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the first of this month, at nine hundred yards off the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a month, and nothing added and nothing taken off: **the figures were read out whole in a yard of about twenty-five people and were entered as they were read, in order, with the hour on each. The day-minus rules were read out loud before either number and the drawer was not opened. The seventh column is forty-one and the six other columns on that door have been blank for four years and are not written in. The standing rule was read out in full before the figures were closed and was not used. THE COMPOST LINE FOR LAST MONTH IS ENTERED AT NINETY POUNDS, with eighty pounds underneath it and ten pounds beside it and the reason and the hour, the word before the figure being SHALLOWER, the run at thirteen months not discharged in thirteen, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced. The word and the figure have come apart twice in five months and are not a pair. The count of requests that a man's initial be put against nothing is fifty-three. The man of about seventy was fetched for the eighteenth time and said nothing and was given no explanation and was not thanked. The reader of this body goes on the cart road at the second hour of the morning of the second day of this month and is gone five days. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And then, dated the first of this month, in the hand of the engineer of record and read back before it was initialled, is the reason the tenth of last month is not on the standing figures at all, entered once and not entered twice.

*The first month's line is on the page at ninety pounds and the eighty is under it and the ten is beside it, and the ten is fifty hundredweight a month carried out of a bin by hand at the hour of the carrying, for forty-eight months, by a woman who is not in this yard and does not know this line exists. I am putting it down once. I am not going to put a figure about her on a line that four bodies of households and about sixteen households in this holding all read, and a number about a person that is on a line is a number about a person that belongs to the line, and in about four years somebody will take it off the line because it does not flatter the line.*

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything tender and the fen entered both of those.

And at about the second hour of the afternoon the reader of this body went into the seed house and shut the door behind him, which he does not do, and the person who keeps the seed house looked at him through the glass and let him, and the fen entered that she looked and let him.

There are three books on the table in the middle of that room and any person in this holding may walk up to that table and read any of the three without asking anybody, and that is not a rule and is not a charter and there is no form for it anywhere, and it exists because a man decided two years ago that a thing anybody may walk up to is worth more than a thing anybody may ask for, and the count of people who have ever asked to read one of the three books is two.

His own book is the middle one and it is a shop book and it has been his since he was twenty-two, and the two figures he has for the west sill at the Four Elbows are in the back of it on a page with nothing else on it, written on the first of last month and on the sixth of this month, both in his own hand, both with the day and the hour on them, and it is the only page in that book with two lines on it and no heading and no ruling.

He looked at the two lines for a while and did not write anything under them, and the fen entered that he did not, and entered the reason, which is that a man who writes a third line under them is writing it for somebody he has not met, and that he has never in four years written anything in that book for somebody he has not met.

And on the second day of this month, at the second hour of the morning, five people went out of the gate of this holding with a barrow each and a book and a tape, and the man of about fifty had the book open on his arm before the cart road was under him, and the reader of this body did not write the road down and entered the reason, which is that a road that a man is on instead of a ground he is standing on has changed the subject, and that there is no count of roads in this book and there is not going to be one.

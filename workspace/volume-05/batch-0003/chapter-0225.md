# Chapter 225

## Twenty-Five Hundredweight A Day

The seventeenth of this month was a Sunday and the eighteenth was a Monday and the nineteenth was a Tuesday, and on the Monday the reader of this body went up onto the high ground on foot with the man of about fifty and a tape, and gave the reason out loud at the gate before they set off, and it was a true one and it was not the whole one.

"**I am going up there to be in a yard when a village prices what it has lost, and the reason I am not saying that out loud in this book is that it would read like an engineer who has come to hear what a thing cost. I am going because that yard said on the ninth of this month that a man from a fen could stand at its gate, and I am standing at its gate.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he was not thanked for standing at a gate and was not going to be.

He stood at the gate from about the second hour of the afternoon until about the sixth, and about two hundred yards inside it there are nineteen people and a stone, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the four copies of the sheet on the stone and the ditch figures on a second paper and the fen did not see either of them from the gate and did not ask to.

The licensed man was not there and the fen entered that he had not been asked and had not come, and entered the reason, which is that there is no form in this district for what happened at a stone kerb on the eleventh of this month and a man who is on the roll is not going to be the eleventh person in a village yard that has not asked him.

They priced it on the stone, and they priced it in their own arithmetic and not in his, and the man of about fifty wrote it all down and did not say one word of it, and the woman of about thirty-eight read it out twice at the end, and it went up the hill with a woman on a cart and came to Errow Reach the next morning, and the reader of this body read it standing in the road.

The arithmetic took about forty minutes and it went wrong once, and the man of about fifty counted wrong in the middle of it, and Hesta Lyle said so from about nine feet off the stone in the middle of a yard of about nineteen people and did not raise her voice, and the woman of about thirty-eight did not take it back and did not argue, and said what the wrong figure was and said how she had got it and said that she had got it because she had used the figure off the counting paper, which is a figure of wanting, and that anybody who has been in this yard for four years knows that the third column is a count of wanting and is not going to be corrected today.

And the old man of about seventy-four with the stick, who is one of the seven that decide, said the other half of it, and the fen heard it from the gate.

"**She is right and the man is wrong and neither of them is going to say so, so I am going to say it. The figure on that counting paper is not a village and we all said so in our own yard on the twenty-first of last month and I was one of the nine who said no to the man who offered it back, and if that figure is on a piece of paper in a yard in a fen then it is a figure again and it is going to be a figure in four years. That is the whole of what I have come to say and I have said it and I am going to sit down.**"

And he sat down on the flat place at the foot of the bank with his stick across his knees, in the second month of the year, on a stone that has a mason's mark on the back of it that is the same mark as the third part of the kerb four hundred yards below, and about two hundred yards from a gate where the engineer of record of a holding with no charter was standing with a tape he had not used, and nobody said anything, and the fen entered that nobody said anything and entered that he did not go up the bank and did not ask, and entered the reason, which is that an old man who has said a thing and sat down is a man who has finished and a man who is asked a second question is a man who has to start again in front of nineteen people.

> **MARDEN'S OWN FIGURES, written on the back of the gate count on the eighteenth of this month by the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, in her own hand, read out twice in Marden's own yard in about nineteen people, and carried down the hill and up the cart road at Marden's own cost:**
>
> **THE LAUNDER CARRIED TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY. IT WAS MEASURED AT THE HEAD OF THE SILL WITH A TAPE ON THE NINTH OF LAST MONTH BY THE MAN YOU SENT UP WITH YOUR LICENSED MAN AND IT IS IN HIS BOOK, NOT OURS.**
>
> **TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT AT FOURPENCE THE HUNDREDWEIGHT IS ONE HUNDRED PENCE A DAY. THIRTY DAYS OF IT IS SEVEN HUNDRED AND FIFTY HUNDREDWEIGHT AT THREE THOUSAND PENCE. A POUND IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE. THREE THOUSAND PENCE IS TWELVE POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS A MONTH.**
>
> **AND WE ARE ABOUT THREE HUNDRED PEOPLE, AND THREE THOUSAND PENCE OVER THREE HUNDRED PEOPLE IS TEN PENCE A HEAD A MONTH. THE OTHER WAY IS ONE HUNDRED PENCE A DAY OVER THREE HUNDRED PEOPLE, WHICH IS A THIRD OF A PENNY A HEAD A DAY, AND THIRTY DAYS OF A THIRD OF A PENNY IS TEN PENCE. THE TWO WAYS AGREE. WE HAVE CHECKED THEM TWICE.**
>
> **AND FOR THE SAKES OF THE OTHER VILLAGES, BECAUSE SOMEBODY WILL WORK IT OUT WITH THE WRONG NUMBER: THE WHOLE SHORTFALL AT THAT NODE IS SIX HUNDRED HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE A HEAD A MONTH ACROSS ABOUT FIFTEEN HUNDRED PEOPLE, AND WHAT WE HAVE LOST IS TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY AND TEN PENCE A HEAD A MONTH ACROSS ABOUT THREE HUNDRED PEOPLE, AND THOSE ARE NOT THE SAME FIGURE AND ANYBODY WHO PUTS THEM IN ONE COLUMN IS GOING TO MAKE ONE OF THEM LOOK LIKE THE OTHER ONE.**
>
> **THE GROUND ABOVE THAT VILLAGE IS TURNED ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH, WHICH IS IN THREE DAYS, AND A TURN TAKES A FORTNIGHT WITH WATER IN THE DITCH AND A MONTH WITHOUT. THE DITCH IS NINETY FEET AND IT HAS BEEN DRY AT BOTH ENDS SINCE THE TWELFTH OF THIS MONTH. WE ARE GOING TO TURN IT WITHOUT.**
>
> **WE ARE NOT SENDING THIS TO YOUR FEN TO BE PUT IN A BOOK. WE ARE SENDING IT BECAUSE A MAN WROTE A SHEET WITH HIS NAME AT THE TOP OF IT AND GAVE IT TO US AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO USE IT AND HIDE IT.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, and entered that he was standing at a gate about two hundred yards off and heard all of it and was not in the room.

Then the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and the woman who keeps the four books both came out of that yard and down the lane to the gate, and the fen entered that both of them came out and that neither of them had to and that one of them is a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a gate count and the other one has kept four books for nineteen years, and that he had been in a yard with one of them four times and the other one nine and that neither of those numbers is anything to do with what happened at that gate.

And Hesta Lyle said the sentence that stopped it, and she said it in Marden's yard's hearing because the gate is in Marden's yard's hearing, and the fen entered that and entered that she gave the reason first.

"**You have been up here three days standing at a gate and you are about to say a thing in a road about what a village gives up, and I am going to stop you, and I am going to say why first, and the why is that I have kept the four books for nineteen years and I have watched a great many people find the right words for a thing that has already happened, and every one of them was standing further away from it than I am.**"

"**And the second why is that a thing that gets said out loud in a road by a man from a fen about a village is a thing that village has to live inside afterwards, and this village has a count at a gate on the first and the fifteenth and I keep it, and I am not going to be the one who hands a sentence to about three hundred people and then has to look at them at the next gate.**"

And the reader of this body did not say it, and the fen entered that he did not say it, and entered the reason, which is that he was going to say it, and that a man who has been stopped from saying a thing in a road is not a man who was going to say nothing.

And before she said that, the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said the thing about the turn of the ground, and she said it to the fen and not to the yard, and the fen entered the hour of it and entered the sentence and did not enter why.

"**We are going to turn it without. Eleven of us, on the first of the third month, and we will be doing it until the twelfth and the ground will come up in clods and there is a stone in that field that has been in the way of a plough since my father's time, and we will go round it. And if the water comes back in the ditch in about four weeks then the turn will be done badly and it will be done in the wrong weather and that is what four weeks does to a field in this ground.**"

"**I am not telling you that to be thanked for. I am telling you because you are going to have to say something about it in a yard somewhere and I would rather you said that than the other one, and the other one is about how much it was worth.**"

Then the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said the thing that the fen had been going to say, and she said it to the fen and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he had worked it out on the fifteenth of this month at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and had not said it out loud to anybody.

"**You are going to say that a village gives up the thing it was already short of. I have been thinking about that since the ninth of this month and I have got it wrong twice and I have got it right once, and I am going to say my one and then I am going to go back up that lane.**"

"**We gave up the thing we were already short of. Nobody asked us what we had. The sheet said a branch has to come off and the branch it named was ours because we carry the least, and I have turned that round about forty times in ten days and there is no other way round it, and I have looked for one, and there is not one.**"

"**And I am not going to stand in a road and be told by anybody what that means. It means we have lost ten pence a head a month and a fortnight's work and there is a turn in the ground on the first of the third month. That is what it means. Write that one down and not the other one.**"

And she went back up the lane, and nobody thanked anybody, and the fen entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that he had not been thanked and had not been thanked-for and that he was not going to be.

> Entered by Hesta Lyle, in her own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the eighteenth of this month, in Marden's own yard, on the standing of a figure, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road, and not by the engineer of record, who was at the gate: **the loss of the east launder was priced in that yard in that village's own arithmetic, derived twice, the two agreeing: TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY AT FOURPENCE THE HUNDREDWEIGHT IS ONE HUNDRED PENCE A DAY, and thirty days of it is seven hundred and fifty hundredweight at three thousand pence, and a pound is two hundred and forty pence, and three thousand pence is TWELVE POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS A MONTH, and across about three hundred people that is TEN PENCE A HEAD A MONTH, and the second derivation is one hundred pence a day over three hundred people, being a third of a penny a head a day, and thirty days of a third of a penny is ten pence, and the two agree. The whole shortfall at that node is six hundred hundredweight a day and forty-eight pence a head a month across about fifteen hundred people, and what Marden has lost is twenty-five hundredweight a day and ten pence a head a month across about three hundred, and those are not the same figure and must not be put in one column. THE GROUND ABOVE THAT VILLAGE IS TURNED ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH AND THE DITCH IS NINETY FEET AND DRY AT BOTH ENDS SINCE THE TWELFTH. The figures were sent down the hill and up the cart road at that village's own cost and are not for a book in this holding. The reader of this body stood at the gate of that yard for about four hours and was not in the yard. HESTA LYLE STOPPED HIM FROM SAYING A THING IN A ROAD ABOUT WHAT A VILLAGE GIVES UP AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST, which is that a right sentence said out loud by a man from a fen about a village is a thing that village has to live inside afterwards, and she keeps the gate count. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN SAID THE SENTENCE HE HAD BEEN GOING TO SAY AND TOLD HIM TO WRITE DOWN WHAT IT MEANS AND NOT WHAT IT MEANS ABOUT. THE STANDING COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN STANDS AT NINE AND WAS NOT ENTERED AGAIN IN THIS BLOCK, because the reader of this body said out loud in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month that he was going to stop counting for the rest of the season on the subject of questions, and a question was put in that yard on the eighteenth of this month and nobody took it and the count of that was not written, and the question is in this holding's book, and the reader of this body did not write and ask which book. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the reader of this body put the whole of that into his own book at about the ninth hour of the evening of the nineteenth of this month, in her hand and not his, and did not write and ask for the rest of it, and entered the reason, which is that a village that has priced its own loss in its own arithmetic and sent it up a road at its own cost has not done that so that a man from a fen can send it back down with a heading on it.

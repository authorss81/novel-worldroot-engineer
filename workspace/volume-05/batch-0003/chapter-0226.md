# Chapter 226

## The Thirteenth Figure Came Down The Road

The twentieth of this month was a Wednesday and the ordinary post came up the cart road to Errow Reach at about the ninth hour of the morning, and there was one thing in the bag and the bag came out of the same leather as everything else that has arrived at this holding for four years, and the thing in it was four folded lines and a fifth thing that was not folded the same way, and the reader of this body said the hour out loud before anybody opened it.

"**The fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth at a yard of four households four hundred miles off, at that household's own cost, on the same four-day road, and the ninth hour of the morning of the twentieth at this gate, and the four days are the four days. That is a road and I am not going to say anything else about it tonight except that a four-day road does not get shorter because a man is in a hurry.**"

About nine people were in the yard. He did not open it, put it flat on the table and stood back, and the man of about fifty put his hand on it and did not open it either, and the fen entered both of those.

Then Hesta Lyle said the standing before anybody spoke, and it is the seventh time in forty days that she has said one at that table, and the count is entered.

"**I read out what is on a piece of paper in a yard when the yard asks to hear it read, and I read it out in the hand it was written in and not in mine, and I am not going to explain it and I am not going to be asked to explain it.**"

And she opened it and read the lines out as they were, and the man of about fifty wrote them down and read them back before the reader of this body said anything.

> **THE FOUR LINES, read out in a yard at Errow Reach at about the ninth hour of the morning of the twentieth of this month by the woman who keeps the four books, in the hand they were written in, and read back, and entered whole and not summarized:**
>
> **THE WELL AT CAULDRON REACH. SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH. FOURTH HOUR. FIVE FEET.**
>
> **WROTE IT BECAUSE A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF SPENT A MONTH FINDING OUT IT WAS AIMING AT THE WRONG DAY, AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE HANDED A NUMBER FOR A MONTH I DID NOT READ BY A WOMAN IT HAS NEVER MET. THE GAP IS ON THE WALL WHERE THE GAP IS. I HAVE PUT CHALK UNDER IT SO YOU CAN SEE I KNOW IT IS THERE.**
>
> **A MONTH BACK THERE WAS HALF AN INCH. IT IS STILL HERE IN CHALK ON THE STONE BY THE DOOR AND IT IS NOT A READING AND IT IS NOT IN THE COLUMN AND I AM NOT ADDING IT UP.**
>
> **PAID AT THIS HOUSEHOLD'S OWN COST AND NOBODY ELSE'S, AND NOBODY HAS EVER OFFERED.**

And then she read the fifth thing out, and it was a sheet with nothing at the top of it and a line for the day and a line for the hour and a line for the figure, and under the line for the figure two more lines, and the second of the two was headed NOTHING ELSE and it was empty, and the first of the two was not.

> **MEASURED TWICE.**
>
> **HE SENT ME A SHEET WITH TWO LINES UNDER THE FIGURE AND HE DID NOT ASK ME ANYTHING. I HAVE FILLED IN ONE OF THEM. I AM NOT GOING TO WRITE UNDER THE OTHER ONE AND I AM NOT GOING TO SAY WHY, BECAUSE HE WILL GET IT IN FOUR DAYS AND WILL NOT KNOW WHAT WAS GOING TO GO IN IT, AND I THINK THAT IS CORRECT, AND I HAVE BEEN DOING THIS WALL FOR TWELVE YEARS AND HE IS THE FIRST PERSON WHO HAS EVER SENT ME ANYTHING.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the line under NOTHING ELSE is empty and that he will not know what was going to go in it, and that he sent that sheet on the fourth of this month through the ordinary post with nothing else in the bag, unsigned, with no name and no stamp on it, and did not ask her to do anything, and entered the reason, which is that on the seventh of this month he stood in a yard of about eleven people and read out a paper with his own name at the top of it that had come off one reading, and that a woman four hundred miles off who has been sending a figure down a road at her own cost for twelve years had never been sent anything at all.

"**I am not going to make anything out of it in a yard. She read it twice, she wrote two words, and she left the other line empty on purpose and told me so in writing, and that is all that has happened, and I did not ask her to and I am not going to thank her for it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then he read the figure against what this holding already had, and he did it out loud, and the woman who does the sums had the four earlier figures in her own hand off the wall of the fen's own book before he asked for them.

"**Five feet on the sixteenth of a month four months back. Five feet on the sixteenth of a month two months back. Five feet on the sixteenth of last month, and five feet on the sixteenth of this month. Four figures and the same figure four month running, and I said in a yard on the seventh of this month that the difference between that and a sheet in four pockets is not the figure and is not the distance.**"

"**And I am going to say the other half of it in about four sentences because I have had nine days and because I have stopped being able to say it well. A number that has been read once says one thing. A number that says how many times it was read says something a village can act on, and the four villages could not act on that one either, and they acted on it anyway, and what they lost is real, and it is in a village's own arithmetic with the two ways checked.**"

"**That is not an argument about reading wells twice. A woman has read a well four times a year on her own wall with a nail and a string for twelve years and did not have to be told. I have had a rule in my own book for two years about a stop and it has never stopped me until the eleventh of this month, and it stopped me then.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he had not improved on any of it, and that this is the fourth time in forty days that he has said a thing in a yard and had somebody else finish it, and three of the four were women.

Then he entered what had not moved, and he did it in the ordinary voice and the man of about fifty wrote it and read it back, and the fen did not add to it and did not take anything off it.

"**The use log stands at fifteen lines through twenty days in which a sheet went to four gates, a launder was cut, a standing rule was used and prevented something, and a woman four hundred miles off read a well twice. The reason is entered so that nobody has to ask. A sheet is not a contact. A standing rule is not a contact. A launder cut by a village is not a contact. A not known is not a contact. A figure read twice by somebody else's hand is not a contact.**"

"**The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two, and it has been two for fifty days, and one of them is me, and it did not move this month and there is no reason it should. The count of persons who can hold a pattern for five is one and it is me and it did not move either. A man of sixty-eight says he has about four turns left in him before he cannot stand at that kerb at the second hour of a morning, and he is not going to be thanked for saying it and is not.**"

"**The four accounts of the same arm in this holding's book are four and they reconcile none of one another, and the head that went on the twenty-third of last month and did not go for a day and a night is not a mark and is not entered as one, and nothing said about that arm in twenty days is a fifth.**"

"**Whose order it is has been asked four times in this book and answered never, and it was not asked a fifth time this month and is not going to be asked in the yard on the thirtieth.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then, dated the twentieth of this month, in the hand of the engineer of record and read back before it was initialled, is the fifty-third note under section nine of the charter schedule. There is no charter, and the note says so on its face, and he wrote none in the middle of twenty days away and entered the reason.

*One reading. A figure that came off it went into four villages in four copies in five days and a village priced its own loss in its own arithmetic at ten pence a head a month and did not ask me one question, and the loss is real and it is entered with the ditch and the turn of the ground and the day, and it is not a thing I am going to write a paragraph under. A sheet read out in a yard of about eleven people carries a man's name at the top of it and the number of readings is not on it, and I did that, in about four seconds, on an evening when I was tired and could not put my arm in a trunk twice. Nobody said that out loud at the time and nobody has said it since and the man who finally said it was the man I hired to hold the other end of a barrow, in his own village, standing on a stone, without me in the room, and I have entered that he is not to be thanked for it and is not.*

*Two hundred and thirty-one trees on the ground above the west kerb, in my own hand, checked by me, since the twenty-fourth of a month four months back. A man of about fifty-eight said the number out loud on a Thursday in a stand of withy. A man of sixty-eight said he had known the same thing since the second of this month and had not said so. That is two of us and neither of us said it, and he was nine days late and I was four seconds late, and I do not know which of those is worse, which is his sentence and I am putting it down because I have not got a better one.*

*A draw that was not begun, on a rule of four lines with no fifth line, read out by a licensed man in his own words and entered by him as a thing not begun and not as a thing stopped. The count of the uses of that rule this season is one and it went up on a thing I did not do. Last season it was two and I used it twice to do what I wanted. A rule that is only counted when it is used to do something is not a rule about care.*

*Twenty-five hundredweight a day. Twelve pounds and ten shillings. Ten pence a head a month across about three hundred people, checked two ways by the village that owns the number. A village gave up the thing it was already short of and nobody asked it, and a woman of about fifty-one stopped me saying that as a sentence in a road before I said it, and the stopping is in this holding's book in her words.*

*And on the twentieth of this month a sheet came down a four-day road with a line on it that has never been on a line off that wall in twelve years, and it is two words, and I sent the sheet, and I did not ask her for anything, and I am not going to say one word about it in this note.*

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything tender and the fen entered both of those, and entered that the count of times he has caught himself and not said a thing in this month's history is three, and that the sentence about a man who is the only person who can do a thing being a bottleneck and not a system has been said in this fen five times and is not coming down, that he said it for the first time in a yard on the eleventh of last month and has said it since in this holding, in a yard at Errow Reach, in a yard at Marden and in a shed, and that the five times are the same sentence and he is not going to improve on it and is not going to stop saying it.

> Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the twentieth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a reading, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **four folded lines and a fifth thing that was not folded the same way came up the cart road in the ordinary post at about the ninth hour of the morning of the twentieth, being the fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth at a yard of four households four hundred miles off, at that household's own cost, on the same four-day road, and the hour of the reading and the hour of the arrival and the four days are all entered. THE FIGURE IS FIVE FEET, the fourth month running, and this holding now has four figures of that well and still does not have the tenth, and the month between the ninth and the eleventh is on no page in this book. The wall shows the gap where the tenth is not, the four feet of shortfall against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month nine months back is not decomposed, and the half inch of a month seven months back is not carried forward here. NO ENTRY ANYWHERE FOR THAT WELL COMING BACK IS MADE AND NONE IS GOING TO BE. On the same paper there was a line that has never been on a line off that wall in twelve years, being MEASURED TWICE, and a second line headed NOTHING ELSE which is EMPTY, and the reader of this body sent that sheet on the fourth of this month through the ordinary post, unsigned, with no name and no stamp on it, and did not ask her to do anything, and entered that he is not going to make anything of it in a yard and is not going to thank her. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND THE REASON IS ENTERED: a sheet is not a contact, a standing rule is not a contact, a launder cut by a village is not a contact, a not known is not a contact, and a figure read twice by somebody else's hand is not a contact. THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO AND HAS BEEN TWO FOR FIFTY DAYS and the count for five is one and neither moved this month. The four accounts of the same arm are four and nothing said about that arm in twenty days is a fifth. The standing rule was used once this season, on the eleventh, on a draw that was not begun, and the count last season was two and the two counts are two counts. Whose order it is has been asked four times and answered never and was not asked a fifth time this month. THE COUNT OF REQUESTS THAT A MAN'S INITIAL BE PUT AGAINST NOTHING IS FIFTY-THREE and has not moved since the first of this month, and the man of about fifty has not asked since the first of this month and the reason is entered, which is that a man with four names in four books this month is not a man who needs a line of his own with nothing against it. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And that was the twentieth of this month and there are ten days left in it. The door at nine hundred yards off is read on the first and the thirtieth and was not read on the twentieth, the seventh column stands where it stood on the first, the drawer was not opened, and the man of about seventy was not fetched a second time in one month. The standing count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is not written down in this paragraph, because the reader of this body said out loud in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month that he was going to stop counting for the rest of the season on the subject of questions and he is stopping, and a count a man has promised to stop keeping is not a count he may keep quietly.

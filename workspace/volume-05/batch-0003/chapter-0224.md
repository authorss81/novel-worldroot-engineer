# Chapter 224

## Ninety Hundredweight, And A Yard Of Four Households

The sixteenth of this month was a Saturday and the ninth day of every ninth day came round again for the thirteenth time since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back, and about eleven people were in a yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the morning, and the man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it, and the fen entered that and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is four and has not moved in six days.

Harlan Vetch came round the corner of the building with the barrow about ten minutes to the hour, and it went on the steelyard, and the man of about fifty read the figure out and each of the four bodies of households read it back after him, one at a time, standing where they could see the dial.

> **THE FIGURE ON THE STEELYARD, read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn: NINETY-ONE HUNDREDWEIGHT, at about the second hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month, on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles, by one man, in about an hour and a third. It is a different number from the number on the seventh and it is the same man.**

And it went into the hollow on the west side inside nine square yards, and it took about four minutes, and the four of them watched it go in, and nobody said anything while it did, and the fen entered that nobody said anything while it did and entered that about nine feet off the hollow the bare ring in that ground was nine yards wide on the fourteenth of this month and is not narrower this morning, and that the fen entered that and did not say anything about it, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has been told on Thursday that the ground he measured was two hundred and thirty-one trees is not going to walk out nine feet and look at a ring of bare ground and pretend he has not.

The barrow came back about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the lids on and the wheel making the noise it makes, and it went into the shed it goes into, and the man of the north row with the cough carried one of the two baskets up off the ground without being asked and put it down, and the fen entered that, and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is four and has moved, and that this one is the fifth, and that he was not asked to be in it and that he was not asked to carry the basket either, and that the difference between the two is about four seconds and that a body that has been in a yard four times this season without being asked has stopped noticing the difference.

And then a name went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY, and a name went into the space headed WITNESSED BY, and that is the fourth time, and the first rule of that sheet has had a name in it four times in eleven years and the second rule has had eleven years of figures in it and one of them this morning is not a return.

Harlan Vetch did that on his own and did not ask, and the fen entered that he did it on his own and did not ask and entered the reason, which is that the man had been in that yard on the fourteenth of this month at about the eighth hour of the morning and had stood in a stand of withy for two hours and had heard a figure about a sill, and that a man who has heard a figure is not obliged to carry it and had carried it.

> **THE SECOND RULE OF THE SCHEDULE, headed OBSERVATIONS AND REMARKS, into which every figure that man has made in eleven years has gone, and into which, for the first time in eleven years, a figure that is not a return went on the sixteenth of this month at about the fourth hour of the evening, in his own hand, without being asked, and read back by the man of about fifty and by the woman who keeps the four books: STANDING WATER ABOVE THE SILL AT THE WEST KERB, ONE FOOT TEN INCHES, FOURTH HOUR OF THE EVENING. HE PUT THE DAY ON IT. HE PUT THE WORD HE DID NOT KNOW ON IT AS WELL, WHICH IS THAT HE DID NOT TAKE IT, HE TOOK IT OFF A MAN WHO TOOK IT FOUR TIMES.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was at the wall and said nothing and that the count of times he has been in a yard and said nothing this month is one.

And then the reader of this body said the thing he had been carrying since the fifteenth of this month, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty-six.

"**I am going to say a thing in this yard about a well four hundred miles off and it is going to sound like a joke and it is not a joke, and I am saying it because I have been not-saying it since the seventh of this month and a thing you are not saying in a yard is a thing you are keeping, and I do not keep things.**"

"**That wall has the same figure on it four month running. Five feet. And the first of this month there is a number on a piece of paper in four pockets in four villages that came off one reading at one kerb at one hour, and I said out loud on the seventh that I had not put it into hundredweight, and the reason I gave was arithmetic. The real reason is that there is a difference between those two things and the difference is not the figure and it is not the distance.**"

"**On that wall there is nothing on it that says how many times anybody read it. And on that sheet there is everything on it except that. And I have had three weeks to work out how to say that in a yard without making a speech and I have not worked it out, so I am going to say it badly.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and did not fill it, and the man of about fifty did not write any of the last three paragraphs down, and the fen entered that he told him not to, out loud, before he said them, and that the reason was that a figure somebody has said out loud in a yard can be argued with and a figure nobody has written down can only be believed or not, and this holding has nine questions in a book and none of them has been answered and he was not going to put the tenth thing into it.

And then, at about the fourth hour of the same morning, four hundred miles and four days off, in a yard of four households that this holding has never stood in and has never sent anybody to, a woman who is not of this holding and whose name is not in this book let a string with a bit of lead on the end of it down her own well, and brought it up wet to a nail in the plinth of her own door that she drove in herself in the first year, and set it against the plinth and marked it with the nail at the level of the water and not at the level of the kerb, and that is the whole of the method and it has not changed in twelve years.

Then she let it down again and brought it up again and marked it again on the same wall, and then she wrote the thirteenth figure under the twelfth in her own hand, and the figure is **five feet**, and it is the fourth month running that the figure has been five feet, and the yard was cold and there was nobody else up, and one of the four households had a lamp in a window and did not come out, as on every other reading day in twelve years.

The wall, in wall order, from the left where the sun comes in: nine feet. Five feet and a half. Five feet five inches. Five feet four inches. Five feet and two inches. Five feet three inches. Five feet and two inches. Five feet and one inch. Five feet. And then a place where there is nothing at all, the chalk has never touched it and the plaster under it is the same color as the plaster round it, and it is the widest gap on that wall and it is a whole month. And then five feet. And then five feet. And then five feet.

She went along the wall and put her thumb under the two marks she had just made, one under the other, and then wiped the thumb on her apron, and that is not a method and it is not a reading and it is not going in anything.

There is a ledge on that wall about four feet up that the figures stop at because she cannot reach above it, and the last figure is on the ledge, and above the ledge the plaster is bare and has been bare for twelve years, and she has never once been able to write a figure above the ledge and has never once said so, and the ladder in that yard does not reach.

And the four households in that yard are four households because four households share that well and one of them has had a lamp in a window on every reading day in twelve years and has never come out, and she has never said who that is, and nobody at four hundred miles and four days off has ever asked her, and the fen entered that nobody has ever asked her and entered that he is not going to and entered the reason, which is that a man who has begun sending sheets to a yard of four households this month has one figure in his hands at that well and four hundred miles of road between them and nothing else at all.

And the four lines went down the four-day road at that household's own cost that morning, folded and sealed and given to the ordinary post, and this month there was a fifth thing in the packet, and it had come up the same road eight days before in the same bag with nothing else in it, and it is a sheet with nothing at the top of it and a line for the day and a line for the hour and a line for the figure, and **under the line for the figure there are two more lines and the second of the two is headed NOTHING ELSE**, and there is no instruction on it anywhere and it is not signed and there is no name on it and there is no stamp, and it came to a yard of four households through the ordinary post because a man put it in a bag.

She filled in one of the two lines and left the other one empty, and she wrote the reason for leaving it empty on the face of the sheet under nothing else, in her own hand, and the four lines went down the road with the figure and the day and the hour and the two sentences she has written every year since the eleventh, and under all of it, the new thing.

> **MEASURED TWICE.**
>
> **HE SENT ME A SHEET WITH TWO LINES UNDER THE FIGURE AND HE DID NOT ASK ME ANYTHING AND HE IS FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF. I HAVE FILLED IN ONE OF THEM. I AM NOT GOING TO WRITE ANYTHING UNDER THE OTHER ONE AND I AM NOT GOING TO SAY WHY, BECAUSE HE WILL GET IT IN FOUR DAYS AND HE WILL NOT KNOW WHAT WAS GOING TO GO IN IT, AND I THINK THAT IS CORRECT, AND I HAVE BEEN DOING THIS WALL FOR TWELVE YEARS AND HE IS THE FIRST PERSON WHO HAS EVER SENT ME ANYTHING.**

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a figure, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **the thirteenth hold since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back was made at about the second hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month and the return was NINETY-ONE HUNDREDWEIGHT, weighed on the steelyard and read back by each of the four bodies of households in turn, which is a different number from the seventh. A name went into the first rule of the schedule and a name went into WITNESSED BY, and for the first time in eleven years a figure that is not a return went into the second rule of that schedule, being the standing water above the sill at the west kerb at one foot ten inches at the fourth hour of the evening, put in by Harlan Vetch in his own hand without being asked, and he put the day on it and he put on it that he did not take the reading himself and took it off the reader of this body, who has taken it four times and could not be got to take it a fifth. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD that a wall four hundred miles off carries the same figure four month running and that a piece of paper in four pockets in four villages came off one reading, and that the difference between those two things is not the figure and is not the distance, and that the wall says nothing about how many times it was read and the paper says everything except that. HE TOLD THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY NOT TO WRITE IT DOWN, OUT LOUD, BEFORE HE SAID IT, and gave the reason, which is that a figure said out loud in a yard can be argued with and a figure nobody has written down can only be believed or not, and that he was not going to put a tenth thing into a book that has nine questions in it. FOUR HUNDRED MILES AND FOUR DAYS OFF, in a yard of four households, the occupier of the well at Cauldron Reach read that well for the thirteenth time at about the fourth hour of the same morning, with her own nail and her own string, in her own hand, on her own wall, and the figure is FIVE FEET, the same figure four month running, and the wall shows the gap where the tenth figure is not, and the two marks are on the wall under each other for the first time in twelve years and she wiped her thumb and it is not a method. FOUR LINES WENT DOWN THE ROAD AT THAT HOUSEHOLD'S OWN COST, and for the first time in twelve years there was a fifth thing in the packet, being a sheet with a line for the day and a line for the hour and a line for the figure and two more lines under the figure of which the second is headed NOTHING ELSE, and there is no instruction on it and no signature and no name on it, and she filled in one of the two lines and left the other empty and wrote under it that she is not going to say why. NO ENTRY ANYWHERE FOR THAT WELL COMING BACK IS MADE AND NONE IS GOING TO BE. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the man of about fifty did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing, and the fen entered that he did not ask and entered that the count of that request stands at fifty-three and that it has not moved since the first of this month, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has four names in four books this month is not a man who needs a line of his own with nothing against it.

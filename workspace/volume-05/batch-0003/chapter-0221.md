# Chapter 221

## The Cut

The eleventh of this month was a Monday and it rained in the morning and stopped at about the first hour of the afternoon, and eleven people of Marden came down off the high ground at about the third hour of the afternoon with two saws and a barrow each and a bucket, and the bucket was for the sawdust and somebody had written on the side of it in chalk, and the fen entered the writing and entered that he did not ask and did not want to.

The east kerb is the first of the four parts and it is the same stone as the second and the fourth, and the third part is the one that is not. The sill is a flat stone about nine inches down and the launder comes up to it out of the stone in a cut a foot and a half wide and a foot and four inches deep at the head, and it is the smallest of the four and it has always been the smallest of the four, and the man of about fifty had measured it on the ninth of last month with a tape because the licensed man had asked him to, and the measurement is in his book with the day on it.

They cut at the head of the sill, in the stone, about a foot and a half back from the edge, and the water was in it up to about four inches and it was at that height because the fourth hour of the afternoon is when the launder is lowest, and a body of households had worked that out for itself over eleven years without being told.

They came up off the track at about the third hour of the afternoon in a line and not in a group, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came last and came on foot and there was nobody in that eleven who had been given a reason to come except the sheet and the sheet does not say why the fourth hour of the afternoon.

The water in the east launder at the second hour of the afternoon is about a foot up on the sill and at the fourth hour it is about four inches, and that is not a fact about anybody's belief, it is a fact about about two thousand yards of drain above that village that has been the same for a hundred years, and a body of households worked it out for itself over eleven years without being told and the fen entered that the count of things a village has worked out for itself that an engineer has had to be told is three, and that he is not going to put the other two in a book because one of them is about a gate.

The reader of this body was there and held nothing and did not touch the branch and did not touch the saws, and the fen entered the distance at which he stood, which was about nine feet, and entered the reason, which is that a man from a fen standing at nine feet off a cut that a village is making is either a supervisor or a witness and a village that has been asked to cut its own launder by a man with a name at the top of a sheet does not need either of those two things in its yard.

The man of the north row with the cough was at the kerb and was not asked to be there and did not go near the saw, and the fen entered that and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is four and that he has now stood in one that was not a yard and was about two hundred yards off, and that the count of yards is not the number and he is entering it anyway because his own book has a column for it.

Nobody thanked anybody.

And about thirty-eight minutes in, the man of about fifty said the fact out loud, and the fen entered that he said it and did not add to it and that four people said it after him in four different voices in about two minutes, and the fact is this: **the cut is at the kerb and not at the core.**

"**We are cutting a launder in a stone and we are not touching the heartroot. Everything under this ground from the board to about four feet down is untouched, and the water on the other three sides is not in the same pipe as this and has not been for eleven years.**"

And somebody at the back said the second half of it and it is the half that matters, and the fen entered it in the same entry and entered that it was said by a man with a new-handled saw and not by anybody else.

"**And that is the only reason eleven of us can do this in an afternoon. If you want to find out what a launder does after you cut it you have to go to the core, and the core is under a board, and none of us is going under a board.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then at about the fourth hour and fifty minutes the two saws were through, and the water in the cut above the sill went down in about nine seconds and made no noise, and the man of about fifty caught what came out of it in the bucket, and the eleven of them stood and looked at a stone with nothing coming over it, and the fen entered the length of that and entered that about forty seconds of it is in his own hand with the day and the hour on it and that he is not going to say in this book what forty seconds of it looked like to a man with a mark on his arm.

And the licensed man did not write one word down for the whole thirty-eight minutes, and afterwards the reader of this body asked him why and he gave the professional half of his voice and the fen entered that it is the professional half.

"**Because there is no form for it, and a man who writes on the back of a sheet at a stone kerb is a man who has made a form, and I have been on the roll eleven years and I have watched four men make forms in four districts and three of them were worse than no form. When I write this afternoon I am going to write it in my own book, where it is a note by a licensed man and not a record by anybody, and that is the only place it can go and it will stay there.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the licensed man was right and that the fen had been about to ask the man of about fifty to write the whole afternoon down, and that the count of times this body has been stopped by a man who is not the engineer of record in this month's history is one, and that he was going to be the second one in two years and did not have to be.

And the water in the ditch above Marden, ninety feet of it, is not fed by that launder. That is not a fact about this day. It is a fact nobody had ever looked at.

Then at about the fifth hour of the afternoon the reader of this body went to the stone and put his hand on the edge of the sill and said out loud what he was going to do, and gave the reason before he did it, in front of about eleven people, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty-three.

"**I am going to find out whether the ground under this sill is still carrying. If it is still carrying then this launder is shut and it may be opened again in this season. If it is not carrying then it is not shut, it is dead, and nobody can find that out without going to the core, and I am not going to the core, and the difference between those two is worth a great deal to about three hundred people and it is not worth me.**"

"**And I am going to do it the way I have to do it, which is a draw, and there is a rule about draws and I wrote it and it is four lines and there is no fifth line and you have all heard it read out at a yard at the fen on the first of this month.**"

And the licensed man said the first line out loud, before anything else happened, in the professional half of his voice, and he said it to the engineer of record and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he said it to him and not to the yard and that the fen did not ask him to say it to the yard.

"**The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.**"

And then he said why the return was not carrying, and it was the fen who said that, and it was the plainest sentence he said that day.

"**The return on this side of that ground is not carrying because the water that was coming up that stone is in a bucket and a barrow and eleven people's boots, and I can see all of it, and there is nothing under this sill tonight that is doing the work it was doing yesterday morning.**"

And nobody argued with it and the fen entered that nobody argued with it and entered that the licensed man wrote it in his own book, in his own hand, with the hour on it, and that he wrote that the draw was not begun and not that it was stopped, and that the difference between those two is in his book and not in this one and that the licensed man gave the reason for the difference out loud and the reason was that a draw that is not begun is a thing a man decided and a draw that is stopped is a thing a rule did, and this holding's book is a book about what rules did.

"**Then the count of the uses of that rule this season is one, and it went up this afternoon on a thing I did not do, and I am entering that, and I am entering what it means, because I have had that rule in my own book for two years and I have never once used it to stop myself.**"

"**I have used it to let myself do a thing with a licensed man standing there so that a man with a mark on my arm has somebody else's name on the hour. And I have counted those, twice last season, and I have counted them because they were things I wanted to do. A rule that is only ever counted when it is used to do something is not a rule about care. It is a permission with a number on it. Today it stopped me and nobody is going to put that on a count about what I got done, and I am putting it on the count about the rule.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of the uses of the standing rule this season is one and the count last season was two, and that the two counts are two counts and are not the same count, and that the use log has fifteen lines on it and this got no line, because a draw is not a contact and a rule is not a contact and this holding does not have a column for either.

Then the loss went in, whole, and it went in in the man of about fifty's hand because he had the book and because the licensed man had gone back to the sill and the eleven of Marden were putting sawdust in a barrow.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the sixth hour of the afternoon of the eleventh of this month, at the east kerb of the Four Elbows, on the standing of a loss, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **THE EAST LAUNDER OF THE FOUR ELBOWS WAS CUT IN THE STONE AT THE HEAD OF THE SILL at about the fourth hour and fifty minutes of the afternoon of the eleventh of this month by eleven people of Marden with two saws and a barrow each and a bucket, at the reading of a sheet that carries one man's name at the top of it. THE CUT IS AT THE KERB AND NOT AT THE CORE, and four people said that in about two minutes and one of them said the half of it that matters, which is that it is the only reason eleven of a village can do it in an afternoon, and that nobody is going under a board. TWENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY IS NOT COMING UP THAT LAUNDER AND WILL NOT COME UP IT THIS SEASON. THE WATER IN THE DITCH NINETY FEET ABOVE MARDEN IS NOT FED BY THAT LAUNDER, WHICH IS A FACT NOBODY HAD EVER LOOKED AT AND WAS NOT LOOKED AT TODAY. THE GROUND ABOVE THAT VILLAGE IS TURNED ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH, WHICH IS IN TWENTY DAYS, AND A TURN TAKES A FORTNIGHT WITH WATER IN THE DITCH AND A MONTH WITHOUT. THE STANDING RULE WAS READ, ITS FIRST LINE BY A LICENSED MAN IN HIS OWN WORDS, AND THE DRAW WAS NOT BEGUN, AND IT WAS NOT BEGUN BECAUSE THE RETURN ON THAT GROUND IS NOT CARRYING, AND IT WAS ENTERED BY THE LICENSED MAN IN HIS OWN BOOK AS A THING NOT BEGUN AND NOT AS A THING STOPPED, AND THE REASON HE GAVE FOR THAT DIFFERENCE WAS THAT A DRAW THAT IS NOT BEGUN IS A THING A MAN DECIDED. THE COUNT OF THE USES OF THAT RULE THIS SEASON IS ONE AND IT WENT UP ON A THING THAT WAS NOT DONE, and the count last season was two, and the two counts are two counts. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND THIS ENTERED NOTHING ON IT. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And this is the whole of what the licensed man put in his own book that afternoon, and he read it out in a yard at about the eighth hour of the evening in about nine people, and the reader of this body did not ask him to and did not stop him, and the fen entered that he was asked for nothing by anybody and that he read it out because he is a licensed man and it is his own book and that is the whole of his standing and he has never asked for more than it.

> **Coll Renner, licensed, for the Tarrow district, eleven years on the roll. My own book, my own hand, the eleventh day of this month, at about the eighth hour of the evening. I was at the east kerb of the Four Elbows from about the third hour of the afternoon until about the seventh. The east launder was cut in the stone at the head of the sill by eleven people of Marden with two saws. I did not hold it. I did not touch it. I wrote nothing down while it was being done and I want it written down that I wrote nothing down, because there is no form for this and the only thing I can do with a thing there is no form for is stand near it.**
>
> **At about the fifth hour of the afternoon the engineer of record of a holding with no charter stated that he was going to begin a draw at that kerb to find whether the ground under the cut is still carrying. I read the first line of his standing rule out loud before anything was done. The return on that ground is not carrying. THE DRAW WAS NOT BEGUN. I enter it in this book as a thing not begun and not as a thing stopped, and my reason is that a draw that is not begun is a decision and a draw that is stopped is an act of a rule, and the district record is a record of acts of rules, and if I put a decision in it under a rule then in about four years somebody is going to read this page and think that a rule did something on a ground it was not written for.**
>
> **I have never seen that happen before and I have been on the roll eleven years. I record it and I do not endorse it and I am not going to write a second page about it, and if the Assembly writes to me about it I will send them this page.**

And the barrow went off up the hill at about the seventh hour of the evening with the sawdust in it and the two saws went back in the shed and the man of about thirty-eight of Marden wrote the sawdust out of the gate count in her own hand, and the fen entered that she did and did not ask about it.

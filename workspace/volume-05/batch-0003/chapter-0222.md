# Chapter 222

## One Foot Ten Inches, Again

The twelfth of this month was a Tuesday and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden sent a line down the hill to a man who was not her village's, in the hand of a woman of about thirty-eight, and it went up the cart road with a woman on a cart, and it was four lines long.

> **THE DITCH ABOVE MARDEN IS DRY AT THE TOP END AND DRY AT THE BOTTOM END. THE TOP END WAS DRY ON THE TWELFTH AND THE BOTTOM END WAS DRY ON THE NINTH. WE ARE NOT ASKING YOU ANYTHING. WE ARE TELLING YOU BECAUSE YOU WERE AT THE TOP OF IT ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF LAST MONTH AND SAID IT WAS THE GROUND AND NOT THE CUT.**

And the reader of this body entered it in his own book with the day on it and did not write and ask which of the two ends went first, because a man who has a figure in a book with his name at the top of it does not get to ask a village a second question about a ditch until he has been asked one about the sill.

And he did not answer it either, and the fen entered that he did not answer it and entered the reason, which is that there is a second answer and it is the one he gave in a yard on the twenty-ninth of last month, and that a man who has said a thing once and then has the ground confirm it is a man who is going to say it again whether or not it is confirmed, and that this is the fault the sheet was not about and is the fault he has got, and that the count of the two is two and he is entering them together.

And Coll Renner was not on the stand on the fourteenth of this month and was not at any of the three kerbs that evening, and the fen entered that he was at his own head under a form and a rate four miles west, and entered the reason, which is that a licensed man who has written a page in his own book about a rule doing something at a stone kerb does not also stand in a stand of trees on the third day afterwards, and that the reason for that is not humility and is that he has nothing to add to a page of his own and a man who is standing in a wood looking at leaves is adding to it.

The thirteenth of this month was a Wednesday, and the reader of this body read the west sill at about the fourth hour of the evening and it was one foot ten inches, and that was the third time in forty-two days and it was the same figure, and he did not go to bed.

The fourteenth of this month was a Thursday and at about the eighth hour of the morning the reader of this body, the man of about fifty, the man of the north row with the cough and Harlan Vetch went out onto the eleven acres and stood in the west stand of willow for about two hours, and the reader of this body did not take a rule and did not take a tape and did not measure anything, and the man of about fifty did not write anything down, and both of those are entered because both of them were unusual and neither of them was explained at the time.

They stood in it because the reader of this body had a figure that would not go anywhere, and the man of about fifty said one thing about that on the way out and the fen entered it and did not answer it.

"**You have read that sill three times and it is the same three times, and a figure that has stopped moving is a figure you cannot get any further with, and there is only one other thing a man can do with a number that has stopped, and that is go and look at what is on top of it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And what was on top of it was a hundred and forty-one trees, of a kind that is called withy on the west side of that ground and has been called withy by four generations of three villages, standing in about four acres of eleven, and they were in leaf, all of them, to the top, and the ground under them was black with last year's leaves and wet about two inches under those, and there was a smell coming off it that anybody who has stood in a willow stand in the second month of the year would know in about four seconds.

The stand is planted in lines and the lines run about eighteen feet apart and the lines run north and south, so that the west kerb is at the foot of them, and the first line of them is about nine yards from the kerb and there is bare ground between the kerb and the first line about nine yards wide and about two hundred yards long, and that bare strip is the only bare ground on that side and there is no reason anybody has ever given for it and the fen entered that there is no reason anybody has ever given for it and that he has walked it four times this month and has not found one.

A withy stand cut for withy is cut on a rotation and the rotation is about three years and the last cutting on that side was two years ago, and the fen entered that he knows the rotation and that he has not been the one to cut any of it and that the man of the north row with the cough has been the one to cut it for nine years and did not say so on the day and said it on the fourteenth in about nine words and then would not say anything else about it.

And the man of the north row with the cough said one thing there, and the fen entered it, and the man of about fifty did not write it down because he was not asked to and because it was not a figure.

"**I have known that stand was in leaf since the second of this month and I was in a cart shed at Errow Reach on the second of this month and I did not say so, and you had a sheet in your hand from the seventh of last month saying a branch has to come off, and I had nine days and a shed and about eleven people in it, and I did not open my mouth.**"

And then Harlan Vetch said the sentence, and it took him about four seconds to say and he did not say it twice, and the fen did not take it off him and did not improve it and did not say a word for about a minute afterward.

"**I have been putting ninety hundredweight of half-rotted willow and the litter off one holding and leaf mould out of one stable into that ground on the ninth day of every ninth day for eleven years, and it is dead when it goes in, and I have never once seen it drink, and I have never once seen anything come up out of it either, and I have never known what it does.**"

"**And you have had two hundred and thirty-one in your own hand since the twenty-fourth of a month four months back, given to you by two people and checked by you, and the west side is a hundred and forty-one of them, and they went into leaf between your two readings, and you had the number of them and you did not put the two figures together, and I am not saying that to make you feel something. I am saying it because I have been putting dead wood into that ground for eleven years for nothing and I would like one person in this book to write down that it was for nothing, and there is not going to be anybody else who is going to say it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough did not say anything during that minute and a half, which is the first time in thirty-five days that he has been in a place where a man said something and said nothing.

And then they stood there and read the west sill at about the fourth hour of the evening of the fourteenth of this month, the same kerb, the same sill, the same hour, the same man, the same rule against the same stone, and it is the fourth time and it is the same figure, and then they read the other two, which nobody in this book's history has ever read.

The north sill, the third of the four parts, which is the one that is not the same stone, was **two feet one inches**, and there is no earlier figure for it anywhere in this book and there is not going to be one, and the reader of this body entered that he is not going to go back and make one, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has found out that a figure of his has been read once will get a second reading of everything he can reach in about four years, and that the second reading of the north sill will be taken in about four years by somebody and not by him, and that this is correct and that he is not going to go and take it first.

The north sill takes Havergate's launder and there are about ninety trees on the north side of that ground and they were in bud and not in leaf, and the fen entered that he looked at them and entered the reason he looked, which is that if ninety trees in bud and a hundred and forty-one in leaf produce a difference of nine inches in a trunk then the north side is the test and the north side is the control, and that a figure with a control in it is a figure a village can act on, and that he had a control in front of him at about the fourth hour of the evening of the fourteenth of this month and had not used it until the following week, and that this is the second time in six days that he has had something in his hand that would have answered the thing he had published, and that the count of that is two and he is entering both of them in one line.

The east sill, the first of the four parts, which had been cut in the stone four days ago, was **two feet four inches**, and it was carrying nothing at all, and a dry sill is a number about a stone and not a number about a ground.

And the three of those were not figures that anybody could act on and the reader of this body said so out loud, at about the fifth hour of the evening, to about nine people at a wall, and the man of about fifty wrote it down and read it back, and the fen entered the count of asks in a yard with the reason given first and it is twenty-four in this month's history and he gave the reason before the number.

"**I am not going to put a difference in hundredweight on any of those three numbers and I am not going to tell you what nine inches is worth, and I gave the reason for that in a yard on the seventh of this month in front of about eleven people and I am not going to give it again because a reason given twice in a yard becomes a rule and I have had enough of those.**"

"**And I am going to say the three things that are true about them, and none of them is a number.**"

"**The one that fell is the one with a hundred and forty-one trees standing on the same ground, and the one that did not fall is the one with about ninety on a different part of it, and the one that did not fall is carrying nothing at all because a village cut it four days ago and nobody in this yard had anything to do with it.**"

"**The falling stopped. It is one foot ten inches and it has been one foot ten inches for eight days and the fall was over inside those eight days and it is not a wound, because a wound does not stop, and I have been calling it a thing going down since the sixth of this month and I would like that word struck out of my own book.**"

And then the man of the north row with the cough said the other half of it, and the fen entered that he had known it was coming and had not said it himself and entered that he is not going to.

"**And the falling stopped on about the second or third of this month, on about the say of a fortnight ago, and the man in the fen went up a hill on the ninth and had a sheet in his hand, and I was in a shed at Errow on the second of this month with a hundred and forty-one trees coming into leaf in my head and I did not open my mouth.**"

And the reader of this body said the one thing he said that evening, and it was one sentence, and it was said to about nine people at a wall, and the man of about fifty wrote it down and read it back and the fen did not take it back and did not add to it and did not apologise, and the reason he did not apologise is entered and is that there is nothing to be sorry for, because a man measured a thing correctly and did not measure it twice, and a village acted on it, and the loss is real, and the loss is not his fault and is his.

"**A man who has a figure in a book with his name at the top of it does not go back to the ground to look for a second reason, and that is not a fault of method, and I have got the method right, and I want the two of those written down in the same line because everybody who reads that sheet in four years is going to be told that I made a mistake about inches, and I did not.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that no hold was made on that day by anybody and that the count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two and did not move, and that he is not going to say that count out loud in a yard again in this season, and that a count a man has said five times is a count other people say for him, and that it was going to be six times on the fourteenth of this month and that he found out about that in a yard on the twenty-second of last month and has been not saying it since.

And the standing count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken stands at nine and was not moved on that day and was not said out loud, because he said in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month that he was going to stop counting for the rest of the season on the subject of questions and that he is stopping, and a count that a man has promised to stop keeping is not a count he is allowed to keep quietly.

And the count of times he has caught himself and not said a thing in this month's history is two, and the second one was a man of about fifty at a gate on the third day of this month about a stand of trees.

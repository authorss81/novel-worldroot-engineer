# Chapter 218

## One Foot Ten Inches

They were on the cart road five days and the reader of this body did not write any of it down, and gave the reason on the first morning at the gate with the barrow in his hand, in front of the other four, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty-one.

"**I am not going to write a road down. A road that a man is on instead of a ground he is standing on has changed the subject, and this holding's book is a book about grounds.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the fen entered that nobody said anything to that, and entered that the man of about fifty wrote none of the five days down either and did not ask him to, and that the two of them had agreed on that on the second morning without talking about it.

The second day was a Saturday and the fourth was a Monday and the sixth was a Wednesday, and on the third day they passed the eleven acres of standing willow from the top of the cart road and the man of about fifty said one thing about it and the fen entered the thing and did not improve on it.

"**A hundred and forty-one on the west side and about ninety on the north, and the west side is in leaf and the north side is a fortnight behind it, and I have been looking at those two stands every time I have come up this road for a month and I have not written one word about it, and the reason I have not written it is that it is a thing about trees and I keep a book about water.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and did not say a word back to him about it, and the count of times he has caught himself and not said a thing in this month's history is one and it is not going to be two for a man who is not the engineer of record.

The cart road goes east for two days along a dyke and then north-east for three along the top of the high ground, and on the fourth day they passed the mouth of the drain that takes the water off the flats at Low Wether, and the water in it was about two feet down and brown, and the man of about fifty said one thing about it and the fen entered it.

"**That drain has been about two feet down every time I have come up this road since the eleventh of last month and I have written it in nothing, and there is a quarter of a mile of ground at the top of it that a man from a fen said he did not know about in a yard on the twenty-fifth of last month, and those two facts are in the same drainage and I have put them in the same place in my head for twenty days and I have not put them on the same line in a book and I am not going to today.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and did not answer it, and entered the reason, which is that there is nothing in the question yet and a man who puts a coincidence in a book on the twentieth day of a month is a man who will have put three coincidences in a book by the fortieth.

They came into the yard at Errow Reach at about the third hour of the afternoon of the sixth of this month, which was a Wednesday, five days and not four, and the man of about fifty entered the day and the hour and the number of days, and the fen entered that he had said five days in a yard at the fen on the first of this month and that five is what it took.

The road is five days and it has been five days for the eleven years anybody has been counting, and the fen entered that he has made it eleven times in thirty days, and entered that he is entering that in a yard of about nine people because one man in it has not been east of the dyke since the eleventh of last month and is owed a fact about it.

There were about nine people in that yard and there had been about eleven on most days of the last month and the fen entered the difference and did not ask about it, because a yard is not a count and two bodies of households that have stopped walking five days east have not gone anywhere and have not stopped needing the water either.

The man of the north row with the cough was at the wall with his back against it and he had not been asked to be there, and the fen entered that, and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is three and has not moved in twenty-nine days.

Coll Renner was there and had been there for four of the five days and had opened his own head under a form and written a figure and an hour in his own book and shown it to nobody, and the fen did not ask to see it and did not ask what the figure was.

And Harlan Vetch was not there, and the fen entered that he was not there and that the man of about fifty had said at the second gate on the fifth day that the man would be at his own place and would come when the ninth day came, and that the ninth day is the seventh of this month.

Then at about the fourth hour of the evening the reader of this body walked out to the west kerb with a rule and a bit of chalk and a lamp, and Hesta Lyle came with him because she said she was going to come and he did not ask her to and did not tell her not to, and there was nobody else at that kerb but the two of them and a lamp.

The west kerb is the third of the four parts and it is not the same stone as the other three. The main trunk is cut in the stone under the ground and is brought up to a sill at the kerb, and the sill is a flat stone about nine inches down and about five feet back from the edge, and the water stands on it, and anybody at that node can walk to it in the dark.

He put the rule against the sill and he read it, and then he wrote it on the back of a sheet in his own hand with the day and the hour on it, and then he stood there with the lamp in his hand for a while, and the fen entered how long.

"**One foot ten inches.**"

And the woman who keeps the four books said the other one out loud, because she had his own book in her hand, because he had handed it to her at the gate on the second day and told her to bring it, and she had not opened it since.

"**The first of last month. The same kerb. The fourth hour of the evening. Two feet seven inches.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the arithmetic himself, out loud, to two people and a lamp.

"**Thirty-one inches and twenty-two inches, and thirty-one less twenty-two is nine inches. And the sixth of this month less the first of last month is thirty-five days. Nine inches in thirty-five days, at the same kerb, at the same hour, by the same man, with the same rule against the same sill, and I did all of that correctly and I am going to write that down as well and I am not going to let the fact that the answer is bad take the method off it.**"

The west kerb is the third of the four parts and it is the one that is not the same stone as the other three, and it is about nine inches down and about five feet back from the edge, and the cut above it is a foot and four inches deep and a foot and a half wide and the stone of the cut is dressed and the dressing is old enough that there is a lip on the left side of it about a quarter of an inch deep where a hundred years of somebody's hands have gone in and out with a bucket.

The water standing on the sill is not still. It moves about once in every four or five seconds from the west side, and it makes no sound at that, and the lamp does not show anything on the surface at all, and the only way to read it is to put the rule in against the sill and let the water come up the wood and go down again, and that is what he did, twice inside about ninety seconds, and the first time he took the rule out and looked at the wet mark on it and did not say the number, and the second time he said the number.

And the woman who keeps the four books said the other half of it, and the fen did not take it off her and did not soften it.

"**You have two figures and they are thirty-five days apart and you have read that sill once tonight. In four years this holding has a thing in it where a man puts his hand in a thing and reads it, and I have watched you do it about nine hundred times, and you have never once read a thing twice in one evening on the same evening for the same sheet.**"

"**I know why and I am not going to ask you in a yard about it and I am not going to pretend I do not know why, and if you want to tell me then tell me and if you do not then I am going to stand here until you have put the lamp down.**"

And the reader of this body said why, and it was true, and it was about his own body after the twenty-third of last month, and he gave it as a fact and not as an excuse, and the fen entered that a true reason given in a yard is still a reason and that a man who has a true reason and does not have a figure is going to end up with a true reason and no figure.

"**Reading that sill means putting my right hand into that trunk up to the forearm and holding it there while a man with a lamp counts out loud, and I did that on the twenty-third of last month for four hours across two branches and the mark on this arm is four inches and branches twice and it is not a second mark, and the head went at the second hour of the afternoon and it did not go for a day and a night.**"

"**So that is why I did not read it a second time, and I am telling you the reason before the conclusion and I am entering that the reason is mine and is not anybody else's business, and I am entering the other half of it now and it is this: I did not read it a second time because a second reading would have taken another twenty minutes and it is the sixth day of a month and there are about nine people in that yard and every one of them came here to get something.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it.

And then the reader of this body asked her a question, and he gave the reason for asking it before he asked it, and the count of that in this month's history is twenty-two, and the man of about fifty wrote it down and the fen entered that he wrote it down because a licensed man is five days west and does not go home on a Wednesday evening.

"**Is there a number for that sill in any of the four books. Not mine. Any of yours.**"

"**No.**"

"**Has anybody ever asked for one.**"

"**No, and I have been keeping four books for nineteen years and I have never been asked for a number of anything at that node by anybody, and there is no column for it in any of the four, and the four of us have been counting what comes out of that ground for nineteen years and not one of us has ever counted what is in it.**"

And then she said the half of it that is hers and not the fen's, and the fen entered that it is hers and did not improve on it.

"**You have just been given a not known by a woman with four books instead of by an engineer, and I have not had to look at anything to do it, and I would like that written down somewhere, because in about four years somebody is going to say that the engineers of this district do not know what is under that ground, and they are right, and it took me nine seconds and a man took nine days.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of not knowns standing against him in this book's history is two, and that he is not going to improve on either of them and is not going to say which of the two he has thought about more.

Then Hesta Lyle said the last thing she said that evening, and she said it to the sill and not to him.

"**Nine inches in thirty-five days is not a small number and it is not a large number and I have been keeping the four books for nineteen years and I have never had a number for that sill at all, and you have had two in your own book and you have never had two at once. That is not a fault tonight. I am putting it down because in about four years one of the four of them is going to want a third and there is not going to be a rule about which of the two anybody has to keep.**"

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the sixth day of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a figure, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **the party was on the cart road five days, from the second to the sixth of this month, and no part of the road is entered, and the reader of this body gave the reason at the gate on the first morning and the man of about fifty wrote none of it down and did not ask him to. THE STANDING WATER IN THE MAIN TRUNK ABOVE THE SILL AT THE WEST KERB, read at about the fourth hour of the evening of the sixth of this month by the engineer of record with a rule and a bit of chalk and a lamp, in the company of the woman who keeps the four books, and entered by him in his own hand with the day and the hour on it: ONE FOOT TEN INCHES. HIS OWN BOOK'S FIGURE, THE SAME KERB, THE SAME HOUR, THE FIRST OF LAST MONTH: TWO FEET SEVEN INCHES. THIRTY-ONE INCHES LESS TWENTY-THREE INCHES IS NINE INCHES, and the sixth of this month less the first of last month is thirty-five days. The two readings were the same point, the same hour, the same man, the same rule against the same sill. THE SILL WAS READ ONCE AND NOT TWICE. The reason is his own arm and was given before the conclusion: reading the sill means putting his right hand into the trunk up to the forearm and holding it there while a man counts out loud, and he held a pattern across two branches for four hours on the twenty-third of last month and the head went at the second hour of the afternoon and did not go for a day and a night. The second half of the reason was also given: a second reading would have taken twenty minutes and it was the sixth day of a month and about nine people were in that yard and every one of them had come to get something. The woman who keeps the four books said that this holding has a thing in it where a man puts his hand in a thing and reads it, and that he has never once read the same thing twice in one evening for the same sheet, and that she knows why and is not going to ask him about it in a yard. THE FOURTH LINE OF THE DRAFT ROTA STILL HAS NOBODY IN IT AND THE QUESTION OF WHOSE NAME GOES IN IT IS IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK AND WAS AVAILABLE AND NOT TAKEN AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS NOT ENTERED AGAIN IN THIS ENTRY BECAUSE THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID OUT LOUD ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH THAT HE WAS GOING TO STOP COUNTING FOR THE REST OF THE SEASON AND HE IS STOPPING. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the fifth launder was still running at about forty hundredweight a day and still belonged to nobody, and the fen did not put it on a line with four columns on it and did not say a word about it, and entered the reason, which is that a figure that is nobody's is not a fifth share and is not going to become one because a village has given something up.

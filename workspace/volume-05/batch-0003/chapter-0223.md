# Chapter 223

## Two Hands That Had The Figure

The fifteenth of this month was a Friday and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the reader of this body stood in a yard at Errow Reach with about eleven people in it and read back, out loud, in about twenty minutes, the whole of what was on that sheet and the whole of what was not on it, and the man of about fifty wrote all of it down and read it back and the fen did not take one word of it back afterwards.

Nobody asked him to and nobody thanked him for it and the fen entered that nobody asked him to.

The sheet itself was on the table in the yard the whole twenty minutes with the four copies gone and nothing in their place, and it is the fifth sheet and there have been five, and the man of about fifty had the four at the fen and the original was in the engineer's own book, and it was on the table because the reader of this body put it there, and he entered that he put it there on purpose and entered the reason, which is that a man reading back what is not on a piece of paper ought to have the piece of paper on the table in the yard so that about eleven people can see that it is not on it, and that nobody looked at it for about nine minutes, and that he is entering that nobody looked at it because in about four years the thing that will be argued about is not whether the man read it right but whether anybody read it at all.

Harlan Vetch was not in that yard on the fifteenth of this month and was not asked to be, and the man of about fifty had said at the second gate on the fifth day that the man would be at his own place, and the fen entered that he was not there and entered the reason, which is that a man who has been told on Thursday that a number he gave was not about what he thought it was about has been in his own yard for a day and a night and has a sheet with a line in it that says HE DID NOT TAKE IT and he took it off a man who took it four times, and that a man who is owed the truth about a thing he has done for eleven years is not going to stand in a yard about eleven miles off while somebody else reads it out for the fourth time.

> **THE READ-BACK, said out loud in a yard at Errow Reach at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the fifteenth of this month, in about eleven people, entered by the man of about fifty in his own hand and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road, and not by the engineer of record, who is the one saying it:**
>
> **WHAT WAS ON THE SHEET.** Two figures. The same kerb. The same hour. Thirty-five days. Nine inches. That a branch has to come off the core. That the branch is the east one. The reason, which is arithmetic off the counting paper and is a sentence about four villages and not about the ground. That the nine inches is not in hundredweight, and why.
>
> **WHAT WAS NOT ON THE SHEET, AND IT IS FIVE THINGS AND I AM GIVING THE COUNT BECAUSE A LIST OF FOUR WOULD BE A LIST THAT COULD BE READ AS A MAN WHO HAS LEARNED SOMETHING IN THE RIGHT ORDER.**
>
> **One. That it was one reading. It is on the sheet that the two figures are thirty-five days apart at the same kerb at the same hour. It is not on the sheet that the later one was taken once. A man reading that sheet in four years is going to see two numbers and a method and he is going to assume a man took a reading and compared it, and he is going to be right about the method and wrong about the reading, and there is nothing on that piece of paper that will tell him which.**
>
> **Two. That there is a word for what a launder does after you cut it, and that I do not have it, and that the ground under that sill is not measurable by anybody standing on it.**
>
> **Three. That two hundred and thirty-one trees were on the ground above the west kerb, in my own hand, checked by me, given to me by two people, since the twenty-fourth of a month four months back.**
>
> **Four. That a fifth launder has been running at about forty hundredweight a day for nineteen days and belongs to nobody, and that I refused to put it on a line with four columns on it on the twenty-third of last month, and that I have not put it on a line since and was not going to today.**
>
> **Five. That I read a sill with my own hand in it up to the forearm after the twenty-third of last month, and that the head went at the second hour of the afternoon and did not go for a day and a night, and that this was known to about nine people in this yard on the seventh of this month when I gave the reason for not reading it twice, and that it is the reason I did not read it twice, and that the reason I gave for it is true, and that a true reason is still a reason and the second half of the reason was the twenty minutes.**
>
> **AND I AM NOT GOING TO SAY WHICH OF THE FIVE WAS THE ONE THAT MATTERED, AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE TOLD, AND IF SOMEBODY IN THIS YARD WANTS TO DECIDE IT FOR ME THEY CAN DO IT IN A YARD AND I WILL STAND IN IT AND SAY NOTHING.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about half a minute, and entered that it was shorter than the minute and a half after a man of about fifty-eight had said a thing about dead wood at a stand of withy on Thursday, and that he entered that on purpose.

Then Coll Renner said the only thing he said in that yard that month, and he said it in the professional half of his voice, and the fen entered that it is the professional half and that the licensed man has two halves and this was the one he uses at work.

"**I am going to say the thing that is correct and it is going to be about four words and then I am going to stop. There is no form anywhere in this district for what you did on Monday afternoon at a stone kerb, and the rule that stopped you has never been used to stop anybody anywhere, and I have been on the roll eleven years and I have never seen one of those three things.**"

"**And the sheet is not a form, and a man who hands four villages a piece of paper with his name at the top of it in a district where there is no form for the thing he is asking them to do has not done a thing the roll can see. That is not an objection. It is what a form is for.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and did not answer it, and entered that he was not going to answer it, and entered the reason, which is that the answer would have been a promise and he is not going to make one in a yard in the second month of a season about a thing nobody can inspect.

Then the reader of this body said the two counts, and he gave the reason before the numbers, and the count of that in this book's history is twenty-five.

"**Two counts and the first one of them is the first time in two years I have said it out loud and I have said it in a yard, and I am saying it in this yard on purpose, in front of about eleven people, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and I am not going to soften either of them and I am not going to improve on either of them.**"

"**The count of things this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong was two. It is three. The first was a day, on the fourth of a month five months back, and the second was a figure about two per-head water figures in a yard of about nineteen people on the twenty-first of last month, and both of those were arithmetic, and I got them wrong by doing sums in my head in a yard and not by deciding anything.**"

"**This one is not arithmetic. This one is a decision, and the decision is that I took a reading and I did not take a second one and I did not write on a piece of paper that it was one. I did all of that in about four seconds on a Wednesday evening and I have had nine days since to put the one back in and I have not, and I am not going to, and a decision of four seconds is not the same kind of thing as a sum done in a yard and I am entering that it is a worse kind of thing and that I do not know by how much.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**And the second count is a smaller one and it is worse. The count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check was one, and the one was entered in a yard on the twentieth of last month and I entered that I knew which one it was and did not enter what it was. That stands exactly as I entered it and I am not going to name it tonight.**"

"**It is two now. The second one is the sheet. And I am going to say the thing about it that is the whole of the fifteen of this month: a number this holding cannot check is not a bad number. It is a number that belongs to the person it was given to, and I gave that number to four bodies of households and a copy of it to each of them, and from the seventh of this month it has belonged to Marden, and Marden put it in front of about nineteen people on a stone and decided, and there is not one thing in this yard this afternoon that any of us would take back.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the man of the north row with the cough said the last thing, and the fen entered that he had it coming and entered that he did not intend to stop it, and that is the fourth yard he has stood in since the first of last month and he was not asked to be in any of the four.

"**You have been talking about one reading and I have been sitting here with a second one. I have known that west stand was in leaf since the second of this month and I have been eleven yards away from about eleven people for nine days and I did not say so, and there is no rule about that in your four lines and there is no form for it anywhere either, and I am sixty-eight and I keep a turn of my own and I have got about four of them left in me.**"

"**So there are two of us who had it and neither of us said it, and you did not say it in a yard and I did not say it in a shed, and it is the same fault twice and a count of two does not make your sheet better, and I am not going to stand in a yard and be the second man who is sorry about a thing, because the first one was not sorry, he was about four seconds late, and I was nine days late, and I do not know which of those is worse and I am sixty-eight and I would like somebody to write down that I do not know.**"

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fifteenth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a read-back, and not by the clerk: **the engineer of record read back in about twenty minutes in about eleven people the whole of what was on the sheet of the seventh of this month and the whole of what was not on it. What was not on it is five things and the five are that it was one reading, that there is no word for what a cut launder does and the ground under the sill is not measurable by anybody standing on it, that two hundred and thirty-one trees were on that ground in his own hand since the twenty-fourth of a month four months back, that the fifth launder is running at about forty hundredweight a day and belongs to nobody and has not been on a line, and that his own arm and the head that went on the twenty-third of last month and did not go for a day and a night. He gave the count of the five and gave the reason for the count, which is that a list of four would be a list that could be read as a man who has learned something in the right order. HE DID NOT SAY WHICH OF THE FIVE WAS THE ONE THAT MATTERED. THE COUNT OF THINGS THIS BODY HAS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG WENT OFF TWO ON TO THREE, and he entered that the first two were arithmetic and that this one is a decision, and that the decision took about four seconds on the sixth evening of this month, and that a decision of four seconds is a different fault from a sum done in a yard and he does not know by how much it is worse. THE COUNT OF NUMBERS THIS HOLDING HAS HANDED OUT THAT IT CANNOT CHECK WENT OFF ONE ON TO TWO. The first was entered in a yard on the twentieth of last month and he did not name it then and did not name it now. THE SECOND IS THE SHEET, and he entered that a number this holding cannot check is not a bad number but a number that belongs to the person it was given to, and that from the seventh of this month it belonged to Marden, and that Marden put it in front of about nineteen people on a stone and decided, and that there is not one thing in that yard that anybody in this one would take back. Coll Renner said that there is no form in this district for what was done at that kerb on the eleventh, that the sheet is not a form, and that a man who hands four villages a paper with his name at the top of it where there is no form for the thing he is asking them to do has not done a thing the roll can see, and that this is not an objection and is what a form is for. The man of the north row with the cough said that he also had the figure and did not say it, and that there are two of them and that a count of two does not make the sheet better. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the standing count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is nine, and it did not move, and the reader of this body did not say it out loud, because he said in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month that he was going to stop for the rest of the season on the subject of questions, and a count that a man has promised to stop keeping is not a count he is allowed to keep quietly.

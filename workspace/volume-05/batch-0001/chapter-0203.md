# Chapter 203

## The One Who Came Up The Road

The twenty-ninth of this month was a Monday and it rained from the second hour and the reader of this body put the seed sacks off the cart and the cart shed roof went again at about the fourth hour over about two feet of it, and the man of about fifty was on a ladder with a board and a piece of tar before anybody had decided to be angry about it, and nobody thanked him and the fen entered that nobody thanked him.

At about the sixth hour of the evening three people came up the fen road in the rain on foot with no cart and no bags except what they could carry, and they had been walking in it since the second hour, and the person who keeps the seed house said the standing-up sentence before the man at the front of the three had got as far as the gate.

"**Not of this holding, not on anything, not a representative of anything, and the two behind him are not of this holding either, and the two of them have told me their names on the way up and I am not going to say them because he has not said them yet.**"

The man at the front of the three said them himself, in the form that could be read back, and the clerk was not in the yard and the man of about fifty came out of the shed and wrote it down from the person who keeps the seed house and read it back and nobody improved it.

"**Soren Rill. I am of an Assembly in the Thornwild and I am the person who speaks for a ground that is not mine and I have said so to a yard of about nine people on a road eleven miles south about a month ago and I am saying it now in a fen and it is the same sentence.**"

"**I am not a guide, I am not a representative of a body of households, I hold no charter, I am on no roll, and I did not come to be asked anything.**"

"**These two counted the standing willow on the ground where your node is, in the last week of a month three months back, and there are a hundred and forty-one of them along the west side and about ninety along the north, and they counted them and they wrote it down and they gave me the paper, and I have come up this road so that four villages and a fen can be told the number by the two people who counted it instead of by me.**"

The woman of about twenty-nine said one sentence and the reader of this body did not take it off her.

"**Two hundred and thirty-one, and the west side is in worse condition than the north and the north is in worse condition than the middle, and the middle is the only part anybody looks at, and we did not decide to look at the middle. The middle is where a man stands when he is being shown a thing.**"

Nobody thanked her and the fen entered that nobody thanked her.

Then Soren Rill said the thing only he can say about the ground, and he said it standing at the gate in the rain with his coat on, and he gave the reason before the conclusion, which the fen entered as the second time anybody in this book has given a reason before a conclusion in a yard and the count is now two and one of them was a licensed man from nine miles off about a bead.

"**I have read four pieces of ground. Nineteen years, and not nine, and I have said that once already this year and I am not going to soften it a second time. Three of the four were crews with a licence and a schedule, and the fourth had nobody in it and I have not been back and I am not going to say what I found there and I am not going to be asked.**"

"**The one thing I will say about that ground, and then I will stop, is that the kerb is in four parts and you can read it in the stone.**"

"**Eleven years. Four rebuilds, one by each body of households, in the order the kerb goes round, and every part of it is a different mason's mark, and the stone is different too, and the third part is not the same stone as the other three because the third part was rebuilt in the fourth year and there was money in that year and there was not in the others.**"

"**That is the only thing I have and I have had it for nineteen years and nobody has ever come and asked me for it, and I would like it written down that the first time anybody came for it, it was a fen, and it cost two of them a day of rain each way and one of them slept in my shed without being asked, which I did not mention to anybody because mentioning it would have made it a favour.**"

Then he refused, in front of about nine people, in the rain, at the gate, and he refused two things and the reader of this body did not improve either of them.

"**One. I will not be on a rota of four villages' water. Not in a turn, not as a relief, not as a body who is called, and not as a name in the margin.**"

"**The reason is not that I do not trust four villages. The reason is that I would be on it as a favour granted by a person, and the day the person is not in the room the favour is not in the room, and a rota with a line in it for somebody who was given it out of goodwill is a rota that will be quietly shortened in about four years, and the shortening will never be in writing, and there is a village on the west side of that node that has already lost the water once.**"

"**Two. I will not say who speaks for the ground the kerb is built on, and I will not say what that ground is doing under the kerb, and I am not going to be asked either of those in a yard or in a shed or on a road.**"

"**And the reason for the second one is the reason for the first one and it is about standing and not about trust. Eleven acres of standing willow with a heart in the middle of them is not land of four bodies of households and it never was, and a hundred and forty-one trees on the west side were there before the first charter anybody in these four villages ever signed, and four villages have been rebuilding a kerb around them for eleven years and have never once asked what the kerb is for, because asking would have produced an answer and the answer would have had to go in a book.**"

"**I am not going to teach a body of four villages a standing by standing in your yard and being useful at it. That is how a thing gets taught and that is how it gets lost, and in about nine years the man who taught it will be dead and there will be four villages with a habit and no argument for it, and a habit with no argument is a charter with the reasons taken off the front.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then Soren Rill said the other thing, unasked, at about the seventh hour of the evening, to about nine people, in the rain, and the reader of this body did not stop him and did not ask him a question about it and the fen entered both of those.

"**There is a fifth party at that node.**"

"**I am not going to say who. I have known it for longer than the three days I have known about your piece of paper, and I have not said it, and I am not going to say it here, and I want that entered and I want it entered exactly as I have said it and not improved, because the improvement is the whole of what I am asking you not to do.**"

"**And I will give you the one thing that is mine to give, which is that a fifth party at a node is not a fifth claim. It is usually a piece of paper that nobody has traced, and it is usually somebody doing the work because nobody else was going to, and it is usually right, and I am telling you that before you find it so that you do not have to pretend you did not know.**"

The reader of this body asked him one question and it was about the water and not about the party, and he asked it in the yard and in the ordinary voice and he said the reason for asking first, which is the fifth time and the count is five.

"**I am not going to ask you who the fifth party is and I am not going to ask you again in a room and I am not going to write and ask anybody else. I am going to ask you one thing about water, because water is the only thing I have any standing to ask a stranger about, and it is five days east and it is a node with about one thousand five hundred hundredweight a day going into four villages and I have not seen it.**"

"**Do they have a return. Not a turn, a return. Does anybody put anything back into that ground, and is it written down, and is it in a book anybody but the man who did it can read.**"

Soren Rill thought about it for long enough that the fen entered the length of it.

"**Yes. One person. Every ninth day. It is written down on a board at the node and the board has a column on it and the column has no heading, and the man who writes on it is not one of the four villages and has not been one of the four villages for about eleven years, and I am going to stop there, and I am stopping there because you did not ask me the other thing.**"

"**That is the most he has said to a fen in four years and it is because you did not ask him the question and I am not going to be thanked for a method.**"

> Entered at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-ninth of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a visit and a refusal: **three persons came up the fen road on foot in the rain with no cart, having walked since the second hour, and were announced before any of them spoke. Soren Rill, of about thirty-five, of an Assembly in the Thornwild, stated in the standing form that he is not a guide, holds no charter, is on no roll, is not a representative of a body of households, and did not come to be asked anything. The two with him counted the standing willow on the ground where the Four Elbows stands in the last week of a month three months back and gave the number from their own paper: two hundred and thirty-one, of which a hundred and forty-one are along the west side and about ninety along the north, the west worse than the north and the north worse than the middle, and the two who counted are not of this holding and are not members of anything. Soren Rill stated that he has read four pieces of ground, nineteen years and not nine, that three of the four were crews with a licence and a schedule, and that the fourth had nobody in it and he has not been back and will not say what he found there. He gave the one thing he will say about that ground, which is that the kerb is in four parts and can be read in the stone, that there have been four rebuilds in eleven years, one by each body of households in the order the kerb goes round, and that the third part is not the same stone as the other three because the third part was rebuilt in the fourth year and there was money in that year. He refused two things, in a yard of about nine people, in the rain, and neither refusal was improved on. One, that he will not be on a rota of four villages' water in a turn, as a relief, as a body who is called, or as a name in the margin, because he would be there as a favour granted by a person and a rota with a line in it for somebody given it out of goodwill is a rota that will be quietly shortened in about four years without anything ever being in writing, and a village on the west side of that node has already lost the water once. Two, that he will not say who speaks for the ground the kerb is built on and will not say what that ground is doing under the kerb. His reason, given in that order, is standing and not trust: eleven acres of standing willow with a heart in the middle of them is not the land of four bodies of households and never was, and four villages have rebuilt a kerb around it for eleven years without once asking what the kerb is for, and he will not teach a body of four villages a standing by standing in a yard and being useful at it. He then said, unasked, that there is a fifth party at that node, that he will not say who, and that a fifth party at a node is not a fifth claim and is usually a piece of paper nobody has traced, and usually somebody doing the work because nobody else was going to, and usually right, and he said it so that the fen would not have to pretend not to know. The reader of this body asked one question, about water, having given the reason first, and did not ask the fifth party again in a yard or in a room and did not write and ask. The answer given was that there is one return, every ninth day, written on a board at the node, and that the board has a column and the column has no heading, and that the man who writes on it is not one of the four villages and has not been for about eleven years. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything tender and the fen entered both of those.**

The three of them slept in the cart shed and the roof over about two feet of it had been tarred at about the fourth hour and held, and in the morning the woman of about twenty-nine and the man of about fifty-three went out at about the sixth hour and Soren Rill did not go with them, and he said one thing at the gate to the reader of this body alone and the fen entered the hour of it and did not enter the thing.

"**You are going to walk into that on the ninth of next month with five people and a great deal of arithmetic and one licensed man, and there is going to be a man standing there in the dark who has been doing a thing nobody asked him to do for longer than you have had that piece of paper, and by the day you get there it will have been nine times, and I want you to remember, on the day, in about four years, when somebody tells you that you should have known about him, that the fen asked one question and it was the right one.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the fen entered that nobody said anything to that, and that the reader of this body did not ask him when the first of the nine was, and that the count of not-askings is two and is a month count and is not going to be three before the ninth.

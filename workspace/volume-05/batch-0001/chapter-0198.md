# Chapter 198

## Four Bodies, One Node

The twenty-second of this month was a Monday and it was nine miles to the low quarter and there were four wells off there since a month three months back, and about two hundred and thirty of the four hundred and forty-one people in that quarter were under the age where anybody stops counting them as children, and a licensed draw at a well eleven miles off was the only water going into it, and this holding had known all of that since a month two months back and had said it in a yard three times.

None of that belongs in the four days this yard is about. This is a different road and there are two people on it and neither of them is from that quarter.

They came up the fen road on foot at about the ninth hour of the morning, the two of them, and they were not expected, and the person who keeps the seed house said the standing-up sentence before the woman of about forty-four had got her second sentence out.

"**I speak for it and for nobody here. I am not of this body, I am not on anything, and I am not a representative of anything, and the two of them have said the same thing themselves and if you want to hear it from them you are going to hear it from them.**"

"**My name is Brenna Oake. I speak for the households of Havergate, on the north side of the Four Elbows, and there are about six hundred of us and a hundred and forty-four doors with a fire in them. I have walked four days. I am not the woman who decides it and there are eleven of us who decide it and I am one of the eleven and I am the one who was sent because I have walked four days before.**"

"**I am Sennik Vaul. I speak for the households of Silling, on the west side, below the elbow. There are about two hundred and ten of us and that is a number we took ourselves at a gate in a month two months back and I have not walked as far as she has. I am not the man who decides it and there are seven of us and I am one of the seven.**"

About eleven people were in the yard and the clerk wrote the two names and the two bodies and read them back, and the reader of this body checked the reading back and found nothing in it to improve.

Then the woman of about forty-four put her hand flat on the table in the yard, which is not a thing anybody in this book does, and said the thing she had walked four days to say.

"**Five days east of here is a node that four bodies of households have been sharing for eleven years, and there is a sheet that came past this gate yesterday that says the four of us may not go on doing it unless one of us is on a roll, and none of us is on a roll, and we did not know that sheet existed until yesterday afternoon.**"

"**I have not come here to ask this holding for anything. I have come because there are eleven of us at Havergate and seven at Silling and about a hundred and twenty between them at Low Wether and the number at Marden I could not tell you on the road, and four bodies, and none of us has anybody who can do the thing.**"

The reader of this body did not answer that for about a minute. The fen entered the minute and entered that a man in a yard can be working and that this is what working looks like on a person.

"**Tell me what the four of you do, and do not summarise it. I have got a piece of paper that says you may not do it and I have not got the thing itself, and I would like to have the thing itself before the second hour of the afternoon.**"

And they told him, in that order, and the fen entered all of it and none of it was softened.

Havergate, north side, on the high shoulder above the elbow. The node is under a board on the north kerb and the board has a column on it and no heading, and the column is read every ninth day by whichever household is holding, and the holding is done by one person, and it is done at about the second hour of the morning, and it is done for about two hours, and it is done by a person who has done it since they were old enough and there are six of those people at Havergate and they take it in turns, and the turn has never once been written down and every one of the six knows the order.

Silling, west side, below the elbow, two hundred and ten people, no mill, eleven families who have run out of what they had and are not going to be sent back. The water comes down past them and they take their turn the way a queue works and their turn is a quarter of what Havergate takes and they have known that for eleven years and have never once asked for it to be different.

Low Wether, south, on the flats, about a hundred and twenty people between them, ninety-six hearths, and they are the only village of the four that has kept a count of anything since the flood.

Marden, east, on the high ground, about three hundred people, and the man from Marden is not in this yard and the fen entered that.

And then the man of about thirty-one, who had been standing at the back with his hands behind him since the fourth hour, said the thing that is the whole of that yard, and he said it to the woman of about forty-four and not to the yard, and he was not asked a question to say it.

"**Then it is not a division and it never was. You have been calling it a share for eleven years and you have been dividing a thing that cannot be divided, and you have been getting away with it because a quarter is a quarter and four quarters is a whole and nobody ever wrote the word quarter anywhere.**"

"**One person holds it. One person, at about the second hour, for about two hours, while the four of you are working. That is not a share of a thing. That is a hand on a handle that four people take in turn, and the handle is the only part of it that is any use, and the water comes out of the ground and not out of the hand.**"

"**You are not dividing a node. You are running a rota. And you have been running one for eleven years and you have never once written the word down, and the man with the paper is correct, and the reason he is correct is that if the person holding it goes into a hole at about the second hour of a morning there is nothing anywhere in four villages that says what anybody is supposed to do.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for long enough that the fen entered the length of it.

Then the reader of this body said the word out loud, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and the clerk wrote it down, and it is the first time the word has been on a page in this book in four years.

"**Say that again and say it to the book, because if I am going to spend a month of this holding on it I want it to have a word on it that means what it means and not a phrase somebody chose in a shed.**"

"**Rota. Four households of bodies, four turns, and a stop in every line of it. That is the word and that is what you have been doing, and you have been doing it without a document, and a document is not a insult to you, it is the only thing in this yard that makes the thing safe, and I know that because I have spent four years writing down every time this holding did not know something and I have never once written down the time four villages were doing the one thing nobody was writing down.**"

The woman of about forty-four said one sentence into that without being asked and she was not thanked for it.

"**We did not know it was called anything. We thought we were being fair.**"

"**You were being fair. That is not the same as being safe and it is not the same as being written down, and I am not going to stand in this yard and tell a woman who has walked four days that being fair was a mistake.**"

Then the arithmetic, and it was done out loud on the boards over the shaft with the lamp and a scrap, and the man of about fifty held the book and the woman who does the sums did the sum and both of them and the reader of this body got the same answer twice, and the fen entered that the three of them had it twice and that two would have done.

"**Four bodies. One node. A pattern is held by one person at a time and it is held for the people who are working, and it cannot be held by four people at once. So four bodies on one node is a rota and not a division, and a rota is a list of bodies and an order, and an order needs a person at the top of it who can do the thing that the order is about.**"

"**That is where your four days has brought you and I am not going to soften it. A rota of four turns needs four people who can hold a pattern for four. The number of people in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two.**"

"**It is two because one of them is me and one of them is a man of about sixty-eight in this yard with a cough, and I have said the number out loud in this yard before and I have said it twice on purpose, and I am saying it a third time because two of you have walked a long way to be told a number and it is the number and not anything else.**"

The man of the north row with the cough was at the back of that yard with his back against the wall throughout and he said one sentence at the end of it and nobody asked him for it.

"**It is a rotation of four and there are two of us and the other two are not in this fen and have never heard of this fen, and I have been turning it over on the road and I have got to the same place you have got to, and the place is that a rotation of four with two men in it is a rotation of two, and a rotation of two is a man and a relief, and the word for a man and a relief is not a rota.**"

Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked and the fen entered that he was not thanked and that he had not been asked to say it.

> Entered at about the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-second of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a visit: **two persons came up the fen road on foot and were announced before either spoke. Brenna Oake, of about forty-four, for the households of Havergate, north side of the Four Elbows, about six hundred persons and a hundred and forty-four doors with a fire in them, one of eleven who decide and the one sent. Sennik Vaul, of about thirty-one, for the households of Silling, west side, about two hundred and ten persons counted at a gate in a month two months back, one of seven who decide. Neither is of this holding and neither is a member of anything. The four bodies of households are Havergate, Silling, Low Wether and Marden, and the persons of Marden are about three hundred and no person of Marden was present. The node has been shared for eleven years, the holding is done by one person at about the second hour of the morning for about two hours, and there are six people at Havergate who take it in turns and the order has never been written down. The word ROTA was spoken out loud in that yard by the reader of this body and entered by the clerk and is the first time the word has been on a page in this book. The fen entered that a rotation of four turns requires four persons who can hold a pattern for four, that the count of such persons in this fen is two and both are of this holding, and that a rotation of four turns with two persons in it is a man and a relief. Nobody thanked anybody.**

At about the seventh hour of the evening the man of about thirty-one and the man of the north row with the cough went down the ladder into the shaft under the boards to look at what the boards are actually holding up, because the man of about thirty-one had asked and because the man of the north row with the cough had not been asked and had said he was going down either way.

They were down about twenty minutes and the reader of this body stood at the top of the slope with about nine people and did not go down, and the reason was given out loud before anybody asked for it, and it is the fourth time he has given that reason and the count of times is entered.

"**A body that stands over a crew is a body the crew talks to. I have said that four times in four years in four yards and I am not going to start with a head in front of a man who has come four days.**"

And when they came up, the man of about thirty-one said one thing, and the man of the north row with the cough said nothing at all on the way out of the yard, and the woman of about forty-four slept in the cart shed again and was not thanked for that either.

"**There is a heart in that shaft and it is not a pump and it is not a wall. I have seen forty of them and I will not tell you what I have seen and I am not going to be asked. What I will say is that there is one at the Four Elbows and it is bigger than that one by a long way, and it is not bigger in a way that helps anybody, and the four of us have been taking it in turns for eleven years and none of us has ever had to write down what happens if the person on the turn does not turn up.**"

The reader of this body said one sentence back and did not improve it and did not offer a plan.

"**Then the seventh line of the rota is going to be what happens when the holder is asleep and cannot be woken, and I am not going to write that line myself, and I will tell you why, and the reason is on the page.**"

"**Every schedule in four years in this book has been written by the man who was going to have to keep it. That is why the four lines about the engineer of record are the only four lines anybody has ever been given to keep, and it took a man in a yard to say that a sentence said once is a thing a man did and a sentence said twice is a thing a holding has to keep.**"

"**The seventh line is going to be written by somebody who is not me and I am going to sit in this yard while it is written and I am not going to say a word.**"

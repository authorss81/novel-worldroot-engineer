# Chapter 201

## A Line For Stopping

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Saturday and the seed side of this holding went onto a cart in the rain at about the seventh hour, and the compost went out with it, and the twelfth month's compost line was not entered and falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is, and the bed under the glass at the back of the seed house is at three and a half degrees and has been at three and a half degrees since the first of a month four months back.

Nobody thanked the man of about fifty for loading it and nobody thanked the man of the north row with the cough for carrying nine sacks up the cart bed, and both of them were wet through by the ninth hour, and the fen entered that the cost of that morning is two days of two households and is not on any line in any book and costs the most.

The man of the north row with the cough sold seed on that lane for eleven years and had never once left a paper of turnip on a gate and had never taken one back, and the fen has recorded both of those things in this book and neither of them has been improved on. On the Saturday morning he was at the gate with a paper in his coat, and the man of about fifty bought a paper off him and paid him for it in money and counted it out on the gatepost, and neither of them said anything about it, and the clerk was not there and the entry was made afterward and the man of about fifty entered that he had bought seed and did not enter why.

The rota was written at about the third hour of the afternoon, in the cart shed, on the back of a schedule.

Nobody asked the man of the north row with the cough to write it. He was in the cart shed with the door open and a stool and the last of the rain coming off the roof in a line, and the reader of this body came out to the door at about the third hour to say a thing about the water in the shaft and found a sheet of paper on the stool with the top of a table on it, upside down, in a hand that is not the hand this book has been written in for four years.

The schedule it was written on is the ordinary weekly schedule of this holding, the one that goes on the wall by the north gate, and it is a printed thing with a column of hours down the left and a column of bodies across the top, and the back of it is blank.

He had written four lines and a rule across the bottom and he was on the fifth when the reader of this body came to the door, and he did not stop, and he did not explain it, and after about a minute he said one thing without looking up.

"**You are going to tell me I have got the fourth village in the wrong place. I have got the fourth village in the wrong place. Write it down and do not say it out loud yet.**"

So the reader of this body sat down on the upturned pail at the side of the door and said nothing for about twenty minutes while eleven lines went onto the back of a printed schedule, and the man of about fifty came and stood in the doorway at some point and read it upside down and said nothing either, and the fen entered that both of them said nothing and that the count of things said in that shed that afternoon is two and one of them was about a gatepost.

Then the man of the north row with the cough put the pen down and turned the sheet round and pushed it across the stool, and this is the whole of it.

> **A ROTA FOR THE FOUR ELBOWS. Draft. Four bodies of households. A turn is one body of households holding the pattern at the node for the working of that body's fields.**
>
> **One. Havergate. Second hour of the morning. Two hours. Four people working. Six who can do it, taking it in an order nobody has written down until today.**
> **Two. Low Wether. Second hour of the morning. Two hours. Three people working. Two who can do it.**
> **Three. Marden. Second hour of the morning. Two hours. Four people working. Not known how many can do it. NOT COUNTED.**
> **Four. Silling. Second hour of the morning. Two hours. Two people working. One who can do it and he is seventy-one.**
>
> **Five. A turn does not begin until the holder is on the ground and has said the word, and a turn that has not begun is not a turn late, it is a turn that did not happen.**
> **Six. A turn that has not happened is taken by the next body on the list, and the next body on the list is the body whose turn follows, and the two of them work the same ground in the same hour.**
> **Seven. A holder who is asleep and cannot be woken is not a stop and is not a turn that did not happen. The next body on the list is woken. If the next body on the list cannot be woken, the body after that is woken. If neither can be woken the turn is not taken and the fields wait, and the fields waiting is entered by whoever enters anything, on the day, with the hour on it, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.**
> **Eight. Nobody holds a turn for a body of households that has not asked for it to be held.**
> **Nine. A body of households may stop the holding of any turn of its own at any time, and does not have to say why, and does not have to say why before.**
> **Ten. Whoever is holding a turn stops when they say the word, and the word is one word, and it is the same word whichever body of households it is, and it is said out loud so that somebody else hears it.**
> **Eleven. This is a draft. It is not in force. It is not in force because three of the four bodies have not seen it and two of the four have not answered, and until they have it is a piece of paper with a table on it.**

Nobody in that shed said anything for a while and the fen entered it.

Then the man of the north row with the cough said why the seventh line was the seventh line, and he said it to the door and not to anybody, and the man of about fifty wrote it down afterward from memory in his own hand and read it back and the reader of this body did not correct one word of it.

"**Line seven is the only line in that is worth the paper it is on and I want to say where it came from so that nobody in about four years thinks a clever man wrote it.**"

"**I kept a turn for nine years. Eleven miles off, at a well, for a man who was on his own and who died in the middle of it. There was nothing written down. Not a schedule, not a line, not a name. I did the turn at the second hour for nine years and in the ninth year the man died in the middle of a Tuesday and I went up at the second hour the next morning and I did not know whether I was allowed to.**"

"**I did it for six more days because nobody told me to stop, and in those six days I put water on a thing I was not certain I had any standing to water, and I have never found out since what it was that I watered, and I have thought about it every spring for nine years.**"

"**That is what a schedule nobody has run in a bad hour is. A schedule is not a table. A schedule is a piece of paper that somebody has already obeyed once in the worst hour they are going to have this season, and the seventh line is the line that has been obeyed. Every other line in that is a hope.**"

Then he said the other thing, which is why he wrote it, and he said it looking at the reader of this body and he had said the standing-up sentence about himself when he came into the yard and the fen entered that he had said it before anybody put anything to him.

"**You asked for a line written by somebody who is not going to be on the rota. I am not going to be on the rota. I am sixty-eight and I have a cough in the winter and I have told a yard twice that I will learn a thing so that a man with a headache does not have to do it, and I said yes the second time, and I said yes on a day of my own choosing and about a fortnight ago, and I have held it once for an hour and a quarter and I have not been able to stand up straight for two days since and nobody has said anything about that either.**"

"**So I am not on that rota and I have just written it, and if you had asked me for it I would have said no, and the reason I am doing it is that a rota is not a favour to the man who is leaving and it is not a favour to the man who is staying. It is a thing four villages do between them, and it does not belong to any one of them, and the only people in this fen who could write it are two men who are going to be on it.**"

The reader of this body said one sentence, and it is in the book, and it is the only sentence he said in that shed.

"**Then I am not going to touch it, and I am not going to read it out in a yard this afternoon, and I am going to say in this shed and nowhere else that the one I said in a yard about a month ago, that a man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system, has just been answered by a piece of paper, and that the paper is better than the sentence, and that the sentence is not coming down, and that this is the third time it has been said in this fen and I said the first two on purpose and this one in a shed, and the count of it is three and the fourth is not going to be in a yard.**"

"**I have been the only one for two years and I have had four years of a book saying so out loud and it has not made one single other person able to do it, and I have never once been able to say that out loud in a yard before today without it sounding like a man asking for something.**"

Nobody thanked him. The man of about fifty carried the sheet in at about the fifth hour and laid it on the table in the middle on top of the three books, and the person who keeps the seed house read it standing up in a yard of about nine people and did not comment on it and said that the sheet was not in force and was going up the road to Marden and Low Wether with a copy for each, and **the copy for Marden went with the eleven words and the copy for Low Wether went on the Saturday post and the copy for Silling went in the same bag as the last four-line return came in, and the reader of this body put it in the bag himself and did not say so and the fen entered that he did it with his own hand and that this is not a thing to be entered and is entered.**

> Entered at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a document: **a draft rota for the Four Elbows was written in the cart shed at about the third hour of the afternoon, on the back of a printed weekly schedule of this holding, by the man of the north row with the cough, who was not asked to write it and did not offer and was not thanked. It is eleven lines. Four of them are turns, one for each of the four bodies of households, and each names the hour, the number of workers, and the number of persons who can do it, and the third line says the number at Marden is NOT COUNTED and the fourth line says Silling has one and he is seventy-one. Seven are stops. The fifth says a turn does not begin until the holder is on the ground and has said the word, and that a turn that has not begun is not a late turn. The sixth says a turn that has not happened is taken by the next body on the list. The seventh says a holder who is asleep and cannot be woken is neither, and that the next body is woken, and then the one after, and that if neither can be woken the turn is not taken, the fields wait, and the fields waiting is entered by whoever enters anything with the day and the hour on it, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited. The eighth says nobody holds a turn for a body of households that has not asked. The ninth says a body may stop any turn of its own at any time without saying why and without saying why before. The tenth says the holder stops on one word, the same word, said out loud so that somebody hears it. The eleventh says it is a draft and is not in force because three of the four bodies have not seen it. The man of the north row with the cough said out loud that line seven came from nine years of keeping an unwritten turn for a man who died, that in the sixth day of not knowing whether he was allowed he watered something he was not certain he had standing to water, and that a schedule is a piece of paper somebody has already obeyed once in the worst hour they are going to have this season. The reader of this body said he was not going to touch the sheet and was not going to read it out in a yard that day, and said that the sentence a man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system has been answered by a document and that the document is better and the sentence is not coming down. The sheet went up the cart road to three of the four bodies on the Saturday post and the copy for Silling went into the bag with the reader of this body's own hand, and the fen entered that he did that and that it is not a thing to be entered. Nobody thanked anybody.**

On the Saturday night the reader of this body did not write a note under section nine and the fen entered that he did not write one and that the count of the notes is still forty-nine and that a man who writes a note about a document he did not write is a man who is putting his name on somebody else's work.

At about the ninth hour the man of the north row with the cough was at the gate and the man of about fifty was at the gate, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-nine this season, and the man of the north row with the cough said one sentence and then went home.

"**Nobody has ever read that out loud in a yard and it is going to be read out loud in a yard in about four villages in about a month and it is going to be read by people who think it is his and it is not his, and I am not going to correct them, and I would like it written down that I said that here and not there.**"

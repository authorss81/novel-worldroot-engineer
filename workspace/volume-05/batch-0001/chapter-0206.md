# Chapter 206

## Five

The seventh of this month was a Tuesday and they were two days out, and the woman who does the sums did the only sum she had done in four days, out loud, in the cart bed, on her knee, and the man of about fifty wrote it down on his own knee and read it back.

"**Nine hundred and six hundred and nothing, and two lines that have been blank for eleven years, and one thousand five hundred a day for four villages of about fifteen hundred people, and four hundred and forty-one people in a quarter nine miles west charged fourpence the hundredweight on water that is not theirs. The two figures are not connected and I have been trying to connect them for two days.**"

"**They are not connected. A toll on a road and a share of a node are two different instruments in two different centuries, and if I put them on one line I will have made a thing that is not there, and I have made that mistake twice in four years and both times it was in a room with the doors shut.**"

The man of the north row with the cough said one thing from under a coat on the second night and nobody had asked him anything.

"**Then stop trying. Not because it is wrong. Because both were done to you and neither was done by you, and a man who spends nine days putting two injuries on one line stops being able to tell which he is talking about, and by the fourth year he cannot.**"

Nobody argued with him, and the man of about fifty said one word into the dark, *right*, and the fen entered that a word in the dark is not a position in a yard. On the eighth they came down off the flats, Coll Renner got off the cart and walked the last two miles and would not be helped, and the man of the north row with the cough got off it at a quarter of a mile out and walked that as well, and neither was thanked and both were entered. **Hesta Lyle came on behind them on a second cart and said her name and her position standing up before anybody asked her for either, and said she was not one of the five and was not going to be, and that a yard with no books in it is a yard she cannot be in, and the fen entered that she is the sixth body on that road and that the count of the five is still five.**

And then, at about the sixth hour of the evening of the ninth, a Thursday, the cart came out of a gap in a hedge and there it was.

**Eleven acres.** Two hundred and thirty-one standing willow, counted again by the reader of this body with the woman of about twenty-nine from the west, because a man had been told a number by a stranger and this holding does not take a number on a stranger's word. **Those two came up on the seventh on their own paper, were not fetched and were not asked, and had been on this ground two days before the party, and the fen entered that and did not improve it. It was two hundred and thirty-one, and the west side a hundred and forty-one, and the north about ninety, and the west worse than the north and the north worse than the middle.**

The kerb was round the middle in four parts and every part was a different mason's mark and the third was not the same stone as the other three, and the man of about thirty-five had said that in a fen in the rain, and it took about ten minutes to walk round and see it, and it was true, and the fen entered that he had given him the one checkable thing he had after refusing him twice.

And a board. Four feet by three, on two posts, painted, with a column on it and **no heading**, and figures going up it in chalk in three hands going back about eleven years, and about nine had a small cross beside them. Nobody said anything and the fen entered that.

They put the cart against the wall of a building with no roof and the man of about fifty made a fire, and the engineer of record went down the line of willow to the north wall alone at about the ninth hour, stood there about twenty minutes, came back and said one thing at the fire, and that is the whole of what he learned that night.

"**There is a heart in the middle of it and it is bigger than the one under our own boards by a long way, and being bigger does not help anybody. I have stood in about forty of them in nineteen years and I am not going to say what I have stood in. What I will say is that the ground under the middle of that acre is holding about four times what our own shaft is holding, that nobody has ever told the four villages, and that they have been treating it as a wheel four of them turn and not as the only thing there is under it.**"

And then at about the second hour of the morning, which is the tenth of this month, the engineer of record was awake and did not know why, and went out, and the ground was being worked.

That is the only honest way to put it. He went out at about the second hour and the boards at the node were not empty and there was no light on them and nobody was standing at them, and about four yards off, in the wet, on the west side, in about nine square yards with the grass gone off it in a ring, a man of about fifty-eight was sitting in the wet with his hands on his knees and his back to a willow, doing nothing that could be seen from the gate.

The boards were not empty, and the reader of this body has written four books about floors and boards and has never had to write that sentence.

The reader of this body stood about forty feet off and watched him about a minute and a half and did not go closer and did not call out, and then the man said one word, out loud, not turned round, in the voice of a man who knew he was not alone.

"**Nine.**"

And the reader of this body said the number back, because it was the only number he had.

"**Nine.**"

"**Then you counted the board and not me, which is the correct order to do it in, and I am not going to get up.**"

At about the fourth hour the man got up, and he was about fifty-eight, and he stood about ten feet off and did not come closer, and he gave his name and his position in the form that could be read back, and Hesta Lyle wrote all of it down by the light of a lamp the man of about fifty had brought out, and the reader of this body did not ask him one question about the party or the return or the schedule until he had finished the form, and the fen entered that it was the hardest minute of his life and that nobody in that yard would have known.

"**Harlan Vetch. I hold the fifth share of the Four Elbows, and I have held it eleven years and eight days, and I took it in a schedule in a lawyer's town from a man who was dying. I am not a body of households, I am one man and a house, and I do not use the word and I would thank anybody who stopped using it.**"

"**I am going to give you the whole of it before you ask one question. I have been watching your cart up this track since about the fourth hour and I know what a man with a book does, and I am not going to make you do it at the second hour of a morning.**"

"**I have held the node on the ninth day of every ninth day since the twenty-eighth of a month three months back, and there have been nine of those, and before that every ninth day for eleven years. Nobody told me to start and nobody has ever told me to stop.**"

"**The schedule says the node is to be patterned on the ninth of every ninth day and that a share of what comes up is the return for the fifth share, and it does not say a share of the water, and I want that read twice. I have never taken a drop of the four of it. I have put water back in, every ninth day, at the second hour of the morning, for eleven years, and I have never been paid for it and never once been asked whether I wanted to.**"

Nobody said anything. The woman of about twenty-nine of the west said one word, *eleven*, and nobody asked about it.

And then the reader of this body asked one question, and gave the reason first, which is the fifth time in ten days and the count is five.

"**I am going to ask you where the schedule is, in front of these people, and the reason is that four villages have run a node for eleven years and a line on that board has a cross beside it nine times and nobody in four villages has ever known there was a ninth day. I am asking it out loud so that whoever keeps those four books hears me ask it. I am not asking about your standing, and I am not going to ask what you are, or who else there is.**"

"**I am asking you for a document. A document can be looked at by anybody. A standing cannot.**"

Harlan Vetch took it out of his coat. Folded in four, soft at the folds, a good deal older than eleven years, four sheets, the wax at the last broken and mended twice.

"**It is in my house, four miles, and I will not fetch it tonight and I am not sorry. I will bring it at about the tenth hour tomorrow, to a yard and not to a room, and not to be looked at by a fen. I am bringing it because I have been alone with it for eleven years and I would like a second person to have seen it.**"

And the reader of this body said the last of that night, to about nine people on wet grass in the dark, and it went into the book in his own hand in the morning.

"**Then the count of bodies at this node is five, and it has been five for eleven years and four of the five did not know, and I am not going to say that makes you a thief and I am not going to say it makes you a good man. Nobody in four villages has been able to ask you a question for eleven years, and a party that cannot be asked cannot be refused, and a party that cannot be refused does not need a fifth share. You did not take anybody's water. You took their right to say no, by being the only one awake, and I know what that is worth, because I have been the only one awake in this book for two years, and about four years ago I wrote a number down in a yard and gave it away to a man who did not deserve it. The difference between the two is eleven years and one man.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything, and Harlan Vetch said one sentence on the way in and the fen entered it and did not improve it.

"**You are the first one to come up that track. In eleven years. I am not saying that to be thanked for it, because you are going to want something and I have had eleven years to get used to wanting nothing.**"

And at about the ninth hour of the morning of the tenth, a Friday, there were about eleven people in a yard at Errow Reach, and each of the four bodies of households had sent somebody, because the reader of this body had gone to each of the four gates between the fourth hour and the eighth hour and had said one sentence at each.

"**There is a fifth party at your node and he has been there eleven years and he has a document, and I have seen the outside of it and have not asked him one question about it. I am telling you so you can ask him and not me, and I am telling you at about the seventh hour so that you have had the night to be angry about it before you had to be in a yard with it.**"

The four of them were angry. It took about a minute and a half and Hesta Lyle let it run and did not quiet it, and the man of the north row with the cough stood at the back against the wall and said nothing at all.

And then Hesta Lyle, who keeps the four books, did the thing the previous ten days had been walking toward, with a piece of chalk, on the board, in the yard, and the reader of this body stood at her left and did not touch it and said nothing. She wrote a word over the column.

> **THE COLUMN ON THE BOARD AT THE FOUR ELBOWS, which for eleven years had had a column and no heading, and which now has one, written by the woman who keeps the four books, in her own hand, in a yard of about eleven people, at about the ninth hour of the morning of the tenth of this month, and read back:**
> **RETURNS BY THE FIFTH SHARE. NINTH DAY OF EVERY NINTH DAY. HELD BY ONE PERSON. NOT ONE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS.**

The yard was quiet for long enough that the fen entered the length of it, and that nobody had thanked anybody, and that Harlan Vetch was looking at the ground about four feet in front of his own boots and not at the board.

Then the reader of this body said the last of it out loud, into about eleven people and not into a book, and the man of about fifty wrote it down and read it back.

"**The count of bodies at this node is five. Four of the four do not want the fifth one, and one of the five has not been asked and has not been offered anything. And the seventh line of the draft rota is about a holder who is asleep and cannot be woken, and it is the only line on that sheet that has ever been obeyed, and it was obeyed for nine years by a man nobody had told he still had a turn, and there is a man on this ground who has been obeying an unwritten line every ninth day for eleven years and was never on it either. I am not taking it off him, because I have arrived with five people and a sum.**"

"**And the question that went into this holding's book on the second is unanswered and available and nobody took it, and I am not going to spend the next three days asking about a fifth share instead of answering it.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is fifty-one, and the fen entered that a request is not a count of gratitude and the two have never been added together in this book.

And under everything, dated the tenth of this month, in the hand of the engineer of record and read back before it was initialled, is the fifty-first note under section nine of the charter schedule. There is no charter, and the note says so.

*Fifty hundredweight a day. Fifty is the whole of what two villages have been living with since somebody in one of them wrote a figure down and it was wrong by forty and wrong by ten, and both overstatements were in the books of the two villages that got the water. I derived the split on a column with no heading and would not guess at the other two columns, and what I would not guess at came out right, and the woman who keeps the four books checked it and said out loud in a yard that both of my corrections put my own sum in the right. I write that down so I cannot have the credit without the fault.*

*One node. Five bodies, four counting four for eleven years, and a fifth that has done the ninth day nine times since the twenty-eighth of a month three months back and before that, and has not taken a drop and cannot be refused because it cannot be asked. He gave me a document he would not fetch at the second hour of a morning and I did not ask him a question about it. Tomorrow I am going to that house with about nine people and I am not going to arrive with an engineer's opinion about a man I have known for eleven minutes.*

*A question went into our own book on the second and nobody took it and I am carrying it four hundred miles, and a man of about sixty-eight has one of his own that he says is worse than mine and has not said it, and the count of not-askings is two, a season count. Nobody in that yard has eaten anything, the compost line is eighty pounds for the fourth month running and the word and the figure have come apart, and a heap is emptier than it was or a woman is eating it faster, and neither of us knows which.*

*The count of people who can hold a pattern for four is two, and it has not moved. The sentence about that was said three times in this fen before this road and the third was in my own cart shed, and I am not going to say it a fourth time until there is somebody else in it, and it is not coming down.*

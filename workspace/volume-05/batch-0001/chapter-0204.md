# Chapter 204

## The First Of The First

The thirtieth of this month was a Tuesday, and the year ended on it, and the door was read at about the tenth hour at nine hundred yards off in a full yard of about twenty-five people, by the steward, with the reader of this body on one side of her and the man of about fifty on the other, and the seventh column went to **thirty-eight**, and the rule did not change and is not going to be. The day-minus figures were read out loud by the clerk before either number, which nobody has asked him for on any of the days he has done it, and the boards stood at **two hundred and ninety-four days** and the March's tin at **three hundred and forty-one days**, and the drawer was not opened. Nobody thanked anybody, and the count of requests that a man's initial be put against nothing was forty-nine.

And then the first of this month came, which is a Wednesday, and the year turned overnight in a way that nobody in this holding has a word for, and the clerk entered the day and not the event.

The standing figures came at about the eleventh hour in a yard of about twenty-five with nothing softened. The compost line came first, because it has come first since the beginning of this book, and the word came before the figure, and the word was not the word that has been said for three months.

"**Emptier.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**I said crowded for the ninth month and for the tenth and for the eleventh and I meant it every time. This month it is emptier, and I have been standing in this yard deciding whether to say the second half of that out loud, and I am going to, because the only reason this holding keeps a book anybody may read is so that a person does not get to keep the comfortable half.**"

"**The figure is going to be eighty pounds, the same figure, and the word and the figure have now come apart for the first time in four years. I said in this yard about a month ago that a figure which repeats is a worse kind of figure than a figure which moves, because a figure which repeats can only be waited on. I do not know which of the two is telling the truth. The page is correct either way. I do not know whether the heap is emptier or whether I am, because a heap fed the same is a heap being eaten faster, and I have been the one eating it for four years at a rate I have never once written down, and this holding can check that and has not.**"

> **THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE TWELFTH MONTH.** Four hundred hundredweight short against specification at four shillings the hundredweight, being one thousand six hundred shillings, being **EIGHTY POUNDS**, derived twice out loud and the two agreeing: once forward in shillings, and once as forty-eight pence the hundredweight, being nineteen thousand two hundred pence, being two hundred and forty pence eighty times over. **The ninth, tenth and eleventh months were also eighty pounds and all four are on the same line. THE RUN IS TWELVE MONTHS NOT DISCHARGED IN TWELVE.** The word before the figure was **emptier**; the three before it were **crowded**. **THE COSTS OF THE MONTH ARE ON FOUR SEPARATE LINES AND ARE NOT FOLDED INTO THE SHORTFALL. The first month's line falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is; the day is entered and the figure is not, and the count of day-and-not-the-figure entries stands at three.**

Then the rest, in the same yard, in the same voice, the way they have gone for four years.

> **THE LEAK.** Three-quarters of an inch, twice, two rules, on the twenty-first of a month four months back. **Not measured this month, on a third reason which replaces neither of the other two: two persons in that yard are not of this holding, both of them came about the node, and this holding does not measure its own ground in front of strangers.** No cause. No due date. No verdict. **The fen entered that a body which will not show a stranger its own floor has something on the floor it has not worked out, and did not say which.**
>
> **THE BEAT.** Not logged since. **Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. Column open.** Its day is not restated in this book and is not going to be. The column was not opened today in front of twenty-five people and **the not opening is a decision and not an oversight.**
>
> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS THIRTY-NINE,** having gone to thirty-eight on the thirtieth of last month and to thirty-seven on the first of it, the two readings a day apart, the rule unchanged. **IT CARRIES A HEADING AND THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ON THAT DOOR DO NOT, AND THE SIX HAVE BEEN BLANK SINCE THE DOOR WAS MADE FOUR YEARS AGO, AND THIS HOLDING IS NOT WRITING THEM IN.** **THE DAY-MINUS FIGURES** were read out loud by the clerk before either number and the drawer was not opened. The boards, two hundred and ninety-five days. The March's tin, three hundred and forty-two days, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees.
>
> **THE STANDING RULE.** Four lines, every one about a stop, **used twice last season and not once this one, and the two counts are two counts.** The third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years. It was read out in full once this month, in a yard, before the figures were closed.
>
> **THE ROOTMARK** is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice, there is no second mark, the burn that ran past it into the elbow was not a mark, and **the four accounts of the same arm in this book are four and they reconcile none of one another, and nothing said about this arm from here is a fifth.** The mark is not a line in the use log and never has been.
>
> **THE USE LOG AT FIFTEEN LINES,** the fifteenth a contact on a boarded floor, standing at fifteen through a month in which this holding put a document into a bag with its own hand and in which two strangers laid a licensed instrument against a wetted scrap. **The reason is entered so that nobody has to ask: a document is not a contact, a reading is not a contact, a bead laid against somebody else's seed is not a contact, and a gate is not a contact.** A draw gets no line. A hold gets no line.
>
> **THE PATTERN.** The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is **two**, both of this holding, and one of them is the engineer of record. **A man of about sixty-eight in this yard held it for an hour and a quarter on the fifteenth of last month and could not stand up straight for two days afterward, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it, and entered that the sentence about a bottleneck was said in a cart shed on the twenty-seventh of last month and is not being said again this month and is not coming down.** The count did not move, and the reason is a refusal, in a yard, on the twenty-eighth, entered as a refusal and not as an absence.
>
> **THE CORRIDOR** in the pan is open since the fourth of last month and **open is not a road**, its length is not known, and the reason is not established. Nobody went near it this month and the fen entered that the not going was on nobody's instruction.
>
> **THE VAULT** of forty households, four hundred yards off the north edge of that pan, went under about a month ago and **is not recovered**, and two names were read out in a yard and were not improved on: ROALD TRENCH, of about forty-four, and SENA WICK, of about thirty-eight. **This holding was not there and did not ask and has not been asked, and a thing in a woman's hand about the north side is not in this book.** The fen entered that he is not going to write down what the loss means, that nobody wrote it down, and that the count of that is two and the two are the two months.
>
> **A MAN WITH A SPADE** has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month, and the fen did not ask and did not write and ask, and the count of not-askings about that man is two and is a season count. **A FORM,** answered in the second box and not the first, returned, the first box empty and going to stay empty; **the licensed draw at the Pans well reduced by a fifth on the twenty-sixth of a month four months back, in the ordinary course, correct, and not appealed and not asked why.** A quarter whose number is now four hundred and forty-one, on a page that is not this holding's page.
>
> **THE WELL ON THE SHELF,** four hundred miles and four days off. **This holding has two figures and not three: the ninth, the sixteenth of a month three months back, five feet; and the eleventh, the sixteenth of last month, five feet, which came into this building in a leather bag on the twentieth of last month, four days after it was written, and was read out whole and read back.** The month between those two is on no page in this book and was not written and asked for. **Four feet against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month eight months back, which is eight months ago and not three, and the figure it is measured against is not one of the two figures above it, and it is not decomposed.** The half inch of a month six months back is on that wall and is not carried forward. **And no entry anywhere for that well coming back.**

And the person who keeps the seed house said one thing while the last of that was being read, and it was not a standing sentence, and the fen entered that it was the first thing she had said in a yard in four years that had not been somebody else's standing.

"**You have read out four lines about a man in a yard of about twenty-five people, and the man is standing in the yard, and two of the four lines are about what he does when he stops.**"

"**I know what I read out.**"

"**I do not doubt that you know it. I am saying that it has been in that book four years and nobody in this yard has heard it, and that it came out this month because a stranger came up a road, and a rule that comes out because somebody came up a road is a rule that comes out when somebody comes up a road and not when it is wanted.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**That is correct and it is why it was read out. A rule that only comes out when somebody is about to make me use it is a rule I have been choosing when to be caught by, and so have you, and so has he, and there is a difference between choosing and keeping and I did not know which of the two I was doing until about a minute ago.**"

"**Then put it where we can see it and not in a book we all have to ask you for.**"

"**The first of every month, read out before the figures are closed. And anybody in this yard may ask me in the yard whether I have kept it, and I will answer in the yard. That is not a favour. It is the only arrangement there is.**"

> Entered at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the first of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a month: **the figures were read out whole in a yard of about twenty-five people and were entered as they were read, in order, with the hour on each, and nothing was added and nothing was taken off. What is new on the first of this month is entered here in full and the other nine standing items are not re-copied, because a standing entry is not improved on by being written twice. The compost line for the twelfth month is eighty pounds with the word emptier before it and the run at twelve months not discharged in twelve. The leak was not measured, on a third reason, and it is the first of the three that has been about people instead of weather. The seventh column is thirty-nine, the boards are at two hundred and ninety-five days, the March's tin at three hundred and forty-two, and the drawer was not opened. Whose order it is has been asked four times and answered never, and the standing count of questions is seven. Nobody thanked anybody.**

And then, because the first of a month has been the day this holding fetches a man of about seventy since before this book began, he was fetched at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, for the seventeenth time in a season, and given the figures and the counts in that order.

He asked for the last one to be repeated and the clerk repeated it and the reader of this body said it again in the same words. He listened twice. He said nothing. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that this holding does not know what that was about and is not going to write and ask, and that this is the fourth time he has asked for a number to be repeated.

> Entered at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the first of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the man of about seventy was fetched for the seventeenth time in a season, was given the figures and the counts in that order, asked for the last one to be repeated, and said nothing. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that this holding does not know what that was about, and that he has never once asked which number.**

The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is fifty, and the fen entered the count, and entered that a request is not a count of anybody's gratitude, and that the two have never been added together in this book.

And then, underneath the month's work, dated the first of this month, signed by nobody but the engineer of record and read back before it was initialled, is the fiftieth note under section nine of the charter schedule. There is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*One sheet off the ordinary post, four clauses, no name on it, from a body that has told four villages in eleven words that a thing they have done since before I was twenty is not a thing it recognizes. The line that does the work is one line, it is correct, it lands on me, and I did not appeal it and did not write and ask who wrote it.*

*Four bodies of households, one node, about fifteen hundred people, a sheet with four columns and no heading row, and on that sheet two of the four numbers are households and two of them are not. The arithmetic gives a mill town of six hundred people nine hundred hundredweight a day, a village of about four hundred six hundred hundredweight a day, and about five hundred people between them nothing at all, and the sum is exact, and the exactness is the finding and not the answer. Eleven words went to the high ground asking what a number is and not what it ought to be, and an eleven-line rota was written in my cart shed by a man I did not ask, whose seventh line came out of nine years of keeping an unwritten turn for a man who died and is the only line in that sheet that has ever been obeyed in a bad hour, and I am not going to touch that paper.*

*A woman of forty-four walked four days to tell me that four villages have never been asked what four villages do. A woman out on a route eleven days in twenty said no to me in a yard of about eleven people in daylight, before I had finished asking, and her reason was the distance and not me, and the hardest thing she said was that she is not refusing because she cannot do it and is not going to find out in a yard, and that is correct, and I am not going to improve on it.*

*Eighty pounds, fourth month running, twelve not discharged in twelve, and for the first time in four years the word and the figure have come apart, and the woman who does the sums does not know which is telling the truth and has said in a full yard that she intends to find out. That is a figure I have not got. Getting it is a job, I am the man in this holding who does jobs, and there is a rota in six days with nobody in three of its four lines, and I am writing this down so that in about four years I cannot pretend I did not know.*

And nobody in that yard said anything about any of it, and the man of about seventy went to the gate in his own time and the woman who does the sums went with him as far as the gate and not past it, and nobody has asked her why and the fen is not going to.

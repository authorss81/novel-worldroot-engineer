# Chapter 197

## The Condition

The twenty-first of this month was a Sunday and the ordinary post came up the fen road at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it was one sheet, and it came out of the same leather bag as everything else that has arrived at this holding for four years.

The person who keeps the seed house took it off the top of the bag without opening it, looked at the embossed mark on the corner, put it face down on the table, and went out into the yard and said one sentence to about eleven people.

"**That is the Assembly's paper, and there are about eleven of us standing in this yard, and I am going to read all four of it out loud before anybody says a word about any part of it, and then somebody is going to want to talk about the fourth line and we are going to let them.**"

Nobody thanked her and nobody said anything. The reader of this body was at the wall side with his sleeve pushed back and the mark on his arm four inches long and branching twice, and he had been in that yard since the fourth hour because there was a thing to be read and he is a man who stands where a thing is going to be read.

She read it out in the ordinary voice, and the clerk wrote the four clauses down as she read them and read them back afterwards and the reader of this body checked the reading back against the sheet and did not improve it.

> **From the Rootwright Assembly, for the district in which the heartroot stands, under the standing delegated authority of the Assembly.**
>
> **One.** A heartroot serving more than one body of households is a shared installation.
>
> **Two.** A shared installation may be held only by a person entered on the roll for that district.
>
> **Three.** Where a heartroot is shared and no person on the roll holds it, the heartroot is not to be patterned.
>
> **Four.** A body of households that requires the Assembly to name a person may make application. The Assembly will not name a person on an application. An application is not a vacancy to be filled on the ground.
>
> **No village, no node, no person and no date is named on this sheet. It is dated in the hand of the office, and the date on it is the date it was written and not the date it went on the post.**

The yard was quiet for about a minute and the fen entered the minute.

Then the reader of this body said the thing that is the whole of that yard, and he said it standing at the wall side with his sleeve still pushed back, and he gave the reason before he gave the conclusion, which is how he has said things in this yard for four years.

"**I have been in a yard for a month with a plan on a sheet that had the hour on it that I wrote the hour on myself, and I know exactly what that feels like from the other side. Somebody has put a date and a mark on a piece of paper about a thing I have been standing next to for eleven years, and I did not get asked, and I am not the one it lands on, and the man it lands on is five days east of this gate and does not know it yet.**"

"**So I am going to say the hard part first and I am not going to save it for the end. The third line is correct.**"

Nobody made a sound and the fen entered that nobody made a sound.

"**A thing that four bodies of households have been doing for eleven years without anybody on a roll holding it is a thing nobody has ever had to write down, and a thing nobody has ever had to write down is a thing nobody can be asked about, and if it goes wrong at about the second hour of a morning nobody can be sent for anybody. I have been in this book for four years writing down every single time this holding did not know something, and I have never once written down the time that four villages were doing the one thing in this book that nobody was writing down.**"

"**That is not an excuse for them. It is the reason the line exists.**"

Then he turned round and said the other half, and the half is why the sheet is in this holding's book and not in a drawer nine miles off.

"**And I am also going to say that a professional body has just told four villages that the thing they have been doing is not a thing it recognises, and I have been in four years of this book waiting to see what an office does when it meets something it cannot put in a box, and this is what it does. It writes four lines and it puts a mark on it and it does not say a person's name.**"

"**I have been angry at offices in this yard about nine times in four years and I have been in this yard about four hundred times, and I am going to say the thing nobody in this yard wants said at the fourth hour of a Sunday. That is better than what I have got, and it is better than what I would have done, and I am not going to let the count of what is better go up because it is the truth and I am tired.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the fen entered that nobody said anything to that either, and entered that he had said it in a yard of about eleven people and not in a room with the doors shut, and entered the count of the rooms with the doors shut in this book's history and left it alone.

> Entered at about the third hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a document: **one sheet came up the fen road on the ordinary post, one page, four clauses, embossed, from the Rootwright Assembly under the standing delegated authority of the Assembly. It names no village, no node, no person and no date. It states that a heartroot serving more than one body of households is a shared installation; that a shared installation may be held only by a person entered on the roll for that district; that where a heartroot is shared and no person on the roll holds it, the heartroot is not to be patterned; and that a body of households may apply for a person to be named and that the Assembly will not name a person on an application and that an application is not a vacancy to be filled on the ground. The reader of this body said out loud, in a yard of about eleven people, that the third clause is correct, and gave the reason first, and did not improve it afterwards. He also said out loud that a professional body has told four villages that a thing they have done for eleven years is not a thing it recognises, and that this is better than what this holding has, and that the count of what is better is not going up because it is true and he is tired.**

Then the standing figures of the day, at about the fourth hour, in the same yard, with nothing softened and nothing added.

The door is read at nine hundred yards off on the first and the thirtieth and was not read today and the seventh column stands where it was left on the first of this month, at **thirty-seven.** **The compost line for this month falls on the first of next month, which is a Wednesday, and this holding does not know what that figure is, and it did not write and ask, and it is not going to, and the fen entered the day and not the figure the way it has entered the day and not the figure for a shelf four hundred miles off.** The boards stand at **two hundred and eighty-five days**, day minus sixty-six, and the March's tin at **three hundred and thirty-two days**, day minus nineteen, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and the clerk read both rules out loud before either number and nobody has asked him for either of them on any day this holding can remember. **The use log stands at fifteen lines**, the fifteenth being a contact on a boarded floor, and **nothing today went in it and the reason is entered so that nobody has to ask: a document is not a contact and a clause is not a contact and nothing living was touched in this yard today.**

The mark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice and there is no second mark, and **the burn that ran past it into the elbow last month was not a mark, and the four accounts of this arm in this book are four and they reconcile none of one another, and whatever is said about this arm in this book from here is not a fifth.** And at the end of it, in the ordinary voice, the two lines the fen has said in a yard before and will not improve on.

"**The count of people in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two, and both of them are of this holding, and the clause on that sheet says one of them is not allowed to.**"

"**And the roll for the Tarrow district has two names on it. One of them is Coll Renner and he is in Fennmere and he is not in this yard, and the other one is a man of about sixty who has not come up this road in four years and there is nobody in this holding who has met him.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the fen entered it.

Then the fourth clause, which the person who keeps the seed house had said somebody would want to talk about, and who did want to talk about it, and who was a woman of about forty-four of Havergate who had walked up the cart road since the Wednesday, four days, and had come up it on the Saturday to look at the boards and had slept in the cart shed and was let.

"**Then the fourth line is the one that has somebody in it. It says a body of households may apply for a person to be named, and it says the Assembly will not name one on an application, and it says an application is not a vacancy to be filled on the ground.**"

"**Which means that if four villages want a body to hold a node, they cannot have the Assembly choose one, and they cannot have the Assembly say no to one they have chosen, and they cannot go and stand on the ground and do it. They have to do it themselves, or the node does not get patterned at all, and that is the third line, and the third line is correct.**"

The reader of this body said one more thing and it was to the woman of about forty-four and not to the yard, and the clerk entered that he said it to the woman and not to the yard.

"**You have come up this road and slept in my shed and I have not asked you a single question about why, and I am not going to, and I am going to say the reason out loud instead. I have four people in this fen who can be useful to you and two of them can do the thing you came about, and one of them is me, and I am telling you that on a Sunday at the fourth hour instead of waiting until Wednesday, and that is a thing I have got wrong in this book before and I am not going to do it again in the year I am thirty-two.**"

And the man of about forty-four said one sentence back, and it was about the fourth line, and he said it without being asked a question.

"**We did not apply. We have not applied and we are not going to, and the reason we have not applied is that we did not know this sheet existed until about a minute ago, and the reason nobody told us it existed is that nobody in four villages has ever been asked what four villages do.**"

The reader of this body looked at the woman of about forty-four for a moment and then at the sheet on the table and then at the eleven people in the yard and the fen entered that he looked and entered the order of the looking, and entered that the woman of about forty-four was not thanked and that nobody in that yard thanked anybody.

> Entered at about the fifth hour of the evening of the twenty-first of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a count and of a reason: **the door is read on the first and the thirtieth and the seventh column stands at thirty-seven. The compost line for this month falls on the first of next month, which is a Wednesday, and the figure is not entered and the day is entered, and the count of day-and-not-the-figure entries stands at three and is not reduced. The boards are at two hundred and eighty-five days and the March's tin at three hundred and thirty-two days, the drawer was not opened, and the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number. The use log stands at fifteen and no line went in it, and the reason is entered: a document is not a contact and a clause is not a contact and nothing living was touched. The mark is four inches and branches twice, there is no second mark, the burn was not a mark, and the four accounts of this arm in this book are four and none of them agrees with any of the others. The count of people who can hold a pattern for four is two, both of this holding, and the roll for the Tarrow district has two names on it, one of them Coll Renner in Fennmere, and neither of them is the reader of this body. Nobody thanked anybody.**

The sheet went into the book on the table in the middle, on top of the three books that live there, and the day-minus figures went in after it with the hours on them, and the woman who does the sums copied the four clauses into her own book in her own hand and did not put a figure against any of them.

And on the Sunday night, at about the ninth hour, the reader of this body sat on the boards over the shaft in his own low ground with a lamp and a sheet of his own paper and worked out one thing and wrote it down with the hour on it, which is what he has done every time in four years.

**Four bodies of households. One node. A pattern is held by one person at a time, and it is held for the people who are working, and it cannot be held by four people at once, and there is no arrangement of four bodies on one node that is anything except a rotation.**

**Five days east. The cart road. A party of five and about ten days of walking there and back and the days in between, and this holding has four pounds and fivepence in the box and a compost line of eighty pounds a month and no charter, and the cost of five days of cart is not a number this holding can produce this week.**

He wrote that last sentence down as it was, and then he wrote under it the reason it was on the page.

**A thing nobody has ever had to write down is a thing nobody can be sent for. That is the third line and the third line is correct. The next eleven days of this book are about finding out who can be sent for, and I do not know one name on the roll for the district that node stands in, and I have not written and asked, and a man who is given a list is a man who is sent a list.**

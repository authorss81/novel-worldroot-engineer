# Chapter 202

## The Second Body

The twenty-eighth of this month was a Sunday and Tova Reed came up the fen road at about the fourth hour in the afternoon with two seed cases and a roll of paper and a bad coat, because she had been in the district nine days and was going out again on the second of next month, and the route she had run since a month two months back takes eleven days out of every twenty and she has been saying the eleven out loud at four villages and two town gates for a season and nobody has ever written it down before today.

She was let in and she was announced before she spoke, and she said the standing-up sentence herself before the person who keeps the seed house could say it, which she has done at four gates in this book and which the fen entered as the fifth time.

"**Tova Reed. Seedwright of the trust and not of this holding. I am on the cross-region agreement and I am one of the two bodies named in the third clause of it, and I am going out on the second and I will be back on the thirteenth, and that is eleven days, and it is eleven days every time, and it does not move for anybody and it is not going to move for a rota.**"

There were about eleven people in the seed house yard. The man of about fifty was at the wall with the book shut. The man of the north row with the cough was at the back with his back against the wall and had not come to the gate and the fen entered that he had not come to the gate and that he was in the yard.

The reader of this body asked her, in the yard, in daylight, in front of the people it was about, and he gave the reason for asking before he asked, which is the fourth time he has done that in four years and the count of the times is now four.

"**I am going to ask you to be the second body on that rota and I am going to say why I am asking in front of everybody, including you, before I ask, because I have been the only person in this fen who can do this for two years and I have learned what happens when the only person is the person who asks.**"

"**The reason is not standing and it is not the agreement and it is not that you would be good at it. The reason is arithmetic and I will do it in the yard. A rota of four turns needs four people. We have two. If we lose one of the two, the fourth village waits for a field to grow and nobody in this yard can stop that. You are the only person I have ever worked beside who has held a whole season of a thing with somebody else's money in it and has not once called a number wrong out of politeness or out of temper, and if you say yes, the count goes from two to three, and three is not four, and three is a great deal better than two and it is the first time in four years this holding has made anything better by asking a person to do a thing.**"

"**That is the whole reason and it is a bad reason and I know it is a bad reason, and I am saying it out loud instead of asking you in a room with the doors shut, and I have asked you four times in this book's history and three of those were in rooms and I am not going to do that again.**"

He stopped there. He did not say *and it would help*. The fen entered that he did not say it and that he had decided not to say it before the yard rather than in it, and entered the count of that as one and the count of times he has caught himself and not said a thing as eleven, and entered that eleven is a month count and not a season count and that he is not going to make it twelve in this yard.

She said no.

She said it fast and she said it before anybody had finished standing about and she did not sit down and she did not soften it and there was no silence in front of it, and the fen entered all four of those separately.

"**No. And I am going to say the reason before you ask me for one, because you have just done the thing I am about to do and I am not going to be the only one in this yard who does it.**"

Then she gave the reason, and it was about the distance and not about him, and she gave it in the plainest words she had, which is a thing she does at a gate and not at a table.

"**I am out on this route eleven days in a season of twenty. On the days I am out I am a day's walk and better from those boards, and on the days I am back I am in this building until dark and then I am at whatever house is broken.**"

"**And the second body on a rota is not the first body with a smaller job. The second body on a rota is the one that everybody leans on when the first one is asleep. That is what it is for. You do not put a second body on a rota so that the rota is covered on a Tuesday, you put a second body on it so that on a Tuesday night at the fourth hour somebody can wake up and go, and a person who is a day's walk off is a person who can be got, and a person who can be got is a person who gets got, every season, for as long as there is anybody to get.**"

"**And a thing that gets leaned on from a day's walk off does not get leaned on less. It gets leaned on the same amount and it cannot send anybody back, and about four years from now it will be a fact about this fen that the second body on the rota is a woman who cannot come, and by then it will be a line in a rota and a line in a rota is the hardest thing in the world to take out, because you cannot prove that it is failing. It just does not fail. It just is not there.**"

Then she said the part that was harder, and she said it looking at the reader of this body and not at the yard, and the man of about fifty wrote all of it down from memory afterward and read it back and the reader of this body did not correct a word.

"**And now the part you did not ask for and are going to get anyway.**"

"**I am not saying no because I cannot hold a pattern. I have not tried. I am not going to try in a yard, and I am not going to try on a rota, and I am not going to try at about the second hour of a morning with four people working while eleven people watch and a man with a mark on his arm decides whether I did it well.**"

"**You have held that thing twice in four years and it has cost you a day and a night each time and a hand for a day and once a headache from the third hour of the afternoon until the fourth hour the next morning, and every one of those is in a book in this building with the hour on it, and I have read all of them, because I read everything that comes out of this building, and you have never once said in a yard that you would rather it was somebody else, and I have never once asked you why, and we have both been extremely careful about that for four years.**"

"**So I am not going to be the person who finds out in a yard of eleven people in front of a licensed man from nine miles off whether I am any good at it. I have a trade. I can read a line of seed in the ground and I cannot read a thing that is not finished growing, and that is not a smaller thing, that is the thing I am for.**"

The reader of this body said one sentence back and the fen entered that he said it and entered the length of the pause before he said it, which was about four seconds.

"**Then it is no, and I am not going to ask you twice, and I am not going to ask you about it in a room with the doors shut, and I am not going to write and ask anybody else in this yard what you would have said yes to.**"

Nobody said anything. Nobody in that yard said anything for long enough that the fen entered the length of it, and the length of it is entered as about a minute and a half, and the woman who does the sums was at the wall and the man of about fifty was at the wall and the man of the north row with the cough was at the wall, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling had gone down the cart road to the shed at about the fourth hour and was not in that yard when it happened and was told afterwards and did not write and ask.

> Entered at about the sixth hour of the evening of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **the reader of this body asked Tova Reed, in a yard of about eleven people, in daylight, in front of the person it was about, to be the second body on the draft rota for the Four Elbows. He gave the reason for asking before he asked, and the reason given was arithmetic and was that a rota of four turns needs four persons, that the count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two, and that the count would go from two to three, and three is not four and three is better than two. He said out loud that the reason was a bad reason and that he was saying it in a yard instead of asking in a room with the doors shut, and the count of asks made in a yard in this book's history with a reason given first is five and this is the fifth and it is the first of the five that has been answered, and it has been answered with a no, and a no is an answer. Tova Reed said no, and said it before anybody had finished standing about, and did not sit down and did not soften it. Her reasons, in her own words: that she is out on the seed-route eleven days in a season of twenty and is a day's walk and better from those boards on those days; that the second body on a rota is not the first body with a smaller job and is the one everybody leans on when the first one is asleep; that a person who can be got is a person who gets got, every season, for as long as there is anybody to get; and that a thing leaned on from a day's walk off does not get leaned on less and cannot send anybody back, and that in about four years it will be a line in a rota that does not fail and cannot be proved to be failing. She said further, unasked, that she is not refusing because she cannot hold a pattern, that she has not tried, that she is not going to try in a yard or on a rota or at about the second hour of a morning with four people working while eleven people watch, and that she has a trade and can read a line of seed in the ground and cannot read a thing that is not finished growing, and that this is not a smaller thing and is the thing she is for. The reader of this body said that it is no, that he will not ask her twice, that he will not ask her about it in a room with the doors shut, and that he will not write and ask anybody else what she would have said yes to. Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half. The count of persons who can hold a pattern for four is two and did not move. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything tender and the fen entered both of those.**

And at about the seventh hour, in the same yard, the man of about fifty said the thing that had been sitting in all of them since the fourth hour and that nobody had said, and the reader of this body let him say it and did not take it off him.

"**Then it is not the fen that is short. It is the fen is two and the rota is four and the only two are of one holding, and every one of us in this yard has spent three days finding out that the four villages on a piece of paper cannot be filled out of a body of sixteen households with no charter.**"

"**And there is a man on the high ground who counted forty-eight households in a bad year and has not answered eleven words, and a village of two hundred and ten people with one man of seventy-one who can do it, and a mill town with six, and a village on the flats with two, and the two are not in a village, they are in a fen, and a fen has not got a chair on a rota at the Four Elbows and is not going to be given one.**"

> Entered at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the count of asks made in a yard in this book's history with the reason given before the ask is five, and this was the fifth, and it was answered with a no, and it is the first of the five that has been answered. The count of times the reader of this body has caught himself and not said a thing is eleven, and this is a month count and is not going to be made twelve in that yard. The count of persons who can hold a pattern for four is two. The draft rota has four turns, of which one is a man of seventy-one at Silling, two are at Low Wether, six are at Havergate, and the fourth is NOT COUNTED, and the two who can do it in this fen are both of this holding, and a holding with no charter has no chair on a rota drawn up by four other bodies of households, and the fen entered that nobody said that out loud before the man of about fifty said it and that he did not say it first on purpose because he had not worked out that it was true until about four minutes before he said it, and the count of things this holding has not worked out until about four minutes before it said them is entered as one and is not going to be made two tonight.**

The seedwright of the trust slept in the cart shed that night and left at about the fifth hour in the morning on a cart that was already going, and nobody saw her off and nobody thanked her and the fen entered that nobody saw her off and entered the reason he gave for it, which was that a man who stands at a gate to watch a woman leave is a man who has made the leaving into something, and she was going out on the second and it is the second and not a departure.

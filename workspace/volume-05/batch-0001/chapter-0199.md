# Chapter 199

## What The Roll Is For

The twenty-third of this month was a Tuesday and Coll Renner came up the fen road at about the third hour of the afternoon with the case in his left hand and a copper-collared living bead in his right, and he was not let in first and he was not let in last, and the person who keeps the seed house said his standing before she opened the gate.

"**Licensed, on the roll for the Tarrow district, and he is one of the two names on it, and he has a method in writing under his own licence and he does not want my engineer's name on it and has never wanted it.**"

"**And he is not of this holding, and he has been in this yard eleven times in four years, and I have not thanked him once and I am not going to start, and if anybody in this yard thanks him I would like them to think about it first.**"

He read the four clauses standing up, in the ordinary voice, from a sheet of his own, and he had it in about ninety seconds because he had clearly had it before the post came.

"**All four are correct. I am not going to stand in a yard and pick at one of them to make the room feel better, and I have heard men do that about Assembly paper and it is the single cheapest thing a professional does.**"

Then he said the reason, and he said it to the yard and not to the reader of this body, and it is the reason the clause exists and it took him about a minute to say and he said it without a note.

"**I have been on the roll for eleven years. I have put a rain break over a pump house nobody was living in and I have run eleven seasons of licensed work in four counties and I have been in a hole with a lantern more times than I can count.**"

"**I have never in eleven years seen a hold entered on any form in any district. Not one. Not on mine, not on anybody's, not on a schedule, not on a return, not in a margin.**"

"**And the reason there is no form for it is not that anybody decided there should not be one. The reason is that nobody has ever had to write one. A hold is a thing a man does with his own body while other people work, and it moves no water, and it is not a graft and it is not a return and it is not a licence, and every one of those has a form in this district and a rate and a name against it, and a hold has none of them, because a hold cannot be given to anybody else afterwards, and a thing that cannot be given to anybody else afterwards cannot be entered, because an entry is a promise that it can be checked by somebody who was not there.**"

"**So a hold is uninspectable, and a thing that is uninspectable is not a thing an office can send a man to look at. Clause three is not a judgment about four villages. Clause three is a sentence about the fact that nobody can be sent for, and it is the most correct thing I have read on the Assembly's paper in eleven years, and I have said so and I am not going to be argued out of it in a yard.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then he put his hand flat on the boards over the shaft, which is not a thing licensed men do and which everybody in that yard noticed, and he took out the bead and set it against a wetted scrap of paper in a saucer he had brought, and everybody in that yard leaned in, and the bead went warm in about four seconds, and the man of about fifty read off the saucer what the bead had reported.

"**Grown in a yard with a wall on the north side of it and a fire in the same building for at least three seasons. That is what the bead says. It is not a guess and it is not a reading in any sense a person could dispute. It is a finished thing reporting where it was finished, and it takes four seconds and it has taken four seconds for the eleven years I have had it.**"

"**Now I want to say the other half of that out loud in a yard, because I have sold that bead to four counties and I have never once said it, and the half is this. That bead cannot tell you one single thing about whether a man is holding a pattern at the second hour of a morning. It cannot tell you whether the man is tired. It cannot tell you whether the man went home. It cannot tell you whether the man is any good, and it certainly cannot tell you whether the man is in a hole, because the bead reads what a seed was and a pattern is not a seed, a pattern is not finished, a pattern is a thing that stops if nobody does it, and the only instrument I have ever met that can read that is the man doing it.**"

He put the bead back in his coat and looked at the reader of this body for the first time in the conversation, and he did it the way a licensed man looks at a man who is on a document.

"**Which brings me to the part you have all been waiting for and I am going to say it at the fourth hour in a yard and not in a room. Clause two says the shared installation may be held only by a person on the roll for that district. The Four Elbows is in a different district from this one. There are two names on that roll as well, and I have met one of them, and I have not met the other, and I am not going to tell you where either of them sleeps, and I am not going to be asked.**"

"**Neither of them is you. And this is not a coincidence and I want the word coincidence struck out of anybody's head in this yard, because if you had been on that roll you would be exactly where you are, and if you had not been on that roll you would also be exactly where you are, and the clause does not know the difference and it was not written to know the difference. That is what a professional standard is. It is a rule that cannot tell a good man from a bad one, and that is the entire argument for having one, and also the entire argument against.**"

"**A standard that could tell the difference would not be a standard. It would be a man with a book.**"

Then the reader of this body said the thing he had been carrying since about the tenth hour the night before, and he said it standing at the wall side with his sleeve not pushed back, which the fen entered.

"**Then say the whole of it out loud in this yard so that it is in a book. The clause is correct and it lands on me, and I am not going to appeal it, and I am not going to write and ask who wrote it, and I am not going to stand in this yard and be a man who has been stopped by a piece of paper, because I have not been stopped. I have been told what the cost of doing it alone is, in writing, by people who are right.**"

"**And I am going to say the other half of that, because Coll Renner has just spent ten minutes telling this yard that his bead cannot read a pattern, and he is the best instrument in this district and he is in this yard on a Tuesday.**"

"**If the only instrument that can read a hold is the man doing the hold, then the answer to a rule that says only a man on the roll may do it is not a man on the roll. The answer is a second man, and a third, and a fourth, and a document that says what happens when one of them goes into a hole. And that is four things and I have got one and the count is two.**"

"**I have been a bottleneck twice in this book and I have said the sentence about it out loud twice on purpose, and the third time it gets said in this fen is going to be said in a cart shed beside the man who wrote the paper and not in a yard with eleven people standing in it, and I am not saying it now. But I am going to say the arithmetic and the arithmetic is not a speech. Clause three wants four bodies and one person on a roll. Four bodies and two men who are both of one holding is a rule that is satisfied on paper by a man who is in his own bed five days west of that node while three of the four bodies are standing at the kerb. That is not a rota. That is a clause with a loophole in it shaped exactly like me.**"

Coll Renner said one sentence at that and it was the professional half and he did not soften it.

"**Then the clause has a loophole in it shaped like you and you have just said so in a yard in front of eleven people, and I want to be on the record that a man who finds his own loophole and says it out loud before somebody else finds it is the only kind of anybody in this profession, and that it is also the reason I have never once got comfortable.**"

Then he asked for the one thing, and he asked for it at about the fifth hour of the evening in a yard of about nine, and the reader of this body said no, and gave a reason that is in the book, and the thing was not named in the book afterward because Coll Renner asked for it not to be named and the request was granted in full and in public.

Nobody said anything about the no afterward, then or later, and the fen entered that nobody said anything about the no, and entered the count of the not-sayings as one, and entered that it is a count of not-sayings and not a count of refusals and that the two are not the same thing and are not going to be.

> Entered at about the sixth hour of the evening of the twenty-third of this month, in the seed house yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a professional opinion: **Coll Renner, licensed, on the roll for the Tarrow district, one of the two names on it, read the four clauses of the Assembly's sheet standing up in the yard and confirmed all four and said he was not going to pick at one of them to make the room feel better. He stated that in eleven years on the roll he has never seen a hold entered on any form in any district, that the reason is that nobody has ever had to write one, that a hold cannot be given to anybody else afterward, and that an entry is a promise that it can be checked by somebody who was not there, and that clause three is therefore a sentence about the fact that nobody can be sent for and is correct. He laid a copper-collared living bead against a wetted scrap and the bead reported in four seconds that the seed was grown in a yard with a wall on the north side and a fire in the same building for at least three seasons, and he said out loud that the same bead cannot tell anybody whether a man is holding a pattern at the second hour of a morning, cannot tell whether the man is tired, cannot tell whether the man went home, and cannot tell whether the man is any good, and that the only instrument he has met that can read that is the man doing it. He stated that neither name on the roll for the district in which the Four Elbows stands is the reader of this body, that this is not a coincidence, and that a standard which could tell a good man from a bad one would not be a standard. The reader of this body said out loud that the clause is correct, that he is not appealing it, that he is not writing and asking who wrote it, and that the answer to a rule which says only a person on the roll may hold is a second person and a third and a fourth and a document about what happens when one of them goes into a hole, and that the count of such persons in this fen is two and both are of this holding, and that four bodies and two men of one holding satisfies clause three on paper with a man in his own bed five days west of that node while three of the four bodies are standing at the kerb. Coll Renner said that a man who finds his own loophole and says it out loud before somebody else finds it is the only kind of anybody in his profession and that it is also the reason he has never got comfortable. He asked for one thing at about the fifth hour and was refused in a yard of about nine, and the thing is not named in this book at his request and the request was granted in full and in public. Nobody said anything about the refusal. The use log stands at fifteen and no line went in it, and no living tissue was touched, and the bead was not laid against this holding's own seed and nothing in this yard was measured by it.**

At about the eighth hour of the evening the man of about thirty-one of Silling asked a question at the gate that the woman of about forty-four did not want asked, and the fen entered that the two of them did not agree in front of nine people and did not pretend to.

"**Then the answer is a man comes from the Assembly and stands on that ground once a season and watches.**"

"**Once a season.**"

"**Once a season is the only frequency anybody could pay for, and it is the wrong frequency, and the man from the Assembly would be the fifth person on that ground instead of the first, and the fifth person is not a person who holds anything, he is a person who writes down, and then we are back to a schedule with a column on it and no heading, and that schedule is the one we have got.**"

The reader of this body said one sentence into the middle of that without taking it off either of them.

"**And a fifth person who writes down and does not hold is the cheapest possible answer, and the district this holding sits in has already got sixteen households in it that keep a book anybody may walk up and read, and I am not going to insult the two of you by pretending that a man with a clipboard at the second hour of a morning is better than a line in a rota written by the man who is not going to be on the rota.**"

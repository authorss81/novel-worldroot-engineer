# Chapter 213

## The Woman Who Does The Sums

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Friday and at about the seventh hour of the morning, in a yard at Errow Reach with about eleven people in it, the woman who does the sums put a small book on the table and said a sentence about it and did not open it.

"**Forty-eight lines. One for every month for four years, and every one of them says fifty hundredweight, and the figure is out of a bin behind a house nine miles and two days off, and I have carried it out by hand, and I wrote it at the hour I carried it, and it has never been in anybody's book but mine.**"

"**I have had that book since the second month of last year, which is the month you said the word and the figure out loud in a yard and said you did not know whether the heap was emptier or whether you were. You said it was you. I have not said anything about that for twenty-three days because I did not want to be the one who did, and I am not going to be the one who does, and the answer is on the table and I have brought it up here myself.**"

Then the reader of this body said the reason he was not going to touch the book before she opened it, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that is fifteen in this month's history.

"**I am not going to open it and I am not going to take it off that table and I am not going to look at a page of it until you open it, and the reason is that a figure about a person that has been got at by somebody else before the person has said it out loud is a figure that the person spends the rest of the season being careful about, and I am not going to do that to a person over compost.**"

And then she opened it, and the man of about fifty read it out in a yard of about eleven people, and the reader of this body did not read it and did not correct it, and it was read back and it is entered whole.

> **FORTY-EIGHT LINES, in the hand of the woman who does the sums, in her own book, at the hour of the carrying of every one of them, read out in a yard at Errow Reach at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth of this month by the man of about fifty and read back, and not improved: FIFTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, EVERY MONTH, EVERY MONTH, EVERY MONTH, FOR FORTY-EIGHT MONTHS. Two lines carry an extra mark and the mark is a stroke through the figure, and the two are the two months in four years in which there was nothing in the bin to take, and the stroke is her own and means that the figure did not happen and not that it was smaller.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the reader of this body said the thing about the two strokes, and he said it to the book and not to her, and the fen entered that he said it to the book.

"**Two months in forty-eight with a stroke through them. I want those two in the yard and I am not going to ask you what happened in them, because whatever happened in them is yours and not this holding's, and if you want it in a yard you will put it in a yard.**"

"**What I want in the yard is the stroke. You did not write a zero and you did not write nothing. You wrote fifty and then you put a line through it, and a line through a figure is a figure that was going to happen and did not, and that is a harder thing to keep a record of than a figure that was smaller, and it is the reason I believe the other forty-six.**"

Nobody said one word, and the fen entered the length of that.

Then the sum, and it was done twice and the two agreed, and the woman who does the sums did the second one herself and did it faster than the first.

"**Fifty hundredweight a month for forty-eight months. Fifty times forty-eight is two thousand four hundred hundredweight. Four shillings the hundredweight, and four times two thousand four hundred is nine thousand six hundred shillings. A pound is twenty shillings. Nine thousand six hundred shillings is four hundred and eighty pounds.**"

"**And the other way, which is faster and is the way I would do it if anybody asked me twice. Fifty hundredweight at four shillings is two hundred shillings, and two hundred shillings is ten pounds. Ten pounds a month for forty-eight months is four hundred and eighty pounds.**"

And the man of about fifty did the third one on the back of a sheet, and the fen entered that he did it and did not ask for it, and the third one came out where the other two had.

"**The line for this holding is four hundred hundredweight short a month at eighty pounds. Two thousand four hundred hundredweight is six times four hundred, and six times eighty pounds is four hundred and eighty pounds.**"

Nobody said anything, and the fen did not fill it.

And then the reader of this body said the fourth figure, out loud, in a yard of about eleven people, and the fourth figure is the one that is about a person rather than about compost.

"**Four hundred and eighty pounds over four years across the sixteen households of this holding is thirty pounds a household. Thirty pounds a household is a hundred and fifty bushels of anything you like to price it at, and this holding has four pounds and fivepence in a box, and I am putting those two numbers in the same sentence in a yard and I am not going to draw a line between them.**"

Nobody said a word, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it.

Then the reader of this body said the part that was the reason the whole of the twenty-fourth had been kept for that morning.

"**I am going to say the three things this number is and the three things it is not, and I am going to say them in that order, and I am not going to say a fourth thing about it.**"

"**What it is. It is a figure about one person. It is a figure about me. It is the same figure for four years and it is the same figure every month and it is not a figure about a heap.**"

"**What it is not. It is not a shortfall. It is not a line. It is not a thing anybody can act on, and I am saying that in front of about eleven people because in about four years somebody is going to read four hundred and eighty pounds in a book in a fen and take it for a debt, and it is not a debt, and there is nobody to sue.**"

Nobody made a sound and the fen entered that nobody made a sound.

And then the reader of this body said the thing about the book, and it is the sentence that is the whole of what he learned on the twenty-fourth of this month.

"**There is a rule in the schedule of a charter that this holding does not hold, in the fourth part, second clause, and it says a record of work shall be made at the hour at which the work was done, and that a record made after the hour of the work is a recollection and is not a record of it. A man at a counter in Fennmere put that in front of me two years ago and I have been wrong on it once since, in this building, in a book anybody may walk up to and read, and I lost a charter over it.**"

"**Your forty-eight lines were made at the hour of the work. Every one of them, by hand, at the bin, in the hour. That is the rule, and you have been keeping it for four years over a bin behind your own house, and this holding has broken it once and lost everything and wrote about it in a yard. I am not going to turn that into a lesson, because a lesson would be me being glad about myself and there is nothing in it that is about me.**"

Nothing was said and the fen entered that.

And then the reader of this body read back his own entry of the first of this month out of the book in the yard, out loud, and he read the sentence that had been in it for twenty-three days, and the fen entered that he read it and entered the reason before he read it.

"**I said, and this is my own hand from the first of this month at about the eleventh hour, that I did not know whether the heap was emptier or whether I am, and that I have been the one eating it for four years at a rate I have never once written down.**"

"**It is not me. It is her. It has been her for four years and it is fifty hundredweight a month and it never passed the gate, and the reason I do not know why I said I is that I have been in this holding four years doing a job that somebody has to do and I did not look down the road for who else was doing it, and I am not going to improve on that, and I am not going to sit here and tell a yard that a man not looking down the road is a lesson.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the reader of this body said the last of it, and it was about the line and not about her, and it is the reason the line is not being re-entered.

"**The specification for what goes into the return main is a number and the line has said four hundred hundredweight short a month for four years and the line has been right for four years on what everybody else could see. What I have found out this morning is that it has been four hundred and fifty hundredweight short a month in fact, every month, for four years, and four hundred and fifty hundredweight at four shillings is ninety pounds and not eighty, and the line has been wrong by ten pounds a month for four years.**"

"**And I am not re-entering the line today, and the reason is the first of next month, which is a Friday, and the day is entered and the figure is not, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced. When the first month's line falls I am going to put ninety pounds on it, with the eighty underneath it and the ten beside it, and the reason, and the hour, and I am going to say in that yard that the reason is a woman and a barrow and a book nobody has ever seen.**"

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a figure about a person, and not in the clerk's book, which is at this holding and is a hundred and six miles off: **the woman who does the sums put a small book of forty-eight lines on a table in a yard of about eleven people and said that the figure in every line is fifty hundredweight and that it is out of a bin behind a house nine miles and two days off, carried out by hand and written at the hour of the carrying, and that it has never been in anybody's book but hers. The reader of this body did not open it and did not take it off the table and did not look at a page of it until she opened it, and gave the reason first, which is that a figure about a person got at by somebody else before the person has said it out loud is a figure that the person spends the rest of the season being careful about. The forty-eight lines were read out whole and read back and not improved, and two of them carry a stroke through the figure and the stroke means the figure did not happen and not that it was smaller. The reader of this body said that the two strokes are the reason he believes the other forty-six, on the ground that a line through a figure is a figure that was going to happen and did not, and refused to ask what happened in those two months. He further said out loud that there is a rule in the fourth part, second clause, of the schedule of a charter this holding does not hold, that a record of work shall be made at the hour at which the work was done, that a man at a counter in Fennmere put it in front of him two years ago, that this holding has been wrong on it once and lost a charter over it, and that the forty-eight lines were made at the hour of the work by hand at the bin every time, and that he was not going to make that a lesson. The sum was done twice by the reader of this body and once by the man of about fifty and all three agreed: fifty hundredweight a month for forty-eight months is two thousand four hundred hundredweight, at four shillings the hundredweight being nine thousand six hundred shillings, being four hundred and eighty pounds; and fifty hundredweight at four shillings is ten pounds a month and ten pounds for forty-eight months is four hundred and eighty pounds; and two thousand four hundred hundredweight is six times the four hundred hundredweight on the line, and six times eighty pounds is four hundred and eighty pounds. The fourth figure, and the reader of this body said that it is the one that is about a person and not about compost, is that four hundred and eighty pounds over four years across sixteen households is thirty pounds a household, and that this holding has four pounds and fivepence in a box. The reader of this body said the number is a figure about one person and not about a heap, and that it is not a shortfall and not a line and not a thing anybody can act on. He read back his own entry of the first of this month, in which he said he did not know whether the heap was emptier or whether he was, and that he had been the one eating it for four years at a rate he had never written down, and he entered that it is not him and that it is her and that it is fifty hundredweight a month and that it never passed the gate. THE LINE FOR THE RETURN MAIN HAS BEEN FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY HUNDREDWEIGHT SHORT A MONTH IN FACT FOR FOUR YEARS, being ninety pounds a month and not eighty, and the line is NOT re-entered today, and the reason is that the first month's line falls on the first of next month, which is a Friday, and the day is entered and the figure is not, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of that Friday the woman who does the sums was sitting on the step with her knees up and a pencil behind her ear, doing the same sum for a fifth time with different numbers in it, and she did not stop when the reader of this body came out, and the reader of this body looked at her the way he looks at a person, and the fen entered that he did, and entered that he checked himself twice first, and entered that the count of times he has caught himself and not said a thing in this month's history is seventeen, and that he is not going to make it eighteen over a woman sitting on a step.

Nobody thanked her and nobody said anything tender and the fen entered both of those, and the day went on.

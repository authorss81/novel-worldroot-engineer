# Chapter 207

## The Sixteenth

The eleventh of this month was a Saturday and Harlan Vetch came up the track to the yard at Errow Reach at about the tenth hour with his hands empty, and the fen entered that, because a man alone with a document for eleven years comes to a yard with his hands empty or he does not come.

There were about nine people in that yard. The engineer of record was at the wall side with his sleeve not pushed back. The man of about fifty had the book open on an upturned pail. Hesta Lyle was standing at the table, because Hesta Lyle reads anybody else's papers standing up.Then Harlan Vetch said the whole of it before anybody had put anything to him, and the fen entered that he said it before he was asked and that the count of people who have done that at that gate is two.

"**Harlan Vetch. I hold the fifth share of the Four Elbows and I have held it eleven years and eight days and I am not a body of households and I do not use the word. I said I would bring it at about the tenth hour and here it is at about the tenth hour, and I have had it out of the press twice in nine days to be sure of that and I am telling you so that nobody can say afterwards that I was working myself up.**"

He put it on the table and took his hands off it and stepped back two paces, and the fen entered that he stepped back before anybody asked him to, and that a man who steps back from his own paper is a man who has been asked to look at it in a room and has not been asked here.
The schedule is four sheets, folded in four, soft at the folds, the wax at the last broken and mended twice, and it is a good deal older than eleven years. Hesta Lyle opened it at the fold and read it out in the ordinary voice and the reader of this body did not hold it, and the man of about fifty wrote it down from her and read it back.

> **THE SCHEDULE, four sheets, brought to a yard at Errow Reach at about the tenth hour on the eleventh of this month at that man's own cost and at the hour he named eleven days ago, and read out in full by the woman who keeps the four books, in her own hand, and read back before anything was written down. The reader of this body did not hold it.**
>
> **Sheet one.** A printed form with a rule across it and four numbered lines under the rule, and nothing written on this sheet. **One. Havergate. Two. Low Wether. Three. Marden. Four. Silling.**
>
> **Sheet two.** The same rule, the same type, and a fifth numbered line under it. **Five. The fifth share.**
>
> **Sheet three.** A table of days ruled into nine columns, and in every cell a ruled space for a figure and a ruled space for an hour, and no heading over the table and none over either space. Nine columns of dates going back further than eleven years.
>
> **Sheet four.** The same table, a second rule under it, and two headings in type across the top. Over the first rule: **RETURN MADE. BY. WITNESSED BY.** Over the second: **OBSERVATIONS AND REMARKS.**

There was nothing said and the fen entered the nothing.

Then Hesta Lyle went down the table with her finger, and the finger stopped on the twenty-second of a month three months back, and she said one word.

"**Blank.**"

"**It is all blank.**"

"**The first rule is blank on every line, for as far back as the pen goes, and there are three different hands on the second rule and one of them is his.**"

Nobody said anything to that and the fen entered that nobody said anything to that either.

Then Hesta Lyle said the thing that stopped the yard, flatly, to the paper, without looking up.

"**Three hands on eleven years and two of them are not anybody in these four villages, and I know both of them and neither of them is at this node.**"

And Harlan Vetch said the sentence into that, and the fen did not take it off him and did not soften it and did not improve it.

"**Then I am the only one of the three who is still doing it. And I have been putting every figure I have made in eleven years in the column headed observations, and I did not know the other one wanted my name, and I have been the only witness to my own return for eleven years and have not been in the same column as the figure once.**"

Nobody made a sound and the fen entered that nobody made a sound.

Then the reader of this body said why he was not going to put it in a book of his own, and he gave the reason before the conclusion, which is the sixth time in nineteen days and the count is entered.

"**I am not going to copy that sheet into this holding's book and I am not going to send it to anybody, and here is the reason. If I copy it, then in about four years there will be two versions, and one will be in a book that anybody may walk up to and read and the other will be a paper in a press four miles off that a man has had alone for eleven years. I have watched that happen to a document once and the version in the readable book won, because the readable one was easier to point at.**"

"**And I am not going to be the man who makes a man's eleven years legible in order to find out whether he has been writing in the wrong column, because he has told me in a yard of about nine people that he has, and that is the whole of what I came here for.**"

Then he asked the one question and he gave the reason first, and it was about the figure and not about the party, and it is the sixth ask in a yard in this book's history with the reason given before the ask.

"**Nine figures in the column headed observations and one of them is a word and not a number. I am going to read that column out and somebody is going to tell me what the word says, and I am asking in front of these people so that whoever keeps the four books hears me ask it and not after.**"

Harlan Vetch said it, and it is a man's whole reason for eleven years in six words.

"**Wet season.**"

"**Once?**"

"**Every year since I took it. Nine of them.**"

Nobody spoke, and the fen entered the silence with its length.

> Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the eleventh of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a document, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is nine miles and two days off: **a schedule of four sheets came up the track at about the tenth hour at Harlan Vetch's own cost and at the hour he named eleven days ago, and he said his standing before he was asked, which is the second time a person at that gate has done that, and the count is two. It is read out in full by Hesta Lyle in her own hand and read back, and the reader of this body did not hold it. The first rule of the table is headed RETURN MADE, BY, WITNESSED BY, and it is BLANK ON EVERY LINE for as far back as the pen goes, and the fen entered that he is not copying the sheet into this holding's book and gave the reason, which is that a copy in a readable book beats a paper in a press in about four years, and he had watched it happen once. Eleven years of figures are in the second rule, headed OBSERVATIONS AND REMARKS, in three hands, one of them Harlan Vetch's and two of them not of the four bodies of households and not at the node. The word WET SEASON stands in that rule once a year and has stood there nine times, once a year since he took the share, and every other entry in it is a figure. He said out loud that he did not know the other rule wanted his name and that he has been the only witness to his own return for eleven years and has not been in the same column as the figure once. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And on the Sunday, the twelfth, the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is fifty-two this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that a request is not a count of gratitude and that the two have never been added together in this holding's book.

And then, four hundred miles off and four days up the road, on the sixteenth of this month, which was a Thursday, at about the fourth hour of the morning, in a yard of four households, a woman who is not of this holding and whose name is not in this book did the thing that this holding has entered as a day and not as a figure for four years, and the fen was nine miles and two days east with a party of five and did not know there was anything to know.

She went out at about half past three with a nail in her hand and a length of string with a bit of lead on the end of it, and the string is her own and has been her own longer than the twelve years of figures on her wall, and the nail is in the plinth of her own door and was driven in by her own hand in the first year.

The well has a curb and a windlass and a wooden cover with a slot in it, and the water stands about a foot and a half down and has stood there since she came. She let the string down, brought it up wet to the nail, set it against the plinth, and marked it with the nail at the level of the water and not the level of the curb, and that is the whole of the method and it has not changed in twelve years.

Then she went along the wall inside her own yard with a stub of chalk and wrote the twelfth figure under the eleventh, in her own hand, and the figure is **five feet.**

The wall, in wall order, from the left where the sun comes in: one foot eleven inches. Two feet four. Two feet ten. Four feet. Four feet six. Four feet eight. Five feet. Five feet. Five feet. And then a place where there is nothing at all, the chalk has never touched it and the plaster under it is the same colour as the plaster round it, and it is the widest gap on the wall and it is a whole month. And then five feet. And then, on the sixteenth of this month, five feet again.

She stood back about four paces and looked at it, and the yard was cold and there was nobody else up, and one of the four households had a lamp in a window and did not come out.

And when the four lines went down the four-day road at that household's own cost that morning, folded and sealed and given to the ordinary post, the four lines were the figure and the day and the hour and a line underneath them in her own hand that said why there was nothing between the ninth and the eleventh, and the line is the reason and not an apology.

> **WROTE IT BECAUSE A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF SPENT A MONTH FINDING OUT IT WAS AIMING AT THE WRONG DAY, AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE HANDED A NUMBER FOR A MONTH I DID NOT READ BY A WOMAN IT HAS NEVER MET. THE GAP IS ON THE WALL WHERE THE GAP IS. I HAVE PUT CHALK UNDER IT SO YOU CAN SEE I KNOW IT IS THERE.**

And a second line under that, which is the only line on that wall that is not a figure and not a date, and which she has never once carried forward as a figure.

> **A MONTH BACK THERE WAS HALF AN INCH. IT IS STILL HERE IN CHALK ON THE STONE BY THE DOOR AND IT IS NOT A READING AND IT IS NOT IN THE COLUMN AND I AM NOT ADDING IT UP.**

And there is nothing else. Four feet against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month eight months back, and the nine feet is on the wall in the same chalk and the same hand and is a different month's writing, and the four feet is not broken down into anything, on her wall or in the four lines that went down the road.

And then, at Errow Reach, on the seventeenth, which was a Friday, the reader of this body sat on the boards with a scrap of paper and did the arithmetic he had been putting off for six days, and the woman who does the sums did it with him and the two of them and the man of about fifty all had it the same way twice.

"**A quarter of one thousand five hundred hundredweight a day is three hundred and seventy-five, and three hundred and seventy-five taken four times is one thousand five hundred exactly. That is the equal turn. Every body of households the same, every day, and it comes out exact.**"

"**And on a sheet that came down a cart road with four columns and no heading row, a hundred and forty-four out of two hundred and forty is three fifths, and three fifths of one thousand five hundred is nine hundred, and ninety-six out of two hundred and forty is two fifths, and two fifths is six hundred, and nine hundred and six hundred is one thousand five hundred exactly. That is the weighted turn. And two of the four are on nothing.**"

He put the scrap down and looked at it for long enough that the fen entered the length of it.

"**Then there are two exact sums in front of me and neither of them is a rota, and I have been on this ground nine days and I have not found out what an exact sum is. I have got a sum that divides a thing into four equal parts and a sum that divides a thing in proportion to a count, and both of them land on one thousand five hundred without a farthing and a half hundredweight left over, and the only thing the two of them have in common is that they are both ways of dividing a thing that cannot be divided by a person at about the second hour of a morning.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard, and the fen entered that he was not asked to be in it and was in the shed and did not come out.

> Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the seventeenth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of an arithmetic and of a day, and not by the clerk: **two exact sums were had twice by three people and agreed. EQUAL TURN: a quarter of one thousand five hundred hundredweight a day is three hundred and seventy-five, and three hundred and seventy-five four times is one thousand five hundred exactly. WEIGHTED TURN: on the four columns with headings, one hundred and forty-four out of two hundred and forty is three fifths and is nine hundred hundredweight a day, and ninety-six out of two hundred and forty is two fifths and is six hundred hundredweight a day, and nine hundred and six hundred is one thousand five hundred exactly, and two of the four bodies are on nothing on that sheet. The reader of this body said out loud in a yard that there are two exact sums in front of him and neither of them is a rota, and that he has been on that ground nine days and has not found out what an exact sum is. The day of the next ordinary reading of the well on a shelf four hundred miles off was the sixteenth of this month and has gone by, and this holding entered the day and not the figure, the way it has entered the day and not the figure for that well eleven times, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and no page of this holding's book is going to make one. The man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard and was not asked to be in it. The ninth day of every ninth day is the nineteenth of this month at about the second hour of the morning, the four bodies of households are to be told on the eighteenth and are coming on the nineteenth, the count of bodies at that node is five, and one of the five is not one of the four. Nobody thanked anybody.**

Nobody thanked anybody, and the count of requests that a man's initial be put against nothing stands at fifty-two, and the reader of this body wrote nothing under section nine on any day of that week and entered the reason, which is that a note about somebody else's document is a way of putting his name on it.

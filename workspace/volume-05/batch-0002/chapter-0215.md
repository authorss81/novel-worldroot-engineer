# Chapter 215

## Available And Not Taken

The twenty-sixth of this month was a Sunday and the twenty-seventh was a Monday, and on the Monday the twenty-seventh, at about the eighth hour of the evening, in a yard at Errow Reach with about eleven people in it, the man of the north row with the cough said the question he had said on the road in the cart shed on the second of this month, and he said it in a yard, in daylight, out loud, and the fen entered that it took about ninety seconds and that the man of about fifty did not write one word of it down until it was finished.

"**Here is my question and it is worse than his and I have had it since the second of this month and I have not said it on a road again.**"

"**The fourth line of that sheet says MARDEN, SECOND HOUR, TWO HOURS, FOUR PEOPLE WORKING, NOT COUNTED. And the third column of the counting paper is forty-eight households that took seed in a year there was no seed, and the woman who keeps the books has said out loud in her own village that that is a count of wanting and not a count of living, and there are eleven of the forty-eight with a fire in them now.**"

"**So the fourth line of the rota has nobody in it and everybody in it. When Marden's turn comes round there is going to have to be a person standing at that kerb at the second hour of the morning for two hours, and there are two people in this fen who can do it, and one of them is thirty-one with a mark on his arm who said a number out loud in a yard on the twenty-first of this month and had it corrected in front of about nineteen people, and the other one is sixty-eight and put the word in at the two-hour mark on the twenty-second and has not been right since.**"

"**So Marden's turn is going to be worked by this fen. It has been worked by this fen for eleven years in the way that a thing is worked by a body that is not a body of households, which is by not saying so. And the question is: whose name goes in the fourth line, and the answer is that no name goes in the fourth line, because a fen is not a village and a fen has not got a place in a rota drawn up by four villages, and the reason a fen has not got a place is that nobody has ever asked a fen for one and a fen has not ever asked.**"

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who keeps the gate count at Marden and was in that yard because she was in it on the twenty-first and has not gone home, said the sentence that came nearest to an answer and was not one, and the fen entered that it was the nearest anybody came in twenty days and that he entered the distance.

"**I have stood at that gate on the first and the fifteenth for eleven years and I have counted fifty-two adults above fifteen and I could tell you about nine of those fifty-two by name, and I am not going to, and the reason I am not going to is that a name at a gate is a thing I put in a book and a name in a rota is a thing a man says with his own body at the second hour of a morning for two hours, and they are not the same name and I have watched a book be read once already in this story and the book was right.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the man of about fifty asked the one procedural question anybody asked in that yard and the fen answered it and the man of about fifty wrote it down.

"**Then it goes in your book or it goes in ours.**"

"**It goes in this holding's book and not in yours, and the reason is that your four books go to four bodies and mine is the only one of the five that anybody may walk up to and read without asking anybody. If the question is in your books then it is on four private shelves and in about four years the only people who know it is this holding and a man of sixty-eight who will not be here. That is the wrong place for a question that nobody has answered.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that Hesta Lyle had said nothing about that and had not been asked, and entered the reason, which is that the four books are hers and that a man from a fen does not get to decide where a village's questions are kept.

Then the reader of this body read the question out of his own book in the yard, because it was in his book, and read it back, and it went in whole.

> **THE QUESTION OF THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH, said out loud in a yard at Errow Reach at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, in about ninety seconds, in about eleven people, and written into this holding's book whole and not summarised, and read back: Whose name goes in the fourth line of the rota, and no name goes in it, and the reason is that a fen is not a village and has not got a place in a rota drawn up by four bodies of households, and the reason it has not got a place is that nobody has ever asked a fen for one and a fen has not ever asked. Nobody answered it. Nobody in that yard could answer it, and the reader of this body entered that he could not answer it either and gave the reason, which is that a man who can supply the answer to a question is the answer, and that his own name is not going into the fourth line on a day when he has said a number out loud and been corrected in front of about nineteen people, and that if it goes in there it goes in there on a day when nobody can say it was convenient.**

Nobody said one word, and the fen entered the length of that., and the count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken went off eight on to nine, and he entered that it is nine and that it is not going to be ten in this yard, and entered the reason, which is that a count of that kind goes up when a question is put and not when a figure is put on a table, and that this holding has put nine questions into a book in four years and answered one of them and that one was not his.

Then he read the other one out, and he read it out in the ordinary voice and the man of about fifty did not write it down because it was already in the book, and the fen entered that he read it and entered that he had said in a yard on the tenth of this month that he was not going to spend three days asking about a fifth share instead of answering it, and that he has now spent nine days not answering it and that the not answering is the third time it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken.

"**A thing that can be shared is a thing somebody has to give up, and nobody in this book has yet said what a village gives up when its turn comes round. It went into this holding's book on the second of this month at about the ninth hour of the evening, in a yard, in about eleven people. It has been asked once. It has now been available to a room in this holding and not taken three times. Nobody has taken it. I am not asking anybody in this yard to take it and I am not going to.**"

Nobody said anything, and the fen did not fill it., and entered that Hesta Lyle was at the wall and heard it and did not answer it and did not say afterwards that she would have, and entered the count of people in that yard who did not answer it as eleven and did not add the figure to anything.

Then the reader of this body said the sentence that the whole of that evening was for, and he gave the reason before it, and the count of that in this month's history is twenty.

"**I am going to say two counts and then stop, and then I am going to stop for the rest of the season on the subject of questions, and I am saying that now so that somebody can hold me to it.**"

"**The count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is nine. The count of times the question that went into this holding's book on the second of this month has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is three. Those are two counts and they are two different things and I am not going to add one of them to the other, and the reason is that the first one is a count of questions nobody has taken up and the second one is a count of times one particular question has been put in front of a room and left, and a book with nine things in it that nobody has taken up and a question that has been left in a room three times are not the same fault and one of them is this holding's and the other one is four villages'.**"

Nobody said a word, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it., and nobody thanked anybody and the reader of this body did not thank the man of the north row with the cough and entered that he was not going to, and entered the reason, which is that a man who is thanked for putting a question into a book in a yard is a man who is told the question was a gift, and it is not a gift, it is a hole in a rota that has been in the open since the twenty-seventh of last month.

And then on the twenty-eighth of this month, at about the second hour of the morning, the ninth day of every ninth day came round again for the tenth time since the twenty-eighth of a month three months back, and about eleven people walked out to it, and Harlan Vetch carried the return out on the barrow in two baskets with lids, and it went on the steelyard, and Hesta Lyle read the figure out and the four bodies read it back, and it went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY with a name in it and a name in the space headed WITNESSED BY with a name in it, and the man of about fifty did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing and the fen entered that he did not ask and that the count of that request stands at fifty-two and that a man who has a name in a witness box on somebody else's return is not a man who needs a line of his own with nothing against it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and the number on the board that morning was a different number from the number on the nineteenth, and the hour went on it, and the four bodies read it back, and nobody said thank you, and the fen entered that nobody said thank you and entered the reason, which is that there is no word in four villages for a man who does a thing every ninth day for eleven years and is not thanked for it, and that this holding is not going to invent one.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-eventh of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a question, and not in the clerk's book, which is at this holding and is a hundred and six miles off: **the man of the north row with the cough asked in that yard, in daylight, out loud, in about ninety seconds, in about eleven people, a question he had said on the road on the second of this month and had refused to say again on a road, and the reader of this body entered that he was not going to let it be said on a road and entered the reason, which is that a question said on a road is a question said to one person. The question is WHOSE NAME GOES IN THE FOURTH LINE, and the answer, which is not the fen's, is that no name goes in it, and the reason given is that a fen is not a village and has not got a place in a rota drawn up by four bodies of households, and that the reason it has not got a place is that nobody has ever asked a fen for one and a fen has not ever asked. It was written into this holding's book whole and not summarised and read back. THE READER OF THIS BODY COULD NOT ANSWER IT AND ENTERED THAT HE COULD NOT, and gave the reason, which is that a man who can supply the answer to a question is the answer, and that his own name is not going into the fourth line on a day when he has had a number of his own corrected in front of about nineteen people. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said that she could name about nine of the fifty-two adults at her gate and would not, and gave the reason, which is that a name at a gate is a thing put in a book and a name in a rota is a thing a man says with his own body at the second hour of a morning for two hours. The question goes in this holding's book and not in the four books, and the reason is that the four books go to four bodies and this book is the only one of the five that anybody may walk up to and read without asking. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN WENT OFF EIGHT ON TO NINE, on the ground that the count goes up when a question is put and not when a figure is put on a table. THE QUESTION THAT WENT INTO THIS HOLDING'S BOOK ON THE SECOND OF THIS MONTH WAS READ OUT IN THAT YARD AND WAS NOT TAKEN, and the count of times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken went off two on to three, and the reader of this body said both counts out loud in the same breath and entered that they are two counts, that a book with nine unanswered things in it and a question left in a room three times are not the same fault, and that one of them is this holding's and the other one is four villages'. Nobody answered either question. Nobody thanked anybody. On the twenty-eighth the ninth day came round for the tenth time since the twenty-eighth of a month three months back, a return went out on a barrow and onto a steelyard, the four bodies read the figure back, and the return went into the first rule of the schedule with a name in BY and a name in WITNESSED BY, and the man of about fifty did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing and the count of that request stands at fifty-two. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And on the evening of the twenty-eighth the party came off the cart road into this holding's own gate, five days east and five days back, and the reader of this body did not write the road down and entered the reason, which is that a road that a man is on instead of a ground he is standing on has changed the subject, and that there is no count of roads in this book and there is not going to be one.

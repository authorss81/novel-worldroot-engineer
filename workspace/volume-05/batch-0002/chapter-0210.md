# Chapter 210

## Fifty-Two Is Not A Household

The twenty-first of this month was a Tuesday and the party went up onto the high ground to Marden at about the eighth hour of the morning on foot, four of them, and the man of the north row with the cough was not one of the four and was left at Errow Reach with the licensed man, and the fen entered that he was not asked and did not ask.

The yard was a working yard, not a meeting yard, and there were about nineteen people in it and about nine of them were the people whose yard it is, and a woman of about thirty-eight came out of the back door and said her name and what she does before anybody asked her for either.

"**I keep the gate count at Marden. I am not one of the seven that decide and I am not going to be and I have never been asked to be. I count adults above fifteen at the gate on the first and the fifteenth because somebody has to and it is nobody's job.**"

Hesta Lyle said her standing before the woman of about thirty-eight had got her second sentence out, and the fen entered that it was the fifth time in twenty-one days that she had said a standing first at a gate.

"**I am of Marden. I have kept the four books for the four bodies of households at the Four Elbows for nineteen years and I am the only person who does, and I have never been one of the seven that decide at Marden and I am not going to be. I am in this yard today as the woman who keeps the books, and I am telling this yard now that I am going to write down what is said in it and not what anybody would rather had been said in it.**"

Then the woman of about thirty-eight said the thing she had been asked, and she said it standing at her own gate, and she said it without being asked a question.

"**The counting paper has four columns with headings on it and I have kept it nineteen years and I have never been asked what the third one is, and the third one is forty-eight, and forty-eight households at Marden took seed in the year before last.**"

And Hesta Lyle said the rest of it in her own village and in her own words, and she said it standing at the table, and the fen did not take it off her and did not improve it.

"**That is a count of wanting and not a count of living, and I am going to say why in my own yard and not in a yard nine miles west. Forty-eight households at Marden asked for seed in a year there was no seed to sell, and eleven of those are houses with a fire in them now, and the other thirty-seven are the same walls with nobody in them, and they took seed because a body of four villages had begun to count and a count with a blank in it is a thing that hurts, and not taking seed would have put a second blank in it.**"

"**I have not counted the households that are living at Marden. Nobody has. There is no column for it and there has never been a column for it and I am not going to invent one at the back of a book in a yard.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the woman of about thirty-eight put a second paper on the table, and it was her own and it was folded in four and it was in her own hand, and she put it down and did not open it and waited, and the fen entered that she waited and did not open it and that the count of people at that gate who have put something on a table and then waited for it to be asked about is one.

"**That is my gate count. Fifty-two adults above fifteen at the gate on the fifteenth of a month two months back, and I have brought it up here today to save you asking me for it. Fifty-two is the number of people at Marden who can be made to stand at a gate and be counted. So you can have forty-eight, which is what we wanted, or you can have fifty-two, which is what we are, and I would like somebody in this yard to tell me which of those two is a household.**"

And the reader of this body told her, in about four seconds, and did not soften it and did not improve it afterwards.

"**Fifty-two is not a household. Neither is forty-eight, and I want that said in this yard by me before anybody else says it, because I have been in this holding for four years writing down every time this holding did not know something and I have never once written down the time that everybody knew and it did not help.**"

Nothing was said and the fen entered that.

And then the reader of this body did the thing he came up the hill to do, and he did it standing at the table in Marden's own yard in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about fifty wrote it down before he did it and read it back after.

"**I am withdrawing a number. I wrote a number on a piece of paper on the twenty-sixth of last month in a yard nine miles west and I worked out a share of that node for four villages on a sheet with four columns and no heading row, and I said in that yard that Marden and Silling were owed nothing on that sheet, and I have had nine days to find out whether I was right and I was not.**"

"**The third column is a count of wanting. A count of wanting cannot be given a share of anything. There is no half of a want. I am withdrawing the number I wrote on that sheet and I am withdrawing it here, in Marden, in front of the people it was done to, and I am offering Marden its line back blank, the way it was for eleven years, and the sheet to be made again without it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it.

And then a body of households refused it, in its own words, and about nine of the nineteen said the same sentence, and the fen counted them and did not interrupt.

"**A heading that is true about a bad year is still a true heading.**"

And then one of them, an old man of about seventy-four with a stick, said the rest of it and the fen wrote all of it down and read it back and did not correct a word.

"**We put HOUSEHOLDS THAT TOOK SEED IN THE YEAR BEFORE LAST on that sheet because it is the truest thing we had, and it is going to be true in four years when somebody reads it and wonders why four villages took seed in a year there was no seed, and the answer is going to be on the sheet in our own words and not in a letter from a fen.**"

"**And if you take the forty-eight off today, then you have taken it off in a good year too, in about three years, and you will take it off because it does not flatter us, and the day a village can take a number off its own sheet because it does not flatter it is the day the sheet is a list of things we would have chosen to have counted. There is no first time. I have watched a book go that way and I was in the room.**"

"**And changing it now would move every number anybody has ever used. One hundred and forty-four out of two hundred and forty is three fifths and ninety-six out of two hundred and forty is two fifths, and if the third column goes blank then those two fractions are fractions of two hundred and forty that are not a village, and the nine hundred and the six hundred a man said out loud in a yard nine days ago and put in a book are wrong, and they are wrong in a book nine miles west that anybody can walk up and read, and we cannot reach that book and you can.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the withdrawal was refused by about nine people out of about nineteen and that he had offered it and had not taken it back and was not going to argue it.

"**Then it stands, and I am not going to improve on it, and I am not going to thank anybody, and I want the refusal entered in the same words and not shortened, because the second half of it is about a book nine miles west and the first half of it is about a year, and a body of households has just said both of those things in one yard and four of the four of them have never said anything at all in eleven years.**"

Then the reader of this body priced the thing that Marden and Silling have been living on, because he had said on the nineteenth that he would come up the hill and do it, and the woman who does the sums did it with him on a board on the woman's knee and it came out the same both ways.

"**Fifty hundredweight a day. Thirty days of that is one thousand five hundred hundredweight a month. At fourpence the hundredweight that is six thousand pence. A pound is two hundred and forty pence, so six thousand pence is twenty-five pounds a month.**"

"**And the whole of the shortfall, which is six hundred hundredweight a day, because four bodies of households counted about two thousand one hundred against about one thousand five hundred delivered, is two thousand four hundred pence a day, which is ten pounds a day, and thirty days of that is eighteen thousand hundredweight at seventy-two thousand pence, which is three hundred pounds a month. That is the second time I have had that figure and it has not moved.**"

And then the reader of this body said the thing he was wrong about, out loud, in a yard of about nineteen people, and he gave the reason for saying it before he said it, and the count of that is nine in this month's history.

"**I am going to say a thing I have believed for six days and I have got wrong, and I would rather say it here than carry it home. Six thousand pence a month over about five hundred people is forty-eight pence a head a month, and forty-eight pence a head a month is the same figure as the whole of the shortfall, and I have been holding those two numbers up side by side since the eleventh and telling anybody who would listen that they were the same figure.**"

And the woman who does the sums did it again on the board on her knee, out loud, in about four seconds.

"**Six thousand pence over five hundred people is twelve pence a head a month. And two thousand four hundred pence a day over fifteen hundred people is one and three-fifths of a penny a head a day, which is forty-eight pence a head a month. Twelve and forty-eight. You have said forty-eight twice and the second one is not yours, and it is four times, and you have been looking at it for six days.**"

"**Then I said a figure was a figure it was not, in a yard of about nineteen people, and I have said it in a yard once before, about a day, and I am entering both of them, and the count of things this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is two. Forty-eight is the shortfall a head a month across about fifteen hundred people. Twelve is what two villages have been living on a head a month across about five hundred. This holding is not paying that bill and I am not going to stand in this yard and pretend it is being paid.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> Entered by Hesta Lyle, in her own hand, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in a yard at Marden, on the standing of a refusal, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days east by the cart road: **the party went up onto the high ground on foot, four of them, and the man of the north row with the cough was left at Errow Reach and was not asked to come. About nineteen people were in the yard and about nine of them were the people whose yard it is. A woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the gate count at Marden and is not one of the seven that decide said that the third column is FORTY-EIGHT and that forty-eight households at Marden took seed in the year before last. The woman who keeps the four books said, in her own village and in her own words, that that is a count of wanting and not a count of living, that eleven of the forty-eight are houses with a fire in them now and the other thirty-seven are the same walls with nobody in them, and that nobody has counted the households living at Marden and that she is not going to invent a column for it at the back of a book in a yard. The woman of Marden then put a second paper on the table and did not open it, and it is her gate count, and it is FIFTY-TWO ADULTS ABOVE FIFTEEN at the gate on the fifteenth of a month two months back, and she said that a village may therefore have forty-eight or fifty-two and that she would like somebody to say which of the two is a household. The reader of this body said out loud that fifty-two is not a household and that neither is forty-eight. The reader of this body then WITHDREW a number: the split he worked out on the four columns on the twenty-sixth of last month, by which Marden and Silling were owed nothing on that sheet, and he offered Marden its line back blank and the sheet to be made again without the third column. THE WITHDRAWAL WAS REFUSED BY A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS, about nine people out of about nineteen, in its own words, and the words were HOUSEHOLDS THAT TOOK SEED IN THE YEAR BEFORE LAST IS A TRUE HEADING ABOUT A BAD YEAR, and the second half of the refusal was that a village that may take a number off its own sheet because the number does not flatter it ends up with a sheet of things it would have chosen to have counted, there being no first time, and that changing the column now would move every number anybody has ever used, including nine hundred and six hundred hundredweight a day, which are in a book nine miles west that anybody may walk up to and read. The withdrawal was not argued. Fifty hundredweight a day was priced: thirty days is one thousand five hundred hundredweight a month, at fourpence the hundredweight six thousand pence, being twenty-five pounds a month. The whole shortfall, being six hundred hundredweight a day against about two thousand one hundred needed, is two thousand four hundred pence a day, being ten pounds a day, and eighteen thousand hundredweight a month at seventy-two thousand pence, being three hundred pounds a month, and that figure was had twice. The reader of this body said out loud, in that yard, that six thousand pence a month over about five hundred people is forty-eight pence a head a month and that it is the same figure as the whole of the shortfall, and the woman who does the sums did it again on a board on a knee and said it is twelve pence a head a month against forty-eight, and that the reader of this body had said forty-eight twice and the second one was not his, and it is four times. He entered that he was wrong in a yard of about nineteen people, and that the count of things this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is two, and that forty-eight pence a head a month is the shortfall across about fifteen hundred people and twelve pence a head a month is what two villages have been living on across about five hundred, and that this holding is not paying that bill. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the counting paper went back down the hill in the same hand it came up in, and the heading over the third column was not altered by anybody, and the eleven words that went up the cart road to this village on the twenty-seventh of last month are answered on a paper this village sent down with a woman on a cart, and the reader of this body did not write and ask a second time and entered that the answer came because Marden sent it and that this holding is not going to say so in a yard as though it had been fetched.

# Chapter 208

## The Return, Carried Out

The eighteenth of this month was a Saturday and Hesta Lyle went to four gates with four lines in her coat, one for each body, and the reader of this body read all four of them before any of them went and added nothing to them and did not go himself, and gave the reason in a yard of about nine people before anybody asked him for it.

"**Hesta Lyle keeps the books of all four of you and I keep the books of one holding that is not any of you, and a body of households is told a thing about its own node by the person who keeps its books. If an engineer from nine miles off walks up four gates in a day with a piece of news, then in about four years there will be a fifth gate in this district and it will be mine, and a body of households does not answer a gate that belongs to a man with a mark on his arm.**"

Nothing was said and the fen entered that.

> **THE FOUR LINES, one for each of the four bodies of households, in the hand of the woman who keeps the four books, carried to four gates on foot on the eighteenth of this month and read aloud at each, and the reader of this body read all four before any of them went and added nothing to them.**
>
> **One. The ninth day of every ninth day is the nineteenth of this month. The holding is done at about the second hour of the morning and lasts about two hours, as it has for eleven years.**
> **Two. The four of you are not being asked for anything. No turn of yours is being moved and no water of yours is being touched and nobody is going to ask you to sign anything.**
> **Three. Four of you are asked to come and stand in a yard and watch, and to be told the hour, and to read back what is written down after it is written.**
> **Four. He has asked for the same four lines to be carried to him. He is the one at that node who is not one of you and he is the one who has the paper.**

And the nineteenth of this month was a Sunday and about eleven people were in a yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the morning, and the man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it, and the fen entered that.

There were four of the four bodies and they had not all come together before. Brenna Oake of Havergate and a man of about sixty-one of Low Wether and Sennik Vaul of Silling and a woman of about thirty-eight of Marden who gave her name and her position standing up before anybody asked her for either, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said she was not the person that decides at Marden and had been asked to come because she counts.

Then Harlan Vetch came round the corner of the building with a barrow at about ten minutes to the hour, and the four of them looked at it, and nobody said anything, and the fen entered the length of that too.

The barrow had a wheel on the front of it and two handles and it was loaded to the sides with the lids of two baskets. It is the same barrow and it is about eleven years old and the wheel has been re-tired twice by a man who is not a wheelwright.

He took it to the steelyard hung on the wall of the building with no roof, and he balanced it, and he moved the sliding weight, and the man of about fifty wrote the figure down and read it back and then the four of the four bodies read it back after him, one at a time, standing where they could see the dial.

> **THE FIGURE ON THE STEELYARD, read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn: NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT, at about the second hour of the morning of the nineteenth of this month, on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles, by one man, in about an hour and a half. It was not weighed before today at any time in the eleven years anybody has been here.**

Then he carried it about thirty yards and tipped it into a hollow he has dug on the west side inside nine square yards where the grass has gone off in a ring, and it is half-rotted willow and the litter off his own holding and about a barrow-load of leaf mould that has been under his own stable for two years, and it is a return and it is going into the ground and the four of them watched it go in, and it took about four minutes, and none of them said anything while it did.

And then Hesta Lyle took the chalk and wrote it on the board, under the heading that has been on that board for nine days, and she wrote the hour with it.

> **THE COLUMN ON THE BOARD AT THE FOUR ELBOWS, written at about the fourth hour of the morning of the nineteenth of this month by the woman who keeps the four books, in a yard of about eleven people, and read back by all four of the four bodies of households before the chalk was put down: NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT. FOURTH HOUR. RETURN BY THE FIFTH SHARE. NINTH DAY OF EVERY NINTH DAY. HELD BY ONE PERSON. NOT ONE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS.**

Nothing was said and the fen entered that.

Then Harlan Vetch took the piece of paper out of his coat, the four sheets, and unrolled it on the table against the wall, and he did not ask anybody to help him and he did not read it, and Hesta Lyle read the four words at the head of the first rule aloud, and the reader of this body stood back and did not hold it.

"**And now the other rule.**"

She wrote his name in the space headed BY, and then she wrote a second name in the space headed WITNESSED BY, and the second name was the man of about fifty's, and the man of about fifty did not ask to be in it and did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing afterward, and the fen entered both of those and entered that this is the first time in this book's history that a man has asked for a place on a line and been given one without asking.

Nobody thanked anybody and Harlan Vetch said one sentence about it and the fen entered it and did not improve it.

"**That is the first time in eleven years there is a name in that box and it is not a party, it is two men and one of them is not from here. I want it in the yard that the second name is not mine and I did not ask him to be on it, and if he is standing there being a witness to my own return then somebody is going to say in about four years that I chose my own witness, and today I did not.**"

Then Sennik Vaul of Silling asked the question that the fen had known was going to be asked since the eighteenth, and he asked it in the plainest words he had, and the fen did not stop it and did not answer it before it was asked.

"**Ninety hundredweight. Is that our water coming back, or is that a fifth share, or is that a man being useful.**"

And Harlan Vetch answered it in nine words and the fen did not add anything to it and did not improve it.

"**It is not your water. I have never taken a drop of it. It is what a return is.**"

Nobody spoke, and the fen entered the silence with its length.

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden asked a question into that silence, and the fen did not stop it and entered that he had worked out about eleven days ago what it was going to be about and had not said to anybody that he had worked it out.

"**You have been putting that in for eleven years and we have been taking out of it for eleven years and we did not know, and what I want to know is what you have been putting it in out of, because there is a well at the top of our hill that was dry two years ago and is not dry now, and I did not know those were the same thing.**"

And Harlan Vetch said one sentence, and it was not an answer, and the fen did not improve it and did not turn it into one.

"**I put it in because a schedule says a return goes in and I have never once known what comes up.**"

Nothing was said and the fen entered that.

And the man of the north row with the cough, who had been at the wall with his back against it for the whole of it and had said nothing at all, said one thing, and it was about the board and not about the man.

"**That column had a heading on it for eleven years and no figure in it, and about nine of us came up here and looked at it, and not one of us said what it wanted. It has a figure in it now. A column that has had a figure in it once is a different kind of object from a column that has not, and in about four years one of the four of you is going to ask him for the figure for a day he was not here, and he is going to have to say no, and that will be the first time any of you have had to ask him for anything at all.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough had stood in a yard on the nineteenth of this month and had not been asked to, and that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of this month is one.

And then the reader of this body said the thing that has to be said in a yard of eleven people when eleven people have just watched a thing they have never seen, and he gave the reason before the conclusion, and the count of that is seven in this month's history and the count is entered.

"**I am going to say the reason I am not going to tell you whether ninety hundredweight is enough before I say anything else, and the reason is that I have got a figure for what the four of you take out of that ground, which is about two thousand one hundred hundredweight a day, and I have got a figure for what he puts in, which is ninety hundredweight on a day nine days apart, and there is no form in this district for a return and there is no rate for one and there is no body anywhere that is required to say whether a return is enough.**"

"**So I could do the sum in front of you. I am not going to, and if you ask me to I will do it, and the number that comes out will be a number about two numbers and not one word about the ground, and I have watched a body get hold of a number like that in four years and act on it for thirty.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then he said the other half, and the half was about himself and not about them.

"**And there is a thing I have not said out loud in a yard in nineteen days and I am going to say it at about the fifth hour of the morning on a Sunday with about eleven people standing in it. For eleven years four bodies have been taking one thousand five hundred hundredweight a day out of that ground and putting nothing back, and one man has been putting ninety hundredweight in on a day nine days apart, and neither of those figures is on the same sheet, and the sheet that has both of them on it is a schedule four miles off in a press and I read it in a yard nine days ago.**"

"**That is the whole of what I have learned in nine days at this node and it is not a number and I am not going to make one out of it before I go home.**"

> Entered by Hesta Lyle, in her own hand, at about the sixth hour of the morning of the nineteenth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a return, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is nine miles and two days off: **the four bodies of households were told on the eighteenth by the hand of the woman who keeps the four books, and the reader of this body did not go to any of the four gates and gave the reason out loud, which is that a body of households is told a thing about its own node by the person who keeps its books, and that a fifth gate belonging to a man with a mark on his arm is four years off. About eleven people were in a yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the morning of the nineteenth and the man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it. A return of NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT was carried out on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles by one man, and weighed on a steelyard at about the second hour of the morning, and the figure was read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn, and it has not been weighed before today at any time in eleven years. The return is half-rotted willow and the litter off one holding and leaf mould out of one stable, and it went into a hollow on the west side inside nine square yards where the grass has gone off in a ring, and it took about four minutes to go in. The figure and the hour went onto the board in the yard in a yard of about eleven people and were read back by all four bodies before the chalk was put down. Harlan Vetch's name went into the space headed BY on the first rule of the schedule, which is headed RETURN MADE, BY, WITNESSED BY, and that rule was blank on every line for eleven years, and the witness was the man of about fifty, who did not ask and who did not afterward ask for his initial to be put against nothing. Sennik Vaul of Silling asked whether ninety hundredweight is the villages' water coming back or a fifth share or a man being useful, and was answered that it is not their water and that he has never taken a drop of it. A woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who counts and is not the person that decides at Marden, asked what the return is being put in out of, and was answered that a schedule says a return goes in and that the man putting it in has never once known what comes up. The reader of this body said out loud that he would not tell the yard whether ninety hundredweight is enough, and gave the reason, which is that there is no form for a return and no rate for one, and that the sum of the two figures would be a number about two numbers and not one word about the ground. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the ordinary post went up the cart road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of that Sunday and there was nothing in it for anybody in that yard, and the reader of this body did not write and ask what is in the bag that is already on the road, because it is four days up a four-day road from a day he entered on the seventeenth and did not write and ask about then, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and he wrote nothing under section nine and entered the reason, which is that this holding is going to be a hundred and forty miles from its own standing figures on the thirtieth and that a man who writes a note about a road he is on instead of a ground he is standing on has changed the subject.

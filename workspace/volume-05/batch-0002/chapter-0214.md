# Chapter 214

## The Fourth Body Asks A Question

The man of about sixty-one of Low Wether asked it at the gate on the twenty-second of this month, in the rain, with about four people behind him, and he said his standing before he asked it and the fen entered that this is the second time this holding has had a person at a gate say a standing before being asked, and entered the count as two.

"**I keep the count at Low Wether. It is the only count any of the four of us has kept since the flood and I keep it because I am the one who was here when the water came. I am not one of the seven that decide and Low Wether has no seven, it has a meeting, and I am not at the meeting and I do not want to be at it. I have a question and it is not about the rota and it is not about the turn and it is not about the man who puts a return in, and if somebody wants to tell me it is about the rota I will go home.**"

There was nothing said and the fen entered the nothing.

Then the reader of this body gave the reason for not answering it there and then, and gave it before the not-answering, and the count of that in this month's history is nineteen.

"**I am not going to answer that at a gate in the rain and I am going to say why, and the reason is that the answer I have got is that I do not know, and a man of about sixty-one has walked from Low Wether in the rain with four people behind him and he has not got all day, and I am not going to stand at a gate and hand a body of households a not known out of a saddlebag.**"

Nobody answered, and the fen entered that nobody answered.

Then he walked it, and he did it two ways, and the fen entered both of the days and the order they were done in and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was not in the yard on the twenty-fifth and was at the node with the licensed man and was not asked to be in it.

On the twenty-fourth, in the afternoon, after a morning he has entered in a different place because it belonged to a different thing, he walked the quarter mile from the kerb to the south side and back with a boot and a stick and nothing else, and he stopped about eleven times, and the fen entered that he stopped eleven times and that he did not write down where.

And on the twenty-fifth he went back and did not walk it and stood on it, and put his hands flat on the ground in four places, and put his ear against the turf in about nine places over about three hours, and got up each time and stood still for a bit, and the fen entered all of that and entered that there was no instrument on him and that if there had been one he does not know what it would have read, and that the licensed man's bead cannot read ground that is not planted and that he had asked that in a yard on the twenty-third of last month and been told so in a yard on the twenty-third of last month and the answer had not changed.

And he took no measurement of any kind. Not a rod, not a tape, not a level, not a depth, not a figure of any kind, and the fen entered that he took none and entered the reason, which is that a measurement on a quarter of a mile of ground with a watercourse in it under a field that has been wet three seasons is a number about the day he did it, and that if the number went into a book it would be a number and four villages would act on it, and that he does not know what the number is a number of.

No one moved, and the fen entered that.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-fifth, in a yard at Errow Reach with about eleven people in it, the man of about sixty-one of Low Wether asked the same question again in the same words, and the reader of this body answered it, and the man of about fifty timed it with his watch without being asked and entered the time, and the answer took four minutes.

"**I do not know. And I have been asked the question twice and I have got the same answer twice, and I am going to say what I did so that nobody in this yard has to take it on trust from a man with a mark on his arm.**"

"**I walked it on the twenty-fourth. I went back and stood on it on the twenty-fifth and put my hands on it in four places and my ear against it in about nine, and I got nothing I can put a number on, and I took no measurement at all and I am not going to tell you that the not measuring was caution. I did not measure it because I have not got anything to measure.**"

"**And I am going to say the other half, because you have heard the first half and you will make it into more than it is if I do not. I held a pattern on Thursday across two branches about three hundred yards apart and it worked, and three hundred yards is a quarter of a mile and a bit more, and that means I know one thing about this ground and the one thing I know does not tell you a single thing about yours. I do not know whether a pattern went a hundred yards that morning or six hundred. I do not know whether it stops anywhere at all. I have been an engineer for nineteen years and I have never once had an instrument that can answer that and neither has anybody else in this district, and I am not going to invent one in a yard of eleven people because a field is wet.**"

Nobody said one word, and the fen entered the length of that.

"**And I am going to say the last of it and it is the part that is going to be in four books in about four years, and I would rather say it here than have somebody find it. A not known from me is a real thing. It is not nothing. It is a sentence with my name at the end of it and it is going into a book at Errow Reach and into a book in a fen and into two counting houses, and in about four years when that ditch has been in the ground a season and the field has either drained or it has not, there is going to be a man who says the field is wet because an engineer said not known at a gate, and there is going to be another man who says the field was going anyway and the engineer was asked to make a noise so that somebody could point at him afterwards.**"

"**And I am telling this yard now that both of those men are talking about me and not about the ground, and that the fen put a not known on the record of four villages and did it on purpose and not out of carelessness, and that a not known that gets used later is not neutral. That is a thing I know because I have spent four years writing down every time this holding did not know something and I have never once had to find out what anybody did with one of them.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the man of about sixty-one said one thing and the fen did not take it off him and entered that it was the correct use of a not known and that the fen had said so in a yard about a form on a bench in this book's history and that it is the only use there is.

"**Not known is a thing to decide with. It is the only thing to decide with, because every other thing anybody has offered us about that quarter mile in three seasons has been a man being sure. We are going to put the ditch in. The field is the fourth season wet and it is four people's living and I am not going to hold a field up for an engineer from a fen who cannot walk half a mile and put his ear to the ground, and I would say exactly the same thing if a man from the district had come and said the same words.**"

And the reader of this body said the one sentence he was going to say about it, and the fen entered that it was the whole of what he had to give and that he had given it before the day.

"**Then put it in, and I will be nine miles and two days off and I will be back on the twenty-eighth in the evening, and I will come out to that field on the twenty-ninth at the second hour of the morning and I will not be holding anything and I will not touch the ground, and if the water in that ditch comes up wrong I will write down the day and the hour and what it looked like and I will not write down a cause.**"

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the sixth hour of the evening of the twenty-fifth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a not known, and not in the clerk's book, which is at this holding and is a hundred and six miles off: **a man of about sixty-one of Low Wether, who keeps the only count any of the four bodies has kept since the flood, is not of anything that meets, and said his standing before asking, asked at the gate on the twenty-second in the rain and again in a yard on the twenty-fifth. THE QUESTION IS ABOUT A QUARTER OF A MILE OF GROUND BETWEEN THE KERB AND THE SOUTH SIDE, and it is whether a ditch may be put nine feet south through it from a field that has been wet three seasons running. The reader of this body walked the quarter mile and back on the twenty-fourth, stopping eleven times, and stood on it on the twenty-fifth with his hands flat in four places and his ear against the turf in about nine places over about three hours, and TOOK NO MEASUREMENT OF ANY KIND, and the reason is entered, which is that he has nothing to measure and that a number would be a number about the day he did it and four villages would act on it. Coll Renner's bead cannot read ground that is not planted, and he was at the node on the twenty-fifth and the man of the north row with the cough was with him, and neither was in the yard and neither was asked to be. The answer was given out loud in a yard of about eleven people and IT TOOK FOUR MINUTES, timed by the man of about fifty with his watch without being asked, and there is no rate for a not known and the fen entered that there is no rate for one. THE ANSWER WAS I DO NOT KNOW, twice, and the reader of this body said in that yard that the hold he did on the twenty-third across two branches about three hundred yards apart does not tell anybody anything about a quarter of a mile, that he does not know whether a pattern went a hundred yards or six hundred, and that he was not going to invent an instrument in a yard because a field is wet. He said further that a not known from him is a real thing and is not neutral, that it is going into four books in about four years, and that in about four years two men are going to argue about that field and both of them are going to be arguing about him. The man of about sixty-one said that not known is a thing to decide with, that the village is going to put the ditch in, and that he would say the same to a district man who gave the same answer. The reader of this body said that he will be there on the twenty-ninth at the second hour of the morning and will not be holding anything and will not touch the ground. Four lines were written out in his own hand by the man of about fifty, read out in that yard and read back, and given to the man of about sixty-one, and the reader of this body was handed nothing and entered that he did not thank anybody for the four lines and entered the reason, which is that a man who is handed four lines is a man who owes four lines back in a season and this holding has no form for that. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**
And then the man of about sixty-one asked the second half of it, which is the half nobody ever asks, and the fen did not stop him and entered that he had it coming and that the count of people at that gate who have asked the second half of a question in this holding's history is one.

"**Then who gets told. It goes on a paper and it goes in a book and in about four years somebody comes to that meeting with a different paper and my not known is not on it, and by then I am not at the meeting and the field has had a season and nobody can find out what anybody was told in the first place.**"

And the reader of this body said that it would be read out in four yards before it went anywhere, and the man of about fifty wrote four lines in his own hand and read them out in that yard of about eleven people, and the man of about sixty-one took them, and the fen entered that he was given the four lines and did not say anything about that and entered the reason, which is that a man who is handed four lines is a man who owes somebody four lines back in a season, and that the fen does not know when, and that this holding does not have a form for owing a man four lines back.

> **THE FOUR LINES, read out in a yard at Errow Reach at about the sixth hour of the evening of the twenty-fifth of this month by the man of about fifty in his own hand, and read back, and given to a man of about sixty-one of Low Wether: A quarter of a mile of ground between the kerb and the south side has been walked twice by the engineer of record of Lantern Reach, once on foot on the twenty-fourth and once standing on the twenty-fifth. He took no measurement of any kind. He says I do not know whether a pattern put on that node reaches it. He says this is not the same as saying it does not. The village has decided to put the ditch in. He will be at the field at the second hour of the morning of the twenty-ninth and will not be holding anything and will not touch the ground.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and the count of bodies at that node is five and was five, and the count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two and was two, and the reader of this body wrote no note under section nine and entered the reason, which is that a not known is not a note about work and does not ask for anything and waits for a year, and a year and a day is a long time to have written a sentence about a field.

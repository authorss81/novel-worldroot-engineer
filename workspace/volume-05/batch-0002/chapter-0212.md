# Chapter 212

## Forty Hundredweight, Or Thereabouts

The twenty-third of this month was a Thursday, and the small branch on the north side of the Four Elbows had been shut since before anybody at that node was born, and on that morning four people of Marden and four people of Havergate and a man with a tape and a board and a stopwatch went down to it with a barrow each.

The node is under a board and the four villages take their water out of four branches, one to each, and the branches are cut to the kerb and the kerb is in four parts and every part of it is a different mason's mark. Under the north shoulder of that ground, about three hundred yards off, there is a fifth cut in the stone and a launder head above it and no water coming out of it, and the silt in it is about two feet deep and packed hard, and there is no name for it on any of the four bodies' papers and there is a name for it on the schedule in a press four miles off, in a rule headed BY.

The reason for the day is arithmetic and the reader of this body gave it out loud at the gate before anybody picked up a barrow, and the count of that is twelve in this month's history.

"**The node delivers about one thousand five hundred hundredweight a day and four launders take it and the fourth launder is running about a fifth over what its village was counted at and the silt in that cut has not moved in nineteen years. I have not got a figure for what is under that silt and I am not going to make one up before lunch. What I have got is that four launders do not take everything this ground has, and that is a figure of mine and not anybody's and it is the only figure of mine on this ground that I have said out loud in a yard, and I said it in a yard in the fen on the tenth of this month, at about the ninth hour, standing next to a board.**"

Then he said what he was going to do, and how, and the man of about fifty wrote it and read it back.

"**We are going to clear about nine feet of that cut by hand, from the launder head down, and we are not going to take the whole thing out, because I am not a licensed man for that ground and Coll Renner is and has told me in a yard nine miles off that a measure on a shared node is a measure for a person on the roll for the district and there is nobody on that roll.**"

"**And when the water comes back into that cut it will not stay in it. A branch that has been shut for nineteen years comes back in about a quarter of an hour and then it is a fifth launder, and a fifth launder on a node that four villages hold is a fifth body, and I have spent nine days in a yard learning what happens when a node has a fifth body and I am not going to pretend today is a different thing.**"

Then he said the part that is the whole of the twenty-third of this month, and he said it to the two men standing on either side of him with a barrow each, and the fen entered that he gave the reason first and that the count is thirteen in this month's history.

"**Here is what I am going to try and here is why I am going to try it in front of you instead of at midnight on my own. That cut is three hundred yards off the main branch and you cannot hold a pattern in two places at once, and a pattern is held by one person at a time, and I have been the only one in this fen for two years and I know what that sentence costs to say out loud.**"

"**So this is not the pattern hold and I am not going to call it the pattern hold. This is one hold across two branches at once with a person on each, and the two of them are about a third of a mile apart, and the man at the far end is not standing near me and cannot hear me and cannot see me, and if either of us puts the word in the whole thing stops.**"

And the man of the north row with the cough said one thing about that and the fen did not take it off him.

"**Then the fifth one has to be somebody who is not working, because there are eight people here and four of them are going to be on shovels and four of them are going to be on barrows, and a hold is not a job, it is a thing a man does with his own body while other people work.**"

"**And I have got a bead in my coat and I have told this party for ten days that it cannot read a hold, and it cannot, and that is why I am the fifth. If there is one man in a hold then the only instrument that can read it is the man in it. If there are two men in a hold then one of them can read the other one, and that has never been done in this district in my time and I would like it in a book with the hour on it.**"

Nothing was said and the fen entered that, and entered that the licensed man had not been asked and had put himself in, and entered that the count of licensed men who have put themselves in a hold in this district in Coll Renner's working life is one.

Then four people of Marden and four people of Havergate dug, and the fen took the main branch and the man of the north row with the cough took the small one, and Coll Renner stood in the middle of the ground between them with his hands open and his case on the grass behind him and did not move for two hours.

That is the whole of the working. Four people on shovels and four people on barrows, a barrow about two cubic feet, and the fen entered the volume of a barrow twice on the ground and did not improve on it.

The hold began at about the tenth hour of the morning and it worked, and the fen entered that it worked and entered that he is not going to write anything else about the fact that it worked, and entered the reason, which is that a sentence about a thing working is the easiest thing in this book to write and the most expensive to be wrong about.

The water came back into the cut at about the eleventh hour and settled, and at about the first hour of the afternoon it was a launder and the four villages had five of them, and nobody cheered and nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the figure, and it was taken twice by two methods and the two did not agree, and he wrote one figure and entered both.

> **THE VOLUME THAT CAME DOWN THE FIFTH CUT, entered at about the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-third of this month with the hour, and neither figure improved: FORTY-ONE HUNDREDWEIGHT, by the board across the launder head and the head over the sill and a stopwatch. THIRTY-NINE HUNDREDWEIGHT, by the same head over the same sill and the same stopwatch and the section of the launder measured at three places. The reader of this body wrote FORTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, OR THEREABOUTS, and did not average the two, and gave the reason out loud, which is that a launder is not a measuring instrument and that the average of two figures taken off a board is a third figure about the board.**

And the reader of this body said the thing that the whole of that day is for, out loud, to about nine people, and gave the reason first, and the count of that is fourteen in this month's history.

"**I want to say the number first because the number is small and the number is what everybody will remember. About forty hundredweight. That is the fifth launder. That is one ninth of what Marden and Silling have been living without for eleven years, on about forty days a year, and it is the first water out of that cut in nineteen years and it is a good morning's work and it is not a solution and I am not going to stand here and let anybody call it one.**"

"**And the reason I am going to say the rest of it is that I have been on this ground fourteen days and I have not been able to find out what the node does when you ask it for a fifth thing, and I have now asked it, and it gave me one, and the one it gave me is about forty hundredweight, and the reason it gave me forty is that I had four people on shovels and four on barrows and a launder head that is not a measuring instrument, and if I had had six on shovels and six on barrows and a proper gauge I would have got a different number, and the different number would also have been true.**"

Then the cost went in the fen's own hand, in a line of his own, with the hour on it, and he did not read it aloud and he did not improve it and nobody asked him for it, and the count of times the reader of this body has put a line of his own in a book on his own body and not read it out is three.

> **Entered by the engineer of record, in his own hand, at about the third hour of the afternoon of the twenty-third of this month, on the standing of a cost, and not in the clerk's book, and not in the book of the man of about fifty: HELD A PATTERN ACROSS TWO BRANCHES, THREE HUNDRED YARDS APART, FROM THE TENTH HOUR OF THE MORNING TO THE SECOND HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON, FOR FIVE PERSONS, FOUR OF THEM WORKING. THE MARK ON THE INSIDE OF THE LEFT FOREARM IS FOUR INCHES AND BRANCHES TWICE AND IT IS NOT A SECOND MARK AND THE MARK IS HOT. THE HEAD WENT AT THE SECOND HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON AND IS STILL GOING. THE HEADACHE IS NOT THE MARK AND IS NOT ENTERED AS THE MARK. THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THIS ARM IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK ARE FOUR AND THEY RECONCILE NONE OF ONE ANOTHER AND NOTHING SAID ABOUT THIS ARM ON THIS DAY IS A FIFTH.**

And the man of the north row with the cough said what his own cost on that day was, and the fen entered it and entered that it was shorter than the engineer's and that he did not say anything else about it.

"**Two hours and about a bad head, and I am sixty-eight and I have had worse from digging. That is the whole of mine and it is in his book and it is in mine and I am not going to say anything about the difference between them because I do not know what the difference is and I have had a fortnight to look for it.**"

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden asked the question that about eleven people in that yard had come to ask, and the fen entered that he had known it since the eleventh of this month and had not said anything about having known it.

"**Forty hundredweight a day for a third of the year. Whose is it.**"

"**It goes into a launder and a launder is not a village. I am not going to put it on a line with four columns on it, and the reason I am not going to is that I stood in a yard in your village on Monday and offered your village its own column back blank, and about nine people out of nineteen told me that a village which may take a number off its own sheet because the number does not flatter it ends up with a sheet of things it would have chosen to have counted.**"

"**So I am not going to hand four bodies of households a fifth column with forty hundredweight in it on the second afternoon of the twenty-third of a month, in a yard, with eight men standing on a silted cut. Somebody is going to have to sit down and do that properly and it is not going to be me, and the count of things this body has said in a yard that are not his to decide is six and the fen entered that he does not know which of the six were right.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the licensed man said the only thing he said on that ground all day, and he said it to the fen and not to the yard, and the fen entered that it was said to him and not to the yard.

"**A hold for five has just gone into a book in your own hand with the hour on it, in a form nobody has ever used, in a district where there is no form for it, and the count of persons who can do it is one and the count is you. I have been on the roll for eleven years and I have never seen that arrangement anywhere and I am not going to tell you that it is safe. I am going to tell you that the reason it is not safe is not the hold. It is that the count of it is one, and a count of one is not a system, and you have said that sentence in a yard four times in twenty days and you have not once said it about yourself in a way that would let somebody else hold you to it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and did not answer it, and entered that he was not going to answer it, and entered the reason, which is that a man who is the only one has spent two years finding out that he cannot be sent for anybody and does not know how to be sent for himself.

Then the reader of this body said the counts, and both of them, and he gave the reason before the numbers, and the count of that is fifteen in this month's history.

"**Two counts and I am going to say both of them and I am going to say out loud that one of them is mine and that the other one is not, and that I do not know which.**"

"**The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two. It has not moved in fourteen days and it is not going to move today, and a hold across two branches three hundred yards apart with a licensed man standing in the middle of it is not a hold for four, and if anybody in this yard thinks it is, I would like them to say so now and not on the thirtieth.**"

"**The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for five is one, and it is me, and it started this afternoon at about the second hour, and I am entering that a new count of one is the easiest thing in this book to be proud of and the reason I am entering it in the same line as the count of two is that a man who enters one of them by itself has entered a boast and a man who enters both of them has entered a fact.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And at the fifth hour of the following morning, which was the twenty-fourth, the reader of this body was still in bed at that node with the headache on and got up, and the rest of that day is entered where it belongs and is not entered here, and the fen entered the reason, which is that a day entered twice becomes a day that was about a number, and this one was about a number, and about forty hundredweight is not a number anybody is going to be able to leave out of a yard in about four years.

And that was the whole of the twenty-third, and nobody thanked anybody, and the man of about fifty did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing, and the fen entered that he did not ask and entered that this is the fourth time in fourteen days that he has not asked and that the count of the request stands at fifty-two and that a man not asking for a thing is not the same as a man not wanting it and this holding has not worked out which it is.

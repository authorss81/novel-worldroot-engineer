# Chapter 211

## A Hold By Somebody Who Has Not Held One

On the evening of the twenty-first of this month, in the shed at Errow Reach, with about nine people in it and the lamp on the nail, the man of the north row with the cough said the thing he had come to say and he said it before anybody asked him for anything, and the fen entered that he said it before anybody asked and that the count of that at that shed, over the seventeen days since that party came to this ground, is three.

"**The third line of that sheet says NOT COUNTED, and NOT COUNTED is not a count, it is a hole with a word over it. There are four people working at Marden on Marden's turn and the turn is at the second hour of the morning and there is nobody in this fen who can hold for four but the two of us, and one of us is thirty-one with a hold in a book and a mark on his arm and a headache that starts about the second hour of the afternoon, and the other one is sixty-eight with a cough in the winter.**"

"**I am not offering because I am brave. I am offering because I have been in a yard four times in three weeks and three of those times I was not asked to be there, and a man who is put in a yard and not asked is being put there to be looked at, and I would rather be asked.**"

Then the reader of this body asked him, out loud, in front of about nine people, and he gave the reason before he asked, and the count of that in this month's history is ten.

"**I am going to ask you to hold a pattern for four working people and I am going to say why in front of them before I ask. Marden's turn is a hold for four. The count in this fen of persons who can hold a pattern for four is two, and one of them is me, and I have done it twice and the second time cost me a night and a hand and a headache that ran from the third hour of the afternoon to the fourth hour the next morning, and all three of those are in a book nine miles west with the hour on them.**"

"**And I am not going to be able to stand at that kerb at the second hour of a morning for four weeks running, and neither is anybody else, and if I do not ask you then Marden's turn does not get taken and four people do not work and the rota has a hole in it before it has been in force a day. That is the whole of the reason and it is arithmetic and I would like it written down that it is arithmetic.**"

And the man of the north row with the cough said yes, and he said it in nine words, and the fen did not ask him twice and the fen entered that he did not ask him twice and entered that this is the ninth time in this book's history that a person has been asked in a yard with the reason given first and that the first of the nine was answered with a no and that the no was right.

"**Yes. And I want the word said out loud before I stand there so that I know I am going to be able to say it.**"

And the reader of this body said the word out loud in a shed with about nine people in it, once, and it is the tenth line of the draft rota and it is the reason line ten is the line it is, and the word is **enough**, and it is one word, and it is the same word whichever body of households it is, and it is said out loud so that somebody else hears it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the twenty-second of this month was a Wednesday, and the second hour of the morning of it is the second hour of the morning, and four people of Marden were working on the headland above the village with a ditch and a barrow, and the man of the north row with the cough stood at the head of it in the wet with his back to the wind and held a pattern for them for about two hours.

This is not a yard. It is a headland four hundred yards off the nearest wall and there is no wall round it and nobody said anything in a yard, and the fen entered that nothing said at that headland is entered as anything said in a yard, and entered the reason, which is that a book that lets a man change a room by walking ten paces out of it is a book about the man.

The man of about fifty stood about twenty feet off with the book open on his arm and a pencil in his hand and Hesta Lyle stood beside him, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood by the ditch with a barrow, and Coll Renner stood about thirty feet off with his hands behind his back and his case on the ground and did not come closer for two hours, and the fen entered all of it and entered the places.

The four of them worked. That is the whole of what a turn is and the reader of this body has said so in a yard before and is not going to say it here.

At about the fourth hour of the morning the man of the north row with the cough said the word out loud, once, and he said it to the four of them and not to anybody behind, and the turn stopped.

Nobody moved for a moment and then the four of them stood up straight and one of them put his hand on the barrow and nobody said anything, and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the man of the north row with the cough sat down in the wet where he had been standing, on the bank, and he could not get up for about an hour, and nobody helped him and he did not ask, and the fen entered that nobody helped him and entered that he did not ask and that the two of those facts are the same fact and that this holding has four years of notes about a body that stands over a crew and the count of times the reader of this body has put a hand out is not going to be entered, because it has not happened.

Then the cost, in his own hand, and it went in as he said it and the man of about fifty wrote it and read it back and the man of the north row with the cough said it was right, and the reader of this body did not add a word to it and did not take a word off it.

> **Entered by the man of the north row with the cough, in his own hand, at about the sixth hour of the morning of the twenty-second of this month, at the headland above Marden, and not by the man of about fifty and not by the clerk: HELD ABOUT TWO HOURS FOR FOUR PEOPLE WORKING. SAID THE WORD AT THE FOURTH HOUR. SAT DOWN IN THE WET AFTER AND COULD NOT GET UP FOR ABOUT AN HOUR. COULD NOT LIFT THE SEED SACKS ON THE SECOND DAY. WAS SICK ON THE SECOND NIGHT AND AGAIN ON THE THIRD MORNING. THAT IS WORSE THAN HIS AND I HAVE READ HIS, AND HIS IS IN A BOOK NINE MILES WEST WITH THE HOUR ON IT, AND MINE IS NOT IN A BOOK AT ALL UNTIL I SAY IT HERE. I DO NOT KNOW WHETHER I COULD HAVE DONE THE TWO HOURS IF I HAD NOT BEEN THINKING ABOUT WHAT IT WAS GOING TO COST ME, AND I AM NOT GOING TO FIND OUT, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO ASK ME IN A YARD.**

Then what was left of the work was measured, and the man of about fifty measured it with a tape and entered it, and the fen entered that he had not asked for the measurement and that he had asked for it, and that both of those are entered.

> **THE DITCH, measured at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-second with a tape by the man of about fifty, at the request of the engineer of record and without anybody asking for it: THIRTEEN FEET, four feet deep at the top and two feet nine at the bottom, dug by four people in about two hours. The line of the draft rota about a turn that has not happened says the fields wait, and the fields waiting is entered by whoever enters anything, on the day, with the hour on it, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.**

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing about that line and the fen did not take it off her and entered that no line of that sheet had ever been read back to him in a yard by somebody who did not write it, and that she has no standing to do it and that he has no standing to tell her she has not.

"**Your line seven says the fields wait and nobody is blamed for a field that waited, and that line has been in a cart shed since the twenty-seventh of last month and it is the first one anybody at Marden has ever been given. Thirteen feet. The ditch wants to be about ninety. We will do it Thursday.**"

Then the licensed man said one thing, and he said it in the professional half of his voice and he did not soften it, and the fen entered that it was the professional half and that Coll Renner has two halves and this was the one he uses at work.

"**I have been on the roll for eleven years and I have never seen a hold entered on any form in any district, and I have said so in a yard and I am saying it here. This is the first one that has been put in front of me by the man who did it. It is a bad one. I have never seen a good one. Those are two facts and they are not the same fact, and I am not going to stand on a headland at the fourth hour of the morning and pretend that they are, and I am not going to say that a man stopping at two hours is a professional act.**"

Nobody said anything, and the fen did not fill it.

Then the reader of this body entered the count of line seven, and the count is two.

"**The seventh line of a draft written on the back of a printed schedule in a cart shed has now been obeyed twice in the twenty-five days since it was written, and both times in a bad hour, once by a man of sixty-eight who did not know whether he was allowed and once by a man of seventy-one who is not in this book and whose name nobody has. A line that has been obeyed twice is not a rule. It is a line that has been obeyed twice, and I am going to write it down in those words and not in any other.**"

Nobody said a word, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it.

Then the reader of this body said the count, out loud, on a headland, to about nine people, and the reason with it, and he gave the reason before the count, which is the eleventh time in this month's history.

"**The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two. It was two when this holding arrived at that node and it is two now and I have said that number out loud four times since the twenty-second of last month and this is the fifth and it has not moved, and I am going to say why it has not moved, because the reason is the whole of this morning.**"

"**A man who has held it once and said the word at the two-hour mark is a man who has held it once. That is the whole of it. I have got a number in front of me that says I can hold for four and a number in front of me that says a man of sixty-eight did it for two hours and then said enough, and I am not going to add a third number to them, and I am not going to take either of them off.**"

"**And I am entering the other half of it and I am entering it because it is the half that would have made me a better engineer. He did not hold for four. He held for four about two hours and then he stopped, and a person who has stopped is not a person who can hold, and that is not a judgement, that is the definition. If the count of persons who can hold a pattern for four were a count of people who have held one, it would be three, and it is two, and it is going to be two when the four of us go home.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-second of this month, at the headland above Marden, on the standing of a hold, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days east by the cart road: **the man of the north row with the cough was asked in a shed at Errow Reach on the evening of the twenty-first, in about nine people, with the reason given before the ask, and he said yes, and the reader of this body did not ask him twice. THE THIRD LINE OF THE DRAFT ROTA SAYS MARDEN, SECOND HOUR, FOUR PEOPLE WORKING, NOT COUNTED, and the turn is a hold for four. A turn was held on the headland above Marden at about the second hour of the morning of the twenty-second with four people working, and at about the fourth hour the holder said the word ENOUGH out loud, once, to the four of them and not to anybody behind, and the turn stopped. The word is the tenth line of the draft rota, was named out loud in a shed the night before by the reader of this body at the request of the man who was going to have to use it, and is one word and is the same word whichever body of households it is. The headland is not a yard, is four hundred yards off the nearest wall, and nothing said at it is entered as anything said in a yard. The holder sat down in the wet after and could not get up for about an hour and nobody helped him and he did not ask. THE COST WAS ENTERED IN HIS OWN HAND AND IT IS WORSE THAN THE ENGINEER OF RECORD'S, and he said so himself, and it is not in a book nine miles west because it is not in a book at all until he said it. Coll Renner said that this is the first hold he has ever had put in front of him by the man who did it, that it is a bad one, that he has never seen a good one, and that the two facts are not the same fact. The reader of this body entered that the seventh line of the draft has now been obeyed twice in the twenty-five days since that sheet was written, both times in a bad hour, once by a man of sixty-eight who did not know whether he was allowed and once by a man of seventy-one who is not in this book and whose name nobody has, and that a line that has been obeyed twice is not a rule. THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO AND IT DID NOT MOVE, and the reader of this body entered the reason, which is that a man who has held it once and said the word at the two-hour mark is a man who has held it once, and that a person who has stopped is not a person who can hold, and that the count is a count of persons and not a count of holds, and that if it were a count of holds it would be three and it is two. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

And the man of the north row with the cough said one sentence on the way down off the headland that was not said in a yard and was not said on a road and that the fen entered with the hour on it and did not improve.

"**Then find out whether it was the four or whether it was the man. I have been sixty-eight for two years and I have been holding my own turn for nine years and I have never once had to hold anybody else's, and there is a difference between those two things and I have felt it this morning and I cannot tell you what it is.**"

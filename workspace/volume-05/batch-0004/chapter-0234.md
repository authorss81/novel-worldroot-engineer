# Chapter 234

## The First Of The Third Month

The first of the third month was a Sunday and the compost line for the second month fell on it, and the woman who does the sums brought the small book of forty-eight lines out of her own bag at about the tenth hour of the morning and put it on the table in the middle on top of the three books that live there and opened it, and about nine people were in the yard, and the person who keeps the seed house said the standing-up sentence and the reader of this body said his own back and neither of them improved it.

She read the forty-eight lines out at about the seventh hour of the morning of the first of the month two months back and the reader of this body had not opened that book and had not looked at a page of it, and the same thing happened again this morning and he did not touch it, and the fen entered that he had now not touched that book twice and that twice is a habit and that a habit is a thing you do not decide about on the morning you do it.

"**Fifty hundredweight, every month, every month, for forty-eight months, and the two of them with a stroke through the figure, and the stroke is mine and it means the figure did not happen and not that it was smaller. I have said that once already in a yard and I am not going to say it better this morning, so I am going to say the rest of it and go straight through.**"

"**The line is four hundred and fifty hundredweight a month at four shillings the hundredweight. That is one thousand eight hundred shillings. That is ninety pounds. That is the line, and it has been ninety pounds two month running, and before that it was eighty pounds four month running, and the specification on the return main is four hundred hundredweight a month at four shillings, which is eighty pounds, and the specification is a figure in a charter this holding does not hold.**"

"**Now the ten.** Fifty hundredweight a month at four shillings the hundredweight is two hundred shillings, which is ten pounds a month. Ten pounds a month for forty-eight months is four hundred and eighty pounds. **Four hundred and eighty pounds of a line that has never once been in a line.** Two thousand four hundred hundredweight over four years is six times four hundred hundredweight, and six times ninety pounds is five hundred and forty pounds, and the line has said eighty pounds a month and the line was never more than seventy pounds a month of compost and the other ten was my back. I have been the compost. That is the whole of the finding and it took me four years and a fortnight and it is not a discovery anybody could make in a yard, and I am not going to pretend it is one."

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then she read the line itself, and she read it out in the ordinary voice, and the man of about fifty read it back before a word of it went on a page, and the fen did not add to it and did not take anything off it.

> **THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE SECOND MONTH, entered on the first of the third month at about the eleventh hour of the morning, in a yard at this holding, in about nine people, read out by the woman who does the sums and read back by the man of about fifty, and entered whole:**
>
> **THE WORD BEFORE THE FIGURE IS PAID.**
>
> **THE FIGURE IS ONE HUNDRED POUNDS.** Ninety pounds is the line. Ten pounds is not in the line and has not been in the line for four years and four months and is fifty hundredweight a month out of a bin behind my own house, carried by me, at the hour I carried it, in a book nobody has ever seen until the twenty-fourth of a month ago.
>
> **THE RUN IS FOURTEEN MONTHS NOT DISCHARGED IN FOURTEEN.**
>
> **AND THIS IS THE FIRST WORD BEFORE THAT FIGURE IN FIVE YEARS THAT IS NOT A WORD ABOUT A HEAP.** The words before it were EMPTIER, CROWDED and SHALLOWER, and all three of them were about a heap and this one is not, and I want it said out loud in a yard that I chose it, and a word is a thing a person picks and a figure is a thing a person cannot pick, and I picked the word and the figure came out of a bin I have been emptying since before any of the four bodies of households had a column with a heading on it.
>
> **AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE THANKED FOR IT AND ANYBODY WHO THANKS ME CAN BE TOLD TO STOP.** Four hundred and eighty pounds over four years across sixteen households is thirty pounds a household, and I am not a household and I am not going to be turned into one, and the figure is about a heap for the first time in four years and that is the only good thing in it and I am not going to be thanked for the only good thing in it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody in that yard said anything tender and that he entered both of those.

And then the reader of this body said the one thing he had, and he said it to the book and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he said it to the book.

"**I said in a yard on the first of this month that the word and the figure had not been a pair for four years and that I did not know which of the two of them was telling the truth, and I said that I was going to find out. I have not found out. What has happened is that the word has stopped being about a heap, and that is not the same thing and it is not better, and I am not going to say it is better in a yard about nine people.**"

"**A line of ninety pounds a month with a word in front of it that says the heap is shallow is a line that has been honest for five years about a thing that is not the problem. A line of one hundred pounds with a word in front of it that says PAID is a line that has been dishonest for five years about the problem. One of those two lines is a better line and I do not know which one it is and I am not going to vote.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the person who keeps the seed house read the standing for the month, and it is short, and it is entered as it was read, and the reader of this body did not add to it.

> **THE STANDING FOR THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH, read out at about the eleventh hour of the morning in about nine people, and entered as it was read.**
>
> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FORTY-TWO,** read on the thirtieth of last month and read again on the first of this month, because the door is read on the first and the thirtieth, and it stood at forty-two on the thirtieth and it stood at forty-two on the first and it has stood at forty-two since, and the reading on the first of this month was at about the first hour of the morning of it and not at the tenth, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be. **THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY ASKED AND IS NOT GOING TO BE ASKED BY THIS HOLDING.**
>
> **THE DAY-MINUS RULES.** The boards stand at three hundred and fifty-five days and the March's tin at four hundred and two days, and the clerk read both out loud before either number on the thirtieth of last month and is five days east by the cart road this morning, and they were read out this morning by the reader of this body and not by the clerk, and that is entered and it is not a fault and it is a fact about a road.
>
> **THE NINTH-DAY CYCLE** began on the twenty-eighth of a month five months back. There have been fourteen turns of it since and this holding has weighed and read back every one of them, and the last three figures are ninety on the seventh of last month, ninety-one on the sixteenth of last month and eighty-nine on the twenty-fifth of last month, and the fifteenth is on the fourth of this month and is not yet in this holding's book. The count of names in the first rule of that schedule is fourteen and the count of marks in the column on the board at the node is fifteen, and both numbers stand and neither is struck out.
>
> **THE SHORTFALL** is six hundred hundredweight a day and is three hundred pounds a month and is forty-eight pence a head a month across about fifteen hundred people, and it did not go up when a launder was cut and it has not come down, and the four launders measure nine hundred, six hundred, nothing and twenty-five.
>
> **THE LEAK** is three-quarters of an inch, twice measured with two rules, on the twenty-first of a month six months back, and it was not measured on the thirtieth of last month on a third reason, and no fourth reason was invented. No cause. No due date. No verdict.
>
> **THE BEAT** is not logged, cause not established, column open, and its day is not restated and is not going to be, and the column was not opened in front of nine people this morning and the not opening is a decision.
>
> **THE WELL ON THE SHELF.** This holding has four figures of it and they are all five feet and the tenth is not among them and has not been asked for twice. Four feet against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month ten months back, and it is not decomposed, and the half inch of a month eight months back is not carried forward as a figure here. **THE NEXT ORDINARY READING IS THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE SIXTEENTH OF NEXT MONTH AND THE DAY IS ENTERED AND THE FIGURE IS NOT,** and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and no page of this holding's book is going to make one.
>
> **THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES.** The reason is entered so that nobody has to ask. A schedule is not a contact. A reading is not a contact. A launder cut by a village is not a contact. A gate count is not a contact. A hold is not a contact. A rota in force for ten mornings is not a contact. **A BARROW OF LEAF MOLD CARRIED BY ONE WOMAN FOR FORTY-EIGHT MONTHS IS NOT A CONTACT AND IT IS NOT ON THE LOG AND IT WAS NEVER GOING TO BE, AND THAT IS THE WORST SENTENCE IN THIS MONTH'S STANDING AND IT IS MINE AND I AM NOT TAKING IT OUT.**
>
> **THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO.** Both are of this holding and one of them is the engineer of record. It has been two for sixty days. It did not move in this month. The count of persons who can hold a pattern for five is one and it is him.
>
> **THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE SAME ARM ARE FOUR** and they reconcile none of one another and nothing said about that arm in this month is a fifth.
>
> **WHOSE ORDER IT IS** has been asked four times and answered never and it was not asked a fifth time in this month. The standing count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken was not said out loud this morning and is not going to be, and the count of times the question that went into this holding's book has been asked is one and the count of times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is three, and the three counts are three counts.
>
> **THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED AGAIN IN ONE MONTH** and the count of the times he has been fetched stands at nineteen and the count is not to be brought up again inside a month and this holding is not going to write and ask why not.
>
> **THE REQUEST COUNT IS FIFTY-THREE** and it has not moved since the first of this month and a request is not a count of gratitude and the two have never been added together in this holding's book.

And then the reader of this body read the notice out, which came up the fen road on the thirtieth of last month and is one sheet and names the seventh of this month and is on the ordinary notice, and he read all of it out loud before anybody said a word about any part of it.

"**Four lines. One: the Assembly will examine the ground and the persons at the Four Elbows on the seventh of this month at about the tenth hour. Two: the body may have any person it likes present at every hour of the examination and the person may read anything the examiner reads. Three: the examination is not to be stayed or delayed and the examiner is not to be fed, and if the body has anything to say it is to say it on the day. Four: the examination does not stop a turn, and a turn is to be taken on the ordinary hour, and the examiner may stand in the way of a turn if the examiner chooses to and the turn is then a turn that was taken and the hour is the hour.**"

"**And it is correct. Every line of it is correct and I have read it three times looking for the line that is wrong, and there is not one, and the fourth line is the fourth line of a document that was written to be correct, and I am walking five days east this afternoon to be in a yard when eleven things are read out and I am not going to be able to do one single thing about any of them.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> Entered by the woman who does the sums, in her own hand, at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the first of this month, in a yard at this holding, in about nine people, on the standing of a line, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days east by the cart road, and not by the engineer of record, who read it out and added nothing to it: **THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE SECOND MONTH. The word before the figure is PAID. The figure is ONE HUNDRED POUNDS, of which ninety pounds is the line and ten pounds is fifty hundredweight a month at four shillings the hundredweight out of a bin behind my own house, carried by me, at the hour I carried it, for forty-eight months, in a book nobody has ever seen until the twenty-fourth of a month ago. The two strokes are mine and mean the figure did not happen and not that it was smaller. THE RUN IS FOURTEEN MONTHS NOT DISCHARGED IN FOURTEEN. This is the first word before that figure in five years that is not a word about a heap, and I chose it, and a word is a thing a person picks and a figure is a thing a person cannot pick. The reader of this body said out loud in that yard that he has not found out whether the word or the figure was telling the truth and that the word has stopped being about a heap, and that a line that was honest for five years about a thing that is not the problem is not thereby a better line than a line that was dishonest for five years about the problem, and that he is not going to vote. I am not going to be thanked for this and anybody who thanks me can be told to stop. The standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and the next ordinary reading of the well on the shelf is the fourth hour of the sixteenth of next month and the day is in and the figure is not, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back. The use log stands at fifteen lines and a barrow of leaf mold carried by one woman for forty-eight months is not a contact and was never going to be, and that sentence is the worst one in this morning's standing and it is the engineer of record's. THE COUNT OF PERSONS WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO AND HAS BEEN TWO FOR SIXTY DAYS. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything tender.**

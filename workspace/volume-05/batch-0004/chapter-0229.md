# Chapter 229

## The Fifteenth, Or The Fourteenth

The fifteenth of this month was a Monday and the ninth day of every ninth day came round for the fourteenth time since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back, and about eleven people were in a yard at Errow Reach at about the second hour of the morning.

The man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it, and the fen entered that, and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is seven and has moved, and entered that a man of sixty-eight has now been in seven yards this season without being asked once and that the count of things he has said in seven yards is one and that the one was about arithmetic.

Harlan Vetch came round the corner of the building with the barrow about ten minutes to the hour, and it went on the steelyard, and the man of about fifty read the figure out and each of the four bodies of households read it back after him, one at a time, standing where they could see the dial.

> **THE FIGURE ON THE STEELYARD, read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn: EIGHTY-NINE HUNDREDWEIGHT, at about the second hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month, on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles, by one man, in about an hour and a third. It is a different number from the one on the seventh of this month and a different number from the one on the sixteenth, and the same man, and the three numbers are entered as three numbers and not as one number with a wobble on it.**

And it went into the hollow on the west side inside nine square yards, and it took about four minutes, and the four of them watched it go in, and nobody said anything while it did, and the fen entered that nobody said anything while it did.

And then a name went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY, and a name went into the space headed WITNESSED BY, and that is the fifth time, and the first rule of that sheet has now had a name in it five times in eleven years and the fifth is the same man in both.

And then Sennik Vaul of Silling, of about thirty-one, one of seven who decide and the one who was sent, said the thing that had been sitting in him since the first of this month, and he said it to the board and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he said it to the board.

"**It is the fifteenth. The board has fifteen marks in the column and the first rule of that schedule has fourteen names in it, and I am not saying one of you is wrong, because you cannot both be right and you cannot both be wrong, and one of those is a pair of marks on a board with no heading and the other is a pair of names on a schedule with a heading.**"

And then the woman who keeps the four books put her hand flat on the board without touching the chalk, and the fen entered that she did not touch the chalk, and she said the half of it that was hers to say and no more.

"**The column on that board has had a heading since the tenth of this month, in my hand, and the heading is RETURN BY THE FIFTH SHARE. It was a column and no heading for eleven years and a man wrote in it who is not one of the four bodies, and a heading put on a column in the eleventh year does not know what the first ten years of marks on it were. I am not saying the fifteen is wrong. I am saying that a column that has had a heading for five days cannot be counted against a rule that has had one for eleven years, and the fifteen is a count of marks and the fourteen is a count of mornings and those are not the same count and this board is a different document from that schedule.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the reader of this body did not rule on it, and gave the reason out loud, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-one.

"**I could settle it in about an hour. I could take the two documents side by side and count the mornings and find the mark and say which of the two documents is right, and one of them would be right and one of them would be wrong and I would be the man who decided which.**"

"**And a count of things that happened is not the sort of figure that ought to be settled by a man who has a mark on his arm and is standing next to the figure. So the fourteenth is the fourteenth because the cycle is a cycle and it began on the twenty-eighth of a month four months back and there are thirteen whole ninth days between that morning and this morning and this morning is the fourteenth, and the board says fifteen and the board is a different document, and both numbers go in this holding's book with the day on them and I am not going to strike one of them out in front of you.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said nothing and entered that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in has not moved and that he said nothing in this one and that the count of times he has been in a yard and said nothing this month is three.

And then he entered one thing beside the fourteenth that was not about the fourteenth, and he gave the reason before he gave it, and the reason was short.

"**The next ordinary reading of the well on the shelf is at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of next month, which is twenty-one days from this morning. I have written the day on the page in my own book and I have not written the figure, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and this holding has four figures of that well and they are all five feet and the tenth is not among them and has never been asked for twice.**"

"**And the reason I am saying it in this yard on this morning is that it is on the page under a figure and a page that has a figure under it is a page somebody reads, and a page that has a day under it and no figure is a page that a body can read and cannot do anything with, and there are four of those in that book and this is the fourth hour of the morning and I would rather it was said while eleven people are standing about than never.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then he entered the other half of the morning, and it is four lines long in his own book, and he read it out because he said he would.

"**The first line of that sheet came round at the second hour of this morning and it was taken at the second hour of this morning, with the word, in about nine minutes, by the same six and with the same barrows, and the man of twenty-six of Havergate said the word first this time because he asked me on Friday where to find it and I told him on Friday at the fourth hour of the afternoon on the road and not on the hill.**"

"**I am entering that I got it wrong on Friday and right on Monday and that a man who got a thing wrong on Friday because he did not know which of two things was right has been lucky and not careful. The field on Friday did not get watered and the row of it is about nine yards long and there are two hundred and thirty-one trees on the ground above it that a man of about fifty-eight counted in his own book, and none of those trees is in that row and none of that is in my favour.**"

"**I am also entering what I did not write down, and it is this. On Friday I stood two hundred yards off at a stone the size of a bread oven and I did not go up, and I told myself the reason was the sixth line, and the sixth line gave that morning to a man of seventy-one at about the fourth hour and to six men of Havergate at about the fifth hour and neither of them had the word, and the turn had already happened once at the second hour, and so the reason was true. It was also true that I did not go up because on Friday morning the fourth line of that sheet was going to be empty on Sunday morning and I already knew it, and I have been asked once in this holding's history to be the second body on a rota and I said no in a yard in front of about eleven people, and a man who has said no in a yard does not go up a hill two days afterwards to find out whether anybody else is going to say it.**"

"**That is the second time in a month that a reason I gave out loud was true and was not the whole one, and the first one was on the fifteenth of this month about a ground I did not know the length of, and I am not going to count them, and the not counting is the point.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the man of about fifty said the whole of the thing that the man of thirty-one had started, and he said it into the board, and he did not say it out loud so the fen entered that it was said into the board and not out loud and entered the length of it.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the same day the reader of this body put his own book in the inside of his coat and went down to the cart road, and the man of about fifty came out of the shed with a barrow and did not go with him and the man of the north row with the cough was still in the yard and was not asked to walk to the cart road either.

And the fen gave the reason for going out loud, at the gate, to about four people, and entered the length of it and entered that nobody said anything.

"**I am five days from that yard by this road and I have not been five days from it since the second of this month. There is a thing on the twenty-fourth that I have to be in and I am not going to be late to a thing I have told four bodies of households I will be in. The first line of that sheet came round this morning and it was taken this morning, and I was two hundred yards off and did not go up, and I am going to write down that I did not go up and why.**"

"**The fourth line is going to come round again on Thursday and it is going to be empty on Thursday morning, because I am not going to put my name in the fourth line, and I said that out loud in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month and I have not been asked since and I am not going to be asked now. So on Thursday there is nobody at that headland for a village of about three hundred people, and the reason is a sentence I said in a yard, and a sentence I said in a yard is a thing I own, and this is what owning one costs.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a return, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road: **the fourteenth ninth day since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back was held at about the second hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month and the return was EIGHTY-NINE HUNDREDWEIGHT, weighed on the steelyard and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn, one at a time, standing where they could see the dial. A name went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY and a name went into WITNESSED BY, and that is the fifth time in eleven years, and the three returns in this month are NINETY, NINETY-ONE and EIGHTY-NINE, and they are entered as three numbers and not as one number with a wobble on it, and the reader of this body said that out loud in that yard and entered the reason, which is that a sequence of three numbers with a heading is a series and a sequence of three numbers with no heading is a column, and this holding has had both and has not decided which one it is running. A man of about thirty-one of Silling said the fifteenth, to the board and not to the yard, on the ground that the board carries fifteen marks in the column and the schedule carries fourteen names in its first rule. The woman who keeps the four books said that a column that has had a heading for five days cannot be counted against a rule that has had one for eleven years, and that the fifteen is a count of marks and the fourteen is a count of mornings. THE READER OF THIS BODY DID NOT RULE ON IT, and gave the reason, which is that he could settle it in about an hour and that a man who settles it is a man who has decided which of two documents of four bodies of households is right. BOTH NUMBERS GO IN AND NEITHER IS STRUCK OUT. The next ordinary reading of the well on the shelf is the fourth hour of the sixteenth of next month, which is twenty-one days from this morning, and the day is in this book and the figure is not, and the standing of the day and not the figure is three and is not reduced, and the four figures this holding has of that well are all five feet and the tenth is not among them. THE READER OF THIS BODY LEFT THIS HOLDING ON THE CART ROAD AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON OF THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH and did not go up to the first line of that sheet, which was taken that morning, and entered the reason, which is that the fourth line is going to come round on Thursday and is going to be empty, and that a sentence said out loud in a yard on the twenty-seventh of last month is a thing he owns. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything.**

# Chapter 227

## Eleven Days Of A Rota

The twenty-first of this month was a Thursday and the draft rota for the Four Elbows went into a yard at Errow Reach at about the eleventh hour of the morning, read out whole, and it was in force from the second hour of the morning of the twenty-second, and it is in force.

About eleven people were in the yard. The man of the north row with the cough was in it and was not asked to be in it, and the fen entered that, and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of last month is six and has moved, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has been in a yard six times this season without being asked has stopped being a guest and become a fixture, and this holding has not decided to notice that yet.

Hesta Lyle read it. She read it off the sheet and not off a copy of the sheet, and the sheet is the back of a printed weekly schedule of a holding that has no charter, and the reader of this body did not touch it and did not hold it and was not thanked for not touching it because nobody thanked anybody.

> **THE ELEVEN LINES, read out in a yard at Errow Reach at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the twenty-first of this month by the woman who keeps the four books, in the hand they were written in, and read back, and entered whole and not summarised.**
>
> **THE FIRST LINE. HAVERGATE. SECOND HOUR. TWO HOURS. SIX WHO TAKE IT IN TURNS.**
>
> **THE SECOND LINE. LOW WETHER. SECOND HOUR. TWO HOURS. TWO.**
>
> **THE THIRD LINE. SILLING. SECOND HOUR. TWO HOURS. ONE, AND HE IS SEVENTY-ONE.**
>
> **THE FOURTH LINE. MARDEN. SECOND HOUR. TWO HOURS. FOUR PEOPLE WORKING. NOT COUNTED.**
>
> **THE FIFTH LINE. A TURN DOES NOT BEGIN UNTIL THE HOLDER IS ON THE GROUND AND HAS SAID THE WORD, AND A TURN THAT HAS NOT BEGUN IS NOT A LATE TURN.**
>
> **THE SIXTH LINE. A TURN THAT HAS NOT HAPPENED IS TAKEN BY THE NEXT BODY ON THE LIST.**
>
> **THE SEVENTH LINE. A HOLDER WHO IS ASLEEP AND CANNOT BE WOKEN IS NEITHER, AND THE NEXT BODY IS WOKEN, AND THEN THE ONE AFTER, AND IF NEITHER CAN BE WOKEN THE TURN IS NOT TAKEN, THE FIELDS WAIT, AND THE FIELDS WAITING IS ENTERED BY WHOEVER ENTERS ANYTHING WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR ON IT, AND NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED.**
>
> **THE EIGHTH LINE. NOBODY HOLDS A TURN FOR A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS THAT HAS NOT ASKED.**
>
> **THE NINTH LINE. A BODY MAY STOP ANY TURN OF ITS OWN AT ANY TIME WITHOUT SAYING WHY AND WITHOUT SAYING WHY BEFORE.**
>
> **THE TENTH LINE. THE HOLDER STOPS ON ONE WORD, THE SAME WORD, SAID OUT LOUD SO THAT SOMEBODY HEARS IT.**
>
> **THE ELEVENTH LINE. IT IS A DRAFT AND IS NOT IN FORCE BECAUSE THREE OF THE FOUR BODIES HAVE NOT SEEN IT.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then she read the eleventh line out and stopped, because the eleventh line had stopped being true about eleven days before the twenty-first, and the man of about fifty put his hand flat on the table and said one sentence about it.

"**It is out of date on its own face and it has been out of date since the ninth of this month, when the last of the four bodies saw it. I am not going to cross a line out of a sheet a man wrote in my shed and I am not going to write on it at all, and that is a thing I am saying in a yard and not a thing I am doing.**"

And he did not write on it, and the fen entered that he did not write on it and that a sheet with a line on it that has been overtaken is worth more than a sheet with a line rubbed out, and entered the reason, which is that a man who rubs out a thing in front of you has told you what the thing was and a man who does not has not.

Then Renn Ashby, of about twenty-six, of Havergate, one of the six who take it in turns and who had come up the hill on foot with five others, asked a question at the gate at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and he asked it out loud and in front of about nine people, and the question was one word long.

"**Word?**"

And the reader of this body did not answer him, and gave the reason before the yard had finished turning round, and the count of that in this holding's history is twenty-nine.

"**The fifth line says the holder is on the ground and has said the word. It does not say what the word is. I know what the word is and I am not going to say it in a yard, and the reason is that a word I say at a kerb in about eleven people is a word that in about four years is a word four villages got from a man with a mark on his arm, and the man with a mark on his arm is the man nobody will put in the fourth line of this sheet, and I would be doing the fourth line from the other end of it and I am not going to.**"

"**And I am not going to write it on his paper either. He is sixty-eight and he has a cough in the winter and it is the wrong end of the year to send a cart five days for one word, and I have been sitting on this for about four hours and I have had the word in my mouth twice.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty said he was not going to guess a word either, and that a guessed word is worse than no word because a guessed word gets into four mouths in a winter.

Then at about the fifth hour of the afternoon the reader of this body went down to the gate and said the word out loud, once, to the man of about sixty-one of Low Wether and to nobody else, and the man of about sixty-one of Low Wether repeated it back to him to be sure of it, and the reader of this body entered both of those and did not improve on them.

And the fen entered the reason for the gate and not the hill, and put the reason in his own book that night and read it back to nobody.

*Two bodies of households were going up that hill at the second hour of the morning and I was coming down to this gate at the fifth hour of the afternoon, and I had the word in my mouth twice and I gave it to one of them. I did not know at the fifth hour of the afternoon which of those two was the right thing to do and I have not found out since. I am entering that I chose, and that a man who chose at the fifth hour of the afternoon and got two fields to wait for it is a man who chose.*

So the twenty-second of this month was a Friday, and the second line of that sheet came round at the second hour of the morning, and the man of about sixty-one of Low Wether was on the ground with two men and a barrow each, and he said the word out loud, and the turn happened in about nine minutes.

And then the sixth line gave the morning on, and it gave it to the next body on the list, which is the third line, and the third line is a man of about seventy-one with one man and a barrow, and he was on the ground at about the fourth hour and he did not have the word, because nobody had given it to him and he was not in a yard.

And the turn did not begin. And by the fifth line that is not a late turn. And by the seventh line the fields wait, and the fields waiting is entered by whoever enters anything with the day and the hour on it, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

Then the sixth line gave the morning on again, and it gave it to the first line, and six men were on the ground at the headland above the ditch at about the fifth hour of the morning with a barrow each, and the holder was Renn Ashby of about twenty-six, and nobody said the word, because nobody at that headland had it.

And that turn did not begin either, and the seventh line said the fields wait, and the fields waiting was entered by whoever entered anything with the day and the hour on it.

And then the reader of this body entered the thing about the two of them, and it is the only figure he put in his own book that afternoon, and it is this, and he did not read it out.

*Neither of the two men who came to that kerb has one of the nine mornings in it. The second line was taken at the second hour and the sixth line gave that same morning on twice afterwards, and those two things are on one sheet and cannot both be right, and I am not ruling on it. A turn that is not the turn of that morning is not a turn, and a morning that was taken at the second hour is not a morning with a late turn in it, and a man who settles a sheet for four bodies of households in about an hour is a man who has decided which of two of their documents is right.*

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, and entered that about two hundred yards off the reader of this body was standing at a stone that is the size of a bread oven with a mason's mark on the back of it and did not go up, and entered the reason, which is that a man who goes up to a headland at the second hour of a morning when two bodies of households are standing about not knowing what to do is a supervisor, and the sixth line takes the turn and does not need one.

And then the man of seventy-one asked for a thing to write in, and the man of about fifty gave him a sheet and a pencil and did not write one word of it for him, and the reader of this body did not write one word of it either and entered the reason, which is that a failure entered by the engineer of record is a failure the engineer of record has put in a book, and a failure entered by the person who failed is the only one of the two that anybody in about three hundred people can use.

> Entered by a man of about seventy-one of Silling, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-second of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach, on the standing of a turn that did not begin, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road, and not by the engineer of record, who was two hundred yards off and did not come up: **THE SECOND LINE WAS TAKEN AT THE SECOND HOUR OF THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF THIS MONTH AND I WAS GIVEN THE MORNING UNDER THE SIXTH LINE AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR. I HAD BEEN ON THAT KERB NINE YEARS AND I CAME WITH ONE MAN AND A BARROW. THE FIFTH LINE SAYS A TURN DOES NOT BEGIN UNTIL THE HOLDER IS ON THE GROUND AND HAS SAID THE WORD, AND I HAVE SAID IT EVERY MORNING FOR NINE YEARS AND NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED ME WHAT IT IS, AND IT IS NOT ON THAT SHEET, AND I HAVE LOOKED AT THAT SHEET AND THERE IS NO WORD ON IT. THE TURN DID NOT BEGIN. The fields waited and the seventh line says nobody is blamed for a field that waited and I am not asking anybody to blame me and I am writing it down because it was my morning. Low Wether took that morning first and I was the second. Nobody thanked anybody. I have been asked for my name three times in eleven years for that board at the node and I have not given it three times, and I am not giving it tonight, and if this page wants a name at the top of it then it can have mine when I am not here.**

Nobody thanked anybody and the reader of this body did not thank the man of seventy-one for writing it down, and entered that he was not going to and entered the reason, which is that a man who is thanked for putting a thing in a book is a man who has been given the book and the thing together and can be given the second one without the first one, and this holding has four hundred and eighty pounds of a figure about a person in a bin and does not have a second one of anything.

And then Renn Ashby of about twenty-six of Havergate wrote his own page out and the man of about fifty read it out in that yard and read it back, and the reader of this body did not ask to see it and did not ask for a copy of it, and the fen entered that he did not ask for a copy and entered the reason, which is that a man of about twenty-six has been asked by a stranger from a fen for a copy of the worst morning of his working life inside a fortnight of it happening, and that is a thing this holding has done to him once already with a figure and is not going to do twice.

And the man of about fifty did not ask for his initial to be put against nothing, and the fen entered that he did not ask, and entered that the count of that request stands at fifty-three and has not moved since the first of this month, and entered the reason, which is the reason it has been the same reason four times this month, that a man with four names in four books this month is not a man who needs a line of his own with nothing against it.

And then the reader of this body said the part of the sheet that nobody had said anything about, and he said it to the sheet and not to the yard, and the fen entered that he said it to the sheet.

"**The ninth line. A body may stop any turn of its own at any time without saying why and without saying why before. That is a good line and it is the best line on that sheet and I am going to say out loud in this yard that I hope one of the four bodies uses it this week, because if a body stops a turn in the first ten days of a rota instead of the tenth month then it has told us something about the rota and it has told us it in the only way a body can be told something by a person who cannot be got.**"

"**And nobody is going to use it in the first ten days. I know that the same way I know the third column on that counting paper is a count of wanting, and I am not going to say that out loud in this yard either, and the reason is that a man who says a thing like that about four villages at the eleventh hour of the morning on the second day a rota is in force is a man who has read the fourth month of it in the first week.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough had said nothing at all through the whole of it, and that the count of times he has been in a yard and said nothing this month is two.

And then he entered what the sheet is, and it took him about a minute and he did not read it out.

"**It is four turns and seven stops. Four bodies of households and one sheet, and the sheet is standing this morning with one turn out of the three that came round taken and two of the three not taken, and it is the second morning, and the first night of it was a Thursday, and I am going to keep this in my own book every morning until somebody stops me and I am not going to be the one who stops me.**"

"**And I am going to write down the thing I did not write down, which is that at the fifth hour of the afternoon yesterday I had a word in my mouth and I gave it to one man at a gate and I did not give it to a man of seventy-one on a kerb or to six men on a hill, and that is the whole of what happened in the first morning of this rota, and no line on that sheet accounts for it, and the line that would account for it is a line about the person who hands things out and not about the person who takes them, and a man who is not on a rota should not be handing the words out.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

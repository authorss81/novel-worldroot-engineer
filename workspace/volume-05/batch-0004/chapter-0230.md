# Chapter 230

## What A Turn Is, And What It Costs To Be In One

The twenty-sixth of this month was a Tuesday and the twenty-seventh was a Wednesday, and the reader of this body was on the cart road for both of those days with a bag and a rule and nothing else in it, and he slept the first night of the walk in a barn at about the second mile because the holding there does not let anybody in after dark and he did not ask.

He walked it. He had walked it five times in this season and he had gone five days on it in a month three months back with a barrow, and there were about nineteen miles of it that a cart does and about thirty-one that a cart does not, and the difference between those two numbers is the four hours of a day a cart saves, and the cart is not faster, it just does not stop.

The twenty-sixth of this month was the second morning of the cart road and the second line of that sheet came round at the second hour of the morning, and the fen was nineteen miles off and did not see it, and did not ask anybody to tell him, and entered that he did not ask and entered the reason, which is that a man who asks a body of households to report to him whether their own turn went is a man who has made their turn into a column in his book, and this holding has nine columns with nothing in them and he is not opening a tenth.

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Wednesday and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon he came into a yard at a holding on the cart road with about nine people in it, and a crew of five was in a ditch about forty feet off with three spades and a barrow each, and a woman of about forty-four of that holding was on the head of the ditch with a line pegged out and a string, and she did not stop working when she saw him and he did not stand at the head of her ditch.

He stood about nine feet off, the way he stands, and he said out loud what he was going to say and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-two.

"**I am going to say a thing in this yard about a crew of five and about a schedule, and it is not a speech and it is about four sentences, and I am saying it to you and not to your ditch because you are the only person on that crew who has ever done it more than eleven times.**"

And the woman of about forty-four said the first half of it before he got to the second half, and the fen entered that she did.

"**I know what you are going to say and you are going to say it about a committee and not about me, and you should say it about the committee, because I am on the committee.**"

"**Say it. And then I am going to say the half of it that you have not got, and I am going to say it first, and then you can say yours, and it will be worse after mine than it was before mine. That is not rudeness. That is what a schedule is.**"

And the reader of this body said it, in four sentences, and he did not say it well, and the fen entered that he did not say it well and entered the length of it.

"**A crew of five cannot be trusted to a schedule written by a body with an interest in the schedule, and the body with the interest in the schedule is four villages, and the four villages are standing in this yard when I say it because I have been at that kerb for nineteen days and one of them is a man of about fifty who writes figures down and one of them is a woman of about fifty-one who carries compost out of a bin with her own back and has been doing it for four years, and neither of those two is a body of households and both of them are on a rota that four bodies of households drew.**"

"**And the reason it cannot be trusted to the schedule is not that anybody in it is dishonest. It is that a schedule is a list of mornings and a morning is a thing you can be in, and a person can be in a morning, and the sheet has a column that says how many and no column at all that says where, and a holder working under a schedule with no where-column in it is being asked all morning to be lied to by four villages that are not lying to him. That is the fault of the sheet and not of the five and the schedule was written by people who had never held one.**"

Then the woman of about forty-four of that holding said the other half of it, and she said it to the five in the ditch and not to the reader of this body, and the fen entered that she said it to the five and not to him and entered that he was not thanked for having said the first half of it.

"**I have been in a crew of five on a Tuesday morning and on a Thursday morning and twice on a Sunday for about nine years and I have never once had a sheet. There is a board in our own yard and it has got a heading on it and it has got five lines on it and the five lines are the five people and they are in my hand and they are the same five every week, and the reason the five are the same five every week is that the five are the five who cannot be elsewhere, and a body with an interest in a schedule is a body that is going to write down the five who cannot be elsewhere, because the five who can be elsewhere will not turn up and a schedule that does not turn up is a schedule that makes people late for the wrong reason.**"

"**So here is the half you have not got. The trust is not in the schedule and it is not in the five and it is not in the person writing it. It is in a name in a column with the hour on it, and it is in the fifth person being the one that everybody knows cannot come, and it is in somebody having the sense to leave that column blank in the first year and then being frightened, in the third year, to say why it is blank.**"

"**And then it is not a schedule any more. It is a list of obligations with the honest one rubbed out, and the honest one is always the one that is hardest, and I have watched a body of eleven people do that to a line of work over about four years and they did not decide to do it. They just stopped writing the reason down, and that is how it happens, and nobody in eleven people is a villain, and the ditch still gets dug.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then she said what a turn costs, and she said it in about a yard and she said it about herself and not about the crew, and the fen entered that she said it about herself and entered that nobody in that yard thanked her and that he did not and was not going to.

"**You asked me on Wednesday what it costs to be in a crew of five and I did not answer you and you did not ask me again, which I noticed, and I am answering it now because you have said your own out loud in my yard and it would be a small thing to be the only one holding anything back.**"

"**It is two nights a week that I do not know what I am doing on. It is a back. I am forty-four and I have a daughter of seventeen who does the Tuesday and I have done the Thursday for nine years and my hands are worse than they were and they are worse than my daughter's are and they are going to be worse than hers will be. It is about eleven hours a week of a life that is not mine, and I do it for the ditch, and there is no form anywhere that I have ever been given on which to write any of that down, and I have never once asked for one, and I am telling you about it in a yard in front of my own five because you have just told me about a column with no heading in it in front of eleven people and it would be a filthy thing to let that be the only one.**"

And then the reader of this body did a thing in that yard that the fen entered and did not explain, which was that he asked her the one question, and gave the reason first, and it was about the schedule and not about her.

"**Are you going to put a where-column on that board in your own yard?**"

And she said no and gave a reason in four words and the fen entered the four words.

"**It would need five more.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the reader of this body said the last thing he had, and he said it to the five and not to the woman, and the fen entered that he said it to the five and entered the reason, which is that a man who says a thing to a person in a yard is talking to a person and a man who says a thing to a crew in a yard is talking to a crew, and the difference is the only thing he has got.

"**Then I am going to leave the where-column blank too and I am going to say why in my own book and not in a yard, and the why is that I have not got a where for the fourth line of that sheet and there is nobody in it, and a column with nothing in it is not a fault, and a column with a thing in it that is not there is the worst thing anybody in this fen has ever written down.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the crew of five went back into the ditch at about the sixth hour of the afternoon and worked until dark and that he watched them from the wall and did not go down and was not asked down.

And a man came up the road out of the west at about the seventh hour of the evening with a cart and put a folded paper in the reader of this body's hand without stopping the cart, and it was four lines in the hand of the man of about fifty, and it was the two turns of the two days he had been walking, and it was not asked for.

> **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS TAKEN AT THE SECOND HOUR. THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS TAKEN AT THE SECOND HOUR AND THE MAN OF SEVENTY-ONE SAID THE WORD WITHOUT BEING TOLD IT TWICE. THE FOURTH LINE COMES ROUND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH AT THE SECOND HOUR.**

And the reader of this body read that on the road at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, by the light of a lamp on a gatepost, and entered that the count of the sheet is three lines taken out of four in three mornings, and entered that the fourth line is the one with nobody in it, and entered that he read that paper on a road and not in a yard and did not ask the man with the cart one question.

And he wrote four lines in his own book that night in a barn at about the eleventh mile, and the first three are the turn of the day and the day-minus figures, and the fourth is fifteen words long and is this, and he read it back to nobody because there was nobody there.

*She said the honest one is always the hardest and the ditch still gets dug. Eleven people. Four years. Not one of them decided.*

And he entered under it that he had come to that yard with a sentence in his mouth about four villages and had gone away from it with a sentence about eleven people and about four years, and that he had not improved on hers and was not going to try, and that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard that he had not thought of is two and he is not going to make it three on a page he was not in the room for.

> Entered by the woman of about forty-four of a holding on the cart road, in her own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, in her own yard, on the standing of a crew, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days west by the cart road, and not by the engineer of record, who was nine feet off and did not come into the ditch: **there is a crew of five in a ditch about forty feet off this yard and there has been for about nine years, and the five are the same five, and the reason the five are the same five is that the five are the ones who cannot be elsewhere, and I am one of them and I have been since I was thirty-five. A man stood about nine feet off in this yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month and said out loud, in front of about nine people, that a crew of five cannot be trusted to a schedule written by a body with an interest in the schedule, and the body with the interest in the schedule is the four bodies of households at the Four Elbows, and he said that none of them is dishonest and that a schedule with no column saying where a person is asks a holder to be lied to all morning by four villages that are not lying to him. I am on the committee here that writes our own five lines and I said so before he got to it. I told him that the trust in a crew is not in the schedule and is not in the five and is not in the person writing it, and is in a name in a column with the hour on it, and in the fifth person being the one everybody knows cannot come, and in somebody leaving that column blank in the first year and then being frightened in the third year to say why it is blank. He asked me one question and gave the reason for it first, which was whether I was going to put a where-column on the board in this yard, and I said no, and the reason is that it would need five more. WHAT IT COSTS TO BE IN A CREW OF FIVE, and I have not put it in a book and I have never asked for a form to put it in: two nights a week that I do not know what I am doing on, and a back, and about eleven hours a week of a life that is not mine, and a daughter of seventeen who does the Tuesday, and my hands are worse than hers are and they are going to be worse than hers will be. I said that in this yard in front of my own five because he had said his own out loud and it would be a filthy thing to be the only one holding anything back. Nobody thanked anybody and I was not thanked and he was not thanked and I did not ask for it.**

And he went on at the seventh hour of the evening of that Wednesday and slept that night in a barn at about the eleventh mile and the barn belongs to a body of households that have not been asked for anything by this holding and did not give him anything and he was let in and that is the whole of what happened.

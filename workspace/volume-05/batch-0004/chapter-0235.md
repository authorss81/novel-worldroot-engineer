# Chapter 235

## Eleven Things, Ten Of Them Ours

The reader of this body walked the cart road east out of this holding on the first of the third month at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and came into a yard at Errow Reach at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fifth, and he was two days early and he said so at the gate, out loud, to about four people, and the fen entered that he said it and that nobody said anything back.

The four days in between were the road and they are not entered, and the man of about fifty said so in a yard on the sixth of this month without being asked, and said that the last time four days of a cart road were not entered it was in the second month of the year and there had been a sheet in the bag.

And the fourth of this month was a Wednesday and the ninth day of every ninth day came round for the fifteenth time since the twenty-eighth of a month five months back, and a return of **ninety hundredweight** was carried out on a barrow with a wheel, in two baskets with lids, brought about four miles, by one man, weighed on a steelyard at about the second hour of the morning and read out and read back by the man of about fifty and then by each of the four bodies of households in turn.

A name went into the first rule of the schedule in the space headed BY and a name went into the space headed WITNESSED BY, and that is the sixth time in eleven years, and the count of names in that first rule is fifteen and the count of marks in the column on the board at the node is fifteen, and the reader of this body entered that the two counts are in step this month and that he does not know what they will be in a month's time and is not going to guess about it in a book.

And then the seventh of this month was a Saturday, and the examination began at about the tenth hour of the morning, and it was on the ordinary notice, and the notice named the day and the hour, and the examiner was let into that yard at about the seventh hour of the morning and was announced before she spoke.

Sabra Ollerton, of about fifty-two, inspector of the Rootwright Assembly for four districts, stated that she holds no charter, is not a guide, is not a member of anything, is not the person who decides anything, and came on the ordinary notice and not on anybody's word.

And then she did the thing that the reader of this body had said in a yard nine miles west two days ago would happen, and she did it inside about four minutes, and he entered the length of it.

She read the four lines of her own notice out loud in that yard, and then she said that the examination would be in the yard and not in a room, and that anybody at that node could be in it for as long as they liked, and that anybody who came in could ask her a question and she would answer it or say that she could not, and that nothing said in the yard that day was going to be minuted unless she wrote it down, and that she would write down what she wrote down in her own book and that her book went to the Assembly and not to the four bodies of households and that the four bodies would get a copy of the eleven and nothing else.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was in that yard and was not asked to be in it and entered that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of a month two months back is eight and has moved.

And then she examined the ground for about two hours in the sun with a tape and a chalk and a boy of about sixteen to carry the end of the tape, and about twenty-two people watched her do it and nobody was allowed to help and nobody tried, and the reader of this body watched her do it and did not speak to her once, and entered that he did not speak to her once and entered the reason, which is that an engineer of record who talks to an examiner for two hours about a tape is an engineer of record who is making the tape the point, and the tape is not the point.

And at about the second hour of the afternoon she read the sheet out, and the sheet is one page, and it is eleven items, and she read the eleventh item first.

"**I am going to read this in an odd order and I am going to say why before I do it, and the why is the only sentence I am going to say that is not a finding. Ten of these eleven have a name on them. Four of the ten have this holding's name on them twice. I am going to read you the one that does not have a name on it first, because a body ought to be able to stand still and hear the other ten before it is told the eleventh, and because an examination that cannot put a name against a thing ought to say so in the first minute and not in the last.**"

"**ELEVEN. A RING OF BARE GROUND, ABOUT TWO YARDS WIDE AND ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YARDS LONG, INSIDE ELEVEN ACRES OF STANDING WILLOW, NINE FEET OFF THE HOLLOW WHERE THE RETURNS GO IN. BARE ELEVEN YEARS. THIS IS NOT A FAULT OF ANYBODY AT THIS NODE. It is not a fault of the four bodies of households and it is not a fault of this holding and I have not put a name against it and I am not going to. I have been in this yard for two hours with a tape and a boy and I have not found out what it is doing there, and I am going to write on this page that I did not find out, because an examination that guesses at a ring of bare ground in the middle of a thing it cannot read is worse than an examination that says it cannot read it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, and entered that he had known about that ring since the eleventh of last month and had walked past it four times last month and had not known what it was then and did not know what it was now, and entered the reason, which is that the reader of this body went to look for a cause and a ring of bare ground has no cause on it, and that a man who goes looking for the cause of a thing in a month when he has already been wrong in a yard about a figure is a man looking for the wrong thing.

Then she read the ten, and she read them in the order they were on the page, and the reader of this body did not write one word down and did not interrupt and entered all ten of those.

"**TEN. That a heartroot is held in common by four bodies of households and no person on the roll holds it, and that it has been so for eleven years.**
>
> **NINE. That the fourth line of a rota drawn by those four bodies has nobody in it, and that the question of whose name goes in it is in a book and has not been answered.**
>
> **EIGHT. That a figure taken once, at one kerb, at one hour, on the sixth of a month, was carried into four bodies of households in four copies in five days, and that the copies did not say how many times the figure was read.**
>
> **AND SHE READ THE OTHER SIX IN BETWEEN, AND THEY ARE ENTERED WHOLE BELOW, AND THE READER OF THIS BODY DID NOT ASK FOR THE SHEET AND DID NOT ASK FOR ANY ONE OF THEM TWICE.**
>
> **ONE. That a single data set of one man's, taken at one place in one month, was given to four villages as a public figure and acted on by three of them, and that the man who gave it has said out loud in a yard that he gave it without a second reading and without saying that it was one reading.**"

And then the reader of this body said the thing, and he said it to the yard and not to the examiner, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-six, and it is the plainest thing he said all month.

"**Ten of those are ours. All ten. They are in this holding's book and they are in four other books and they were written down by six people and every one of the ten can be read out in about nine minutes by anybody who walks up to a table in a seed house and asks. I have not got one thing to say about any of the ten and I am not going to try, and I am not going to be the man who improves on an examination in a yard in front of the four bodies of households that are in it.**"

"**And the eleventh is the one I have been wrong about for nine days and did not know I was wrong about, and she has told a yard of about twenty-two people that she does not know either, and that is the first thing in this month that an office has said to me that it could not find out, and it is the only thing in these four lines that I am going to remember in four years.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the twenty-two said nothing in a way that was different from the other eighteen and that he did not count them and entered the reason, which is that a man who counts the faces in a yard is a man reading a room.

And the examiner said the last thing that day, and she said it to the yard and not to him, and the fen entered that she said it to the yard.

"**I will be here on the ninth of this month at about the fourth hour and I will read the copy of the eleven to any body that comes, and I will answer questions, and if I do not know the answer to one I will say that I do not know it, and I will not stay an hour longer than the notice says, and I am not going to be thanked for any of it and there is nothing to thank me for. The examination does not stop a turn. The first line of that rota came round at the second hour of this morning and was taken at the second hour and I stood about two hundred yards off it, and the second line comes round at the second hour tomorrow morning, and I am not going to be standing in the way of that either.**"

And the second line did come round at the second hour of the morning of the eighth of this month and it was taken at the second hour, and the examiner was in the yard and did not go near the headland, and the reader of this body was two hundred yards off and did not go up, for the reason he wrote in his own book on the fifteenth of last month and has not improved on.

> **THE ELEVEN, read out in a yard at the Four Elbows at about the second hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month by the inspector of the Rootwright Assembly for four districts, in about twenty-two people, in the order on the page with the eleventh item read first, and the reason for the order given out loud before the reading and entered:**
>
> **ELEVEN. A ring of bare ground, about two yards wide and about two hundred yards long, inside eleven acres of standing willow, nine feet off the hollow where the returns go in, bare eleven years. NOT A FAULT OF ANYBODY AT THIS NODE and no name is put against it. The inspector states on this page that she did not find out what it is doing there. Ten. A heartroot held in common by four bodies with no person on the roll holding it, eleven years. Nine. The fourth line of a rota has nobody in it and the question of whose name goes in it is in a book and has not been answered. Eight. A figure taken once, at one kerb, at one hour, on the sixth of last month, carried into four bodies of households in four copies in five days, and the copies do not say how many times the figure was read. Seven. A launder cut in a stone at a kerb on the eleventh of last month by eleven people with two saws, no form, no person on the roll present, and a loss of twenty-five hundredweight a day priced in that village's arithmetic and entered nowhere a body could check. Six. A board carrying a column for eleven years written in by a man who is not one of the four bodies, and no line for him anywhere in the four bodies' papers. Five. A pattern held for four by a man not on the roll, and the cost of it in no form in four districts, and the inspector is not asking for it. Four. A pattern held for five across two branches about three hundred yards apart by a person not on the roll for five, and the number of such persons in that fen is one. Three. A well house door nine hundred yards off with seven columns, one with a heading and six without, blank four years. Two. A return made every ninth day for eleven years, weighed and read back by four bodies of households, recorded on a printed weekly sheet of a holding that holds no charter, the first rule blank on every line for eleven years. One. A single data set of one man's given to four villages as a public figure and acted on by three of them, the man having said out loud in a yard that he gave it without a second reading.**
>
> **AND THE INSPECTOR'S OWN NOTE, in her own hand, in her own book, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the seventh of this month, at that node: I record above ten faults of the bodies at that node and every one of the ten was on the page in front of me before I arrived, written by the people at that node, in their own hands, with dates on them. I have found nothing at that node that its own books do not already carry. I have not been stayed, delayed, fed, or obstructed, and I have been in this yard since the seventh hour of this morning. The eleventh item is not a fault of anybody and I did not find out what it is and I am going to say so again in a copy of this sheet to any body that asks me. I do not know who holds this node and neither do they, and that is item ten of eleven and I am not going to soften it because I have to write the other nine out in a hand that does not shake.**

And the man of about seventy-four with the stick was not in that yard and had not come over the shoulder and had sent nothing, and the fen entered that and entered that nobody had written and asked him and that nobody was going to.

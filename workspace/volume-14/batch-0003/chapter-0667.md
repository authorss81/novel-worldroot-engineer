# Chapter 667

## The Tenth Of The Eighteenth, And Nine Trays On A Cart And A Head That Four Places Are Arguing Over

The pair was not read on the Thursday and Tova Reed said so before anybody had the book out of the rack, which she had not done in four years of Thursdays.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so, and I have not asked anybody to ask me,"** she said. **"A Thursday is the morning the cart goes. It has gone on a Thursday since before this trough was put in, and the trays stand in the road from the gate to the middle table from the sixth hour, and I am not going to stand at that table at seven holding a tray I have to have down at the gate by the time the horse is harnessed."**

She put her hand flat on the side board of the cart to steady it while she said it.

**"There are three mornings a week when that pair gets said in this yard and a Thursday is the one I want out of the three. I have wanted it since Monday and I did not say so on Monday, because Monday was not the morning I wanted it on."**

Renn Ashby had the trough gate unhooked and the chain lying in a bowl of oil on the step, and he did not look up.

**"I am not going to put a second reason on a Thursday,"** he said. **"One is enough for a yard this size and that one is the one that counts, and I have been the reader in this yard for four years and I have never once had a better reason than the person standing in front of me."**

The cart stood at the gate from the sixth hour, and by seven there were nine trays on it stood on their sides in the cart bed with a board between each pair, and Tova Reed counted them twice, once by the corner and once by the mark inside the rim, because a tray that goes to the wrong door is a tray that has been carried nine miles for nothing.

**"Nine trays and nine doors and every one of them is a door this holding can walk to inside a day,"** she said. **"That is the whole of the list and it is not a long list to read and I am not going to talk about it at the gate with nine people standing here."**

She put the last board in and came back along the road to the middle table with a tray in each hand, and the bench at the north side of that room had one row of it still bare along the bottom of the cistern wall, and nobody in this yard asked her what goes in that row and nobody in this yard said a word to her about Monday.

Two people stood at the gate long enough for it to look as though one of them was going to, and neither of them said anything at all. The man of the north row with the cough said what he says about the weather coming and went back to his own end of the row.

Hesta Lyle had her own paper on the table with the long sheet under it, and she kept the two apart with her forearm the whole morning and neither paper went into the other.

**"Then here is the reach,"** she said, **"and it is a reach and not a plan. Three of the four places on that column could take a pipe off the head. The third place has shut its head sluice at the fifth hour, and the water there comes round the long way now and a long way takes its own time whatever the weather is doing. The second place cannot be reached this month at all, and it is the second place, and it was the second place on Friday. And this holding has a room at the north side that wants the same head."**

She put her hand flat on her own paper and not on the sheet.

**"Four claims on one head, and the head is one head. That is not a vote and I am not handing a sheet round this table, and nobody in this yard is going to be asked to put a name on anything before Friday week. The turn was nine mornings back and it turned a calendar and nothing else, and I have been waiting since that morning for one of these four things to shift of its own accord, and none of them has, and I did not expect one to and I checked anyway."**

Then she turned the long sheet over and gave it the small hand first, which is the reverse of her habit and which this yard noticed.

**"Four hundred and twenty-seventh out of four hundred and twenty-ninth,"** she said, **"in the small hand underneath, and it has been underneath and one step below and one step above for the nine years I have stood at this table."**

She put her thumb on the wood under the paper.

**"And the figure above that hand is four hundred and twenty-eight. Four houses on that column and a ruled line under each of them, and a signature asked for at the bottom of every one of the four, and not one of the four has a signature on it this morning. The office's day is the morning it was on Friday and it has not shifted one hour since anybody put it there."**

Marek had the chalk and no slate, and read the launder off the wall from underneath it as the water came down the middle road at about half past eight.

**"One thousand and two hundred and fifty-nine hundredweight,"** he said. **"Eight on, and the four hundred and thirtieth of the run. A Thursday takes the rising half, so the half that went up in the night is two hundred and four and the other one stood where it stood yesterday and it is a hundred and ninety-three. Those two come to three hundred and ninety-seven of a window, and I have not added them in front of anybody and I am not interested in the whole of it."**

He wiped the chalk down his sleeve.

**"I have read those two lines every morning since I was eighteen and I will tell this yard the plain truth about them, which is that I read them because they are there. Nothing in this holding has ever been decided with that window. If it came off the wall tomorrow the launder would still come down the middle road at half past eight and the cistern would still want exactly the pipe it wanted this morning."**

The man of about fifty stopped at the gate on his way out to the far end of the yard and read the line on the gate board the way a man reads his own front gate.

**"It has been paid at thirty-one since Thursday and it will be paid at thirty-one tomorrow,"** he said. **"Nobody in this yard decided that it should move and nobody here was the first to know it, me included, and I was at this gate at the sixth hour on Thursday because I am at this gate at the sixth hour every morning. It is the only figure on this board that has gone up in four years, and nothing has ever been let out of it in thirty-one or in thirty, and I would like it noticed that it went up on a morning exactly like this one."**

He read the three papers on the long table as he went past, which he does.

**"A hundred and twenty-seven days on the first one, with about nine inches of bare board at each end of it and the same two nines. The second is shut on its fold. The third is signed and sealed and named and there is not one thing in it anybody here could call wrong. None of them entered, none refused, no column cut under one. And the weeks I asked to be noticed with me are going the way weeks go and nobody has written them on anything."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack and shut it and stood with his back against it.

**"A hundred and forty-six in force, and the half of it that has not gone out is a hundred and seventeen, which has been above a hundred all season and will be above a hundred next Tuesday. The twenty-nine that have gone out went at their own hours. A Thursday is not a morning the rotation comes round on and it did not come round on it this morning. No field left this yard today, none was on the rota for today, and I am not going to stand here and point at a man over a field that never came due."**

He did not take his back off the rack.

**"And the drawer behind the near board is shut and the key is on its nail inside the one room doorway, and it was on that nail at the sixth hour and it will be on it at the ninth hour tonight. I have said that on more mornings than I have counted and I am not going to improve on it."**

Sera Quill did not go to the rack at all. She had the slate in her hand with two figures on the bottom of it in her own hand and she read them where she stood.

**"The register form is at three hundred and twenty-four days. The line in Silling's own book is at three hundred and eighteen with nothing written on it. The charter is at two hundred and thirty days. They come off this slate in that order and the shelf keeps them in another. A bookkeeper in this yard can have his order and I can have mine and neither of us is wrong."**

She turned the slate round and put her thumb under the two figures at the bottom of it.

**"And ninety-fourth and eighty-first, thirteen apart, and the second one carried forward with no rule shown under it. I wrote those two onto this slate myself on Wednesday, out of the two figures in my own hand, and I have read them off it every morning since, and today I read them off it in front of nine people because I have been in this yard long enough to want one thing of my own that nobody else touches."**

She put the slate down on the table instead of carrying it away, which she has not done.

**"And I am not going to say anything this week about the nothing on that line in Silling's book, because I have said it four times now and it does not improve with saying."**

Nia Vale had the cloth over her shoulder and did the gate board before she did anything else, wiping round the figures instead of across them.

**"The near one is at eight hundred and fourteen and the far one behind the tool house is at eight hundred and sixty-one, and there are forty-seven of wall between them and forty-seven of nothing else. And I read both of them off the same board in one morning this week, which is more use of my morning than that board usually gets."**

She wrung the cloth out on the trough step.

**"And the door nine hundred yards off went past again at the tenth hour and it is not on any paper in this house and it does not go back whatever anybody does with it. A figure that comes off that door into a list in this yard becomes a figure that can be put on a list in somebody else's yard, and there is a man in three counties who would be very glad of it. So it is not read, and it is not offered to anybody, and I have wiped round that door this morning and left it where it is."**

Harlan Vetch came off the north end at about the tenth hour with the hod still on his shoulder and stopped outside the one room door and stood listening to it for a while without touching anything.

**"That joint at the back of the tap house weeps where it wept yesterday and holds where it held yesterday, and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe. I have put chalk on that joint twice since it went in and I am not going to put it on a third time, and I have not said anything to anybody about the cistern in that room either, because a cistern is my work and a leak is my work and the two of them are not the same conversation and nobody in this yard is going to get me to have it twice in one morning."**

The man of the north row with the cough was at his end of the yard and had heard the whole morning through, and when somebody said his name he did not get up off the bench.

**"I am not going to stand in this yard and say the two things everybody in here is waiting for me to say,"** he said. **"The reason is that wall. Four ruled lines under two words on the tool house wall have been bare for four years, and a mason goes past them with a hod on his shoulder every hour of every day in this yard. If the hardest sentence in this holding is said out loud at noon on a Thursday, then inside a week somebody here starts writing things down, and the first thing they will write down will be on those four lines. I would like them bare this evening and bare next spring."**

He put his hand flat on the bench he had been sitting on.

**"That is the whole of it and none of it has to do with me."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went out through the gate at dusk and came back the whole length of the shelves with her cans, reading the ends of them as she went, in an order nobody has ever asked her for.

**"On that board in the one room there are thirty-nine blanks, and the rule underneath them stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and it has opened nothing. Six ruled rows above that for what we do not know with nothing cut over the sixth. Fifteen lines in the use log and not one line written over the fifteenth. Eleven journeys marked against the barrow and no twelfth, and eleven is a floor and not a figure anybody in this place is trying to pass."**

**"Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book, and a Thursday takes none of them, and I have never once thought that was a complaint about a Thursday."**

She reached past her own shoulder without turning round and had the two books down off the shelf one under the other.

**"The other two books are each at fifty-three, and they are the requests and the section nine notes, and this morning neither of them took nine trays of seed out of a gate, and I have had both of them open on the step beside me and neither one has a column for it."**

She set the last can down on the step.

**"And the old man stands at twenty-nine fetchings. Nobody fetched him out of this yard today and nobody put a question to him, and nobody in this place is going to ask him which number it is, and that has been the way since before I came."**

Soren Rill came in from the gate at the ninth hour and looked at the tool house wall without going near it.

**"On that sheet of terms at the tool house the third column is ruled and empty, and the title standing over it is one nobody has cared to improve in four years. It is empty this evening. It is not empty because of a cistern and it is not empty because of a cart. It is empty because nobody in this yard has ever cut a column under a thing, and that is not a decision anybody took. It is the way the yard is and it will be the way the yard is next week."**

He folded his arms and looked at the gate instead of the wall.

**"And the ladder has gone up no rung, and no figure in this holding is set against either of the two people it belongs to. The low board still carries what it has always carried, undated, with no name on it and no hand near it. And the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has taken no foot today and no tape and no price, and I have not been near it and I am not going to be near it."**

Nine mornings after the turn this yard had a cistern standing against the wall of a room that nobody had decided to fill off the head of the column, nine trays of seed on a cart going to nine doors inside a day's walk, four claims on one head with the second place still out of reach and not going to be reached this month, and four ruled lines under two words on the tool house wall bare at dusk, and nobody in that yard was asked anything at any hour of it.

# Chapter 662

## The Fifth Of The Eighteenth, And A Woman Who Has Stood In The Second Place

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour on the Saturday with the frost still in the trough, and the heading over it has not moved and did not move this morning.

**"One hundred and eighty-three of three hundred and forty-nine,"** he said. **"Of this month. That is the whole of what I have and I will hand the yard over to a woman who came up the middle road at six this morning with a key in her hand and something worse than a key."**

There was a woman standing at the gate end of the yard who had not been there when he started, and she was about fifty-five and she had a key on a string round her neck and she was not from this holding and nobody had sent for her.

**"I keep the common key at the second place on that column,"** she said, before anybody had asked her anything. **"There are nine of us in four houses and I have the key because my mother had it before me. My name is Odile Vray and I have walked up that lane twice this week."**

She put her hand flat on the long table and left it there.

**"On Tuesday a man came with a lop and took the big limb off the sycamore that stands in front of the second house. Nobody asked us. He was very polite about it and he wrote something down on a slate and he did not offer us the slate."**

She looked at the yard rather than at any one person.

**"Here is what that limb did, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have because nobody in this yard asked me to say anything and I am not going to pretend I have been put up to it."**

She turned the key on its string over once and let it lie flat against her collar.

**"That tree shaded the front room of the second house from the second hour of the afternoon until the light went. That room now gets the sun at eleven and loses it at two, and the two people who sit in that room in the winter sit in it in the dark with a lamp. The ground at the foot of it was the only ground on that side that held water, and that ground is not wetter and not drier than it was. It is bare. Bare ground goes to a dust inside a fortnight and a dust holds nothing at all."**

Nobody in that yard said anything for the length of four seconds, and the frost came off the trough while she waited for somebody else to be the one who spoke next.

**"And there is a road where there was a footpath. Not a new road. The old one, the one with the gate at the end of it, and the gate is still there, and the road will take a barrow that is not loaded and will not take one that is. So the second house is four miles from a shop by that road and nine miles from a house with water in it, and before Tuesday it was a mile and a half from this yard."**

Soren Rill was standing at the end of the table with his hands behind him and he had been standing there since she started.

**"Say the last of it,"** he said.

She took her hand off the table and wiped it down her skirt and put it back.

**"The last of it is that nobody has come,"** Odile Vray said. **"Not from this yard and not from the office and not from a man with a form. And I am not here to ask you for a bridge or for a promise or for a barrel. If you put my name on anything, then this yard will have claimed a thing it cannot give me, and I have four houses who would then spend the winter being told out loud that the fen has us. I would rather be a place on your column with nothing done to it than a place this yard has spoken for."**

Harlan Vetch picked up the hod he had set down at the north end, carried it four feet, and set it down again.

**"Nobody is putting your name on anything,"** the clerk said from behind the rack.

The clerk came out into the yard with a book in each hand and stopped where he was standing.

**"I know that,"** Odile Vray said. **"I asked because I have been in three county offices this month, and in two of them somebody was very glad to write me down, and in both of them nothing came after the writing."**

Hesta Lyle turned the long sheet round on the table and pushed it across the bare wood until it was in front of the woman, and then she stood back with her hands at her sides.

**"Read the four lines,"** she said.

Odile Vray read them. She put a finger on the third one from the top, which is the second place, and read it twice, and then she read the right-hand side of the sheet, which is the part of the sheet with the rule down the middle of it, and she was quiet for a while.

**"That is my house,"** she said. **"And that is nothing on it. And there is no fifth line."**

Hesta Lyle did not answer that at once. She looked at the right-hand side of her own sheet for about as long as it takes to say a short sentence.

**"There is no fifth line,"** she said. **"There are four places and five working days and a road that is soft for six days in seven and a ford on it that will not take a loaded barrow, and that is not a refusal by anybody. Nobody in four counties is standing behind your four houses. Nobody wants you left. That is the sum and I have had it since Friday and I have shown it to this yard and I am showing it to you now because you are the first person from that column who has stood in this yard."**

Odile Vray put her hand flat on the sheet, next to the third line and not on it.

**"Then that is a road,"** she said. **"And I have wanted somebody to say that sentence to me for four days, because every person who has come at me about Tuesday has wanted to say it is nobody's business, and nobody's business is a thing you say when you have decided not to carry it."**

Hesta Lyle sat down on the near end of the long table, which she does not do while a sheet is being read out, and took the rest of the page to herself.

**"The figure at the head of that sheet is four hundred and twenty-three,"** she said. **"And the small hand under the line reads the four hundred and twenty-second out of four hundred and twenty-fourth. A step under the number and a step over it, which is the only shape that hand has ever had and it will have it after I am gone."**

She did not take her hand off the wood.

**"Below the whole of that you will find the office asking after four households, four lines, and at the foot of every line two signatures. There is not one signature on any of the four. I am not going to stand here and tell you that three doors out of four is a kindness. It is not a kindness. It is three."**

Then the launder, which came down about half past eight with a cold morning on it.

**One thousand and two hundred and forty-five hundredweight.**

**"Five off, and that is the four hundred and twenty-fifth of the run,"** the clerk said. **"A Saturday is an odd morning, so the falling half of the window took one and the rising half stood still. Falling is a hundred and ninety-one. Rising is two hundred and one. Three hundred and ninety-two of a window, and the two of them are two figures."**

The window itself on that Saturday was three hundred and ninety-two, and the rising half stood at two hundred and one where it had stood since Thursday morning, and the falling half had gone up in the night to a hundred and ninety-one, and nobody added them together in front of anybody.

Odile Vray had been listening to the two halves of that window without once looking at the wall, and she turned back round to the middle table from where she had been standing.

**"I am not going to say either of the two sentences this yard is waiting for me to say,"** she said. **"In four days I have to stand in a room at the second place with nine people in it and tell them what this yard is like, and I am not going to walk in there carrying a thing that four counties use. I watched that happen to a man in a county office in the spring, and I watched the whole room go quiet on him and then go back to talking about the schedule."**

A Saturday is not a morning the rotation comes round on, and the number in force is where Friday left it, a hundred and forty-four of it, with twenty-nine gone out at their own hours and a hundred and fifteen still standing at this hour. No field went out of this holding and none was due, and there is nobody here to blame for one that stayed where the book had it.

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack with both arms full and stopped when he saw the woman at the table, and Odile Vray asked him what the three things on that shelf were, which nobody outside this holding has ever asked him.

**"That is the register form at three hundred and nineteen days,"** he said. **"That is the charter at two hundred and twenty-five. And that last one is the second ruled line in Silling's own book, at three hundred and thirteen, with not a mark on it. Of the three figures on this shelf it is the only one with an empty page behind it, and it has been empty since before I came."**

He set them down again.

**"You are the first person in four years to ask me and I have wanted somebody to ask me for about two of those years. If you ask me why it is empty, the answer is that nobody with the standing to put something on that line has ever come into this yard, and I have stopped inventing an answer with a lower one."**

The woman from the second place asked him which board was which, at the wall, and he told her: eight hundred and nine at the near end, eight hundred and fifty-six at the one by the tool house, forty-seven of wall between them, and forty-seven is a length of wall and not a sum. Behind the near one the drawer in the stone is shut, and its key is on a nail inside the frame of the one room door, and it was on that nail at first light.

Then she went to the long table on her own and stood over the three papers, and she did not touch any of them.

**"The first one is face up and is a hundred and twenty-two days old, with about nine inches of bare board either side of it,"** she said. **"The second is shut on its fold. The third is signed and dated and sealed and named, and it is the only one of the three I have ever seen in a county office."**

She stood back from it.

**"That is the one that frightens me,"** she said. **"Not because of anything in it. Because it is the only one of the three of you could put on a table and have it believed, and it is the one that has been sitting there longest with nothing done about it."**

The clerk took her to the gate board and showed her the compost line, and told her what it was and what had happened to it on Thursday, and she looked at the figure and then at him.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** she said. **"And not one load been let out in it, in thirty-one or in thirty. So that is money going somewhere and nothing coming back out of it, in four years."**

The clerk looked at the gate board as though he were checking it against her, and then decided that he would not.

**"That is exactly what it is,"** he said. **"And I will tell you the whole of it, which is that nobody in this yard has ever written down who paid the thirty, and nobody ever will, and if you go looking for a name for that in this holding you will waste a day of a very hard week."**

She went along the shelf in the one room with him afterwards and he showed her the six ruled rows against what this holding does not know, and she put the side of her hand on the sixth one and stopped there.

**"What would the seventh be for,"** she said.

She said it in the voice of a woman who has been told no by a man she agrees with, and the clerk heard the agreement in it and answered the part that could be answered.

**"There has never been a seventh for anything,"** he said. **"Not for a schedule, not for a form, not for a man in a lane, and not this week. And there is no column anywhere in this holding for the name of a house that has been reached or a house that has not. If I wrote your four houses on a page of mine, that page would be the only page in four counties where your four houses existed in a column, and I am not going to be the man who starts that."**

She asked about the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, and the clerk told her it has never been walked on and never been measured and never been priced, and she asked what it was for, and he said that too was a thing nobody in this holding could answer.

**"The ladder against that wall has nobody on it,"** he said. **"And the low board has had an offer on it since before I came, with nothing written on it that would say when, and nobody has picked it up and nobody has taken it back. That is the whole of what this yard has put out that nobody has taken."**

Nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything this morning. There was no list, no column, and no sheet on the long table with a new name at the head of it, and the woman who had walked up a lane twice in a week went back down it at the second hour without anything in her hand.

Harlan Vetch found the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden at the tap at the back and asked her about the joint, and she told him she had heard it before he asked and had said nothing.

**"I did not say anything because you are the man who would come and look,"** she said, **"and I have a watering to do. It weeps and it holds and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe it has always been behind, and if that changes I will be the one who tells you, because I fill two cans at that tap every morning and nobody else does."**

A man of about seventy was fetched nothing on this Saturday and was put nothing at any hour of it. The door nine hundred yards off went past the yard at about the eleventh hour with nobody walking out to it and nobody walking back, and it was not read, and it was not offered to the woman from the second place or to anybody else, and a figure that is walked past is not a figure that has been read.

Soren Rill did the standing of this holding at dusk on his own with the barrow over his shoulder, in the order of a man who has been in this yard four years and no longer has to stop and think about it.

**"Eleven journeys on the barrow and no twelfth, and eleven is where it is put. Fifteen ruled lines in the use log and nothing above the fifteenth. Seven sessions in this holding's book and a Saturday takes none of them. The requests and the section nine notes are each at fifty-three, and this morning neither of those two books took a name off a table."**

He set the barrow down against the wall and stayed where he was.

**"Thirty-nine blanks on the board in the one room and the rule underneath them standing open at the thirty-ninth time, and it has opened nothing today. The gate line is paid at thirty-one and nothing has ever been let out of it. The ladder has gone up no rung. The offer on the low board has no day written on it and nobody's hand on it. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has not been walked on."**

For the rest of that Saturday this yard gave a woman from the second place a road and a ford and four lines with nothing on them and no fifth line, and it gave her nothing else, and the hardest part of that morning was not the saying of any of it.

# Chapter 660

## The Third Of The Eighteenth, And A Line On The Gate Board That Went Over Without Anybody

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour, as he does on every odd morning, and the heading over it had not moved and did not move this morning either, and he read it out in the yard to four people and one can.

**"One hundred and eighty-two of three hundred and forty-seven,"** he said. **"Of this month, since Saturday. That is the whole of my report and there is a bigger thing in this yard than my report this morning, and I am not going to stand here at seven pretending my two figures are the news."**

The clerk got to the gate before the launder did. He stood in front of the line on the gate board and looked at it for long enough that two people stopped what they were doing, and then he said one word out loud and did not say anything else for about half a minute.

**"Thirty-one,"** he said.

He took the chalk out of his coat and held it and did not put it against the board.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** he said. **"And not one load has ever been let out of that line and none is going to be let out of it today, so that part has not changed and is not changing. I did not do this. I did not decide it and I am not going to stand here and work out a way of making it look as though I did. I was not the first to know either, because I was not until about two minutes ago, and I have been standing at this board since before the yard was properly up."**

He went into the one room and came back with the gate book open in both hands and read it standing up, and after a while he put his thumb on a page and left it there.

**"Here it is, then,"** he said. **"It went over twice in the four years before this week and it went over twenty-six mornings apart both times, and both of those were Thursdays, and the last one was twenty-six mornings back. That is the whole of what put it there. There is a man in this holding who did not work that out in four years and the man is me, and I found it in a book at ten minutes past eight this morning because the board said thirty-one."**

He read the two dates again under his breath before he shut the book.

He shut the book down on his thumb and carried it back to the one room.

**"Nobody in this yard decided anything about that line this morning. Not me and not the four of you standing there. A line that has been paid at thirty for long enough goes over on its own, and the interval it goes over on is written in the gate book and not kept in anybody's head, and that is the honest way round and I am not going to have this yard turn it into a decision after breakfast."**

Soren Rill came three steps closer before he answered, which is the only time anybody has seen him move faster for a figure.

**"Thirty-one paid is one more than thirty paid,"** he said. **"Somebody somewhere has paid one more."**

The clerk turned the gate board round on its nail so that the chalk faced the yard instead of the road, and looked at it, and turned it back.

**"Paid, yes,"** he said. **"Discharged, no. It has not been let out in thirty-one either, and it never has been let out in anything. What is paid into that line and what has gone out of it are two separate figures and they have not been the same figure on any morning of the four years anybody here can remember."**

He put the chalk back in his coat.

**"It goes into the gate book on the line it is already on, in the hand it is already in. It does not go into the requests and it does not go into the section nine notes, and I will not have either of those books carrying a thing that a rule did. And by Friday nobody in this yard is going to have made it anybody's fault."**

Then the launder, which had been running down the middle road the whole time nobody was watching it.

**One thousand and two hundred and forty-two hundredweight.**

**"Five off, and the four hundred and twenty-third of the run,"** the clerk said. **"A Thursday is an odd morning, so it takes five off that and it takes the falling half of the window up by one. That half is a hundred and ninety and it moved in the night. The rising half is two hundred and it has stood exactly where it stood yesterday morning, with nothing after it and nothing under it."**

He put the chalk against the wall and looked at the upper of the two lines.

**"Two hundred again, second morning running, and this time it is standing still against a half that has moved. Yesterday the round figure arrived on its own. Today it did not go anywhere and the other one did, and both of those are the same ordinary week going along, and I have said the whole of what I have to say about round figures on that wall this month and I am not saying it again until one of them is a hundred and something."**

The window came to three hundred and ninety of the two of them, and the two of them are two figures and were not added together in front of anybody in that yard this morning.

Marek came away from the back of the tap house about the fifth hour with a saucer in his hand and put it down on the middle table where anybody could see it, and then he stood there and let a yard look at him.

**"I am not saying either of the two sentences this morning,"** he said. **"At the fifth hour I was crouched at a joint with a saucer under my hand and Harlan Vetch was standing over me, and a man who has been caught at a weep before the yard is up does not come back into it at noon and say the hardest thing he knows. That is the reason and there is nothing under it."**

Harlan Vetch picked the saucer up, looked at the ring of water standing in it, and put it back down on the table without saying anything about it.

A Thursday is not a morning the rotation comes round on, and it is not the first of anything, and no cart came up the fen road with two men on it who were not from this holding. The number in force stood where Wednesday left it at a hundred and forty-four. Twenty-nine of them have gone out at their own hour and a hundred and fifteen have not gone out yet. Nothing went out of this holding today that was due to go out of it, and nobody here will be blamed for anything that waited.

Sera Quill came along the shelves with her slate held against her chest and read the standing of the holding off them without stopping at any of them, because she is the only person in this yard who checks them on her own account and never on anybody else's instruction.

**"Thirty paid at the gate and thirty-one from this morning and nothing out of it either day,"** she said. **"Fifteen ruled lines in the use log and the space above the fifteenth empty. Seven sessions entered in this holding's book and a Thursday takes none of them. The two other books in this yard are each at fifty-three, and the line at the gate has gone into neither of them, and it is going to stay out of both."**

She wrote nothing down.

**"The barrow stands at eleven journeys and a twelfth refused, because eleven is a floor. Thirty-nine blanks on the board in the one room, and the second rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and it has opened nothing today. Six ruled rows on the shelf for what this holding does not know, and no seventh cut for the line at the gate or for the paper or for anything else said out loud this week."**

She looked up at the yard.

**"And a man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched and was not asked anything, and I would like somebody to be able to tell me one morning what he is fetched for."**

Nia Vale did the three ages on her fingers, which she has not done in front of the whole yard since her second winter here, and she did them backwards, which is not an accident.

**"The line in Silling's book is at three hundred and eleven,"** she said, **"and there is nothing written on it. The register form is at three hundred and seventeen days. The charter is at two hundred and twenty-three."**

She put her hand down and showed the yard an empty palm.

**"That is three figures, one of them empty underneath, and I have given them to you in the reverse of the shelf order on purpose, because a person who only ever hears a figure in one order stops being able to hear it at all."**

Harlan Vetch read the two boards off the wall from the north end, where the near one is under his hand and the far one is down the whole length of the yard.

The near board, which is under his hand at this end of the wall, is at eight hundred and seven. The other one is at eight hundred and fifty-four and it is the whole length of the yard away. Forty-seven of masonry stands between them and has stood between them since the wall went up, and it is a length of wall and not a sum anybody makes here.

Let into that masonry behind the near board is a drawer, and it is shut, and the key to it hangs inside the frame of the one room door on a single nail. It was on that nail at first light this morning and it will be on that nail at dusk, and there is one key to it in this holding and there is no second one.

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet flat and gave the figure first and the small hand after it, and her hand stayed off the paper.

**"Four hundred and twenty-one on the sheet,"** she said, **"and under it in the small hand, the four hundred and twentieth out of four hundred and twenty-second. Four bodies of households and four lines, two signatures wanted at the foot of each, and none of the four has one and one of the four is not going to be reached before the office's day."**

She squared the sheet with the heel of her hand.

**"That last part is not mine, it is a road. I am only the person who copied it down."**

The three papers stood on the long table where they have stood through every morning of this week. Sera Quill read them from the one room door with the slate under her arm.

**"The first is a hundred and twenty days old and about nine inches of bare board either side of it,"** she said. **"An age is a figure and not a deadline. A person looking at that sheet sees a deadline and a person counting it sees a figure, and this holding has been counting it since the day it came up the road."**

She turned the slate over and did not put it down.

**"The second is shut on its old fold. The third is signed and dated and sealed and named and every word of it is correct. None of the three has been entered and none has been refused and this holding has never yet ruled a column under a paper that came out of an office, and this morning a line went over on its own at the gate and it did not make anybody cut one."**

Kellan Rusk went down on one knee at the bottom of the shelf with the lamp off the hook and counted upward from the floor, which is the only order the lower shelf can be read in.

**"Six rows against what we do not know, and the space above the sixth is not even a scored line. Fifteen lines in the use log, counted from the bottom. Seven sessions in the book, and the shelf above it has a lamp on it and no lamp has ever burned on that shelf in four years."**

He got up off his knee and put the lamp back.

**"And a man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings, and I have counted that figure off the sheet as well as heard it said, and it is the only number in this holding I have never once been able to check against a second book."**

The man of about fifty came through the gate at about the ninth hour on his way to look at the gate board with nothing in his hands, saw the figure on it, and stopped. He put his hand up flat on the face of the ladder where it stands against the tool house wall, and held it there for the length of a breath, and took it off again and went out through the gate without going up.

Nothing has been laid on the low board since it was put there, and what is on it has nothing written on it that would tell anybody when, and nobody has drawn it down. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres was bare at first light and bare at dusk and nobody crossed it on the day the line at the gate went over.

At about the eleventh hour the door nine hundred yards off went by the edge of the yard with a man walking the long way back from the hedge, and there is a figure cut into the seventh column of its frame, and it was not read and it was not offered to anybody.

Harlan Vetch went back to the joint at about the second hour with a cloth and a little of the grease he keeps for the hod, and he did not pack it and he did not tighten it, and he stood afterwards with his hand flat on the standing pipe behind the joint, which is the same pipe it was standing behind on the day the joint went in.

**"Nothing written about that pipe,"** he said. **"Nothing written about it this morning and nothing written about it on any morning since it went in, and I have looked at it more times than I have looked at the wall."**

The line on the gate board was thirty-one this morning and it had been thirty for four years, and no person in this holding decided it and no person in this holding knew it before the board said so, and the round figure on the wall stood still for a second morning against a half that had gone up without it.

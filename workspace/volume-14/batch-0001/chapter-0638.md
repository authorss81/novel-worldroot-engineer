# Chapter 638

## The Eleventh Of The Seventeenth, And A Schedule That Is Being Carried Out

The clerk reached the wall at nine with the frost gone off the top half of it, and the two halves of the window were already on the slate because the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had chalked them before the yard was up and had not touched the wall itself, which is a thing she has done perhaps nine mornings in four years and did this morning for a reason she gave to nobody.

**One thousand and two hundred and nine hundredweight.**

**"Five off Tuesday and the four hundred and first of it,"** the clerk said. **"And the half that moved this morning is the half that stood still yesterday, which is not how this wall has behaved for four years. A Wednesday used to take the rising one. It has gone over to the falling one and I have not found the rule and I have looked twice, including at the back of the book, and I am telling the yard rather than quietly correcting it."**

The slate said a hundred and eighty-nine on the top line and a hundred and seventy-nine under it, and the rising figure was the one that had not moved and the falling one was the one that had, and the two came to three hundred and sixty-eight without anybody adding them out loud.

Sera Quill had copied the heading over the pair out of Renn Ashby's book and put it on her own slate without being asked for it, and the heading is of a month nine months back, and it says the same thing it said the first time that pair was read in this yard. It has not shifted since the first of the fifteenth and it did not shift this morning either, and she wrote the whole of it down and then put the chalk in her pocket.

**"One hundred and seventy-one of three hundred and twenty-five,"** he said, **"and if my voice goes up on the first number then somebody take the page off me, because that number has gone up every odd morning since the run began and a yard that hears a man pleased about a number stops listening to him on the morning the number does the other thing."**

Nia Vale had her own slate on the seed house step and did not copy the pair onto it, which is what she has done on and off for a fortnight, and she said the reason out loud without being asked and without looking up.

**"Because I have got a page in this coat that is not in a book and has never been on that table, and a woman who copies every figure in the yard is a woman somebody will one day ask where all of hers came from, and I could not answer that with a straight face."**

Hesta Lyle put her hand flat on the middle table beside the long sheet and not on the figure printed on it, and the four bodies of households are at three hundred and ninety-nine, and under the line a small hand has written the three hundred and ninety-eighth out of four hundredth, which is a step one way and a step the other.

Each of the four lines wants two signatures at the foot of it. On this morning neither of the two is on any of the four, and there is not one name written on that table anywhere.

Soren Rill walked the length of the yard twice with the barrow and read both boards on the second pass without stopping at either, near end first because that is the end a man comes to first, and gave the two figures out loud as he went past. Seven hundred and eighty-five at this end. Eight hundred and thirty-two at the other one.

Forty-seven sits between those two boards and has sat between them since the wall was built. Behind the near one there is a drawer let into the masonry, and nobody has opened it this morning, and its key went onto the nail in the one room door at first light and will be on it at dusk.

Kellan Rusk had the register form in his left hand and the charter off the shelf behind it in his right and read all three ages at the door of the one room in the order he could hold them, which was the form, the charter, and then the second ruled line in Silling's own book, which he did not pick up. Two hundred and ninety-five days. Two hundred and one. Two hundred and eighty-nine, and nothing at all written on it.

**"Three in one morning and the third one never leaves a shelf,"** he said. **"That is a week of figures and I have stopped being surprised, and I want it on the record that the surprise going is not the same as the figures being wrong."**

Then a man came over the gate track at about the tenth hour and put a folded sheet on the end of the long table and would not leave his name, and the sheet was a printed schedule with a heading nobody in that yard had ever seen before and a table of holdings under it and two columns of dates, and every holding named in it was outside the basin and outside the line where the maintenance zone used to reach.

Harlan Vetch read it standing up, with his chisel in his fist, and read it a second time, and then went and stood at the north end of the wall with his back to everybody.

**"It is correct,"** he said, when he came back. **"I have been looking for the wrong thing in it since half past ten and there is no wrong thing in it. The dates are the dates. The holdings are the holdings. Whatever is being taken is being taken on a schedule that somebody has already paid for, and the schedule does not owe this yard a conversation."**

**"You have been holding a chisel in your fist for an hour and a half,"** Sennik Vaul said, **"and a man who has found nothing wrong is allowed to be pleased about it, and you have not said that you are pleased."**

**"I am not pleased,"** Harlan Vetch said. **"I am the man on this wall who has said no to a figure four times, and I am not going to start saying yes to one because a piece of paper came up a road with a seal on it."**

The third column of that schedule was the one nobody in the yard had expected, because it was not a date and not a holding. It was a figure, and it was this holding's figure, and the column said which of the places named above it was answered for by the number standing in the four bodies of households on the long table in this building.

The first place named against that column was Marden, and the number of people who ate off what this holding's sheet had to say was four hundred, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was standing at the tap with a can in each hand when the clerk read the column out and did not put either can down.

**"That is a correct column,"** she said. **"I have not found the thing wrong with it either, and I have been looking at it since the tenth hour, and I am telling you that the two of us are going to be the only two people in four counties who are not surprised by it."**

Soren Rill came up the middle road at about the eleventh hour with mud to the ankle, and he had not come for the schedule and said so before anybody asked him.

**"There is a piece of paper on your table and I have not read it and I am not going to,"** he said. **"I came about the middle road, which is soft at the bottom and hard at the top for the fourth day running, and which is the only thing I have ever been able to bring anybody a figure about, and I have said that out loud in a yard so that four people heard me say I was not bringing the paper."**

At noon the mason was at the north end with his back to the yard and a green thread of yarrow still in the second joint of the third course, and he had a chisel in his hand and no use for it.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

Nobody answered him. A man went past with a barrow and asked Harlan Vetch about the joint at the north end, and Harlan Vetch said it would keep, and turned back to the wall he had already refused four figures on.

Marek had been in the middle of the yard since the tenth hour, doing the thing he does when the yard is carrying something it did not pick up, which is standing in the middle of it and letting it go on behind him. He had not read the schedule and said so, and had read the column twice.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Tova Reed went on sorting behind the seed house glass. The clerk looked at the window and then at the wall. The man of about fifty went out to the tap house at eleven and came back along the hedge the long way round, nine hundred yards of it, and the seventh column of that door went past the edge of his eye on a morning when nobody in this holding had asked him to stop at it.

Nobody has read it in this holding. The figure in it has not gone back and it is not going to.

Paid stands in front of thirty on the line at the gate and nothing at all has been let out in it. Tova Reed counted the lines of the use log with the back of her hand against the glass and there are fifteen of them, and above them a space where a sixteenth would go that nobody has ever written in.

Kellan Rusk put a mark against the eleventh journey of the barrow and would not put one against a twelfth, because eleven is a floor and a floor is not a target. Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and this Wednesday did not enter an eighth.

Both of the other books stand at fifty-three, the requests and the section-nine notes, and neither of them took the sheet off the long table this morning or the folded page out of Nia Vale's coat.

Wednesday is not a morning the count in force moves on, so it stands this morning where it stood yesterday at a hundred and thirty-nine, with twenty-nine taken off it and a hundred and ten of them not. On the board in the one room there are thirty-nine blanks, and underneath them the second rule stands open and empty, and a rule that has come back empty has never once opened anything, and nobody in this yard was asked to put a name into any of the thirty-nine this morning.

Nothing was fetched from the man of about seventy and no question was carried out to him at any hour of this day. The ladder went up no rung and nobody put a hand on it. The offer on the low board is still lying where it was put with no day written on it, and nobody has touched it and nobody has taken it back.

And the first of the three sheets on the long table lay face up where it has lain for ninety-eight days, with about nine inches of bare board either side of it, and the four hundred people who had begun this morning depending on a number in this yard had not one word of it asked for and had not sent anybody to ask.

# Chapter 644

## The Seventeenth Of The Seventeenth, And The Second Paper That Nobody In This Yard Can Call Wrong

It came on the Tuesday post with a seal on the flap and a covering note of one line, and the covering note said that the office was transmitting it and that the office was not asking this holding for anything, and the clerk read both of those sentences twice and then read the enclosure.

It is one page. It is correct in every particular. Harlan Vetch read it standing up. Sera Quill read it with her thumb on the corner. Tova Reed read it through the glass and said it was right. The man of about fifty read it at the gate and said the word correct out loud to nobody, and came in and said it again to the clerk, who wrote it down, because it is the first time anybody in this yard has written that word on this holding's own paper about somebody else's.

There is no column in this holding for what it is and the clerk made one, because the paper is the office's and not this holding's, and the entry is entered in the one room and the entry is against his own hand.

> Of the standing of the four bodies of households. Received at the gate on the Tuesday post, sealed, in the office's hand, and the form is in order, and the office has issued it and the issue is the office's and not anybody else's:
>
> **First. The figure carried on the long sheet in this holding is the answer for the four places named in the third column and for no others, and the four places are those four and no others, and a figure that answers for a fifth place is a figure that has been added to by somebody.**
>
> **Second. Each of the four lines wants two signatures at the foot of it. Two signatures are two and are not one, and a line with one signature on it is a line that has not been signed, and this holding has four such lines and no others.**
>
> **Third. The reaching of those four lines is the duty of the person named in the office's own hand, and that person is the clerk of this holding, and the office has named him, and the office has not asked this holding whether it agrees.**
>
> **Fourth. The four lines are to be reached before the twenty-ninth of this month. That day is the office's day. This holding has not been asked about it.**
>
> **Entered: in the one room of this holding, on the standing of the paper itself and against the clerk's own hand, and the count of blanks on the board in that room is thirty-nine and is not altered by this entry, and the second rule under those blanks stands open and empty and opens nothing.**

Then the clerk stood up and read the third item of it out loud in the yard, and gave the reason before anybody asked him for it.

**"It is not that I object to it,"** he said. **"I have had four years of doing a thing nobody named me to do and I have never once minded. The reason I am standing here saying it out loud is that this morning a piece of paper has put my name on a job, and if I do not say the sentence now, then in nine days somebody is going to say it for me, and it will sound as though I had agreed to it in advance."**

**"You have agreed to it in advance,"** Hesta Lyle said. **"You made a column for it."**

**"I made a column for the office's paper,"** the clerk said, **"which is a different thing from agreeing to be the man, and if the yard cannot tell those two apart on the morning they arrive together then the yard is going to have a very interesting month."**

Nobody called it a lie. Nobody in this yard has called it anything else, and nobody forgave it either, which is a thing the yard has been careful about since Wednesday and which took two people a certain amount of effort.

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour and the heading over the pair is of a month nine months back and has not moved and did not move this morning.

**"One hundred and seventy-four of three hundred and thirty-one,"** he said, and shut the book, and **"and I have noticed that nobody has looked at the wall since I stopped talking, which is what I read it for."**

Then the launder, which is the clerk's own figure and which nobody in this yard has ever been able to call wrong.

**One thousand and two hundred and eighteen hundredweight.**

**"Five off, and the four hundred and seventh,"** he said, **"and the falling half has moved again and is a hundred and eighty-two. The rising half is a hundred and ninety-two and has not moved since yesterday. Three hundred and seventy-four, and I have said that figure out loud in this yard on every morning of the four years I have stood in it, and I have never once been bored of it, which surprises me."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the end of the long table with the paper in front of her and her thumb on the fourth item, and she gave the reason before she was asked, which she has now done often enough that the yard waits for it.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** she said, **"and the reason is that I am the only person in this holding who has refused to do a thing that would have worked, and that happened on Sunday at six in the morning at a tap, and it was a decision and it took nine seconds. If I stand in front of nine people today and say the other sentence, then by Friday the refusal I made will be a character. And a character can be argued with, and a decision cannot, and I have watched eleven years of men discover that difference about three weeks too late."**

Four bodies of households, four hundred and five, and a clause that reads the four hundred and fourth out of four hundred and sixth and sits either side of the figure. The four lines under the whole of it want two signatures each and have neither of the two on any of them, and the paper that came this morning supplies no signature and asks for none.

Nia Vale had the register form on the Monday and not on the Tuesday, and the three ages were read at the rack by the woman of thirty-eight, who took them off the shelf in the order they came and gave the last one with her hand flat on the shelf beside it and not on the figure.

The register form is at three hundred and one days. Behind it the charter stands at two hundred and seven. On the second ruled line in Silling's own book there is two hundred and ninety-five and nothing written on it, and it is the third morning this week that figure has been given out loud with a hand on the shelf instead of a mouth.

On the near board, seven hundred and ninety-one. On the far one, eight hundred and thirty-eight. The forty-seven between them is a distance and not a figure anybody in this holding has ever added to a third thing, and behind the near board there is a drawer in the masonry with a key on a nail in the one room door, and the drawer is shut and the key is on its nail.

The count in force did not rise on a Tuesday. It stands at a hundred and forty, with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and eleven not, and the third of the four is the only one of them that has ever needed saying.

Eleven journeys stand on the barrow and eleven is a floor. Seven sessions stand in this holding's book. The requests and the section-nine notes are both at fifty-three, and the paper that came this morning went into neither of them, because the paper is the office's and those two books belong to this holding, and there is no column in either of them and there is not going to be one.

A man of about seventy was not fetched today and no question found its way to him. The seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off carries a figure that has not gone back and was not read this morning.

Four ruled lines under two words are bare. Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know, and nobody has cut a seventh, and the four things a seventh row is not cut for were not said out loud by anybody in this yard today. The ladder took no rung. The offer on the low board is undated, untaken, not taken back. No foot went onto the ring of bare ground. Paid is in front of thirty and has been let out in nothing.

On the long table, the first of the three sheets is a hundred and four days old and face up, with nine inches of bare board on either side of it. The second of the three papers to reach this holding in four days now has a name on it, and the name is the clerk's, and he read it out loud in a yard at eleven this morning, and nobody in that yard called it wrong, and nobody in that yard forgave it either.

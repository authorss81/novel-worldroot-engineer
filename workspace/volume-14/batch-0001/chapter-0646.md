# Chapter 646

## The Nineteenth Of The Seventeenth, And A Place Asked For A Figure

The ask came into the yard on the Thursday and it did not come as a letter, because a letter can be put in a book and this could not.

Marden sent the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden across the fields at seven in the morning, and she had walked it, and she stood at the end of the long table for a while before she said anything at all, and what she said first was not the ask.

**"I did not go on Sunday,"** she said. **"I have not slept about it and I have not changed my mind. Marden has sent me because Marden has heard in about four days that there is a number, and a number that belongs to Marden, and Marden would like to know what it is before anybody else does."**

**"That is the whole of the ask?"** the clerk said.

**"That is the whole of the ask, and I am carrying it because I would not go and be told it, and I am going to say that in the same breath, because a woman who refuses a door and then is sent through it has not refused it."**

The ask was this. What does this holding stand at, and where is that figure written, and who keeps it, and when did it last move, and is it the same figure for Marden that it is for the other three places on the column, or is there a different figure for each.

Nobody answered any of it before the wall.

**One thousand and two hundred and twenty-one hundredweight.**

**"Five off, and the four hundred and ninth,"** the clerk said. **"One of the halves of the window has gone and the other has not, and I am going to tell you which in the order I always tell you, and the order matters this morning because you are going to write it down somewhere I will never see."**

He put two fingers on the top line of the slate and then on the line under it.

**"The falling half has moved and stands at a hundred and eighty-three. The other has not moved since Wednesday, which is three Wednesdays running, and it is a hundred and ninety-three. Three hundred and seventy-six, and a woman from Marden is going to write that down, and she is going to write it in the wrong order, because that is the order I gave it to her in."**

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour with the stone on the corner and the heading over the pair is of a month nine months back and has not moved.

**"One hundred and seventy-five of three hundred and thirty-three,"** he said, and then, without being asked, **"and five voices on the last sentence of it today, and I am not going to tell the yard which five, and I want it known that five is a different number from the four that have been on it all week and I have noticed the difference and I have not done anything about it."**

For the four bodies of households the long sheet says four hundred and seven, with a clause around the figure that reads the four hundred and sixth out of four hundred and eighth. Every figure in that sentence is on a piece of paper inside this building, and not one of them is the figure Marden has asked for.

The clerk stood at the end of the table with the register form still on the shelf behind him and did not take it down, and gave the reason before anybody put the question to him, and the yard has by now stopped remarking on that.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, **"and I am going to be short about why. A place that is answerable to this holding has just asked this holding for a figure in a yard, and there are about eleven people standing in this yard. If I say the sentence that names the end of a thing in front of that ask, then in about a week there will be a version of this morning in which this holding refused Marden a number and said something else instead, and the two of them will be the same story, and I have watched four years of this yard turn one morning into two by saying a second thing in the same air."**

Then he answered the ask, which he did do, and did it properly, and it took a quarter of an hour.

**"The figure is on the long sheet and it has been on the long sheet for four hundred and seven days, and it is a figure about this holding's four bodies and not a figure about Marden's water or Marden's ground or Marden's seed. It was not made for Marden and it is not kept for Marden, and it went up by one this morning the way it goes up every morning. I can tell you who keeps it and it is me, and I can tell you when it last moved, and it moved at the second hour this morning when nothing came off the low field."**

**"Is it the same figure for the other three places,"** the woman said.

**"It is the same figure for the four of you, and that is the part I would like you to take back to Marden, because the same figure for four places is a thing an office can use and a thing four places cannot argue with separately. If you want to argue with it, then four of you have to argue with it at once, and I am not going to be the man who says whether that is allowed."**

**"That is an answer about the paper,"** the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said. **"You have not told Marden what it stands at."**

**"No,"** the clerk said, **"and I have told you why, and the reason is that I have got a book in this building with four lines in it and two signatures wanted on each, and the office has put my name on the job of getting them, and a man who is asking four households for a signature does not also hand out the number that the signature is about. I have thought about it for an hour and that is where I got to."**

Nobody published anything. The requests book did not take it and the section-nine notes did not take it, and both of them stand at fifty-three and fifty-three and neither has moved this morning. No sheet came off the long table, and the three of them are still lying where they have lain, and about nine inches of bare board stands either side of each one, and no column has been ruled under any of them this morning or on any morning in four years.

Soren Rill was standing at the tool house end of the yard with his back to everybody for most of the ask, and he came over at about eleven, which is later than he has ever come over.

**"I will tell you the thing I have been not saying all morning,"** he said. **"Thornwild is on that list. Thornwild is the second of the two places nobody had heard of on Wednesday, and Thornwild has sent nobody anywhere and asked for nothing, and I have known that since Tuesday and I have not said it in this yard until now, and I am saying it because a yard that has been asked a question by one of its own four should not be able to say afterwards that the other three were not in the room."**

**"You have been standing there since seven,"** Hesta Lyle said.

**"I have been standing there since seven,"** Soren Rill agreed, **"and there is a thing I am going to have to say out loud in this yard tomorrow, and I have been standing here working out how, and I would rather work it out near a person than at the far end of a yard on my own."**

The three ages were read at the rack by Nia Vale, in the order of her own choosing, which was the ruled line first and the charter second and the form last, and nobody stopped her. Two hundred and ninety-seven on the second ruled line in Silling's own book. Two hundred and nine on the charter. Three hundred and three days on the register form.

Seven hundred and ninety-three on the near board and eight hundred and forty on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board is shut and the key to it is on the nail in the frame of the one room door.

Nothing about the rota moved on a Thursday, and the count in force is a hundred and forty-one in force, twenty-nine taken, a hundred and twelve not. Paid is in front of thirty and has been let out in nothing. Eleven journeys on the barrow and eleven is a floor. Seven sessions in this holding's book. Four ruled lines under two words are bare and six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know and nobody has cut a seventh. The ladder took no rung. On the low board there is an offer with no day on it, and it has not been taken up and it has not been taken back. Nobody walked onto the ring of bare ground at the top of the low field.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was put to him. Nobody read the sixty-six in the seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off and it did not go back. Nobody was asked to choose anything.

At about the fourth hour the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden walked back across the fields, carrying no answer to the number and the whole of an answer about the paper.

On the long table, face up and untouched, is a sheet that is a hundred and six days old. Beside it, in a week, three papers have come to this holding, and the second of the three is the one with the clerk's name on it, and not one of them has been entered anywhere.

# Chapter 639

## The Twelfth Of The Seventeenth, And A Paper That Came By Hand With A Covering Letter On It

The paper came by the middle road at the eighth hour in an envelope with a seal on the corner, and the clerk took it in at the gate and weighed it against the day's other post on the scale that has not been right since the winter before last.

He wrote it into the mail book with a time against it. Then he stood and looked at the entry for a good deal longer than the entry needed looking at.

**"It is signed,"** he said, to nobody in particular. **"Whoever brought yesterday's copy up the gate track would not give a name, and this one has got three of them on the foot of it, and they are not names anybody in this yard has ever been introduced to."**

The window had gone the other way overnight, which is what an even morning does, and the falling half of it was a hundred and seventy-nine and had not moved since Wednesday, while the rising half had gone up to a hundred and ninety. The two made three hundred and sixty-nine, and Kellan Rusk added them out loud this once because the slate was in his hand and not on the wall.

Then the clerk read the launder, because a wall is read at nine whether or not anything has come up a road.

**One thousand and two hundred and seventeen hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, and the four hundred and second,"** he said, **"and I am aware that four hundred and second has a hundred in it, and so does the thing I am going to say, and they are two figures and this yard does not add them."**

The long sheet carries the four bodies of households and this morning it carries four hundred, with nothing on the end of it. The small hand underneath has the three hundred and ninety-ninth out of four hundred and first, one under and one over the figure they sit either side of.

Harlan Vetch put his hand flat on the table beside that figure and not on it, and looked at it for about as long as it takes to be sure of a thing.

**"Four hundred,"** he said. **"A round number, and the first round number that has ever been on that sheet, and I have been laying wall for thirty-one years. A round number is a number a man can finish in his head. In about nine years there will be a man in an office who cannot finish this one in his head, and he will write down whatever he does finish in his head, and that is how a wall ends up with a figure on it that I did not agree to."**

**"It is going to be nine hundred and something,"** Sennik Vaul said.

**"It is going to be nine hundred and something,"** Harlan Vetch agreed, **"and I have not said that this one is wrong. I have said that a round figure is the first morning of a thing that ends in an argument, and I have said it to a room and not to a clerk."**

The covering letter inside the envelope was one page and it was correct in every particular. It named the schedule, it named the phase, it gave the dates, it gave the holdings, and it gave a third column, and the third column named the place whose standing is answered for by the figure on the long sheet in this building.

Marden was in that column again, and there were two more places under Marden that had not been in the copy which came up the gate track yesterday, and neither of the two is a place anybody in this holding has ever sent anybody to.

Nobody in the yard could put a finger on the thing wrong with it. The clerk read it twice and could not. The man of about fifty read it standing at the gate and could not.

Tova Reed read the third column through the seed house glass with her hand flat on the pane, and said out loud what she had found so that the yard would have it from her and not out of a clerk's mouth.

**"I have been inside three county offices in my life and I have never once been able to say that about a piece of paper that was going to cost somebody something,"** she said. **"I am not going to stand at that window and pretend that wanting it otherwise is a fault. The paper is right. That is the whole of my finding and I am not going to improve on it by the evening."**

The paper went onto the long table beside the three sheets and did not go into the requests, and it did not go into the section-nine notes. Both of those stand at fifty-three and have stood at fifty-three since before this holding had a schedule to be handed, and no column has been ruled for it in either, and nobody went looking for a pencil to rule one with.

Soren Rill had been in the yard since before light and had not gone out to the tap house, and at the seventh hour he stood well back from the doorway on purpose, with his back against the tool house where the pair is not read.

**"A yard has started answering for four hundred people it has never met,"** he said, when Hesta Lyle asked him why he was standing over there. **"I am the only man in this yard who belongs to somewhere on that list, and if I am in that doorway at the seventh hour on a morning when a paper like that is open on that table, then whatever I say in the next two hours stops being mine and becomes the other place's opinion of the paper. So I chose the spot. Put that down."**

**"Nobody is going to put down that you chose a spot to stand in,"** the clerk said.

**"Then I chose it,"** Soren Rill said, **"and that is the part of it I can be held to."**

Nia Vale came in from the seed house at ten with the envelope still in her hand and gave the yard the thing it was waiting for, which was whether anybody had an opinion yet.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** she said. **"I have read that covering letter twice and every word of it can be checked by somebody, and there is one sentence in this yard that no paper in this building can check. I am not going to put the two of them on the same table on the morning a paper turns up that a man can check every word of. Somebody will write down afterwards that the paper and the sentence are the same kind of thing, and they are not, and I will not be the one who lays them side by side."**

Harlan Vetch had the far board in his eye while he said that, standing at the end of the table with his back to the near one, and the boards this morning are seven hundred and eighty-six at this end and eight hundred and thirty-three at the other, and the forty-seven between them is the same forty-seven and always has been, and the drawer let into the masonry behind the near one has not been opened and its key has been on the nail in the one room door since first light.

Kellan Rusk had the three ages by eleven and gave them in the order of the shelf and not the order of their size. Two hundred and ninety-six days on the register form. Two hundred and two on the charter behind it. Two hundred and ninety on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, which he read with two fingers resting on the shelf and not with his mouth, and the room noticed that and nobody said so about it.

The count in force did not rise on a Thursday and stands where it stood on Wednesday, a hundred and thirty-nine, with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and ten not.

Thirty-nine blanks stand on the board in the one room and the second rule under them stands open and empty. It opened nothing this morning, and it has never opened anything, and there is a day coming when this holding is going to find that out twice over.

Paid is in front of thirty and has been let out in nothing, and the barrow is on its eleven journeys with eleven a floor, and the sessions in this holding's book are at seven and a Thursday is not an eighth, and the requests and the section-nine notes are at fifty-three and fifty-three and took nothing off that table this morning.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was put to him at any site. The sixty-six in the seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off was not read and has not gone back, and the reading is standing there for anybody who ever wants to spend it.

Nobody climbed the ladder and no figure stands against either of the two people who belong to it. The offer on the low board has no day on it and has not been touched, taken up or taken back. The ring of bare ground at the top of the low field took no foot this morning and has taken none in four years.

The first of the three sheets is ninety-nine days old and lying face up, and the second is folded back on the other crease, and the third is headed with a name and a date and a hand and a seal and is correct in every particular, like the one that came this morning, and about nine inches of bare board stands either side of each of the three.

At the end of that same table a fourth piece of paper lies with no inches marked out for it and no column anywhere in this holding that it has been given, and it is the only one of the four that anybody has signed for.

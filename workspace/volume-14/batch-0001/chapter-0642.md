# Chapter 642

## The Fifteenth Of The Seventeenth, And Three Doors Out Of Four

The schedule's third column has four places in it and the long sheet has four bodies of households in it, and the two lists are the same four places in the same order, and the yard spent a Sunday morning finding out what that meant in practice.

It meant that four people had to walk out of this gate and stand in four doorways and be told something, and that each of the four lines on the long sheet wants two signatures at the bottom of it, and that nobody in this holding had ever seen a signature go onto any of those four lines.

Nobody put one there this morning either. Nobody refused one. Nobody wrote down that a household had been asked.

Soren Rill took the first one because he knew the lane, and it took him half an hour, and he came back at about the eleventh hour and said the household had heard him and had not written anything and had asked him what he was paid by, and he had answered that honestly, and that was the whole of the visit.

**"Half an hour,"** he said. **"Four words in, four words out, and a woman at the door who wanted to know who pays for a man walking up her lane. I told her. I would tell her the same on a Monday. Nobody wrote and nobody said no, and I am going to be honest with this yard about what that means, which is that a thing that happens and leaves no mark has not happened."**

Nia Vale took the second and it took her the best part of three hours, because the second household is the one whose door is on a private track and the track is not on any sheet anybody in this holding has, and she found it by asking a boy at the gate, and she was let in.

**"They have heard about the column,"** she said, at the gate, on her way back, with the coat buttoned to the throat. **"Not from us. Somebody has been and told them there is a column and that a number in it is theirs now, and they have had about four days with that, and they want to know what we are going to do about it. I did not have an answer. I gave them the truth about the paper instead, which is that it is correct, and the woman wrote the word correct on the inside of the door frame where she could see it from the kitchen."**

The third was the man of about fifty, and he had been up since before light, and the third household is four miles off on the far side of the fen and he came back at dusk having been turned back at a gate by a man who would not open it and would not say why.

**"A gate,"** he said. **"Not a wall, a gate, and he was behind it, and he wanted to know what I was and I told him and he shut it. That is all. He did not say no to the column. He said he did not know what I was, and then he shut a gate on a man who had told him the truth."**

The fourth place is Marden, and nobody went to it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tap at six with a can in each hand when somebody said the fourth was still standing there, and she put both cans down on the slab, which she does not do.

**"Then I will go,"** she said.

Nobody said anything, and she picked the cans up again, and said the other thing.

**"I will not go, and I am not going to pretend it is because it is my own place. There is a version of this morning where a woman of Marden walks into Marden on a Sunday and is told by eight people that she is one of them, and every one of those eight people is entitled to say she is, and not one of them is entitled to say she is the woman who went. You would get a signature off Marden by Friday and Marden would not have agreed to anything. I have seen that done."**

She picked one can up and turned it a quarter turn on the slab and put it down again in the same wet ring.

**"I did not do it. I would rather we turn up empty-handed in a week with a reason we can say out loud."**

**"That is the fourth reason anybody has given this week for not doing a thing that would have worked,"** the clerk said.

**"It is the first one that was about a person who lives there,"** she said, **"and you may write that distinction down, because I am not going to be the one who explains it twice."**

The wall came at nine and the launder with it, and the launder came down this time.

**One thousand and two hundred and fifteen hundredweight.**

**"Five off and the four hundred and fifth,"** the clerk said. **"The falling half of the window has moved and is a hundred and eighty-one. The rising half is a hundred and ninety-one and it has not moved since yesterday, and the two of them are three hundred and seventy-two, and I am aware that I have said a version of that sentence on six mornings this week and I am going to stop saying it after today whatever the figures do."**

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour with his back to the wind off the low field and the book open against his own chest, and a stone taken out of the wall laid on the corner of the page to hold it down. Over that pair the heading says a month nine months back, and a woman in this yard has walked the length of the yard and looked at it twice this morning without touching it, because it has stood exactly where it stood on the first of the fifteenth and standing exactly there is what it is for.

**"One hundred and seventy-three of three hundred and twenty-nine,"** he said. **"Four voices on the last sentence of it, and I have told the yard before that I will not say which four, and I am not going to start."**

He shut the book and put the stone on the cover of it and looked at the four doors of the yard instead of at the wall.

**"The two lanes I knocked at this morning were opened by people who do not know this pair exists and would not have cared, and I would rather be the man in that position than the man who tells them about it."**

Then Sennik Vaul, who had been standing at the end of the long table with his coat on since eight.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, **"and the reason is that this is the first morning this holding has spent on other people's doorsteps, and I have been one of the men who sends other people to other people's doorsteps for eleven years, and I know exactly what this yard is about to become. If I stand in the middle of it at noon and say the sentence about the far end of things, then in three weeks somebody in one of those four doorways is going to hear about it and I will have put it there. I would rather be a man who went to a door and came back."**

He had the three ages off the rack before he said it, in the order of his own pocket rather than the order of the shelf. The register form is two hundred and ninety-nine days. The charter is two hundred and five. The second ruled line in Silling's own book is two hundred and ninety-three, and he said that one out loud because the yard had been quiet about it for two days.

The long sheet stands at four hundred and three for the four bodies, and the clause around it is the four hundred and second out of four hundred and fourth. The four lines under it carry two signatures each, and the two signatures on a line are two and not one, and four lines of two is a thing the yard has not added up and is not going to.

The boards are seven hundred and eighty-nine at the near end and eight hundred and thirty-six at the other, and the forty-seven between them has not changed, and the drawer behind the near one is shut and its key is on the nail in the one room door at this hour and at every hour of a Sunday.

The count in force did not rise on a Sunday, and it stands at a hundred and forty with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and eleven not. The compost line is paid at thirty and has been let out in nothing at all, and there is a heap at the top of the low field that nobody has named an hour for.

The barrow went out eleven times this week and eleven is a floor and not a target. The sessions in this holding's book are at seven, and a Sunday is not an eighth. Nothing has gone into the requests this morning and nothing has gone into the section-nine notes, and both of those books are still at fifty-three apiece. The first of the three sheets on the long table is a hundred and two days old and lying face up with nine inches of bare board either side of it.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was put to him at any site this morning.

Nobody went up the ladder, and the offer on the low board still has no day written on it, and the ring of bare ground at the top of the low field is bare and stayed bare through the whole of Sunday. The four ruled lines under two words carry nothing at all this morning. Against the sheet of what this holding does not know there are six rows ruled and no seventh, and the seventh was not cut for the column and not for the schedule and not for the paper and not for the other thing, and nobody in this yard said those four out loud.

The yard got through a Sunday with three doors out of four and no signature on any line and nothing written down about any of it, and at dusk the clerk turned the yard key, and the fourth place on the third column was still standing there with nobody sent to it, and the woman who would have gone was filling a can at the tap and saying nothing.

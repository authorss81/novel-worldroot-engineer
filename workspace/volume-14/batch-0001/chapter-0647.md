# Chapter 647

## The Twentieth Of The Seventeenth, And A Place That Would Not Be First

Soren Rill stood on the middle road at about the ninth hour on a Friday with his boots in the mud and the rain coming off the fen behind him in a sheet, and he had asked to say it outside the yard, and the yard had let him.

About six of them went out to the gate to hear it.

**"Thornwild will not publish first."**

Nobody said anything for a while, and then Hesta Lyle said the obvious thing, which was that nobody had asked Thornwild to publish anything.

**"That is true,"** Soren Rill said, **"and it is also the reason. Nobody has asked, and there is a paper in this yard with a column on it that says a figure in here is the answer for four places, and in about nine days somebody is going to ask, and Thornwild has decided in advance what the answer will be. That is what I came to say."**

He shifted his weight in the mud and did not say it more quietly.

**"I asked to do it out here and not because I am ashamed of it. A thing said at a gate belongs to two places. A thing said in a yard belongs to one. Now I am going to give the reason properly, because nobody has asked me for it yet and I would rather give it than be asked twice."**

**"A place that publishes first is the place every other place is measured against,"** he said. **"Thornwild was measured against for eleven years by people who had never come up this road, and we had no way to argue with a number about ourselves that we had never seen. I am not going to hand that to a second lot of people in a second column. That is the whole of it. There is no man in it."**

**"Then there is nothing to be angry at,"** Nia Vale said.

**"Then be angry at the sum,"** Soren Rill said, **"because that is where it is, and I have come four miles in the rain to put it in the right place, and I would like somebody to write down that I said it and not that I was relieved to."**

The rain went on. Nobody in that yard offered him a figure, and nobody offered him a place, and nobody told him the paper was only a paper. One man said the word fair, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden told him to take it back, and he took it back.

Then the wall, and the launder, and the two of them going the way a Friday goes.

**One thousand and two hundred and twenty-nine hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, and the four hundred and tenth,"** the clerk said. **"The rising half is a hundred and ninety-four. The falling half is a hundred and eighty-three and has not moved since Thursday. Three hundred and seventy-seven."**

He put the chalk down and did not pick it up again straight away.

**"I have said a version of that on six mornings running and I am aware of it, and I am not going to stop. A clerk who stops saying a figure because he has said it six times is a clerk who has begun to be bored of his own holding, and this holding has had a week I would not wish on a man who was bored of it."**

Harlan Vetch had come in from the north end and was standing under the eaves with the water coming off the coping behind him, and he did not go to the doorway.

**"There will be nobody in that doorway at the seventh hour and I am the reason, and I would rather it was said at once than explained at noon. A man who reads a number out of a doorway in front of nine people is handing nine people an opinion formed out of a figure. I have refused a figure on a wall four times in four years for exactly that reason, and this yard knows it, and this morning is not the morning to hand anybody a second one to be angry with."**

He turned the chisel over in his hand and did not go on to the wall.

**"This morning a place four miles up the road has told us no in the rain. I am not going to hand a room a figure to be angry with. There is a wall at the north end and it wants a man who does not have one of those."**

Sera Quill was sitting on the trestle under the tap with her back to the water butt and her slate under her arm, and she had not gone near the doorway either.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** she said, **"and I would like the reason written down by somebody who is not me, because I have said three sentences in this yard this week and I would rather this one were a record than a speech."**

Somebody wrote it down, badly, and she did not correct the writing.

**"A place has said no to this holding on a Friday morning, and it said it with a sum in it, and there is not a villain anywhere in it. That is going to be a hard thing for this yard to hold on to, and if this yard stands up and says the sentence that names the end of a thing over the top of a refusal, then the refusal becomes a page in something we tell about ourselves."**

She put the slate on her knee.

**"I have copied two numbers forward by hand for nine years because I do not trust this holding to be careful by itself. Being careful is the whole of what I have got."**

Nia Vale had the three ages off the rack and gave them in the order of the shelf, and the second ruled line in Silling's own book was said out loud by a mouth this morning for the first time in four days. Three hundred and four days is the register form. The charter behind it stands at two hundred and ten. On the ruled line in Silling's book there is two hundred and ninety-eight and nothing else, and she said all three at once without stopping between them, which is a thing she does when she does not want a room to get used to one of them.

**"Two hundred and ten on the charter,"** the clerk said, after a moment. **"It went past its round figure on Wednesday morning, which was the first morning of this run, and I did not notice the day, and nobody else in this yard did either."**

He put the charter back into the rack square, which he did not need to do.

**"A figure that goes past a round number on a Wednesday that nobody was watching is a figure that will go past a great many other things on a Wednesday that nobody is watching, and this holding has spent four years finding that out the slow way, and I would like it written down today while it is still cheap."**

**"That is the first thing you have said all week that was not a figure off a wall,"** Harlan Vetch said.

**"It is a figure off a charter,"** the clerk said, **"which is the same sort of thing, and I will thank you not to make a virtue of it."**

For the four bodies of households the long sheet says four hundred and eight, and the clause around that figure reads the four hundred and seventh out of four hundred and ninth. The four lines beneath it want two signatures each. One place on the list has said in the rain that it will not publish first. One place was not reached at all on Sunday. One household wrote the word correct on the inside of a door frame. One man shut a gate on a stranger, and then opened it.

Seven hundred and ninety-four on the near board, and eight hundred and forty-one on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board is shut at this hour and at every other hour of this Friday, and the one key to it is on the nail in the frame of the one room door and will be on it at dusk.

Nothing about the rota moved today, so the count in force stands at a hundred and forty-one, with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and twelfth not.

The compost line is paid at thirty and nothing has ever been let out in it. The barrow has its eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. There are seven sessions in this holding's book and a Friday is not an eighth. Nothing went into the requests this morning and nothing went into the section-nine notes, and both of those books are where they were, at fifty-three apiece, and no sheet came off the long table for anybody.

On the long table, face up and untouched, lies a sheet that came a hundred and seven days ago, with about nine inches of bare board either side of it. The second is folded back on the other crease and has never been opened. The third carries a name, a date, a hand and a seal, and there is nothing anybody in this holding can put wrong in it. It has never been entered, never been refused and never been ruled under, and no column in this holding has ever been cut for it.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was put to him at any site today.

A figure stands in the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off. Nobody read it this morning. It is not going back, and the reading is there in the morning for whoever wants to spend it, and this morning nobody wanted to.

The ladder took no rung this year. On the low board, an offer with no day on it, which has not been taken up and has not been taken back. Nobody put a foot on the ring of bare ground. Under two words there are four ruled lines and they are bare, and beside them a sheet carries six ruled rows against what this holding does not know, with nobody's seventh row cut for any of the four. Nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything today.

The rain stopped about the fifth hour. At dusk the clerk turned the yard key in the gate, and the one other key in this holding was on its nail, where it is at every hour of every day.

Under the thirty-nine blanks on the board in the one room, the second rule was open and empty, and it has never once opened anything. Not one of the four lines on the long sheet carried a signature. And one of the four places had said no, in a sum, and not in a threat.

Somebody has put a number into a column with a day against it, and in about nine days there is going to be a day that is not his, and not one of the figures on the wall in this yard moved an inch to meet it.

# Chapter 652

## The Twenty-Fifth Of The Seventeenth, And A Return That Nobody In This Building Has To Answer For

It came up the fen road at about the ninth hour with two men and a cart and neither of the two men was from this holding and neither of them stopped at the gate. They came up the middle track as far as the wall, put it down where Soren Rill pointed, and went back down the fen road without a word to anybody, and that is the whole of what a return is.

> A return on the nine day interval, carried up the fen road at the ninth hour and put down at the wall and put away. This holding keeps no ordinal for a return and keeps no weight for one, and has kept neither since before the wall was built, and no allowance is carried here for any such thing. The count of blanks on the board in the one room is thirty-nine and is not altered by this entry, and the second rule under those blanks stands open and empty and opens nothing.

Under those thirty-nine blanks the second rule stands open and it has stood open and empty for as long as anybody in this room has been alive, and the standing for how many times it has stood empty is the thirty-ninth time, and it is the thirty-ninth time this morning and it was the thirty-ninth time before the cart came up the fen road, and a rule that comes back empty opens nothing, and nobody in this yard put a name into any of the blanks because the coming of a cart is not a reason to put a name in a blank.

**"Forty mornings it is possible I have watched a thing like that go past and waited for somebody to answer for it,"** Hesta Lyle said, **"and this is the first time I have watched one go past and thought that nobody in this building is going to be asked anything about it at all. That is the whole difference and there is no villain in it anywhere."**

The clerk had the office's paper out on the middle table and had been at it since before the yard was properly up, and he did not stop for the cart.

**"Four lines under the whole of it,"** he said, **"and every one of the four wants two signatures at the foot of it, and I have written out four sheets of my own paper this morning with the address of each household at the top of it, and every one of those four sheets is in my coat and not in any book in this holding, because there is no column in either of the two books for a sheet that a man wrote himself."**

Sennik Vaul was sitting on the end of the middle table with his knees hanging and he had not moved for the cart either.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning and I will tell you why before you ask,"** he said. **"Because something came up that road at nine this morning with nobody on it but a cart and two men, and not one person in this holding is answerable for that, and I have spent nineteen days in this yard watching every single thing that happens here get tied back to somebody. That is what a yard is. If I stand here at noon and say the other sentence, then the one morning out of this week where nothing in this holding was anybody's business stops being that, because I made it mine."**

He got off the table.

**"A man who has spent a week learning what a place feels like when it is nobody's fault has a very short time before he starts calling it everybody's business. I would rather keep the one morning."**

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour. The heading over it stands where it has stood since the first morning this pair was ever read in this yard, which is a heading of a month nine months back, and it did not move this morning and he checked it before he said the numbers.

**"One hundred and seventy-eight of three hundred and thirty-nine."**

He closed the book on his thumb and left his thumb in it.

**"Four years at seven in the morning, and not once in any of them have I got used to it, and I want that said in my own words and not somebody else's, because a man who gets used to a number stops hearing it."**

The clerk waited until he had got the launder out before he said his own, because the launder comes down about half past eight and it does not wait for anybody.

**One thousand and two hundred and thirty hundredweight.**

The clerk gave it in the order he can say it without looking at anything.

**"Three hundred and eighty-two. Falling, a hundred and eighty-six, and it moved overnight because it is an odd morning and it is the half that moves on one. Rising, a hundred and ninety-six, standing still since Tuesday. And the launder at one thousand and two hundred and thirty hundredweight, five off, which is the four hundred and fifteenth."**

He wiped the chalk on his sleeve.

**"I will say to this yard once and then never again that I am aware a fall on the launder and a rise on the window's falling half are the same morning of the week and have been since the turn, and there is no rule I can print that will stop me reading them as two separate things. That is a fact about me and not about the wall."**

Soren Rill gave the two boards as he went past with the barrow, without stopping, because stopping is what makes it a performance.

**"Seven hundred and ninety-nine at this end of it,"** he said. **"Eight hundred and forty-six at the one behind the tool house. Forty-seven is how far apart they are, which is a distance and not a figure anybody sums."**

The drawer behind the near one is shut, and its key went onto its nail at first light and will be there at dusk.

The three ages came off the rack at about the tenth hour in the order Kellan Rusk can hold them, and there were four people in the one room door and every one of them heard it.

**"Three hundred and nine days on the form. Two hundred and fifteen on the charter,"** Kellan Rusk said, and stopped, and then gave the third figure on its own, which he has started doing this week. **"Three hundred and three on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, and nothing at all written on it."**

He put the book back on its nail and then took it off the nail again and looked at the line, which he has not done since the figure went past three hundred on Sunday.

**"There is a clerk in this holding who says a round figure in a book means a man has been counting without noticing,"** he said, **"and I have been not noticing for four years and it turns out that is what she meant. I am not going to argue with her about it today. I would like to be able to keep arguing with her about it next week."**

Hesta Lyle gave both halves of the small hand under the line, because on a Wednesday the yard expects that hand to come from somebody who is not the clerk, and she has never once let him give it on a day like this.

**"The sheet says four hundred and thirteen,"** she said, **"and the small hand under the number says the four hundred and twelfth out of four hundred and fourteenth, which is the way I have read that hand every Wednesday morning for two years."**

She put her finger under the line and not on the number above it.

**"A step below it and a step above it, the way that small hand has read since before I came to this table. A yard that stops giving it out loud is a yard that has lost the only way it has of telling which of the two figures actually moved."**

A cart standing at the wall with half a load on it is exactly the sort of afternoon that makes a yard mark one more journey, and nobody in this yard marked one.

**"Eleven on the barrow is a floor and not an invitation,"** Soren Rill said. **"Thirty paid on the compost line and not one load let out in it in four years. Fifteen lines in the use log and nothing above the fifteenth. Seven sessions in this holding's book and this Wednesday took none. Fifty-three in the requests and fifty-three in the section nine notes, and between them they took the office's paper and nothing at all, and they did not take a sheet out of my coat either."**

Harlan Vetch read the three papers on the long table from the north end, at a distance, without coming over to them, which is how he reads everything he has no part in.

**"The first of them is face up and has been a hundred and twelve days, with about nine inches of board either side of it,"** he said. **"The second is folded back and shut. The third is the one with the seal."**

He turned away from it.

**"And none of the three has been entered, none has been refused, and this holding has never cut a column under any of them, which is the one thing I would want written down."**

A Wednesday is not a morning the rotation comes round on. The count in force therefore rose by nothing, and it stands this morning where it stood on Tuesday, and of the number in it twenty-nine have been taken off it and a hundred and twelve have not. No field was taken. Nobody was blamed for a field that waited, because a field that waited did what the book says a field does when it waits.

Tova Reed counted the use log through the glass with the back of her hand and stopped at fifteen, and did not go on to a sixteenth, and then she went along the shelf where the sheet of six rows is kept and put her thumb beside the last of them and left it there, and that is how the yard knows there has been no seventh row cut for anything this week.

Harlan Vetch looked at his own mark on the tool house wall as he went past, four inches with two forks and no third fork anywhere on it, and nobody has ever put a sign under it or asked him what it means. The man of about seventy had nothing fetched from him and was asked nothing anywhere today. Nobody has climbed the ladder. The offer on the low board is undated, unpicked, and has not been withdrawn. The ring of bare ground is still bare. The door nine hundred yards off was walked past at the eleventh hour and not read.

What this yard did on a Wednesday morning with a return standing at the wall in front of it was go on working, and the cart went back down the fen road at the eleventh hour without anybody having to sign for it.

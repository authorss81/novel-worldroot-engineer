# Chapter 655

## The Twenty-Eighth Of The Seventeenth, And A Name Said Once In The One Room

Renn Ashby did not read the pair on the Saturday and did not come to the middle table at the seventh hour, and he sent word by the boy at the gate that he was not coming down, which is not a thing he does.

Harlan Vetch carried the message and gave it in his own words because he would not have it carried in anybody else's.

**"He says the reading is off this morning because he read a thing out of a book on Friday that he wishes he had not said out loud at the time,"** Harlan Vetch said, **"and a man who has put a number in the air does not get to do it again the next morning while the number is still in the air. He says it is not that the day is bad. He says he will read on Monday and that the heading has not moved and has not been moved by him staying away."**

The clerk was not at the wall when the launder came down at about half past eight, so Soren Rill read it off the slate, which he has done perhaps nine mornings in four years and always on a morning when the clerk is somewhere else.

**"One thousand and two hundred and forty-one hundredweight, eight on, and it is the four hundred and eighteenth,"** he said. **"And the window is three hundred and eighty-five, made of a hundred and eighty-seven falling, which has not shifted since Friday, and a hundred and ninety-eight rising, which moved in the night."**

He handed the slate back over without looking at it, because he has been handing it back over four years without looking at it.

**"Say that one out loud twice some week,"** he said. **"Not the figure. The fact that a man can hand a slate back without looking at it after saying two numbers off it."**

The boards were given from the far end, because Soren Rill started at the tool house end that morning.

**"Eight hundred and forty-nine at the one I started at,"** he said, **"and eight hundred and two at this one, and forty-seven between them, which is a walk and not a sum."**

Behind this one there is a drawer let into the masonry. It is shut. Its key is on a nail in the frame of the one room door and it will be on that nail at dusk.

Marek went into the one room at about the eighth hour and shut the door behind him, which he has not done in that room in four years, and Nia Vale was in there with her coat on and the day's sorting in a basket on the table, and she did not ask him what he wanted and she did not get up.

He stood on the far side of the table with both hands on the edge of it for a while.

**"My father's name was Aldren,"** he said.

Nia Vale did not move and did not say anything and did not take a slate out. She has carried a sheet of paper in that coat for a fortnight that is not in a book and has never been on a table, and she looked at the basket on the table and then at the wall behind it.

**"There was an afternoon,"** Marek said. **"A room like this one with a table in it and two men in it who are both dead, and one of them said out loud what was going to be done to the branches, and the other one wrote it down, and he wrote it differently. Not wrongly. Differently, in a way that a man could defend in an office. I have got the written one, because it is in a file, and the other one is the same afternoon and it has been in my head since I was old enough to hold it, and I have never once been able to check one against the other because both men are dead."**

He took his hands off the table.

**"That is what I have been carrying up and down this holding for years, and on Monday a piece of paper came up a road with a column on it saying that four hundred people in four counties are answered for by a figure in this yard. And I thought about what happens next, which is that people will come, and the question they will ask is what was said. And there is one place in four counties that knows, and it is not a file and it is not an office. It is a man in a room."**

He did not say anything for a minute. Nia Vale did not fill it.

**"So I am giving it up,"** he said. **"Not to you and not to the office and not to anybody in this yard. There is nowhere to put it. I am saying the name of the thing once in a room so that one other person has heard that it existed, and then it is not anywhere, and it will never be anywhere again, and I will not be able to go back into that afternoon and I will not be able to ask myself what he said, ever, and that is the whole of what is gone. Not the man. Not the year. The afternoon."**

**"Marek,"** Nia Vale said, and then she did not finish it, and she did not put her hand out.

**"Do not,"** he said. **"Not because of anything. Because if somebody touches me on the arm this morning then this is something that happened to a man in a room and it was not. And do not tell anybody, and do not write it on anything, and if I say that name again to you then you have heard it twice and that will mean something has gone wrong with one of us."**

**"It has gone wrong with one of us,"** Nia Vale said. **"That is exactly what I am not going to argue about at eight o'clock on a Saturday."**

She picked up her basket and put it on her arm and went out through the one room door and did not close it behind her, and she did not look back, and she went to the seed house and worked there for the rest of the morning with her back to the yard.

Marek came out of the one room about twenty minutes later and did the whole of the rest of the morning in the yard. He went to the north end and asked the mason about the weep at the tap joint and did not touch the pipe himself. He read the three ages off the rack because nobody else had read them yet, and he said them in the order of the shelf with his thumb resting on the shelf edge, which is what he has been meaning to stop doing.

**"The ruled line in Silling's book stands at three hundred and six with nothing on it,"** Marek said, **"and the charter at two hundred and eighteen, and the form at three hundred and twelve days."**

He put the book back on the nail.

**"That is the third time this week the empty figure has come out of somebody here with a hand against the shelf for support, and it is the third time it has needed to. I have been keeping count of that without meaning to and I have not told anybody."**

Then he said the thing he had to say, which nobody had asked him for and which four people in that yard had been waiting four days for.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said. **"At seven o'clock this morning I gave away the only place in this holding where it could be checked against anything at all. What was written down, I know, and that what was written down was edited, I have known for years, and as of this morning I have not got anything else."**

He put his hand flat on the table, on the bare board, and not on the paper.

**"So I am not putting a sentence about a thing being held where I cannot get to it, and an instrument that will not say which, on top of that in the same breath in front of nine people. One of those two is gone and cannot be got back. The other is a thing a man says when somebody is standing in front of him, and I have had one of the two already today and I am not spending the other one in the same hour."**

**"You have told nobody what it cost,"** the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said, from the tap.

**"I am not going to,"** Marek said. **"And that is mine to decide and not anybody else's, and I would like it noticed that I have not been offered anything for it and I have refused nothing for it either. Nobody in this yard has tried to make me feel better about it and I am not going to stand here and thank a yard for that, and I am not going to be told that it will keep."**

Nobody in this yard told him that it would keep.

Hesta Lyle squared the long sheet in front of her and gave the small hand under the line, because on a Saturday that hand is hers by nine years of habit and nobody in the yard disputes it.

**"Underneath a figure of four hundred and sixteen, the four hundred and fifteenth out of four hundred and seventeenth,"** she said.

She did not touch the figure itself.

**"Four bodies of households and four lines under them, and two signatures asked for on every one of the four, and not one signature on any of them this morning, or any morning of the week."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden filled two cans at the tap and gave the holding's standing in between the two of them, which is the only kind of reciting anybody does in this yard that is not for a book.

**"Thirty paid on the compost line and nothing let out in it. Eleven on the barrow and a floor. Fifteen lines in the use log and no sixteenth. Seven sessions in this holding's book and this Saturday entered none. Fifty-three and fifty-three in the other two, and neither of them took anything this morning."**

She set the second can down.

**"And thirty-nine blanks in the one room, with the second rule under them open and empty and opening nothing, and nobody put a fortieth name in any of them today or any day this week."**

Saturday is not a morning the rotation comes round on, and the count in force rose by nothing and stands at a hundred and forty-one, twenty-nine taken off it, a hundred and twelve of them not. No field was taken on this morning, and nobody was asked to put anything to anybody, and the whole of that is fortunate rather than tidy, because what happened this morning in the one room was not a thing that can have a question put beside it.

Hesta Lyle counted the bare board along the top of the long table with two fingers, the way she does when a sheet has been lying somewhere a long time, and came back with nine inches at that end and nine at this one.

**"A hundred and fifteen days on the first of them,"** she said. **"The second is still on its old fold and still shut. The third has a name and a date and a hand and a seal and not one thing in it that nine of us could go to. And there are three of them sitting here and not one of them has ever been entered, refused, or ruled under, and I would like that in the record with the two nines on it."**

Nia Vale counted the six ruled rows on the shelf in the one room with the side of her hand and stopped, because that is where she stops. There is no seventh row cut for the schedule, or the column, or the paper, or the other thing.

Out in the yard the ladder has gone up no rung, the offer on the low board is undated and untaken and not taken back, the ring of bare ground has not been walked on, and the man of about seventy was not fetched and was not asked anything at any site.

A door stands nine hundred yards off with a figure scratched into its seventh column, and at the eleventh hour it went past unread, as it has gone past all week.

What this yard was told about the twenty minutes in that room was nothing at all, and that was the end of it.

At dusk the man who had been in there turned the yard key in the gate and pulled it to behind him. The other key in this building was hanging on its nail inside the one room door, and there is one key in this holding and it is never anywhere else.

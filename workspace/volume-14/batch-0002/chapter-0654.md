# Chapter 654

## The Twenty-Seventh Of The Seventeenth, And Two Places That Cannot Both Be Reached On One Day

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour and nobody in that yard was listening to it, because the yard had been up since five and Hesta Lyle had a sheet of her own on the middle table with a line ruled down the middle of it and the four households named at the top, one under the other, and she had been at it with a pencil since before the light came.

**"One hundred and seventy-nine of three hundred and forty-one,"** Renn Ashby said, and shut the book, and **"and that heading has not moved either and I have stopped expecting it to."**

At the tenth hour she put the pencil down and asked the yard to come and look, which nobody in this holding does without a reason, and she gave the reason first.

**"Because it is going to be wrong in about eleven days and I want it to be wrong in front of everybody,"** she said. **"There are four households on that column. The office wants four lines reached and every one of them wants two signatures, and the office gave a day and it is the twenty-ninth of this month. That is five working days and there are four places."**

She put her finger on the second line down.

**"The second one is nine miles off the fourth one by road and the road is soft for six days out of seven and there is a ford on it that is not passable with a loaded barrow. So one man cannot stand at the second door and then stand at the fourth door on the same day and get his boots dry. Not this week. Not next week. It is not a question of how early he leaves."**

The yard looked at the sheet. Nobody said anything for a while.

**"So write it down plainly,"** Hesta Lyle said. **"Three places and one place. There is no fifth place. There is no version of this week in which a man walks to the second door and then at the fourth one, and I have gone at it three ways this morning with a pencil and the third way was the one where I wanted it to come out different."**

Then she looked up, because she had been told by two people in the last fortnight to say a thing like this out loud before she said it out loud, and she said the rest of it before anybody could ask her for it.

**"There is no man in this. I want that said by me and not by somebody else later on. Nobody is standing behind any of those four places. Nobody wants the second one left. This is a road that is soft and a ford that will not take a barrow and one man who is the only one in this holding that four counties will open a gate to. That is all of it. That is what a refusal by a village would have sounded like and this is not one."**

**"I am not saying the two things this morning either,"** Hesta Lyle said, and did not put the pencil down. **"I have had four days to find a way to draw that line so it comes out with four places on it, and there is not one, and I did the sum three times."**

She tapped the pencil once on the paper.

**"A woman who has just shown a yard a road and a ford and a number of days has said the hardest thing there is in this holding about its own reach. A second sentence at noon would mean the yard remembered the second one and forgot the ford, and the ford is the part anybody can go and check."**

Soren Rill, who had been standing at the end of the table with his hands behind him since she began, spoke next.

**"I have been angry at a sum for four days,"** he said. **"And I came into this yard on Monday ready to be angry at Thornwild for a whole morning, and I want to say one thing about that before this week turns over. I was angry at the right sum and the wrong reason, and the right reason is on that sheet, and it is a road."**

He looked at the four names at the top of it and did not put either hand in his pockets.

Then the wall, and the launder, and the boards, which were given quickly because it was a Friday and everybody in the yard was standing about with the look of people who had just been shown a sheet of paper they would be thinking about all afternoon.

**One thousand and two hundred and thirty-three hundredweight.**

**"Five off, and that makes the four hundred and seventeenth of it,"** the clerk said. He put the chalk against the wall and left it there.

**"The half that went overnight was the falling one and it is a hundred and eighty-seven. The rising one is a hundred and ninety-seven and it has stood since Thursday. Three hundred and eighty-four."**

He wiped his hands down his coat.

**"I am not adding those two together in front of this yard today. There has been enough sum in it since ten o'clock to last a week, and the two halves are two figures and I would rather they stayed two."**

Harlan Vetch walked to the near board in the middle of the day and put his own eyes on it rather than take anybody's word for it, which he does about twice a week, and came back and gave the pair in the order he saw them. Eight hundred and one at this end, and then the walk of forty-seven to the other one, which stands at eight hundred and forty-eight. Forty-seven is how far apart they are and not a third number that goes with them. The drawer behind the one he started at is shut, and the only key to it is on a nail in the one room door.

By the eleventh hour the yard had gone back to work and wanted the figures out of the way before the light went, so the clerk gave the long sheet in one breath and did not put his hand near it.

**"Four hundred and fifteen on the sheet, and the clause under it is the four hundred and fourteenth out of four hundred and sixteenth."**

He wiped the chalk on his sleeve.

**"I am aware that I have said those two numbers in that order on a great many mornings. I am also aware that a man who reads a clause before he reads the figure is a man who will get one of them wrong eventually, and I would rather that happened on a morning nobody had a sheet out for."**

Kellan Rusk went to the rack with both hands occupied and read the three off the tops of them where they stood.

**"Three hundred and eleven on the register form, two hundred and seventeen on the charter, three hundred and five on the ruled line in Silling's book with nothing on it,"** he said, and put them back square.

**"I have stopped leaning on the shelf to say that last one and I am going to keep stopping,"** he said, **"because a hand against a shelf is the first step to a figure being a habit instead of a report."**

He said it without the hand, and nobody in that yard noticed except Nia Vale, who noticed and said nothing.

Nia Vale stood at the end of the long table and gave the standing of the holding off the shelves in the order she can reach them, one hand on each.

**"Thirty on the compost line and not one load out of it. Eleven journeys on the barrow and a floor. Fifteen lines in the use log. Seven sessions in the book, and a Friday enters none. Fifty-three in the requests and fifty-three in the section nine notes, and neither of them took Hesta Lyle's sheet with the ruled line down the middle of it, which is the correct answer and I am glad of it."**

She let her hand fall.

**"And thirty-nine blanks in the one room, which has not moved either, and a rule under them standing open and empty and opening nothing."**

The count in force did not rise on a Friday and rose by nothing, and it is a hundred and forty-one with twenty-nine taken off that number and a hundred and twelve of them not. No field went out this morning and nobody is blamed for one that stayed in. And nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything, because the choice on that sheet was not left lying about for somebody to pick up: a pencil settled it at ten o'clock in front of eleven people, and the road and the ford settled it before that.

Nobody went near the long table on this Friday. It stood down the middle of the yard with three papers on it: the first face up where it has lain a hundred and fourteen days, the second folded back on a crease older than any of this, and the third with a name and a date and a hand and a seal.

There are about nine inches of bare board either side of the first. Not one of the three has been entered, none has been refused, and no column has ever been cut under any of them in this holding. Nothing in the third of them is wrong, which is the difficulty with it.

Nia Vale opened the board in the one room at about the fourth hour and shut it again without writing anything on it, which is her Friday habit. Thirty-nine blanks, the same at seven as at noon, and underneath them a rule standing open and empty and opening nothing.

The shelf for what this holding does not know has six ruled rows on it and no seventh. Nothing was fetched from the man of about seventy and nothing was put to him anywhere on the day.

The ladder stands against the tool house wall with nobody on it. The low board holds its offer with no day written on it, unpicked and not withdrawn. The ring of bare ground is still bare. And the door nine hundred yards off went past at the eleventh hour and was not read.

Nobody thanked anybody in this yard on this Friday for the sheet and nobody forgave anybody for it either, and a man with a barrow stood in the middle of the yard at noon looking at four names at the top of a piece of paper and did not put either of his hands in his pockets.

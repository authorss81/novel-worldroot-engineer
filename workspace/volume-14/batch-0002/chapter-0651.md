# Chapter 651

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Seventeenth, And A Decision Said Once And Not Made Into Anything

The pair was not read on the Tuesday and Nia Vale said so at the middle table before the book was out of the rack, and then she said why, which nobody had asked her for.

**"Because a week ago I copied every figure in this yard onto my own slate to be sure of them, and I have had a piece of paper in this coat for a fortnight that is in no book and has never been on that table,"** she said. **"A woman who stands in a yard at seven in the morning and reads a pair of figures aloud is a woman who is being trusted with figures. I would rather be the woman who says no to a reading this once, on a morning when nothing depends on it, than be the woman everybody checks a sheet in."**

She put the book back on the rack herself and squared it, which nobody asked for either.

**"It is not modesty. If you want the honest reason it is that I have got something in this coat that would look very odd on the table beside a figure I had just said out loud, and I would rather nobody worked that out from watching me."**

Then the launder, which came down about half past eight with the middle road soft for the sixth day running, and the clerk was at the wall with the slate before it had finished running.

**One thousand and two hundred and thirty-five hundredweight.**

The clerk had the whole of the window written out before the launder had finished arriving.

**"Three hundred and eighty-one,"** he said. **"A hundred and eighty-five falling and a hundred and ninety-six rising. The rising one went up by one overnight, and a Tuesday is an even morning and an even morning takes it, and the falling one has stood still since Monday."**

Only then did he look down the road.

**"One thousand and two hundred and thirty-five hundredweight, and the four hundred and fourteenth of it, on eight on."**

Tova Reed was at the seed house bench at the seventh hour and she was still there at noon, and between those two hours she said one sentence, and she said it to the yard, and about nine people heard it.

**"I am giving the seed vault up,"** she said. **"It goes to the water. Not the seed work. The seed work stays where it is and I stay at this bench. The vault is the room at the north side with the three shelves in it, and that room is going to be a water room, and I am going to be the one who goes in there with a chalk line on the floor."**

She put a tray down on the bench and squared it with her hand.

**"That is all the announcement there is. I am not standing here to be given anything and there is nothing to be given. Do not make a thing of it in this yard and I will not have made a thing of it anywhere."**

Soren Rill said the question that was in everybody's mouth and he asked it plainly and did not soften it.

**"Why now, and why water."**

**"Because of what I said on Friday and I have not moved my position by a yard since I said it,"** Tova Reed said. **"The branches going are the ones outside the zone, and the zone ended four miles off a long time ago and did not end politely."**

She put one hand flat on the glass.

**"A house on the wrong side of that is a house with no shade and a road instead of a path, and the last hour of its light is gone, and I can put a tray on that step and it will sit there. Water goes up a pipe that does not care whose land it came out of. Seed only reaches a person who is standing somewhere it can reach, and there are four hundred people in this column and a growing number of them are not standing anywhere."**

She looked at the trough.

**"I have been in three county offices and not one of them had that in it and one of them had a schedule that says what is going and does not say what a person should do about it. So that is my answer and it took a fortnight and it is not a sacrifice and I will thank nobody for hearing it, and if anybody in this yard tries to put a hand on me about it I will go back inside and shut the door."**

Nobody put a hand on her. Nobody said that it was a shame and nobody said that she had given something up, and the mason, who was at the north end and who has refused a figure on a wall four times, said the only thing that was said about it in the yard for the rest of the day.

**"A room is a room,"** Harlan Vetch said. **"It does what it is for. That is the whole of what I know about a room."**

The man of about fifty was on his way out to the far side of the yard at about the eleventh hour and came back the long way along the hedge, nine hundred yards of it, and did not stop at the door with the number scratched in its seventh column, and he did not stop because nobody asked him to stop and nobody said anything to him about it on the way past, which is the thing the four mornings behind this one were written down for.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, at the gate, to nobody in particular. **"Because in six weeks I am going to hand over a key and a drawer, and the six weeks have not been noticed with anybody yet because the notice is still a word and not a paper. A man who says the hardest sentence he knows in a yard this week is a man who has decided something about himself, and I have a key to hand over and I have not decided anything about myself except that I want the six weeks noticed with me."**

He put his hand on the key on the nail as he said it, which he has done perhaps twice in four years, and did not take it off.

**"If it is paper then somebody can hold me to it and the date does the carrying instead of me. That is why it is a word. You may write that down."**

Kellan Rusk had the three ages and gave them quickly at the rack, because a Tuesday is a day the whole yard wants two figures out of him before noon. The form is at three hundred and eight days and the charter at two hundred and fourteen. The second ruled line in Silling's book stands at three hundred and two with nothing written on it, and he gave that one last, with his thumb on the shelf and not on the book.

Hesta Lyle put the long sheet flat with the heel of her hand because the wind had got under one corner of it.

**"Four hundred and twelve,"** she said, **"and the small hand underneath reads the four hundred and eleventh out of four hundred and thirteenth, which is a step one way and a step the other, the way it has been every morning of the four years I have stood at this table."**

She squared the paper up again.

**"Four lines under the whole of it, each wanting two signatures, and not one signature on any of them, and still no column anywhere in this holding cut for what a signature would be agreeing to."**

The near board is seven hundred and ninety-eight and the far one is eight hundred and forty-five, and the drawer behind the near one is shut, and its key is on its nail at this hour and was on it at first light.

The count in force stood where it stood on Friday, at a hundred and forty-one with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and twelve not, because a Tuesday is not a morning the rotation comes round on. No field was taken and nobody was blamed for a field that waited.

Harlan Vetch counted seven things off the wall of the tool house on his way in, in the order he passes them, and got to the end and said nothing, which is the whole of his report.

Thirty paid on the compost line at the gate, and nothing let out in it. Eleven journeys on the barrow, and a floor, not a target. Seven sessions in the book, and a Tuesday takes none. Fifty-three in the requests and fifty-three in the section nine notes. Six ruled rows against what this holding does not know, with the seventh space clear. Thirty-nine blanks in the one room, and the rule under them open and empty. And the mark on this wall, four inches, and two forks, and no third fork.

Harlan Vetch, who has been building the north end of that wall for four years, has never once been asked to look at the long table, and he said so on his way out.

**"Three papers on that table and I have never read one of them,"** he said. **"I know the top one is a hundred and eleven days old because a clerk says so out loud every morning of the week. I know there are about nine inches of clear board either side of it, because I have walked past it four times a day for four years."**

He shifted the hod off his shoulder.

**"The second is folded on a crease and shut. The third carries a name and a date and a hand and a seal and nobody here can put a finger on anything wrong with it. In all that time none of the three has been entered, refused, or ruled under. I would not want it different, because the second I take down I would have to read."**

Harlan Vetch found the weep at the joint at the back of the tap house about the second hour and put his thumb on it and left it there long enough for two people to see him do it, and then he went and looked at the standing pipe behind it, which is the same pipe it was standing behind when the joint went in.

**"It weeps and it holds,"** he said. **"A Tuesday has now happened to it and a Tuesday proves nothing. A hard week is not a Tuesday. If anybody in this yard says it has settled after a week I will take the chalk off that wall myself."**

Nobody said it had settled.

Nothing was fetched from the man of about seventy on this Tuesday and nothing was put to him at any hour of it, and Nia Vale went past the board in the one room on her way to the long sheet and stopped in front of it and did not write anything on it. Thirty-nine blanks, and a second rule underneath them standing open and empty.

Six ruled rows for what this holding does not know, with nothing cut above them. The ladder stands un climbed. The low board holds its offer, undated, unpicked, unreturned. Nobody has put a foot on the ring of bare ground.

Nobody in this yard was asked to choose anything on this Tuesday morning, and the one decision that was made in it was made by the only person in the yard who had the standing to make it, and she made it about a room.

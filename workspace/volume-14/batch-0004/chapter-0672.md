# Chapter 672

## The Fifteenth Of The Eighteenth, And A Cistern Being Filled By Hand Because No Pipe Had Been Agreed On

The pair was read on the Tuesday at the seventh hour by Renn Ashby and it was read quickly, because on the Tuesday everybody in this yard is on the middle road by half past seven.

**"One hundred and eighty-eight of three hundred and fifty-nine,"** he said, and shut the book on his thumb, which is not how he shuts a book. **"Of this month, and I am off down the middle road by eight with the barrow, and if a yard wants a reader who lingers over it they can go and hire one."**

He got off his heel and went out through the gate and there was no comment from anybody about it.

The cistern in the north-side room had been standing against that wall for a week with nothing in it, and on the Tuesday morning the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden began putting water into it by hand from the trough at the end of that room, and everybody in the yard watched her do it and nobody said anything about it for about two hours.

**"The trough is empty by the second hour,"** she said, on the fourth trip, without stopping. **"It has been empty by the second hour since the branch came back on the Friday and it was empty by the first hour twice last week. That cistern takes nine of these a day on its own and I have done the first three of them, so put that against whatever anybody in this yard wants to put against it."**

She set the can down on the floor by the cistern and went back for the fourth.

**"I am not asking this holding to feel anything about that. I am telling the yard how many trips a day it is, because every figure that gets read out in this place is somebody's arithmetic and this one has been nobody's until this morning."**

Odile Vray took the ninth trip without being asked and without making anything of it, and she was in the yard for a second day running because the second place had told her to sit where the second place's word could be repeated.

**"Nine trips is not a pipe,"** she said, on the step. **"Nine trips is what happens in a room while a pipe is being argued about, and if a pipe comes in the spring then there is no ninth trip, and if a pipe does not come then this is a cistern that fills itself by hand until somebody comes and looks at it."**

Nia Vale came through with her cloth and wiped the floor of the north-side room where the water had gone across the boards, and did not say anything to either of them about it.

**"That line is paid at thirty-one, and not one load has ever been let out of it in thirty-one or in thirty,"** she said. **"It came up a fortnight ago and that is the whole of what happened to it."**

She looked at the cistern while she folded the cloth.

**"Three sheets on that long table and I have gone round every one of them with the cloth this morning. The oldest has been lying there a hundred and thirty-two days. The middle one I have not opened in four years and I am not going to open it today. The third is the one an office would call correct, and I have read it and I cannot see the fault in it, and I have stopped looking for the fault."**

She stopped with the cloth in both hands.

**"Not one of the three has been filled in, none of them has been turned down, and this holding has never yet drawn a column under a sheet of paper, and I have wiped round all three and put the cloth back over my shoulder."**

She hung the cloth back over her shoulder and turned to the wall.

**"Eight hundred and nineteen at this end of the wall and eight hundred and sixty-six at the far end of it, and forty-seven of wall between the two, and the drawer behind this end is shut and the key is on its nail inside that doorway."**

The launder came down at half past eight and the clerk was at the wall with the trough side of the yard between him and the sun.

**"One thousand and two hundred and sixty hundredweight,"** he said. **"Five off, and the four hundred and thirty-fifth of the run. A Tuesday is an odd morning and on an odd morning in this holding the half that falls in the night is the one that falls first. The falling half is a hundred and ninety-six and the rising half stood at two hundred and six."**

He went along under the line with his thumb to make sure the chalk had taken, and it had not.

**"Four hundred and two between the two of them. I have not added them in front of anybody and I am not going to start on a morning when this yard has nine trips of water coming through it."**

Hesta Lyle came through with the long sheet under her arm and put it on the middle table and did not open it, which nobody in the yard had seen her do.

**"You want the shape of that sheet and not the sheet, because the sheet has been on this table every morning of the four years and this morning it is in my coat and I have not decided whether that is a decision or a Tuesday."**

She put her hand flat on the wood.

**"The figure on the top of it is four hundred and thirty-three. Two smaller ones are written underneath, four hundred and thirty-second on the low side and four hundred and thirty-fourth on the high side, and it is the same hand in the same two places that it has been for nine years."**

She put her hand flat on the wood.

**"Four places and no fifth. The second place has said its word. The first place has said nothing and the third place has said nothing and I have been waiting nine mornings for one of them to speak and none of them has and I did not expect one and I checked anyway."**

Sera Quill had the slate out and read the three ages off it and then put it back in her coat without turning it round for the yard, which is the first time she has done that on a morning when the slate was out.

**"Three hundred and twenty-nine days on the form. Two hundred and thirty-five on the charter. And the third of those ages, which belongs to a ruled line in Silling's own book, stands at three hundred and twenty-three with nothing written against it and nothing going to be written on it by me."**

She got the slate back into the pocket of her coat and shut the flap.

**"I am not saying either of the two sentences this yard has on it this morning,"** she said, **"and the reason is not modesty and I am tired of saying it is not modesty. It is that a slate in this holding is the only thing anybody here can check. If I put the slate away and then said a permanent sentence in the same hour, then the two of them go together in every account of this morning that anybody keeps, including mine."**

Harlan Vetch was on the north end with his back to the yard all morning and came down about the eleventh hour and stood at the cistern door and looked at the nine wet marks on the boards.

**"Nine marks wet on the boards again,"** he said, **"and a cistern half up and a hose that goes nowhere, and every one of those marks is a woman in this holding going down to that trough and coming back up with a can. I would like this yard to notice that I have built nothing this week."**

He put his thumb on the door frame and not on the brick.

**"The joint at the back of the tap house weeps where it wept and holds where it held and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe. I have said that at this yard six times now and I am going to go on saying it until somebody stops me, and what stops me is somebody saying it has settled and I will take the chalk off that wall with my own hands."**

He looked at the boards inside the door rather than at the cistern.

**"There are eleven journeys marked against that barrow and no twelfth, and eleven is a floor and a floor is not a target. Fifteen lines in the use log with nothing laid above the fifteenth. And seven sessions stand in this holding's own book, and a Tuesday enters none of them."**

He put his thumb on the cistern door frame and looked at the wet marks instead of at the yard.

**"Two other books in this yard sit at fifty-three apiece, the one being the requests and the one being the section nine notes, and no two mornings of the four years of them have been the same morning. I count courses for a living, and a thing with a floor under it is a different kind of thing from a thing with a top on it, and this holding is built out of the first kind."**

He turned round and looked back along the yard.

**"And I am not putting a figure on that north end and I have said so five times now, and this morning somebody in this yard is going to want the sixth, and the reason is the same reason it has been every time, which is that a wall is not a debt and a mason is not an office."**

The man of about fifty stopped at the cistern on his way to the far end of the yard with the drawer key not in his hand, because the key is never in his hand, and looked at the boards for a moment.

**"Thirty-nine blanks on the board in that room with the rule underneath them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and a return is not due in this holding until the next one is due,"** he said. **"Six ruled rows on the shelf for what we do not know, and nothing has been cut above the sixth. And there is a man of about seventy in this yard at twenty-nine fetchings who was not fetched today and was not asked anything."**

He went on up the yard.

**"The weeks I asked to be noticed with me on that key are going on being noticed with me, and the drawer is shut, and the key is on its nail inside that doorway and it will be on it at the ninth hour tonight."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had said it at the fourth trip and she said the rest of it at the ninth, with the can in her hand and nobody in the room.

**"I have two sentences I could say in this yard this morning and I am going to say neither of them,"** she said. **"The reason is that I have carried water past this gate every morning for four years and not one morning of it has ever had a line of its own in a book in this holding. A woman with no line does not stand up in front of one and make a permanent speech. Let somebody with a line say it."**

She set the can down and went out through the gate for the tenth.

Soren Rill came in about the second hour with the mud to the ankle again and stood at the middle table with his hands flat on the wood and did not look at anybody.

**"Nobody has climbed anything. The ladder against that tool house wall has no rung of its own gone and no rung of anybody's on it, and no figure anywhere in this yard is set against either of the two people it belongs to. There is a thing on the low board that has never been dated, never been picked up and never been taken back. And the bare ground inside those acres has taken no foot of mine today and no tape and no price."**

He lifted one hand off the wood and put it on the end of the trough.

**"There are nine trips of water going into a cistern in that room because four places on a column cannot agree on a pipe, and I have watched this yard all morning and not one person has said the word cheap about it. I want that noticed while it is true, because it will not be true for the rest of the month."**

Tova Reed came in from the seed house at the fourth hour with her sleeves pushed up and stood at the north-side room door and looked at the bench along the cistern wall where one row of it was still bare.

**"That row is still bare and it is going to be bare until I know what goes in it, and I do not know yet,"** she said. **"Nobody in this yard has asked me and nobody in this yard is going to ask me."**

She looked down the bench.

**"The ladder against the tool house wall has nobody on it and nobody standing under it. The low board carries something with no date written on it and no hand near it. And the bare ring inside the eleven acres is not a thing I am going to walk round this week to look at, and I have not walked it."**

She went back into the seed house.

Odile Vray went out through the gate about the fifth hour with the second place's word still in her own mouth and not on any paper of this holding's, and there is a door nine hundred yards off that has a figure cut into the seventh column of its frame, and it went past her eye as she walked and she did not stop for it and nobody in that yard put it in front of her.

Nine times that day a woman went down to the trough at the end of that room and came back up with a can, and the cistern stood against the wall at dusk with a hose that went nowhere, and the count in force was where Saturday left it because a Tuesday is not a morning this holding gives anybody out.

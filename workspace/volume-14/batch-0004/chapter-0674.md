# Chapter 674

## The Seventeenth Of The Eighteenth, And A Woman In A Coat Naming The Place She Was Not Going To Stand In

The cart was loaded at the gate before the seventh hour on the Thursday, and Renn Ashby read the pair at seven with the book propped open on the middle table, and the yard stood round it while it took.

**"One hundred and eighty-nine of three hundred and sixty-one,"** he said. **"Of this month. It takes four minutes and I am going to take four minutes and the cart is loaded and can wait four minutes for me."**

Tova Reed had the last tray on the cart bed with a board between it and the next one and she did not put the board in until he had said the figures.

**"That is the whole of what I came back for,"** she said, and got on the cart and went out through the gate with nine trays to nine doors inside a day's walk.

Harlan Vetch put the hod down outside the one room door about the ninth hour and did not go straight back up to the north end, and stood there longer than his work required, which is a thing he does about once a season.

**"This yard has two sentences of its own and I am going to leave both of them where they are this morning,"** he said, to nobody in particular. **"The reason is that I said one of them once in this yard, four years ago, at noon, and nobody answered me, and I have never once minded that nobody answered me."**

He wiped his hands down his coat.

**"A man who has said one of them does not get to stand up afterwards and say that he is not saying them. That is not a rule anybody in this yard made. That is what happened to me, and I would rather be the only man who remembers it properly than be the only man who says it."**

Then Sera Quill came in through the gate with her slate under her arm, and she had been outside since the sixth hour, and she came the whole length of the yard to the tap joint at the back of the tap house and stood and listened to it for a while, and said what she had come to say standing up.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

A man went past with the barrow and asked Harlan Vetch whether the cistern wanted filling again, and Harlan Vetch said it wanted filling and did not stop, and the yard did what this yard does with a thing nobody was expecting on a Thursday.

Hesta Lyle came out of the one room doorway with her coat on over her work clothes, and she had the long sheet under her arm, and she looked at Sera Quill for a while without saying anything about what had just been said.

Then she put the sheet on the middle table and kept her hand on it.

**"I am not going to pretend that what she said had nothing to do with what I have been standing at this table with for eleven mornings,"** she said. **"It had. And here is the place, and I am going to name it once, because it has not been named in this yard by anybody."**

She turned the sheet over and put her thumb on the head of it.

**"That column has a far end. Not a man at this table. A far end, and it is up the road in a room none of you has been in, and it is where the head is owned. That far end has sent this holding paper four times in a month and every piece of it is correct, and every piece of it is correct because the far end does not hold anything."**

She took her hand off the paper.

**"It issues. It does not carry. A person who owns a head for forty years has never once had to get up at the fifth hour and open a sluice, and it never will, because the day it had to would be the day it found out that it had been holding it. I am not saying anything against the room. I am saying that the room at the end of that column is the last place on it that will ever be asked to stand in the rain about a pipe, and I am not going to be the one who asks it."**

Somebody asked her what that meant for the pipe in the north-side room, and she answered him without turning round.

**"It means the pipe comes off a head whose far end is somebody else's problem on a wet morning. It means a cistern in this yard can be full every morning of next spring with nobody in an office knowing whether it is full or empty."**

She squared the sheet.

**"And I am not going to be one of the two that holds it. I have stood at this table nine years with a sheet in front of me and I have a road and a ford and a number of days in my head and that is the whole of what I am, and I would rather be the woman who brought the sheet than the woman who is holding the far end of it."**

Odile Vray said something from the gate about there being two and neither being her, and about who the two would be, and nobody in the yard answered her, and she came in and put her bag down by the cistern door and said nothing more about it.

Kellan Rusk said it flatly and once and did not soften it.

**"Then the number of people holding that is two and this holding is not one of them,"** he said. **"No offer has been made in this yard this morning by anybody and I would like it recorded that no offer was made."**

The water came down at half past eight, and the clerk had the copy sheet out and read the line to his slate rather than to the yard, because Tova Reed's cart was still standing at the gate and nine people were sitting where a voice goes.

**"Six on, and it is one thousand and two hundred and sixty-three hundredweight, which is the four hundred and thirty-seventh run,"** he said. **"Thursday is an odd morning and on an odd morning here it is the lower half that drops. That is a hundred and ninety-seven, and the upper half stood at two hundred and seven where it stood yesterday. Four hundred and four of a window between them, and I have not said the whole out loud and nobody has asked me to."**

Sera Quill had her slate out by then and did not put it on the table and did not turn it round, and read it in her own order with her thumb moving down the board rather than across it.

**"The first age on this slate is two hundred and thirty-seven days and it belongs to the charter, because a cistern answers under a charter and I have watched four men in this holding be surprised by that fact,"** she said. **"The second is three hundred and thirty-one and it belongs to the form."**

She moved her thumb down the board to the last of the three.

**"And there is a third of them, three hundred and twenty-five, and it belongs to a ruled line in a book that is not mine, and I am going to say no more about that one than the figure itself, because a man put a slate on a table in front of nine people at nine o'clock today and told them where his figures came from, and I am not going to be the second object in this yard that arrives out of a pocket with no rule shown under it."**

She wrote on the slate in her own hand, small, at the top where there was room.

**"A long sheet I have never seen the front of has four hundred and thirty-five on the top of it,"** she said. **"Four hundred and thirty-fourth sits a step under it and four hundred and thirty-sixth a step above, in the same hand on both. I copied those two off the back of that sheet in the spring and I have had no use for them until a woman named the far end of a column in this yard at ten o'clock."**

She put the slate away in her coat.

**"The two reckonings are on there and they are thirteen apart and I have said that seven times, and the thirteen is the honest part of it."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the gate board first and the two boards after it, which is the other way round from what she usually does, and the yard noticed.

**"It is paid at thirty-one, and not one load has ever gone out of that line either at thirty-one or at thirty, and it came up by one a fortnight ago and has not shifted since,"** she said. **"If it is written down anywhere in this holding I would like it written down in those words and I would like somebody else to write it."**

She came back up the wall from the gate.

**"Eight hundred and twenty-one at the near end. Eight hundred and sixty-eight behind the tool house. Forty-seven of wall between them, and a drawer behind the near one that is shut, with the key on a single nail inside the doorway of the one room where it was at the sixth hour and will be at the ninth hour tonight."**

She folded the cloth over her shoulder and stood at the cistern door looking down at the wet marks on the boards.

**"Nine marks. That is a floor and it is not a figure anybody in this yard is trying to pass."**

She came back up the wall.

**"Three sheets on that long table, and the oldest of them is a hundred and thirty-four days old with about nine inches of board standing clear at either end of it, and I went round that one with the cloth the way I go round a figure. The middle one is folded shut and I have not opened it in four years. The third has a name, a hand and a seal on it."**

She put the cloth down on the table beside the second one.

**"Not one entered, not one refused, and no column cut under any of the three."**

Odile Vray was at the gate when the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came out past her with her cans, and she counted the standing of this holding out loud on the step in the order of what she would have had to carry.

**"That barrow has eleven journeys marked against it and nobody has ever put a twelfth beside them. The use log stops at fifteen lines and there is a clean space above the fifteenth where a sixteenth would go. And there are seven sessions in this holding's book and a Thursday enters none."**

She put her bag down at her feet and put her thumb on the middle item.

**"That is the list a woman from another place would want, and I have just said it out loud on a step in front of a person from this holding, and neither of us is going to write it down."**

She put her hand on the gate post.

**"Inside that room there is a board with thirty-nine blanks cut in it, and the rule underneath those blanks stands open with nothing behind it, and today is the thirty-ninth time that rule has come back that way. On a shelf above the blanks there are six ruled rows set out for what nobody in that room can say, and there is nothing cut above the sixth one."**

She put her bag down at her feet.

**"I read all of that off a door frame on my way past this morning without stopping for any of it, and I am saying it now so that the second place knows what it is not being lent."**

She picked the bag up again.

**"The other two books are each at fifty-three and neither of them took the sentence that was said in the tap house this morning, and I would not have let them. And there is a man of about seventy in this yard whose figure is twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, not asked, and I am not going to ask him either."**

Harlan Vetch came back at the seventh hour and stood at the one room door with his hand on the frame.

**"It weeps and holds and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe,"** he said. **"Seven Tuesdays now. I am not going to be the man in this holding who says it has settled, and I have said that seven times and I will say it eight times in a fortnight."**

He looked at the cistern.

**"And if the far end of a thing is held by two people then a cistern is held by whoever keeps the hose off it, and that is me, and I have not said I would rather it was somebody else and I am not going to."**

Soren Rill came in through the gate about the eighth hour and stopped at the low board without touching it.

**"That ladder has gone up no rung of itself and I would like the yard to notice that nobody has ever made it go up one either. And there is not one figure in this yard set against either of the two people it belongs to. What is on the low board has never been dated and never been picked up and never been taken back."**

She looked across the middle of the eleven acres.

**"And the bare ring inside those acres has taken no foot today and no tape and no price, and I have been in this holding long enough to know that nobody is going to be the first to walk into it."**

He looked up and down the yard.

**"And a woman stood at the back of the tap house this morning and said a thing that has been said once in four years, and then a woman stood at a table and named the far end of a column out loud for the first time, and then nobody in this yard offered to be one of the two."**

He put his hands in his coat.

**"I have been waiting eleven mornings to hear whether this holding would put itself at the end of that column and it has not done it and it is not going to do it. That is the finding of the week and I am content to leave it there until somebody else speaks first."**

Later on, with the cart back in the gate empty and nine doors behind it, the cistern in the north-side room stood three fingers higher than it had stood at the sixth hour, the long sheet was back on the table where it lives, and the far end of a column had been named out loud in a yard for the first time in nine years by a woman who said before she spoke that she was not going to hold it.

# Chapter 668

## The Eleventh Of The Eighteenth, And Four Claims On One Head And A Word From The Second Place

The pair was read on the Friday at the seventh hour by Renn Ashby with the book propped open on the middle table and his thumb where it has been for four years.

**"One hundred and eighty-six of three hundred and fifty-five,"** he said. **"Of this month. I have said of this month every morning since the turn and nobody has come to tell me the frame moved, so I will go on not saying the frame."**

He got up off his heel in front of nine people, which is what he does, and nobody in this yard has ever once remarked on it.

Marek was in the north-side room with a length of string and a stub of chalk, laying out the line a pipe would have to take along the bottom of the north wall to reach a stopcock above a cistern that Harlan Vetch had finished a week ago and that no water has ever been in.

**"Four inches off the floor and not lower,"** he said, without looking up. **"Any lower and the trays standing along that wall sit in the damp by the middle of the week. If anybody in this yard wants to make that argument to the woman who works at those trays they can come and make it to me first and I will give them the whole of it in one sitting."**

He tied the string off at the corner and stepped back down it to see whether it ran true.

**"And it goes nowhere,"** he said. **"It is chalk on a floor. It is there so that when the pipe does come I know which way it wanted to come from, and if the second place stays shut to us to the end of the month then this chalk lies here until somebody scrapes it off with a boot."**

The water came down the middle road at half past eight. The clerk was at the wall with his thumb under the chalk and gave the two figures out in the order he writes them, which is the only order he has ever read them in.

**"One thousand and two hundred and fifty-four hundredweight,"** he said. **"Five off. And the four hundred and thirty-first of the run."**

He wiped the wall under the line with the side of his hand before he said the next part, which is what he does.

**"And here is the thing I want said out loud in a yard this morning, because I have been saying it at this wall for nine months and somebody in this place has it the other way round in his head. A Friday is an odd morning. On an odd morning in this holding the half that goes down in the night is the one that goes first. That is the falling half. The falling half came down to a hundred and ninety-four in the night and the rising half stood where it stood yesterday at two hundred and four, and the two of them are a window of three hundred and ninety-eight."**

He put his thumb on the wall under the first of the two figures again.

**"The last county had it the other way round, and it had it that way round for four years before that, and a man who has read those two lines since he was a boy will go on reading them the wrong way round for the rest of his life unless somebody says it out loud in daylight. So I have said it. Say it back to me at the table if you like. It does not change what the wall says."**

Nobody at the table said it back to him, and two of them looked at each other over a piece of paper.

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet squared in front of her with her forearm laid across it, the way she holds it when the yard is in it, and she did not turn the paper round.

**"Four hundred and twenty-nine at the head of it,"** she said. **"And underneath, in the small hand, four hundred and twenty-eighth out of four hundred and thirtieth. One step under and one step over, and that hand has made that shape every morning for nine years and I did not teach it."**

She put two fingers on the wood beside the paper and not on the paper.

**"And that column carries four places and there is no space cut into it for a fifth. This holding has asked that head for a pipe and I have not put the request on that sheet, because a sheet with our name at the top of it is a sheet that somebody in another county can hold up, and this yard has never once asked to be held up by anybody."**

She turned her head very slightly toward the gate.

**"And nobody in this yard is going to put a figure on what this holding has already borne either. That is not modesty. There is no column here for it. A man who says that is being careful with somebody else's money."**

The woman from the second place was at the gate when that was said and she came the whole length of the yard with her hood down, and nobody had sent for her and she had come nine miles.

**"Then I will say the thing I came for and I will say it once,"** she said, **"and the second place will not be first."**

The yard did what this yard does and waited.

**"You all know what first costs, because it is written on a sheet in your own holding. The second place has done its own sum twice and it comes to the same figure both times, and the sum says that a place which is first pays the whole of the pipe and gets the water. The second place cannot lay a loaded barrow across the ford this month whatever the weather does, so the second place could be first on paper and would not be first in the ground until the spring."**

She put both hands flat on the side board of the cart where it stood by the gate.

**"So the second place says no to being first, out loud, to somebody who will write it down, and nobody here is to come back to us in a week and ask us to put our name on a page again. If you want the second place to be first then wait until the ford will take a loaded barrow and ask us again, and I will come nine miles again if the answer has changed."**

Kellan Rusk took his copy sheet off the rack and wrote it down in the small hand he uses for things that are not ours, and then he did not write it on anything else.

**"That is on my sheet and it is on my sheet only,"** he said. **"The column's own paper has four places on it and a ruled line and two signatures wanted at the foot of each, and this yard does not cut columns. I will carry that to the office on the ordinary day with the rest of it and it will arrive as a fact about the second place and not as a line in this holding's hand."**

He squared the copy sheet on the table and put his weight on his back heel.

**"And the count in force stands at a hundred and forty-six and it does not come round on a Friday, because a Friday is not a morning this holding gives anybody out. Twenty-nine went at their own hours on the ordinary run. A hundred and seventeen are on the books at this hour waiting for a turn that has not arrived yet, and the half of them is above a hundred and will be above a hundred next Friday as well."**

He did not shut the rack, which he usually does at that point.

**"No field left this holding today. None was due out of it. There is nobody in this yard who will be blamed for a field that stood where the book had it standing, and I have said that on this morning of this kind more times than I have bothered to count."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and wiped round the two figures rather than across them, which she does and has never explained to anybody.

**"This end of the wall says eight hundred and fifteen. The end of it past the tool house says eight hundred and sixty-two. Forty-seven of masonry is all that stands between those two figures and there is not one other thing between them."**

She moved along the wall to where the stone changes.

**"A drawer is let into the stone behind the figure at this end. It is shut and it stays shut. Its key is on a nail in the doorway of the one room, and it was on that nail when this yard came up at six this morning and it will be on it at the ninth hour tonight."**

She wrung the cloth out on the trough step.

**"There are three papers on that long table and the first has been lying there a hundred and twenty-eight days with about nine inches of bare board at each end of it, and those two nines have not moved since Thursday. The second is folded shut. The third carries a name, a hand and a seal, and there is nothing in it that anybody in this yard could call wrong. Not one of the three has been filled in, not one has been turned down, and no column has ever been cut under any of them."**

She put the cloth over her shoulder and stood looking at the cistern wall instead of at the wall with the figures on it.

**"And the compost line is paid at thirty-one, and nothing has ever been let out of that line, in thirty-one or in thirty. It went up by one on the Thursday before last and it came up because one more load was paid in and not because one went out. I would like somebody in this yard to write that sentence down so that it stops coming back the other way round."**

Sera Quill had the slate in her hand and turned it face up on the end of the long table rather than read it off the rack.

**"Three hundred and twenty-five days on the register form. Two hundred and thirty-one on the charter. And the second ruled line in Silling's own book is at three hundred and nineteen with nothing written on it and nothing going to be written on it by me."**

She put her finger under the last of the three and left it there.

**"I have said what is on that line four times in a yard now and it does not improve with saying, so this morning I have said it once and stopped. A rule with nothing behind it is not an oversight. It is a rule."**

Harlan Vetch left the north end at about the ninth hour, walked the length of the yard with the hod on his shoulder and the hod still in his hand, and stood in the doorway of the north-side room looking down at the chalk line along the floor.

**"That is four inches,"** he said. **"That is a foot and a half clear at the top of that course and you have put the run along the skirting so it will not come up through the trays. I would have chalked it the same way and I have never once said so to you and I am not going to start this week."**

He turned his head towards the wall at the north end without going near it.

**"Behind the tap house there is a joint that has done the same two things every day since the day it went in, which are weep and hold, and the pipe standing behind it is the pipe that was there before I ever touched it. I have chalked that joint twice this year and a third time is not going to happen, and if anybody in this holding tells me it has settled after a week I will take the chalk off that wall with my own two hands."**

He set the hod down outside the one room door and listened to it for a moment without going to look.

**"A pipe is my work and a leak is my work and they are not the same conversation, and this yard is going to have to keep them apart for as long as that cistern stands there."**

Marek wiped the chalk off his own thumb and did not put the stub down, and there was a stretch of the morning where nobody in that yard spoke and it was not an awkward silence.

**"There are two sentences this yard has on it and I am not saying either of them,"** he said. **"The reason is my own hands. I have had a stick of chalk in one of them since the sixth hour and I am going to have it in one of them until this chalk line comes up or comes off, and a man who says that sentence with a piece of chalk in his hand is a man who is working."**

He put the stub in his pocket.

**"When I am not holding anything I will not be saying them either, and I would rather that the yard got used to the not-saying than got used to the saying."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went the length of the shelves going out at dusk, reading the ends of them as she went, in an order nobody has ever asked her for, and stopped at the two books.

**"Fifteen lines in the use log and the space above the fifteenth is as bare as the day it was started. Eleven journeys marked against that barrow and no twelfth, and eleven is a floor and not a number anybody in this place is trying to pass. Seven sessions in this holding's book and a Friday takes none of them."**

She set the last can down on the step.

**"Inside the one room there is a board with thirty-nine blanks cut in it. The rule that sits under those blanks has come back open and with nothing behind it at the thirty-ninth time and it has never once opened anything."**

She picked up the cans and turned them over in her hands.

**"On a shelf above that board are six ruled rows set against what nobody in this holding knows, and there is nothing cut above the sixth. Two other books in this yard sit at fifty-three, the one being the requests and the one being the section nine notes, and this morning neither of them took a pipe off a wall."**

She picked her cans up again.

**"At the end of the north row there is a man of about seventy and the figure that stands against him is twenty-nine fetchings. Nobody fetched him today and nobody questioned him about it, and this place has never once gone over to that bench to ask him what number it is. That has been the arrangement since before I came and it will be the arrangement after I have gone."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end again at the eighth hour with nothing in his hands, which is not how he comes down that wall, and stood at the one room door.

**"Nine marks wet on the boards of that room this morning and a cistern that has been standing empty for a week,"** he said. **"I have not built one single thing this week and I would like that in the record in a mason's handwriting, because when a yard eventually does get its pipe there will be a man in it who remembers that every mark on those boards was made by somebody else's legs."**

He turned the hod over on his shoulder and looked at the cistern instead of at the chalk.

**"The four inches of chalk along that skirting is the right height and I would have chalked it the same way."**

Soren Rill came in from the gate near the end of the afternoon and looked at the tool house wall without walking up to it.

**"Nothing in this holding has been climbed, and there is no figure in this yard set against either of the two people that ladder belongs to. What is lying on the low board carries nothing that would say when it was put there, and nobody's hand has been near it, and it has been that shape for four years."**

He folded his arms and looked at the gate instead of the wall.

**"That ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has taken no boot of mine today, and no tape, and no price, and I am not going to walk over to it either."**

He looked round at the yard.

**"That word came down that lane from the second place this morning and it is the right word and I will not pretend to you that it makes anything easier. Four places and now four that have all said something about being first, and not one of them has said they do not want the water. That is the whole of what this afternoon has produced and I am going to let it stand at that for tonight."**

By dusk the chalk line lay along the skirting of the north-side room with nothing piped to it, four claims were on a sheet that had four places cut into it, and a woman who had walked nine miles was sitting on the gate step with her hood still down, and nobody in that yard had been asked anything about any part of it.

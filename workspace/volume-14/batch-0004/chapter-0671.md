# Chapter 671

## The Fourteenth Of The Eighteenth, And A Monday In Which A Sentence Came Out Of A Woman Without Anybody Answering It

The pair was not read on the Monday and the man who brought a notice up the lane in his own hand was the one who said so, which nobody in this yard expected.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so, and I am saying so before I have said anything else, because I have a paper in my coat that I came here to put into a hand and not into a yard."**

Idris Vane did not take the paper out. He stood inside the gate with his coat buttoned and the road still on his boots.

**"I take these in person because a notice served by post is a notice a man can argue with for a fortnight before anybody has to answer it. That is the whole of my trade and I do it the same way at four places on this column and I would do it the same way at a fifth."**

He looked at the middle table and at the rack and at nobody.

**"I have been in this yard at the seventh hour on a Monday before and there is a man in four counties who writes down what is read at a table and what is not, and he does not care which. I am not going to stand in front of a trough at the seventh hour being read at while I have a paper in my coat. Read it after I have been. I will still be here."**

He was still there at the ninth hour, and he gave the notice into Nia Vale's hands at the long table and she read it standing up and gave it back, and he did not ask her anything about it and she did not tell him anything about it.

Then the man of about fifty was on the step of the one room doing the thing he has done every morning for a season, which is standing beside the drawer that is let into the wall behind the near board.

**"Six weeks were asked to be noticed with me on that key and I have said the same three things about it every morning since and I am going to say them again this morning because that is the arrangement."**

He had his hand on the frame of the doorway and not on the drawer.

**"The first is that the notice is a word and not a paper and nobody in this holding has written it down anywhere, and the second is that the key is on its nail and the drawer is shut, and the third is that nobody is going to hurry me and nobody is going to stand here and tell me it is a hard thing to be asked to do. It is not a hard thing. It is a key and a drawer and a man of about fifty who would rather it was somebody else's."**

He turned round in the doorway.

**"The handover has not happened. It is going to happen in six weeks or it is going to happen later, and I am not going to be the man who decides which, and there is nothing in that for anybody here to be sorry about."**

Nobody in that yard said he was sorry about it, and nobody said it would be all right, and nobody put a hand on him, and that is now the whole of what this holding does about it.

The launder came down at half past eight and the clerk was at the wall.

**"One thousand and two hundred and sixty-five hundredweight,"** Harlan Vetch said, from the top of the north end, without coming down. **"Eight on, and the four hundred and thirty-fourth of the run. A Monday is an even morning, so the upper half is the one that takes it. Two hundred and six. The other one stood at a hundred and ninety-five where it stood yesterday, and the two of them make a window of four hundred and one."**

He wiped under the line with the side of his hand.

**"Three hundred and ninety-eight was Saturday, four hundred was yesterday morning, and this is four hundred and one. Three mornings and a round number in the middle of them and I have not written anything under it and there is nothing to write."**

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet squared in front of her and she did not turn it round.

**"That sheet has four hundred and thirty-two on the top of it,"** she said. **"Underneath the top there are two figures written small, and the lower one is four hundred and thirty-first and the higher one is four hundred and thirty-third, in the same hand at the same table for nine years, and I did not teach that hand and I cannot improve it."**

She kept two fingers on the wood beside the paper.

**"And the column has four places on it and no fifth, and the second place said its word on Friday and the third place has not said anything at all this week, and one of them has to be first and I have not got a name and I am not going to go and get one by asking anybody to choose."**

Tova Reed was in the north-side room at about the ninth hour moving the last of the trays along the floor and putting two boards under the ones nearest the wall, and nobody in that room asked her what goes in the bare row of the bench.

**"The boards are off a stack that was going to the tool house wall,"** she said. **"The floor is colder than the shelf and that is better for the seed and worse for my knees and I decided that a fortnight ago and I have not changed my mind about it since."**

She knelt down to get at the one behind the others.

**"And I am not saying either of the two sentences this place has on it,"** she said. **"The reason is that row. If I stand up in this yard and say a permanent thing then by lunch this room will have a crowd round the bench looking at the one row I have left bare and asking me what goes in it. I have not got an answer for that row and I am not going to manufacture one in a hurry to get round to a sentence."**

She got the tray in and stood up again.

**"Leave the row alone. That is all I am asking anybody for this morning and I am not going to be thanked for asking."**

The window at the north end was open on its hinge and the sound of the tap house came through it, and Nia Vale was in the room with the trays before anybody had thought to look for her, and she stood at the open window with her back half to the yard.

Somebody in the yard had asked her about the gate and somebody else had asked her about the notice, and she answered the first one and she answered the second one, and then she said the thing.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

A barrow went past outside the window. Harlan Vetch was on the north end and did not come down. Kellan Rusk was at the rack and turned a page he did not need to turn. The man of about fifty was still in the doorway of the one room with his hand on the frame, and he stayed exactly where he was.

Nobody answered her.

Nobody in that yard said a word to her about it, and nobody said it was a hard thing to say, and nobody thanked her for saying it and nobody said anything at all about her father, and nobody apologised to her for anything, and nobody's arm was touched, and the morning went on.

Marek was out of the yard at the fourth hour with the tape on his belt and he came back at the fifth hour and did not ask her about it, and that is the whole of what he did about it.

Sera Quill had the slate and turned it over and did not read anything off it.

**"The charter is at two hundred and thirty-four days and the register form at three hundred and twenty-eight, in that order, because that is the order a man wants them in if he is going to build something this spring."**

She put her thumb flat on the slate to hold the page.

**"And the third of those three ages is three hundred and twenty-two and it belongs to a ruled line in Silling's own book with an empty space behind it, and that space has been empty since before I came to this yard."**

She put the slate in her coat instead of leaving it on the table.

**"I have said what is on that line five times in a yard and I said it this morning once and then I put the slate away, and the slate has gone in my coat this morning for the first time in four years, and I would like that noticed and I would like nobody to ask me what it means."**

Nia Vale came out of the north-side room a little after the fifth hour and did the gate board with her cloth.

**"That gate line is paid at thirty-one, and no load has ever been let out of it."**

She came along the wall from the gate.

**"The two numbers on that wall this morning are eight hundred and eighteen and eight hundred and sixty-five, and the forty-seven of wall between them is the only difference there has ever been between them. The drawer behind the near one is shut and its key is on its nail in the one room doorway, where it will be at the ninth hour tonight."**

She wiped round the figures rather than across them.

**"Three sheets on that long table. The oldest has been lying there a hundred and thirty-one days, and there is about nine inches of clear board standing either side of it. The middle one is shut on its fold and has not been opened by anybody in four years. The third carries a name, a hand and a seal and there is nothing in it that I could object to if I sat down and tried."**

She came the rest of the way along the table and stopped opposite the second one.

**"Not one of the three has been entered, not one has been refused, and there is no column cut under any of them, and I have built a wall four feet from that table for four years and have never once been asked to read a word of it."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at the sixth hour and stood at the door of the tap house for a while without going in.

**"It weeps and holds and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe,"** he said. **"I am not chalking it again and I am not going to be the man who says it has settled after six Tuesdays."**

He put the hod down outside the one room door and stood listening again.

**"And I have said the two things I have to say about my own work this month, and neither of them is the one this yard is waiting for. If somebody in here thinks a mason has got nothing in him but a wall and a stick of chalk, they are welcome to that opinion right up until a cistern in this yard actually wants filling."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went the length of the shelves going out and read the ends of them as she went, without stopping at any of them, because she had said them often enough to say them at a walking pace.

**"Eleven journeys on that barrow and no twelfth. Fifteen lines in the use log and the space above the fifteenth as bare as the day somebody started it. Seven sessions in this holding's book, and a Monday takes none of them."**

She came back to the step for the can she had left.

**"There are two more books on that rack and both of them are at fifty-three, and they are not the same list and never have been, and neither of them took a sentence out of anybody's mouth this morning."**

She set the can down and picked it up again.

**"And there is a man of about seventy in this yard at twenty-nine fetchings, not fetched, not asked. And a door nine hundred yards off went past at the tenth hour, and it was not read, and it was not offered to anybody, and I have wiped round it and left it standing."**

Odile Vray had been at the middle table since the ninth hour with the second place's word in her own mouth and the second place's figures in her own head, and nobody had given her a piece of paper, and at about the eleventh hour she put both hands flat on the wood and gave the yard the shape of what she had been carrying since Saturday.

**"Eleven journeys marked against your barrow, and no twelfth. Fifteen lines in the use log with a clean space above the fifteenth. Seven sessions in that book and a Monday enters none."**

She put her thumb on the last item of her own list.

**"That is what a woman from another place would have had to write down, and I have not written it down, and there is nobody in this yard I would hand it to."**

She took her thumb off it.

**"And I will tell you the three things you would have wanted next to it. Two more books on that rack, both at fifty-three. A board in that one room has thirty-nine blanks cut into it, and the rule underneath those blanks stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. A shelf above it carries six ruled rows for what nobody in there can say, and nothing has been cut above the sixth."**

She took her thumb off the last item of her own list.

**"The man of about seventy in this yard stands at twenty-nine fetchings and nobody in this place has ever asked him which number it is, including me, and I have said on this step that I am not going to be the first."**

Soren Rill came in through the gate about the seventh hour with the mud dried on his boots from a road he had walked twice.

**"Nothing has been climbed in this holding in four years and there is no figure in this yard standing against either of the two people that ladder belongs to. What is on the low board still carries no date and has not been picked up and has not been taken back."**

He looked over at the middle of the eleven acres.

**"And the bare ring inside those acres took no foot today and no tape and no price, and I would like the yard to keep one day this week on which nobody walks over to look at it."**

He stood at the gate with his hands in his coat.

**"And this yard said the thing it says in the north-side room this afternoon and then it carried on, and I have been in this holding four years and I have never known a morning to carry on afterwards, and I would like that put down as the finding rather than anything else that was said in it."**

Two trays stood on the boards at the back of the north-side room floor and the row of bench along the cistern wall was bare at dusk, a notice had gone into a hand and not into a yard, and the count in force stood where Friday left it with nobody blamed for a field that had not come round.

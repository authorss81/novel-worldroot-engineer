# Chapter 677

## The Twentieth Of The Eighteenth, And A Sum That Came Out With One Place In It And Nobody Holding A Second

The pair was not read on the Sunday and a man with a leather case said so, which is the first time in four years that anybody who is not of this holding has put a morning in this yard out of his own mouth.

Ansell Dree stood by the gate with the case at his feet, square to the edge of the wall the way he put it down the last time, and he had a good coat on and he had been in the yard since the seventh hour.

**"It is not read this morning,"** he said. **"Not because it is my yard. I have said it because I have been standing in that gate for two hours with a case in my hand that has a figure in it, and I would rather stand here at the seventh hour with nobody reading anything out of anything than have nine people in this yard look at me while they count."**

Nobody asked him what the figure was.

**"It is in the case,"** he said, to nobody. **"It has been in the case since Wednesday. I am not going to put it on a table in a yard and I am not going to put it on the long sheet and I said when I left here that I would come back with a figure of my own, and I have come back with it, and it is still in the case."**

He put his hand flat on the case and left it there.

**"I will carry it back down that road the way I carried it up, and if somebody in this holding asks me next week why I did not open it, the answer is that the yard had a woman doing arithmetic in it at ten o'clock on Friday and a man refusing an offer at a cistern door at ten on Saturday and I am not standing in the middle of that with a piece of paper."**

Renn Ashby was at the middle table at that moment with the book closed under his hand and he did not open it.

**"And I am not saying either of the two sentences this place has on it,"** he said. **"I have stood at that table at the seventh hour four hundred times and the only thing I have ever had to do with this yard is put my thumb in a book and say two figures and shut it. If I stand up here one Sunday and say the permanent thing then from Monday morning every person in this holding who has ever listened to me will hear it under the two figures, and it will be under them on every morning after that, and they will not be able to hear the two figures again."**

He took his hand off the closed book.

**"A reader who says it once has taken the pair off the table for good. I am not going to do that on a Sunday for the sake of an hour."**

The rotation came round at about the eighth hour, because a Sunday is one of the twelve mornings it comes round on, and it came round onto a yard that had spent two days saying no to things.

**"The book says a hundred and forty-nine in force this morning,"** Kellan Rusk said, **"twenty-nine of them already gone out at whatever hour their own hour is, and a hundred and twenty of them not gone out."**

He did not put the second book down.

**"Ten mornings of this kind have now gone by and the figure has risen by one at each of them and by nothing else, and it will rise by one on the next one and on the one after that. And this holding gave nobody out today, because nobody was down for it, and there is nobody in this yard I am going to stand here and point at over a field that never came round."**

He squared the book against the table.

**"And a man with a case is standing at that gate and has not opened the case and there is nobody in this holding who has asked him to, and I would like it noticed that the first thing a man does with a figure of his own in a yard is put it on somebody else's table."**

Then Hesta Lyle put the long sheet on the middle table and opened it at the second fold, which she has not done in front of the yard in nine years, and there was nothing behind the second fold, and the yard noticed that.

**"I am going to say where the sum comes out,"** she said, **"and I am going to say it once, and then this yard is going to have to live with it until Wednesday morning whether it likes it or not."**

She put her hand on the sheet.

**"That sheet carries four hundred and thirty-eight on the top of it. Two smaller figures sit under the top one, four hundred and thirty-seventh a step down and four hundred and thirty-ninth a step up, in the hand that has written them since before I stood at this table,"** she said. **"Nine years of the same two figures in the same two places, and this is the morning that sheet comes out with something else written round it, and it is still the same two figures underneath."**

She kept her hand on the paper for a moment and then took it off.

**"The column's money is one pipe and one laying. Four places. Marden's own arithmetic went into this yard on Friday and it is the only arithmetic that has ever come from the place it is about, and it says that the ditch at the top of that lane has to be cut new and that cutting it takes the first half mile of somebody else's ditch dry."**

She looked at the gate and then at the table.

**"The somebody else is the second place. And the second place is nine miles off on the wrong side of a ford that will not take a loaded barrow in the ground we have got left, and it has been the second place for eleven mornings and it is the second place this morning."**

She drew her hand off the paper.

**"So it is not Marden, because Marden first is Marden twice. It is not the third place, because the third place's water comes round the long way whatever we do and a pipe laid there does not put one cup back into that long way inside this season. And the fourth place sent figures and I have not improved on them and I am not going to read them out in front of a man with a case."**

She put two fingers on the wood.

**"That leaves the second place, and the second place cannot be reached, and therefore there is no pipe this month, and that is the whole of what this sum says. It is one answer. I have not got a second one and I am not going to be asked in this yard for a second one, because there is not one in the sum."**

Somebody asked her whether that was the only possible answer.

**"It is the only answer this arithmetic gives,"** she said. **"That is a smaller thing and I would like it said properly. There are four places in this column and a head at the end of it and a road with a ford in it, and the sum has got whatever you have put in it. Nobody in this yard has any right to stand up and tell the county that the county cannot do better than a road and a ford, and I am not going to be the one who says it."**

She squared the sheet.

**"And nobody has put anything on the column's own paper. Four places, a ruled line and two signatures wanted at the foot of each, and not one of the four has a signature on it this morning and not one of them is going to have one before Wednesday."**

The launder came down the middle road at half past eight, and the clerk read it at the wall with the yard behind him and a man with a case standing in front of him.

**"Eight on. One thousand and two hundred and seventy-four hundredweight, and it is the four hundred and fortieth of the run."**

He said it and went on to the halves without any change in his voice and without looking at the number he had said.

**"A Sunday is an even morning, so the upper half is the one that takes it and it is two hundred and nine, and the lower half stood where it stood yesterday at a hundred and ninety-eight."**

He wiped under the line with the side of his hand.

**"Four hundred and seven of a window between the two of them and I have said both halves and I have not said the whole."**

Sera Quill had the slate out and turned it face up on the table and put her thumb under the two figures at the bottom of it.

**"Ninety-seventh and eighty-fourth, thirteen apart,"** she said. **"There is a third line under those two on my slate, and it says the second of them is carried forward, and that nothing printed in this holding ever moves it."**

She kept her thumb where it was.

**"I have said that sentence six times in a yard and this is the seventh and I am going to say it the seventh because a sum has just come out with one place in it, and the only thing I have ever had in this yard that a yard could not argue with is a pair of figures that have never once agreed."**

Kellan Rusk had not been near the rack all morning and came out from behind it now.

**"Three hundred and thirty-four on the register form, two hundred and forty on the charter, and three hundred and twenty-eight on the line in Silling's own book,"** he said, and then, a beat later, in the same breath and a lower voice, **"with nothing written against it, and I have said that seven mornings running and I am not going to say it an eighth without somebody else in the room hearing it."**

He said the last part to Nia Vale and not to the yard.

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the gate board and the two boards in one pass and did not stop at the cistern.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and nothing has ever been let out of it,"** she said. **"Near board eight hundred and twenty-four, far board eight hundred and seventy-one, forty-seven of wall between them, and the drawer behind the near one shut with its key on its nail inside the one room doorway."**

She wiped round the figures rather than across them.

**"Three sheets on that table, and I counted them from the gate without coming in. The oldest has been there a hundred and thirty-seven days, with about nine inches of clear board standing either side of it and nothing written on the board at all. The middle one is shut on its fold. The far one is signed and sealed and named, and I have been trying since Wednesday to find the thing wrong with it."**

She folded the cloth.

**"None of the three entered, none refused, no column cut under one, and no column cut under a case standing by that gate either."**

Ansell Dree nodded at that from the gate and did not open the case.

**"There is one figure on your board that has gone up in four years,"** he said. **"Every other figure in your yard moves on a rule and that one moved without one. I have been in three counties this month and I have not found another."**

He put his hand flat on the case at his feet and left it there.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and nothing has ever been let out of it, in thirty-one or in thirty,"** Nia Vale said. **"It came up by one on the Thursday of the week before last because one more load was paid in, and it has stood at thirty-one every morning since, and there is no rule for it because no person in this holding decided it and nobody was the first to see it except the man who copies the board."**

**"That is the only honest sentence I have said in this yard in two visits,"** Ansell Dree said, and did not open the case.

Kellan Rusk came out to the step at dusk with the rack key in his hand and put the key back on its nail inside the one room doorway before he said anything.

**"Thirty-nine blanks are cut in that board and the rule underneath them stands open with nothing behind it, and it is the thirty-ninth time that rule has come back empty,"** he said. **"On the shelf above them are six ruled rows for what nobody can say, and above the sixth of those there is nothing cut and nothing written. The barrow is at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. The use log stops at fifteen lines. And there are seven sessions in this holding's book and a Sunday enters none."**

He shut the rack on the last of it.

**"Two more books sit on that rack, the requests and the section nine notes, and both of them are at fifty-three. Not one line of either of them took the place that sum named this morning, and I am not writing it in, and nobody else in this yard is going to write it in."**

He shut the rack on the last of it and the key went into his pocket.

**"And last off that rack. The man at the end of the north row is on twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched and unasked, and I said the same thing in this yard four mornings ago and I am saying it again because a man with a case stood at that gate all morning listening and I would like him to have it in somebody's own hand."**

Soren Rill came in at dusk with the mud dried on his boots and stood at the gate beside the man with the case for a moment and then went on to the low board.

**"That ladder has gone up no rung, and there is nothing in this yard set against either of the two people it belongs to, and what is on the low board has never been dated, never unpicked by anybody and never taken back. And the bare ring inside those acres has taken no foot today, no tape and no price."**

He stood with his hand flat on the gate post.

**"A sum came out this morning with one place in it and nobody in this yard found a second one, and I am not going to call that a decision because it is not one. It is a sum with a road in it. On Wednesday it goes up to an office with a column number on the corner of a form and no box filled in, and it will be there or it will not be there."**

He looked at the man with the case.

**"And I have not asked him what is in it and I am not going to. Whatever is in that case is his and it will stay his until he puts it on a table, and this holding has not asked anybody to choose anything for nine days running and it is not going to start on a Sunday."**

One place had been named out loud that morning by a woman with a sheet of her own against her arm, and it had not been named as a threat and it had not been named as a plan, and the cistern was full by hand and the sheet was folded with nothing behind the second fold and a figure of somebody else's was still shut in a case at the gate.

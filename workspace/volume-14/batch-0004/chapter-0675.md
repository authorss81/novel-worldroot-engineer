# Chapter 675

## The Eighteenth Of The Eighteenth, And A Place On That Column Doing Its Own Arithmetic Out Loud

The pair was not read on the Friday and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was standing at the gate with her cans when she said so, which is not a thing she has ever done.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so,"** she said, **"and I will tell you why and then I will get on with what I came to say, because the two are connected and I would rather they were in that order."**

She set the two cans down on the step.

**"I have come down from Marden at four this morning with the fourth place's own figures in my head and my own place's answer to them, and I am not going to stand in a yard at the seventh hour and read somebody else's pair out of a book before I have said a word in my own voice. A man who has come nine miles with a sum does not start the morning by lending it."**

Then she did not say anything else for about an hour, and she went out and came back with her cans and put the water in the trough, and the yard got on with the morning.

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end about the ninth hour and put his hand on the cistern and then took it off again.

**"Nine marks wet on the boards and a cistern a little higher than it was yesterday,"** he said, **"and every one of those marks is a woman in this holding going down to the trough and coming back up with a can, and I would like this yard to notice that I have built nothing this week."**

He went to the tap house and came back.

**"The standing pipe behind that joint is the same pipe it was the day it went in and the joint is doing the two things it has always done, which is weep and hold. Nine Tuesdays. I will not chalk it again and I will not be the man who calls that settled."**

At about the tenth hour the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden put both cans down on the middle table and turned round to face the yard.

**"Then here is Marden's arithmetic and it is mine and it is not a copy of anybody's,"** she said. **"I have done it three nights. I will do it the fourth night if you want me to and I will not do it a fifth."**

She put her hand flat on the table, and not on Hesta Lyle's sheet, which was lying four inches away with its head figure on it.

**"A pipe off the head at the top of the lane costs the place that is first one laying and one ditch, and the ditch at the top of that lane has to be cut new because there is no fall left in it for a mile. I have been up that ditch four times in eleven months and I know what is in it and it is not nothing."**

She looked round the yard and took her time.

**"The cutting is a week of two men, in the dry part of the week, and the laying is a day and a half of three men and a horse. After that comes the part that does not appear in anybody's sum: when a new ditch is cut at the top of that lane the first half mile of the ditch below it goes dry, and the somebody below it is the second place, and that place is nine miles off on the wrong side of a ford."**

She lifted her hand off the table.

**"So the first place pays the pipe and then pays again in three weeks when its own water does not come, and that is the whole of what is in that sum. I have not put a figure on the second part and neither will anybody in this yard, because I do not know what half a mile of dry ditch costs a place until somebody in this yard goes and looks at it and measures it."**

She put her cans back in her hands.

**"That is Marden's arithmetic. That is the only arithmetic that has been said out loud in this holding that came from the place it is about."**

Nobody said anything for a moment, and the clerk put his pencil down flat on the copy sheet, which he does not do.

Hesta Lyle took her hand off her own long sheet and squared it against the edge of the table.

**"I have a sheet in front of me with a figure under each of four headings and I am not going to compare it with what she has just said,"** she said. **"She has a ditch in it and I have a pipe and a laying, and those are two different objects and putting one against the other is how a yard makes a figure up out of nothing."**

She drew her hand back off the paper and squared it against the edge of the table.

**"I will give you the shape of that sheet since you have asked me for it twice this week and had it twice without a word,"** she said. **"The top figure is four hundred and thirty-six. Under it, written small, one step down and one step up, are four hundred and thirty-fifth and four hundred and thirty-seventh, and it has been that hand in those two places for nine years."**

She put her palm flat over the middle of the sheet so that the figures were covered.

**"I have just covered it. I do that twice a year. Nobody in this yard has ever asked me why and I am not going to be asked today, and I will take my hand off it in a moment and it will be four hundred and thirty-six again."**

She lifted her hand off the paper and the figure came back under it.

**"If the cutting of that ditch is a week of two men then the office's day is not the day it is on the sheet at that place, because that place has to get it done before the dry part of the week goes out of the ground and the dry part of the week is not the same dry part in three counties."**

She took her hand off the paper.

**"That is a fact about a road and a calendar and it is not a figure, and I am not going further than that this morning and I am not going to be asked further than that."**

Soren Rill stood at the gate with his coat off and his sleeves rolled and did not come to the table at all while that was being said, and when the yard looked at him afterwards he stayed where he was.

**"Two sentences this yard carries and I am leaving both of them where they are this morning,"** he said. **"The reason is that a woman has just stood in that yard and done arithmetic in her own place's voice, and if the bridge of this holding stands up afterwards and says the permanent sentence then she will hear it as this holding having finished with her, and it will go back up that lane on a cart tonight and be the second place's business by Friday."**

He rolled his sleeves down.

**"I have said the word temporary about that lane once and I am not going to say it again, and I have never once said it, and I am aware that I have thought the word twice."**

The launder came down at half past eight and the clerk was at the wall.

The launder came down the middle road at half past eight and the clerk had it out before he had the slate ready.

**"Eight on,"** he said. **"One thousand and two hundred and seventy-one hundredweight, the four hundred and thirty-eighth of the run. Friday is an even morning, so the upper half is the one that goes and it is two hundred and eight, and the lower half is standing at a hundred and ninety-seven."**

He wiped under the line with the side of his hand.

**"Four hundred and five between the two of them and I have said both halves and neither of those is the whole."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack with the count in force not due for two more mornings and read the count anyway because that is what he does on the days it does not come round.

**"A hundred and forty-six in force, twenty-nine gone, a hundred and seventeen not,"** he said. **"That is where Saturday left it and it will be where Saturday left it on Sunday too. A Friday does not give anybody out of this holding and nobody is going to be blamed here for a field that stood still."**

He took the second book out and put it back.

**"No drawer has been opened and no key has come off its nail at any hour of this morning. And the charter is at two hundred and thirty-eight days and the form at three hundred and thirty-two, and the third of those three ages is Silling's own ruled line at three hundred and twenty-six with nothing written against it. I said that last part out loud at seven this morning so that whoever heard the woman at the gate heard the same thing I said."**

Nia Vale did the gate board and the boards in one pass with her cloth, in the order she does them.

**"Paid at thirty-one, and there is no discharge against that line at thirty-one or at thirty,"** she said. **"Eight hundred and twenty-two is cut into the stone at the near end of that wall and eight hundred and sixty-nine at the tool house end, forty-seven of wall between them, and the drawer behind the near one is shut with the key on its nail inside the one room doorway."**

She wiped round the figures rather than across them.

**"Three sheets on that long table, and the one at the end nearest you is a hundred and thirty-five days old, and there is about nine inches of clear board either side of it. The one in the middle is shut on its fold. The one at the far end is signed and sealed and named, and there is not one thing in it anybody in this yard could call wrong."**

She put the cloth over her shoulder.

**"Not one entered, not one refused, and no column cut under any of them, and none cut under what that woman said at ten o'clock either."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stopped in the middle of the yard about the fourth hour with a sheet of her own against her forearm and asked Kellan Rusk for three figures and then asked him to stop her from writing them down, which he did not do.

**"Three hundred and thirty-two on the register form. Two hundred and thirty-eight on the charter. Three hundred and twenty-six on the line in Silling's own book and I did not ask him what is written on it because I have been told four times by three different people in this yard that it is nothing."**

She looked at her own sheet, which had the two of them on it and not the third.

**"That is two figures and a piece of paper and it is Marden's own arithmetic and it is not this holding's, and the third one is nobody's and I am not carrying it up that lane."**

She put the sheet away inside her coat.

**"The other two books are each at fifty-three, and neither of them took the ditch at the top of my own lane, and I have not put it in either of them and it does not need to be in either of them to be true."**

She put her own sheet away inside her coat and left the cloth where it was.

**"And while I am standing here with a sheet of my own against my arm, I will tell you what I could read off that door frame without stopping for any of it. Thirty-nine blanks cut in the board inside that room, and the rule under them open with nothing behind it, and this is the thirty-ninth time. A shelf with six ruled rows on it for what nobody in there knows, and nothing cut above the sixth. Eleven journeys marked against your barrow with no twelfth beside them. A use log that stops at fifteen lines. Seven sessions in that book of yours, and a Friday enters none."**

She picked her cans up.

**"And do not anybody in this yard go over there and ask the man of about seventy which number he thinks he is on. Twenty-nine fetchings is the figure and he is not being fetched this month and I have not got a reason and I have not gone looking for one."**

She put her cans up on her hip.

**"And there is a door nine hundred yards off this yard, with a figure cut into the seventh column of its frame, and it went past me at the tenth hour this morning while I was walking towards it, and nobody read it and nobody put it in front of me and I did not ask for it."**

At dusk the long sheet was flat on the middle table with Hesta Lyle's hand still square to its edge, two cans were standing on the step of the one room door, and the ditch at the top of a lane nine miles off was on nobody's paper in this holding and was not going to be, and nobody in that yard had said a word about improving it.

# Chapter 673

## The Sixteenth Of The Eighteenth, And A Figure That Went Into Somebody Else's Hands And Was Not Carried Out Of The Yard

The pair was not read on the Wednesday and Marek said so himself, from the cistern door, with the tape still on his belt.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so,"** he said. **"I have a straightedge and a tape and I have to be on that north wall before the light comes off the coping, and a man who stands at a trough at the seventh hour with a tape in his hand is a man who has said the wrong thing about himself to nine people before breakfast."**

He went down the middle road with the chalk in his pocket and the yard let him go.

Then the rotation came round, and it came round into somebody else's hands, which was not how it had come round on any morning in four years.

Kellan Rusk wrote the three figures on a slate in chalk and set the slate on the middle table in front of Odile Vray instead of reading it himself.

**"Read that out,"** he said. **"You are going back to that place and you are going to say what this holding said this morning, and I would rather the second place heard the words than a copy of them, because a copy of words gets read back at you wrong and words do not."**

She picked the slate up in both hands and read it, slowly, the way a person reads a thing they have not seen before.

**"A hundred and forty-eight in force,"** she said. **"Twenty-nine gone out at their own hours. A hundred and nineteen not gone out."**

She turned the slate over, looked at the back of it, and put it down again on the table face up.

**"That is a hundred and nineteen over a hundred and it has been over a hundred since I started counting things in this yard. It goes up by one on a morning of this kind and it goes up by one and never by two, and that is nine mornings now."**

Kellan Rusk did not take the slate back.

**"And nothing left this holding today that was due to leave it,"** she said. **"I did not see a field go out and I did not see one come round, and I am not going to say that I have never seen one because I have only been in this yard two days."**

She looked at him rather than at the slate.

**"Can I take that off this table."**

**"No,"** Kellan Rusk said.

Soren Rill was at the gate with his coat open and had been listening to all of it.

**"No,"** he said, **"and I will tell you why in one line and then I will go and stand somewhere else. That count belongs to this holding and it does not travel. It is not a standing anybody can hold up and it is not a figure that goes on a column, and the second place has just been told the words by a person, and that is the whole of what has happened in this room."**

Odile Vray left the slate where it was and wiped her hands down her coat.

**"I was not going to carry it,"** she said. **"I asked so that you would have to say it out loud in front of a person instead of a piece of paper. There is a difference between a number that somebody reads to you in a room and a number that turns up in a ledger in another county with your place's name against it, and everybody up that road knows the difference and does not say so."**

She put her hands in her sleeves.

**"And I am not saying either of the two sentences this yard has on it,"** she said. **"The reason is that I came here to be lent a figure and I have just been lent three of them, and a woman who is being lent figures all morning does not stand up and make a permanent speech in the same room. Put those two together and the lending starts to look like a favour, and it is not one, and I will not have it read as one."**

She turned to the table again.

**"The three of you read them to me and I heard them. Put them back on the shelf."**

Then the reckoning, which came in on the slate's other side and which Sera Quill read off the edge of it in her own hand while it lay on the table between them.

**"Ninety-sixth and eighty-third,"** she said. **"Thirteen apart."**

She put her finger on the third line under the two figures and did not pick the slate up, because it was not hers this morning.

**"There is a third line under those two and it says that the second of them is carried forward and that nothing printed in this holding ever moves it. I have said that line out loud in this yard six times. I have never once said it with the slate lying on somebody else's hands before and I would like it recorded that the sentence came out exactly the same."**

Kellan Rusk came out from the rack with the slate's owner not looking at him and took the three off the shelf in a different order from the one they stand in.

**"The order a man who is going to build something needs those in is the charter first, because a cistern answers under it,"** he said. **"Two hundred and thirty-six days. Then the form, because a form is what a holding is counted on, and that is three hundred and thirty. And then the one in Silling's own book, and that one last, because it is nobody's: three hundred and twenty-four, with nothing written against it."**

He put all three back the way he takes them out.

**"That is a different order from the shelf and I have given a different order nine days running this week and I would like it noticed that the shelf does not care."**

At half past eight the water came down the middle road as it does every morning, and the clerk met it at the wall with the slate, because his copy sheet was wet and he had put it inside his coat.

**"One thousand and two hundred and sixty-eight hundredweight, and it is the four hundred and thirty-sixth of the run, eight on,"** he said, writing on the slate. **"Wednesday is an even morning, so the upper half is the one that moves and it is two hundred and seven, and the lower one is standing at a hundred and ninety-six."**

He turned the slate over so the yard could read it upside down if they wanted to, and put the chalk in his pocket.

**"Four hundred and three of a window between them. I have said both halves and I have not said the whole and neither has anybody else in this yard today."**

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet on the table and had it square and had both her hands flat on it, which she does when she intends to move it.

**"That sheet carries four hundred and thirty-four on the top, with four hundred and thirty-third below it and four hundred and thirty-fifth above it, one step down and one step up, in the small hand,"** she said. **"And I have carried that sheet round this yard for nine years and I am telling the yard now that it is going back into my coat between the hours, and nobody is to make anything of it, because a sheet that is put away on a Wednesday comes out on the Thursday."**

She kept it under her forearm.

**"Four places on that column and no fifth, and the first place has now said nothing for eleven mornings, and the third place has said nothing for eleven mornings, and I have stopped expecting anything from either of them and I check anyway."**

Tova Reed was at the bench on the floor in the north-side room and the two boards she had put under the back trays were damp at one corner where nine cans had gone past them twice a day.

**"The seed is dry,"** she said. **"The boards are wet and I will turn them over at the end of the week and the seed will be dry, and that is the whole of the argument and I am not going to move eleven trays back onto a shelf over it."**

She put the last tray in and looked at the cistern and the nine wet marks and did not say anything about either.

**"And I am not saying either of the two sentences this place has on it this morning, and the reason is not the row of bench and it is not the cistern. It is that a man came up the lane yesterday with a notice in his coat for somebody in this room and I do not know yet whether it was for me."**

She set the tray down square.

**"I have said hard things in this yard before and I have never once said a hard thing and then wondered all week whether I had said it in front of the wrong person. That is a thing I have got to learn this month and I would rather learn it without an audience."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the gate board and the figures on it in the order she always does them.

**"That gate line is paid at thirty-one and there is no discharge against it at thirty-one or at thirty,"** she said. **"There is a number cut into the stone at this end of that wall and another at the tool house end, and they are eight hundred and twenty and eight hundred and sixty-seven, and there is forty-seven of wall between them."**

She wiped round the first of the two rather than across it.

**"The drawer behind that first number is shut and its key is on a single nail inside the doorway of the one room."**

She went round each of the numbers with the cloth and left every one of them standing.

**"Three sheets on that long table. Nobody in this holding has ever put a fourth one down there and nobody in this holding has ever taken one of the three off it. The oldest of them is a hundred and thirty-three days old, and the middle one is shut on its fold, and the third is signed and sealed and named."**

She wiped the last of the three and folded the cloth over her arm.

**"About nine inches of bare board sits either side of the oldest of them, and I have wiped round all three. Nothing entered, nothing refused, no column underneath any of them, and none underneath a slate either, whatever anybody here happened to be feeling at eleven o'clock this morning."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end about the eleventh hour with the hod on his shoulder and stopped at the cistern door.

**"Nine marks wet on the boards again,"** he said, **"and a cistern that is half up and a hose that goes nowhere, and I have built four things in this yard and not one of them stood up while somebody else was still carrying water to it."**

He put the hod down outside the one room door and listened to the tap house without going in.

**"Seven Tuesdays now on that joint at the back of the tap house. It weeps where it has always wept and it holds where it has always held, and the pipe behind it is the same pipe. I am not going to chalk it a third time and I am not going to be the man in this yard who calls a weeping joint a sound one."**

He looked at the slate lying face up on the middle table.

**"And whatever that is, put it back on the shelf where the rest of it lives. A chalk number on a slate left face up in a yard is a thing that gets carried, and everything on that table is a thing that stays on that table."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step at dusk and put his own copy sheet down on it, which is the first time he has put that sheet on anything that was not a table.

**"There are two more books on that rack and both of them are at fifty-three. The one is the requests and the one is the section nine notes, and the two of them have never once been the same list. Neither took a slate today."**

He put his thumb on the space above the last line of his own sheet.

**"There is a shelf in there with six ruled rows cut on it against what this holding does not know, and the row above the sixth has never been cut and was not cut today for a cistern, a pipe, a notice or a man with a form. The barrow is at eleven journeys with no twelfth beside it. The use log stops at fifteen lines deep and above the fifteenth there is a clean space where a sixteenth would go."**

He turned the slate back over so the yard could read it again.

**"And there are seven sessions entered in this holding's book, and a Wednesday enters none of them."**

He put the copy sheet down flat on the step and kept his thumb on the corner.

**"There is a board in the one room with thirty-nine blanks cut into it, and the rule underneath them stands open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and a Wednesday is not a morning a return comes up the fen road."**

He picked the sheet up off the step.

**"The last item on that sheet of mine is a man of about seventy, and the figure against him is twenty-nine fetchings. He was not fetched today and he was not put to about it, and that is the only line on my copy sheet I have never had to check twice."**

He went in.

Soren Rill came back in through the gate at the eighth hour and stood at the low board for a moment without touching it.

**"There is a ladder against that wall and its lowest rung is still sitting in the spring mud, and no figure in this yard is set against either of the two people it belongs to. Nothing on the low board has ever carried a date or a name, and nobody has ever reached out and picked it up or taken it away."**

He turned and looked across the middle of the eleven acres.

**"And that ring of bare ground has taken no foot of anybody's today, no tape, and no price, and I have walked the inside edge of those acres twice this week without once looking in."**

He looked at the middle table.

**"And three figures were read out in this room this morning by a person who came from another place and then three of them stayed on this table, and I would like that written down as the finding of the day, because it is the first morning in this holding's memory that somebody from a column has been handed this holding's own arithmetic and has left it where she found it."**

Wednesday went by in that yard with a hundred and forty-eight in force and a hundred and nineteen not gone out and a slate put back on a shelf, the cistern a little higher than it was and no pipe under it at all, and nobody in it was asked anything and nobody climbed anything.

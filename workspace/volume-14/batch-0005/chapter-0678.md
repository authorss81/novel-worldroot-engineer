# Chapter 678

## The Twenty-First Of The Eighteenth, And A Name That Was Standing In Somebody Else's Mouth Before Anyone Here Had Said It

At the seventh hour on the Monday Renn Ashby put his thumb into the book at the middle table, and read out what was written there, in front of about eleven people who have heard him do it four hundred times.

**"The heading over this has not moved since before I came, so it is a heading of a month nine months back and it stays that way, and here are the two figures under it: one hundred and ninety-one of three hundred and sixty-five. Of this month."**

He got off his heel in front of the yard and nobody in it has ever remarked on that.

He got off his heel in front of nine people and nobody in that yard has ever remarked on it.

The clerk had the copy sheet out on the middle table before the yard was properly up, and he had it face up, which he does not do, and about eleven people were round that table by the time he began.

**"I am going to read this out because it goes up on the ordinary day with the rest of the copies and this is the last morning it is going to be a private piece of paper in my coat,"** he said. **"It is not this holding's hand. It is a woman's from the second place, and it went into mine on Friday and it has been in mine since Friday, and I have not altered a word of it."**

He put his thumb under the first line of it and read it.

**"The second place says no to being first. Not this month. Not until the ford at the low crossing will take a loaded barrow in the ground we have left, and it will not be asked again before then, and if it is asked again the answer will be the same one and not a worse one."**

He lifted his thumb off the paper.

**"That is in her mouth and not mine, and it has been in her mouth since she said it to me on Friday at the gate, and I have carried it since then without saying it in a yard."**

Somebody at the back asked her whether she had been told.

**"I have been told by the man who copied it and not by anybody else,"** he said, **"and that is the correct order and I would like it kept."**

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet squared on the table in front of her and had both her forearms on it, and she had been in that position since before the reading.

**"You have all now heard the second place's word in the second place's own mouth,"** she said, **"and I am going to say the thing I said on Sunday and I am going to say it in the order I said it in, because the order is the whole of what I have."**

She did not turn the sheet round.

**"Four hundred and thirty-nine at the head of it. Under the head, in the small hand that has been making that shape for nine years, the four hundred and thirty-eighth a step down and the four hundred and fortieth a step up. One under and one over. I did not teach that hand and I cannot improve it and I have not opened my own book."**

She put two fingers on the wood beside the paper and not on the paper.

**"And the sum that came out with one place in it on Sunday came out of that sheet and out of Marden's own arithmetic and out of a ford, and the place in it is the second place, and I did not choose it on Sunday and I am not choosing it this morning."**

She drew her forearms back off the paper.

**"A name in a yard is not a decision. It is a piece of arithmetic that everybody in the room now has to work with, and the four of us at this table are going to have to work with it whether we like it or not."**

Then she said the other thing, and she said it while the clerk was still standing there with the copy sheet in his hand, which is the only reason it landed.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

Nobody answered her. The clerk put the copy sheet face down on the table, which he has never done with it, and the yard went on with the morning, and nobody said it was a hard thing to say and nobody thanked her for saying it.

The launder came down the middle road at half past eight and the clerk met it at the wall with his copy sheet back in his coat and his thumb under the line.

**"Five off, and this is the four hundred and forty-first of the run. A Monday is an odd morning, so the half that goes first is the one on the low side of that line, not the one on the top, and I have said that out loud in this yard at this wall for four years and I will keep saying it."**

He wiped the chalk and read the weight afterwards.

**"One thousand and two hundred and sixty-nine hundredweight."**

He wiped under the chalk with the side of his hand.

**"I have said that number four hundred and forty times this year and I want it noticed that this morning it is the four hundred and forty-first and not the four hundred and fortieth, because a man who has been reading a run off the end of a wall for four years can be a whole one out and never know it."**

Marek was at the cistern door with a tape on his belt and a length of chalk in his hand, and he read the two halves off the wall on his way past because he had to go past it.

**"The half that took the overnight is the lower one at a hundred and ninety-nine,"** he said. **"The upper half stood where it stood yesterday at two hundred and nine. Four hundred and eight of a window between the two of them, and I am not going to add them in front of nine people on a Monday."**

He wiped the chalk on his thigh and went in.

The count in force was not due until the morning after tomorrow, and Kellan Rusk gave it out over the spine of the closed book with one hand on the cover, because a bookkeeper who has said a thing four hundred times can say it without opening the book.

**"A hundred and forty-nine in force, and the rotation does not come round on a Monday and will not come round on the Wednesday either, so that figure has not moved since Friday."**

He put the book back and took it out again.

**"Twenty-nine of them have gone out at whatever hour their own hour is and a hundred and twenty have not gone out, and there is nobody in this yard who is going to be blamed for ground that never came up."**

He put the book back on the shelf, took it out again, and put it back a second time.

**"And nobody left this holding today, because nobody was down for it."**

Nia Vale came along the wall from the far end, which is the reverse of the way she does it, and started at the tool house end and worked back toward the gate.

**"Eight hundred and seventy-two is cut into that stone at this end,"** she said, **"and eight hundred and twenty-five is cut into the other one, and there is forty-seven of wall between them, and I have started at the far end this morning because a woman stood at that gate on Friday and told me the far end of her column was somebody else's problem and I have not stopped thinking about it."**

She wiped round the figure rather than across it.

**"The drawer behind the near one is shut. The key is on its nail in the doorway of the one room and it was on that nail at six and it will be on it at the ninth hour tonight."**

She stopped at the gate post, where the compost line is in its own chalk, and Odile Vray was standing at it with her bag at her feet getting ready to go nine miles.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** Odile Vray said. **"And nothing has ever been let out of it, in thirty-one or in thirty. It came up by one a fortnight ago because one more load went in, and I have now heard four people in this yard say that sentence and I am going to say it back to the second place, because the second place will be told it fell."**

She picked her bag up.

**"It did not fall. It went up, once, and there is nobody in this holding who decided it and the only man who saw it happen is the one who copies the board."**

She went out through the gate at about the sixth hour and did not stop at the door nine hundred yards off, and nobody walked out with her and nobody put anything in front of her on the way.

There were four people in the doorway of the one room at about the ninth hour and all of them heard Kellan Rusk give the ages off the rack, and he gave them in the order a man who means to build something wants them in and not in the order they stand on the shelf.

**"Kellan Rusk gave out three figures from that shelf and gave them in the wrong order on purpose. The one that is nobody's came first: three hundred and twenty-nine, on a ruled line in Silling's own book, nothing written against it. Then the two that are ours: the form at three hundred and thirty-five days and the charter at two hundred and forty-one."**

He put all three back the way he takes them out.

**"I have been giving that first one out by itself since a Tuesday and I am not going to stop."**

He tapped the shelf beside the book rather than the line in it.

**"It belongs to a ruled line in Silling's own book with nothing written on it, and I have been giving that one out by itself since a Tuesday and I am not going to stop."**

He put the three back on the shelf in the order he takes them out of it.

**"And the first of the three papers on that long table is a hundred and thirty-eight days old this morning and the middle one is shut on its fold and the third one is signed and sealed and named, and not one of the three has been entered, none has been refused, and no column has ever been cut under any of them."**

Sera Quill had the slate out and did not put it on the table.

**"I am not saying either of the two sentences this place has on it,"** she said, **"and the reason is not mine to keep back. A woman in this yard said one of them whole at the seventh hour, before this yard had finished being a yard, and if I stand here at the ninth hour and say the other one then this morning has two permanent sentences standing in it and I know which of them a person will be able to remember at the gate tonight."**

She put the slate back in her coat and shut the flap.

**"The frame on that book has not moved and it is not going to move, and the two figures on it are the two figures."**

Harlan Vetch was at the one room door by ten with the hod still up on his shoulder, and he stood with his ear turned towards the tap house behind the wall rather than going in to it.

**"Eleven marks wet on those boards and a cistern that is full for the fourth day running because nine trips a day is what it takes, and I have built nothing this week and I would like that said in a mason's hand."**

He set the hod down and listened again.

**"Two things at the back of that tap house and only two, and they are the same two as last year. Water comes out of the joint and the joint stays shut. The pipe standing behind it is the pipe that was there before I ever put my hand on it."**

He shifted the hod off his shoulder and set it down by the trough.

**"Nine Tuesdays is not a week and a half of Tuesdays and it is not a settling. Put it down that a mason has said it nine times and has not once added anything to it."**

Going past the north-side room he put his eye along that chalk line on the floor rather than down at his own boots, and he went round the end of it instead of over it.

**"Four inches off the floor and it forks twice, and it has forked twice since before I came and there is no third fork coming, and I would have chalked it the same way."**

Marek came out of the north-side room at about the eleventh hour and went to the gate and stood with his hands on the gate post looking up the middle road.

**"The line goes in tomorrow at the top of the north wall and comes out at that stopcock, and it is four inches and it is two days of a man and a half, and it comes off the head at the top of the middle road and not off anything else."**

He did not say whose head.

**"That is all I am going to say about it in a yard, and I would like it noticed that I said it without being asked, because I have been in this holding eleven mornings saying nothing about where the water comes from."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in at the fourth hour with her two cans, put them down on the step of the one room door, and stood for a moment with her hand on the rim.

**"Nine trips today again, and the ninth one is mine and I have got nothing to put against it,"** she said. **"A name got read out at that table this morning that is nine miles off and on the wrong side of a ford, and I have not been asked how I feel about it and I am not going to tell anybody, and I am going to carry water instead."**

She picked the cans up and went out through the gate.

Soren Rill came in about the seventh hour with the mud to the ankle and went the whole length of the yard without sitting down and stopped at the low board.

**"Nothing has been climbed. What is on that board has never carried a date and nobody's hand has been near it and nobody is going to be told what it means this week either. The ladder has its bottom rung in the same spring mud it was in."**

He looked across the middle of the eleven acres and did not go towards it.

**"The bare ring inside those acres took no boot today and no tape and no price. And a yard that has read a name out loud this morning has not agreed to anything, and I would like that written down in somebody's own hand before the twenty-ninth."**

He put his hands back in his coat.

**"Four places on that column and no fifth, and this holding has not asked anybody to put a name on anything, and I am not going to be the one who starts on a Monday morning."**

Somebody stood in that yard for most of the morning on Monday with a name in its mouth that it had not chosen, and the yard got on with the work of the day around it, and the cistern was full at dusk, and the copy sheet was face down on the middle table with the second place's own words on it and not one of them altered.

# Chapter 684

## The Twenty-Seventh Of The Eighteenth, And A Sheet Coming Up The Fen Road In The Office's Own Hand That Nobody In This Yard Was Able To Call Wrong

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour on the Sunday and about eleven people were standing round the middle table because a Sunday in this yard has always been the morning with nowhere to be.

**"Of this month, and here they are: one hundred and ninety-four of three hundred and seventy-one."**

He got off his heel and stood with the book shut under his hand.

**"A piece of paper came up that road at eleven today with good news on it, and I read those two figures before I knew what the paper said. That is the correct order and I am not going to be told it is the wrong one."**

He shut the book on his thumb and stood with his hand on it.

**"Somebody put a paper on that table this morning that says a good thing, and I am glad of it, and I am also going to be standing here at seven next Sunday reading these two figures in exactly the same voice, and I would like both of those to be understood at once."**

At about the half hour the water came down the middle road again, and the man from the fourth place was waiting at the wall with a piece of chalk in his hand that he had never been given.

**"One thousand and two hundred and seventy-eight hundredweight,"** he said, **"five off, and it is the four hundred and forty-seventh of the run."**

He ran his thumb along under the chalk and found that it had taken this time.

**"The lower half is at two hundred and two and it got there overnight, because it is an odd morning and the lower one is the one that goes on one. The upper half has not moved since Friday and is on two hundred and twelve."**

He put the chalk in his pocket and looked up the middle road.

**"Four hundred and fourteen of a window between them, which is the figure it came to on the Sunday three weeks ago with the two halves moving in the other order, and I am not going to make anything of that either."**

The rider came up the fen road at about the eleventh hour with a single sheet folded twice and did not come to the gate, and put it on the middle table and went back down the road, and that is the whole of what a rider from another county is.

Hesta Lyle picked it up and did not open it for about a minute, and then she opened it at the second fold and turned it round so that the yard could see it, and she did not say anything for some time after that.

**"This is printed,"** she said. **"Not written. Printed, and in the office's own hand at the foot of it, and it carries the seal that the third of the three sheets on this table carries, and I have looked at both seals and they are the same seal and the same two marks on it."**

She put her finger on the heading.

**"It is the return of the first phase. And it says that the phase is finished in the core, and that the core is the whole of the ground the phase names, and that the harvest off that ground has come in, and that the figure it came in at is the figure the schedule said it would come in at."**

She took her finger off the paper.

**"Every district named on it is inside the line the schedule drew. Not one of them is outside that line. And the second district on that sheet is nine miles north of this yard and has a name I have never heard spoken by anybody in this holding."**

Somebody at the back said the word liar and Harlan Vetch turned round before the word was finished.

**"Do not,"** he said. **"I have spent nine mornings telling this yard what a round number is worth and I am telling you now what that word is worth in this room, and it is nothing at all, because I have read that seal and there is not one thing on that sheet that I could put a finger on."**

Nobody said it again.

Kellan Rusk came round the table and read it standing up, from the middle of the yard, in the voice he uses for things that are not his.

**"It is a correct document,"** he said. **"I have checked it against two other papers from the same office that have been in this yard for four years and the paper agrees with itself and it agrees with them. And I have been looking for a fault in this office's paper since the first of them came, and I have not found one, and I am going to say that this morning without anybody having to ask me."**

He put his hand flat on the sheet and then took it off again so that the yard could see that he had not covered any of it.

**"And I am not accepting it and I am not refusing it. It is a sheet with a harvest on it from a place four days away and it has nothing in it that says one word about this holding, and this yard is not going to be asked to have an opinion about it before it has had a week."**

Nobody in that yard disagreed with him about the paper. Nobody said the office was right either, and nobody said it was wrong, and nobody said anything at all about the woman whose name was not on it and whose hand was on the seal.

Sera Quill stood at the wall with the slate under her arm and did not put it down all morning.

**"Four hundred and forty-five at the head of that long sheet,"** she said, **"with the four hundred and forty-fourth a step under it and the four hundred and forty-sixth a step over it, and I have copied that hand twice off the back of that sheet in four years and I have had no use for it until this morning."**

She turned the slate over and did not show the back of it to anybody.

**"There is a ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and thirty-five and it has nothing written on it, and before it there are two ages that other people keep: the form at three hundred and forty-one days, the charter at two hundred and forty-seven."**

She put the slate in her coat without turning it round for anybody.

**"And this morning a correct paper came up the fen road with a harvest on it. Both of those things are standing in this room and I would like the yard to notice that neither one of them has moved the other."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and was stopped twice by people who wanted to ask her about the sheet, and she answered both of them and then came and stood at the long table with her hand flat on the back of her own coat.

**"I am not saying either of the two sentences this holding has on it,"** she said, **"and I am going to say the reason before I am asked for it. There is a piece of paper folded in this coat that nobody in this holding has ever seen, and the first good news that has come off that lane in eleven days is not going onto a page of mine with something I said standing next to it."**

She took her hand off the coat.

**"I am the woman who writes down what people decide in this yard. I have not written anything down since Wednesday and I am not going to write anything down on a Sunday morning because a piece of good news has come up the road and I have been in a yard."**

She picked the cloth up and went to the wall.

**"Nobody has ever let a load out of that line, not at thirty and not at thirty-one. It is paid at thirty-one and it has been that since a Thursday a fortnight ago when one more load came in rather than one going out."**

She folded the cloth once over her forearm.

**"There are two figures cut into that wall and the one at the tool house end is the elder of the pair: eight hundred and seventy-eight, at about the height of a man's shoulder. Forty-seven of masonry back toward the gate brings you to eight hundred and thirty-one."**

She wiped the two of them in turn and left both of them standing.

**"The drawer behind this one is shut and has been shut all day, and its key is on a single nail in the one room doorway where it will be tonight."**

She came back to the long table and stood over the three sheets that have been lying on it for a hundred and forty-four days.

**"Three papers have lain on that table since before I could reach the top of it. The eldest was a hundred days old the week before I came here and it is a hundred and forty-four days old today, and there are nine inches of bare board at each end of it and there have been for four years."**

She turned the cloth over in her hands.

**"The second of them is shut on its fold. The third is signed and sealed and named. And a rider came up the fen road this morning with a fourth sheet in his hand carrying the same two marks on the wax that the third one carries, and I am not going to say what that makes and nobody else in this yard is going to say it either."**

She wiped round all three and folded the cloth over her arm.

**"Not one entered, not one refused, no column cut under any of them, and the paper that came up the road this morning has not been entered either and there is no column in this holding to enter it under."**

Somebody's two cans stood on the step of the one room door from the fourth hour, and it was about half an hour before their owner came back for them, and when she did she did not say a word about where she had been.

**"Nine trips again,"** she said, **"and I heard the rider come up the road and I did not go out to him, because a rider has his own errand and I have a cistern."**

She looked up the middle road towards where the reach was running east.

**"And there is a harvest four days north of this yard that has come in on the day it was said it would, and there is a mile of lane a mile up that with nine households on it and an empty trough, and both of those are true at the same time and I have been standing in this yard trying to hold both of them since eleven o'clock."**

She picked the cans up again and went out through the gate.

Odile Vray came up the lane from the second place at about the third hour in the afternoon, alone, and she did not go to the table and she did not go to the cistern.

**"I have been up and down that lane twice since eleven o'clock and I am going to go again at dusk,"** she said, **"and I am not going to tell this yard how it is going because a woman who tells a yard how it is going is asking the yard for something and I am not asking it for anything."**

She put her bag down by the cistern door and sat on the step beside it.

**"And I am not going to sit here and be a woman whose place is the one on the good sheet, because I am not on the good sheet, and I have read it and I have put my thumb on my own name and it is not there."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end and stood at the north-side room door with the hod on his shoulder and read the sheet from about eleven feet off, the way he reads everything.

**"That is printed,"** he said. **"I have never printed anything in my life and I have built a good deal, and I am not standing in this yard saying a word against a man who printed that."**

He set the hod down.

**"There is one joint behind that tap house and it has done the same one thing every morning since the day it went in. There is a pipe standing behind it that was standing there before I ever came to this holding, and I have said how many Tuesdays that is and I am not going to say it again this morning.**"

He looked at the wet marks on the floor of the north-side room instead of at the wall.

**"And the pipe in the wall of that room is holding as well, and it is not filling that cistern, and there will be eleven marks wet on those boards this evening the same as there were this morning.**"

He looked up the middle road for a moment.

**"A good thing has come up that road and a bad thing went out of it on Wednesday, and I have been in this holding four years and I have never known a yard to have to hold both at once, and I would like it noticed that nobody in this yard has picked the good one up and thrown the bad one away."**

Soren Rill came the inside edge of the eleven acres at dusk and stood at the low board without touching it.

**"Nobody in this holding has been on the top of anything in four years. There is nothing on that low board that has ever been dated, and nobody has picked it up and nobody has carried it back to whoever left it, and there is a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres that has taken no boot of mine today and no tape and no price."**

He put his hands in his coat and left them there.

**"And a correct paper came up the fen road today and it is the fourth one out of that office that this yard has not been able to find a fault in, and I have stopped saying what that is worth and I am going to start saying what it is worth, which is that it is worth a yard its whole attention on the morning it arrives."**

He looked at the middle road.

**"Nobody has been asked to choose anything. Nobody has died of that harvest. And nobody in this holding has been saved by it either, and I would like that said in the same breath and by the same person."**

What had come up the fen road that morning was a printed sheet with a harvest on it in the office's own hand, and it was correct in every particular, and nine people read it standing up, and not one of them found a fault in it, and not one of them said a word against it, and one of them said out loud that he would not be picking it up and throwing the other thing away.

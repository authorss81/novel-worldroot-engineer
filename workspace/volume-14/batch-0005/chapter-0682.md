# Chapter 682

## The Twenty-Fifth Of The Eighteenth, And A Holding Working On A Morning That Had Already Gone Wrong And Was Not Going To Be Told Why

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour and read it slowly, because it was Friday and because he had gone back to reading it the way he used to after a Wednesday like the one behind.

**"Here is the pair for the Friday, and it is of this month: one hundred and ninety-three of three hundred and sixty-nine. Read in that order and no other."**

He shut the book on his thumb and left it there.

**"I said that last week in a different order and I have been asked twice why I change it. I change it because the first figure is the one a man wants and the second is the one he has to have, and I am not going to explain that again on a Friday."**

He got off his heel and nobody in that yard remarked on it, and nobody mentioned Wednesday, and the launder came down at half past eight and went east under the road and out of that yard's sight for the first time since the trough was put in.

**"One thousand and two hundred and seventy-five hundredweight,"** the clerk said, **"five off, and it is the four hundred and forty-fifth of the run, and I have said that for five days this week with the water going the other way and I am not going to start reading it differently because the direction has changed."**

He wiped under the line and moved his thumb along it past the figure, which he does not usually do.

**"A point came off the lower half in the night and it is at two hundred and one. Nothing has come off the other one, which is on two hundred and eleven and has been on that figure since Wednesday."**

He wiped under the line with the side of his hand.

**"Four hundred and twelve of a window between the two of them. I have said both halves and I have not said the whole, and I am not going to stand in a yard that has gone wrong in one place and decide that a second thing has gone wrong too."**

He stood with his hand on the wall after he had said it, and there was nothing else he wanted to do with the morning, and he went and sat down at the middle table.

Odile Vray went out of the gate at the sixth hour with four cans and a barrow and did not say anything about where she was going, and came back at about the eighth hour with the cans empty and set them against the wall and went out again, and the yard counted her going and coming and nobody asked her.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did her nine trips and said one sentence at the fourth of them and then said nothing else for the rest of the day.

**"The cistern at the north side is still being filled by hand on the fifth day, and there is a pipe in the wall of that room with water standing in it, and I have asked two people this morning whether that pipe is going to fill the cistern and both of them gave me the same face and no answer."**

She set the can down on the boards.

**"I am not going to ask a third time this month. Nine trips is nine trips and I have done it since the Sunday before last and I am going to go on doing it until it stops being the only thing there is to do in that room."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack in the middle of the morning with the count in force not due until the morning after tomorrow.

**"The rotation does not come round on a Friday and the figure is where Wednesday's rotation left it, and it will be the same figure on Saturday and on the morning after that, and I am going to stop saying the two smaller ones out loud until the day it moves and I would like the yard to notice me doing that."**

He put the book back on the shelf and stood with his hand on the spine of it.

**"Three of the books on that rack have gone past three hundred and they go round in an order I have used for a month. The register form is at three hundred and thirty-nine days. The charter is at two hundred and forty-five. And the third of the three is a ruled line in Silling's own book at three hundred and thirty-three with nothing written against it."**

He put the shelf back the way he takes it out.

**"And eleven journeys on that barrow with no twelfth beside them, fifteen lines in the use log with a clean space above the fifteenth, and a Friday enters none of the seven sessions in this holding's book."**

He looked up at the gate and then down at the book.

**"That board in the one room has thirty-nine blanks cut in it and the rule underneath has come back open with nothing behind it at the thirty-ninth time, and a return is not due in this holding for six days and will not put a fortieth blank in there when it is."**

He stopped with his thumb above the space.

**"And a cart standing at a wall is not a reason to put a name in a blank, which is the reason I have got for thirty-nine."**

Tova Reed had the last tray of the day on the boards at the back of the north-side room and did not move it back onto the shelf, and she had been in that room since the sixth hour with the door open.

**"Six trays on the floor and six on the bench and the row along the cistern wall is bare and it is going to stay bare this week,"** she said. **"The seed is dry and the boards under the back ones are wet at one corner and I will turn them at the end of the week and the seed will be dry, and I am not moving eleven trays back onto a shelf over a wet corner."**

She wiped her hands on her skirt although they were dry.

**"And I am not saying either of the two sentences this place has on it,"** she said, **"and I am not going to give the yard a reason dressed up as modesty. There are two people a mile up a lane with an empty trough and I am not one of them and I cannot do anything for them from this room, and a woman who cannot do anything does not stand up and say a permanent sentence."**

She went back inside and shut the door behind her to the width of a hand.

Nia Vale came along the wall at about the tenth hour and did the gate line first and the two boards second, and she was slower than usual and she did not speak for about twenty minutes.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** she said, **"and nothing has ever been let out of that line, in thirty-one or in thirty. It has not moved since the Thursday of the week before last and it is not going to move on this Friday, and I have said that sentence six mornings running now."**

She went the other way up the wall with the cloth over her shoulder.

**"Near the gate the stone says eight hundred and twenty-nine. Away at the tool house it says eight hundred and seventy-six. There is forty-seven of wall between those two ends and there has never been any other difference between them whatever, and a drawer is let into the stone behind the near one and it is shut."**

She stood at the end of the wall with the cloth doubled across her forearm and did not use it on anything.

**"One key, one nail, inside the one room doorway, and it was on that nail at six this morning."**

She stopped at the long table and stood with the cloth in both hands and did not touch any of the three sheets.

**"The first of these is a hundred and forty-two days old. The middle one is shut on its fold. The third has a name and a seal on it and there is not one thing in it anybody in this yard could call wrong."**

She wiped round the three of them and folded the cloth across her forearm and left them where they lay.

**"Not one entered, not one refused, no column cut under any of them, and two other books on that rack at fifty-three apiece which have never once been the same list and neither of them took Wednesday."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at about the eleventh hour and put the hod down outside the one room door and went into the north-side room and stood inside it for a while looking at the pipe in the wall.

**"The pipe is in and it is charged and it is holding,"** he said, when he came out. **"I have looked at four joints on it and I have not found one of them that I would call a soft one. That is the whole of what I have got to say about the work and I am going to say it and stop."**

He put his thumb on the door frame.

**"Behind the tap house there is a joint that does one thing and one thing only, and the one thing it does is come away wet. Nine Tuesdays now, and every one of those Tuesdays it came away wet and stayed where it is."**

He looked at the pipe shoulder on the wall rather than at the boards.

**"The pipe standing behind it is the pipe that was there before I ever touched it, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about the north end today."**

He looked along the boards at the wet marks.

**"And there are five things in this holding that I have built and the fifth one is in that wall and it is doing the one thing it was built to do, which is carry water, and it is not carrying any to that cistern, and I am a mason and I am not going to stand here and tell this yard why."**

Soren Rill came the inside edge of the eleven acres at about the seventh hour and did not go near the low board, and he came and stood at the middle table instead.

**"I have had a day and a night to find a third thing and there is not one and I am going to say that plainly and once, and then I would like nobody in this yard to ask me again today."**

He put his hands flat on the wood.

**"I have said the word about it four times this morning to myself before I got through the gate and it is the same four times. And there is no version of Wednesday that ends with that place having water in it. That is not hardness. That is what a sum is when it has been run twice."**

He lifted one hand off the table and looked at the middle road.

**"The ladder has gone up no rung and nothing on the low board has been dated or picked up or withdrawn and the bare ring inside those acres took no boot today and no tape and no price. And nobody in this holding has been asked to choose anything since I came through the gate, including me, and I have been the one offering things to be chosen for nine days."**

The man of about thirty-four of the fourth place had not got up off the step all morning and he said what he said at about the second hour, to nobody in particular, and got up afterwards and drank from the trough.

**"I said four things in this yard on Tuesday that I have not said anywhere in three years, and one of them was that nobody has ever asked us,"** he said. **"And a man who has been given a hearing for the first time since he was twenty does not get to stand up the next morning and say the permanent thing, because the hearing and the permanent thing would leave this yard in the same cart and only one of them would ever be referred to again."**

He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand.

**"I am going to be here on Saturday and on the morning after that, and I am not going to be the man who says it at any point in between."**

Hesta Lyle was at the long table for about an hour in the middle of the afternoon with the sheet face up in front of her and nobody standing near it, and she did not read the sum out and she did not cover the sheet with her hand.

**"The head of that sheet is four hundred and forty-three, and underneath it in the small hand, one step down is the four hundred and forty-second and one step up is the four hundred and forty-fourth. That hand has been making that shape at that table for nine years and it went on making it yesterday.**"

She put her palm flat over the three of them and left it there for a moment and then took it off.

**"And I am not going to read what is under the sum today. There is a sum under it and it came out with one place in it on Sunday and this yard has not enjoyed that fact, and I am not going to read it out again on the Friday after a Thursday like the one behind us in order to see whether anybody in here has changed their mind about it."**

At about the fifth hour she squared the sheet against the edge of the table and left it there and went and sat down on the gate step, which nobody in that yard had seen her do.

After a week of mornings that went wrong one at a time, Friday went wrong all day at once, and the cistern at the north side was full at dusk out of nine cans a day, and a man went up the lane a mile at the end of it with an empty barrow to fill it, and nobody in that yard had been told whose fault anything was.

# Chapter 685

## The Twenty-Eighth Of The Eighteenth, And A Printed Sheet With Four Terms On It That Every Person In This Yard Could Defend

The pair was not read on the Monday and the girl from the second place said so from the gate, and she was about twenty-two and she had come down the lane on foot with a bundle under her arm.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so, and I have never stood in a yard in my life and I walked down that lane at five this morning so I would be here before it started."**

She put the bundle down on the gate step.

**"There are four of us who came down with copies and there are nine households up that lane and every one of the nine has sent something down to be answered. So there is a bundle on this step with more paper in it than one morning's reading, and I am not having a day when the two figures out of that rack are the only figures anybody says out loud."**

She sat down on the step beside the bundle.

**"I will be in this yard till noon and then I am going to walk back up, and I am not here to be impressed by anything and I have not come to ask you for anything."**

The rotation came round at about the seventh hour, because a Monday is one of the twelve mornings it comes round on, and it was the twelfth and last of them.

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack and put the book down open and did not read it out straight away, and he read it out twice.

**"A hundred and fifty-one in force, which is one more than yesterday and one more than the morning before that, and this is the twelfth and last morning of this kind the book is going to carry before the days turn over."**

He put his thumb under the first line and left it there.

**"Twelve mornings, and it has gone up by one on every one of them and by two on none of them, and I have watched it happen and I have not once been able to get a word out of it that explains anything."**

He put his thumb under the second line.

**"Of those, twenty-nine have already gone out at whatever hour their own hour is, and the rest of them are a hundred and twenty-two, still on the books at this hour, and that is above a hundred and will be above a hundred on the morning after tomorrow.**"

He closed the book and squared it against the edge of the table.

**"Twelve mornings of this kind have now run in this holding and this one is the twelfth of them, and I have said on every one of them that a field which stood still is a field that did what the book says a field does when it waits. I am not going to say it a thirteenth time."**

He set the second book down on the table rather than back on the shelf.

**"Nobody in this yard is to be blamed for anything that stayed where it was. And if the office four days north wants a column with a name against every one of those twelve mornings, it can have the book and not my opinion."**

Sera Quill had the slate face up at the end of the long table and put her thumb under the two figures at the bottom of it.

**"Ninety-ninth and eighty-sixth,"** she said. **"Thirteen apart, and in four years of carrying that slate the gap has not once closed, and this is the twelfth rotation morning of them and I have said that sentence eleven times in a yard and I am not going to improve on it this morning."**

She moved her thumb down onto the third line.

**"There is a third line under those two, in my own hand, and it is the only thing on that slate anybody can check me against. What it says is that the second of the two is carried, and that nothing printed in this holding ever moves it."**

She turned the slate face down on the table and left her hand flat on the back of it.

**"So the eighty-sixth is not visible anywhere in this yard this morning, and on the morning after tomorrow there will be a different figure under that line, and it will be one higher, because it has never once been anything else."**

The clerk had a printed sheet out of his coat before the yard was up and had been carrying it since the seventh hour and had not opened it.

**"This came down the lane with the riders at first light on a sheet of the same print as the one that came up the fen road yesterday,"** he said, **"and it carries the same seal, and it has four terms on it and a place for a name at the foot of it, and I am going to read it out and I am going to read all four of them, including the one that is not in this holding's favour, because the one that is not in this holding's favour is the reason this yard is going to have to answer something."**

He read it out from the top.

**"First. The central harvest of the completed phase is guaranteed in writing for the length of one season and not beyond that season, and the guarantee is the office's and is not this holding's to check. Second. The Crown is given continuous access to the regional flow ledger and to the figures in it, and the access is not a copy and is not a report. Third. Every regional council may petition these terms in writing and no council may reopen a branch. Fourth. The caretaker link passes to the engineer of record on the office's own day and holds for the length of one season only."**

He put the sheet down on the middle table.

**"That is all four and that is all of it. And I have not left out a clause because I did not like a clause, and there is a fifth heading at the bottom of it with nothing under it, and I have looked at that fifth heading three times this morning and I am not going to say what it says."**

Nobody in that yard called the sheet wrong. Nobody called it contemptible and nobody called it soft, and nobody found anything in the first three terms that this holding could put a finger on, and nobody found anything in the fourth either.

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end and stood in front of the sheet and did not touch it.

**"Every one of those four is buildable,"** he said. **"That is what I have to say about it and I am the only man in this yard whose trade it is to say that about a thing. A man who lays pipe for a living can look at those four and not one of them is a thing you could not lay."**

He set the hod down outside the one room door.

**"And I am not going to stand in this yard and tell anybody that a mason can look at a sheet of terms and tell them whether to put a name at the foot of it, because a mason cannot, and there is a pipe behind that tap house that has come away wet nine Tuesdays running and will come away wet on the tenth.**"

Then the fourth place's man read the launder, because he has read it every morning since Tuesday and nobody asked him to stop.

**"The four hundred and forty-eighth of the run came down that channel this morning at one thousand and two hundred and eighty-six hundredweight, which is eight on, because a Monday is an even morning."**

He wiped under the line with the back of his hand and stopped with his hand flat on the stone.

**"I have been putting the weight first and the ordinal second for a week and a man who watches a number for a week starts believing the order he reads it in. Upper half two hundred and thirteen, lower half two hundred and two, window four hundred and fifteen."**

**"That is the highest figure this channel has come down at in the whole time I have been standing next to it,"** he said, **"and I have been standing next to it for two days, and I am not going to say how long the man who has been standing next to it for four years has been reading a different number off it, and I am not going to say it is a good number or a bad one. It is the highest and there is a man coming down this road tomorrow who is going to read it off the same stone."**

**"Standing where it stood yesterday, on the low side of that window, is two hundred and two. The other one took a point overnight and stands at two hundred and thirteen, because a Monday is an even morning. Add them if you like and you get four hundred and fifteen, and I have not added them and I am not going to."**

He wrote it on his own copy sheet, and then he came back to the printed sheet and did the entry.

> The terms of one season's caretaker link, offered in the office's own hand and printed and sealed, carried down the lane at first light on the morning of the twenty-eighth and entered against the clerk's own hand and the standing of the paper itself:
>
> **First.** The central harvest is guaranteed in writing for one season and not beyond it, and the guarantee is the office's and is not this holding's to check.
>
> **Second.** The Crown is given continuous access to the regional flow ledger and to the figures in it, and the access is not a copy and not a report.
>
> **Third.** Every regional council may petition in writing and no council may reopen a branch.
>
> **Fourth.** The link passes to the engineer of record on the office's own day and holds for one season only.
>
> The count of blanks on the board in the one room is thirty-nine and is not altered by this entry, and the second rule under those blanks stands open and empty and opens nothing.

The clerk put his own hand flat on the entry and left it there and then took it off.

**"That is entered,"** he said, **"and it is entered on the paper and not in either of the two books, because neither of those two books has a column for a thing that is not this holding's own standing. And I entered it because it was put in front of me and not because anybody in this yard asked me to."**

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet in front of her and did not open it at the second fold, and she said the head of it once and then said nothing else about the paper on the middle table.

**"Four hundred and forty-six at the head, with the four hundred and forty-fifth under it and the four hundred and forty-seventh over it, in the small hand."**

She squared the sheet against the wood of the table.

**"And a printed sheet has come down that lane with four terms on it and a place for a name at the foot of it, and every one of those four terms is defensible, and that is a harder thing for this yard to sit with than a bad one would have been."**

Kellan Rusk had the second book open on his forearm and read the three ages off it without being asked and in the order of a man who has run out of other things to read.

**"Those three of ours are the charter at two hundred and forty-eight days, the register form at three hundred and forty-two, and a ruled line in Silling's own book standing at three hundred and thirty-six with nothing on it whatsoever."**

He shut the book on the last of the three.

**"I have said them in that order every morning for a month and this morning I am saying them in that order because a printed sheet has come down the lane with four terms on it and not one of those four is ours, and it is a strange thing to be glad of and I am going to be glad of it."**

He shut the book and put it back on the shelf.

**"And the two other books are at fifty-three apiece and have never once been the same list, and neither of them took a printed sheet off a rider this morning, and I did not put that sheet in either of them and I am not going to."**

She looked at the girl on the step.

**"You have come down that lane at five this morning with a bundle and you have sat on that step and you have not asked this holding for anything, and I am not going to ask you what your household thinks of that sheet either, because you did not offer it."**

Nia Vale came along the wall and did the two figures on the wall before she did the gate line, and she did not wipe the printed sheet at all.

**"Two numbers are cut into that wall and I will give you the one you have to walk furthest for. Eight hundred and seventy-nine is in the stone at the tool house end at about the height of a man's shoulder. From there it is forty-seven of masonry back to the gate, and the second number is eight hundred and thirty-two."**

She folded the cloth once and left it in her hand.

**"There is a drawer behind the near one. Shut, all day, and the key for it on a single nail in the one room doorway, and I went past that nail three times today without looking at it."**

She came down to the gate post.

**"There is no discharge against that line at thirty-one or at thirty, it is paid at thirty-one, and I have been saying that figure out loud in a yard for a week with a piece of chalk in my hand and I would put it in a book this morning if there were anywhere in this yard to put a book."**

She stood over the three sheets that had been lying on the long table for a hundred and forty-five days.

**"The sheet at the near end of that table is a hundred and forty-five days old today and about nine inches of board stand clear at either end of it. Next to it, folded shut on its fold. Then the signed and sealed and named one, which nobody in this yard can find a fault in. And now the printed sheet that came down the lane this morning, with a space ruled at the foot of it for a name and nothing yet written in the space.**"

She folded the cloth over her arm and stood with it.

**"Not one of the four has been entered, and the one that has been entered today is not one of those four, and I would like a yard that keeps figures straight to notice the difference."**

Soren Rill came in from the gate at about the seventh hour and stood in the middle of the yard and did not go near the low board.

**"There is a ladder against that tool house wall and nobody has been on it, and nobody is standing at the bottom of it either, and I would like that noticed by anybody who has come up that lane expecting to be asked to climb something."**

He stood at the low board and put his hands in his coat instead of on it.

**"What is on that board has no date and has not been picked up and has not gone back to anybody, and the two people it belongs to are both in this yard and neither of them has a figure against their name in this holding or in that office."**

He looked across the middle of the eleven acres.

**"And the bare ring in there has gone through this week exactly as it went through the four years before it: no foot, no tape, nothing said about what it would cost."**

He looked at the sheet on the middle table.

**"And a man is going to arrive at that gate tomorrow morning from four days north with the paper in his own hand, and I have been in this holding four years and I have never once known what to do about a morning before it happened. I would like somebody in this yard to have a piece of advice for me tonight and I am not going to get one and I am not going to ask twice."**

The terms lay on the middle table all afternoon with a ruled space at the foot of them and nothing written in it, and every person in that yard who read them found something in them that they could defend, and one of the nine read them and found four things and said so out loud and then sat down on a step.

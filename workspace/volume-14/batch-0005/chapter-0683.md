# Chapter 683

## The Twenty-Sixth Of The Eighteenth, And A Man In A Doorway Saying The Only Sentence He Had Left After Six Weeks Of Being Asked To Be Noticed

The pair was not read on the Saturday and Odile Vray said so from the gate with a barrow on its side behind her and the canal wheel turning.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so, and I will not be in this yard much longer so I would rather say it now than at noon."**

She came round the end of the cart.

**"I have been in this yard four days and I have read four figures off a wall and three off a slate and two off a copy sheet, and every one of those came at me while I was carrying something. And a woman who is carrying something cannot also be a woman standing still at seven in the morning hearing two figures read out loud, and I have found that out four times in four days and I am not going to find it out a fifth."**

She put the barrow down.

**"The second place has not got anybody who can be asked what that pair means. There are nine households up that lane and the oldest of them is sixty-one and not one of them has ever read those two figures off that wall. So I am not the woman who hears it, and today I would rather be the woman with a barrow."**

She took one end of it and went out through the gate, and the yard let her go.

The launder came down at half past eight and the man of about thirty-four of the fourth place read it, which he has done every morning since Tuesday, and he is getting better at it and worse at stopping.

**"It is the four hundred and forty-sixth of the run, and it came down at one thousand and two hundred and eighty-three hundredweight, which is eight on."**

He wiped under the line with the back of his hand.

**"A Saturday is an even morning and on an even morning the upper half is the one that goes, so I will tell you which of the two took the point before I tell you the halves: the upper one."**

He ran the chalk back along the line under the figures.

**"The top half of that window is on two hundred and twelve and took a point in the night. The half underneath has not moved since Friday and is on two hundred and one. Four hundred and thirteen of a window between them."**

He put the chalk in his pocket because he had picked it up out of habit and there was no chalk in his hand to put away.

**"I am going to say the same thing on the last morning of this week that I have said on the other six, which is that a number on a wall is not a conversation, and I have four sheets of my own in a satchel that nobody has signed and they are not on any column anybody has shown me."**

Then the man of about fifty stood up off the low ledge inside the one room doorway, where he has sat every morning for six weeks, and the yard had got used to a man being in that doorway.

**"It is not read and I am not saying either of the two sentences this holding has on it,"** he said, **"and this is the sixth week I have asked to be noticed with this key on this nail and nobody in this yard has ever once said no to that to my face."**

He put his hand on the frame of the doorway.

**"Nobody has hurried me. Nobody has stood in this doorway and told me it is a hard thing to be asked to do. It is a key, it is a drawer, it is a man of about fifty who would rather it was somebody else, and I have said those three things every morning since the day I asked and this morning I am going to say something else instead of them."**

He stopped, and the yard waited, and Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at that moment and stopped outside the one room door with the hod on his shoulder and did not go in.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Harlan Vetch put the hod down outside the door and did not pick it up again. Nobody answered him. Nobody said it was a hard thing to say, nobody said it had taken guts, nobody thanked him, nobody apologised to him for the six weeks, and nobody in that yard said one word about his own father or anybody else's, and nobody's arm was touched, and the morning went on with a man standing in a doorway who had been asked to be noticed for six weeks and had said a different thing.

He sat back down on the ledge inside the doorway about a minute later and did not say anything else for the rest of the day, and nobody went in and asked him what he had meant by it.

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack and did not read the count out, because he said on Friday that he would stop saying it until it moved.

**"The rotation does not come round on a Saturday,"** he said. **"It comes round on the morning after tomorrow and it goes up by one and by one, and the two smaller ones under it have not moved since Wednesday and I am not going to say them out loud twice for no reason."**

He put the second book on the table and opened it and shut it again.

**"That shelf holds three ages. Three hundred and forty days on the register form, and two hundred and forty-six on the charter, and the third of the three belongs to a ruled line in Silling's own book and stands at three hundred and thirty-four with nothing against it, and nobody in this yard has written on it since before I came.**"

He squared the book against the edge.

**"And there are six ruled rows on that shelf against what this holding does not know and there have been six for four years, and no row has been cut above the sixth for a pipe, a phase, a machine or a satchel, and I am not going to cut one, and anybody who wants to cut one can come and find the chisel."**

He stopped himself and looked at what he had just said.

**"That was a bad sentence and I am leaving it standing, because I have had a way of saying that figure for four years and this morning I could not get round to it in a straight line. Six is the figure and it is a figure and not a sum."**

Harlan Vetch went in and came out again and went and put his hand on the pipe in the north-side room wall and left it there for a moment.

**"The pipe in that wall holds and it is holding against a head that has gone east, and I would like this yard to sit with both halves of that sentence for as long as it takes."**

He came back out and looked at the wet marks on the floor rather than at the wall.

**"And nothing behind the tap house has improved either. I said nine Tuesdays this week and I am not saying it a tenth time, and the day anybody in this yard uses the word settled back there I will take the chalk off that wall myself."**

He came out and looked along the floor of that room where nine cans had gone past twice a day all week.

**"And there are eleven marks wet on those boards and there is a cistern a hand's breadth higher than it was at six this morning, and I have built five things in this yard and the fifth one is in a wall and it is not filling anything, and I am not going to stand in a yard on a Saturday and explain a thing I have not understood myself."**

Nia Vale did the two figures before the gate line for the second time in five days and nobody said anything about the order.

**"Eight hundred and thirty at this end, eight hundred and seventy-seven at the tool house end, forty-seven of wall between them. The drawer behind the near one is shut, the key is on its nail in the doorway, and the man who came to that doorway at the seventh hour this morning did not touch it."**

She went up the wall.

**"It is paid at thirty-one and it has never once been discharged out of that line in thirty-one or in thirty, and I want both numbers said because there was a fortnight ago when half this yard believed the figure had come down."**

She wiped the chalk round the line and not over it.

**"It went up by one, and the reason it went up is that a load was paid in on a Thursday. Nobody in this holding has ever let a load out of that line and nobody in this holding has been paid for letting one out."**

She put the cloth over her shoulder and went on up the wall, and stopped at the long table.

**"There are three papers on that table and there have been three for four years. The eldest is a hundred and forty-three days old and about nine inches of clear board stand either side of it. The middle one is folded shut. The third is signed and sealed and named, and there is nothing wrong with it, and I have said so seven mornings running and I am not going to soften it today because the yard is in a mood.**"

She wiped round the third one.

**"Not one entered, not one refused, no column cut under any of them, and no column cut under a barrow or a satchel or a thing said in a doorway."**

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet face up on the table from about the fourth hour and she did not read it out and she did not cover it, and at about the ninth hour a man asked her whether the yard was going to say anything about the morning before last and she answered him without lifting her eyes.

**"It carries four hundred and forty-four on the top and the small hand underneath has the four hundred and forty-third a step down and the four hundred and forty-fifth a step over, and it has made that shape for nine years and it is not going to start carrying the mornings now.**"

She put her palm flat over all three of them and left it there.

**"I read one sum out in this yard last Sunday and there was one place in it and there is not going to be another, and I am not going to be a woman who reads the same sum twice because a yard has had a bad day.**"

She squared the sheet.

**"Four places on that column and no fifth, and a man from the fourth place has been sitting on a step for four days with four sheets nobody has signed, and I have not signed them and this yard is not going to."**

Soren Rill came in from the gate at about the seventh hour and went to the low board and stood in front of it and did not touch it.

**"Go and look at that ladder if you want the whole of it. Nobody has been up it and nobody in this yard is standing underneath it waiting to be asked."**

He rested the flat of his hand on the low board itself and kept it there, clear of the paper.

**"What is lying there has been lying there four years. It has never told anybody when it arrived, nobody has picked it up to look at it, and nobody has carried it back to whoever left it. Both of those people are in this holding and there is not one figure in this yard set against either of them."**

He looked across at the middle of the eleven acres and did not go towards it.

**"And that ring in there went past my boots at the seventh hour and again at dusk and on neither occasion did I stop, and it has still got no tape on it and no price on it and no foot on it."**

He put his hands in his coat and looked across the middle of the acres without going towards it.

**"And nobody has been asked to choose anything in this holding in nine days and I have stopped keeping the figure of it, because a man who keeps a figure of how often he has not asked a thing is still keeping a figure."**

Two left a holding and got on with it. The cistern stood full at dusk with a hose off it that went nowhere, a man who had been asked to be noticed for six weeks sat in a doorway with his hand on a frame, and the long sheet lay face up on the middle table with nobody's hand on it and a shape of three figures at the head of it that had been the same shape since before a man in this yard was born.

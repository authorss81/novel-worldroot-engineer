# Chapter 686

## The Twenty-Ninth Of The Eighteenth, And An Offer Made Across A Table, And A Refusal, And A Woman Writing It Down In Her Own Hand

There were nine people standing round the middle table at the seventh hour on the last morning, and Renn Ashby opened the book on it and put his thumb in and read out what was written there.

Nobody in that yard told him to hurry and nobody told him to stop.

**"One hundred and ninety-five of three hundred and seventy-three,"** he said. **"Of this month."**

He shut the book on his thumb and did not take his thumb out, and stood there.

**"That is the last time I will say those two figures in this yard at this trough,"** he said, **"and I am not going to make anything of it and I am not going to thank anybody for it. Tomorrow there will be two figures and the day after there will be two figures and the heading will not have moved, and whoever reads that book next will be standing where I am standing."**

He came off his heel and reached back for the chair back and stood against it for a moment, which is the first time anybody in this yard has seen him touch that chair at all.

The clerk took his copy sheet out of his coat at about the eighth hour and put it into the hands of the rider who came down the lane at first light with three other riders behind him, and he did it at the gate and not at the table.

**"That is what I carry,"** he said. **"The second place's own word in the second place's own mouth, and four sheets off the fourth place with nobody's name at the foot of any of them, and the whole of this holding's own standing on my own paper and not one figure of it on the column's. I have said all of it out loud in this yard on the mornings I said it, and you are welcome to any of it."**

The rider put it inside his coat and did not look at it in the gate, and went back up the lane.

Then the launder came down the middle road at half past eight and the highest of the run had gone by on the Monday and this morning it was five off that, and the fourth place's man read it because he has read it every morning for a week.

**"Four hundred and forty-ninth of the run,"** he said, and then, a beat later, **"and one thousand and two hundred and eighty-one hundredweight, which is five off, because a Tuesday is an odd morning and the lower half is the one that moves on one."**

He wiped under the line and left his hand on the stone.

**"Five off, because a Tuesday is an odd morning and on an odd morning the half that falls in the night is the one that goes first. That one is on two hundred and three. The other has not moved since Monday and is on two hundred and thirteen."**

He took his hand off the wall.

**"Four hundred and sixteen of a window between the two, and that is the largest that figure has been in the whole run, and nobody in this yard has said a word about it and neither am I."**

He took his hand off the wall and put it in his pocket.

**"I have said that number every morning this week and this is the last of them, and I do not know what I am going to do on the morning after this one, and I have not got a plan and I am not going to make one in a yard."**

Sera Quill was at the wall when he finished and she had her slate under her arm and she said what she said without putting the slate down or opening it.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

Nobody answered her. Nobody said it was a hard thing to say, nobody thanked her for saying it, and nobody in that yard said a word about what it had to do with the sheet that came down the lane yesterday or the one lying on the middle table now.

**"I said it once before in this holding about two weeks ago, at the back of the tap house, and I have not said it since,"** she said, **"and I am saying it now on a morning when somebody four days north of here is going to come down that lane, and I would like it on the record that I said it before she got here and not after."**

She put the slate in her coat and shut the flap.

Hesta Lyle came down to the long table at about the eighth hour and put the sheet she keeps on the table in front of her and squared it and did not open it at the second fold.

**"That sheet has four hundred and forty-seven standing at the top of it. The small hand under the top has the four hundred and forty-sixth one step down and the four hundred and forty-eighth one step up, and it will have a third figure under it by Thursday and a fourth by the end of the month."**

She took her hand off the sheet.

**"And I am not going to read the sum out this morning either. A woman who had four places on a column and no fifth on the twenty-second of this month does not get to arrive on the twenty-ninth with a different one, and I would like this yard to have that out of me in my own hand rather than finding it out."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack with the count not due for four more mornings.

**"The rotation does not come round today. It came round yesterday for the last time and it left a hundred and fifty-one in force with twenty-nine of them gone out and a hundred and twenty-two not gone out, and it will not move again until the day after tomorrow, and I am not going to say any of those three figures out loud again this morning because I said them yesterday and they have not changed since."**

He closed it with his thumb where his thumb goes and squared it against the end of the shelf.

**"Three of those books have gone past the three hundreds and I have not opened any of them this morning, and here they are in the order I would want them in if somebody were about to build something at the end of a month."**

He turned the charter round on the shelf before he read it.

**"Charter first, two hundred and forty-nine days, because a cistern answers under a charter and I have watched people be surprised by that. Then the register form at three hundred and forty-three. And then the one that belongs to nobody, three hundred and thirty-seven, on a ruled line in Silling's own book with nothing written on it."**

He put the charter back the way it stands.

**"That is a different order from the shelf and it is the same order I have used all week, and the shelf does not care, and neither do I, and that is the last thing I am going to say about those books."**

Then the riders came down the middle road at about the ninth hour, and there were four of them and one of them was not a rider.

She came through the gate on foot with her own people behind her and she was about fifty-five and she had walked the last half mile, and nobody in that yard knew her and one of them knew her very well.

**"Chancellor Vey,"** the fourth place's man said, from the step, and he did not get up.

There is no reading frame in that yard and there has not been one for four years, and this is what the gate looked like: a woman in a good coat standing just inside it with a folded sheet under her arm, and a long table four feet away from a wall that a mason built, and nine people standing round that table who had all been up since six.

**"You have had three documents from me in four years,"** Iona Vey said, **"and you have entered none of them, and you have not found a fault in any of them, and I have read the copies of that from an office four days north and I am not here about it."**

She walked the length of the yard and put her hand flat on the middle table.

**"I will read the terms, because you have not and because four of you were saying yesterday that they were defensible, and I would rather you heard them in the voice that wrote them than in the voice of a bookkeeper."**

She read them out from the sheet without looking at it more than once.

**"Every regional council under the third of these may petition in writing, and under no council's power is a branch reopened. The link, under the fourth, passes to the engineer of record on this day and holds for one season and not one season more. The central harvest of the completed phase, under the first, is guaranteed in writing for that season. And under the second, the Crown is given continuous access to the regional flow ledger and to every figure in it, and it is not a copy and it is not a report.**"

She put the sheet on the long table beside the other three and squared it.

**"Every one of those is defensible and I am not going to pretend to you that one of them is a kindness. The third one is the one that costs you the most and it is the one I would keep if I were sitting where you are sitting, and I have kept a good deal longer for less."**

Somebody asked her whether the offer was the one on the table with the ruled space at the foot of it, and she said it was, and she said the space at the foot of it was for a name and not for a seal.

Then Marek came in from the north wall at about the tenth hour with a length of chalk in his hand and put the chalk on the table before anybody asked him for it.

**"I have been in this yard eleven mornings saying nothing about where the water comes from,"** he said, **"and on the fourteenth of this month I laid a pipe in the wall of a room off a head at the top of the middle road, and on the Thursday I opened that head myself with my own hands, and I did not do it because anybody asked me."**

He did not wipe his hands and he did not sit down.

**"There is one thing I have lost that I am never going to have again, and there were four before this month, and there are four now, and I am not going to tell this room what any of them was this morning because it is not a thing you say across a table to a woman you met an hour ago."**

He put his hand flat on the ruled space at the foot of her sheet and left it there.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Nobody in that room answered him. Nobody said it was a hard thing to say, nobody said it was why, and nobody in that yard thanked anybody for anything at any point in the rest of the day.

Then he took his hand off the ruled space and he said the rest of it in one breath and he did not soften it afterwards and he did not qualify it and he did not put anything in front of it or behind it.

**"No. The link is not taken and it is not held by me and it is not held for one season or for any number of seasons, and this holding will not put a name in that space today or on any other morning."**

**"Then it is refused,"** Iona Vey said.

**"It is refused,"** Marek said.

Nobody in that room said the word again and nobody asked him a second time and nobody asked her a second time either, and the refusal was not refused in its turn, and it was not put off, and nobody in that yard said anything that would have made it a later.

Then Nia Vale came round the end of the long table and took her own sheet out of her coat, and it was the one that has been folded in there since the Saturday before this one, and she put it on the table and wrote on it standing up, and she wrote it in her own hand and it took her about a minute and a half.

**"Is that the holding's record?"** Iona Vey asked.

**"It is mine,"** Nia Vale said. **"Nobody else in this yard signs it and nobody else in this yard has been asked to. The clerk keeps copies and that is his trade and not this. You will have a copy because I will give you one, and you will not have the sheet."**

She wrote three lines, and then she put the pen down and squared the sheet against the edge of the table and read the first line of it out loud to the room, and her hand was steady and she did not read the other two.

**"One season is refused. The link is not taken. The name is not given."**

Then she said the rest of it to the woman rather than to the yard.

**"I am not going to say this to you again and I am not going to thank anybody in this room, because there is nothing to thank anybody for. A man has said a word and I have written it down. That is a morning's work in a yard and it will be the same on the morning after tomorrow."**

Iona Vey took up her own sheet, and squared it against the table the way everybody in this yard squares paper, and she did not tear it up and she did not leave it and she did not put her name anywhere.

**"Then the ledger is yours and the seasons are yours,"** she said. **"I am not going to come back down this lane to be told the same word twice, and I am not going to take anything off that table except what I brought down it."**

She walked out through the gate at about the eleventh hour with her own people behind her and got on a horse at the far side of the wall, and nobody in that yard followed her out and nobody spoke to her and nobody put a hand on her arm, and she went up the middle road in the same state she came down it in.

The yard stood about for a quarter of an hour and then got on with the morning, and that is the whole of what the morning did after that.

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the two figures and the gate line and the three sheets on the long table in the order she does them, and the fourth sheet stayed on the table where it had been squared.

**"Paid at thirty-one,"** she said, **"and it has not been discharged in thirty-one or in thirty, and I have said that sentence in this yard seven mornings running and tomorrow morning I will say it again and the day after that."**

She came up the wall.

**"Nearest the gate, where you are standing, the stone says eight hundred and thirty-three. Walk the wall away from me to the tool house and it says eight hundred and eighty. Forty-seven of wall is the whole of the difference and it has been the whole of the difference since the wall went up."**

She wiped each of the two in turn and neither of them was touched by the cloth.

**"The drawer behind this end is shut and has been shut every hour of the four years anybody here can remember. One key, one nail, in the doorway behind you, and it will be on that nail tonight."**

She stopped at the long table with the cloth in her hand.

**"Four sheets are on that table now. One of them is a hundred and forty-six days old and has about nine inches of clear board standing either side of it. One is folded shut on its fold. One is signed and sealed and named. And one has a space ruled at the foot of it with nothing in the space.**"

She wiped round all four of them.

**"Not one of the four entered, not one refused, and no column cut under any of them in this holding's book. And the thing that was written this morning went onto a sheet of my own and not onto any of them."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in at the fourth hour and did her nine trips and put the last can down on the boards.

**"Nine trips on the last morning of the month and nine trips on the morning after it,"** she said, **"and there is a woman four days north of here this afternoon who has been told no by a man with a piece of chalk in his hand, and I have carried water in this yard through both of those and I would like somebody to write down that neither of them put a stop on the ninth trip."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at about the twelfth hour and stood at the north-side room door with the hod on his shoulder and did not go in.

**"Behind the tap house there is one joint and it has come away wet every morning since the day it went in, and there is a pipe standing behind that joint which was there before I ever came to this holding."**

He looked at the cistern door rather than at the wall behind the tap house.

**"I have said nine Tuesdays. If there is a tenth then somebody else can count it, because a man who counts his own leak is a man who has started keeping a figure on it."**

He set the hod down outside the door.

**"And I stood in front of that woman's paper yesterday and I said every one of those four was buildable, and I have not taken a word of that back this morning and I am not going to. A man who withdraws what he said about a thing he was asked to look at is not careful, he is only loud."**

He looked along the floor at the eleven wet marks.

**"And I have built five things in this holding and none of them has changed what a man in a good coat can come down a lane and ask for."**

At dusk Soren Rill came in past the tool house and went along the inside edge of the eleven acres the way he has gone along it for four years, and then stood in front of the low board with his hands still in his coat.

**"Nobody in this holding has been climbed in on by anybody in four years, and nobody climbed anything this morning while a woman stood at that table with a ruled space on it and room in the space for one."**

He laid one hand flat on the low board itself, well clear of what was lying on it.

**"The ladder belongs to two people who are both in this yard and there is no figure set against either of them here or in that office, and there was none set against either of them at ten o'clock this morning while that space was lying open on the table."**

He took his hand off the board and stood with his back to it.

**"What is on that low board has never been dated and has never been picked up and has not been taken back. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres took no boot today and no tape and no price."**

He looked up and down the middle road.

**"And nobody in this holding was asked to choose anything today. Not one person. And I have been in this yard nine days offering things to be chosen between and this morning I offered nothing and there was a space on a table for somebody to fill and nobody filled it and I was not disappointed by that and I am not going to say I was."**

The rack key went onto its nail inside the one room doorway at dusk, and Kellan Rusk said what he had to say from inside the room rather than from the step, and nobody moved out of his way to hear it.

**"Thirty-nine blanks cut in that board, and the rule under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and a cart is not due at that wall for six days. Six ruled rows for what we do not know and nothing cut above the sixth."**

He shut the rack on the last of it.

**"Seven sessions in this holding's book and a Tuesday enters none. And eleven journeys marked against that barrow with no twelfth beside them, and a use log that stops at fifteen lines with a clean space above the fifteenth."**

He shut the rack on the last of it and the key went into his pocket.

**"And the man at the end of the north row is at twenty-nine fetchings, unfetched, unasked, and I have said it in this yard four mornings running and I am not going to stop now."**

The man of about fifty was still sitting inside the one room doorway at dusk and his hand was on the frame, and the six weeks he asked to be noticed with went on being noticed with him, and he had said nothing at all since the seventh hour.

The girl from the second place was still at the gate at the end of it with her bundle under her arm, and she had said nothing at all since five in the morning either, and nobody in that yard asked her one word about anything.

On the office's own day, at about the ninth hour, a woman four days north of this yard came down that lane and put four defensible terms on a table in a holding of four hundred people, and a man refused them in one breath and a woman wrote it down in her own hand and nobody in that room thanked anybody, and at dusk the launder came down the middle road for the four hundred and forty-ninth time of the run, the pipe in the north wall was holding, and the yard went on working.

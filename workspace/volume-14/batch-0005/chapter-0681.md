# Chapter 681

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Eighteenth, And The Reach Going Out Far Enough, And What It Left Standing On The Wrong Side Of A Ford

The pair was not read on the Thursday and the man of the north row with the cough stood up off his bench at the seventh hour and said so before anybody had the book open, which he has not done in two months.

**"It is not read this morning and I am the one saying so, and I have got a reason and it is not my chest."**

He stood at the end of the middle table with both hands on it.

**"Something went out of this gate at four this morning that has never gone out of this gate before, and it went out on a cart with three men on it, and I was awake when it went because I am always awake at four. And I have been sitting on this bench for four years waiting for a first thing to happen in this holding, and one has, and I am not going to stand at that trough at seven and put two more on top of it."**

He took his hands off the table.

**"A man who has been up since four does not get to be the man who says the permanent thing in the same morning. That is not a rule anybody made. That is just what a morning is."**

Marek took the line up at the fourth hour from the head at the top of the middle road, with two men from this holding and one man from the fourth place, and he did it himself with his own hands on the valve, and he wrote nothing down at the time and has said since that he could not have written anything.

The reach went out along the old course under the middle road and up past the tool house wall and away east, and the four counties that the line runs through do not lie in a straight line and nobody in that yard had ever seen how much of it there was.

Odile Vray went out of the gate in a cart with the man of about thirty-four of the fourth place's driver at about the sixth hour and said she was going up the lane to her own place and that she would be back before dark, and nobody asked her to and nobody thanked her afterwards for having gone.

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack at about the seventh hour because the rotation came round, and the rotation does come round on a Thursday, and the yard had not had one for three mornings.

**"The book says a hundred and fifty in force, and that is the eleventh of twelve mornings of this kind and the figure has gone up by one on every one of them and by two on none of them."**

He put the second book down on the table and left his hand flat on it.

**"Of that hundred and fifty, twenty-nine went out at whatever hour their own hour is. A hundred and twenty-one are still on the books at this hour waiting for a turn that has not come round yet."**

He closed the book.

**"And nothing left this holding today. Nobody was down for it. That is not a fault and it is not going to be written up as one, and I have said that on nine mornings of this kind in this yard and I am saying it on this one because this morning it is the truest thing anybody will say."**

He put the second book down on the table and left his hand flat on it, and the yard went on past the middle table without looking at either of them.

Sera Quill had the slate out on the end of the long table and turned it face up and put her thumb under the two figures at the bottom of it, which she has done at every morning of this holding when the rotation comes round.

**"Thirteen apart, and here are both of them: ninety-eighth, and eighty-fifth. I have never once seen that gap narrow in four years of carrying this slate and I have stopped expecting it to."**

She turned the slate over onto its face.

**"The eighty-fifth is not printed anywhere. It is carried under that slate and nothing in this holding, on paper or on stone, has ever moved it, and I have just turned the slate over so that you can see there is no second figure on the back of it for anybody to check me against."**

She kept her thumb where it was.

**"I have said that line out loud nine times in a yard and this is the tenth, and I am going to say it a tenth time on this morning because a thing went out of the gate at four this morning and I would like on the record that the one object in this yard that admits out loud that it is carrying a figure it cannot account for is still being carried."**

Nobody argued with the slate, and the yard got on with the morning.

Then the water came down at half past eight and Marek read it himself from the top of the north wall, where he had been all morning, and he read the whole of it in one go without stopping, which nobody in that yard had ever seen him do.

**"Upper half two hundred and eleven this morning, and it took one in the night, because a Thursday is an even morning and on an even morning the upper one is the one that goes. The lower half is standing at two hundred, bare, where it stood yesterday."**

He wiped the chalk on his sleeve and went back down the line of the wall with his thumb under the other figure.

**"Four hundred and eleven of a window between those two, and that is the second morning they have stood exactly like that, and this yard is not going to make a speech about it."**

**"And the water in the channel is one thousand and two hundred and eighty hundredweight and it is the four hundred and forty-fourth of the run. I have read that number off that stone every morning for four years and this morning I read it off it while it was going east."**

He came down off the wall and stood in the middle of the yard and said the other thing, and he said it with the chalk still in his hand.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

A man went past with a barrow. Kellan Rusk turned a page he did not need to turn. Nobody answered him and nobody said it was a hard thing to say and nobody thanked him for it, and the morning went on with the reach running out east under the middle road and nobody in this yard able to say where it had got to.

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at about the ninth hour with the hod on his shoulder and stood at the cistern door and looked at the wet marks on the floor.

**"Eleven marks wet on those boards and a cistern that is full and a pipe in the wall of that room that has not got a stick of water in it yet, because a pipe that is charged is not a pipe that is filling,"** he said. **"I have built five things in this holding and the fifth one is that pipe, and I laid the first four of them myself and I have laid the fifth one myself, and none of the five has ever carried anything at four in the morning before this week."**

He put his thumb on the door frame and took it off again.

**"It weeps and it holds and the standing pipe behind it is the same pipe. Nine Tuesdays. I am not going to be the man who says it has settled, and I would like the yard to notice that I have said the same thing on ten mornings and not once added anything to it."**

He went up the north end again and did not look at the chalk line on the floor, because there was a pipe in the way of it.

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet out on the middle table with the yard standing round it, and she did not open it at the second fold and she did not cover it with her hand.

**"Four hundred and forty-two at the head of it,"** she said, **"with the four hundred and forty-first a step under and the four hundred and forty-third a step over, in the small hand, and that hand has been making that shape on this yard's mornings for nine years and it is not going to know what happened today."**

She squared the sheet against the edge of the table.

**"And I am not going to read the sum out again. I read it out on Sunday and it had one place in it and no second answer and none in reserve, and this morning that place is nine miles off on the wrong side of a ford and there is nothing further I can do with a piece of arithmetic about it."**

She put her hand flat on the wood.

**"Somebody is going to ask me this afternoon whether this holding knew. And the answer is that this holding knew on Sunday morning, in this yard, out loud, with a sheet on the table, and that a name in a yard is not a decision and I have said that twice now and I am not going to say it a third time in a voice that is trying to be heard."**

Odile Vray came back into the yard at about half past eleven with the cart and got down off it and did not say anything for about a minute, and then she said it at the gate post where the compost line is.

**"The head is drawn and the water at the top of our lane has stopped coming at about the ninth hour this morning,"** she said. **"Not a trickle. Stopped. Our trough at the top has been full since six and it is empty now and there is nothing in the channel below it and there is not going to be anything in it today or tomorrow."**

She stood with her hand on the gate.

**"Nine households take off that channel. There are two children at one of them and there are four old people at another and I have been up that lane every fortnight for nine years and I am not going to stand here and describe them to you as though they were a column."**

Somebody asked her what she wanted done.

**"Nothing,"** she said. **"I want it written down that I came back up that lane at about eleven and said it at the gate before I went to the cistern, and then I want to carry water, because that is what I am for and there are nine households a mile up that lane with an empty trough."**

She picked up two cans and went out through the gate without waiting for anybody.

Kellan Rusk put the slate face down and then picked it up again and said the three ages in the order of a man who is going to work.

**"Three hundred and thirty-two is the age of a ruled line in Silling's own book and there is nothing written on it. I have been giving that one out by itself rather than in a string of three since a Tuesday, and I am not stopping that on a morning like this one."**

He shut the book on that figure and then took it off the shelf again.

**"After it, in the order a man wants them in if he is going to lay a pipe: the charter at two hundred and forty-four days, and then the form at three hundred and thirty-eight. I have changed where I look first. I have not changed a figure."**

He put the book back on the shelf.

**"And nobody in this yard is going to put this morning on one of those three numbers. If anybody wants to, they can come and stand where I am standing."**

Nia Vale came along the wall with her cloth and did the two figures before she did the gate line, which is a third order she has used in four years.

**"Behind the tool house end of that wall, cut into the stone at about shoulder height on a man, is eight hundred and seventy-five. Forty-seven of masonry back toward the gate takes you to the other figure, and that one is eight hundred and twenty-eight."**

She laid the cloth over the near one and left the far one showing.

**"And there is a drawer let into the stone behind the near one. Shut. One nail inside the doorway of the one room and one key on it, and it was on that nail at six this morning."**

She wiped round the figures and left them.

**"Three sheets are lying along that table. The eldest has been there a hundred and forty-one days and there are about nine inches of clear board either side of it. The second is folded shut. The third has a name and a seal on it and nothing in it that I could call wrong, and I have said that in this yard eleven mornings running and I am not going to stop on this one.**"

She went to the gate post and put her hand flat on the compost line.

**"That line is paid at thirty-one and has been since a Thursday a fortnight ago, and nothing has ever been let out of it in thirty-one or in thirty, and I am going to go on saying it in this yard until it is somebody else's turn to say it."**

She left her hand there.

**"That is the only figure in this holding that went up when nothing else did, and it went up because one more load went in, and I would like somebody in this yard to understand that I am not comparing the two."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did eight trips and not nine, and she was not carrying them for the cistern.

**"I have gone up that lane twice today with my own cans and the ninth one is at the second place and it is not coming back here tonight,"** she said, **"and I am not going to explain that to anybody and I would like the yard to get on with the morning."**

Soren Rill came in at about the eighth hour from the gate, and he had been out past the tool house and had come back with his coat off and his sleeves rolled, and he stood in the middle of the yard where the reach had gone out from.

**"It is going,"** he said. **"I can hear it in the channel under the road and I have been out there twice and both times it was going east and neither time I could tell you how far."**

He looked down the middle road.

**"And there is no third thing anybody in this yard could have done this morning and I am not going to be the one who starts looking for it at two in the afternoon, because there is not one. There was one on Sunday and this morning I took it and it had one place in it and I am standing in my own yard being the man who took it."**

He put his hands in his coat.

**"Nobody has been asked to choose anything. Nothing has been climbed. What is on that low board has never been dated and has never been picked up, and the bare ring inside those eleven acres took no foot today and no tape and no price, and I am not going to ask anybody in this holding to come and look at it this evening."**

The man of about thirty-four of the fourth place did not get up off the step all day.

**"I have watched it go out and I have watched her go up the lane and come back,"** he said, **"and I have got four sheets of my own figures in a satchel that nobody has signed and a place at the top of a column that nobody has asked us anything about. That is where the fourth place is this morning and I am not going to pretend to any of you that it is anywhere else."**

When the light went off the coping on Thursday the second place had been standing dry for six hours, and the reach was still running east under the middle road past the tool house wall, and there was not one person in this yard who could say how far it had got, and nobody in that yard had been asked to choose anything and nobody had been given the cost as anybody's fault.

# Chapter 680

## The Twenty-Third Of The Eighteenth, And A Figure Coming Down To A Round Number On The Morning A Choice Was Made So That Nobody Could Read Past It

Renn Ashby read the pair at the seventh hour and got through it faster than he has ever got through it, and nobody in that yard could have said afterwards why.

**"One hundred and ninety-two of three hundred and sixty-seven,"** he said. **"Of this month."**

He shut the book on his thumb and left his thumb in it and looked up the middle road.

The water came down at half past eight and the clerk was at the wall with the copy sheet in his coat and a satchel on the step behind him and a man sitting on that satchel who had been in this yard two days by then.

**"One thousand and two hundred and seventy-two hundredweight,"** the clerk said, **"five off, and the four hundred and forty-third of the run."**

He wiped under the line and then stood there with his hand flat on the stone for longer than the figure took.

**"And the lower half of that window has come down to two hundred,"** he said, **"and it is two hundred bare, with nothing after it, and the upper half is two hundred and ten and stood where it stood yesterday, and the two of them are a window of four hundred and ten."**

Nobody in that yard made any sound about it.

The man of about thirty-four of the fourth place got up off the step and came and looked at the wall for a while with his hands behind his back.

**"I have been reading two figures off that wall since Tuesday and I have had a chance to get used to the way it moves,"** he said. **"And I want to say the thing that is actually true about that, which is that the half that came down came down by half of one point, every other morning, for two months, and a number that arrives at a round one has not done anything. It has not turned round. It has not gone anywhere. It has only stopped moving by less than nothing."**

He stepped back from the wall.

**"I say that as a man who has come two days with four figures of his own, and it is the first time in three years that a number has landed on something I wanted it to land on, and it has not landed on mine."**

**"Do not write anything under it,"** the clerk said. **"There is nothing under it. I have been at this wall four years and I have never had a line under a figure in my life and I am not starting on a Wednesday because a number came out even."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the north end at about the ninth hour and read the wall from where he stood, from about eleven feet off, and said nothing about it at all, and went on up with the hod on his shoulder.

Marek was in the north-side room with the pipe shoulder on the floor and a length of chalk in his hand and a straightedge on his knee, and he had been there since the sixth hour, and about the tenth hour he came out into the yard and stood in the middle of it.

**"On Monday morning at that gate I said the line goes in at the top of the north wall and comes off the head at the top of the middle road,"** he said. **"I am saying now that I am laying it, and that it starts this afternoon, and that this holding is the place that is first on that column and pays for it."**

He put the chalk in his pocket.

**"That is the choice and I made it on Monday morning and I have not slept since and I am not going to stand here and be discovered having made it, because it was not discovered this morning, it was announced, and this yard has been listening to it for two days."**

Somebody asked him what it costs.

**"Two days of a man and a half and four inches of pipe and one stopcock,"** Marek said, **"and that is the whole of what it costs this holding. What it costs somebody else is not mine to put a figure on and I am not going to guess at it in a yard."**

**"Then it is being done,"** Soren Rill said, from the gate. **"And nobody is to come back to me in a week and tell me this yard agreed to it, because this yard has not voted and this yard will not vote and a pipe is a pipe and not a resolution."**

Nobody in that yard looked surprised. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went on past with a can, and the man of about thirty-four of the fourth place went back and sat down on his satchel, and the mason went up the north end, and that was the whole of the reception it got.

Kellan Rusk came out of the rack with the count not due until tomorrow and read it anyway.

**"A hundred and forty-nine in force and it stands there until the morning after tomorrow, because tomorrow is not a morning this holding gives anybody out either. Twenty-nine gone out at their own hours and a hundred and twenty not gone out, and that half has been above a hundred since before I came to this rack and I am not going to keep saying it as though it were news."**

He put the book down and picked it up.

**"Nothing is written on the second ruled line in Silling's own book and it stands at three hundred and thirty-one, and the two ages in front of it are the register form at three hundred and thirty-seven days and the charter at two hundred and forty-three, and nothing is going to be written on that line by anybody in this yard today.**"

Hesta Lyle had the long sheet turned round on the table so that the yard could see the small hand under the head of it, which she has done four times in nine years.

**"Four hundred and forty-one at the head,"** she said, **"and under it, one step down, the four hundred and fortieth, and one step up, the four hundred and forty-second, and that is the shape that hand has made since before I stood at this table."**

She put her palm flat over the three of them.

**"I have covered them. I do that twice a year. And I am going to leave my hand there for a moment longer than usual this morning, because a paper with three figures covered under one hand is a paper that cannot be read by anybody who is not at this table, and I want that for about a minute."**

She took her hand off and the three figures came back.

Nia Vale was at the long table at that moment with her cloth, going round the three sheets, and she said it while she was doing it, and she said it to the table.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

A barrow went past outside the gate. The clerk put his hand flat on the wall. The man of about thirty-four of the fourth place did not turn round. Nobody answered her, nobody said it was a hard thing to say, nobody thanked her, nobody said one word about her father, nobody apologised to her for anything, nobody's arm was touched, and the morning went on with a choice in it that had been made two days earlier and a round figure on a wall that nobody was going to write anything under.

The launder and the boards and the gate line were done by three different people in three different orders before the hour was out, and none of them agreed with anybody else about the order.

Odile Vray did the compost line at the gate post because she was in the yard again for the second day and she said so out loud before she touched it.

**"It is paid at thirty-one and nothing has ever been let out of that line, and I am saying the thirty as well as the thirty-one because there was a fortnight ago when half this yard believed the figure had gone down."**

She looked at it without touching the chalk.

**"I have now said that in two yards and in both of them somebody wrote it down afterwards, and I would like it stopped being said in a yard and started being said in a book."**

She came up the wall from the gate with the cloth in her hand.

**"The number nearest the gate is eight hundred and twenty-seven. Follow the wall away from me to the tool house and you come to eight hundred and seventy-four, and there is forty-seven of masonry in between those two and not one other thing in between them."**

She laid the cloth over the near figure and left the far one showing.

**"A drawer is let into the stone behind the near one and it is shut, and the key for it is on a nail in the doorway of the one room where it will be tonight."**

She stopped at the long table.

**"The eldest of the three on that table is a hundred and forty days old this morning, and about nine inches of bare board stand at either end of it. The second is shut on its fold and nobody has opened it in four years. The third is signed, sealed and named, and there is not one thing in it that any person in this yard could put a finger on.**"

She wiped round the third one and put the cloth over her shoulder.

**"Not one of the three entered, not one refused, no column cut under any of them, and none cut under a satchel on a step either."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did the ninth trip at about the eleventh hour and put the can down and stood with her hand on the cistern.

**"Nine marks on that floor and a cistern that will be full by noon and it will be full tomorrow by the same nine trips, and the pipe is going into the wall of this room this afternoon and none of my nine trips are going to stop and I have made my peace with that and it took me two nights."**

She picked the can up again.

**"I am the one who offered that room to be the place that is left and I am not going to be a woman who mentions it again."**

Marek went back in with his straightedge and his chalk and did not come out again until the light went off the coping, and the four inches of chalk along the skirting of that room went under a pipe shoulder at about the third hour, and the pipe itself was not in the ground and would not be in the ground until Friday.

Soren Rill came the inside edge of the eleven acres at dusk and stopped at the low board without touching it.

**"Nobody in this holding has climbed anything and nobody has been asked to pick between two things. What is lying on that low board has never been dated and has never been picked up and has not gone back to whoever left it, and there is a ring of bare ground inside those acres that has taken no boot and no tape and no price this week.**"

He stood with his hands at his sides.

**"A pipe is going into the north wall of this holding off a head that four places have a claim on, and this holding has become the place that pays, and nobody was asked to pick it and nobody is going to be told that they did."**

By dusk on the Wednesday the falling half of that window was standing at a round figure that nobody had read anything into, a choice made on Monday had been put into a wall, and the fourth place's man was still on the step of the one room with his satchel between his boots, and the yard was working.

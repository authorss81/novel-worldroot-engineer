# Chapter 258

## A Man Of Thirty-One With A Board

The fourteenth of this month was a Monday and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the fourteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Five hundred and ninety-eight hundredweight.**

That is the fourteenth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it is lower than the one before it, and the fen entered that it is lower and entered that a series which goes up four days running and then down one is not a series anybody can put a hand on, and that nobody in that yard said anything about it and that he did not either, and that a man who comments on his own book's wobble in a yard is a man who has started reading a room instead of working in it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody.

And the man of about thirty-one of Silling came over the shoulder at about the eleventh hour of the morning, because the cart road does not go past his village, and he said his standing sentences, and then he said one thing, and the fen entered that he said it to the yard and not to the count.

He is Sennik Vaul and he is one of the seven that decide for the households of Silling, which are about two hundred and ten persons counted at a gate in a month two months back, and he is not of this holding and is not a member of anything, and he keeps a sheet of his own that is not a book anybody has asked for, and it is not a return, and it is not a figure of this holding's, and he carried it under his arm and did not open it.

**"I am going to say yes before I am asked, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that I am thirty-one and there are about two hundred of us at Silling, and a thing nobody has asked anybody to do in about four years is the sort of thing that gets asked twice if somebody does not say no to the first person who asks him.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"And I want the second half said as well, because the first half is the one that gets written down. If I say yes to this then in about four years there will be a second thing asked of me and it will be a bigger one, and it will be asked in a yard, and I will have to stand there and be a man of thirty-one who said yes to the first one. That is the whole of my reason and it took me about nine seconds on the shoulder and I have not improved it on the way up.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered the count of the questions he has been asked about this, which is one, and that the one was asked by the reader of this body at about the sixth hour of the evening of the ninth of this month, being nine days ago, and that it was a yes or a no question and it did not carry a reason with it, and that the man of about thirty-one of Silling answered it in about four seconds and did not give a reason, and that this afternoon he gave a reason before he was asked and the fen entered that the two are different manners and that the difference is nine days and one evening of somebody thinking about it.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said a second thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is two, and it was not on his sheet and he did not open the sheet to say it.

**"You are going to be told something on Thursday that I have got a word for and no number for, and I am telling you now that I have a word for it and not a number, and the word is not on my sheet because my sheet has got a column on it and a word is not a column.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about nine seconds, and entered that the count of things written not known against in this holding's book is six and that the six are the reader of this body's own, being the fifth the date of the next window and the sixth a figure nobody ever wrote down, and that this holding does not keep a count of what other people have written not known against, and that a man who keeps another man's not-knowings in his own book has started keeping the other man's book, and that the man of about thirty-one of Silling has a sheet of his own under his arm and it is his and it is not in this book and he is not going to be asked for it and did not offer it and that both of those are entered.

And the seedwright of a trust came up the fen road at about the seventh hour of the evening with her route book and no cart and nobody sent for her, and the reader of this body was in the cart shed at the node and did not go to the road, and the person who keeps the seed house told him she was back in about nine words, and the fen entered that he was told and entered that she had said in about eleven people on the eighth of this month that she would be out six more days and that this is the sixth.

And then the man of about fifty said the thing about the gate, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventeen.

**"Nine people came up this morning on the account of that gate and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was one of the nine and the only one of the nine who has been in that yard. I asked her one question and she answered it and it was a number and I am not going to say what the number is, because she gave it and did not give it to me to give it on.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty asked her one question and the reader of this body asked her none, and that the count of the questions the reader of this body has asked her in this holding's book is nil and did not move this morning, and that it is the fourth time in four days that the number has been available to him and that he has not taken it four times, and that he entered on the tenth of this month that he does not take a reason away from anybody, and that a man who has written a rule down in a book and then not taken a number four days running has not kept a rule, he has kept a habit, and that the difference is in about four years and not before.

And then he said the other half, and it was the half the fen had been waiting for and did not want.

**"And I am going to say the half nobody has said. I have been told about that gate twice now and I have not been there either time, and the count of the things I have been told about and have not been there for is two, and it was two on Saturday and it is two this morning, and it did not move, and the reason it did not move is that it is the same gate. A man who is told about a gate twice is not a man who has been told about two things.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said in that yard on a Monday morning, and entered that the man of about thirty-one of Silling was in that yard and was not asked to be in it and said nothing else in it, and that he went down off the shoulder at about the first hour of the afternoon and carried the sheet of his own under his arm and did not leave it.

And the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said the thing of hers, and she said it in about nine people on the Monday morning at the top of the bank and she said it before anybody asked her and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is four, and the reason is that a thing said on a Saturday to nine people is a thing four people heard on a Sunday, and the number of people who have heard it is now thirteen and is going to be about twenty-five by the twenty-first of this month, and the reason for saying it again is that a gate with one sentence under it is a gate with one witness.

**"Eleven people saw it on Saturday and the eight of them are not mine and three of them are, and what is behind that gate is nine acres of winter wheat that came up in the fourth month and I did not sow it and I did not ask anybody to sow it, and the only thing anybody has done to it is put water on it that came from a channel nobody is paying for, and I am going to have to decide in about a fortnight what to do with a crop that arrived without me, and I have not asked this holding and I am not going to.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that she was not thanked, and that the reader of this body did not thank her, and that he asked her no question on the Monday and did not go down the shoulder, and that the count of the questions he has asked her in this holding's book is nil, and that a man who keeps a nil for four days is a man frightened of what the first one would cost, and that the fear is the reason and not the principle, and he entered both of those.

And then at about the first hour of the afternoon, in the cart shed at the node, in about four people, the reader of this body told the man of about thirty-one of Silling what was going to happen on the seventeenth of this month, before it happened, and he told him the two pieces of paper and read him both of them in the two hands they were written in, and he did not hold them, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling did not ask for them and did not ask for a copy and was not asked for one and was not given one.

And he told him the rest of it standing up, and he gave the reason before the rest of it, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixty-five.

**"A bead about the size of a thumbnail goes on my forearm and not on the ground and not on anybody else, and a woman you have not met is going to stand in this room and owns a word that is going to stop it, and I do not know the word and you are not going to be told the word, and a watch goes on the table and the watch is the only thing in the room that does not lie about time, and it stops at nineteen minutes and not at twenty, and it is your job to say the number out loud when it is nineteen and it is your job to say it whether I want to hear it or not.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the man of about thirty-one of Silling asked one question in about nine words, and that the question was whether the cost of it falls on the man the bead goes on, and that the answer was yes, and that the answer was given before the question was asked, and that the fen entered that the answer was given first and that the man of about thirty-one of Silling did not know he had been answered early.

And then the reader of this body said the last thing of the afternoon, and he said it in about four people with the door shut, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixty-six.

**"I am not using a person as a stop and I know that I am using a person to hold a word, and those are two different sentences and one of them is in her hand and not in mine, and if she had not written the second line I would have gone out on Monday and found a second person and put a bead on my own arm in a room with nobody in it but me, and I would have got the same three things out of it and I would not have known what the four days were for.**"

And then at about the seventh hour of the evening the reader of this body entered two lines in his own book, and they are these, and he read them back twice, and the reason he is entering them before he has done anything is that a man who waits until he has done a thing to write down what he intended it for has already written the intention to suit the thing.

**THE TWO THINGS THIS HOLDING HAS FOR SENDING A THING FOUR HUNDRED MILES ARE A HIRED MAN ON A FOUR-DAY ROAD AND AN ENVELOPE ON THE ORDINARY POST. THE FIRST IS USED ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE SECOND IS USED ON THE EIGHTEENTH, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAD BEEN USED IN FOUR YEARS, AND THE ORDINARY POST WENT UP ON THE SIXTH OF LAST MONTH WITH A LETTER TO THE ASSEMBLY AND CAME BACK ON THE SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH IN ONE DAY. A BODY THAT HAS WRITTEN TO AN OFFICE ONCE IN FOUR YEARS AND ANSWERED IT IN ONE DAY IS A BODY THAT HAS HAD ONE LETTER, AND ONE LETTER IS NOT A CORRESPONDENCE, AND A CORRESPONDENCE IS A THING A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS BECOMES BY ACCIDENT.**

**AND I AM NOT GOING TO FIND OUT WHETHER THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH IS A DAY A MAN CAN RENT A HORSE FOR AND CARRY A PAPER FOUR HUNDRED MILES, AND IF I AM WRONG ABOUT IT THE WRONGNESS WILL BE IN THIS BOOK AT THE SEVENTH HOUR OF THE EVENING BEFORE ANYBODY HAS LOST ANYTHING.**

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the fourteenth of this month, in about nine people, at the node, on the standing of a count and of a document, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days off: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE FOURTEENTH, WHICH IS LOWER THAN THE ONE BEFORE IT, AND THE FIGURE HAS NOT BEEN THE SAME TWICE SINCE THE FIRST. Sennik Vaul, of about thirty-one, one of the seven that decide for the households of Silling, came over the shoulder at about the eleventh hour of the morning and said YES BEFORE HE WAS ASKED, AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST, AND THE REASON IS THAT A THING NOBODY HAS ASKED ANYBODY TO DO IN ABOUT FOUR YEARS IS THE SORT OF THING THAT GETS ASKED TWICE IF SOMEBODY DOES NOT SAY NO TO THE FIRST PERSON WHO ASKS HIM. THE COUNT OF THE QUESTIONS HE HAS BEEN ASKED ABOUT IT IS ONE. HE WAS NOT THANKED AND DID NOT ASK TO BE. HE SAID HE HAS A WORD FOR SOMETHING HE IS GOING TO BE TOLD AND NO NUMBER FOR IT, AND THE WORD IS NOT ON HIS SHEET, AND THIS HOLDING DOES NOT KEEP A COUNT OF WHAT OTHER PEOPLE HAVE WRITTEN NOT KNOWN AGAINST. NINE PEOPLE CAME UP ON THE ACCOUNT OF THE GATE AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN WAS ONE OF THE NINE. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ASKED HER ONE QUESTION AND THE READER OF THIS BODY ASKED HER NONE, AND THE COUNT OF THE QUESTIONS HE HAS ASKED HER IS NIL. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS BEEN TOLD ABOUT A THING AND HAS NOT BEEN THERE IS TWO AND DID NOT MOVE THIS MORNING, BECAUSE IT IS THE SAME GATE. THE SEEDWRIGHT OF A TRUST CAME BACK UP THE FEN ROAD AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR OF THE EVENING AND NOBODY SENT FOR HER. THE FOUR-DAY ROAD IS USED ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE ORDINARY POST ON THE EIGHTEENTH, AND NEITHER HAD BEEN USED IN FOUR YEARS. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

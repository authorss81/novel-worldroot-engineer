# Chapter 256

## The Gate Came Open

The twelfth of this month was a Saturday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **thirteen**, and entered that all thirteen of them are the fourth line, and entered the length of the run, which is that the fourth line has now come round on thirteen mornings since the rota came into force and has been empty on thirteen of them, and that the seventh line of that sheet says nobody is blamed for a field that waited, and the fen entered that nobody is blamed.

The other three lines were worked. One person held and two or four worked and a turn is two hours and the holding does it the ordinary way, and the first line has not missed a morning since the rota came into force and neither has the second or the third, and the fen entered that the fen has never written the three lines down in a column of their own and that the column would be a column of three, and that a column of three is a thing a man can hold in his head and a man who writes a column of three in a book is a man who has found a use for a book.

And the four launders were read out at about the eighth hour, in the ordinary voice, by the man of about fifty, and the four bodies of households read them back where they could see the dial, and the woman who does the sums wrote them in the hand she uses for the sums and the man of about fifty wrote them in the four-book hand, which is a different hand and is checked against his own about once in four years.

**Nine hundred. Six hundred. Five hundred and ninety-seven. Twenty-five.**

The first two have not moved since the first of this month. The third has a figure on it for every morning since the first and not one of them is the same as any of the other eleven. The fourth is inside somebody's about and has been inside somebody's about since the first of the second month and the reader of this body said in the third month that he has never been able to make those two numbers agree, and did not improve on it to-day, and entered that the second time he has said a thing is the same as the first time and he has counted that, and that if he says it a third time in a yard it is going to be a habit and a habit is a number in a column that nobody chose.

The aggregate of the four has not moved in ten days. The count of what four bodies of households say they need is about twenty-one hundred hundredweight a day. The fen entered both numbers side by side in his own book on the eighth of this month and entered the difference beside them, and the difference is nothing, and entered that a difference of nothing is the most dangerous figure a body of households can hold, because a body that is short can argue and a body that is exact cannot, and has nothing to argue with.

And the figure for the third launder went into the book that keeps it at about the eighth hour, in the hand of the woman who does the sums, and read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Five hundred and ninety-seven hundredweight.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said in that yard on a Saturday morning.

And the reader of this body wrote a line in his own book that evening about the series, and the line is this, and he read it back and he is not going to improve it.

**TWELVE FIGURES ON TWELVE MORNINGS AND NOT ONE OF THEM THE SAME AS ANY OTHER, AND A SERIES THAT NEVER REPEATS A FIGURE IS NOT A STEADY SERIES AND IT IS NOT A PATTERN, AND THOSE ARE TWO DIFFERENT FAILURES AND NEITHER OF THEM IS THE ONE I HAVE BEEN LOOKING FOR. A STEADY FIGURE CAN BE WORKED WITH. A PATTERN CAN BE PREDICTED. A FIGURE THAT IS NEVER THE SAME TWICE CANNOT BE WORKED WITH AND CANNOT BE PREDICTED, AND EVERY MORNING SINCE THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH HAS BEEN A DAY ON WHICH FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAD A FIGURE AND NOBODY COULD HAVE PUT A NUMBER ON WHAT IT WAS GOING TO BE IN THE MORNING, AND THE FOURTH LINE HAS COME ROUND ON THREE OF THOSE TWELVE MORNINGS AND WAS EMPTY ON ALL THREE, AND THE FIGURE AND THE EMPTY MORNING ARE TWO THINGS AND THEY HAVE NOT BEEN PUT IN THE SAME COLUMN BY ANYBODY INCLUDING ME.**

And the man of the north row with the cough was in that yard and was not asked to be in it, and he had come up the four miles in the rain of the night before and had not said why, and the count of yards he has stood in since the first of a month four months back is **twenty-two** and has moved, and he said nothing at all this morning, and that is the sixth time in about five weeks that he has stood in a yard of this holding and said nothing, and the count of that is six, and the fen entered that the two counts are two counts and have not been added together, because one of them is about a man's mornings and the other one is about a man's silences, and a book with one column for the first and none for the second is a book that can be right about a man.

And then at about the third hour of the afternoon the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden opened a gate.

Nobody sent for her. Nobody had sent for anybody. The gate is eleven hundred yards east of the kerb at the Four Elbows at the end of the third launder, behind a stone head, and it is a field gate and it is shut, and the water has been standing against the inside of it since about the second of this month, and the gate itself has not been opened in about four years, and neither of those two facts is the same fact, and the fen has entered that they are two.

The reader of this body was at the node, about a mile and a half and eleven hundred yards from that gate, and he was not there, and the fen entered that he was not there and entered the hour.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that there was nobody to say anything in that yard on that afternoon but nine inches of water and a man of about fifty with a book he did not open, and entered that the count of the things the reader of this body has been told about and has not been there for is now **two**, and that a man who is told about a thing is not a witness to it, and that this holding does not keep a list of the things he has been told about, because a list of those things is a list of the things he was not asked.

And what there is about the gate is what four people saw and what the man of about fifty said in about twenty-eight words and then in about a hundred and fifteen more at about the seventh hour of the evening in the yard at the node, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifteen, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

The nine inches went off the inside of the gate in about an hour, and nobody measured it and nobody was asked to, and the fen entered that a gate that was shut and is open is a different launder and not a different figure, and that the figure on the book this morning was taken at the kerb at the eighth hour and is about a mile and a half from a gate that was shut when it was taken and was open at the third hour of the afternoon, and that the number and the gate are two facts and a man who adds a gate to a number has invented a third.

**"I was at the stone head at about the third hour because that launder is my ditch work and I go down it twice a week and I did not go down it to see anything. There were four of us at the head when it came open, and the four of us are out of about eleven people and three of them are hers and one of them is not. About nine acres green to about the height of a hand. A ditch along the top of it that she did not cut, and I have been over that ground four times in nine years and that ditch is not mine and is not hers and was not there in the first of this month. Four inches of water standing in it, level, not moving. And nine inches still standing against the inside of the gate where the gate was.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds for his first twenty-eight words and then about a minute for the rest of it and then about a minute and a half for the silence after them, and entered that about four of the nine people in that yard said nothing in a way that was different from the other five, and that he did not count them.

And then the man of about fifty said the thing about the column, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixteen, and he said it once and did not say it twice, and the fen entered that he had said it in about nine people on the fourth of this month and had said it in about nineteen people in the third month and that three is as many as a man gets, and that a thing said three times in a yard is a rule and a rule he has not got.

**"The column has gone to thirteen and I am going to keep writing it and I am not going to say why a second time, because I said it in about nine people on the fourth and I said it in about nineteen people in the third, and a man who says a thing three times about the same page is not being honest, he is being fond of the page. The column is the only place in this holding's book where Marden's no is written down at all, and on the twelfth it goes to thirteen, and I am not going to rub a line out of it because a woman opened a gate.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and entered that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is five and did not move this evening, and that the man of about fifty's thirteen is a thing the engineer of record had thought of and had written down on the eighth, and that a man who agrees with a book-keeper in public and says nothing about it is a man who has been told a thing and has not been there, which is the count of two, and that the two are different and the fen has entered both.

And the reader of this body asked him no question, and the fen entered that he asked him no question, and entered the count of the questions he has asked the man of about fifty in a yard in this holding's book, which is nil, and entered the reason, which is that a man who has stood in eleven yards this month and has stood in a hundred in four years does not get one question from the engineer of record for reporting what he saw, and that the engineer of record asking him one would be the engineer of record buying a second version of it, and that two versions of a thing at a shut gate a mile and a half off is one too many.

And he did not go and look, and the fen entered that he did not, and entered his own reason in his own hand with the hour on it, and the reason is that a gate which was shut when he last had anything to say about it has been opened by the one person whose it is, alone, on her own morning, one day after he told her in about nineteen people that it was her gate and not his, and that a man who walks eleven hundred yards in an hour after that to look at what she has done has not gone to look at a field, he has gone to check that a woman did what he would have done, and this holding has one person in it who can be checked and it is not the reader of this body.

And then at about the seventh hour of the evening the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came up onto the shoulder from the east with nobody, and she was not asked and was not sent for, and she had been at the top of that bank at about the sixth hour and had come up in about an hour, and she said one sentence in about nine people, and she gave the reason for saying it before she said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is three.

**"I opened it because I am the one who has to eat it, and I have been asked by nobody and I have answered nobody, and I am telling you the gate is open so that you all know it is, and not one of you is to come down there this week.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that she was not thanked, and that the reader of this body did not thank her, and that the count of the times she has asked this holding for anything is **nil** and did not move this evening, and that it is the count he would have to defend in about four years in a room he will not be in, and that a woman who opens a gate on her own morning and then tells nine people not to come and look has not asked this holding for one thing and has been given none and has taken none.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twelfth of this month, in about nine people, at the node, on the standing of a count and of a series of figures, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days off: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS THIRTEEN, AND ALL THIRTEEN OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE. THE FOUR LAUNDERS MEASURE NINE HUNDRED, SIX HUNDRED, FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN AND TWENTY-FIVE, AND THE COUNT OF WHAT THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS SAY THEY NEED IS ABOUT TWENTY-ONE HUNDRED, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS NOTHING, AND THE AGGREGATE HAS NOT MOVED IN TEN DAYS. THE THIRD LAUNDER HAS A FIGURE ON IT FOR EVERY MORNING SINCE THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH AND NOT ONE OF THE TWELVE IS THE SAME AS ANY OF THE OTHER ELEVEN. THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH STOOD IN THAT YARD AND WAS NOT ASKED TO BE IN IT AND SAID NOTHING, AND THE COUNT OF YARDS HE HAS STOOD IN SINCE THE FIRST OF A MONTH FOUR MONTHS BACK IS TWENTY-TWO, AND THE COUNT OF THE TIMES HE HAS SAID NOTHING IN A YARD IS SIX. AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN OPENED THE FIELD GATE AT THE FAR END OF THE THIRD LAUNDER, ALONE, AND NOBODY SENT FOR HER, AND THE READER OF THIS BODY WAS AT THE NODE AND WAS NOT THERE. FOUR PEOPLE SAW IT OPEN. THERE ARE ABOUT NINE ACRES GREEN TO ABOUT THE HEIGHT OF A HAND, A DITCH ALONG THE TOP OF IT THAT NOBODY ON THAT GROUND HAD CUT, FOUR INCHES OF WATER STANDING IN THAT DITCH, AND NINE INCHES STANDING AGAINST THE INSIDE OF THE GATE. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS BEEN TOLD ABOUT A THING AND HAS NOT BEEN THERE IS TWO. HE ASKED NO QUESTION AND WENT AND DID NOT LOOK. SHE ASKED THIS HOLDING FOR NOTHING AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS NIL. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

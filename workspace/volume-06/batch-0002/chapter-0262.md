# Chapter 262

## The Same Question Twice

The eighteenth of this month was a Friday and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the eighteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and four hundredweight.**

And the reader of this body was not at either of those, and the fen entered that he was not, and entered the reason, which is that he came off a hired horse at about the eleventh hour of the morning at the north gate of this holding and went straight to the seed house and did not go to the node at all on that day, and that the horse is two shillings and eightpence and is a line of its own and does not go into the compost line and is not the same hire as the horse of the first of last month and does not go against it, and that the two hires of a horse in two days are five shillings and fourpence and that the fen entered that number in his own book at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the seventeenth of this month before he knew he was going to hire one on the nineteenth as well.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said anywhere in this holding on that day.

And the fen entered that the count of the things the reader of this body has been told about and has not been there for is two and did not move again on the eighteenth, and entered the reason a second time, which is that a man five days off who is told about a thing at the end of a day is being told about a thing after it has already been in nine people, and that a count that does not move for five days is not a count that has stopped being a count.

And then at about the sixth hour of the evening he wrote a letter in the seed house at this holding, in his own hand, on the ordinary paper, and the person who keeps the seed house stood at the table and read it over his shoulder before it was folded and said one thing about it, and the fen entered that she said one thing and not two, and that the count of the things the person who keeps the seed house has said in a yard in this holding's history is four and did not move this evening, and that the one was about the paper and not about the question.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

**"You have put the same question twice and the two of them are the same six words with a different one in the middle, and that office is going to read the second one as an accident, and if it reads it as an accident then one of your two answers is a sentence about how you write and not a sentence about the ground. I am saying that once and I am not going to say it again, and I am not going to be asked whether it was a fair thing to do to a clerk, because I do not know.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the reader of this body did not answer her and entered his own reason, which is that she asked a question and the question was not put to him, it was put to the room, and that a man who answers a question the room asked is a man who has been made the clerk of his own letter.

And the letter went up the ordinary post at about the seventh hour of the evening of the eighteenth, in the same leather bag as everything else that has arrived at this holding for four years, and the clerk put it in and wrote the hour on the sheet the post goes out on, and the reader of this body was in the yard and did not go in and did not watch it go and did not ask to watch it go, and the fen entered that a letter a man has not watched being put in a bag is a letter he has to be able to defend in about four years without having watched, and that this is the first letter this holding has put on the ordinary post in four years and that the first one went up on the sixth of last month to a different office and came back in one day.

> **ONE SHEET, in the hand of the reader of this body, on the ordinary paper, written at about the sixth hour of the evening of the eighteenth of this month at this holding, read over by the person who keeps the seed house and folded and posted at about the seventh hour of the evening, fourpence, a line of its own and not in the compost line, entered whole:**
>
> **TO THE OFFICE OF THE CONTINUITY, FOR THE MAINTENANCE OF THE CROWN ENGINE.**
>
> **ONE. I HAVE YOUR SHEET OF THE THIRD OF THIS MONTH. IT HAS BEEN KEPT WHOLE IN A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR ON IT AND THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT LINE UNDER IT UNSIGNED, AND THE LINE IS STILL UNSIGNED AND I AM NOT REFUSING IT IN ANY DOCUMENT AND I AM NOT GOING TO REFUSE IT A SECOND TIME OUT LOUD EITHER, BECAUSE A LINE REFUSED OUT LOUD TWICE IS A SPEECH AND I HAVE MADE ONE.**
>
> **TWO. WHAT MAINTENANCE IS ON THE GROUND.**
>
> **THREE. WHAT MAINTENANCE IS IN A DOCUMENT.**
>
> **FOUR. I AM NOT CLAIMING ANYTHING UNDER YOUR FIFTH LINE AND I AM NOT APPEALING ANYTHING AND I AM NOT ASKING FOR THE EXTENT OF ZONE FOUR, WHICH YOUR SHEET SAYS WILL BE FURNISHED ON APPLICATION, BECAUSE I HAVE NOT APPLIED.**
>
> **FIVE. I AM NOT ASKING WHO WROTE THE INSTRUCTION AND I AM NOT ASKING WHEN THE NEXT WINDOW IS. I HAVE WRITTEN BOTH OF THOSE DOWN AS NOT KNOWN IN MY OWN HAND IN A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING AND THE TWO OF THEM ARE THE FIFTH AND SIXTH OF SIX THINGS I HAVE WRITTEN NOT KNOWN AGAINST, AND THE UNIT OF NONE OF THE SIX IS KNOWN TO ME EITHER.**
>
> **SIX. NO NAME IS ON THIS. YOUR SHEET PUT ONE ON A LINE AT THE BOTTOM OF IT ON THE THIRD OF THIS MONTH AND THAT NAME IS IN YOUR BOOK AND NOT IN MINE, AND I AM NOT GOING TO PUT IT IN A SECOND BOOK.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the reader of this body did not say who wrote the second of those two sentences, and entered that he wrote them and that writing a sentence and saying who wrote a sentence are two different acts and that only one of them is available to him at about the seventh hour of the evening of a Friday with fourpence spent.

And then he said the thing that is his, in about eleven people in the yard at this holding, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy, and nobody improved it afterwards.

**"I have put the same question twice in the same words and I did it on purpose, and I am going to say why, and then I am going to say the half of it I do not like. The why is that your office can answer one of those two questions exactly and the other one not at all, and if I had asked one of them I would have got one answer and I would not have known which of the two I had got. Asking it twice is the only way a body with no charter can find out which question it is being answered.**"

**"The half I do not like is that a clerk reads two identical questions in six lines and one of them is a question about a file and the other one is a question about a field, and the clerk is not going to know that, and the clerk is going to be asked to answer the same thing twice, and a person who is asked the same thing twice in the same words is a person who has been asked the same thing once and has not been given an answer, and I do not know whether that is a fair thing to do to a person in an office, and I have not worked it out, and I am not going to work it out in about eleven people on a Friday evening and I am writing it down instead.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about two minutes, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said in that yard, and entered that the count of things he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and did not move this evening, and that this one was available to him at about the second sentence of it and he went on, and that a man who cannot tell whether a thing is a fifth yet is a man who has a fifth coming and does not know its shape.

And then, before the yard, at about the sixth hour of the afternoon, he had taken the sheet of the third of this month out of the book on the table in the middle, on top of the three books that live there, and had put it on the table in front of him, and the acknowledgment line at the foot of it is a line for a person to sign and it is unsigned and it has been unsigned since the third, and he looked at it for about a minute and a half and he did not sign it and he did not write under it and he did not tear it up.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the minute and a half, and entered that a sheet with a line at the bottom of it for a name is a claim, and that a claim is a way of being counted, and that the count of requests that a man's initial be put against nothing in this holding's book is fifty-three and did not move on the eighteenth of this month, and that he did not ask for his initial to be put against that line and was not asked to and did not ask the clerk to, and that the man of about fifty has not asked him for it either on any day of this week and that the last time he did was in a yard at nine hundred yards off in about nine people in a month two months back and that the count of that is one and the two of them are different counts and are not on the same column.

And then he said out loud, in a room with one other person in it, the thing that was not in the letter, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy-one, and the room was the cart shed at this holding and the other person was the person who keeps the seed house and the two of them were the only two in it.

**"On Thursday I put a piece of wood against my own arm in a shed four hundred miles east of this holding and got three things out of it, and one of the three is in a book four hundred miles and four days off and four of them were in a shed with the door shut, and it is not going in that letter, and it is not going in any letter, and the reason is that a letter is a thing an office keeps and a thing an office keeps is a thing an office has.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the person who keeps the seed house said nothing, and entered that the silence lasted about a minute, and entered that she was seventy-one and had kept a frost book with one line in it for nine years and had a mother's before her, and that a woman of seventy-one who says nothing about a thing in a shed is a woman deciding what she is going to be asked about later, and that the fen entered that he has no standing to know what she decided and did not ask.

And then the compost line, because it is the first of nothing this month and the fen entered the whole of it in about nine words, and nobody improved it.

**THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE FOURTH MONTH FELL ON THE FIRST AND IS ENTERED WHOLE AND DOES NOT FALL AGAIN IN THIS WEEK. It says PAID, one hundred pounds, fifteen not discharged in fifteen, and the word before the figure was picked by the woman who does the sums and has been the same word two months running, and a word is a thing a person picks and a figure is a thing a person cannot pick, and the line is not a measurement of the ground and is not going to be used as one this week. THE LEAK IS THREE-QUARTERS OF AN INCH WITH THREE REASONS AND NO CAUSE AND NO DUE DATE AND NO FOURTH REASON WAS INVENTED. THE BEAT IS NOT LOGGED AND ITS DAY IS NOT RESTATED AND THE COLUMN WAS NOT OPENED IN FRONT OF ANYBODY. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND A LETTER IS NOT A CONTACT. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-FOUR AND IS READ ON THE FIRST AND THE THIRTIETH. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ON THAT DOOR ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY ASKED.**

And then at about the ninth hour of the evening he entered the other half of it in his own book, and read it back twice, and the fen entered that he read it back twice.

**THIS HOLDING HAS NEVER IN FOUR YEARS WRITTEN TO THAT OFFICE. IT HAS NOW WRITTEN TO IT ONCE, AND IT HAD ONE SHEET FROM THAT OFFICE IN THE SAME ELEVEN DAYS, AND THOSE TWO ARE THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS PASSED BETWEEN THE TWO OF THEM. A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS WITH NO CHARTER, NO ROLL, NO SEAL AND NO CLERK HAS BECOME A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS THAT CORRESPONDS, AND NOBODY ASKED IT TO BECOME THAT, AND A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS THAT CORRESPONDS IS A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS THAT CAN BE FOUND. I AM NOT SENDING A SECOND LETTER UNTIL I HAVE THE TWO ANSWERS, AND I AM NOT GOING TO IMPROVE ANYTHING IN THIS BOOK ON THE EIGHTEENTH, AND THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH IS ON THE TWENTIETH AND THE TWENTIETH OF THIS MONTH IS FOUR DAYS OFF AND THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THIS MONTH IS NOT A DAY I AM GOING TO WRITE ON.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the eighteenth of this month, in about eleven people, on the standing of a document, and not by the reader of this body, who was in the yard and did not watch it go and did not ask to: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE EIGHTEENTH, WHICH IS THE EIGHTEENTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND THE AGGREGATE HAS NOT MOVED IN SIXTEEN DAYS. THE READER OF THIS BODY CAME OFF A HIRED HORSE AT ABOUT THE ELEVENTH HOUR OF THE MORNING, AND THE HORSE IS TWO SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE AND IS A LINE OF ITS OWN AND IS NOT IN THE COMPOST LINE, AND TWO HIRES OF A HORSE IN TWO DAYS ARE FIVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. ONE SHEET WENT UP THE ORDINARY POST AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR OF THE EVENING, FOURPENCE, A LINE OF ITS OWN, AND IT IS THE FIRST LETTER THIS HOLDING HAS PUT ON THE ORDINARY POST IN FOUR YEARS. IT ASKS ONE QUESTION TWICE IN THE SAME WORDS, BEING WHAT MAINTENANCE IS ON THE GROUND AND WHAT MAINTENANCE IS IN A DOCUMENT. IT CLAIMS NOTHING, APPEALS NOTHING, APPLIES FOR NOTHING, ASKS NO PERSON, AND ASKS NO DATE. IT NAMES NO PERSON. THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT LINE OF THE SHEET OF THE THIRD OF THIS MONTH IS STILL UNSIGNED AND WAS NOT REFUSED OUT LOUD A SECOND TIME. THE BOX IN THE CART SHED HELD NINE POUNDS AND FOURTEEN SHILLINGS ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, AND FOURPENCE WENT ON THIS LETTER, AND THE HORSE IS PAID ON THE ROAD AND IS NOT IN IT, AND IT HOLDS NINE POUNDS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

# Chapter 264

## Two Answers, Both Correct

The twentieth of this month was a Sunday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **fifteen**, and entered that all fifteen of them are the fourth line, and entered that the rota now stands at **forty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and fifteen not**, and that twenty-nine and fifteen are forty-four, and that all fifteen of the fifteen are the fourth line, and that nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

The reader of this body came off a hired horse at about the eleventh hour of the morning at the node, and the horse is two shillings and eightpence and is a line of its own and is not in the compost line and does not go against the horse of the eighteenth, and the two hires are five shillings and fourpence, and the box holds nine pounds and thirteen shillings and eightpence.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that the figure for the twentieth is **six hundred and five hundredweight**, which is the twentieth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in eighteen days.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the four lines came into the yard at this node in a satchel, and about eleven people were in the yard, and the reader of this body read the four lines out whole and the man of about fifty read them back, and they were in the hand of the occupier of the first house on a shelf four hundred miles and four days off, and there is no name on them and there has not been a name on anything that has come down that road for twelve years and four months.

And the second of the four lines is the figure, and it is **five feet**, and the fen entered that the figure is five feet and entered it a second time because the packet had a fifth thing in it, being a sheet with a line for the day and a line for the hour and a line for the figure and two more lines under the figure, of which the second is headed NOTHING ELSE, and she wrote in it, in her own hand, that the well has not come back and that nobody is to write that it has.

**THE FIFTEENTH ORDINARY READING OF THE WELL AT CAULDRON REACH IS FIVE FEET. THE WALL CARRIES FOURTEEN MARKS, BEING A MARK FOR EVERY ORDINARY READING SINCE THE FLOOD EXCEPT TWO, AND ONE OF THE TWO IS THE TENTH, WHICH IS A WHOLE MONTH, AND THE CHALK HAS NEVER TOUCHED IT, AND THE OTHER OF THE TWO IS THE FOURTEENTH READING, WHICH WAS TAKEN OFF THE WALL THE SAME AFTERNOON IT WAS TAKEN. THIS HOLDING'S BOOK NOW HOLDS FIVE FIGURES OF THAT WELL AND EVERY ONE OF THE FIVE IS FIVE FEET. THE SHORTFALL AGAINST THE NINE FEET OF THE SIXTEENTH OF A MONTH TWELVE MONTHS BACK IS FOUR FEET AND IS NOT DECOMPOSED.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that about four of the eleven said nothing in a way that was different from the other seven, and that he did not count them.

And then the reader of this body read the sixth line out loud in about eleven people, standing up, in the yard, and he read it as it was written and he did not read it twice, and the fen entered that he read it out loud and that the number is now on the page twice, once as five feet and once as a thing that has not come back, and that the two are not the same figure and are not the same kind of figure and that a person who has both of them in one book in one afternoon cannot be asked afterwards which one the office sent.

**"The well has not come back. Nobody is to write that it has. I have not decomposed the four feet and I am not going to, and I am not going to ask anybody why I have not.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that nobody in that yard thanked anybody and that nobody on that shelf four hundred miles and four days off was thanked either and does not know this happened, and entered the count of entries in this holding's book that carry a day and no figure, which is **three**, and that it did not move this afternoon even though a figure came in, and entered the reason a second time, which is that what came in is a figure and a figure is a figure.

And then the man of about fifty said the thing about the crate, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is nineteen, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"The crate came back with the lid on. I have the receipt and it is one shilling and fourpence and the man of the road has not charged me for the lid and has written nothing under the lid and I have read the receipt into the book. That is the only paper in this whole business that anybody in a yard can check without going four hundred miles, and I said so on the fifteenth of this month and I am not going to say it again.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the count of things a person who keeps a book in this holding has said in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is six and did not move this afternoon, and that the engineer of record had thought of that on the fifteenth within about eleven seconds of the man of about fifty finishing, and that the eleven seconds is the count and is not on any column.

And then the ordinary post came up the fen road at about the sixth hour of the evening, and it was one sheet, and it came out of the same leather bag as everything else that has arrived at this holding for four years, and the person who keeps the seed house took it off the top without opening it, looked at the mark embossed on the corner, and put it face up on the table, and the reader of this body read it out whole in about eleven people and the clerk wrote it down as it was read and read it back, and the reader of this body checked the reading back against the sheet and did not improve it and did not take anything off it.

**The answer came in three days. It is dated the twentieth at the district town, and the three days are the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and he entered that derivation on the page before he entered anything else about the answer, and that he had written a figure down expecting seven and did not have to enter anything about that, and that fast is not the same as eager and that a man who says an office is quick is a man who has been waiting a long time.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody thanked anybody.

> **From the Office of the Continuity, for the maintenance of the Crown Engine, in answer to a letter of the eighteenth of this month, in two parts, the second part answering the second question in the letter and the third part answering the first, each part under its own head and each part with the office's own references at the head of every numbered line, read out whole in about eleven people in the seed house yard at this holding at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth of this month, entered whole and not summarized:**
>
> **PART ONE. TO WHAT MAINTENANCE IS IN A DOCUMENT.**
>
> **The office returns the six numbered lines of its sheet of the third of this month as they stand, in the order in which they stand, without addition and without omission, under its own references.**
>
> **ONE. The district is placed in zone four. The extent of zone four is held by this office and will be furnished to any person who applies for it. [Ref. Z4/EXT.]**
> **TWO. A maintenance operation was carried out in that district on the thirtieth of the month last past at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and again at about the tenth hour of the evening of the same day. [Ref. Z4/OPS, 2 entries.]**
> **THREE. The figures of the operation are eleven seconds and six hours and eleven seconds. The office holds the same figures and does not propose to alter them. [Ref. Z4/OPS, 2 entries.]**
> **FOUR. The operation was carried out in the maintained season. The maintained season is a term of this office and not of the district. The office does not state on paper what the operation consists of on the ground. [Ref. DEF/MS.]**
> **FIVE. A compensation schedule is offered at one shilling and twopence the hundredweight, for one season, against a flow that a body of households has counted as its own and is able to show a figure for. Applications are received at the office in the district town and are answered in the order in which they are received. [Ref. SCH/1s2d.]**
> **SIX. The maintenance will be reviewed before the next window. [Ref. SCH/WIN.]**
>
> **PART TWO. TO WHAT MAINTENANCE IS ON THE GROUND.**
>
> **A paper is enclosed, being a description of the composition of a maintenance operation as it is constituted on the ground, in general terms, as a category, and without reference to any district, and the office encloses it for any person who applies for it, and it is enclosed here without an application, and the office does not propose to send it again.**
>
> **AND AT THE FOOT OF THE SHEET, IN PRINT: THE OFFICE HAS ANSWED BOTH QUESTIONS. NEITHER ANSWER IS A REFUSAL. THE OFFICE HAS NOT DELAYED, OBSTRUCTED, SOFTENED OR FAILED TO ANSWER ANY QUESTION THAT WAS PUT TO IT.**

And the reader of this body compared the six numbered lines against the sheet of the third of this month, in about eleven people, line by line, with the sheet in his own hand, and the six are the same six, in the same order, in the same words, and the references are new, and they are the office's own, and they are the first thing that office has ever given this holding which is not the six lines themselves.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the answer to the second question is correct, and that the answer to the first question is a document describing what a maintenance operation consists of on the ground in general, as a category, in any district, and that it is also correct, and that it does not say which branches, and that it was not asked which branches, and that the letter of the eighteenth of this month did not ask which branches and did not ask for a date and did not ask for a person, and that those three not-asks are the three most expensive sentences in the letter and that he wrote them in about four minutes and that he has entered that he does not know whether they were an accident.

And then he said the difference out loud, once, in the plain voice, in about eleven people, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy-two, and nobody improved it afterwards and nobody improved it before.

**"Both of those are correct. That is the whole of what I am going to say about it and I am going to say it once. The first answer is about a file and it is the right answer to a question about a file, and I asked about a file. The second answer is about a piece of country and it is the right answer to a question about a piece of country, and it is right in general, and it is right for every district, and it is right for this one, and there is not one word in it about this node, this launder, this kerb or this gate, and it did not have to be, because I did not ask it to.**"

**"And the difference between the two answers is the whole of what I have got from that office in three days, and the difference is not that one of them is true and the other one is soft. The difference is that one of them is a description of what a thing is and the other one is a description of what a kind of thing is, and I asked one question twice and I got one of each, and I put them in the same words on purpose, and I still do not know whether that was a fair thing to do to a person in an office, and I have written that down and I am not going to improve it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said in that yard, and entered that no office that has answered this holding lies, obstructs, delays, softens or fails to answer a question that was put to it, and entered the count of the things he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong, which is four, and that it did not move this evening and that this was not a yard, and entered that the count of not knowns standing against him is **six** and did not move and that the two of the six that are against a piece of paper are the date of the next window and the unit of the figures on it, and that the answer of the twentieth of this month did not move either of them, and that the office has now answered two questions correctly and the date of the next window is still not known, and that a document that is correct about a thing and silent about when the thing will happen again is not withholding anything, it is describing a thing a document does not schedule, and that he wrote that on the third of this month in a shed and has written it again this evening in a yard and that the two sentences are the same and are not a count of anything.

# Chapter 274

## The First Of The Fifth Month

The first of this month was a Thursday and the month turned in the night, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the first went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and nineteen hundredweight.**

That is the thirty-first figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and the series has fallen four times and risen five times in the nine days since the twenty-third of last month, and the thirtieth of last month and the first of this month are two different mornings and a launder does not know that and a book does.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that from this morning in this book *the first* means the first of this month and *the first of last month* means the first of the month that ended yesterday, and that both of those are true at the same time on the same page from to-day forward.

And then the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, and the boards stand at **four hundred and fifteen days** and the March's tin at **four hundred and sixty-two**, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and the fen entered that the two numbers are forty-seven days apart and that the forty-seven has not moved in about four years, and that he has read them out loud on seven mornings in about eleven days, and entered the reason, which is that a number that is a day old is a number and a number that is a month old is a rule, and a man who cannot tell those two apart has a book and not a set of rules.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute.

And then at about the tenth hour of the morning the compost line fell, and it fell whole, and the woman who does the sums chose the word and the man of about fifty wrote it and read it back, and the fen entered the whole of it and did not improve it.

**THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE FIFTH MONTH FALLS ON THE FIRST AND IS ENTERED WHOLE AND THE WORD BEFORE THE FIGURE IS PAID, AND IT HAS BEEN THE SAME WORD THREE MONTHS RUNNING, WHICH IS THE FIRST TIME THAT WORD HAS REPEATED THREE TIMES. THE FIGURE IS ONE HUNDRED POUNDS. THE RUN GOES FROM FIFTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN FIFTEEN TO SIXTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN SIXTEEN. A CONSTANT MULTIPLIED BY A CONSTANT IS NOT A MEASUREMENT OF ANYTHING OUTSIDE THAT DOOR, AND THIS LINE IS NOT A MEASUREMENT OF THE GROUND AND IS NOT GOING TO BE USED AS ONE AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES THE SUMS HAS SAID SO HERSELF AND WAS NOT ASKED.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that the difference between fifteen and sixteen is one and that the figure of one hundred pounds has not moved in three months and that neither of those two facts is a figure about the ground and that both of them are figures about a door, and that a run that has gone from twelve to thirteen to fourteen to fifteen to sixteen in five months is a straight line, and that a straight line on a door is the hardest kind of evidence for anybody to argue with, and that he is not going to argue with it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that the word has now been the same word three months running and that the woman who does the sums picks it and that a person who picks a word can pick a different one and has not, and that he has wondered about the word about four times in three months and has not asked and is not going to, and entered the reason, which is that a man who asks a woman why she keeps writing the same word is asking her whether she is happy, and that is not a question about a book.

And then at about the eleventh hour of the morning the standing for the first of this month was read out at nine hundred yards off in about twenty-five people, and it was read out by the person who keeps the seed house and read back by the clerk, and it took about nine minutes, and the reader of this body was in that yard, and the reason he was in that yard is that he said on the thirtieth of last month, standing in it, that he would be in it on the first, and the sentence is one day old.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that about four of the twenty-five said nothing in a way that was different from the other twenty-one, and that he did not count them, and entered that two mornings running in the one yard a year is a habit and that the fen has entered the word and is not going to do anything about it.

And the standing was this, and it is not summarized, and the only two lines of it that are different from the standing of the thirtieth of last month are the two.

> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS READ TO-DAY AND IS FORTY-SIX. THE DOOR IS READ ON THE FIRST AND THE THIRTIETH AND THE COLUMN HAS MOVED TWICE IN FOUR DAYS, BEING ONCE ON THE THIRTIETH OF LAST MONTH AND ONCE THIS MORNING, AND THAT IS THE FIRST TIME IN FOUR YEARS THAT IT HAS MOVED TWICE IN FOUR DAYS, AND THE REASON IT HAS MOVED TWICE IN FOUR DAYS IS THE SAME REASON IT MOVES AT ALL, WHICH IS THAT SOMEBODY READ A DOOR.**
>
> **AND A MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS FETCHED ON THE FIRST OF A MONTH. HE WAS NOT FETCHED ON THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES HE HAS BEEN FETCHED IS NINETEEN AND IT DID NOT MOVE THIS MORNING, AND THE STANDING ENTERS THAT HE WAS NOT FETCHED AND DOES NOT ENTER WHY.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that a man of about seventy has been fetched nineteen times on the first of a month and has never once asked which number, and that he was not fetched this morning, and that the fen did not ask anybody why and is not going to, and entered the reason, which is that a man who asks a man of about seventy why he was not fetched has asked him a question that is not about a number, and that the twentieth time is available to him and he is not going to take it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard and that the count of yards he has stood in since the first of a month four months back is twenty-five and did not move this morning, and that the count of the times he has said nothing at all in a yard is eight and did not move either, and that the two numbers did not move on a day they could have and that the fen entered that a count that does not move on a day it could have is a count that somebody is not keeping, and that the man of about fifty is keeping it, and that he is not going to be asked to stop.

And then at about the first hour of the afternoon the reader of this body went up to that door with the man of about fifty and stood in front of it, and about four other people stood in front of it too, and the fen entered the five and entered that the five of them had all come up for the standing and had all stayed, and that nobody was asked to stay and that nobody went.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven minutes, and entered the thing that is on that door, and it is entered whole and not summarized.

**THE DOOR IS EIGHT FEET OF BOARD AND IT HAS SIX COLUMNS RULED ON IT AND ONE COLUMN RULED ON IT WITH A HEAD. THE SIX HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THEM AND HAVE HAD NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THEM FOR FOUR YEARS. THE SEVENTH HAS A HEAD AND THE FIGURE UNDER IT IS FORTY-SIX. THE FIVE FIGURES IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK OF THE WELL ON THE SHELF FOUR HUNDRED MILES AND FOUR DAYS OFF ARE ALL FIVE FEET, AND NONE OF THE SEVEN COLUMNS ON THIS DOOR IS ABOUT THAT WELL, AND THE DOOR IS NINE HUNDRED YARDS FROM THE SEED HOUSE AND THE TWO ARE NOT THE SAME THING.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that about four of the five people in front of that door looked at the six places where a heading ought to be, and that nobody has ever written a heading on one of them, and that the fen did not count who looked and entered the reason, which is that a man who writes down which four people looked at six blank places has started a column about looking, and there is already one column in this holding about looking and it belongs to a man of about sixty-eight and not to this holding.

And then the man of about fifty said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is twenty-five, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"You are all looking at the six blanks and I have been looking at them for four years and I am going to say the one thing I have about them and I am not going to say it twice, and the reason is that this is the first of a month and there are about twenty-five people nine hundred yards from my own table and they have not all come up here before."**

**"The six have had nothing at the head of any of them for four years and nothing against any of them for four years, and I have worked out which one of the six I would write in first, and I have known which one for about two years and have not said it out loud to anybody, and I am not going to say which one it is in front of twenty-five people, and if anybody here knows, that is a thing to take up with me in a room, and I will tell you in a room and not in a yard."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that he was not thanked, and that about two of the five people in front of that door said nothing in a way that was different from the other three, and that he did not count them, and entered that the man of about fifty has told a room that he knows something this holding does not and has not told the room, and that the fen entered that this is the first time in about four years that he has said a thing like it out loud, and that a man who has kept a thing in a book for four years and has now put it on the table in front of five people has changed the standing of that thing and not its contents, and that the contents have not moved and are not going to be given to a yard.

And then the reader of this body said the one thing of the morning, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-three, and he said it in about twenty-five people, and nobody improved it.

**"I am going to say one thing about this month and I am going to say it once, and the reason is that the standing of the first and the standing of the thirtieth are the same eleven lines with two of them different, and a body of households that reads the same eleven lines twice a month for four years is a body of households that has stopped reading them, and I would rather say that out loud in twenty-five people than have it worked out in about four years by somebody who was not here."**

**"Those eleven lines are the only thing this holding says out loud every month that anybody outside it ever hears. Everything else in this book is ours. That standing is not ours, it is read off a door nine hundred yards off, and every word of it was written by somebody else, and for four years this holding has read it out twice a month and improved nothing and asked nothing, and that is the whole of what a body with no charter has been able to do in a month for four years, and it is not nothing."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nothing tender was said at nine hundred yards off on the first morning of a month, and entered that the count of things he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and did not move this morning, and entered that the standing count of questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is ten and did not move and is not said in a yard.

And then the last thing of the morning, and it is not a figure, and it is in his own book, and the fen entered it and did not improve it.

**THE MONTH TURNED IN THE NIGHT AND NOTHING ON THIS STANDING SAYS SO. THE COMPOST LINE IS THE SAME FIGURE IT WAS LAST MONTH AND IT IS A DIFFERENT MONTH. THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FORTY-SIX AND IT IS A DIFFERENT MONTH. THE STANDING IS THE SAME ELEVEN LINES AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IT HAS BEEN READ TWICE IN FOUR DAYS. A HELD FIGURE IS A THING A ROOM CAN PLAN AROUND AND A STEADY FIGURE IS A THING A VILLAGE CAN PLAN AROUND, AND A CALENDAR IS A THIRD THING AND IT IS THE ONE THAT WORKS WHETHER OR NOT ANYBODY IS CARING, AND I AM NOT GOING TO IMPROVE ON THAT AND NEITHER IS ANYBODY IN THAT YARD.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the first of this month, in about eleven people, at this holding, on the standing of a month, and not by the reader of this body, who was at nine hundred yards off until about the second hour of the afternoon: **THE MONTH TURNED IN THE NIGHT. THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND NINETEEN HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE FIRST, WHICH IS THE THIRTY-FIRST FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN TWENTY-NINE DAYS. THE DAY-MINUS RULES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER AND THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-TWO. THE COMPOST LINE FOR THE FIFTH MONTH FELL ON THIS MORNING AND IS ENTERED WHOLE. THE WORD BEFORE THE FIGURE IS PAID AND HAS BEEN THE SAME WORD THREE MONTHS RUNNING. THE FIGURE IS ONE HUNDRED POUNDS. THE RUN GOES FROM FIFTEEN TO SIXTEEN. THE LINE IS NOT A MEASUREMENT OF THE GROUND. THE STANDING FOR THE FIRST WAS READ OUT AT NINE HUNDRED YARDS OFF IN ABOUT TWENTY-FIVE PEOPLE AND READ BACK BY THE CLERK AND TOOK ABOUT NINE MINUTES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS READ AND IS FORTY-SIX, AND THE OTHER SIX ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS, AND NOBODY ASKS. A MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS FETCHED ON THE FIRST OF A MONTH AND WAS NOT FETCHED TO-DAY AND THE COUNT IS NINETEEN AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE STANDING DOES NOT ENTER WHY. THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH WAS NOT IN THAT YARD AND HIS TWO COUNTS, BEING TWENTY-FIVE AND EIGHT, DID NOT MOVE. THE COUNT OF THINGS THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FOUR. THE STANDING COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN IS TEN AND IS NOT SAID IN A YARD. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN. THE BOX IN THE CART SHED HOLDS NINE POUNDS AND EIGHT SHILLINGS. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

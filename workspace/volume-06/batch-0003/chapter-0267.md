# Chapter 267

## What A Scar Is And What It Is Not

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Thursday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **sixteen**, and entered that all sixteen of them are the fourth line, and entered that the rota now stands at **forty-five mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and sixteen not**, and that twenty-nine and sixteen are forty-five, and that nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

And the figure for the twenty-fourth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and ten hundredweight.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that it has fallen, and that it is the first time the series has fallen since the twentieth of this month, and that the reader of this body entered the hour and entered that a man who has not seen a figure fall for three days does not know what he is waiting for, and that nobody thanked anybody.

And then at about the second hour of the afternoon four people went up the cart road past the ford and off it and down to the ninth of the eleven places, and the four of them were the same four, and the fen entered that it is the second day of a walk and that a walk is not on the rota and is being done in the hours between the lines, and that a man who does his walk in the gaps between his work has entered that the walk comes second, and that the day he stops doing it the work is still there.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty opened the bag.

And they did a thing with a cord before they did anything with a wire, and the fen entered it because it is the part of the day that a person who was not there would not believe, and it took about forty minutes and it decided nothing.

The eleven places are in four miles and the fen entered that they are not evenly spaced, and that a man reading a page about eleven places would put them evenly because a page is a grid and a valley is not, and so the cord was taken off the man of about fifty's own belt and the eleven were paced out. The shortest is about ninety yards. The longest is about two hundred and forty. And the two nearest each other are the ninth and the tenth at about sixty yards, and the ninth is the branch.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that four people stood on a bank looking at a cord and that nobody could say why the eleven were where they were, and that the reader of this body walked the whole four miles twice more over the two days and did not work it out and did not ask the officer, and entered the reason, which is that a man who asks an office why its work is where it is has asked a question with a person in it, and there is nobody in it, and asking it anyway is how a question with a person in it gets into a book.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is three, and he said it to the man of about fifty and not to the officer.

**"I have paced a thing out before and I am going to say the one thing I know about pacing and then I am going to get on with it, and the reason is that three of you have been talking about the eleven and nobody has said what pacing is for."**

**"You do not pace a thing to find out where it is. You pace it to find out whether you found all of them. I have paced a ditch twice in my life and both times I came back and found a place I had walked past, and both times it was at a corner where the line turned and my eye went across the inside of the turn instead of along it. So there is a fifth place on that line and there may be a sixth, and I am not saying there is, and I am saying the eleven are a figure I could not check this afternoon and it is not a figure about the ground."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that he was not thanked, and that the reader of this body had not thought of it, and that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is six and did not move, and that this was said in a field and not in a yard and is a different man and a different count, and that both of those are entered and neither is added to the other.

And the instrument is this and it is worth a paragraph of its own because it is the cheapest thing in this holding and it is not a professional instrument and it is not a bead and it is not a book.

**A steel wire on a loop, and a rule of eleven inches. The wire is fourpence the yard and two yards came off the roll in a cart shed on the seventh of this month and cost eight pence, and the rule is the man of about fifty's own and has been in that bag since the second month of a year eleven years back, and the bag is his and has been in this holding's keeping ever since. THE PRACTICE IS TWO READINGS AND NO MORE THAN TWO. PASS THE WIRE ROUND THE STEM ABOVE THE MARK AND READ THE GIRTH. PASS IT ACROSS THE CUT FACE AND READ THE WIDTH. THE TWO ARE COMPARED ON THE SAME WIRE IN THE SAME PLACE AT THE SAME HOUR, AND A FIGURE THAT IS SMALLER THAN THE FIGURE BEFORE IT ON THE SAME INSTRUMENT IN THE SAME PLACE IS A REDUCTION.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the man of about fifty has never been paid for the use of it and has not asked to be and that the fen entered that a man with an instrument in a bag is not a professional and does not carry a professional's standing, and that that is the whole of why four people including an officer of an office were able to put a wire on a piece of wood in a field and that an officer with a standing could not have refused him and the officer did not try to, and that the difference between those two things is about eight pence of wire and eleven years of a bag.

And then the four of them read the eleven places, and it took about two hours, and the fen entered the whole of it and improved none of it.

**AT TEN OF THE ELEVEN PLACES THE GROUND WAS OPENED AND MADE GOOD AND THERE IS NO WOOD IN IT AND NOTHING TO READ. AT THE NINTH THERE IS A BRANCH, AND THE WIRE GOES ROUND IT ABOVE THE MARK AND THE WIRE GOES ACROSS THE FACE, AND THE FACE IS SMALLER THAN THE GIRTH. AND THERE IS A ROLL. A ROLL IS THE RIDGE OF NEW WOOD THAT A TREE GROWS OVER A CUT AND IT IS WHAT A SCAR IS MADE OF, AND WHERE THERE IS A ROLL THE WOOD IS CLOSED, AND WHERE THERE IS NO ROLL AND NO CLOSING THE WOOD IS NOT CLOSED, AND THAT IS WHAT A BREAK IS, AND A BREAK AND A SCAR ARE NOT TWO WORDS FOR ONE THING AND THEY CANNOT BE USED FOR ONE ANOTHER.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the officer of the Office of the Continuity took a figure out of the folded sheet and compared it with the wire and did not put it in his own book, and entered the reason, which is that the figure on the sheet is the office's figure of the girth of that stem as at a date the office holds, and the office holds it to the hundredth of an inch, and a figure to the hundredth of an inch taken by a different hand on different ground is not a figure this holding can put a wire to, and that a man who writes both numbers on one line has made a subtraction and called it a comparison.

And then the officer said the thing, and he gave the reason for saying it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two.

**"I am going to say the thing that matters and then I am not going to say it again, and the reason I am saying it now is that you have all four of us in a field and one of you has a wire and three of you have never used one, and the thing that matters is that I do not know how fast a roll grows."**

**"I have a figure for that stem from my office and I have no figure for the roll. There is no figure for the roll in this list or in any list I have seen, and if there were one it would be the most useful number in the whole of what my office keeps, and it is not there. So a face that has healed is a face that has healed and I cannot tell you when. You have got a scar and a scar has no date in it, and everything either of us wants to do with that branch this season depends on a number that neither of us has."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that he was not thanked, and that the reader of this body did not thank him, and entered that the officer had said a thing nobody in this holding had said and had said it in a field and not in a yard, and that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is six and did not move, and that a thing said in a field by a man from an office is not that count and is not on that column and never will be.

And the reader of this body gave the reason before the answer and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy-five, and he said it once and did not say it twice.

**"You have said the thing and you have said it in about four people and I am going to say it back in about four people and not in a yard, and the reason is that a scar with no date in it is the answer to a question I asked in a yard on the eleventh of last month, and it is not the answer I wanted and it is the answer, and a man who keeps asking a question in a yard because he did not like the first answer is not doing engineering."**

**"What I have is: a reduction, undated, on a branch about the size of a man's wrist, and eleven places where the ground was opened and made good, and a roll that says the wood is closed. What I have not got is how long ago, and I am not going to have it today, and I am not going to invent it. The count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and the first of them is not named, and it is going to stay two, and the figure I have just not written is not going to be the third, because a number a man has not measured and cannot get to is a number he can invent, and I have seen what that looks like and it looks like a page with a figure on it and a week afterwards somebody reading it."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nothing tender was said and that nobody thanked anybody.

And then he said the last thing of the afternoon and he named a rule and did not use it, and the count of that naming in this holding's history is four, and four is the count of the namings and is not a use.

**"I am going to name the standing rule on the wall of that seed house and I am not going to use it, and I want the man of about fifty to enter that I said I was not using it, and the reason is that it is about a draw and what we are doing on that bank is a wire and a bag and four pence of steel, and a rule about a draw does not reach a wire."**

**"I have named that rule three times in about nine days and used it not at all, and a fourth naming is not a use, and I would like anybody in this field who thinks otherwise to say so now and not in about four years."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the standing rule was not used and that the count of its uses in the last two seasons is two and one and that neither of them was this, and that a rule named four times in about ten days and not used is not a rule anybody is working and is a sentence a man is holding over himself in front of other people.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in about nine people, at the node, on the standing of an instrument, and not by the reader of this body, who was on the shoulder until the light went: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS SIXTEEN, AND ALL SIXTEEN OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT FORTY-FIVE MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND SIXTEEN NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND TEN HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH, WHICH IS THE TWENTY-FOURTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IT IS THE FIRST FALL SINCE THE TWENTIETH OF THIS MONTH. THE INSTRUMENT IS A STEEL WIRE ON A LOOP AND A RULE OF ELEVEN INCHES, AND THE WIRE IS FOURPENCE THE YARD AND TWO YARDS COST EIGHT PENCE, AND THE RULE AND THE BAG HAVE BEEN IN THE KEEPING OF THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ELEVEN YEARS. A FIGURE SMALLER THAN THE FIGURE BEFORE IT ON THE SAME INSTRUMENT IN THE SAME PLACE IS A REDUCTION. AT THE NINTH OF ELEVEN PLACES WHERE THE GROUND OVER THE LINE WAS OPENED AND MADE GOOD THERE IS A BRANCH, AND THE FACE ON IT IS SMALLER THAN THE GIRTH, AND THERE IS A ROLL OVER THE FACE, AND A ROLL MEANS THE WOOD IS CLOSED, AND NO ROLL AND NO CLOSING IS A BREAK, AND THE TWO WORDS MAY NOT BE USED FOR ONE ANOTHER. THE OFFICER OF THE OFFICE OF THE CONTINUITY COMPARED THE FIGURE ON HIS OWN SHEET WITH THE WIRE AND DID NOT PUT THE TWO IN HIS BOOK, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE HAS NO FIGURE FOR HOW FAST A ROLL GROWS AND THAT NO SUCH FIGURE IS IN ANY LIST HE HAS SEEN. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID IN ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE THAT A SCAR HAS NO DATE IN IT AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO INVENT ONE, AND THAT THE COUNT OF NUMBERS THIS HOLDING HAS HANDED OUT THAT IT CANNOT CHECK IS TWO AND IS GOING TO STAY TWO, AND NAMED THE STANDING RULE ON THE WALL OF THE SEED HOUSE AND DID NOT USE IT, AND ASKED FOR THAT TO BE ENTERED, AND THE FOURTH NAMING IS FOUR NAMINGS AND IS NOT A USE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

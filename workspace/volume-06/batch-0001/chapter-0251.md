# Chapter 251

## The Frost With No Sky

The sixth of this month was a Sunday and there was a frost on the ground at this holding at about the fifth hour of the morning, and it held until about the eighth hour, and it was not a white one.

It was thin. It stood in the furrows of the seed house beds and in the ruts of the cart track and in the shadow side of every post and stake, and it was not on the road at all, and the road had two carts on it by the fifth hour and neither of them left a print.

The person who keeps the seed house went out at about the fourth hour and came in at about the fifth and said one thing to about eleven people and gave the reason for saying it before she said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is three.

"**The sky was clear at the fourth hour and it is clear now, and I have been at that door since the fourth hour and I have not seen a cloud in it, and I am saying that in about eleven people at the fifth hour in the morning because in about four hours it will be warm and in about four days there will be nobody left who was here for it.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

The frost book is at this holding and it is thirty-one years old and it is kept by the person who keeps the seed house and she has kept it for nine years and her mother kept it before her. It is a page of ruled paper with a line for every frost and a column for the hour and a column for the sky. There is **one** line in it between the turn of the year and this morning and the line is the twelfth of the second month, and the hour in it is the sixth, and the sky in it says clear.

She read the whole of that out loud in about nine seconds and nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the count of one, and entered that thirty-one years of that page have one line in it and that this holding has never had a second one and is not going to have an opinion about why.

And the man of about fifty had been breaking the ice in the cattle trough at the wall side with the back of a spade and stopped when she said it and stood there with the spade, and he said the one thing that is his about a trough, and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fourteen.

"**That trough has ice in it about four mornings a year and it has had it twice this month, and the first was on the second and the second was this morning, and I am not saying that means anything because I do not know what it means and I have got nine inches of water in a trough and eleven beasts to water and a frost does not make me thirsty.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and entered that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is five and did not move this morning, and that a trough is a trough and that a figure about nine inches of water is a figure about a trough.

And the man of the north row with the cough was in that yard and was not asked to be in it, and he had come back up the fen road with the man of about fifty on the third and had been at the gate of this holding at about the ninth hour on the fourth and had not said anything then either, and the count of yards he has stood in since the first of a month four months back is nineteen and has moved.

And the reader of this body said the caution out loud in about eleven people at about the seventh hour, and gave the reason before the caution, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-five, and what he said was not tender and was not a thank and nobody thanked anybody.

"**A frost in the fourth month is a figure and it is not a cause. I have got a page with one line in it that a woman of about seventy-one has kept for nine years and a book of her mother's before her, and one line is a number, and I have been in a yard in this book for four years saying that a number is not a reason, and I am not going to stop saying it because the number is one instead of a hundred and it came in a week when somebody else was doing something on a schedule.**"

"**So I am not going to be the one who stands in this yard this morning and tells eleven people what put a skin on that ground, and I want it written down that I could have, and that in about four days a room in this holding is going to be asked to find out, and that I am going to be in it.**"

"**And I have said four things out loud in a yard and got wrong, and I know the number, and I have known it every morning since Wednesday, and knowing a number is not the same as being able to do anything about it and I have not found out what to do with it yet.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the count of things the reader of this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and it is four this morning and it did not go to five, and that it was available to him at about the fourth sentence of that and he went on.

And then the third launder's figures for the eight days the book has a line for were read out of the book at about the eighth hour by the woman who does the sums, and entered by the clerk, and they are these and they are in a hand that is not the clerk's and not the reader of this body's.

**Nothing on the twenty-ninth of a month three months back. Nothing on the thirtieth. Five hundred and seventy-five on the first. Five hundred and eighty on the second. Five hundred and eighty-four on the third. Five hundred and eighty-eight on the fourth. Five hundred and eighty-nine on the fifth. Five hundred and ninety-four on the sixth.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the figure has not been the same twice since the first, and that the book has eight days in it and six figures on the six mornings, and that not one of the six is the same as any of the other five, and that the two mornings that have nothing against them have the same nothing on them twice and that a nothing twice is not a figure twice, and that the woman who does the sums put them on the same line with the same hour against each of them and read them back, and that the man of about fifty read them back a second time in a different book, and that the two figures are the same, and entered that two men writing the same figure is not a check and has been entered as not a check twice this month and that the count of that is two.

And at about the sixth hour of the afternoon the woman who does the sums and the person who keeps the seed house went out to the beds and put the mats over the seed, and it took the two of them about forty minutes and it is four mats and a weight at each corner and there are about nine beds, and it was done by two people and not by nine, and the reader of this body was in the yard and was not asked and did not offer and entered that he did not offer.

The two of them argued about it for about nine minutes first, in about the same two voices as a thing about money, and the argument was whether four mats is enough for nine beds or whether four mats and the reed mats out of the back room is enough for nine beds, and the reed mats are eleven and they weigh about four stone each and there is one man in this holding who could lift four stone onto a frame and he is the man of about fifty and he was asked and he did it and he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and it took him about an hour and ten minutes and it took the other two of them about half of that helping, and it is in the book as an hour and ten minutes, and as four mats and eleven reed mats, and as about half an hour of three people, and as a figure that this holding can check.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the whole of it cost eleven pounds and four shillings of a hold's money in wages and canvas and reed, and that it is a line of its own and it does not go into the compost line, and that the reader of this body entered a sentence about it in his own hand with the hour on it, and the sentence is that a body of households that can put eleven reed mats over nine beds in an hour and ten minutes in the fourth month is a body of households that can do a great many things and has not been asked to do any of them, and that he wrote that down at about the seventh hour and then wrote under it that a man who writes a sentence like that about other people at about the seventh hour of the evening is a man who has been watching four bodies of households work for four years and has not once written the other half of it, and that the other half is that he could not have put the first mat down, and that that is in the sentence and is not going to be improved on.

And then the reader of this body walked out to the shoulder above the eleven acres at about the eleventh hour and looked at the ground, and the ground inside the kerb was white in the same thin way and the ground outside the kerb was not, and the ring of bare ground was white along its length and the inside of the ring was not, and he stood at the shoulder about nine feet off the hollow and looked at it and did not go down into it and did not put a rule against anything and did not measure anything, and then he came back down.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about four minutes, and entered his own reason for not going down into the ring, which is this, and that it is that a man who goes down into a thing he cannot explain comes up out of it with a story, and that the story will be about eleven years, and that nobody has any business carrying a story about eleven years of bare ground out of a yard of about eleven people on a Sunday morning, and that this is the fourth thing he has not said out loud this week and it is not on the same column as the other three and does not go on it.

And at about the seventh hour of the evening a woman came up the fen road on foot from the north gate with a bag in each hand and asked at the gate for the seed house, and the person who keeps the seed house walked out and looked at her and walked her in and did not ask her a question, and the reader of this body was at the far end of the yard and did not go to the road.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody had sent for her, and entered that this holding does not know yet that nobody had sent for her, and entered that the reader of this body did not go to the road and did not send anybody to the road, and that he was told about her on the Monday morning by the person who keeps the seed house, in about eleven words, and entered that he was told.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the sixth of this month, in about eleven people and then about twenty-five, on the standing of a count and of a series of figures, and not by the reader of this body, who was in the yard and was not asked to write it down: **THERE WAS A FROST ON THE GROUND AT THIS HOLDING FROM ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR OF THE MORNING UNTIL ABOUT THE EIGHTH HOUR, THIN, IN THE FURROWS AND THE RUTS AND THE SHADOW SIDE OF EVERY POST, AND NOT ON THE ROAD. THE PERSON WHO KEEPS THE SEED HOUSE SAW THE SKY CLEAR AT THE FOURTH HOUR AND AT THE FIFTH AND DID NOT SEE A CLOUD IN IT. THE FROST BOOK IS THIRTY-ONE YEARS OLD AND HAS ONE LINE IN IT BETWEEN THE TURN OF THE YEAR AND THIS MORNING, BEING THE TWELFTH OF THE SECOND MONTH AT THE SIXTH HOUR, SKY CLEAR, AND THE COUNT IS ONE. THE TROUGH AT THE WALL SIDE HAD ICE IN IT TWICE THIS MONTH, ON THE SECOND AND THIS MORNING. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, THAT A FROST IN THE FOURTH MONTH IS A FIGURE AND NOT A CAUSE, AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO BE THE ONE WHO SAYS WHAT PUT A SKIN ON THAT GROUND, AND THAT HE HAS SAID FOUR THINGS OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG AND KNOWS THE NUMBER. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED NOTHING ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF A MONTH THREE MONTHS BACK AND NOTHING ON THE THIRTIETH AND FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIVE ON THE FIRST, FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY ON THE SECOND, FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR ON THE THIRD, FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHT ON THE FOURTH, FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE ON THE FIFTH AND FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR ON THE SIXTH, AND THE FIGURE HAS NOT BEEN THE SAME TWICE SINCE THE FIRST. THE RING OF BARE GROUND INSIDE THE ELEVEN ACRES WAS WALKED AT THE SHOULDER AND WAS NOT STOPPED AT AND WAS NOT MEASURED, AND IT IS THE SECOND TIME IN ELEVEN DAYS. AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR OF THE EVENING A WOMAN CAME UP THE FEN ROAD ON FOOT FROM THE NORTH GATE WITH A BAG IN EACH HAND AND ASKED FOR THE SEED HOUSE, AND NOBODY HAD SENT FOR HER, AND THE READER OF THIS BODY DID NOT GO TO THE ROAD.**

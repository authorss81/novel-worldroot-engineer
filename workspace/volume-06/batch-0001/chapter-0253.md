# Chapter 253

## Four Lines On A Piece Of Paper

The eighth of this month was a Tuesday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered that nobody is blamed and entered the number, which is **twelve**, and entered that all twelve of them are the fourth line.

And at about the fourth hour of the morning of the eighth of this month the hand of the reader of this body came back off the wrist down.

He had had no use of it from the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of a month two months back. It came back on the morning of the eighth, at about the fourth hour, over about nine seconds, and he was standing at the north gate with nothing in that hand and nothing in the other one, and the man of about fifty was about nine feet off and saw it happen and said nothing and entered that he saw it.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the hand came back over about nine seconds and that it is not a cure, and that it is the first time in about nine weeks that it has done anything at all, and that the count of things that have come back on him in four years is one, and that it is on the left side and the mark is four inches and branches twice and there is no second mark, and that nothing said about that arm in this entry is a fifth account of it.

And the fifteenth ordinary reading of the well at Cauldron Reach is on the sixteenth of this month, eight days from this morning, at about the fourth hour, four hundred miles and four days off, and the day was entered and the figure was not, and the fen entered that he entered the day and not the figure, and that the count in this holding's book of entries that carry a day and no figure stands at three and is neither raised nor reduced to-day, because what is entered this morning is a day and not the figure of a reading, and because he is not going to change what that count counts in order to make a morning tidy. The wall at Cauldron Reach carries thirteen marks, and this holding's book holds four of its figures and all four of them are five feet, and there is no entry anywhere in this holding's book for that well coming back.

And then at about the second hour of the afternoon the seedwright of a trust left, and the four hundredweight of boards had gone west on the Sunday with a carter and she had no cart of her own and had been here two nights, and she said the thing about her own road at the door with the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one, and she said it to the yard and not to him.

"**I am out on the route eleven days in twenty and I have been out of it for four, and I am going to be out of it for six more, and that is ten of the twenty and I am giving you the sum because you would do it wrong. I have not asked anybody to cover my route and I am not going to. Your sister is on the western relay and she is well and she is not coming back for the wet, and I am telling you that because you would otherwise ask me in about four days and I would rather say it now than say it well.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of the entries in this holding's book in which that name stands is one and that it is this one, and entered that he did not say thank you and did not say anything else to her.

And on the sixth of this month the reader of this body had written to the Rootwright Assembly, and the letter was four lines long and asked for a person who was not on a road, and it cost eleven pence, a line of its own that does not go into the compost line, and the fen entered that the Assembly's answer came on the seventh, which is one day, and that one day is not remarkable, and that the remarkable thing is that he had written four days down on the sixth expecting four, and had to enter on the seventh that his own expectation of an office was a figure and not a measurement, and that the count of figures of that kind in this holding's book in four years is two.

And the man of the north row with the cough was in the seed house yard that morning and in that afternoon and was not asked to be in either, and the count of yards he has stood in since the first of a month four months back is twenty, and he said nothing at all on the eighth, and that is the fourth time in about four weeks.

And then at about the eleventh hour of the morning, in about eleven people in the seed house, two pieces of paper were read out standing up, one and then the other, in the hand each was written in, and read back by the man of about fifty, and the reader of this body did not hold either of them and was not asked to hold either of them, and nobody thanked anybody.

> **FOUR LINES, in the hand of a seedwright of a trust, on one piece of paper of about the size of a hand, read out standing up and read back:**
>
> **ONE. A WORD. One word of mine stops it. The word is mine and it is not his, and he does not get to choose it and he does not get to hear it said in advance.**
>
> **TWO. THERE IS A SECOND PERSON IN THE ROOM AND THAT PERSON IS NEITHER OF US, AND THEY ARE TOLD WHAT IS HAPPENING BEFORE IT HAPPENS AND NOT AFTER.**
>
> **THREE. HE STOPS WHEN I STOP. HE DOES NOT STOP WHEN HE THINKS I SHOULD, AND NOT WHEN HE THINKS I HAVE NOT STOPPED BECAUSE I AM BEING BRAVE.**
>
> **FOUR. I AM NOT HIS SAFETY DEVICE. IF EITHER OF US IS USING ME AS ONE THEN EITHER OF US ENDS IT AND NEITHER OF US IS ASKED WHY.**
>
> **THREE LINES, in the hand of an engineer, which is Sera Quill's, on one piece of paper of about the same size, read out standing up and read back:**
>
> **ONE. NOT LONGER THAN NINETEEN MINUTES AT A TIME AND NOT FOUR TIMES IN ONE DAY.**
>
> **TWO. THE BEAD GOES ON THE PERSON AND NOT ON THE GROUND, AND THE PERSON IS TOLD WHAT IT WILL COST BEFORE IT GOES ON AND NOT AFTER.**
>
> **THREE. A SECOND STEWARD IN THE ROOM WITH A WATCH AND A STOP, AND THE WATCH IS THE ONLY THING IN THE ROOM THAT DOES NOT LIE ABOUT TIME.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half.

And the reader of this body said the thing about the two lists, and he gave the reason before the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-eight, and it is not tender and nobody thanked anybody.

"**Those two lists differ in one place and it is not the numbers. Tova Reed's is four lines and three of the four are about the person, and Sera Quill's is three and all three are about the instrument, the time and the room, and I am not going to read that out as though I had found it, because it was in front of about eleven people and they could count.**"

"**The reason Sera Quill's has no line about a person in it is not that she does not think about people. It is that she has never once in her life been the person who stops, and a woman who has never been the one who stops does not write a line about being stopped. She writes a line about a watch, because a watch is the only thing in a room she has ever been able to stand behind, and that is a real reason and it is not a better reason than mine.**"

"**Both lists are correct and I am not going to choose between them in about eleven people and I am not going to improve one word of either. I will use both, and when they contradict one another the seedwright's wins, and the reason is not that she is right about instruments, and I am entering that a man of thirty-one has put a seedwright's list over an engineer's and is not the right man to be the one who does that.**"

"**And I am not using anybody yet. There is nothing this week to read and I am not going to go and make one so that I can be the man who tried.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he was not thanked, and entered that the two pieces of paper went into the book on the table in the middle whole, in their two hands, and that the count of the times he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and it is four this morning and that a sentence said out loud in a yard about a list of conditions was not a fifth and that the fen has read it back twice and cannot prove it and has entered that he cannot prove it.

And on the ninth of this month, a Wednesday, Sabra Ollerton was in a room at this holding at about the second hour of the afternoon, and she is about fifty-two and she is an inspector of the Rootwright Assembly for four districts and she is not on the roll for the district the Four Elbows stands in, and she had come up the cart road on the Assembly's time and not on this holding's money, and the Assembly had sent her because the reader of this body wrote to the Assembly on the sixth of this month and the Assembly answered on the seventh, which is one day, and the answer said that the Assembly has no officer who reads patterns and has one that checks returns, and sent her.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and that the Assembly did not delay and did not obstruct and did not soften anything, and that the answer was one day, and that the Assembly told him what it could not do before it told him what it could do, which is not an office being careful, it is an office being ordinary, and that the difference between those two things is the whole of what that sheet has done to him this week.

And Sabra Ollerton said the one thing that is hers, and she gave the reason before it, and the count of that in this holding's history is one, and she said it to a room and not to a yard, and the count of that in this holding's history is two.

"**I am the wrong person for this and I am going to say so before I do any of it, and the reason is that I inspect works. I do not read patterns. I have never read a pattern in my life and I would not know one from a bad wall.**"

"**What I can do is tell you whether a district return says what a woman on a road says it says, and that is an afternoon's work and no more, and I am going to do that and I am not going to be reported afterwards as having done more than that.**"

And she did it, in about forty minutes, out of a return that the district had sent to the Assembly in the ordinary course and the Assembly had sent to her in the ordinary course, and the fen entered that neither office had to be asked for it twice and that neither was asked for it twice.

And then she said the second thing that is hers, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is three, and nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be.

"**I am going to say a thing about my own profession and I am saying it to a room and not to a yard, and the reason is that I have said it to about eleven yards in nineteen years and have been wrong in one of them and have not worked out which one.**"

"**The Rootwright Assembly did not write that sheet of the third of this month and my office has nothing to do with that office, and the difference is embossed on the corner of the paper and took me about four seconds to see and about an hour to say out loud. I have been asked in writing twice in nineteen years to say that a thing my office did not do was not done by my office, and I did both, and one of them is the reason I have not slept properly since about four years ago, and it is not a scandal, it is a piece of paper.**"

"**And I am not going to let anybody use me this week. I check returns and I have checked yours. Anything else is a thing to ask my office for and pay my office for, and if I am asked for it in a yard I will say no, and the reason is that I cannot stand in a yard of twenty-five people and be an Assembly and a woman at once, and that has happened to me once and it cost a village a season.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that she was not thanked, and entered that the reader of this body asked her nothing at all after that, and entered that the count of the things he has asked her is one and it is a thing he asked her at the door about the hour of a return, and that the answer was nine seconds and was correct.

> **The Cormel column, corroborated out of a district return of the district in which Cormel stands, in a room at this holding, at about the third hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in about four people, by Sabra Ollerton, inspector of the Rootwright Assembly for four districts, entered whole and not summarized: THE DISTRICT'S OWN RETURN ENTERS, FOR THE TWENTY-NINTH OF A MONTH THREE MONTHS BACK, A THAW AT THE SECOND HOUR OF THE MORNING AT CORMEL, A SOWING AT THE FOURTH HOUR, AND NO RAIN IN THE DISTRICT FOR THE SEASON. THE RETURN IS SIGNED BY THE DISTRICT'S OWN OFFICERS AND IS THE FIGURE THEY SEND EVERY NINTH DAY AND IT WAS SENT WITHOUT BEING ASKED FOR ON ANY SUBJECT.**
>
> **AND SHE ADDED, IN HER OWN HAND, BELOW THAT: I AM NOT THE DISTRICT AND I HAVE NEVER BEEN IN THAT DISTRICT. I HAVE COMPARED A RETURN WITH A ROAD AND THE TWO SAY THE SAME THING TO THE HOUR. THAT IS ALL I HAVE DONE AND I WANT IT ENTERED THAT NOBODY HAS EVER IN NINETEEN YEARS ASKED ME TO CORROBORATE A FIGURE, AND THAT I CAME AT MY OWN COST AND NOT AT THE REQUEST OF ANY OFFICE, AND THAT THE REQUEST CAME FROM A COOPERATIVE THAT HAS NO CHARTER.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that she was not thanked, and entered that the reader of this body did not thank her, and entered that the count of the times he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and that he has now got a second observer and that a second observer is not a fifth thing said out loud in a yard and that the two are not on the same column and have not been added together.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in about eleven people and then about four, on the standing of a count and of a document, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and was in the room: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS TWELVE, AND ALL TWELVE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE. THE HAND OF THE READER OF THIS BODY CAME BACK OFF THE WRIST DOWN AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE MORNING AND IT IS NOT A CURE. THE FIFTEENTH ORDINARY READING OF THE WELL AT CAULDRON REACH IS ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE DAY WAS ENTERED AND THE FIGURE WAS NOT, AND THE COUNT OF ENTRIES THAT CARRY A DAY AND NO FIGURE STANDS AT THREE AND WAS NEITHER RAISED NOR REDUCED. TWO PIECES OF PAPER WERE READ OUT IN ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE, FOUR LINES IN THE HAND OF A SEEDWRIGHT OF A TRUST AND THREE LINES IN THE HAND OF AN ENGINEER, AND THEY DIFFER IN ONE PLACE, AND THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID THAT HE WILL USE BOTH AND THAT WHEN THEY CONTRADICT ONE ANOTHER THE SEEDWRIGHT'S WINS, AND THAT HE IS NOT USING ANYBODY YET. THE ROOTWRIGHT ASSEMBLY ANSWERED A LETTER SENT ON THE SIXTH OF THIS MONTH ON THE SEVENTH, IN ONE DAY, AND SAID IT HAS NO OFFICER WHO READS PATTERNS AND SENT AN INSPECTOR WHO CHECKS RETURNS. SABRA OLLERTON COMPARED THE CORMEL COLUMN WITH THE DISTRICT'S OWN NINTH-DAY RETURN IN ABOUT FORTY MINUTES AND THE TWO SAY THE SAME THING TO THE HOUR. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

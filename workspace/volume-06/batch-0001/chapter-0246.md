# Chapter 246

## One Hundred And Forty

The first of this month was a Tuesday and the compost line falls on the first, and it was entered whole at about the second hour of the morning in the seed house yard, in the ordinary hand, with the day and the hour on it.

**PAID. One hundred pounds. Fifteen not discharged in fifteen.**

The word before the figure was the word before it on the first of last month, and it was the word before that on the first of the month before that, and the reader of this body entered that a word is a thing a person picks and a figure is a thing a person cannot pick, and entered that the woman who does the sums picked this one twice and said in the third month that she was not going to be thanked for it, and nobody thanked her.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And then the standing for the first was read out at about the eleventh hour of the morning, at nine hundred yards off the seed house, in a yard, in about twenty-five people, by the person who keeps the seed house, and read back by the clerk, and **the reader of this body was five days east on the cart road and was not in that yard and was not sent for and did not ask to be sent the sheet.**

> **THE STANDING FOR THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH, read at about the eleventh hour of the morning, at nine hundred yards off, in about twenty-five people, by the person who keeps the seed house, and read back by the clerk, entered as it was read.**
>
> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FORTY-FOUR.** Read on the first and on the thirtieth, because the door is read on the first and the thirtieth and the rule did not change and is not going to. **THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS ARE BLANK AND HAVE BEEN FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY ASKED.**
>
> **THE DAY-MINUS RULES.** The boards stand at three hundred and eighty-five days and the March's tin at four hundred and thirty-two, and both were read out loud before either number, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be.
>
> **THE COMPOST LINE FOR THIS MONTH IS PAID, ONE HUNDRED POUNDS, FIFTEEN NOT DISCHARGED IN FIFTEEN,** and the word before that figure is PAID, and it is the second month running that the word has been that, and a word is a thing a person picks.
>
> **THE LEAK** is three-quarters of an inch, not measured this month, three reasons and no cause and no due date, and no fourth reason was invented. **THE BEAT** is not logged, cause not established, column open, and its day is not restated, and the column was not opened in front of twenty-five people this morning and the not opening is a decision.
>
> **THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES.** One hundred and forty hundredweight on a steelyard is not a contact. A rule left blank is not a contact. A total that agrees with a need is not a contact. **A FIGURE THAT COMES OUT WELL IS NOT A CONTACT AND A FIGURE THAT COMES OUT BADLY IS NOT A CONTACT EITHER, AND THE LOG COUNTS THINGS THAT TOUCHED SOMEBODY.**
>
> **THE STANDING RULE** was not used this month. Four lines, every one of them about a stop, about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called. The count of its uses in the season before last was two and the count in the season last was one, and the one was a draw that was not begun, and the two counts are two counts.
>
> **THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR IS TWO** and it is two on the first and it did not move, and the reason the man of the north row with the cough was not struck off it is in this holding's book and is not a reason about a man of sixty-eight and his hands. **THE COUNT FOR FIVE IS ONE** and it is him and it did not move. **THE COUNT OF PERSONS IN THIS FEN WHO HAVE A ROOTMARK IS TWO,** four inches branching twice and three inches not branching, and the two injuries are not the same injury and the two men have not been added together. **THE COUNT OF PERSONS WHO CAN HOLD A PATTERN FOR FOUR AND WALK OUT OF THE GROUND AFTERWARDS IS ONE,** and it is him, and the reader of this body entered it in the same line as the count of two for the reason he entered that one in the same line in the first month of the year, and the reason is that a man who enters one of them by itself has entered a boast.
>
> **THE FOUR ACCOUNTS OF THE SAME ARM IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK ARE FOUR** and they reconcile none of one another and nothing said about that arm this month is a fifth. **THE COUNT OF AN ENTRY THAT CARRIES A DAY AND NO FIGURE STANDS AT THREE** and it is not raised and it is not reduced.
>
> **THE REQUESTS ARE FIFTY-THREE AND THE SECTION-NINE NOTES ARE FIFTY-THREE** and neither moved and no new note was written under section nine this month, and there is no charter and the note says so on its face. **WHOSE ORDER IT IS** has been asked four times in the season last and answered never and it was not asked a fifth time. **THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN** was not said out loud this morning and is not going to be.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody.

And the reader of this body had come out of this holding on the twenty-eighth of the month before on a hired horse, and the road to the flats above Errow Reach is five days with a cart and one day on a horse, and the going was ordinary, because the ditches at the first mile and at the fourth mile had been in since the turn of the year, and he had noticed that on the way out and had said nothing about it to anybody and had entered in his own book that it is somebody else's count of a ditch and not his and that a man who notices a thing on a horse is not a man who has been told about it.

The horse had been at the eighth mile both nights and the whole of it came to two shillings and eightpence, at this holding's cost, and it is a line of its own and not of the compost line.

And on the morning of the first of this month he came down onto the flats above Errow Reach with the four bodies of households at the node and the man of about fifty at the steelyard with the book shut, and the man of about seventy-one of Silling, who is the third line and who is not on the rota this morning, had walked up with one man and a barrow, as he has done on the ninth day for nine years, and said one thing to about eleven people before anybody else had spoken, and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-six.

"**The ditch at the second mile below the flats came up on about the twenty-eighth of the month before and it is still up, and it is not rain, and I have walked that ditch twice a week for about nine years and it has not been full at that time of the year in about nine years.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and entered that the count of the times that man has been asked for his name in this holding's book is three and that he has not given it three times, and that the figure of the ditch is his and is not in the four books.

The four bodies of households had asked for this weighing and had sent word on the second and had not sent for anybody, and the reader of this body entered both of those, and entered that the day the launder figure changed and the day of this weighing are the same day, and that four bodies of households had a figure at about the second hour of that morning and did not know what to do with it and did not send for the one man in this fen who might have known, and that this holding did not write the figure and the day on the first and has written them now.

And the eighteenth ninth-day return was weighed at about the second hour of the afternoon, on the steelyard hung on the wall of the building with no roof, and read out by the man of about fifty and read back by him, and then read one at a time by each of the four bodies of households, standing where they could see the dial, and a name went into BY and a name went into WITNESSED BY, and the eighth name-pair in eleven years went in.

**One hundred and forty hundredweight.**

The last four returns were about ninety. Ninety-one, eighty-nine, ninety, ninety-two.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the figure is forty-eight hundredweight more than the last one and that the man of about fifty wrote it down with the hour on it and said nothing about it, and entered that the reader of this body looked at the dial himself afterwards for about four seconds and then wrote the same figure in his own book, and that the two are the same and that two men writing the same figure is not a check.

And Harlan Vetch, of about fifty-eight, who holds the fifth share and has held it eleven years and eight days and who brings it four miles in a barrow in about an hour and a quarter, took the chalk out of his own pocket and stood at the second rule on the board for about nine seconds, and did not write in it, and put the chalk back in his pocket.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

"**I am not writing it because it is not the wet season. I have written that word nine times in eleven years and I wrote it the tenth time at the sixteenth and I am not writing it to-day.**"

"**And I want it said in about eleven people that I left the rule blank and not that I forgot, because a blank is a thing I have to look at every ninth day for the rest of my life, and I would rather look at it than write a word in it that is not true, and that is the whole of my reason and it took me about nine seconds.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and entered that the count of the returns at which he has not written in that rule is two, and that the first of the two is the seventeenth, and that this holding does not know what was in the rule on the seventeenth and is not going to ask.

And the four launders were read out that afternoon and the four bodies of households read them and the reader of this body read them back and the man of about fifty wrote them down in the book in the four-book hand, which is a different hand and is checked against his own about once in four years.

**Nine hundred. Six hundred. Five hundred and seventy-five. Twenty-five.**

Nine hundred and six hundred is fifteen hundred. Fifteen hundred and five hundred and seventy-five is twenty-one hundred. Twenty-one hundred and twenty-five is two thousand a hundred and twenty-five, and the twenty-five is inside somebody's about.

The node's own delivered figure is about twenty-one hundred hundredweight a day. The figure the four bodies of households count that they need is about twenty-one hundred.

**The difference is nothing.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was longer than a minute, and entered that nobody in that yard said anything tender.

And he said the thing about the fourth figure, which he has said before and which he is not going to improve on, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-seven.

"**The twenty-five is inside somebody's about and I said in the third month that I have never been able to make those two numbers agree and I am not going to to-day, and the second time I have said it is the same as the first time and I have counted that, and if I say it a third time in a yard it is going to be a habit and a habit is a number in a column that nobody chose.**"

And then he walked out to the third launder by himself, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and stood at the kerb over the gap.

The gap is about two feet and it is about eleven weeks old and the cut faces are not clean any more, because a saw leaves a face that water takes in a fortnight and this face has been taken in, and the channel under it is the same channel it has always been and it is running, and the standing water in the bottom of it is about nine inches deep and moving at a rate he did not stop to measure.

He did not put a hand in it. He did not put a rule against it. He stood over it for about a minute and a half and then went back up the shoulder.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the minute and a half, and entered that the reader of this body stood over a gap he did not measure in a channel he did not cut and did not repair and **did not put a rule against it, and that a man who puts a rule against a thing in about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the first of a month is a man who is about to have a figure, and that this holding was not going to have that figure that day.** And entered that the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is about two yards wide and about two hundred yards long and is nine feet off the hollow and has been bare eleven years, and that he went past the end of it and did not stop, and that it is the second professional in this holding's history to walk past it and write down that he does not know what it is doing there.

And the reader of this body said the two things he had come five days east to say, and he gave the reason before he gave either of them, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-eight.

"**The third launder is the one that was cut at the kerb on the eleventh of the first month, by about nineteen people, with two saws. It has not been healed and it is not going to be healed this season, and I have not put a hand on it, and I did not cut it, and I was in the yard when it was cut.**"

"**And that is five hundred and seventy-five hundredweight a day going down a channel with a gap in it that a saw made, and the four of us have known it since about the second hour of this morning and not one of us has known where that channel goes, and four bodies of households have signed a document with a figure on the fifth line of it, and I am not going to take that figure back, because the document is four signatures and an objection and a refusal to sign and it is not mine to take anything off.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that the four bodies of households had each been asked for their own figure and had each given it, and that nobody in this yard had been asked where the third launder goes, and that the reader of this body was one of the people who did not ask.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the first of this month, in about eleven people, at the steelyard, on the standing of a figure, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days off: **THE EIGHTEENTH NINTH-DAY RETURN WAS MADE THIS AFTERNOON AND WEIGHED AT ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY HUNDREDWEIGHT AGAINST ABOUT NINETY AT THE LAST FOUR, AND IT WAS READ OUT BY ME AND READ BACK BY ME AND READ BY EACH OF THE FOUR BODIES IN TURN WHERE THEY COULD SEE THE DIAL, AND IT IS THE EIGHTH NAME-PAIR IN ELEVEN YEARS. THE FOUR LAUNDERS MEASURE NINE HUNDRED, SIX HUNDRED, FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIVE, AND TWENTY-FIVE, AND THE NODE'S OWN FIGURE IS ABOUT TWENTY-ONE HUNDRED HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY AGAINST A COUNT OF ABOUT TWENTY-ONE HUNDRED, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS NOTHING. THE THIRD LAUNDER IS THE ONE CUT AT THE KERB ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE FIRST MONTH AND IT IS NOT HEALED. HARLAN VETCH LEFT THE SECOND RULE BLANK AND GAVE HIS REASON OUT LOUD AND HE WAS NOT THANKED. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE READER OF THIS BODY WAS EAST AND WAS NOT IN THE YARD AT THE STANDING ON THE FIRST AND DID NOT ASK FOR THE SHEET.**

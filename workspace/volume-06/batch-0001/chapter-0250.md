# Chapter 250

## Three Columns At One Hour

The fifth of this month was a Saturday and the reader of this body was at the fen gate at about the second hour of the afternoon with about nine people, and a cart came up the road with four hundredweight of boards and a woman walking beside the near wheel, and she was a seedwright of a trust and not of this holding and she was not on the road for this holding and she stopped at about nine feet off and she had a book in her own hand and she gave the reason for stopping before she said anything else.

"**I am not here about the boards and I know I am nine days early for the boards to be a thing to talk about, and I have got four pages out of a route book and I am going to read them standing up in about nineteen people and I am not going to let one word of them be written down, and both of those are said out loud before anybody answers me.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Her name is Tova Reed and the fen entered that she is named, and that he had asked her one question in this holding's book about four months ago and been answered with a no and had not asked twice, and entered that he was not going to ask her anything that day and did not.

And about nineteen people were in the yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and the boards went on behind the cart shed and she read all four pages out standing up in her own hand, and the man of about fifty wrote nothing down, and the woman who does the sums put a hand flat on the table to keep it still and wrote nothing down, and the fen entered that four people in about nineteen were holding themselves still and that the four were not chosen.

> **PAGE ONE, out of a route book, in the hand of the woman who keeps it, read standing up, not copied: CORMEL IN THE SALT MARCH, a village of about two hundred and forty people, about ninety miles off on the road this way. THE GROUND AT CORMEL THAWED AT ABOUT THE SECOND HOUR OF THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-NINTH OF A MONTH THREE MONTHS BACK AND A MAN SOWED AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE SAME MORNING. THERE HAS BEEN NO RAIN IN THAT DISTRICT FOR A SEASON. THE MAN WHO SOWED IS ABOUT SEVENTY-FOUR AND KEEPS A RAIN BOOK THAT WAS HIS FATHER'S.**
>
> **PAGE TWO: THE LONG FUNGAL, on the Thornwild edge, about nine acres of dead willow standing on ground that no office in this holding has ever written to. ELEVEN DAYS OF FRUITING ON IT ON THE SAME DAY, AT ABOUT THE SECOND HOUR. IT SHOULD BE ELEVEN WEEKS. ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY FRUITING BODIES WERE COUNTED ON ONE TREE AND THAT TREE HAS BEEN DEAD ELEVEN YEARS.**
>
> **PAGE THREE: THIS HOLDING'S OWN NODE. THE FLOW WENT UP BY A FIGURE FOR ONE MORNING AND CAME BACK DOWN BY THE MORNING AFTER, AND NOBODY WROTE IT DOWN AT THE TIME, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES THE SUMS HAS THE FIGURE IN HER OWN BOOK AND GAVE IT TO ME ON THE ROAD AND ASKED FOR IT BACK AND I HAVE GIVEN IT BACK.**
>
> **PAGE FOUR, AND IT IS THE OLDEST OF THE FOUR BY NINE WEEKS: OSTERMERE WEIR, IN THE AUBERRON BASIN, about two hundred and eleven miles the other way. THE WINTER WHEAT CAME IN FOUR DAYS EARLY AND IS THE BEST IN NINE YEARS. THE ENTRY IS THE DISTRICT'S OWN OFFICERS' ENTRY AND NOT MINE AND NOT ANYBODY'S ON A ROUTE. IT IS THE REASON I WENT AND GOT THE OTHER THREE.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half.

The boards lay against the north wall of the cart shed by then and the light had come round onto the far side of about eleven faces, and the seedwright read page two twice, because on the first time through she said a number wrong and on the second she said it the other way, and the fen entered that she corrected her own figure in front of about nineteen people without stopping and without asking whether anybody had written the first one down, and entered that nobody had, and that the reason nobody had is not merit and is that four people in that yard were holding themselves still, and that one of the four was the man of about fifty with the book shut in front of him and a pencil he did not put down and did not pick up.

And then the reader of this body did the check, and he did it himself and not with her, and he did it in about eleven people at the seed house table with a nine-year-old chart and a four-year-old book, and he gave the reason for doing it himself before he did it, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-two.

"**I am going to check whether those three places are connected, and I am going to do it myself and not ask you to, because you have got a route book and I have got a chart and a conduit book, and the two of them have not been in the same room, and if I asked you then you would tell me what you know and I would not know whether you had thought of it or whether I had.**"

And he checked it, and it took about an hour, and here is what the check is and it is four things and three of the four are in books.

**THE FOURTH PAGE IS NOT ONE OF THE THREE AND IS NOT COUNTED HERE, and he said that before he began: Ostermere Weir is nine weeks older and it is a different question and it goes on the other set of books.**

**One. This holding and Cormel.** About ninety miles, and the road goes over eleven miles of salt flat and past a salt pan with water in it nine months in twelve, and the flat has had no root of any kind in it within living memory. **A living path does not go through nine miles of that, and he wrote that down in his own hand with the hour on it.**

**Two. This holding and the Long Fungal.** There is no conduit book for it and no chart with a pipe on it, and the ground between is about nine miles of wood and he has walked about four of it. **He could not do this one and he entered that he could not do this one.**

**Three. Cormel and the Long Fungal.** About two hundred and forty miles, and no book of any kind that anybody in this holding holds, and a chart line drawn by a man in the second year of this century that goes along a river on the other side of the country, and the river has been dry at the last four crossings anybody can remember. **The fen entered that a line on a chart drawn by a man who is dead is not a path and is not a channel and is a line, and that a chart is what a man knew, and that the man is not here to be asked.**

**And the check that came out cleanest was the one on the sheet. The fen and the Basin have a lead maintenance conduit between them with a valve about every nine miles, and the last entry on that book is a flushing on the nineteenth of a month seven months back, and the flush went to a standpipe nine miles off and the water in it was put back into the same ground it came out of.**

**And that check is not one of the three, and he entered that he went to it first because it was the one he wanted to be able to use.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the count of things in this holding's book that this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and did not move while he was doing a check that took about an hour, and entered that he checked his own sum twice, because the second hour of the morning of the twenty-ninth of a month three months back is the same hour in three places and he wanted to be sure the word about the hour was his and not a man of about seventy-four's in a village ninety miles off.

And then he said the whole of it in about eleven people, and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-three.

"**Three places. The same day. The same hour of the morning. About nine miles of wood, and about ninety miles with eleven of them of salt flat in it, and about two hundred and forty miles of nothing that anybody in this holding holds a book about, and no living path between any two of them that I can put my hand on, and one of the three I could not do at all.**"

"**And the thing I am not going to say is that this proves somebody did it, because I have got a figure, a distance and an hour and that is three things out of four and the fourth is a person and I have not got a person.**"

"**What I am going to say is the small one and it is the one I can stand behind. A thing that happens at the same hour in three places with no wire between any two of them is not weather. Weather comes from a sky and there is one sky over all three of those places at the same hour and I have got a rain book at Cormel that a man of about seventy-four kept after his father and I have not read it and I am not going to say what is in it in about nineteen people. What I have got is that he sowed at the fourth hour, and a man does not sow in a frost, and that is his book and his ground and not my sky.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that nobody thanked anybody.

And the woman who keeps the seed house asked the one question that anybody asked about a name, and asked it in about nineteen people, and gave the reason for asking it first, and the count of that in this holding's history is two, and she is about seventy-one and is not one of the seven that decide and has never been asked to be.

"**Then what do we call it. I am not asking who. I have read the sheet that came up on the third and there is a word in the fourth line of it and the word is *maintained*, and I have never in seventy-one years heard the word *maintained* put next to a season, and I am asking what the people it is happening to call it, because this holding has a book and everything in the book has a name in it, and I cannot enter *maintained* on a page about our own water and I cannot enter nothing either.**"

And the seedwright of a trust answered it, and she gave the reason before she answered it, and the count of that in this holding's history is one, and she said it in about nineteen people and not to him.

"**We call it the kept weather. I have written it in the margin of a route book about eleven times in four years and I have never once seen it in a sky, and I am not going to tell you what I think it is, because you have had a better hour of it to-day than I have had of anything in nine years, and a person with a better hour of it is not a person whose opinion you want in the middle of your own.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that the reader of this body used neither name out loud in that yard, and that the book on the middle table carries two names for one thing, the first down the ordinary post in an office's hand on the third, and the second brought in on a cart with boards behind it at about the second hour of this afternoon, and that a book which carries two names for a thing is a book that has to carry both of them, and that it is carrying both of them, and that the word season was used in that yard four times in the ordinary sense and was the name of nothing in any of the four.

And the reader of this body said one thing after that, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-four, and nobody thanked anybody.

"**You have brought us four pages and you have not let anybody write them down, and that is right, and I want it said that it is right, and also said that in about four years four people in this holding will have seen those pages and two of them will have forgotten which place was which, and that is nobody's fault and it is going to happen anyway, and a thing that is going to happen anyway is a thing you write down.**"

And then the disagreement happened, and it was not a quarrel and it was not tender, and the reader of this body lost it and did not concede it in a yard.

"**A column of three figures at one hour in three places is a pattern, and a pattern is a maintained thing, and a maintained thing is made, and I can tell you that much to-day.**"

"**It is three observations in a book and it is one person's afternoon, and it is a person whose afternoon it is that makes it an afternoon, and I have walked wet ground for four years and I know what a man's afternoon is worth.**"

"**You are right about the afternoon. I am going to say that once, in a yard, with the reason first, and then I am going to do the thing that says it instead of saying it a second time. I am going to get a second observer who was not on that road and who has never met you, and I am going to ask a professional body for one and not a person, and it is going to cost a body of households money and about four days, and I am not going to ask you for anything at all while I do it, and that is the whole of the concession and it is one I had to make to-day and not one you asked for.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that the count of the times he has conceded a thing out loud in a yard is two, that the first was about a bridge four years ago and this one is about an afternoon, that the two are not the same kind of concession and are one number, and that the count of the rooms with the doors shut in this book's history is one.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fifth of this month, in about eleven people, on the standing of a document, and not by the reader of this body, who was asked to hold the book and did not: **A SEEDWRIGHT OF A TRUST CAME UP THE FEN ROAD WITH FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT OF BOARDS AND FOUR PAGES OUT OF A ROUTE BOOK AND READ ALL FOUR STANDING UP IN ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND DID NOT LET ONE WORD OF THEM BE WRITTEN DOWN, AND BOTH THINGS WERE SAID OUT LOUD BEFORE ANYBODY ANSWERED HER. THE THREE PAGES OF ONE DAY AND ONE HOUR ARE CORMEL IN THE SALT MARCH, WHERE THE GROUND THAWED AT ABOUT THE SECOND HOUR OF THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-NINTH OF A MONTH THREE MONTHS BACK AND A MAN SOWED AT THE FOURTH HOUR IN A DISTRICT THAT HAS HAD NO RAIN FOR A SEASON; THE LONG FUNGAL ON THE THORNWILD EDGE, WHERE ELEVEN DAYS OF FRUITING FELL IN ONE MORNING ON A TREE DEAD ELEVEN YEARS; AND THIS HOLDING'S OWN NODE, WHERE THE FLOW WENT UP BY A FIGURE FOR ONE MORNING AND CAME BACK DOWN. THE FOURTH PAGE IS OSTERMERE WEIR IN THE AUBERRON BASIN, NINE WEEKS OLDER, WHERE THE DISTRICT'S OWN OFFICERS ENTERED A WINTER WHEAT THAT CAME IN FOUR DAYS EARLY AND IS THE BEST IN NINE YEARS. THE READER OF THIS BODY CHECKED THE THREE CONNECTIONS HIMSELF AGAINST A NINE-YEAR-OLD CHART AND A FOUR-YEAR-OLD CONDUIT BOOK, FOUND NO LIVING PATH BETWEEN ANY TWO OF THE THREE, COULD NOT DO THE SECOND PAIR AT ALL, AND ENTERED ALL THREE OF THOSE AND ONE MORE, BEING THE CONDUIT BETWEEN THIS HOLDING AND THE BASIN, WHICH IS NOT ONE OF THE THREE AND WHICH HE WENT TO FIRST BECAUSE IT WAS THE ONE HE WANTED TO BE ABLE TO USE. HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT A THING WHICH HAPPENS AT THE SAME HOUR IN THREE PLACES WITH NO WIRE BETWEEN ANY TWO OF THEM IS NOT WEATHER, AND THAT HE HAS THREE THINGS OUT OF FOUR. THE SEEDWRIGHT SAID A COLUMN OF THREE FIGURES IS ONE PERSON'S AFTERNOON AND IT WAS TRUE, AND HE SAID SO ONCE IN THE YARD AND WROTE IT IN HIS OWN BOOK AT ABOUT THE SEVENTH HOUR WITH THE DOORS SHUT. THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE SEED HOUSE ASKED WHAT THEY SHOULD CALL IT AND WAS TOLD, AND THE BOOK NOW CARRIES TWO NAMES FOR IT.**

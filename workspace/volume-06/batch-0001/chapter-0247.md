# Chapter 247

## Up Out Of The Ground

The second of this month was a Wednesday and the yard at Errow Reach had about nineteen people in it at about the second hour of the afternoon, which is the hour at which the second line is worked, and which on this day was not worked, because the second line had been worked at the second hour and had gone, and there was about half an hour between that and the hour anybody had arranged to be in this yard.

The four bodies of households were there. The man of about fifty was there with the book. The woman who keeps the four books was there. The man of the north row with the cough was there, and had not been asked to be, and had been at the kerb since about the first hour. The reader of this body was at the wall side with his sleeve pushed back, and the mark on his arm is four inches long and branches twice, and there is no second mark, and it was the first warm day since the turn of the year and about nine of the nineteen people had their coats off.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

And the reader of this body said the thing he had come to say, and he gave the reason before he gave the thing, which is how he has said things in this yard for four years, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-nine.

"**I am going to tell you where the five hundred and seventy-five is coming from and I am telling you my reason first, and the reason is that I have a rule and a figure and a launder that has been cut through since the eleventh of the first month, and the figure on that launder was nothing and it is now five hundred and seventy-five, and a shortage that gets made good on a launder that carries nothing has got to be coming from somewhere, and the only somewhere under this holding is the ground under this holding.**"

"**So I am telling you the water is coming up out of the ground under the Four Elbows, and I am telling you that I have got the third launder and I have got a figure and I have got a need, and the three of them agree with one another and there is one more place it can be coming from and I have been out on the ground four days and I have not found a fourth.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that about four of the nineteen said nothing differently from the other fifteen, and entered that he did not count them and entered the reason, which is that a man who counts the faces in a yard is a man reading a room.

And then the reader of this body said the rest of it, and the rest of it was about himself, and he gave the reason for that first, and the count of the times he has said a thing out loud in a yard and got wrong in this holding's history is four.

"**That sentence is wrong, and the wrong part of it is not the sentence, it is the reason I gave before it.**"

"**A man who gets a sentence wrong has got the sentence wrong. A man who gets his reason wrong has got the whole of it wrong, because a reason is the thing the sentence stands on, and I have been four days telling you what a launder that carries nothing must mean, and the launder that was cut on the eleventh of the first month is not a launder that carries nothing. It is a launder that carries something and has been carrying it since the first, and I have known that the whole time I have been saying the other thing, and I had the number in front of me when I said it.**"

"**So the count of things I have said out loud in a yard and got wrong was three and it is four, and the word on the fourth is *up*. The first two were sums done in a yard and the third was not arithmetic and this one is not arithmetic either, and I am entering it in the same line as the other three and I am not going to tell you what kind of a thing it was.**"

"**And it was available to me on the twentieth of a month two months back and I did not take it, and I have taken it to-day, and I do not know which of those two facts says more about me, and I have not got a figure for either of them and I am not going to make one.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute and a half, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that nobody said anything tender.

And the woman who keeps the four books, Hesta Lyle, who has kept the four books for eleven years and who is not one of the seven that decide and has never been asked to be and was not asked, took the covenant out of her own bag and read the fifth line out loud in about nine seconds, and it is a long line and she read all of it.

> **FIVE. THE FIGURE OF WHAT THIS NODE DELIVERS IS ABOUT FIFTEEN HUNDRED HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY AND THE FIGURE OF WHAT FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS COUNT THAT THEY NEED IS ABOUT TWO THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS SIX HUNDRED HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY, AND THAT FIGURE IS THE SAME FIGURE IT WAS BEFORE THIS SHEET WAS DRAWN, AND THIS SHEET DOES NOT CHANGE IT.**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about a minute, and entered that the reading took about nine seconds and that nobody had asked her to read it and that she read it anyway, and entered that the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is four and it did not move this afternoon, and entered the reason it did not move, which is that she had read a line and not said a thing, and that a line is in a book and a thing is in a head and only one of the two is hers.

And then the man of the north row with the cough said the thing he had come to say, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-nine.

"**I am going to say a thing about the saw and I am saying it in about nineteen people and not to one man, and the reason is that a yard where a man has said one false thing ought to have one true thing in it and not two.**"

"**I held the far end of the frame on the eleventh of the first month. I put my knee on the kerb stone to hold it and the stone moved, about a finger, and the saw came down a bit off and the cut is about two feet because of me and not because of the saw, and I have not said that to anybody in about nine weeks, and I am not asking for anybody to say anything about it, and I am not going to be thanked and you may tell the yard I said so.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered the length of it, which was about eleven seconds, and entered that he was not thanked and said not to be, and entered that the count of yards the man of the north row with the cough has stood in since the first of a month four months back is eighteen and has moved, and that he was not asked to be in any of the eighteen and has said eight things in eighteen yards and that two of the eight are now figures about a stone and a saw and neither of those is a feeling and both of them are in a book.

And then the four bodies of households went and looked under the node, because that is where the reader of this body had told them the water was coming from, and they were at the flat of the ground inside the eleven acres and about nine feet off the hollow by about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and they put the auger down in four places, and the four places were worked out among themselves over about an hour and a half and the reader of this body was told where they were going to put it and did not choose a place.

Before they went, the man of about fifty had the book open at the page where the four launders are described, and the description of the third launder stops at the kerb, and it has stopped at the kerb since the eleventh of the first month, because that is the day it was cut and nobody has added a line since, and the reader of this body looked at that page for about a minute and neither of them said anything, and the fen entered that a book which stops at a cut is a book that describes a thing that ends, and that the ending is real and was made in this yard in about nineteen people with two saws, and that the man of about fifty has kept that book for eleven years and did not write one line under the cut, and that the reason he did not write a line under it is not known to anybody in this yard including the man of about fifty.

And the licensed man, Coll Renner, who is on the roll for the Tarrow district and who came at his own cost in the second month of the year and is not on the roll for the district this node stands in, was in that yard and had been in it since the first hour and had not been asked for anything, and he said the one thing he has ever said in this yard, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is nine.

"**This node is about nine miles inside a district that is not the Tarrow district, and the roll for the district it is in has two names on it, and I have known that for eleven years and I have not said it, and I am saying it now because a man has been in this yard telling four bodies of households where their water comes from and he is not on either roll and I am, and that is the whole of what a licensed man is for and I have not been doing it.**"

"**I am not making anything of it and I am not going to say what follows from it, because what follows from it is a matter for an office and not for a yard of about nineteen people, and I have not written to the office and I am not going to, and if somebody in this yard writes to the office I would rather be told than find out.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the reader of this body did not write to any office that night and has not written to any office since, and entered that the count of the times he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and this was not a fifth because nothing he said was wrong, and entered that the fen has checked both of the two things that man said and that both of them are true and that neither of them is anybody's fault.

The ground was wet at about two feet in all four of them and it was the same water in all four of them and the man of about fifty wrote down the depth at each and read it back and the four of them agreed, and the reader of this body got into the second of the holes and put his hand flat on the side of it at about two feet and could not tell whether that water was coming up or going down.

He said so. He said it out loud to about nine people at the side of a hole, and he gave the reason first, and it is in this book in his own hand with the hour on it, and it is the whole of what a man in a hole at two feet can be allowed to know.

"**A man's hand in wet ground at two feet cannot tell the direction of the water in it, and I have wanted it to for about four days, and wanting a thing an instrument cannot do is the first half of getting a thing wrong and the second half is saying it in a yard.**"

"**The instrument that can tell me is the rule, and the rule goes on the launder, and the launder is the thing I have spent four days telling you is not carrying anything. So I am not going to put the rule on it this afternoon, and I am going to tell you why I am not going to, and it is not humility and it is not caution, it is that if I put a rule on that channel at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and it comes out at five hundred and seventy-five, then in about nine seconds I will have a figure that agrees with my sentence, and two figures that agree with a sentence is not a check. It is a man and his own opinion holding hands.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that the reader of this body was down in that hole for about eleven minutes and came up out of it and did not say one more thing out loud that day, and that the count of the times he has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is four and it did not go to five in the afternoon, and that the not going to five was available to him about four times in about two hours and that he is not going to say how many times because he stopped counting them on the twenty-seventh of the month before in a yard in about nineteen people and has not said that number out loud since and is not going to to-day.

> Entered by the man of about fifty, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, in a yard at Errow Reach and afterwards at the node, in about nineteen people and then about nine, on the standing of a statement and of a figure, and not by the clerk, who is at this holding and is five days off: **THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID IN ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE THAT THE FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT A DAY IS COMING UP OUT OF THE GROUND UNDER THE FOUR ELBOWS, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND THE WRONG PART OF IT WAS THE REASON AND NOT THE SENTENCE, AND HE SAID SO OUT LOUD HIMSELF BEFORE ANYBODY SAID IT TO HIM. THE COUNT OF THINGS HE HAS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS THREE AND IT IS FOUR, AND THE WORD ON THE FOURTH IS UP. THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE FOUR BOOKS READ THE FIFTH LINE OF THE COVENANT OUT WHOLE IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS AND NOBODY ASKED HER, AND IT IS A TRUE LINE AND IT IS NOT A TRUE LINE ANY MORE, AND IT IS A LINE ON A DOCUMENT THAT FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAVE SIGNED. THE MAN OF THE NORTH ROW WITH THE COUGH SAID THAT THE KERB STONE MOVED UNDER HIS KNEE ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE FIRST MONTH AND THAT THE CUT IS ABOUT TWO FEET BECAUSE OF HIM AND NOT BECAUSE OF THE SAW, AND HE SAID IT IN ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND NOT TO ONE MAN, AND HE WAS NOT THANKED AND SAID NOT TO BE. THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS PUT THE AUGER DOWN IN FOUR PLACES THEY CHOSE THEMSELVES AND THE GROUND WAS WET AT ABOUT TWO FEET IN ALL FOUR AND IT WAS THE SAME WATER IN ALL FOUR, AND THE READER OF THIS BODY WAS IN THE SECOND OF THE HOLES AND COULD NOT TELL WHETHER THE WATER WAS COMING UP OR GOING DOWN, AND HE DID NOT PUT THE RULE ON THE THIRD LAUNDER, AND THE REASON HE GAVE FOR NOT PUTTING IT ON IT IS ENTERED IN HIS OWN HAND AND IS IN THIS BOOK.**

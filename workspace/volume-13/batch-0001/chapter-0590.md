# Chapter 590

## The Twenty-Third Of The Fifteenth, And Water Over The Stones At The Low End

Rain in the night and hard from the fourth hour, and water over the stones at the low end of the middle road, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road within a finger of its lip after a fortnight of standing a foot down, and cloud breaking by seven and sun by nine.

The clerk of this holding read the launder figure at the ninth hour with rain still on his coat, and Marek heard him from the doorway of the one room and wrote nothing after him.

**One thousand and one hundred and thirty-seven hundredweight.**

Thursday took five off Wednesday, and the three hundred and fifty-third figure of a run that has never repeated itself stands against the launder this morning, and the fall came on an odd morning as it always does.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the two halves from the men at the wall before the clerk had them, and gave them to him with the addition already done, and would not let him thank her for it.

A hundred and sixty-five mornings one way and a hundred and fifty-five the other, and the two come to three hundred and twenty, and she said the total once and put her book face down so that nobody could copy it wrong.

The run of mornings was read at the seventh hour out of the second book where it goes, of a month nine months back, and Thursday is an odd morning and the run is read on odd mornings, and the heading has not moved since the first of this month.

**One hundred and forty-seven mornings of two hundred and seventy-seven.**

The man who reads the schedule off the wall came nine miles in wet boots and stood at the trough a long time without touching it, and then came to the yard and asked for the clerk by function and not by name, and gave the reason first.

**"That trough is not on the schedule and is not in the maintenance line and is fed off a spur that is on the sheet, and I have checked it twice in my own time and put the checking nowhere, and I have no standing and no column."**

He kept his hands out of his pockets while he said it.

**"I am saying it in a yard in front of four people because I said nothing about it for three weeks on a wet road, and a gate shut on a quarter of an inch does not need a line and a trough draining does."**

Nia Vale said about six of the nine places would have gone down in a fortnight like that, and that nobody in any of the six had said one word about a trough to anybody, and that she was not going to be the woman who said it to a body four counties off.

**"Six places and six troughs and six gates, and this holding has one of each and keeps no column for any of them, and that is not an oversight and it is not a decision either."**

She turned away from the trough.

**"Doing it differently is the only thing any of them has that a schedule cannot take off them, and four of the nine have been done differently this month on purpose."**

Three hundred and fifty-one days stand on the sheet that carries the four bodies of households, and the clerk showed the clause to Marek without being asked, being the three hundred and fiftieth out of three hundred and fifty-second, one under and one over, and a different figure from the launder and never added to it.

Seven hundred and thirty-seven days is on the near board and seven hundred and eighty-four on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut through the rain and stands shut now.

The man of about fifty mended a hinge on the low gate with a nail and a stone, and said he had not told anybody he was going to do it and was not telling anybody he had done it, and that it was not a decision anybody had to live with.

**"A nail and a stone and half an hour, and the gate will shut now and will not shut in winter, and I am not putting that in any book."**

He put the stone back where he found it.

**"I have got nothing to carry this month and I am available, and the barrow is against the wall with eleven journeys on it, and eleven is a floor and the twelfth is not coming in this month."**

There are three sheets on the long table. The first is fifty days old and came at the ninth hour of the second of last month and lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been moved and has not been answered and has not been refused.

The second lies folded with its fold turned the other way and has not been opened, from a council of about nineteen households, on the twenty-eighth, saying nothing.

The third lies at the far end and is correct in every particular, with a name at the head and a date and a hand at the foot and a seal, and the man who carried it said out loud that he did not write it.

No column has been ruled for any of the three, and the requests and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and fifty-three.

Sera Quill came to the yard at about the tenth hour with her hands behind her back and read all three sheets standing up, and said what she was before she said anything else.

**"I am a witness to records and not a witness to anything that happened, and I have not been in the outer office since the winter, and I am not going to check anything in front of people."**

She looked at the second sheet without touching it.

**"Two sheets off the same schedule and I am not saying they are the same document, and a seal means wax was pressed and not that a signature was made, and those are four things that can be checked and one that cannot."**

Marek listened from the step of the one room and said one thing about her manner and not about the sheets, and gave the reason first.

**"She told us the four things that can be checked and the one that cannot, and being useful about paper means saying one thing more than can be checked, and I am not asking her for the one more."**

He went back inside.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument in four counties says whether it is on or off. The comfort is not standing and the father is not alive in the wood and the day does not come back."**

The clerk said the ages at the middle table because the ages are derived and not carried, and he derived them out loud so that two people heard him do it.

**"Two hundred and forty-seven days on the form and not filled and not refused, a hundred and fifty-three on the charter, two hundred and forty-one on the second line in Silling's book with nothing on it."**

He shut the middle book.

**"Four ruled lines stand bare under two words and the answer is not due, and the third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty."**

Seven sessions entered and no eighth, six rows ruled for what is not known and the seventh not ruled, fifteen lines in the use log and the sixteenth not written, sixty-six on the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off and not read this morning and not going back, twenty-nine fetchings on the man of about seventy and he was not fetched and nobody asked him which number.

Paid at twenty-eight on the compost line and not discharged in twenty-eight, and it fell because an interval carried it, and the ring of bare ground got no foot and no figure and was not added to the low field, and the offer on the low board was not taken up and not withdrawn and not moved, and nobody climbed a rung and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder.

Ditch water running full and clear past a trough no schedule has, and three sheets lying still, and nobody thanked anybody.

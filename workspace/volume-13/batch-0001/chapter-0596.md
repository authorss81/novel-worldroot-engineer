# Chapter 596

## The Twenty-Ninth Of The Fifteenth, And A Spanner Asked For Out Loud

Bright hard sun at seven and a westerly by nine, and the frame worked, and the middle road hard at the top by nine and soft at the bottom by ten, and standing water in the cart ruts for the first time since the beginning of the month gone by noon.

The clerk read the launder at nine with the book open on the wall, and Marek checked the fall against the day and found it where the rule said it would be.

**One thousand and one hundred and forty-six hundredweight.**

Wednesday took five off Tuesday, and the three hundred and fifty-ninth figure of a run without a repeat stands this morning, and the fall on an odd morning holds.

The halves were a hundred and sixty-eight one way and a hundred and fifty-eight the other, three hundred and twenty-six together, and the addition held and the look was not the test.

The morning run was read at seven out of the second book, of a month nine months back, and the heading has not moved since the first of this month and will not move before the turn.

**One hundred and fifty mornings of two hundred and eighty-three.**

A man with a barrow asked the clerk for a spanner out loud in the yard and was given one, and neither of them said the word for what it was for, and the reason was given first, and four other people heard it and none of them wrote it down.

**"I need it for half an hour and I am asking out loud because asking quiet makes it a favor, and a favor in a yard becomes a number."**

He took the spanner and turned it over once in his hand before he used it, which is what a man does with a tool he has been given rather than one he owns.

**"Eleven journeys on this barrow and eleven a floor, and the twelfth is not coming this month, and I am available and that is not a figure."**

The clerk logged nothing and said the cost out loud, because taking the use log off its shelf twice in ten mornings and writing neither time has a cost and he says it both times.

**"Fifteen lines and the sixteenth not written, and I took it down for a stone and for a spanner and wrote neither, and the cost is that a guess in a book eleven years kept would outlive us."**

He put the log back under its weight.

**"A man with nothing to carry asked out loud and was given, and that is the whole of it."**

Three hundred and fifty-seven days stand on the sheet that carries the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath that figure takes the three hundred and fifty-sixth out of three hundred and fifty-eighth, one under and one over, and the launder is never added to it.

The two boards on the long wall read seven hundred and forty-three days and seven hundred and ninety, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board has been shut at every hour of this morning and of every morning of this month.

Three sheets lie on the long table, the first fifty-six days old and face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, the second folded from the twenty-eighth and unopened, the third carrying a name and a date and a seal and correct in every particular, none of the three entered or answered or refused, and the requests and the section-nine notes standing at fifty-three and fifty-three with nine inches of board between them and nowhere to put a figure.

Warden Halden Pike came to the yard with inspection talk and no paper, and said he retires from old vetoes and teaches with a local veto now, and gave the reason first for saying it in a yard.

**"A blanket veto kept a trough off a schedule, and I withdrew it in public and not in a room, and I am teaching the checking now."**

He looked at the trough line nowhere.

**"Practical and not forgiving, and testimony from opposite sides can agree without either forgiving the other."**

Marek heard him and said nothing about forgiving, because this holding does not make that sentence, and stood in the yard until the man had gone and did not say either of the two standings, though the spanner was still in the borrower's hand and there were five people there to hear them.

**"A man asks a clerk for a spanner out loud and is given one, and that happened, and it happened in front of five people, and I would not put a loss on the end of it to make the morning weigh more."**

He looked at the spanner in the borrower's hand and left it there.

**"The two things I could say are not about a borrowed tool and I am not going to find a way to make them about one."**

The clerk derived the ages at the middle table with two people listening and the form out of the rack.

**"Two hundred and fifty-three days on the form, a hundred and fifty-nine on the charter, two hundred and forty-seven on Silling's line, empty and not refused."**

He straightened the sheets on the table, which he was not permitted to do and did.

**"Four bare lines and the answer not due, terms ruled and empty, seven sessions in this holding's book and no eighth entered, six not knowns and no seventh row ruled for them."**

He took the use log off its shelf twice and wrote on it neither time, and it is at fifteen lines and the sixteenth is not written, and eleven journeys are on the barrow with eleven a floor. Sixty-six is on the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off and does not go back. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched. Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and is not discharged in twenty-eight.

The ring got no foot and no figure. The offer is undated and unmoved. No rung was climbed and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder. No column is ruled under any of the three sheets, and nobody counted the days on the letter out loud.

Nia Vale oiled the barrow wheel with a rag and spun it twice, and it went round true both times and stopped where she let it, and she said true running is not a journey and that the handles stand turned in against the wall where they have stood for four years.

**"Spun twice and true, and eleven journeys and a floor, and spinning is not carrying."**

She put the rag away and turned the barrow so the handles faced the wall again.

**"Available and against a wall, and twelfth not coming this month, and that is standing and not going."**

Harlan Vetch tried the spanner on a nut at the bench and handed it back, and said the nut was sound and the thread was clean, and that sound and clean need no entry, and he put his thumb on the nut a second time before letting go of it.

The borrower wiped the spanner on his sleeve and returned it to the clerk with both hands, and the clerk put it on the shelf where it lives, and no book was opened for any of it.

The clerk checked the steelyard chain at noon and found it sound, because a chain on a return day carries weight and on other days carries dust, and sound is not a figure. He ran the length of it through a rag and left the rag over the rail.

Marek asked whether the heap had moved since morning, and Harlan Vetch said it had not, because a heap watched does not move while watched and moves in the night, and he had put his stick against it at six and the stick was still leaning.

**"Watched all morning and still, and a foot higher than a fortnight ago in the night, and night work is nobody's column."**

He went back to the wall and marked nothing on the stick, and the stick stayed where it was leaning at the end of the morning.

The woman of about thirty-eight rinsed two cans and set them mouth down on the step, because mouth down keeps dust out and dust in a can sours seed, and she counted them on her fingers without saying the number out loud.

Marek carried the empty basket to the shed and brought it back with twine, because twine wanted in the morning is twine fetched at noon and not entered anywhere.

Light on an open yard with a borrowed spanner in it, a heap that did not move while four men watched it, and three sheets lying where they have lain.

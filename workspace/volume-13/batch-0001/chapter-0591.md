# Chapter 591

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Fifteenth, And A Gate Pin Lying In The Grass

Sun at seven with no cloud and a westerly by nine, and the middle road hard at the top and soft at the bottom, and the pack under the near stone dry both ways for the second time this week.

The clerk read the launder at the wall at nine, and the man of about fifty repeated it back to prove he had heard it right, and neither of them smiled at the size.

**One thousand and one hundred and forty-five hundredweight.**

Friday added eight to Thursday, and the three hundred and fifty-fourth figure of a run without a repeat stands this morning, and the rise came on an even morning as the rule says it must.

Tova Reed heard it from the seed house door and did not look up, because a figure crossing no threshold is not an event in her yard, and she kept sieving.

The halves at the wall were a hundred and sixty-six one way and a hundred and fifty-five the other, and the addition was three hundred and twenty-one, and the older of the two men chalked it and the younger rubbed it out because chalk on that wall does not last till noon.

The morning run was not read, because Friday is an even morning and the half of that run is a half number on an even morning, and the book stayed shut and each man in that yard knows why without being told again.

Coll Renner came down the bank at about the eighth hour with a gate pin in his hand and laid it on the bench, and said he had found it in the grass four miles off, and gave the reason first for bringing it in.

**"A gate shut on a hole a quarter of an inch off true wants this pin and has wanted it since winter, and I am not a man for gates and I am bringing it because I found it where I found it."**

He left the pin on the bench and stepped back.

**"I am not asking for a column for a pin. I am putting a pin on a bench in a yard where four people can see it."**

The woman of about thirty-eight looked at the pin and did not touch it, and said a book with one figure and a deliberate space under it is a book, and that she was choosing a hole and choosing it out loud.

**"A standpipe ran dry for a day and a half in my street and I am not putting that in my own book, and I want it on the page that I am choosing the hole."**

She set her book face down.

**"A book with two figures in it is a ledger, and my book is not a ledger for water this holding does not keep."**

Three hundred and fifty-two days against the four bodies of households, with the clause the three hundred and fifty-first out of three hundred and fifty-third, one under and one over, and Marek checked the arithmetic with his finger and did not copy the figure.

Seven hundred and thirty-eight days on the near board and seven hundred and eighty-five on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour and nobody tried it.

Harlan Vetch held the gate for half an hour with Renn Ashby while the pin was tried and found wanting, and both men agreed the hole wants reaming and neither would ream it without being asked.

**"Half an inch in the winter and the gate will not shut, and two men held it half an hour without speaking, and that is the whole of what happened."**

He handed the pin back.

**"Eleven feet of north wall going in ten years and a pin a quarter of an inch out, and I have known both four years and I am saying both once."**

There are three sheets on the long table, the first fifty-one days old and lying face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, unanswered and unrefused and uncopied and unentered, the second folded and unopened from the twenty-eighth, the third correct in every particular with a name at the head and a date at the foot.

No column ruled for any of them in any book, fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes standing where they stood.

Soren Rill walked the headland and came back with a handful of what the low field had given up, and wrung it out on the step and said he had watched that line nine years and moved with it four times and never known it coming.

**"I go back to the road now, and the districts beyond the maintenance zone have been abandoned four years, and a schedule with a line for a place not kept is not a new paper."**

He said it to the wall and not to any man.

**"I have not read the numbers and I am not a man for numbers, and right numbers are not the same as a right thing."**

Marek stood in the one room doorway and answered neither sentence, because the two are never joined in one sentence in this holding, and said his own lines once.

**"The far end is held by two and neither is me, and no instrument says whether it is on or off. The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, the day does not come back."**

The clerk derived the three ages at the middle table and spoke them plainly for the book.

**"Two hundred and forty-eight days on the form, a hundred and fifty-four on the charter, two hundred and forty-two on Silling's second line with nothing on it, none filled and none refused."**

He closed his hand over nothing.

**"Four bare lines under two words and no answer due, the sheet of terms ruled and empty, seven sessions and no eighth, six not knowns and no seventh row."**

Fifteen lines in the use log and no sixteenth, eleven journeys on the barrow and eleven a floor, sixty-six on the door nine hundred yards off and not read, twenty-nine fetchings and no fetching, paid at twenty-eight and not discharged, no foot in the ring and no figure to it, the offer undated and unmoved, no rung climbed and no figure spoken for either of the two on that ladder.

Hesta Lyle brought a bucket of sharp sand to the bench and set it down beside the pin, and said the sand was for the hole and not for the pin, and that she would not put sand in a hole nobody had asked her to ream.

**"Sand for a hole and not for a pin, and I am leaving it on the bench where four people can see it, and if nobody takes it I will carry it back."**

She left the bucket and went back to the seed house, and the pin lay beside it all morning and neither was touched.

Marek lifted the pin and weighed it in his hand and set it down again, and said a pin found in grass is work waiting and not work done, and that waiting is not a column.

**"Found four miles off and carried in, and tried and found wanting, and that is two mornings of work for half an inch."**

He left the bench to the sand and the pin.

The clerk swept the yard at noon and swept around the bench and not under it, because the bench with a pin and sand on it is not his to move.

Can left on a step with water wrung out beside it, and a pin on a bench, and nobody thanked anybody.

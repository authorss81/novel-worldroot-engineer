# Chapter 597

## The Thirtieth Of The Fifteenth, And The Last Time The Cycle Comes Round This Month

Rain before light, hard and short, and four people on the headland for the ninth time this month, and the boundary stones cold under the hand before light and warm by nine.

The fourth line came round and was worked, and the count in force rose by exactly one and by nothing else, and twenty-nine taken did not move.

A hundred and twenty-nine in force with twenty-nine taken and a hundred not, and the two reckonings at seventy-seventh and sixty-fourth, thirteen apart, and the gap holding four years.

The clerk read the launder at nine with rain on the book cover, and dried the cover on his sleeve before he read, and waited till the four on the headland were listening before he spoke the figure twice.

**One thousand and one hundred and fifty-four hundredweight.**

Eight came off Wednesday, and this is the three hundred and sixtieth figure of a run in which no figure has ever gone in twice, and the rise on an even morning carries the alternation across the end of the month, and Thursday is an even morning and a rise is what an even morning does.

The halves were a hundred and sixty-nine one way and a hundred and fifty-eight the other, three hundred and twenty-seven in all, and the men said it together and neither heard the other for the rain on the cover.

The morning run was not read, because the half is a half number on an even morning, and the book stayed shut and the heading of a month nine months back did not move on the last day of the month either.

Sennik Vaul walked the headland with the clerk and counted the force aloud, and said the sums out loud because the addition is the check, and he kept his stick in his left hand so that his right was free to point at the stones they had found in the morning.

**"Twenty-nine and a hundred is a hundred and twenty-nine, and seventy-seven and sixty-four thirteen apart, and I am not closing the thirteen with a guess."**

He put his stick down and stood on the wet ground with his boot going through the surface about an inch, and did not move to firmer ground.

**"This is the last time the cycle comes round inside this month, and I am not writing a figure in the margin where the blanks are counted, because that page opens for a load and not for a day."**

The woman of about thirty-eight said she noticed and would not draw anything out of the two together in a yard with six people in it, and that eleven feet of wall going is a thing going and not a figure.

**"Going in ten years and known four years, and said once because there was nobody to tell it to who would put it in a column."**

She went back to the tap.

**"A dry standpipe for a day and a half I chose to leave out, and I am choosing it still, and a hole chosen out loud is a decision."**

Three hundred and fifty-eight days stand on the four bodies sheet, and the clause under that number takes the three hundred and fifty-seventh out of three hundred and fifty-ninth, one under and one over, and the launder and that sheet are two figures that have never been added together in this holding.

On the long wall the near board reads seven hundred and forty-four days and the far board reads seven hundred and ninety-one, and the gap between them is forty-seven and has been forty-seven for four years, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning.

There are three sheets on the long table. The first is fifty-seven days old and lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, the second is folded and unopened, and the third carries a name and a date and a seal and is correct in every particular. The clerk looked at all three at noon without touching any of them, because looking is not entering, and none of the three was moved or answered or refused, and the requests and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and fifty-three with no column ruled for any of them.

Tova Reed stood at the table without touching any sheet and said she has now said the folded paper line in three yards and is going to stop, because a thing said three times is in people and next time there will be a number.

**"Carried folded for a month because my coat is not a place for paper, and not opened because opening in a room makes it that room's business."**

She stepped back.

**"Four of nine done differently on purpose, and doing differently is the only thing a schedule cannot take, and I am not saying that to a body four counties off."**

Marek stood with his back to the table on the last morning of the month and did not say either of the two things, and the woman of about thirty-eight saw him not say them and said one line about it and no more.

**"You have said them every morning since the second and you have not said them today, and I am not going to be the woman who asks which of the two you have stopped saying."**

He put his hand on the back of a chair and left it there, and said the second of the two things to the shelf where the slates are kept, because the shelf was the only thing in the yard facing away from everybody, and the four people on the headland had come in by then and were standing at the door.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

He straightened two slates that were not crooked and left it at that.

The clerk gave the ages derived at the middle table, with the books open and two people listening, because derivation is the rule and carrying a figure forward is not allowed in this holding.

**"Two hundred and fifty-four days on the form, a hundred and sixty on the charter, two hundred and forty-eight on Silling's line, and all three empty."**

He laid his hand flat on the table and lifted it again without touching the form.

**"Four bare lines with the answer not due, the third column of the terms ruled and empty, seven sessions entered and no eighth, six not knowns and no seventh row ruled, and the sessions are not this morning and nobody is in a room."**

Four people came in off a wet headland at about the tenth hour and not one of them went near the use log, which stands at fifteen lines, the sixteenth not written and not written this morning by a man who had a barrow and a reason. The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. Sixty-six is on the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off and does not go back. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched. Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and is not discharged in twenty-eight.

The ring was not walked and not measured and has no figure against it. The offer is undated and unmoved. No rung was climbed and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder. Nothing was added to anything.

Sera Quill copied nothing and witnessed the counting, and said the force counted aloud is better than counted silent, because aloud can be heard wrong and corrected, and silent can only be wrong.

**"Aloud and heard by six, and thirteen still thirteen, and a margin with no figure in it because the page opens for a load."**

She stood back from the wall.

**"Four things checked and one not, and wax pressed and a hand at the foot, and that is the whole of what paper can say."**

The man of about fifty retied his bootlace on the step and said the headland was soft where the rain had stood, and that soft ground takes a boot deeper morning than evening.

Marek stood the force book upright to dry the cover, because rain on a cover dries and rain in a book spreads, and the book stayed shut while it dried.

The clerk checked the boards at noon and found the pair forty-seven apart as always, because forty-seven is the gap and the gap is not a question, and he put his thumb on the near board under the figure and left a mark in the dust that nobody brushed off for the rest of the day.

Marek asked whether any sheet wanted moving before rain, and Tova Reed said none did, because sheets under a roof are dry and dry is enough, and she said it while standing at the seed-house door with her sleeves rolled and did not come over to look.

**"Under cover and face up, and nine inches between each, and rain before light and none at noon."**

He left them where they lay and did not touch the edge of the first one, which had lifted a corner overnight and settled back on its own weight.

The woman of about thirty-eight swept the step and shook the mat over the yard, because dust on a step walks in and dust in a room settles on paper, and the grit she shook off went onto the wet flagstones and stayed there until the sun took it.

Marek stacked two slates on the shelf with the chalk between them, because chalk kept between slates does not snap and a snapped chalk writes thick, and the third slate was already on the shelf and he left it where it was.

Nia Vale fetched water for the headland party and shared it round in one cup, because one cup shared is quicker than four cans carried and nobody counts mouthfuls, and the four of them had come in with their hands too cold to hold the cup the first time and shared it a second time without anybody saying so.

Roof drip stopping by ten over a counted force, four people on a headland with cold hands, and three sheets quiet under a roof that does not leak.

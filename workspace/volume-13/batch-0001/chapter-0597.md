# Chapter 597

## The Thirtieth Of The Fifteenth, And The Last Time The Cycle Comes Round This Month

Rain before light, hard and short, and four people on the headland for the ninth time this month, and the boundary stones cold under the hand before light and warm by nine.

The fourth line came round and was worked, and the count in force rose by exactly one and by nothing else, and twenty-nine taken did not move.

A hundred and twenty-nine in force with twenty-nine taken and a hundred not, and the two reckonings at seventy-seventh and sixty-fourth, thirteen apart, and the gap holding four years.

The clerk read the launder at nine with rain on the book cover, and dried the cover on his sleeve before he read, and waited till the four on the headland were listening before he spoke the figure twice.

**One thousand and one hundred and fifty-four hundredweight.**

Thursday added eight to Wednesday, and the three hundred and sixtieth figure of a run with no repeat stands this morning, and the rise on an even morning keeps the alternation across the month end.

The halves were a hundred and sixty-nine one way and a hundred and fifty-eight the other, three hundred and twenty-seven in all, and the men said it together and neither heard the other for the rain on the cover.

The morning run was not read, because the half is a half number on an even morning, and the book stayed shut and the heading of a month nine months back did not move on the last day of the month either.

Sennik Vaul walked the headland with the clerk and counted the force aloud, and said the sums out loud because the addition is the check.

**"Twenty-nine and a hundred is a hundred and twenty-nine, and seventy-seven and sixty-four thirteen apart, and I am not closing the thirteen with a guess."**

He put his stick down.

**"This is the last time the cycle comes round inside this month, and I am not writing a figure in the margin where the blanks are counted, because that page opens for a load and not for a day."**

The woman of about thirty-eight said she noticed and would not draw anything out of the two together in a yard with six people in it, and that eleven feet of wall going is a thing going and not a figure.

**"Going in ten years and known four years, and said once because there was nobody to tell it to who would put it in a column."**

She went back to the tap.

**"A dry standpipe for a day and a half I chose to leave out, and I am choosing it still, and a hole chosen out loud is a decision."**

Three hundred and fifty-eight days on the four bodies sheet, clause the three hundred and fifty-seventh out of three hundred and fifty-ninth, one under and one over, never added to the launder.

Seven hundred and forty-four near and seven hundred and ninety-one far, forty-seven apart, drawer shut.

There are three sheets on the long table, the first fifty-seven days old face up with about nine inches of bare board either side, the second folded unopened, the third correct with name and date and seal, none moved and none answered and none refused.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three notes, no column ruled.

Tova Reed stood at the table without touching any sheet and said she has now said the folded paper line in three yards and is going to stop, because a thing said three times is in people and next time there will be a number.

**"Carried folded for a month because my coat is not a place for paper, and not opened because opening in a room makes it that room's business."**

She stepped back.

**"Four of nine done differently on purpose, and doing differently is the only thing a schedule cannot take, and I am not saying that to a body four counties off."**

Marek stood with his back to the table and said the standings whole and unsoftened and once.

**"The far end is held by two and neither is me, and no instrument says on or off. The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, the day does not come back."**

The clerk gave the ages derived at the middle table, with the books open and two people listening, because derivation is the rule and carrying a figure forward is not allowed in this holding.

**"Two hundred and fifty-four on the form, a hundred and sixty on the charter, two hundred and forty-eight on Silling's line, empty."**

He laid his hand flat and lifted it.

**"Four bare lines, terms empty, seven sessions, six not knowns, fifteen in the log, eleven journeys, sixty-six on the door, twenty-nine fetchings, paid at twenty-eight."**

Ring unwalked and unmeasured, offer unmoved, no rung climbed, no figure for either ladder person, no foot and no figure added.

Sera Quill copied nothing and witnessed the counting, and said the force counted aloud is better than counted silent, because aloud can be heard wrong and corrected, and silent can only be wrong.

**"Aloud and heard by six, and thirteen still thirteen, and a margin with no figure in it because the page opens for a load."**

She stood back from the wall.

**"Four things checked and one not, and wax pressed and a hand at the foot, and that is the whole of what paper can say."**

The man of about fifty retied his bootlace on the step and said the headland was soft where the rain had stood, and that soft ground takes a boot deeper morning than evening.

Marek stood the force book upright to dry the cover, because rain on a cover dries and rain in a book spreads, and the book stayed shut while it dried.

The clerk checked the boards at noon and found the pair forty-seven apart as always, because forty-seven is the gap and the gap is not a question.

Marek asked whether any sheet wanted moving before rain, and Tova Reed said none did, because sheets under a roof are dry and dry is enough.

**"Under cover and face up, and nine inches between each, and rain before light and none at noon."**

He left them where they lay.

The woman of about thirty-eight swept the step and shook the mat over the yard, because dust on a step walks in and dust in a room settles on paper.

Marek stacked two slates on the shelf with the chalk between them, because chalk kept between slates does not snap and a snapped chalk writes thick.

Nia Vale fetched water for the headland party and shared it round in one cup, because one cup shared is quicker than four cans carried and nobody counts mouthfuls.

Roof drip stopping by ten over a counted force, and three sheets quiet, and nobody thanked anybody.

# Chapter 589

## The Twenty-Second Of The Fifteenth, And A Return Standing A Quarter Of An Hour

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, which is the gate, and the gate on the low field stood open a hand's width at seven and shut hard by eight with nobody near it, and the middle road held firm at the top through the ninth hour and went soft at the bottom after ten, and water stood in the cart ruts in a thin skin that broke under a boot.

The clerk of this holding set the book on the long wall before light and kept his pencil behind his ear until the ninth hour, when he took it down and read the figure standing up, and four people heard him and none of them wrote it after him.

**One thousand and one hundred and forty-two hundredweight.**

Wednesday came off Tuesday by eight and the three hundred and fifty-second figure of a run in which no figure has ever gone in twice is what the launder carries this morning, and the run has held four years without a repeat and without a gap at a month turn.

Marek stood at the wall with his hands in his coat pockets and heard the clerk read, and nodded once because the rise was due on an even morning, and said nothing about the size of it because a figure is not a season in his mouth.

The two men at the wall gave the halves together and the addition held, being a hundred and sixty-five mornings one way and a hundred and fifty-four the other, and the two came to three hundred and nineteen, and the younger of the two laughed once because the hundred had turned and nobody had marked it.

Nia Vale had chalked the total on the back of a slate for her own book and rubbed it out with her sleeve, and said the crossing was yesterday's business and not this morning's, and that she was not going to make a district out of a wall.

The run of mornings was not read this morning, and the second book stayed shut under its weight, because Wednesday is an even morning and the half of that run is a half number on an even morning, and the heading where it goes is of a month nine months back and has not moved since the first of this month.

Soren Rill came up the middle road at about the eighth hour with mud to his ankles and said the road had changed under him twice between the gate and the yard, and that he had watched it go and had put the watching nowhere.

**"I have walked that road nine mornings and it has been hard at the top and soft at the bottom on all nine, and this morning it went soft an hour later than yesterday, and there is no book in four counties with a line for an hour."**

He knocked the mud off his boots against the step and left it there.

**"I am not asking for a line. I am saying it out loud in a yard so that four people heard me say that I am not asking."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden filled a can at the tap and set it down without spilling, and answered him from the tap and not from the table, and her voice carried across the yard.

**"Right numbers are not a right thing, and an hour is a number, and I have watched two counties find that out a year after everybody congratulated itself."**

She picked the can up again.

**"I am not saying your hour is wrong. I am saying an hour kept by one man on a road is his hour, and his hour is not a column."**

The sheet in this building that carries the four bodies of households lay open on the middle table with the morning light across it, and the clerk turned it to the light so the figure could be seen, and Marek looked at it because it was there and not because he had asked for it.

Three hundred and fifty days stand against the four bodies, and the clause under that figure takes the three hundred and forty-ninth of those mornings out of three hundred and fifty-first, one under and one over, and the launder and that sheet are never added to one another in any book in this holding.

Seven hundred and thirty-six days is on the near board and seven hundred and eighty-three days is on the far one, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning and nobody put a hand to it.

The fourth line stood at a hundred and twenty-seven in force with twenty-nine taken and ninety-eight not, and the two reckonings that never agree stood at seventy-fifth and sixty-second, thirteen apart, and nobody in this building has ever found out what the thirteen counts.

Harlan Vetch walked the headland with the clerk before nine and counted the force with him, and agreed the addition and would not agree the meaning, and said the wall would outlast them both and meant it as comfort.

**"A hundred and twenty-seven in force and twenty-nine taken and ninety-eight not, and the two reckonings thirteen apart, and I have kept that gap four years and I am not going to close it with a guess."**

He put his stick against the wall.

**"I have got eleven feet of top course going on the north wall and I have said so once and I am not saying it twice, and that is a thing going and not a figure."**

The fifty-sixth ninth-day return came up the fen road at the third hour and stood a quarter of an hour and went back, off the steelyard at five hundred and sixty-nine hundredweight, eleven above the last, the forty-sixth pair of names, the two halves reconciled twice and not added, and the second rule on the board left empty again and the count of blanks going thirty-nine to forty on that page and nowhere else.

The clerk said what he says on every return and added one line about the blanks, and said it standing up with the book open, and his pencil did not move while he said it.

**"Fifty-sixth return and forty-sixth pair and five hundred and sixty-nine, and the second rule empty for the fortieth time, and the count goes on the return and not on the day."**

He shut the book.

**"This is the first return of this half of the month and I do not know how many more there will be, and I am not going to fill the rule in the gap, because if it is filled it will be filled by somebody who has been told what it is for."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling stood beside the steelyard with a bar in his hand and gave the reason first before he said the thing, and said he was going up the bank on Friday and was not going to be a name on a thing nobody had asked him about.

**"I carried a folded page in my own book twenty-five years and nobody asked me about it once, and I am not going to be the fifth name on a paper about a bank that four men went over in the winter."**

He set the bar down.

**"I went up last night and I was the fourth and was not named and had a bar in my hand, and that is the whole of what I did and I am not making more of it."**

There are three sheets on the long table. The first of them is forty-nine days old and came up the fen road at the ninth hour of the second of last month and lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, and it has not been answered and has not been refused and has not been copied and has not been entered anywhere.

The second came on the twenty-eighth from a council of about nineteen households and has not been opened and lies with its fold turned the other way.

The third came off a wall nine miles off and is correct in every particular, and it has a person's name at the head of it and a date and a hand at the foot of it and a seal in the corner, and four people have read it and not one has said a word about whether it is right or wrong.

No column has been ruled for any of the three in any of the four books, and the requests and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and fifty-three and have not taken any of them.

Tova Reed worked the seed house from the fifth hour and came to the yard at noon with dust on her sleeves, and stood at the table without touching any sheet, and said one thing about the folded paper and gave the reason first.

**"My coat is not a place for a piece of paper to live in, and I put it down because a coat pocket makes a thing private that nineteen households did not mean to be private, and opening it in a room would make it that room's business."**

She stepped back from the table.

**"I am not opening it this month and I am not asking anybody to open it for me, and that is not a decision about what is in it."**

Marek came out of the one room at about the eleventh hour with his sleeve down and stood with his back to the table, and said the two things whole that no pass may reduce, and said them once and did not soften either.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and I have stopped waiting for the instrument."**

He looked at the gate and not at the table.

**"The comfort is not standing. The father is not alive in the wood. The day does not come back. Neither group of lines is about the sheet on that table and neither cancels it."**

The clerk at the middle table put his hand on the form with four lines and did not write on it, and said the age of it out loud because the age is kept by rule and not by memory.

**"Two hundred and forty-six days old and not filled and not refused, and the charter behind it a hundred and fifty-two, and the second of the four lines in Silling's own book two hundred and forty days with nothing on it."**

He left his hand on the form a moment and took it away.

**"Four ruled lines stand bare under two words in the corner of the one room and the answer they are for is not due, and the third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty."**

Six rows are ruled for what this holding does not know about and no unit has ever stood against any of them, and the seventh row is not ruled, and seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and there is no eighth this morning.

The use log lay under its weight on the shelf with fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth not written, and the barrow stood against the wall with eleven journeys on it and eleven a floor, and the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off stands at sixty-six and does not go back, and the man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched and nobody asked him which number.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and it is not discharged in twenty-eight, and it moved because an interval arrived and not because anybody decided it should, and about nine people in this county found that out at the same hour and not one of them was first.

A ring of bare ground lies inside the eleven acres and no foot went in it this morning and it has no figure against it, and the ground that came back on the low field is a different piece and the two are not added, and the offer on the low board lies undated and is not taken up and not withdrawn and not moved.

Yard dust settling over a shut drawer and three sheets with bare board between them, and a return gone back down the road, and nobody thanked anybody.

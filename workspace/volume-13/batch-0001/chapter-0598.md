# Chapter 598

## The First Of The Sixteenth, And The Month Turn With No Fourth Made Of It

Cloud off the water in the night, hard and bright, wind veering south by the ninth hour, and the month turned in the night without a sound, and the yard lay in the first light of a new month with the same gate and the same road and the same three sheets.

The clerk opened the second book on the first of the month and the heading where the morning run goes stood of a month nine months back and had not moved, and he nodded because a turn resets no count.

**One thousand and one hundred and forty-nine hundredweight.**

Friday took five off Thursday, and the three hundred and sixty-first figure of a run that has never repeated stands this morning, and the fall on an odd morning carries the alternation over the turn.

The halves at the wall were a hundred and sixty-nine one way and a hundred and fifty-nine the other, three hundred and twenty-eight together, and the addition held and three clocks landed on one turn and nobody made a fourth of it.

At seven the morning run was read out of the second book where it goes, of a month nine months back, and Friday is an odd morning for that run, so the run was read, and the heading did not move on the turn.

**One hundred and fifty-one mornings of two hundred and eighty-five.**

Three hundred and fifty-nine days stand on the sheet of the four bodies, and the clause beneath it takes the three hundred and fifty-eighth out of three hundred and sixtieth, one under and one over, and it is a different figure from the launder and is not added to it anywhere in this holding.

The near board of the long wall reads seven hundred and forty-five days and the far board reads seven hundred and ninety-two, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour of the old month and was shut again on the first hour of the new one.

At the third hour on the first of the month the fifty-seventh ninth-day return came up the fen road, stood a quarter of an hour, and went back, and it came off the steelyard at five hundred and eighty hundredweight, which is eleven above the last, the forty-seventh pair of names, the two halves reconciled twice and never added. The second rule in the one room stood empty for the thirty-ninth time. The count of blanks on that board is thirty-nine and did not move on that page or on any other.

The clerk said what he says on every return and said once that three clocks landing on one morning at the turn make no fourth, and that he would not fill the rule in the gap.

**"Fifty-seventh return and forty-seventh pair and five hundred and eighty, and the second rule empty again, and thirty-nine on that board where it has stood since the last month turned."**

He shut the book on the new month and put the weight on it.

**"The turn reset no count and the blanks went on the return and not on the day, and if the rule is filled it will be filled by somebody told what it is for."**

The man of about seventy was not fetched on the turn, and nobody asked him which number, and the count of questions put to him stands nil at every site.

**"The gate is open."**

That is what he said on the morning the month turned last time at twenty-nine fetchings, and this morning he said nothing because he was not fetched, and nobody in that yard made a meaning of the quiet.

Nia Vale said the month turned and three clocks landed and a whole yard refused to make a fourth thing out of it, and that nobody is in charge and nobody has been asked to be.

**"Thirty days and a new first, and the launder and the window and the sheet all carried over, and not one of them needed us to carry it."**

She swept the step.

**"A great many people are looking after what came back and not one was put there, and the evidence is a gate and a trough and a stone and a heap and a column and a book with a space in it."**

There are three sheets on the long table on the first of the month. The first is fifty-eight days old and lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, the second is folded and unopened, and the third carries a name and a date and a seal and is correct in every particular. A new month did not answer any of them or refuse any of them or enter any of them, and the requests and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and fifty-three.

Marek walked to the table and did not touch any sheet, and looked at the bare board between them and said nothing about grouping them, because three pieces of paper about a district are not one thing.

**"No column is ruled under any of them in any book, and the letter went into neither column, and a refusal is not what was written."**

He turned his back to it and said the first of the two to the door of the one room on the first morning of a month, as though the date were the reason, and it was not the reason.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off. Thirty days have gone since I said that last and it has not improved in the speaking and it has not got worse either."**

He waited to see whether anybody would put a month against it, and nobody did, and the clerk wrote the day on the board and not the sentence.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back. Thirty days have gone and not one of those three has moved, and that is what a month is for."**

The clerk derived the ages on the new month and spoke them for the record with the form out of the rack and the charter behind it.

**"Two hundred and fifty-five days on the form and not filled, a hundred and sixty-one on the charter, two hundred and forty-nine on Silling's second line with nothing on it."**

He set the form straight against the edge of the table.

**"Four bare lines and the answer not due, terms ruled and empty, seven sessions and no eighth, six not knowns and no seventh row ruled, fifteen lines in the use log and no sixteenth."**

The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. The seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off is unread and does not go back from sixty-six. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings. Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and is not discharged in twenty-eight.

Not one foot went into the ring of bare ground this morning and no figure stands against it, and the ground that came back on the low field is a different piece and the two were not added. The offer on the low board is undated and unmoved. Nobody climbed a rung and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder. The drawer is shut and nobody tallied anything out loud.

Soren Rill walked the ditch after the return went back, and said the ditch ran full and clear on the first of the month as on the last, and that a turn had moved no water.

**"Full and clear yesterday and full and clear today, and thirty days gone and a first come, and the water did not attend the turn."**

He threw a stick in and watched it go.

**"A stick for a boat and a ditch for a river for half a minute, and no column for either, and that is play and not a record."**

Tova Reed hung seed sheets to air on the line, because air on the first of a month is the same air as the thirtieth, and damp in store is the enemy and not the month.

The clerk ruled no line for the new month before noon, because a month an hour old has nothing to rule, and he kept his pencil behind his ear till the ninth hour as always.

Pail of water drawn at seven on a new first and set on a step, a return gone back down the fen road, and a gate open a hand's width with nobody near it.

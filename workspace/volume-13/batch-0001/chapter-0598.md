# Chapter 598

## The First Of The Sixteenth, And The Month Turn With No Fourth Made Of It

Cloud off the water in the night, hard and bright, wind veering south by the ninth hour, and the month turned in the night without a sound, and the yard lay in the first light of a new month with the same gate and the same road and the same three sheets.

The clerk opened the second book on the first of the month and the heading where the morning run goes stood of a month nine months back and had not moved, and he nodded because a turn resets no count.

**One thousand and one hundred and forty-nine hundredweight.**

Friday took five off Thursday, and the three hundred and sixty-first figure of a run that has never repeated stands this morning, and the fall on an odd morning carries the alternation over the turn.

The halves at the wall were a hundred and sixty-nine one way and a hundred and fifty-nine the other, three hundred and twenty-eight together, and the addition held and three clocks landed on one turn and nobody made a fourth of it.

The morning run was read at seven, of a month nine months back, and Friday is an odd morning for this run and the run is read on odd mornings, and the frame did not move on the turn.

**One hundred and fifty-one mornings of two hundred and eighty-five.**

Three hundred and fifty-nine days on the four bodies sheet, clause the three hundred and fifty-eighth out of three hundred and sixtieth, one under and one over, a different figure from the launder and not added anywhere.

Seven hundred and forty-five near and seven hundred and ninety-two far, forty-seven apart, drawer shut at every hour of the old month and shut on the first hour of the new.

The fifty-seventh ninth-day return came up at the third hour on the first of the month and stood a quarter of an hour and went back, off the steelyard at five hundred and eighty hundredweight, eleven above the last, the forty-seventh pair, reconciled twice and not added, second rule empty again and the count of blanks forty to forty-one on that page and nowhere else.

The clerk said what he says on every return and said once that three clocks on one morning at the turn make no fourth, and that he would not fill the rule in the gap.

**"Fifty-seventh return and forty-seventh pair and five hundred and eighty, and the second rule empty for the forty-first time, and the count goes on the return."**

He shut the book on the new month.

**"The turn reset no count and the blanks went on the return and not on the day, and if the rule is filled it will be by somebody told what it is for."**

The man of about seventy was not fetched on the turn, and nobody asked him which number, and the count of questions put to him stands nil at every site.

**"The gate is open."**

That is what he said on the morning the month turned last time at twenty-nine fetchings, and this morning he said nothing because he was not fetched, and nobody in that yard made a meaning of the quiet.

Nia Vale said the month turned and three clocks landed and a whole yard refused to make a fourth thing out of it, and that nobody is in charge and nobody has been asked to be.

**"Thirty days and a new first, and the launder and the window and the sheet all carried over, and not one of them needed us to carry it."**

She swept the step.

**"A great many people are looking after what came back and not one was put there, and the evidence is a gate and a trough and a stone and a heap and a column and a book with a space in it."**

There are three sheets on the long table on the first of the month, the first fifty-eight days old face up with about nine inches of bare board either side, the second folded unopened, the third correct with name and date and seal, none answered and none refused and none entered, fifty-three and fifty-three standing.

Marek walked to the table and did not touch any sheet, and looked at the bare board between them and said nothing about grouping them, because three pieces of paper about a district are not one thing.

**"No column under any of them in any book, and the letter went into neither column, and a refusal is not what was written."**

He turned his back to it.

**"The far end is held by two and neither is me, and no instrument says on or off. The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, the day does not come back."**

The clerk derived the ages on the new month and spoke them for the record.

**"Two hundred and fifty-five on the form and not filled, a hundred and sixty-one on the charter, two hundred and forty-nine on Silling's second line with nothing on it."**

He set the form straight.

**"Four bare lines and no answer due, terms ruled and empty, seven sessions and no eighth, six not knowns and no seventh row, fifteen in the log, eleven journeys, sixty-six on the door, twenty-nine fetchings, paid at twenty-eight."**

The use log unopened, the barrow against the wall, the seventh column unread and not going back, the ring with no foot and no figure and not added to the low field, the offer undated and unmoved, no rung climbed and no figure for either ladder person, drawer shut, no tally and no counting out loud.

Soren Rill walked the ditch after the return went back, and said the ditch ran full and clear on the first of the month as on the last, and that a turn had moved no water.

**"Full and clear yesterday and full and clear today, and thirty days gone and a first come, and the water did not attend the turn."**

He threw a stick in and watched it go.

**"A stick for a boat and a ditch for a river for half a minute, and no column for either, and that is play and not a record."**

Tova Reed hung seed sheets to air on the line, because air on the first of a month is the same air as the thirtieth, and damp in store is the enemy and not the month.

The clerk ruled no line for the new month before noon, because a month an hour old has nothing to rule, and he kept his pencil behind his ear till the ninth hour as always.

Pail of water drawn at seven on a new first and set on a step, and a return gone back down the road, and nobody thanked anybody.

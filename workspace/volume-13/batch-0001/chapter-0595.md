# Chapter 595

## The Twenty-Eighth Of The Fifteenth, And A Stone Back In Its Bed Without Arranging

Fine and dry with the gate shut at seven and open a hand's width at eleven with the wet back overnight and nobody near it, and a stone off its bed at the outlet end with the pack open to the weather for four hours.

The clerk read the launder at nine and two people put the stone back while he read, without arranging it and without writing it, and neither spoke while they worked.

**One thousand and one hundred and fifty-one hundredweight.**

Tuesday added eight to Monday, and the three hundred and fifty-eighth figure of a run that has never repeated stands this morning, and the rise on an even morning is the rule.

The halves were a hundred and sixty-eight one way and a hundred and fifty-seven the other, three hundred and twenty-five together, and the younger man said the total and the older nodded and neither wrote it on the wall.

The morning run was not read, because the half is a half number on an even morning, and the second book stayed shut and the heading of a month nine months back stood where it stood.

Renn Ashby and the man of about thirty-one of Silling set the stone and tamped the pack with their boots, and the second of the two gave the reason first for not writing it anywhere.

**"Two people putting a stone back without arranging it is work and not a record, and I have had a folded page in my book twenty-five years and nobody asked me, and I am not starting a record with a stone."**

He wiped his hands on his coat.

**"Up the bank in the dark I will not go as a fifth name on a thing nobody asked me about, and last week I went as the fourth and was not named, and those are two different things."**

Nia Vale watched them and said she has counted them and did not tell them, and that she wants it on the page that he said it and the four before him did not.

**"Eleven times asked nothing and carried his share since the ninth of last month, and tonight he had a bar in his hand and was not named."**

She picked up a chip of stone and put it down.

**"That is not an oversight and it is not a decision, and a boot paced a length where a figure was asked for and refused."**

Three hundred and fifty-six days stand on the four bodies sheet, and the clause beneath that figure takes the three hundred and fifty-fifth out of three hundred and fifty-seventh, one under and one over, and it is a different figure from this morning's launder and stays a different figure on every morning of the year.

The near board on the long wall says seven hundred and forty-two days and the far one says seven hundred and eighty-nine, and the gap has been forty-seven for four years, and the drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour of this morning.

There are three sheets on the long table. The first is fifty-five days old and lies face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it. The second is folded with its fold turned the other way and says nothing because it has not been opened. The third carries a heading, a person's name, a table of places and dates, a date and a hand at the foot, and a seal in the corner, and it is correct in every particular. None of the three was answered or refused or entered, and the requests and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three and fifty-three and no column has been ruled under any of them.

The woman of about thirty-eight stood at the tap with an empty can and said she wanted the not-asking on the record, and that she was not going to ask what a column in a stone nine hundred yards off counts besides readings.

**"I want somebody to say what it counts besides readings, and I am not going to ask, and I want the not-asking on the record."**

She filled the can.

**"Six rows ruled and no unit against any of them in eleven years, and the seventh not ruled, and none of what came back is a seventh."**

Marek came out at eleven with the sun on his face and said the second of the two standings, and said the first one on a morning when nobody had asked him anything at all.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

He said it to the shed door and then put his hand flat on the stone that two men had put back in its bed an hour earlier.

**"That is the whole of it and I am not going to decorate it, and a man who decorates a loss is a man who has started to spend it."**

The clerk spoke the ages derived and not carried at the middle table, with the form out of the rack and the charter behind it and Silling's own book open on the weight.

**"Two hundred and fifty-two days on the form, a hundred and fifty-eight on the charter, two hundred and forty-six on Silling's line, and all three of them empty."**

He touched nothing to write and put his hands in his pockets instead.

**"Four lines bare, terms ruled and empty, seven sessions and no eighth, six not knowns and no seventh row."**

Two men put a stone back in its bed this morning and neither of them went near the use log, which is at fifteen lines and has been for a season, the sixteenth line not written, and nothing was added to it by the putting. The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. Sixty-six is on the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off and does not go back. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched. Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and is not discharged in twenty-eight.

The ring got no foot and no figure. The offer is undated and unmoved. No rung was climbed and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder. The letter was not counted out loud by anybody.

Lissa Vale came to the yard at ten with a basket of cuttings and set it in the shade, and said the cuttings were from the low field edge and not from the ring, and gave the reason first so nobody would add them wrong, and put the basket down on the flat stone rather than on the ground so that it would not take the damp up.

**"Cuttings from the edge and not from the bare ground inside, and the two are not added anywhere, and I am saying it before anybody asks."**

She covered the basket with sacking and tucked the edge of it under the rim on all four sides.

**"Edge ground and ring ground are two pieces, and one came up and one lay bare, and neither cancels the other."**

The woman of about thirty-eight helped her tie the sacking, and said the knot would hold till evening and would be undone by morning, and that a knot for a day is enough, and neither of them mentioned what was under the sacking.

Marek lifted the basket to judge the weight and set it down, and said nothing about yield, because a basket judged by hand is not a figure for a book, and he put his hand flat on the sacking afterwards and left it there a moment longer than the weighing needed.

The clerk noted the basket in no column, because baskets live a day and columns live years, and he swept around it at noon and put the grit over the edge of the sacking with the edge of his broom.

The clerk checked the three sheets at noon without touching any, because checking by eye is looking and looking is not entering, and no column was ruled, and he stood at the end of the table long enough to read the third one twice.

Marek asked the yard at large whether any tool was wanting, and nobody answered, because wanting asked in a yard is an offer and an offer in this holding is not dated or moved, and the silence lasted long enough that a dog at the far end of the yard barked once.

**"No tool wanting and no hand idle, and a stone back in its bed and a pin on a bench, and the morning holds."**

He went back to the one room.

Shed shadow falling across a stone back in its bed, three sheets on a long table, and a basket of cuttings covered with sacking against the warm wall.

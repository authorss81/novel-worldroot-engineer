# Chapter 618

## The Twenty-One Of The Sixteenth, And A Pin Taken Out Of A Gate After Three Weeks

Thursday was clear and the tap ran for a day and a night and nobody had to carry anything anywhere, and Renn Ashby took the pin out of the gate at nine in the morning and put it in his coat.

He did it in front of eleven people and he did it slowly, and he made no speech about it while it was happening, and then he said one thing and went and stood at the gate to be near it.

**"Three weeks and two days and it has held the gate every morning since the fourth of this month. I have not touched it once. I have checked it in the cold and in the wet and twice in the dark because I could not sleep and I got up and looked at it."**

**"Then why are you taking it out,"** Harlan Vetch said, from the wall.

**"Because a man cannot stand at a gate for the rest of his life testing a thing that has already been tested, and because there is a latch on that gate and it is not a worse latch than the pin was."**

**"It is a worse latch than the pin was,"** the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said, from the tap. **"A latch lifts. I have lifted it four times this month with a full pail."**

**"Then I will be standing here when you do it and I will hold it,"** Renn Ashby said. **"That is not the same as putting the pin back and we both know the difference, and I am not going to be asked about it again today."**

The gate shut at ten past nine with nothing in it but air, and shut again at twenty past ten with a barrow going through it, and Harlan Vetch watched both of those happen and came down off the wall and said so.

**"Three weeks and it was mine to check. I did not say that then and I am not saying it now. I am saying it shut twice this morning with a barrow in it and I did not have to be anywhere."**

The yard read the wall at nine and the run at seven, and the wall came first because the clerk had gone out to the far end of the yard to see the gate shut.

**One thousand and one hundred and seventy-nine hundredweight.**

**"Five off yesterday, and Thursday is an odd morning, and it is the three hundred and eighty-first figure of a run of forty-nine numbers that has never repeated one of them,"** Hesta Lyle said. **"The two halves are a hundred and seventy-nine and a hundred and sixty-nine and they make three hundred and forty-eight, and I got it right on the first go, and I am not going to be told about it by anybody."**

Renn Ashby read the run out of the doorway with the page turned to him, and the stone on the corner, and the last sentence of it going out with four other voices behind his.

**"One hundred and sixty-one mornings of three hundred and five. Not a percentage. First one steps by one, second one steps by two."**

The four bodies of households stood at three hundred and seventy-nine on the wall, with the three hundred and seventy-eighth out of three hundred and eightieth in the smaller hand underneath, and the near board at seven hundred and sixty-five with the far one at eight hundred and twelve.

Nobody asked for the ages that morning, and the clerk read them anyway at noon, standing at the middle table with the rack open, and said why he was doing it and heard himself saying it.

**"Nobody asked. That is not the same as being asked and I am not going to pretend it is. The form is at two hundred and seventy-five days. The charter is at a hundred and eighty-one. The second ruled line in Silling's own book is at two hundred and sixty-nine, and there is nothing on it and nothing was on it when I started and there is nothing on it now."**

The man of about fifty was standing at the end of the long table when he said the thing he had been carrying since Sunday, and he said it once and stopped, and nobody asked him a question and nobody asked him a second one.

**"I am not going to be the one holding that key past the next load up that road. Not because anybody has told me to let it go and not because I have found somebody better to hand it to. I have not got a reason you would all agree with, and if I gave you one you would take it and start using it, and then in a year the key would be in a pocket and gone."**

**"It is on the nail,"** the clerk said.

**"It is on the nail,"** the man of about fifty agreed, **"and it is going to be on the nail on Saturday and Sunday, and I am telling you this morning so that nobody is surprised in about six weeks and starts asking me whether I remember saying it."**

Then he went out to the gate, where the pin was not, and stood there for a while with his hands behind his back.

Marek came in from the tool house at the second hour with the two pieces of iron trued and finished lying on the bench, and he had said three words to the woman of about thirty-eight at the tap and she had said four back and they were not about the joint.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said. **"The gate shut on its own for the first time in three weeks and the yard has spent the morning finding out what it feels like not to have somebody standing at it, and today is not the morning for the two largest things I know how to say."**

She said that was the ninth morning running and that he had said the sentence about counting on three of them and she had noticed, and he said she had noticed, and neither of them said anything else about it.

The long table carries three sheets with about nine inches of bare board either side of each and the first of them is seventy-eight days old and lying face up. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand in two columns of the books in this building and not one of the three sheets has ever gone into either of them, and a fourth paper is in a coat on a woman's back and is not one of the three.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and there was none entered on Thursday. The board in the one room is carrying thirty-nine blanks and its second rule is open and empty, and a rule that came back empty that morning opened nothing.

Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know, the sixth of them bare, and there is nothing cut below them for the covenant or for the machine or for the schedule or for the confession. There are four lines under two words in that corner and not one word has ever gone on them. The sheet of terms has a third column ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved.

Paid stands at twenty-nine on the compost line and nothing has been discharged in twenty-nine, and it will not move again for twenty-six mornings, which falls on a Saturday a fortnight off and is not this holding's to arrange. The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not written. The barrow says eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. A door nine hundred yards off carries sixty-six in its seventh column and nobody has read it. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched and was not asked anything.

Nobody climbed the ladder today and neither of the two people standing on it has a figure against his name anywhere in this holding. The offer on the low board has not been dated, read out, moved, taken up or taken back. The ring of bare ground took no foot from anybody and carries no figure at all, and the ground that came up on the low field is a separate piece and the two have never been added together.

At noon the clerk turned the yard key and shut the one room behind him. The key to the drawer hung on its nail all afternoon and the drawer behind the near board stayed shut at every hour, and a man said at four o'clock that he was not going to be holding it past the next load, and nobody in that yard wrote one word of it down.

Hinge took the weight of the gate without the pin and did not complain, and by six o'clock the yard had stopped going past the gate to look at it, and the pin was in a coat pocket in a pocket that already had a stone in it, and it was still there on Friday morning.

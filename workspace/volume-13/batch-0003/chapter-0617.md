# Chapter 617

## The Twentieth Of The Sixteenth, And A Joint That Came Apart In Five People Sights

Wednesday was the first mild morning of the week and the tap had to be shut off at the main for two hours and a half, and everybody in that yard learned what a shut tap is like in their own time.

Marek had the joint apart by seven.

He had it apart by seven because he had gone at it the moment the light came and had told the woman of about thirty-eight what he was doing before he did it, and she had said the word she had been carrying since Monday, which was that she would rather have one Saturday that does something than four more packings that do not, and then she had turned the water off at the main herself, which is not his job and which she did anyway.

**One thousand and one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight.**

**"Eight on and Wednesday is an even morning, and it is the three hundred and eightieth of the run,"** Hesta Lyle said. **"The halves are a hundred and seventy-nine and a hundred and sixty-eight and they make three hundred and forty-seven."**

The run was not read that morning because Wednesday is an even morning, and Renn Ashby said the reason from the doorway with the page turned to face him, and four people said the last sentence of it with him again, which is a thing that now happens without anybody arranging it.

The two halves of the joint came apart at nine and would not go back.

He had them apart and clean and a quarter of an inch of fall showing on the level where there should have been none, and he set the two ends to each other dry and found that they met on one side and gaped on the other, and he tried it with the sacking in, and tried it with a piece of leather off the frame saw, and tried it with nothing at all, and the third time he tried it he stood up off the wet grass with his hands open and did not try it a fourth time.

**"It is not me,"** he said. **"It is the taper. Whoever cut this cut it the wrong way round, and when two ends are cut that way a man can bring them together and there is always going to be a corner at the top that has not got anything behind it, and the corner is where it goes."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood in front of him with the water off and no bucket yet.

**"I have to be careful with you,"** she said. **"Tell me the true thing. Can you put it right on Saturday?"**

**"Not on Saturday. I have told you since Monday that Saturday was a day's work for a man who had cut one of these before and I have not cut one since I was twenty-two. What I can do is put your water back on in an hour with sacking in it and have it still be a piece of iron with sacking in it, and then do the job properly over two mornings when I have the taper right, and the first morning I do it will be the morning I put it wrong on purpose so that I know what I am looking at."**

**"Two mornings,"** she said. **"And today counts as one of them or today does not count as one of them?"**

**"Today counts. Today is the morning I found out."**

Then they carried water from the well house for two hours and a half, and five people carried, and Marek carried until the woman of about thirty-eight told him to stop carrying and go and sit down, which he did, and did not argue about.

Tova Reed carried and worked from the side of it where she can hear, and at the end she said one thing about the sound of it.

**"I cannot hear water go down an iron pipe and I never have, and it does not matter this morning because every single thing I was needed for today I could see."**

Nobody said anything else about it, and the seed work went out on the trays at the hour it goes out.

Sennik Vaul came out at ten with his own armful of boards and put the one that carries the number in force on the bench, because the cycle had come round in the night.

**"A hundred and thirty-four, twenty-nine taken and a hundred and five not. It came up by itself and I am telling you who is not to blame for it, which is everybody, and I want it said in that order."**

On the long wall the sheet of the four bodies of households stood at three hundred and seventy-eight, and under it, in a smaller hand, the three hundred and seventy-seventh out of three hundred and seventy-ninth. The near board said seven hundred and sixty-four, the far one said eight hundred and eleven, and the drawer behind the near board was shut.

Harlan Vetch put Sera Quill's slate against the edge of the board so that the two of them could be read together, and it carried eighty-second against sixty-ninth, dated and signed at the top, and nobody was told they had got it wrong because there was nothing in them that anybody could get wrong.

**"Thirteen apart and no field taken,"** Sera Quill said. **"That is the third of these this month and it is the same as the other two and the gap is still open and I have written it down."**

At noon the water went back on, and it ran for half an hour and stopped, which was the fourth packing and the fourth leak, and the woman of about thirty-eight packed it again herself and did not let anybody else touch it.

**"I am not blaming you,"** Marek said. **"That is what I came out here to stop. You packed this thing at six this morning and I had it apart at seven, and the difference between that and a thing that works is a taper I have not cut yet."**

**"I am not blaming you either,"** she said, **"and if anybody in this yard says one or the other to either of us today they can come and say it to me."**

Nobody came and say it to her.

The clerk was in the one room with a slate face up on the middle table for an hour and nobody read it, because the yard had other business, and at the end of the hour Nia Vale read it because people had begun asking whether the day was still going.

**"Form, two hundred and seventy-four days, blank. Charter behind it, a hundred and eighty. Second ruled line in Silling's book, two hundred and sixty-eight, and it is bare too."**

Marek sat on the bench outside and did not go to the wall at any hour of the day, and Nia Vale came out and stood in front of him with her hands in her sleeves.

**"I am not saying the two things and there is no reason at all this time. I have failed in front of five people this morning and I have admitted it out loud, and a man who has just done that does not also get to stand up and announce that he is not going to say something."**

**"You did not fail,"** she said. **"You found out, and then you told her, and then you carried until she told you to stop."**

**"I had it apart in twenty minutes and could not put it back in fifty,"** he said.

The three sheets are still lying out on the long table with the bare board either side of them, the first seventy-seven days old and face up, and there are seven sessions entered in this holding's book with none of them entered today, and fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes in their two columns and none of the three sheets in either.

Thirty-nine blanks are on the board in the one room and the second rule beneath them stands open and empty and it opened nothing. The compost line carries paid against twenty-nine and nothing has been discharged in it. The use log on its shelf has fifteen lines in it and the margin below them is clean. The barrow against the wall says eleven journeys and eleven is a floor. A door nine hundred yards off has sixty-six scratched in its seventh column and nobody went and read it this morning. Nobody has fetched the man of about seventy and he stands at twenty-nine fetchings and had nothing said to him.

Under two words in the corner of the one room four bare lines stand and always have. The sheet of terms has its third column ruled and nothing in it, and the heading over that column has not been improved by anybody. Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know, and the paper has room below the sixth and nothing has been cut there.

Nobody has gone up the ladder and there is a figure against neither of the two people on it. The offer on the low board is undated and has not been moved, taken up or taken back. The ring of bare ground has taken no foot and carries no number at all, and what is standing on the low field is another piece of ground entirely and does not belong to it.

At noon the clerk turned the yard key and the one room was shut behind him, and on his way past the doorframe the key to the drawer was hanging where it had hung since the eighth, and it was hanging there again at four o'clock, and the drawer behind the near board never opened in a morning when five people had carried water.

File was laid on the bench in the tool house with the two pieces of iron beside it and one end of each of them squared and true and ready, and the wet grass where they had been laid out all morning had the marks of them pressed into it, and the marks were there on Thursday morning too and nobody measured them.

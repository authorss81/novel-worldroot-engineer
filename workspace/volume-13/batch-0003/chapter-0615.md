# Chapter 615

## The Eighteenth Of The Sixteenth, And Two Feet Of Iron Measured With A Bottle And A Level

Monday was the first of the two clear mornings and the yard was already busy by seven with a cart at the gate that went away again at eight having taken nothing and brought nothing.

Renn Ashby read the wall at nine and read the second book into the doorway at seven with the page turned to him, and the run was not read that morning because Monday is an even morning, which he said out loud himself.

**"Not read, and that is not me forgetting, and I have said the reason every morning this week so that when somebody asks me in six weeks I will still have it."**

**One thousand and one hundred and eighty-one hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, even morning, three hundred and seventy-eighth of the run. The halves are a hundred and seventy-eight and a hundred and sixty-seven and they make three hundred and forty-five, and I added them before I said them this morning, which is the first time in four years I have ever added them first."**

The near board stood at seven hundred and sixty-two and the far one at eight hundred and nine, and the clause under the sheet of the four bodies of households carried the three hundred and seventy-fifth out of three hundred and seventy-seventh against a figure of three hundred and seventy-six on the line above it, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut with the key on its nail.

Marek went to the tap at half past nine with the level out of the tool house and a bottle in his pocket, and asked the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden whether she would let him do a thing to her joint that would take twenty minutes and change nothing.

**"You may do anything to it you like as long as you tell me first, and you have told me first, and I do not know what half of that means but it is more than the sacking does."**

He set the level on the length of iron above the joint, took the reading, set it below the joint and took it again, and then did it a third time with the level turned end for end so that he would know whether he had it true, and it came the same way round twice out of three.

Then he filled the bottle from the tap and put the level across the top of the joint with the bottle standing on the iron at the far end, and watched the water come up one side of the glass and not the other.

**"There is a fall in it. The far end of this joint sits lower than the near end by about a quarter of an inch over two feet, which means the two ends are not meeting square, which means it was cut, or it was true and it has moved, and there is a mark on it that says somebody moved it once before I came here."**

**"Can you make it right?"**

**"Not in a morning. I can take the two apart and cut them and true them up with a file and a vice and that is a Saturday's work with two men, and I will tell you now that the first time a man fits a joint he has just cut he will get it wrong, because he will leave the burr on the back of the taper and the burr is where the water goes."**

She stood and thought about it with her arms folded.

**"Then I want the Saturday,"** she said. **"I am not having a man measure it and then go away. I have had four packings and one Saturday coming and I would rather have one Saturday that does something than four more packings that do not."**

**"You will have it,"** Marek said. **"And when it fails the first time on the Saturday I am not going to stand here and tell you I did not say it would."**

The clerk got the three ages out at noon and stood in the one room doorway with the documents on the bench behind him and sent the figures out into the yard by shouting, because inside the room he could not be heard at all.

**"Two hundred and seventy-two on the register form!"**

Renn Ashby put his hand round his mouth and shouted back across forty feet of wet yard and got the wrong document, and four people heard both of them get it wrong.

Renn Ashby at the gate shouted back that it was two hundred and seventy-two on the charter.

**"Those are not the same sentence and you have got me on the wrong document, and we are both going to find out which of us is right,"** the clerk said, and came out and looked at the form, and it was two hundred and seventy-two on the form and a hundred and seventy-eight on the charter and two hundred and sixty-six on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, and nothing on any of them, and the yard heard the whole of it including the part where two men were wrong in public and neither of them pretended otherwise.

Sera Quill came out at the second hour with her slate and a dry cloth for the frost on it and found that she had nothing new to write.

**"It is not a morning that comes round and I am not going to write a morning that does not come round. A slate that gets written on every day is a slate that has stopped meaning anything by the second week of the month, and mine has not got there yet and I am not going to hurry it."**

She went back in and put the slate on the shelf face down, which is what she does on a day that is not one of the three.

Kellan Rusk was not in this yard on Monday and Nia Vale came in from the seed house with a tray on each arm and said so before anybody asked her, because she had decided on Saturday that things nobody said out loud got worse, not better.

Marek had the level back in his hand by three o'clock and the bottle in his pocket and stood at the middle of the yard with both.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning and I am not going to be thanked for it. I have got a level in one hand and a bottle of tap water in the other and I have found a quarter of an inch of fall in two feet of iron, and it is the truest thing I have found in this yard this month, and I am not going to stand next to a true small measurement and hand this yard a true large one."**

Nia Vale said that he had said the truest thing he had and then said the largest thing, and that those were different sizes of the same kind, and she went in with the trays.

Harlan Vetch counted the standing of the one room out loud while he waited for Marek to finish with the level, because he counts things when he is waiting.

**"Three sheets on the long table and the first of them seventy-five days old and lying face up. Nine inches of board either side of each one, and I have always thought those nine inches are the most careful thing anybody in this yard does."**

**"The third one is right,"** Sera Quill said, from the doorway.

**"I know it is right. I have never once said it was wrong. Right and answered are two different jobs and this yard has only ever had somebody for the first one."**

The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. Four lines stand ruled under two words in the corner and nothing has ever gone on them. Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know and there is no seventh cut for the covenant or for the machine or for the schedule or for the confession.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes have gone into their two columns and have never taken a sheet off anybody's table. Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and none was entered today. Thirty-nine blanks are on the board in the one room with the second rule open under them and empty, and a rule that comes back empty opens nothing.

The barrow was put back with eleven journeys in chalk on its side and eleven is a floor. The use log has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not written. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched and not asked anything about it, and a door nine hundred yards off has carried sixty-six in its seventh column since before anybody here began counting anything at all.

Paid stands at twenty-nine on the compost line and nothing has been discharged in twenty-nine.

The ladder went up no rungs and there is no figure against either of the two people on it. The offer on the low board lies undated, unmoved, untaken and not taken back. The ring of bare ground has no foot in it and no figure against it, and the ground that came up on the low field is a different piece and is not added to it.

The clerk locked the one room at noon and turned the yard key, and the man of about fifty walked past the nail and the key on it and out of the gate, and by the time Marek put the level away the light had gone off the far board and the wall had gone back to being a wall.

Level went into the tool house between the pincers and the frame saw where it has lain since the spring, and the bottle went up on the sill of the one room and stood there full, and it was the only thing in this holding that had been measured three times and been right twice out of three, and nobody wrote any of it down.

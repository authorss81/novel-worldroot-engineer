# Chapter 609

## The Twelfth Of The Sixteenth, And A Voice That Went At Nine And Was Read Around

Tuesday came in wet from the north and the yard was loud with it, water going off the seed house roof in one long sheet and finding the same place in the middle road it had found on Saturday.

The clerk got to the wall at nine with the book under his arm and opened it and produced no sound at all. He tried twice. On the third try he got out a word with a hole in the middle of it and shut the book again.

**"It has gone. I have got something in my chest and I can read a number all day with it, so somebody is going to have to read it out and I am not going to pretend it is me."**

Nobody argued with him about it, which took the yard by surprise, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came off the tap and dried her hands on her skirt and asked for the book and was given it.

**"Give it to me square and do not turn the page for me, because I will want to say what is on the page and not what you remember being on it."**

She stood square to the wall in the rain with her shoulder against the chalk and read.

**One thousand and one hundred and seventy-two hundredweight.**

**"Eight on since yesterday," she said. "A rise, because Tuesday is an even morning, and an even morning takes eight. That is the three hundred and seventy-second number of a run that has never once come back with a figure it had already used."**

Four people said it at the same time, which nobody had planned, and the woman of about thirty-eight told them not to do it again because a thing said by four mouths at once is a thing nobody can check.

The two halves were on the wall one over the other and she read them in the order they were written, which was the wrong order, and then read them the other way.

**"A hundred and seventy-five and a hundred and sixty-four. That is three hundred and thirty-nine, and I added it twice because the first time I had the day of the month in my head and not the two numbers."**

Against the sheet of the four bodies of households the wall carried three hundred and seventy, and under the line the smaller hand carries the two mornings either side, and she read those and stopped.

**"The three hundred and sixty-ninth out of the three hundred and seventy-first. One under and one over, and that clause is not about the number above it, it is about yesterday and tomorrow, and I am not going to have anybody in this yard add the two of them together this morning."**

The near board stood at seven hundred and fifty-six and the far one at eight hundred and three, and the drawer behind the near board was shut, and the key to it was on the nail in the one room door where it has been since the eighth of this month.

The clerk said, in the wrecked voice, that the run was not read this morning.

**"It is not read because this is an even morning and it is only read on odd ones. And I will tell you the reason I have never liked that rule, which is that a pair where one side steps by one and the other side steps by two is not a thing you can read by halves, and a yard that hears it every other morning will take a half of it away with it."**

Nia Vale wrote the day on the seed house slate in chalk because she had a slate and nothing to put on it, and then rubbed it out again and said the slate was for trays.

Marek came out at ten and asked Sennik Vaul and Harlan Vetch whether they wanted him to take a field, and both of them said no, and neither of them said it the same way.

**"The force is a hundred and thirty-two this morning with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and three not, and it came up by itself this morning because the cycle came round, and I am not spending it on a field on a Tuesday in this rain on the word of a man who came in at ten,"** Sennik Vaul said.

**"Eleven feet of top course wants going on the north wall and it has wanted it for three weeks and nobody has ordered it,"** Harlan Vetch said, and then, before Marek could answer, **"and I am not saying that to get you to do it. I am saying it because it is what the wall wants and the wall has never lied to me."**

Marek took that the way it was given.

**"Then it is not mine to spend and I am not going to find a way to make it mine. If either of you wants a field this month, say the word and I will measure it and not fill it."**

Nobody said the word. The two of them stood there with the water coming down behind them and the disagreement between them being older than either Marek or the man holding this holding.

Sera Quill came out with her slate at the eleventh hour and stood where the clerk could not read her by lip and wrote instead, and then showed the slate round the yard, which is a thing she does and which took four people a minute to understand.

It said: two reckonings, eighty against sixty-seventh, thirteen apart, the same as they were the last time they came round, and no field taken.

**"I have written it down because the first time in nineteen years somebody wrote one of these down and it is on my slate and not on his book, and because I am not going to be the one who says the gap closed. It did not close."**

The clerk looked at the slate and put his own book down on the bench and did not enter it in that.

Marek went and stood at the wall on his own for a while after that, with his back to the yard, and Nia Vale came past with two trays and stopped, because she had learned the sound of him working out how to begin something.

**"I am not saying the two things and I am not going to tell this yard why today either,"** he said. **"A man who explains his not-saying every morning has turned the not-saying into work, and work is the one thing I have never in my life been able to leave alone."**

She took the trays in and did not tell him whether that was right.

At the middle table the clerk worked the three ages out and could not say them, so he held the register form up against the grey light with the sheet facing out and read the figure off the heading itself, and then the charter, and then took his own hand off the second ruled line in Silling's book and turned the book round so that anybody standing at the table could see the bare line.

**"Two hundred and sixty-six days on that form. A hundred and seventy-two on the charter behind it. Two hundred and sixty on the second ruled line in that book and nothing on it."**

Silling was not in the yard and was not asked about any of it.

Tova Reed came out of the seed house with the tray under her arm and stood in the doorway while she said the thing that belongs to her and to the doorframe she was leaning on.

**"Six rows are ruled on the sheet of what this holding does not know. All six of them are bare, and there is no seventh row cut anywhere on that sheet, and one is not going to be cut for the covenant or for the machine or for the schedule or for the confession. I say that once a month and nobody has ever asked me for a reason."**

In the corner of the one room, under two words, four lines are ruled and empty. The sheet of terms has a third column with a heading standing over it that nobody has put a pen to.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood over the compost line for a while before she came off it and wiped her boots.

**"Paid, twenty-nine. There has been no discharge in twenty-nine and there will not be one until the interval gets here, and the interval is not mine and it is not his."**

Sera Quill carried her slate round the yard on Tuesday and has carried it every day since, and it still has one morning's worth of two reckonings on it. On the long table lie three sheets with bare board about nine inches either side of each, the first of them sixty-nine days old and lying face up, and in this holding's books there are fifty-three requests in one column and fifty-three notes in the section-nine column, and no one of those three sheets has ever gone into either of them.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book. Thirty-nine blanks are ruled on the board in the one room, and the second rule underneath them came back empty this morning and opened nothing at all.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings, was fetched by nobody, and nobody put anything to him. A door nine hundred yards off carries sixty-six in its seventh column, and it does not go back.

Harlan Vetch put the barrow away with the hand that had carried grit all morning, and the side of it still carries eleven journeys in chalk, and eleven is a floor, and the use log in the one room holds fifteen lines with a clean margin under them.

Nobody went up the ladder this morning and no figure was written against either of the two people standing on it. The offer on the low board lies where it has lain for four years, and what is growing on the low field is a different piece of ground from the ring of bare ground beside it, and the two are never added and have never been measured together.


The clerk locked up at noon and turned the yard key with his left hand because the book had to stay shut under his right one, and the man of about fifty walked past the nail in the doorframe and did not stop, and the key was on the nail at dusk.

Silt had come down the hard standing by the tap by four o'clock and stood in the low place by the step in a skin an inch deep, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood over it and decided to leave it there until morning, and said out loud that she wanted at least one thing in this yard that had to be dealt with twice.

# Chapter 614

## The Seventeenth Of The Sixteenth, And Forty Minutes Across A Table With A Book Shut

Sunday came in with ice in the butt and hard blue light and nobody working, which in this holding is not the same thing as nobody doing anything.

The yard read the wall at half past eight because it was going to be there all day and there was no reason to keep nine people waiting until nine for a number that had not moved since dark.

**One thousand and one hundred and seventy-three hundredweight.**

**"Five off,"** Hesta Lyle said. **"Odd morning, and the three hundred and seventy-seventh of the run. The two halves are a hundred and seventy-seven and a hundred and sixty-seven and they make three hundred and forty-four, and I have stopped needing to add them twice, which I am telling you is not pride."**

Renn Ashby read the run out of the doorway at seven with the page turned to him and the stone on the corner of the board. Behind him the wall carried the sheet of the four bodies of households with three hundred and seventy-five on the line and the clause beneath it taking the three hundred and seventy-fourth out of three hundred and seventy-sixth, and the near board stood at seven hundred and sixty-one with the far one at eight hundred and eight.

**"One hundred and fifty-nine mornings of three hundred and one. Not a percentage. First one steps by one, second one steps by two."**

He has said the same four sentences every morning for four mornings now, and this morning, for the first time, four people said the fourth one with him.

At noon the long table had eleven people at it and more food on it than this holding has put on that table in a month. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden cooked it and would not sit down, and Nia Vale counted the plates twice and got eleven both times, and the man of about seventy was not fetched and was not at the table and was not missed by anybody out loud.

Silling came at half past twelve and sat down at the end with his own book on his knees under the table, and he did not put it on the table, and Marek sat down opposite him because there was nowhere else to sit and neither of them said anything about it for forty minutes.

They talked about the gate. They talked about the wall and the two feet of course that had lifted, and about Harlan Vetch's four years, and about the water butt and whether the joint at the tap would hold, and about a man up the fen who had come back from the coast with a different name for the same trade. They did not talk about the book and they did not talk about the wood, and twice Marek had a sentence ready and did not say it, and once he was so sure Silling had not noticed that he looked up and found that Silling had noticed.

At ten past one Silling got up, and put his hand on the spine of the book where it stood on his knee, and spoke to nobody in particular.

**"I have had this under my hands for forty minutes and I did not open it and I did not want to, and I have gone thirty years without saying that out loud in a room where anybody was sitting at the same table. I had it here to do it with and I chose the table."**

Then he went and got a second helping and came back and sat down and did not say anything else for the rest of it.

At the middle table the clerk got the three ages out of the rack with a second pair of hands, because Nia Vale was standing at the rack and the clerk could not be heard above a conversation.

**"Two hundred and seventy-one days on the register form,"** the clerk said, and then he put his hand flat on the rack and gave her the nod.

**"And a hundred and seventy-seven on the charter behind it,"** Nia Vale said, **"and two hundred and sixty-five on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, and nothing on it, and I have checked the day off the sheet because I did not trust myself."**

Marek washed up at the end of it, which he has not done in this yard in four years, and put the plates on the shelf above the tap and stood there with his hands wet.

**"I am not saying the two things and there is no reason. I sat opposite a man for forty minutes this morning with his book on his knees and I did not say them once, and it cost me nothing at all to be quiet about them, and a thing that costs nothing when you are quiet about it is not a thing I have been carrying."**

He dried his hands and did not finish that.

The man of about fifty had been at the gate all afternoon and came in at three and asked the yard, in front of six people, for the key to be taken off the nail.

**"I have thought about it for three days and I am asking. People are walking past that door slower than they were walking past it before Friday. They are not stopping. They are slowing down. And I have put that thing on a nail in the open on purpose, so I cannot complain about what it does, and I cannot stop it either, and I would like somebody to move it back into my pocket and tell me it is done."**

Somebody at the far end of the table said nothing at all for a moment, and the man of about fifty waited for it and it did not come.

Nia Vale said no first. Harlan Vetch said no second and said it in one word. The clerk said no third and gave a reason, which was that a key moved back into a pocket in this yard would be a key nobody could see, and a key nobody could see is a key that has not been handed on.

**"So I stand in a doorway being slowed at,"** the man of about fifty said.

**"Yes,"** the clerk said. **"And if you carry it, then in eleven months somebody will have to carry it, and they will carry it in their pocket, and it will be gone before anybody starts walking slowly past it."**

The man of about fifty went out and shut the gate behind him and stood with his back to it for a while, and nobody followed him, and he came back in and asked what there was to do in the yard, and was given a barrow and a heap of grit, and did the heap of grit.

The three sheets lay on the long table after the meal with the boards either side of them, the first seventy-four days old and face up, the second folded, the third with a name and a date and a hand and a seal and nothing wrong anywhere with it, and the one in Nia Vale's coat is not one of the three and is not on the table.

The yard cleared the table at two and a half and what was left on it was three sheets, and the first of them is seventy-four days old, and the third of them is as correct this Sunday as it was the day it arrived.

Nobody at that table asked why a paper that is right in every particular should go untouched for seventy-four days, and nobody would have been able to answer it, and Nia Vale said so out loud on Friday and was right to.

Kellan Rusk's page is still in a coat on a woman's back and is not one of the three and is not in a book and is not under anything anybody can reach in this building.

The sheet of what this holding does not know has six ruled rows and no seventh. The third column of the sheet of terms has a heading over it that has not been improved and nothing under it at all. Four lines under two words in the corner of the one room have been bare since the sheet came and the answer is not due and is not going to be offered by anybody this week.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and no session was entered on a Sunday, and fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes have gone into their two columns and taken nothing from the long table, and the board in the one room carries thirty-nine blanks with its second rule standing open underneath them, and it opened nothing.

Soren Rill put the barrow away and its side still says eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor, and the use log on the shelf has fifteen lines in it, and nobody wrote the sixteenth on the one day of the week this yard would most easily have found a reason.

The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was fetched by nobody today and was not at the table, and there is nobody in this yard with the job of fetching him. The number sixty-six has been scratched in a door's seventh column for four years, and nobody went and read it, and it does not come down.

Paid is written in front of twenty-nine on the compost line and nothing has gone out in it, and a Sunday is not an interval, and the twenty-six mornings that move that line do not care what day they land on.

Nia Vale, stacking the plates at the end of Sunday, named the three things this yard keeps forgetting. The ladder is still at the bottom rung. The offer on the low board has no date on it and nobody has ever taken it up. The ring of bare ground has never had a boot in it, and no figure has ever been set against it, and what came up on the low field is a different piece of ground entirely and does not join it.

Ice was in the ruts by the gate at dusk and in the ruts the barrow had worn and in the shallow place where the drain had been done wrong since before anybody in this holding could remember, and the key was on the nail in the one room door and the drawer behind the near board was shut, and the yard had settled that morning into the business of a key that is not taken down, which was the work of about nine people and had been voted on by none of them.

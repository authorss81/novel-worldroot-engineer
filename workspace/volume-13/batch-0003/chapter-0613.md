# Chapter 613

## The Sixteenth Of The Sixteenth, And A Drip That Came Back On The Fourth Day

Saturday was hard and bright and the frost had taken the standing water in the middle road into a white crust that broke under a boot without sound, and for about ten minutes after seven the chalk on the long wall stood out of the white like something cut into it.

The yard read the wall. Hesta Lyle had the book on the bench and read the figure out of it rather than off the chalk, because the chalk was too clear to need looking at and because she had worked out that reading out of the book is slower and harder to get wrong.

**One thousand and one hundred and seventy-eight hundredweight.**

**"Eight on and it is an even morning, and it is the three hundred and seventy-sixth of the run."**

She got the book shut and squared it to the edge of the bench with the edge of her hand before she went on.

She got the two halves right without hesitating, a hundred and seventy-seven and a hundred and sixty-six, and made three hundred and forty-three of them, and then stood back and looked at the white on the wall for a while.

**"That is the clearest that wall has been in three weeks and it will not be again before the sun is round the far side, and nobody is going to remember that this morning except the two of us."**

**"I will remember it,"** the clerk said, in what he had left of a voice, and wrote it on his slate instead, and put the slate in his coat, and did not say what he had written on it.

The run was not read that morning because Saturday is an even morning, and Renn Ashby read it anyway at seven o'clock with the page turned to face him and got it right, and then read the number of the morning off the top of the board, which is not what the run is, and put his thumb on the page.

**"I have said what it is not. I have got the habit of saying the other thing, which is a thing I have never done in my life, and I would rather say it than leave it."**

The sheet of the four bodies of households carried three hundred and seventy-four on the line and the clause under it carried the three hundred and seventy-third out of three hundred and seventy-fifth, and the near board stood at seven hundred and sixty and the far one at eight hundred and seven, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour with the key on its nail in the one room door.

At ten Sennik Vaul came out of the tool house with a board under his arm and propped it against the bench, because the cycle had come round overnight and the number in force had gone up by itself.

**"A hundred and thirty-three, with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and four not. It came up one because the cycle came round, and not one of us did anything, and I want the yard to hear that from me before anybody goes round the yard working out whose morning it was."**

Marek said that nobody would be blamed for a field that had not been taken and that he had asked for one on Tuesday and been refused in two different sentences.

**"I have still got no field and I am not going to have one today either, and the two reckonings came round this morning at one-and-eightieth against sixty-eighth, and they are still thirteen apart, and nobody in this yard is going to be told they got it wrong because they did not."**

Sera Quill had it on the slate already, dated and signed, because she had been writing it down every morning since the first of the month.

Then Tova Reed's voice came from the tap house, on the far side of the wall, and it was the sound of water hitting a stone from a quarter inch away.

The woman of about thirty-eight had the standpipe joint open by ten and had the sacking out of it in her hand, wet through at one corner, and she stood looking at it with her thumb in the wet place.

**"Fourth day. I packed it on Monday and I said if it starts again it starts again and I will pack it again, and here it is and I am packing it again."**

She went at it with a short strip and a length of wire, and she was slow about it, and Marek came and stood at the end of it and did not put his hands on it and did not tell her she was doing it wrong.

**"You have packed that joint four times,"** he said.

**"I have packed it once and it has leaked three times. There is a difference and it is the only difference I have."**

He looked at the two lengths of iron where they came together and then at the shape of the compression.

**"That is not a leak in the sacking and it never was. That is a joint that was cut and put back with a taper the wrong way round, and every time you pack it you are holding two ends of iron together with cloth. I am going to measure the fall across it and I am going to tell you the truth, and the truth is that it wants cutting and re-tapering and there is no turner in this holding."**

**"Then it is a piece of iron with sacking in it,"** she said, **"and it is the piece of iron I have, and you can measure it whenever you like and you will not be able to have it any other way this week."**

Tova Reed had come out with a tray on each arm and put them down on the step and come back to look at what they were both looking at, and she stood on the side of it where she can hear, with her head turned that way, and worked the whole of it from there.

**"I cannot hear the drip from where I am standing and I am not going to stand on the other side of a wall to find out whether I can,"** she said. **"If it starts again on Sunday it starts again and she packs it again and I carry the trays."**

The clerk read the three ages standing under the lintel of the one room with the documents on the sill and the wind coming across the yard behind him, and read them twice, because the first reading went out flat and four people at the back did not have it.

**"Form, two hundred and seventy. Charter behind it, a hundred and seventy-six. The second of the ruled lines in Silling's own book, two hundred and sixty-four, and it is bare."**

Marek did not go near the wall at ten, at eleven or at noon. He stood at the end of the tap with a cord and a bottle in his pocket and his hands empty and looked at the white coming off the chalk.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, to the yard, and the yard let him, because it has had eleven mornings of him saying that. **"The wall has been saying one out loud for ten minutes tonight and it is doing it better than I would, and I am not going to stand next to it and compete."**

Hesta Lyle went over the six ruled rows on the sheet of what this holding does not know with her thumb, one after the other, and stopped her thumb at the bottom of the sixth and left it there.

**"Six. And there is room below it for one more and nobody has cut it. I have wanted to read out the four things a seventh row is never cut for for a year now and I am not going to make anybody else hear the list."**

The corner of the one room has four lines ruled under two words and nothing has ever gone on them, and the third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty under a heading nobody has been near with a pen.

On the long table are three sheets, the first of them seventy-three days old and lying face up, and the third of them headed with a name and dated and signed and sealed and wrong in no particular anybody here could find. Whatever else there is in this holding, the piece of paper in the seed house coat is not one of the three and is not lying out where anybody can reach it.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three notes in the section-nine column have taken none of them, and seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book, and on a Saturday none was entered. Thirty-nine blanks are on the board in the one room and the second rule beneath them is standing open and empty and it opened nothing.

Nobody fetched the man of about seventy and he stands at twenty-nine fetchings. Sixty-six is scratched in a door's seventh column nine hundred yards off this holding, and nobody in this yard has gone and read it this morning and it does not come down.

The barrow has eleven journeys in chalk on its side and eleven is a floor, and the use log on its shelf has fifteen lines in it and nobody has written a sixteenth.

The compost line went on carrying paid against twenty-nine all through the day, and nothing came out of it, and it will not move again until twenty-six mornings after the last time it moved.

Harlan Vetch went past the ladder on his way down to the wall and it went up no rungs, and there is a figure against neither of the two people standing on it. The offer on the low board is undated, where it has always been, untaken and not taken back. The ring of bare ground has taken no foot all season and carries no number against it, and what is standing on the low field is a separate piece of ground and the two are never added together.

The clerk locked up at noon and turned the yard key with his left hand. The man of about fifty went past the nail, and the key on it, and out through the gate, and did not look at either for the first time since he had hung the thing there.

Sacking was damp and cold in the hand of the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden when she came off the tap, and she hung a dry rag under the joint before she went, and told the yard it was not a figure and not a record and would not be read as either, and it was still dry at dusk and she looked at it anyway on her way past.

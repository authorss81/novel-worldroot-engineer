# Chapter 611

## The Fourteenth Of The Sixteenth, And A Folded Page Given To A Woman And Not To A Table

Thursday was clear and hard after the frost, and the ground had gone the colour of a wet slate where the frost had not got to it yet, and the clerk had a little of his voice back and used it to say that he was fine.

The yard read the wall without him. Hesta Lyle did it again because she had done it twice now and a thing done twice is a thing a person does.

**One thousand and one hundred and seventy-five hundredweight.**

**"Eight on since yesterday and Thursday is an even morning,"** she said. **"Three hundred and seventy-fourth of the run. I am not going to get good at this and I am not going to get worse at it either, so you can all stop watching me do it."**

The halves were a hundred and seventy-six and a hundred and sixty-five and made three hundred and forty-one between them, and Nia Vale said the sum out loud before Hesta Lyle did it, and then said it a second time so that Hesta Lyle would have something to disagree with. The sheet of the four bodies of households carried three hundred and seventy-two on the line, and the clause printed beneath it takes the three hundred and seventy-first out of three hundred and seventy-third, one under and one over, and the near board stood at seven hundred and fifty-eight with the far one at eight hundred and five.

The clerk stood at the end of the wall and waited, and when it was finished he said that the run was not read that morning.

**"It is an even morning. The pair steps differently on each side and it is read whole on the odd ones and not at all on the even ones, and I would rather say that out loud this week than let anybody think a man forgot."**

He said it in a voice that four people at the back of the yard could not hear at all, and Renn Ashby heard it anyway because he was standing at the doorway reading the second book on his own at seven o'clock with the page turned to face him.

**"Seven o'clock every morning I am here,"** he said, to nobody in particular, at about the eighth hour.

Kellan Rusk came back through the gate at half past nine with his coat buttoned to the throat and something inside it that had been inside it a long time, and he came at the long table and stood at the end of it and did not put anything down.

**"I have carried this since the seventh of this month and I have been off this yard for three mornings doing other work, and a thing in a coat is a thing one man is responsible for, and I am not sure I would rather it was a thing one woman is responsible for. That is not a compliment to her. It is where it has got to."**

He took a folded sheet out of his inside pocket and held it out flat, and Nia Vale took it, and he let go of it, and stood where he was.

**"Not the table,"** he said. **"Not the second sheet and not the first. A person. If it goes on the table it is one of three and it gets nine inches of board either side of it and I have watched that arrangement eat two documents in this county that I know of."**

**"I will hold it,"** Nia Vale said, **"and it will not be on the table and it will not be in a book, and if anybody asks me where it is I will tell them it is in a coat, because that is where it is."**

**"My coat,"** he said. **"For now."**

Sera Quill came out with the slate on which she had written the two reckonings on Tuesday and stood at the end of the long table and looked at what was in Nia Vale's hands and then at the two sheets that were lying out with their nine inches of board either side, and wrote something on the slate and did not show it to anybody and took it back inside her coat.

Harlan Vetch went down the wall at ten and found a place where the course had lifted at the north end, and put his thumb in it, and did not take the stone out, and came back and said what he had found.

**"Two feet of the third course has lifted on the north end and it has not fallen and it is not going to fall this week. I am telling you about it now so that when it does go I am not the man who said nothing."**

He put his thumb back into the joint he had found it in, and looked at the thumb, and wiped the grit off it on his coat.

Marek offered to measure the whole run of it properly, with figures and dates and his own hand on it, and Harlan Vetch said no before Marek had finished the sentence.

**"No. And I want to say why, because you will think I have got something against paper. A measured wall is a wall with a figure on it, and a wall with a figure on it is a wall somebody comes and asks to have the figure changed. I have watched that happen at a house up the fen and the wall is still crooked."**

**"That is a real answer and it is not the whole of one,"** Marek said.

**"It is the whole of the one I have to give you this morning,"** Harlan Vetch said, and went back to the bench.

Soren Rill had the barrow out by the seed house door and was throwing the grit off the step onto the low field edge, which is not the heap and not the ring, and Renn Ashby stopped at the gate and asked him what he was doing that for.

Marek stood at the bench with his hand flat on the nine inches of bare board between the first sheet and the second and did not put anything on it.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said. **"There is a piece of paper on a woman's back in this yard and it is not in a book and it is not on that table, and the two things I do not say would put every document in this holding into one kind of thing. I have had a look at what one kind of thing does here and I have watched it for four years from the other side of a yard."**

Nia Vale, who was in the doorway with a tray under each arm, did not answer him and went in.

**"It is not going anywhere,"** Soren Rill said. **"That is the whole of what it is doing. There is grit under the seed house door that has been going under that door since the spring and the door shuts on it a foot above the floor, and if I put it at the edge of the field then in a year somebody can move it and if I leave it under the door then in a year it is under the door and it is a fact."**

The barrow itself stood back against the wall by eleven with eleven journeys in chalk down its side, and eleven is a floor, and no twelfth mark went on it today.

Marek carried the two of the three ages up to the wall at noon because Harlan Vetch was going past the wall anyway and offered to take them, and Harlan Vetch said them into the middle of the yard where the wind was, in the wrong order, the charter before the form.

**"A hundred and seventy-four days on that charter. Two hundred and sixty-eight on the form. Two hundred and sixty-two on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, and there is nothing on that line and nothing is owed on it."**

The clerk did not correct the order. He wrote it on his slate in the order Harlan Vetch had said it, which is a different thing from writing it in the order of the rack, and he did not enter the slate in anything.

Tova Reed was in the seed house with her trays and came out and stood on the step where the grit had been and did not go down the low field, and the seed work went out on the trays as it goes out, and nobody took any of it off her.

Sera Quill put her hand flat on the use log on its shelf as she went past, which is a thing she does without deciding to, and did not open it and did not write in it, and it still has fifteen lines in it. Then she counted the days on the first of the three sheets by touching the edge of it with her thumbnail and not picking it up.

**"Seventy-one days and it has not been answered and it has not been refused, and the nine inches of bare board either side of it is the part I mind, because a thing that is boxed in like that is a thing somebody has decided is a problem and then decided is not theirs."**

The second sheet is folded with its crease the other way and the third is headed with a name and dated and signed and sealed and correct in every particular, and no column has been ruled under any of the three in any of the four books in this building, and fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes have gone into their two columns instead.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book and none of them was entered on Thursday. The board in the one room carries thirty-nine blanks and its second rule came back empty, and it is still empty, and a rule that opens nothing does not have to be opened again. The man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings and was not fetched, and a door nine hundred yards off has carried sixty-six in its seventh column since before the clerk came here, and nobody went and read it.

Kellan Rusk looked at the four bare lines under the two words in the corner on his way out and then at the third column of the sheet of terms, which is ruled and empty under a heading that has never been improved, and the sheet of what this holding does not know has six ruled rows and no seventh cut for any of the four things it has been asked about.

The compost line stands with paid against twenty-nine and nothing has gone out in it, and it will not go out until twenty-six mornings after the last time it moved.

The ladder went up no rungs and no figure stands against either of the two people on it. The offer on the low board has not been dated or moved or taken up or taken back. The ring of bare ground has no foot in it and carries no number, and what is standing on the low field is a separate piece of ground altogether.

The clerk locked the one room at noon and turned the key in the yard lock, and the man of about fifty walked past the nail in the doorframe and looked at the key on it for longer than he had on any of the mornings before, and did not touch it, and went home.

Slates were stacked on the shelf in the one room by four o'clock, and the clerk put the blank one in his coat pocket and left the written one face down on the shelf where anybody coming in could turn it over if they wanted to, and it was the first time the yard had kept a slate instead of a person.

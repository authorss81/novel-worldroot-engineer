# Chapter 602

## The Fifth Of The Sixteenth, And A Seal Put Down Instead Of Pressed

Tuesday gave them a morning with no wind in it at all, which made the frost sit where it fell, and the yard had a stillness in it that four people came into separately and mentioned to each other.

The clerk took the launder at the long wall at nine and read it into the quiet so that it carried to the seed house and did not have to be said twice, and the man of about fifty said from behind him that he could not hear the last of it.

**"Once more off the wall, and not off the page, because I want the sound of it and not the shape of it, and a figure you have only read is a figure you have copied."**

**One thousand and one hundred and fifty-five hundredweight.**

Five came off Monday and that is the whole movement, and it is the three hundred and sixty-fifth figure of a run that has gone four years without repeating itself.

**"A fall of five on a Tuesday because a Tuesday is an odd morning, and the rule has not changed in four years and it is not going to change for me today."**

The halves gave a hundred and seventy-one and a hundred and sixty-one, and those two together are three hundred and thirty-two, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the addition out of her mouth before the clerk got his pencil down.

**"Three hundred and thirty-two, and I have been adding that pair up in my head for two days now and I still get it right and I am still not going to be the one who writes it."**

The morning run was read at the seventh hour out of the second book in the doorway, being an odd morning.

**One hundred and fifty-three mornings of two hundred and eighty-nine.**

The heading over it is of a month nine months back and has not shifted, and the clerk said the run and nothing about what it was for, because the two figures in it do not make a fraction and the making of one out of them would be the making of something.

Three hundred and sixty-three days stand against the four bodies of households, and the clause written under that figure takes the three hundred and sixty-second out of three hundred and sixty-fourth, one under and one over, and the wall figure and that sheet have never been put together in this holding and are not going to be.

Seven hundred and forty-nine on the near board against seven hundred and ninety-six on the far one, and the gap of forty-seven doing what it has done every morning since the boards were cut, and the drawer behind the near one shut at every hour without anybody touching it.

The clerk squared the books at the middle table and then said the other thing, and he said it to the books rather than to the yard, and he gave the reason before the claim.

**"There are two sentences this man has said in this yard. He has not said them this month. I am not going to enter them out of my own memory, and I am not going to enter a line that says they were not said, because a line saying that is a copy of them in the shape of an absence."**

He put his hand flat on the second book and left it there.

**"The clerk's book says what the clerk saw. What I saw this month is a man working a gate and a man standing at a table. If anybody wants the two sentences they have to have been in the yard on the mornings they were said, and there were four of those."**

The man of about fifty brought the seal down off the shelf at about the eleventh hour and put it on the table, and then put his hand flat beside it and did not press it, and gave the reason first to nobody in particular.

**"Nine years I have pressed this and I have not read out one of them, and today I have put it on a table where six people can see it and my hand is not moving, and that is not a protest and it is not a demonstration, it is a tired man's arm."**

He took the seal back and put it on the shelf where the nails are and left it there out of its box.

**"If it wants pressing it will be pressed by somebody told what it is for. That is not me being careful. That is me being finished with guessing."**

The woman of about thirty-eight got down under the standpipe with a spanner and a piece of sacking at about the eighth hour and came up wet to the elbow and told the yard what she had done before anybody asked her whether she had done anything.

**"It has been dripping since the wet came in and it has been dripping into a bucket that a man has to empty, and I have packed the joint with sacking and I am telling everybody that now because in four days the fourth can will be short and I would rather they heard it from me than work it out."**

She wiped her hands on her coat and put the spanner in the tap house where it lives.

**"That is a decision and I have made it, and nobody is to go round behind me and put the sacking back out because the bucket was working."**

Nobody in the yard said anything about the seal for the rest of the morning.

Tova Reed came out of the seed house at noon and stood in the sun with dust on her sleeves and looked at the seal on the shelf from the yard and did not go over and ask about it, and said one line about the sheets and gave the reason for saying it.

**"Three pieces of paper and nine inches of board between each, and the one that is open has been open two months and the one that is folded has been folded a month, and neither of them is going to get better by being handled."**

She went back in.

The clerk derived the three ages and read them in the order they sit in the rack, the form, the charter, Silling's book.

**"Silling's second of four ruled lines first, because it is the one nobody looks at: two hundred and fifty-three days and nothing on it this morning or any morning. Then the charter, a hundred and sixty-five. Then the form, two hundred and fifty-nine, not filled."**

Nothing stands under two words in the corner of the one room, and the third column of the sheet of terms has its heading and no entries under it, and six rows are ruled on the sheet of not knowns with nothing against any row and no seventh row cut for the covenant or the machine or the schedule or the confession.

Three sheets are on the long table, and the first has lain face up for sixty-two days with a hand's width of bare board either side, the second is still folded, the third carries a name and a date and a hand and a seal and is correct in every particular and has not been called anything else by anybody in this yard.

Nia Vale said a thing about the folded one and then stopped and gave the reason she was stopping.

**"I have said my piece about that paper in three yards and I am stopping because the fourth time it comes out of me it comes out with a figure in it and the figure will be the wrong one."**

The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor and not a total. The use log holds fifteen lines. The seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off reads sixty-six, unread, and going no further back. The man of about seventy was not fetched and nobody asked him anything.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and no one has discharged anything in twenty-eight.

Wax in a box out on a shelf where the nails are, and a still yard, and a clerk who will not enter an absence in the shape of a sentence.

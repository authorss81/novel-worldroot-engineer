# Chapter 604

## The Seventh Of The Sixteenth, And A Line Kept Short Because A Yard Is Not A Room

Thursday was clear and cold with the sun coming up over the far board and lying along the long wall by seven, which put the chalked launder figure in light for the first time in three days.

The clerk read it at nine from the wall with the sun behind it and had to shade his own eyes to see the line, and read it again for the two people who had shaded for him.

**One thousand and one hundred and fifty-eight hundredweight.**

Five came off Wednesday and it is the three hundred and sixty-seventh figure of a run of forty-nine distinct numbers that has not repeated in four years of keeping it.

**"A fall of five because Thursday is an odd morning, and no morning in this run has ever stood still, and that is a rule I did not make and would break if I could."**

The halves stood on the wall a hundred and seventy-two one way and a hundred and sixty-two the other, and those two came to three hundred and thirty-four, and the addition was said by four people at once and disagreed about by nobody.

The morning run was read at the seventh hour out of the second book in the open doorway, being an odd morning.

**One hundred and fifty-four mornings of two hundred and ninety-one.**

The clerk said that the numerator and the denominator in that pair do not make a fraction and must not be made into one, and that the denominator stepping by two on an odd morning is why the two halves of it move at different rates and that this is not a fault in the reading.

**"One hundred and fifty-four step by one and two hundred and ninety-one step by two, and if a man writes that as a percentage in a book in this county he will be taken apart by somebody."**

Tova Reed read the sheet of the four bodies of households off the long wall standing square to it, three hundred and sixty-five on the line, and beneath that line the two figures the paper has kept for the mornings either side of it, the three hundred and sixty-fourth and the three hundred and sixty-sixth, and she said then that a sheet being right is not the same thing as being finished with it, and put it back where it lies.

Near board seven hundred and fifty-one and far board seven hundred and ninety-eight, with the drawer behind the near board shut at every hour and not opened by anybody, the man of about fifty included.

The man who has the key is the man of about fifty, and he said so himself, standing by the wall with his hand about a foot from the drawer.

**"It is in my pocket and it has been in my pocket since Tuesday, and I have not used it, and nobody has asked me to, and I am telling you that now so that nobody has to go looking for whether I have used it."**

Then he took it out. It was a short iron thing with a flat bow on a ring that had gone green, and he held it up where the yard could see it, and then he crossed to the door of the one room and hung it on a nail that has been in that doorframe for longer than the holding has had a book, and it hung there in the open where anybody coming in would have to go past it.

**"It is not in my pocket now and it is not behind the drawer and it is not in the yard either," he said. "It is on that nail. If I want it I come back past everybody who comes past that door, and so does anybody who wants to ask me what is behind that drawer. I would like that to be the arrangement for as long as I am the one holding it."**

Nobody took it down. The clerk looked at it for a while and then wrote nothing, and Renn Ashby, who had been in and out past that door all week, said that a nail in a doorframe was the honestest thing he had seen anybody do in four years, and went back to his gate without explaining himself.

Tova Reed had her good ear turned to the wall all morning, because that is where the reading comes from, and she caught everything said at the wall on the first saying and did not ask one person to repeat themselves. At the ninth hour, when the chalk was in the sun and hard to see, she walked round to the far side of the wall and read it out of the shadow instead, and the yard watched her do that and let her.

Marek came out at the tenth hour, looked at the three sheets, then looked at the key on the nail, and did not go over and look at it closer.

**"I have said those two things in this yard before and I am not going to keep saying them here," he said. "Not because a yard is not a room. A yard is a room with more people in it and a gate. It is because there is a man in this yard who has just put a key on a nail in front of everybody, and I would be standing here asking this yard to hold something for me on the morning somebody gave up holding it themselves."**

He put one hand flat on the bare board between the first sheet and the second.

**"They are said whole or not at all, and nobody in this yard is owed a shortened one of either. And if a thing only gets said in the right room then it is not a thing anybody says. It is a thing one room has."**

Tova Reed was at the seed house door with a tray under each arm and had taken the whole of that on the side he had given her, and she said one thing before she went in.

**"You have given that reason in three different shapes this week and the shape was never the reason."**

Marek did not raise his voice to it.

**"It was not on a nail on Tuesday."**

**"No."** She went in with the trays.

The clerk derived the three ages and gave them in the order they stand in the rack.

He put the three in front of the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden face up and let her read them, because a figure another person reads out loud is a figure that cannot be got wrong by one mouth.

**"Two hundred and sixty-one days on that form, a hundred and sixty-seven on the charter, two hundred and fifty-five on the second ruled line. None of them filled and none of them refused."**

She read them twice and gave the papers back in the same order she had taken them.

Nia Vale wiped her hands on her coat at the door of the one room and counted the four bare lines under the two words with the corner of her thumb, one at a time, because counting them takes less time than saying they are empty, and the third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and has nothing under its heading, and on the sheet of what this holding does not know she put her finger on the sixth row and lifted it, and there is nothing cut below the sixth.

There are three sheets on the long table this morning, spaced about nine inches apart with bare board between: the first sixty-four days old and face up, the second folded with its crease turned the other way, and the third with a name at the head, a date, a hand and a seal, correct in every particular. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes, and not one of the three sheets is in either.

Sennik Vaul said the force that was worked on Monday and was not worked again today, and said the number of the morning was a different thing from the number of the work.

**"A hundred and thirty in force was Monday's and Monday is over. I am not carrying it into a Thursday because it would read better in a sentence about how things are going."**

Harlan Vetch took his stick off the wall and left it leaning by the bench.

Nia Vale had the water butt out and was throwing the hard contents on the low field edge, and stopped halfway to say one thing about the fourth can and then went on throwing.

**"It was going to be short on Friday and it is going to be short, and the yard has had three mornings to do something about it and the yard has not, so that is the yard's arithmetic and not mine."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the standpipe joint packed with sacking on Tuesday and came out at the ninth hour to look at it and said it had stopped dripping and gave her reasons in the order she had them.

**"It has stopped dripping, and I packed it and I am not letting anybody undo it on me, and if it starts again on Sunday then it starts again and I will pack it again."**

Renn Ashby came past the gate at the ninth hour and reported that the gate had shut hard three mornings running now and that he had not touched it once since the pin went in, and that he wanted somebody to be told that in case it stopped being true.

**"Three mornings and I have not touched it, and I am saying so out loud because a thing that works can stop working quietly and then everybody decides it never worked."**

He went and stood at the gate to be near the thing he had just reported on.

Kellan Rusk was not in this yard this morning and the folded page stayed in his coat, and Sera Quill copied nothing and witnessed the wall being read, which is a thing she can do standing up in the sun.

The barrow stands along that wall with eleven journeys chalked down its side, where eleven is a floor, and the use log on its shelf has fifteen lines in it with the sixteenth unwritten, and nobody wrote it this morning with a whole day free and nothing in the yard that needed carrying. The door nine hundred yards off has sixty-six in its seventh column, and it was not read, and it does not go back, and the twenty-nine fetchings against the man of about seventy are as many as they were before, and he was not fetched and nobody put a question to him.

The compost line carries paid against twenty-eight and there has been no discharge in twenty-eight, and the fall will arrive on a morning of its own.

That ladder went up no rungs this morning, and there is a figure against neither of the two people standing on it. The offer on the low board lies where it has lain, undated, untaken and not withdrawn. Nothing walked into the ring of bare ground and no figure stands against it, and the ground that came back on the low field is a different piece of ground and the two are not added together.

The clerk shut the one room at noon and the key went on hanging on its nail through the lock-up. When he came out and turned the yard key, the man of about fifty was standing in the middle of the yard to see whether anybody would make him take it down.

Nobody made him take it down. He looked at the nail for a while, and then went and did his work, and it was still on the nail at dusk, and it will be on the nail in the morning, and that is now a fact about this holding and not a fact about one man's coat.

# Chapter 601

## The Fourth Of The Sixteenth, And The Cycle Come Round On A Monday

Monday came in blue and hard and got no warmer till eleven, and the fourth line came round at the second hour and was worked, and Sennik Vaul and Harlan Vetch went out on the headland together because a headland in frost is safer with two than with one.

The clerk counted the force with them and Harlan Vetch counted it a second time separately and both came to the same thing.

**A hundred and thirty in force with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and one not.**

**"Twenty-nine and a hundred and one is a hundred and thirty, and that addition is the check and the check is the only reason I will say the number out loud in a field with nobody in it but us."**

Sennik Vaul put his stick in the ground and said the other half of it, the two reckonings that have never agreed, seventy-eighth and sixty-fifth, thirteen apart, and the gap holding four years.

**"Seventy-eight and sixty-five and thirteen between them, and I have watched four men try to close that thirteen and every one of them closed it by being wrong somewhere else."**

No field was taken this morning. Nobody went out into a strip with a form and a line and came back with something written, and the clerk said that out loud on the headland because it is the morning it is hardest to say.

**"No field on a morning the cycle comes round is a thing a man can be asked for by anybody standing here, and I am saying it before anybody asks, and no one is to be blamed afterwards for a field that waited."**

The clerk read the launder at the wall at nine with his back to the frost line, and Harlan Vetch came in wet to the knee to hear it and stood where he could not see the page.

**One thousand and one hundred and sixty hundredweight.**

Eight came on to Saturday's figure, and a rise on an even morning is what the alternation gives, and this is the three hundred and sixty-fourth number of a run in which nothing has ever been counted in twice.

The halves on the long wall came at a hundred and seventy-one and a hundred and sixty, and a hundred and seventy-one plus a hundred and sixty is three hundred and thirty-one, and Sennik Vaul added it in the air with his stick and got it and did not repeat himself.

**"A hundred and seventy-one one way and a hundred and sixty the other and three hundred and thirty-one, and the rising half moves on an even morning and the falling half moves on an odd one, and neither of them moves because anybody said so."**

There was no figure read for the morning run at all this morning. Monday is an even morning and that run belongs to the odd mornings, and the second book stayed where it was under its weight, and the heading of a month nine months back where the run goes did not move.

Three hundred and sixty-two days stand on the sheet of the four bodies of households, and the clause under the number takes the three hundred and sixty-first out of three hundred and sixty-third, one under and one over, and the wall and that sheet stay two different figures that this holding does not add.

Seven hundred and forty-eight on the near board and seven hundred and ninety-five on the far one, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour from the second to the ninth.

Harlan Vetch said the thing about the two sentences while the two of them were taking their boots off at the door, and he said it plainly and without any weight on it, and he was not asking Marek anything.

**"You have not said your two things and I am not going to be the man on this headland who asks you whether you have stopped saying them or how many times you have said them, and that is the whole of my interest in it."**

He set his boots on the step and left them there.

**"I have kept a number four years that does not agree with another number four years, and that is company enough. I do not need yours."**

The clerk gave the derived ages at the middle table in one line and put his hand flat on the form without touching the writing space.

Harlan Vetch asked for the three ages off the top of his head in the order they sit in the rack, and the clerk gave them, and the addition was not attempted by either of them.

**"Two hundred and fifty-eight on the form, and it is blank. A hundred and sixty-four on the charter. Two hundred and fifty-two on the second line in Silling's book, and it is blank too, and so is the first one. Three blanks on one morning and no ruling on any of them."**

Four lines stand bare in the corner of the one room under two words, and nothing has been owing on them since before this holding had a book about it. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and has nothing in it and its heading has not been improved. Seven sessions are in this holding's book and no eighth was entered and nobody is sitting in a room this morning.

Sennik Vaul found a boundary stone a foot inside the line at about the eighth hour and stood looking at it long enough that Harlan Vetch came over to see what he was looking at, and the two of them lifted it and set it back where the frost ring showed it had been, and neither of them said anything about it at the time.

**"A stone a foot in and out again inside an hour, and that is the whole of the work done on the headland this morning, and I am not putting a foot in anything."**

Harlan Vetch dusted his knees and said a thing he has said before and meant again.

**"A wall outlives the men that set it, and I said that for comfort and I am saying it again because it is true and because a morning like this one wants it."**

Three sheets lie on the long table. The first is sixty-one days old face up with about nine inches of bare board on either side of it, the second folded with its crease the wrong way for anybody who wanted to open it, and the third carrying a name and a date and a hand and a seal and correct in every particular. None of the three was entered and none was refused and no column was ruled under any of them.

Nia Vale said the fifty-three requests and the fifty-three notes were the same number and had been for months and that she had stopped finding that interesting.

**"Fifty-three and fifty-three and none of them is a letter from that council and none of them is the third sheet either, and a number being equal to another number is not a thing two numbers are for."**

The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and the eleventh is a floor. Fifteen lines stand in the use log and the sixteenth is not written and was not written this morning by two men who were out all morning on a headland where nothing needed carrying. Sixty-six is on the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off and was not read.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nobody put a question to him, and the count of questions put to him is nothing at every site.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and nothing has been discharged in twenty-eight and the fall will come on its own morning without anybody's leave.

Nothing walked into the ring and no figure stands against it, and the ground that came back on the low field is a different piece of ground and the two are not added. The offer lies where it has lain. No rung of that ladder was climbed and no number was given for either of the two people standing on it.

Sera Quill came out to the headland at about the tenth hour with her coat buttoned to the neck and stood behind the two of them while the force was read a third time for her benefit, and copied nothing at all.

**"I have now heard the same figure from three men in three places this morning and it has come out of all three of them the same, and that is the only paper I am making out of it."**

She went back in without writing her name against it.

Stones set into the headland wall in a frost with two men on it, a count said out loud so that a wrong one could be caught, and a hole in the lane holding its grit.

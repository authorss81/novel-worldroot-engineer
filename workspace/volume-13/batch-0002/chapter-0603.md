# Chapter 603

## The Sixth Of The Sixteenth, And A Book Copied With Nothing Added To It

Wednesday was the first soft morning of the month and the frost came off the low field in a smell of wet that reached the yard before anybody was up in it.

The clerk read the launder at the wall at nine and read it slowly, because half the yard had come back from the low field with wet boots and was standing about.

**One thousand and one hundred and sixty-three hundredweight.**

Eight came on to Tuesday and the run is at the three hundred and sixty-sixth figure, and a rise on an even morning is not a reward for the weather, it is Wednesday.

**"It came up because Wednesday is an even morning. It did not come up because it rained and I want that said before somebody in this yard decides that rain is a reason."**

Two halves went up on the wall, a hundred and seventy-two and a hundred and sixty-one, and the first of them added to the second is three hundred and thirty-three, and nobody added them anywhere else in the yard this morning.

The morning run was not read. Wednesday is an even morning and the run is an odd-morning thing, and the second book did not come off its weight at all, and the heading of a month nine months back stayed where it has stayed.

Three hundred and sixty-four stands against the four bodies of households on the middle table, with the three hundred and sixty-third out of three hundred and sixty-fifth printed under it in a smaller hand, one short and one long, and a chalk figure on a wall outside and a figure on a sheet inside that are two different numbers that nobody here has added together.

The near board carries seven hundred and fifty and the far one seven hundred and ninety-seven, forty-seven between them, and the drawer behind the near one stayed shut at every hour from before light until the clerk went in at noon.

Sera Quill had the copying slate on the middle table from six and copied nothing onto it all morning, and she said why at nine when a man asked her whether the slate was finished.

**"It is finished. It is finished with nothing on it, and a copying slate with a figure on it this morning would be a copy of a figure I have not watched anybody check."**

She turned the slate round so the empty side faced up.

**"I witness and I copy and those are two trades. I have done the first one for nineteen years and I am not going to start the second one at seven in the morning because a man walked past a door."**

The man of about fifty looked at the seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off when he went past it, the way he does, and did not read it aloud, and sixty-six is still what is on it and it did not go back.

**"Sixty-six and I have looked at it every morning for four years and I have not written it down in any book and I am not going to be the reason a door keeps a number for the rest of its life."**

Kellan Rusk came in about the tenth hour with the folded page still in his coat and asked whether anybody had read the third sheet since the last time he was in this yard, and nobody had, and he said that was the correct state of it.

**"Four people have read it and not one of us has written down what it says, and that is not us being careful, that is four people who have nothing to gain from being the one who wrote it."**

He sat on the bench by the gate, which is the bench the pin was on, and put his hands on his knees.

**"A sheet that is right in every particular does not need a defender in the morning. It needs somebody to say what it says in a room where a decision is being taken, and there is no such room in this holding and there is not going to be one on my account."**

Marek came out of the one room at the eleventh hour and looked at the three sheets on the long table and put his hand behind his back and said the thing about not saying the other thing, and this time the reason was that a room was empty.

**"There are three people in this yard and none of them is going to be here in a month, and there are two sentences I could say to those three, and I am not going to say them to people who will remember them, because the whole of what those two sentences are is that they have not stopped being true and a person who hears them twice starts carrying them."**

He took his hand from behind his back and put it on the table, not near any sheet.

**"They are not softened and they are not shortened and they are not going anywhere. They are just not for this morning with this many people in this yard."**

The clerk derived the ages at the middle table in one breath, form first, then charter, then Silling's book, and did not look up in the middle of it.

Sera Quill asked for the three figures and wrote them nowhere, and the clerk gave them from the rack.

**"Two hundred and sixty days on that form. The charter is a hundred and sixty-six and Silling's second ruled line is two hundred and fifty-four. Three blanks in a row on a morning when a man walked past a door, and that is all the morning did."**

Four bare lines stand in the corner of the one room under two words, and the third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved and is not going to be, and six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know with nothing against any of them and no seventh row cut.

On the long table: three sheets, board either side of each, the first sixty-three days old and face up, the second folded, the third carrying a name at the head of it, a date, a hand and a seal, and right in every particular. Entered, refused, ruled under: none, none, none.

The woman of about thirty-eight said the fourth can would be short on Friday because of the sacking she put in on Tuesday, and that she had already told everybody once and was telling them again on purpose.

**"A thing said twice is not a nag if the second time is for somebody who was not in the yard the first time, and there are always two or three of those."**

Nia Vale wrote nothing on the copying slate, because it was not hers, and went back to the step.

Soren Rill put his hand flat on the wall at the top of the middle road where it was still firm and then went down to where it was not, and came back up without saying anything about the difference, and then said it.

**"Firm at the top and gone at the bottom and I have walked it nine mornings and I am not going to write the hours down, because I have told this yard before that my hour is my hour."**

He left the spade in the hole at the soft end, standing in it rather than leaning.

Tova Reed turned the nine trays over one at a time on the line so the damp went off the other face, and did not bring them in, and nobody asked her why not.

The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor and the twelfth is not chalked anywhere. Fifteen lines are in the use log on its shelf and the space under the last one is clean. The man of about seventy has twenty-nine fetchings against him and was not fetched and was not asked.

Paid is the word in front of twenty-eight on the compost line and the discharge in twenty-eight is nothing at all. The ring of bare ground was not walked and has no figure against it and the ground that came back on the low field is a different piece and the two are not added. The offer is undated and unmoved. No rung was climbed and no figure was given for either of the two people on that ladder.

Ink drying on a copying slate with nothing on it, and wet boots drying on a wall, and a door nine hundred yards off holding a number nobody has written down.

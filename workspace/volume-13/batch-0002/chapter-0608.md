# Chapter 608

## The Eleventh Of The Sixteenth, And A Line That Fell Without Anybody Deciding It

Monday came in flat and grey with the wind in the east and nothing in it, and the compost line at the back of the holding went down in the hour before anybody was up, the way a thing goes down when the interval arrives rather than when anybody decides.

Paid at twenty-nine, and the twenty-six mornings' interval off the last one carried it, and it was not asked for by anybody in this holding, and it could not have been stopped by anybody in this holding either.

The clerk was at the wall at nine and read the launder standing up in the flat light.

**One thousand and one hundred and sixty-four hundredweight.**

Five came off Sunday, because Monday is an odd morning and an odd morning takes five, and this is the three hundred and seventy-first figure of a run of forty-nine distinct numbers.

**"The launder went down five and the compost line went down one and neither of them had anything to do with the other and they are not one figure and they are never added."**

Two figures went up on the wall side by side, a hundred and seventy-four and a hundred and sixty-four, and together they came to three hundred and thirty-eight, which held in the flat light.

Out of the second book in the doorway the clerk read the run at the seventh hour, it being an odd morning.

**One hundred and fifty-six mornings of two hundred and ninety-five.**

**"The two numbers in that pair are not a percentage and are not one against the other in any arithmetic. The first steps by one on every odd morning and the second steps by two, and a man in this county who writes that as a rate will be wrong in a way that takes him four counties to find."**

Three hundred and sixty-nine days on the sheet of the four bodies of households, and the clause printed beneath it takes the three hundred and sixty-eighth out of three hundred and seventieth, one under and one over.

Near board seven hundred and fifty-five, far board eight hundred and two, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of the morning including the hour in which the line went down.

**"Paid is the word in front of the figure and it is not discharged in it. It went down because twenty-six mornings arrived and nobody in this holding asked it to and nobody in this holding could have stopped it, and about nine people in this county found that out at the same hour this morning and not one of them was first."**

The clerk said that standing up with the book shut under his arm, and then he said the other part of it.

**"I have got a book in here with a word in it and the word is paid and it is in front of that figure and it does not mean the figure has gone out. It has not gone out. It has moved, the way the launder moves, and the two of them move on rules that nobody in this yard set."**

Marek was at the compost line before the clerk was at the wall and had been there since six, and he came in with his coat grey to the knee, and he stood in the middle of the yard with nobody near him and said the two things whole, out loud, in a yard with a gate shut hard and six people in it.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

He let that stand on the air for a while. He did not explain it and he did not soften it, and the six people in that yard did not ask him what it was about, and that turned out later to have been the wrong thing for all of them.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

He said both of those as well, and then he said why he was saying them on this morning and not on any of the nine before it, and the reason was given first and it was not a good enough reason and he knew that.

**"I have not said either of them in the nine mornings since the first of the month, and before that I said them five times in thirty days, and I have stood here nine mornings telling this yard that I was not going to say them, and a man who announces that he is not going to speak is still speaking every morning."**

He wiped his hands on his coat.

**"A line that fell at six this morning because twenty-six mornings arrived is not a decision and it is nobody's loss that anybody chose it. Those two sentences are the other kind of thing. They are not chosen and they do not arrive on an interval and they are not this yard's business, and if they are going to be said then they are said whole and said once and not announced."**

Then he did not know what to do with his hands, which is the part nobody in that yard saw, and he found out afterward that the two sentences had cost him nothing that morning and had still cost him everything, and that those two facts are not in the same place and do not cancel.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing about it from the tap and then let it go, and she said it once.

**"He has said a thing five times in a month that I have heard once, and I am not going to be the woman who stands in a yard and asks a man to keep a sentence for her."**

She put a heel of bread and a bit of cheese on the edge of the long table and went back to the tap, and did not look at him again while he was standing there.

**"I am going to be the woman who asks him to eat something."**

Renn Ashby, who had been holding the gate since six, said the one thing he had been holding since Friday.

He went and shut the gate hard and stood with his back to it.

**"I have had that in my pocket for three days waiting for a morning it was worth saying it on, and I did not think it was ever going to be one, and it is not now either."**

Sera Quill came to the middle table and did not sit down, and put her slate on it flat, and said that she had been in this holding nineteen years and had heard a good deal said at the wall and in doorways and that she was not going to write one word of this morning, and that anybody who wanted to know what had been said would have to have been standing at the middle of that yard, which was the only place it had been said.

**"That is not a record of it," she said, getting the slate back up. "That is me declining to make one. Those are different and I have been the only person making the second one for a long time."**

Marek stood in the middle of the yard for a while longer with nobody near him, and then went and sat down at the foot of the long table on the bench, on the end of it, with his hands on his knees the way Kellan Rusk had sat on Tuesday.

Tova Reed came out of the seed house with a tray under each arm, and stopped when she saw where he was, and did not ask him a single question about any of it, and put a tray down on the bench beside him, and sat down on the end of it with the other tray in her lap.

**"You told a yard full of people on Saturday morning that I had taken something off you," she said, after a while, "and I have been seven days getting ready to be angry about it, and I have not been angry once, and I do not know what that is."**

**"I know what it is," Marek said. "It is that you were right and I was annoyed, and being right is a thing you do and being annoyed is a thing I do, and it took me four days to work out that those could be in the same morning."**

Tova Reed did not let him have that one.

**"And you went to the compost line at six because of it?"**

**"I went to the compost line at six because there was nothing else to be doing at six."**

She picked a piece of weed out of the tray in her lap, and then said the other thing, which is the thing she had carried up the low field on Saturday and had not put down since.

**"You can say that to yourself on your own time."**

She turned the tray in her lap and looked into it rather than at him.

**"There is going back in the middle of that heap. Three places this month, and I have not said where from, and I am not going to, because where from is a line and I have not drawn one."**

Marek looked out at the low field for a while.

**"Does it matter what is in it?"**

**"No," she said. "That is the whole of what I have learned this month and I learned it from a man who could not say a sentence he had said five times, so I am not going to insult you by making it long."**

They sat on the end of the bench with the trays and did not say anything else for a while, and the clerk came past with the book shut under his arm and looked at the two of them and went round the other side of the table.

On the long table there are three sheets and about nine inches of bare board on either side of each. The first is sixty-eight days old and lies face up. The second is folded. The third carries a name at the head of it, a date, a hand and a seal, and is right in every particular.

The clerk derived the ages at the middle table and read them in one breath, and the form was two hundred and sixty-five days old and the charter a hundred and seventy-one and Silling's second ruled line two hundred and fifty-nine with nothing on it.

**"The form has been on that table two hundred and sixty-five days. The charter behind it has been in this holding a hundred and seventy-one. The second ruled line in Silling's book has been empty two hundred and fifty-nine days, and a line that has been empty that long is not waiting for anybody."**

Four ruled lines stand under two words in the corner of the one room and not one word has ever gone on them. A third column is ruled on the sheet of terms and stands empty under a heading nobody has improved. Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know and all six are bare, and no seventh has been cut for the covenant or for the machine or for the schedule or for the confession.

Fifty-three requests stand in one column and fifty-three section-nine notes stand in the other, and neither of them has taken any of the three sheets, and the thirty-nine blanks on the board in the one room are thirty-nine, and nothing in this yard moved one of them, and a compost line moving does not count as something that can.

The barrow is against the wall at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor, and the use log holds fifteen lines with the sixteenth not written. On the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off stands sixty-six, and it did not go back today. The man of about seventy was fetched by nobody, stands at twenty-nine fetchings, and had no question put to him and will have none.

Nobody climbed a rung of that ladder on the morning the line fell, and no figure was printed against either of the two people standing on it. The offer on the low board has not been read out, dated, moved or taken back. No foot went into the ring of bare ground and nothing stands against it in figures, and the ground that came up on the low field is a different piece and is not added to it.

The key was still on its nail in the one room door when the clerk locked up at noon, and the man of about fifty walked past it on his way out and stopped and then did not stop, and went home, and it will be on the nail tomorrow.

At dusk the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden put the tap off at the back of the tap house the way she does, and Nia Vale put the bread and the cheese that had not been eaten back in the seed house with a cloth over it, and the two of them did not discuss it.

# Chapter 635

## The Eighth Of The Seventeenth, And A Slate Turned Face Down Until Dusk

Tova Reed put the three figures on the back of a slate at nine in the morning, with a bit of chalk, and then turned the slate face down on the bench by the seed house door and left it there, and nobody was told to leave it and nobody moved it, and it lay that way until the light went at four.

**"It is there if anybody wants it,"** she said. **"It is not there for reading. It is there so that a person who cannot be in this room at nine can be told at four that somebody wrote it down, and that is all it is for, and if anybody turns it over before four I will be annoyed in a way I can explain."**

**"Why can you not be in this room at nine,"** Kellan Rusk said.

**"Because I am at the seed house at nine and the trays go out at nine and they have gone out at nine since before either of us,"** she said. **"That is the whole reason and it is a work reason and you are going to hate it."**

The two hundred and ninety-two, a hundred and ninety-eight and two hundred and eighty-six went onto that slate in her own hand and stayed face down under a flat stone for seven hours, which is the longest any of these three figures has been out of a person's mouth in four years, and the person who said that was Sennik Vaul, who said it out loud at one o'clock to nobody in particular, on the grounds that somebody in this yard should know it.

Renn Ashby was in the doorway at the seventh hour with the second book and did not read it, because it was not an odd morning, and Tova Reed said why from the seed house step with a tray in each hand and did not stop walking while she said it.

**"It is a Sunday,"** she said. **"A Sunday is not an odd morning and there is no pair in the doorway and there is not going to be one. I have been in this yard nine days carrying a piece of paper in a coat and I am not going to be the woman who stands in front of nine people every second morning explaining that the arithmetic does not need my permission."**

**"You do not have to explain it,"** Renn Ashby said.

**"No,"** she said. **"I do not have to and I am giving the reason anyway, because the last woman who did not give a reason in this yard was asked for one in front of eleven people on a Thursday and I was one of the eleven and I remember it."**

The clerk read the wall at ten because Sunday is the day the yard takes longer to arrive.

**One thousand and two hundred and eleven hundredweight.**

**"Eight on,"** he said, **"three hundred and ninety-eighth of the run, and I am going to say the ordinal out loud this morning because a run of forty-nine that nobody has ever said the number of is just a list, and this is the forty-ninth and it is a rise and it is not the biggest of them but it is one of the forty-nine and those are different facts."**

The two halves went on the slate one after another with nothing between them. The rising one had moved again and is a hundred and eighty-eight. The falling one had not moved since Thursday and is a hundred and seventy-seven. They came to three hundred and sixty-five, and the sum has now not been said out loud for five mornings, and the clerk was asked about that on Friday and answered it again on Saturday and was asked about it this morning by Harlan Vetch, who got the same answer and made the same face.

The long sheet is at three hundred and ninety-six for the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath it, which is never read with it, is the three hundred and ninety-fifth underneath and the three hundred and ninety-seventh above. Seven hundred and eighty-two at the near end of the wall and eight hundred and twenty-nine at the far end, and the forty-seven is the forty-seven, and the drawer behind the near one has been shut all day and the key has been on the nail all day.

Paid is in front of thirty on the compost line and has been let out in nothing. Saturday stepped it up a number at an hour nobody chose, and a Sunday is not an interval, and it will not step again for twenty-six mornings.

Fifteen lines in the use log. Eleven journeys on the barrow and eleven a floor. Thirty-nine blanks and the second rule open and empty. Seven sessions in this holding's book and a Sunday is not an eighth.

Fifty-three of one thing and fifty-three of another, and not one of the three sheets has ever gone into either of them, and neither has the page in a coat. The first of the three sheets is ninety-five days old this morning.

The six rows against what this holding does not know stand bare and the paper under the sixth has nothing cut on it, and this morning the four things that would go in a seventh were named in full and out loud, all four of them, by a man who is not a clerk and does not keep any of the other books.

**"Covenant, machine, schedule, confession,"** Harlan Vetch said, at the low field, to nobody who had asked. **"I have heard those four said in this yard maybe nine times in four years and every one of them was said by somebody standing next to a paper, and I have said them this morning standing next to grass, because a name is a name and it does not need a witness with a pen."**

**"There is no seventh row,"** the clerk called from the door.

**"There is no seventh row,"** Harlan Vetch said, **"and I have not cut one, and I would like it noted that the four were said in a place where nobody could rule anything."**

Nobody went for the man of about seventy this morning and no question was put to him at any site. Out on the gate road there is a board door with a figure scratched into its seventh column, and the figure is sixty-six, and it has stood there through five Sundays and was not looked at on this one. A Sunday is a morning a man who is saving something would choose to spend it on, and nobody spent it.

Nobody climbed the ladder. The two people who belong to it were not on it and were not measured by it, and one of them was in the yard in the morning and was asked by Kellan Rusk whether anybody was coming up it this week, and said no, and gave the reason, and the reason was that going up a ladder on a Sunday is the kind of thing a holding does to look as though it has decided something.

**"Then do not,"** Kellan Rusk said.

**"I was not going to,"** the man said. **"I told you so that you would not have to ask."**

The offer on the low board is undated and unmoved and untaken and not taken back. The ring of bare ground at the top of the low field has never had a boot in it and never had a number put to it, and nothing that came up on that field has ever been added to it. The four ruled lines under two words are bare, and beside them a column on the terms sheet has a heading over it that has stood the same since the sheet was ruled, and under the heading there is nothing at all. The mark on the tool house wall is four inches and branches twice and has not grown a second mark.

Marek spent Sunday on the tap house side, at the outer end of the yard, where there is nothing to mend, and came in at two with cold hands and said the thing.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning, and I am going to be honest about the reason, which is that Silling asked me a question in this yard on Thursday and I gave him a whole answer, and I have been chewing on it for two days. A man who has given a full answer has not got a full second answer left in him. If I stand here and be loud about something that cannot be mended while I am still chewing on something I did answer, then one of the two is going to be a performance and I would like to know which one."**

**"Which one is it then,"** Nia Vale said.

**"I do not know,"** Marek said, **"and that is why I am not going to do it."**

The stone came off the slate at four when the light went, and three people read the three figures off the back of it in the failing light rather than ask her to say them again, and one of the three said them wrong and Sera Quill told him which one and by how much, and he wrote nothing down and said he would remember it, which nobody in this yard believes.

The clerk turned the yard key at dusk only, because Sunday has one turn and not two. The key to the drawer behind the near board was on its nail at dusk, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut through the whole of a Sunday on which a woman wrote three figures on the back of a slate and turned it over and left it there until the light went.

Wool is what the seed house keeps in the sacks by the bench, and on Sunday it came out and was beaten and put back, and the beating made enough noise that four people talked over each other for twenty minutes without any of them noticing, which is the closest this holding comes to a conversation that nobody arranged.

# Chapter 632

## The Fifth Of The Seventeenth, And A Woman Who Read Three Figures Across Ninety Yards Twice

The heading of the first of the three sheets on the long table had not been read out loud in the yard by anybody in the four years it has lain there, and Silling asked for it at ten on Thursday in the tone of a man asking for the salt.

**"It is a line and a place,"** he said. **"A place is on it. Somebody put a place at the top of it and I have never once heard anybody say the place out loud in this yard, and I would like it said before the sheet is a hundred days old."**

The clerk did not want to and did it anyway, which is a thing he does about three times a winter.

**"It is headed with this holding and no other,"** he said, **"and the hand under it is not one of ours, and that is the whole of what is written at the top of it. Whoever sent it wrote where it was going, and we have never read that part out because it has never needed reading, and now a man has asked and there it is."**

**"That is our name,"** Kellan Rusk said.

**"It is our name and it is correct and it is not a question and it is not a fault,"** the clerk said, and nobody in the yard looked at the third sheet differently for having heard him say so.

The run went up at the seventh hour and Renn Ashby read it out of the doorway with the page turned his way and the stone on the corner, and one voice came in on the last sentence, one, which he has not mentioned and which two people mentioned for him before noon.

**"One hundred and sixty-eight mornings of three hundred and nineteen,"** he said. **"The heading is of a month nine months back and it is going to be of a month nine months back next week as well, and the reason that it can be is that the heading describes the pair and not the day. If somebody in a hundred years moves that heading to the month they are standing in, then the two figures underneath it stop being a pair and start being a percentage of a fortnight."**

Harlan Vetch came in from the wall in the middle of that and did not apologize for it and nobody asked him to.

**"One voice this morning,"** Nia Vale said, at the seed house step.

**"One voice and a man coming through a gate,"** Renn Ashby said. **"If I start keeping the number of them then the yard will start managing them, and a yard that manages the voices will read it quieter and a yard that reads it quieter is a yard that has stopped reading it."**

The clerk read the wall and got to it before the frost did, because on Wednesday the frost took the top half of the chalk off and he had gone over it with the side of his hand at half past six in the dark.

**One thousand and two hundred hundredweight.**

**"Five off,"** he said, **"three hundred and ninety-fifth of the run, and the figure is a round one for the first time since this run started, which I am not going to make a thing of, because a round figure is not a different figure."**

The two halves had been on the slate before he got to them and the rising one had not moved on a Thursday, which the yard now knows without being told, because the second half of a window only moves when the first one does and nobody has ever asked why.

A hundred and eighty-six. A hundred and seventy-six. Three hundred and sixty-two, and the sum was not said out loud by anybody, and three mornings have now gone by in a row without the sum being said, and Hesta Lyle remarked on that at noon to nobody in particular and was told by the clerk that the figures do not need a ceremony and have never needed one.

Tova Reed read the three ages out to the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden across ninety yards of yard at eleven, standing on the seed house step with the sun behind her and the rack on her own side of the building, and the woman of about thirty-eight stood at the tap with a can under each arm and did not come over.

**"The register form in that rack is two hundred and eighty-nine days old,"** Tova Reed called. **"Behind it, a charter of a hundred and ninety-five. Last of the three, and last in my mouth, is the second ruled line in Silling's own book at two hundred and eighty-three, and nothing has ever been written on it."**

**"Say the last one again,"** the woman of about thirty-eight called back.

**"Two hundred and eighty-three, and it is bare, and I know what I said."**

**"That is twice,"** Tova Reed said, to the woman of about thirty-eight and to the yard and to herself. **"I have read them twice and I want to say what I can and cannot stand behind, because I do not have the same ear for a voice at ninety yards that I had at thirty. The first pair I am sure of. The bare line I am not, and I read it the same both times, and the reason I am not sure of it is that a woman with two cans under her arms does not ask a question twice unless she did not catch it."**

**"Then it is not settled,"** the woman of about thirty-eight said.

**"It is not settled,"** Tova Reed said, **"and I am not going to be the one who says it is."**

Nobody touched her arm and nobody said one word about her ear and nobody took a tray off her.

The long sheet stands at three hundred and ninety-three. Under that line, in the small hand, one less and one more, and the two figures in that clause are the three hundred and ninety-second and the three hundred and ninety-fourth and the line itself is the one in between them. The four lines carry two signatures each and not one name on any of them. The boards are at seven hundred and seventy-nine and eight hundred and twenty-six, and the drawer behind the near one is shut, and its key is on the nail.

Harlan Vetch put his hand flat on the two stones of the third course at the north end and left it there a long time, and then said the thing he has been not saying since Monday.

**"Three times now somebody has offered me a figure for that wall and three times I have said no,"** he said. **"The reason is not that it would be wrong. The reason is that I have dug that bed four inches deeper on purpose, without a level, so that a stone I did not like would sit where I wanted it instead of where it wanted to go, and if there is a number on that wall in ten years then that number will be a record of me deciding something by hand on a Saturday because it was easier."**

**"Then why did you do it,"** the clerk said.

**"Because it is easier,"** Harlan Vetch said. **"And on Friday I will take them out again and put them back at the depth they were at, and you will all be able to see me do it, and afterwards you can put a figure on it or not, and it will not matter, and that is the whole reason."**

The count in force did not rise on a Thursday. Still a hundred and thirty-seven, twenty-nine taken, a hundred and eight not, and the two reckonings on Sera Quill's slate are thirteen apart with the third line under them in her own hand, and the second of the two is carried forward and no printed rule in this holding has ever been shown to anybody for moving it.

Paid is in front of twenty-nine. Fifteen lines in the use log. Eleven journeys on the barrow and eleven a floor. Seven sessions and no eighth. Fifty-three and fifty-three and no sheet and no page. Thirty-nine blanks and a second rule open and empty. Sixty-six in the seventh column, not read, one reading left. Twenty-nine fetchings and no question asked. The ladder and the offer and the ring of bare ground are where four years ago put them, and the six bare rows took nothing this morning, and the four things that would go in a seventh row were not said out loud.

Marek did not go near the wall and did not go near the tap and spent the whole of Thursday on the north end of a third course he was not building, which two people in that yard decided on their own not to mention to him.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, at four, **"and the reason is that Harlan Vetch told this yard at ten o'clock that he dug a bed four inches deeper to make a stone do what he wanted. That is a man quoting his own silence. I am not going to stand up in a yard and be the next voice after that, because the next voice after a thing like that is always a bigger one."**

The clerk turned the yard key at noon and at dusk and looked at the nail on his way past. The second key was on it at both hours, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut through a Thursday in which the top line of a ninety-two-day-old sheet was read aloud for the first time in four years and turned out to be the name of the holding it was already lying in.

Hollow is the word for the inside of a hose, and the standpipe at the far end of the tap house has one, and on Thursday it was not looked at, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went past it eleven times without turning her head.

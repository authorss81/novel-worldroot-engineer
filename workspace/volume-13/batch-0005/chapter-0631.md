# Chapter 631

## The Fourth Of The Seventeenth, And A Man Who Was Offered The Chalk And Would Not Take It

The joint at the back of the tap house was found weeping at a quarter past six by a woman walking past with an empty can, and by eight o'clock three people had put a thumb under the thread and taken it away and agreed that there was nothing to be done about it, which is the correct finding and is much harder to reach than a fault.

Marek did not go out to it. He had said on Monday that he would come out to it when there was something to see, and there was a bead of water on the iron every few minutes and nothing else on the iron at all, so he stayed on the north end of the wall with Harlan Vetch and did not go.

**"A weep is not a leak,"** he said, when the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came past with two cans.

**"No,"** she said. **"And a leak is not a packing. If a man counts those three as one thing then in a year he will have a number and he will not know what the number is counting."**

**"I am not going to count them,"** Marek said. **"And I am not going to pack it, and I want that written on the same slate as the other figures, because in three weeks one of us is going to want to pack it and the other one is going to want to leave it alone."**

**"Then leave it alone,"** she said, and went on with the cans.

Tova Reed brought the trays out at two on the side of the seed house where she can see the water coming, which is where she has worked since the tap went off on Saturday, and where she said this morning that she is going to keep working.

**"I am not asking anybody to move and I am not asking anybody to say anything about it,"** she said, when Harlan Vetch looked over. **"On the Friday I was at the other side and there was a conversation at the wall about a column on a sheet, and I got the words I did not get and none of the words I did get, and I am not going to sit there again being the woman who has to be told."**

**"You want the other bench moved,"** Harlan Vetch said.

**"I want the work where I can see the pails come,"** she said. **"Those are two different sentences and you only had the first one."**

The clerk got to the wall at nine with the frost out of the top half of it, which he had done at half past six in the dark.

**One thousand and two hundred and five hundredweight.**

**"Eight on and three hundred and ninety-fourth,"** he said, **"and Wednesday is an even morning, so I will tell the yard why there is nothing in the doorway before somebody spends the morning looking for it."**

Nia Vale was sitting on the seed house step with her coat buttoned to the throat, and she said it, not the clerk.

**"The pair on the board goes up at the seventh hour on the odd mornings and this is the second of the two days in the week that Renn Ashby is not my problem,"** she said. **"That is the whole reason. I have been in this yard for a fortnight with a piece of paper in my coat that somebody handed me in front of eleven people, and if I say the reason out loud every second morning then I am going to start saying it in order to be listened to."**

**"Nobody has been asking you,"** Kellan Rusk said.

**"No, and it has cost me nothing, and I would like it to go on costing me nothing."**

Harlan Vetch took the two stones out of the third course again at ten in the morning and put them back again inside forty minutes, and he dug nothing, and he used no level, and he told nobody except the two men working next to him, who are the only two people in this yard he has ever told anything to twice.

**"I dug that bed four inches deeper and I have been asked twice now what I want done about it,"** he said. **"And the answer is that I am going to leave it alone until Friday, and on Friday I am either going to put it right or I am going to say why I am not, and I am not going to say anything between now and then."**

**"That is a Thursday you have just invented,"** the clerk said.

**"It has been invented since Monday,"** Harlan Vetch said, **"and I invented it before the frost came, which is the only credit I get for it."**

The two halves came on the slate after the wall and in the order they come on an even morning, which is the rising one first because that is the one that moved. A hundred and eighty-six. A hundred and seventy-five, which does not move on a Wednesday and has not moved since Tuesday. They came to three hundred and sixty-one, and Kellan Rusk added them out loud this once because the frost had taken the chalk off the top half of the wall overnight and the two of them were only on the slate, and he said so before he added them.

Sennik Vaul came into the one room at half past ten and read the three ages off the rack, all three of them, loudly enough to fill the room and not one inch past the door, and then the clerk put the chalk down on the table beside him and pushed it toward him.

**"Put them on the slate,"** the clerk said. **"Both of the last ones. It is not much work."**

**"No,"** Sennik Vaul said.

**"Why not?"**

**"Because of the line under them on Sera Quill's slate,"** he said. **"She wrote down that she copied them forward and that no rule moves them, and the day after I write three figures on a board in here, those three figures will be a fourth copy of a thing nobody can produce a rule for, and in nine years somebody will ask where the first one came from and there will be four places to look instead of two."**

**"That is not a reason to refuse a chalk,"** the clerk said. **"That is a reason to be tidy."**

**"It is a reason,"** Sennik Vaul said, **"and it is mine and it is about a piece of paper and not about you, and I have read them out loud and I have not written them down, and both of those are going to be in this room tomorrow in the same state they are in now."**

He had the register form in his left hand when he started and the charter came off the shelf behind it into his right, and the second ruled line in Silling's own book he read last and did not pick up. Two hundred and eighty-eight. A hundred and ninety-four. Two hundred and eighty-two, and nothing on it at all.

Three hundred and ninety-two is on the long sheet for the four bodies of households, and the small hand underneath has the three hundred and ninety-first out of three hundred and ninety-third, and it is one under and one over, and the four lines still carry two signatures each and not one name between them. The first of the three sheets on the long table is ninety-one days old and lying face up, and the second is folded, and the third is correct in every particular, and nobody has found a column for any of them.

The boards stand at seven hundred and seventy-eight at this end and eight hundred and twenty-five at the other, forty-seven between them as they have been since the day they went up, and the drawer let into the masonry behind the near one has not been opened and its key has been on the nail in the one room door since first light.

The count in force did not rise on a Wednesday. It is still a hundred and thirty-seven, twenty-nine taken, a hundred and eight not, and the sixth row on the sheet of what this holding does not know is still bare, and no seventh row has been cut for any of the four of them, and this morning not one of the four was said out loud at all, only glanced at.

Paid is in front of twenty-nine on the compost line and has been let out in nothing. Fifteen lines in the use log and the sixteenth unwritten. Eleven journeys on the barrow and eleven a floor. Seven sessions in this holding's book and Wednesday is not an eighth, and fifty-three of one thing and fifty-three of another took none of the sheets and did not take the page in Nia Vale's coat.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was put to him. The sixty-six in the seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off was not read this morning and one reading is left in the whole of this holding, and this is a morning on which it could have been read by anybody who walked out and back.

Marek had the north end of the wall from ten until one and went at the mortar with a cold chisel, and did not go out to the tap house at all.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, at one. **"And the reason is a selfish one and I would rather say it was a selfish one than dress it. Tova Reed read three figures out loud across ninety yards of yard to a woman standing at a tap this morning, twice, and said out loud that she could not promise which of her two readings was right. I was forty feet from her for both of them and I heard one and missed one, and I have decided I would rather not be the man who comes back into this yard at one o'clock and is loud after that."**

The six rows and the four ruled lines and the third column on the terms sheet were where they were on Tuesday. The ladder went up nothing. The offer on the low board was not dated and not taken up and not taken back. Nothing has ever been measured in the ring of bare ground and nothing has ever been added to it, and the ground that came up on the low field is a different piece of ground.

The clerk turned the yard key at noon and at dusk. The second key was on its nail at both of those hours, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut through a Wednesday in which a man read three figures out loud in a room and then, when he was offered a piece of chalk, would not write any of them down.

Gravel came off this yard on Wednesday from the wall end, where a man had been chiseling since ten, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden walked it with a broom and stopped at the tap and did not sweep it. It is lying where it fell now. The broom went back into the tool house with its handle the wrong way round and nobody has corrected that either.

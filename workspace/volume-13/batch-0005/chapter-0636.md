# Chapter 636

## The Ninth Of The Seventeenth, And The Last Morning That Pair Is Ever Said On

Renn Ashby read it at the seventh hour with the page turned his way and the stone on the corner, and before he read it he said a thing nobody had asked him to say, and he said it to the yard and not to the book.

**"One hundred and seventy mornings of three hundred and twenty-three,"** he said. **"And that is the last time this pair will ever be said in this yard. The numerator goes on by one and the denominator goes on by two and both of them are true this morning, and there is nothing waiting behind it. Somebody will read this again one day, in a yard like this one, in a building with a door and a nail in it, and it will be a different pair, and none of us will be here."**

Six voices came in on the last sentence of it. He put the stone back on the corner of the book and shut it and stood in the doorway with his hand on the frame for a while.

**"Is that a thing you are allowed to say,"** Nia Vale said.

**"It is a thing I have been saying with my chest for four years and I have never once said it with my mouth. Today I said it out loud and I would like the yard to notice the difference and then never mention it again."**

The clerk read the wall at nine from inside the one room with the door shut, because a load was coming up the fen road and the noise of it goes through that building like water through a pipe. He read it twice, stopped in the middle the first time, and came back to the beginning.

**One thousand and two hundred and six hundredweight.**

**"Five off,"** he said. **"Three hundred and ninety-ninth of the run, and I had to start again because I had a cart behind me, and I am not going to stand here and pretend a man can be at a wall and at a window in his own head at the same time."**

**"You have been doing it for four years,"** Hesta Lyle said.

**"I have been doing it for four years and I have never had a cart behind me on a morning I had to read it, which tells you what four years is worth."**

The two halves are a hundred and eighty-eight and a hundred and seventy-eight. The falling one moved again this morning by one and has not moved since Saturday, and the rising one did not move, and between them they are three hundred and sixty-six.

Nobody in that yard has said the sum out loud for two mornings, and the clerk has now taken the total off the slate altogether and leaves only the two figures on it, which is not an instruction anybody gave him and which he has not asked anybody to confirm.

The three ages were read in the one room at eleven with the door shut and a load still coming up the road outside, and the reader was Nia Vale, who set her wrist flat along the edge of the shelf before she began so that nobody in that room would have to offer to hold anything for her, and she got as far as the second figure and started again before she had been in the room two minutes.

**"The form is two hundred and ninety-three days old,"** she said, and stopped, because the tailboard came off the gate and went past. **"Sorry. Two hundred and ninety-three. And a hundred and ninety-nine on the charter behind it."**

**"And the bare one,"** Harlan Vetch said, from outside the door.

**"The bare one is two hundred and eighty-seven and I have not got to it and I am going to have to start again and I am not going to pretend that I am not angry about it."**

She started again and got all three.

**"That is three times I have come round to that rack this month with one arm and got one wrong in my own mouth,"** she said. **"That is not a good record and I am not going to stand in a room and call it a fine one because nobody else in this building is keeping it."**

Silling said the thing about the pair not being there on a Monday, and he said it in his own words, and he said it differently from the way he said it on Friday, and the yard has stopped hearing it as a rule and started hearing it as a fact about him, which is a different thing and is the whole of what this holding has ever wanted off a man.

Four bodies of households, four lines, two signatures each, and against them a figure of three hundred and ninety-seven. The clause that never gets read with them puts the three hundred and ninety-sixth under the line and the three hundred and ninety-eighth above it, and it has never once settled onto the line it belongs to. Two signatures to every one of the four lines and no name on any of them. Seven hundred and eighty-three at the near end of the wall and eight hundred and thirty at the far end of it, and forty-seven, and the drawer behind the near one shut all day and its key on the nail all day.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden put her hand on the iron at the tap step on the ninth morning and counted to a hundred with her palm on it, and then said the thing she has been waiting since Saturday to say.

**"There has been a Tuesday,"** she said. **"There has been a Sunday and a Monday after it and the joint has not let a drop out of the thread, only the weep, and the weep is not a leak, and I have said all that before. So here is the whole of what a Tuesday proves. A Tuesday proves nothing. What I have now is eight mornings on a joint and one of them is a Monday, and eight mornings is not a month, and I am not going to be the woman in this yard who says it is fixed."**

**"What would you call it then,"** Marek said.

**"I would call it holding,"** she said, **"and I would call that a smaller word than fixed, and I would like it written in whatever hand it gets written in that the smaller word is the one that was used."**

A Monday is not a morning the count in force moves on, and it did not move. It is a hundred and thirty-eight, of those twenty-nine are taken and a hundred and nine are not. The two reckonings on Sera Quill's slate are thirteen apart and will be thirteen apart tomorrow.

Paid is in front of thirty on the compost line and has been discharged in nothing.

In the tool house the use log has fifteen lines on it and nobody has ever opened the space under the fifteenth. The barrow by the gate has eleven journeys in chalk on its side, and a twelfth mark has never been put on it by anybody, and eleven is treated as a floor by everyone who uses it.

On the board in the one room there are thirty-nine blanks, and the second rule stands open under them with nothing in it, and there is not a name in any one of the thirty-nine. The book by the one room door has seven sessions entered in it and Monday did not become an eighth.

Of the two ledgers on the shelf, one holds fifty-three lines and the other holds fifty-three, and not a sheet has ever gone into either of them: the first of the three on the long table is ninety-six days old this morning, and the page in a coat is still in a coat.

Nobody went near the house of the man of about seventy this morning and no question was put to him anywhere in the holding. Nine hundred yards off there is a board door with a figure cut into its seventh column, and the figure is sixty-six. It was not looked at. A Monday is the morning a man here would spend it on, and there is one reading of that figure left in the whole of this holding, and it went unspent.

The ladder went up no rung on Monday and nothing has ever been written against either of the two people who belong to it. On the board beneath the ladder the offer lies with its dust undisturbed: no day on it, no hand on it, no word added and no word taken away. At the top of the low field the ring of bare ground has never been paced or pegged. The six bare rows took nothing, no seventh has been cut for any of the four, and not one of the four was said out loud this morning.

Marek came in from the north end at four with the cold still in him and said the thing and gave the reason before anybody asked him for it.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning, and the reason is the last one this holding has and it is the only one I have got left that I believe."**

**"Then say it,"** Kellan Rusk said.

**"There is one reading left of that door,"** Marek said. **"I did not spend it. I have been asked twice this week by two different people whether I would, and I have said no both times and I have not said why to either of them."**

**"I am saying it now because a man with a thing in his pocket and does not take it out is a man with a thing in his pocket, and this yard has spent four years learning that a figure nobody has read is a figure with a man standing next to it. I will spend it one day, in daylight, with a reason. I will not spend it in a yard on a Monday because somebody in a yard asked me a question about the compost."**

Kellan Rusk did not say anything to that, which was the correct thing to do and which four people noticed and none of them mentioned afterwards.

The clerk turned the yard key at noon and at dusk and looked at the nail on the way past both times. The key to the drawer behind the near board was on the nail at noon and on the nail at dusk, and the drawer stood shut through a Monday on which a man read a pair out loud for the last time in this yard and then said out loud that it was the last time, and a woman counted to a hundred with her hand on a piece of iron and told the yard that a Tuesday proves nothing.

Yarrow grows in the mortar at the north end of a wall if the wall is left alone long enough, and the wall at the north end was left alone on Monday for the first time since Saturday, and there is a green thread of it in the second joint of the third course, and Harlan Vetch saw it at dusk and did not pull it out.

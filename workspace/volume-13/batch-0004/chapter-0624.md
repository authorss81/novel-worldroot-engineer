# Chapter 624

## The Twenty-Seventh Of The Sixteenth, And Three Figures Read At A Seed House Door With The Light Behind The Reader

Wednesday came in clear and hard off the fen with the east wind in it, and the yard was working with its shoulders up by eight, and the standpipe was slow again but running.

The run was read at seven from the doorway by Renn Ashby, with the page turned his way and a stone on the corner of the board, and five voices went with him on the last sentence, one of them at the seed house door with a tray on each arm who stayed to the end of it.

**"One hundred and sixty-four mornings of three hundred and eleven,"** he said, and then he said the two speeds out loud the way he has said them every morning in this run of mornings, and then the five voices came in on the end of it.

The clerk read the wall at nine out of the book and then said what he had said every morning in this run of mornings about the two figures being one pair and not two numbers, and then he put the book down and got the slate.

**One thousand and one hundred and eighty-eight hundredweight.**

**"The three hundred and eighty-seventh of the run is on that wall and it is five off, and Wednesday is an odd morning,"** he said. **"Three hundred and fifty-four is what the two halves come to, and the halves are a hundred and eighty-two and a hundred and seventy-two. I have said that sum out loud twice this week to two different people and neither of them wrote it down, and I would rather it went down than that I said it."**

On the long sheet the four bodies of households are at three hundred and eighty-five, and the small hand under the line carries the three hundred and eighty-fourth out of three hundred and eighty-sixth, one below and one above. Seven hundred and seventy-one is on the near board and eight hundred and eighteen on the far one, and the drawer behind the near board is shut, and its key is on a nail in the one room door where it has been all morning.

Tova Reed read the three ages at the seed house door at half past ten, and she read them off the rack which she had brought out to the step, and she read them facing the yard and not facing the yard, and she said both of those things before she started.

**"Two hundred and eighty-one days on the register form,"** she said. **"The charter is a hundred and eighty-seven. Silling's second ruled line is two hundred and seventy-five, and it has been bare since before the three of us were at this table, and nobody has ever come at me about it, and that is worse than somebody coming at me."**

**"You have not looked at us once,"** the man of about fifty said.

**"No, and I have heard all three of you breathing and I have heard one of you shift its weight, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is enough,"** she said, and carried the rack back in herself and shut the door of the seed house on it.

Kellan Rusk was in the yard on Wednesday with the barrow and did not come near the seed house door at any hour, and Nia Vale noticed him not doing it and did not say anything about it and did not go and thank him for it afterwards, which is a thing she would have done a month ago.

Harlan Vetch found the two feet of the third course at the north end up off the bed again, and they had gone up in the hard frost and not down in the thaw, and he came and found Marek and told him so in the middle of the morning with his thumb in the joint.

**"That is a wall telling me the truth about the weather and not about the wall,"** he said. **"I have had a good frost and a hard wind and the bed under those two stones was never deep enough to hold a frost, and I am not going to put them back on a bed that is not deep enough, and I am not going to be given a figure for it either."**

**"If you let me put the level on the top of it I can tell you whether the course is out of plumb or whether the bed is shallow,"** Marek said.

**"And then you will have a figure on my wall,"** Harlan Vetch said, **"and I have watched a measured wall in this county for four years and every one of them ended up with somebody coming to ask why the figure had changed. You may measure the iron. You will not measure my wall."**

**"You are the only man I have met who refuses a number for a thing that is standing up in the daylight,"** Marek said.

**"I am not refusing it,"** Harlan Vetch said. **"I am refusing to be the one who put it there. Those are different and only one of them is worth anything to anybody."**

Marek came in from that at eleven with the level still in his hand and put it on the bench and left it there, which he has not done in a month.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning and the reason is plain and it is about my hands,"** he said. **"I have cut nothing this week and I cut nothing last week, and on Saturday I am going to cut a thread on a piece of iron and put it into a woman's water on purpose, and a man who stands in a yard and says two large things with empty hands in the three days before he does that is a man who is telling you the work is going to be all right and it is not mine to tell you that."**

**"It is Saturday tomorrow,"** the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said, from the step, with her hand flat on the standpipe.

**"It is,"** he said.

The three sheets are where they have been since they came, and the first of them is eighty-four days old. There is board about the width of two hands either side of each of them, and the arrangement is deliberate, and nobody has ruled a column underneath any of the three in a single book in this building. The two columns hold fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes between them. This holding's book has seven entered sessions, and Wednesday is not one of them.

Thirty-nine blanks are on the board in the one room with the second rule standing open under them and empty, and a rule that comes back empty opens nothing.

Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know and there is nothing cut for a seventh for any of the four things that have been asked about over the years. Four lines stand ruled under two words in that corner and have never taken a word on them, and there is a third column ruled on that sheet, and it is empty, and nobody has improved the title standing over it.

Nobody was fetched for and nobody was put a question, and the man of about seventy stands at twenty-nine fetchings where four years ago left him. Sixty-six sits in the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off, and it was read on Monday by one man who told one person, and nothing has been read from that door since, and it does not come down.

The ladder was not climbed and nothing has ever been written against either of the two people on it. The offer on the low board has never been dated, and it has never been moved, taken up or taken back. Nothing stands in the ring of bare ground and no figure has ever been set against it, and the ground that came up on the low field does not join it and never did.

Cinders had come up out of the tool house stove on Wednesday morning and gone along the middle road in a wind that took them all the way to the gate, and by four o'clock there was a line of them against the inside of the near board where the drawer was, and nobody swept them and nobody put a hand on the board, and they were still there at dusk with the key on its nail and the latch up two inches.

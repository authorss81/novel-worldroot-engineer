# Chapter 628

## The First Of The Seventeenth, And A Joint That Was Cut With The Taper The Right Way Round

Sunday was the second morning of the joint and the water stayed off at the main until half past eleven, and the yard did the walk to the well house again and nobody pretended it was a different walk.

Marek had the iron in the vice at ten past six and the sun had not got over the seed house roof.

**"I am going to cut this the other way,"** he said, to the yard, **"and I said at the fourth hour on Saturday that I had found out something and this is the morning I use it, and I want it said in the middle of the yard and not in the tool house, because a thing said in a tool house is a thing four people heard."**

He cut the thread at seven and it came off the file thick at the top and thin at the bottom, and he held it up and turned it about and did not put it down the wrong way.

**"That is a thread cut the way a thread wants cutting, and I have not cut one since I was twenty-two, and I am going to put it in with three quarters of the sacking she asked for and I am going to put it in square by the bar and not by hand."**

It went in at nine and he tightened it with a wrench and let it go and stood back and looked at it and did not touch it for a quarter of an hour.

The water came on at half past eleven and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden stood at the step with her hand flat on the iron for a count of a hundred, and the iron was cold and then it was not.

**"That is warm,"** she said, **"and warm is the pipe, and there is no water coming out of the thread, and I am not going to tell this yard that it is fixed because a piece of iron with a thread in it that has been right for an hour has not proved anything about a Tuesday."**

**"Then when will it have proved something,"** the man of about fifty said, from the gate.

**"When there has been a hard week on it,"** she said, **"and there has not, and I have told him that and he has written it on nothing, and that is how this holding does it."**

Marek came out of the tap house at noon and stood in the middle of the yard in the sun and did not sit down, and this is the second morning in a row he has come out of a door and stood in the middle of the yard, and the yard has stopped arranging its morning around that.

Renn Ashby stood in the doorway at seven with the second book open in both hands and a stone holding the corner against the wind, and three voices came in on the last sentence of it, and he got the figure right and then said nothing at all about how many had come with him.

**"One hundred and sixty-six mornings of three hundred and fifteen,"** he said. **"It is not a rate. One side of it goes on by a single and the other goes on by a pair, and a yard that hears it on a Sunday and not on a Saturday is hearing two different years of a thing and not one thing."**

**"The heading over the pair is of a month nine months back,"** he said, **"and it has not moved, and it does not move because the date on a piece of paper has gone past that heading a hundred times, and if it moved then the two figures under it would stop being a pair and start being a number with a day on it."**

The clerk read the wall at nine and the date on the slate by the tap said the first, and every other figure in the holding said what it had said on Saturday, and nobody in that yard treated the first as a fresh set of numbers, because a date is not a figure and has never been one here.

**One thousand and one hundred and ninety-four hundredweight.**

**"The run is at its three hundred and ninety-first and it is five off, and it is an odd morning, which means the pair on the board is the one for today,"** he said. **"A hundred and eighty-four, and a hundred and seventy-four, and three hundred and fifty-eight is the whole of it. I have added those two out loud this morning for the first time since Friday and I have added them once, and once is enough."**

The four bodies of households are at three hundred and eighty-nine, and the clause under that line, in a smaller hand, takes the three hundred and eighty-eighth out of three hundred and ninetieth, one either side of the figure on the line itself. Eight hundred and twenty-two stands on the far board this morning and seven hundred and seventy-five on the near one, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of a morning when the water came back on, and the key to it was on its nail in the one room door at every hour of it.

The clerk read the three ages at eleven, all three of them, in the one room, in the order they are in the rack, at the same hour, with the door shut and the yard standing outside it and nobody talking.

**"In the order they are in the rack, and no other order: two hundred and eighty-five days on the register form, a hundred and ninety-one on the charter behind it, and two hundred and seventy-nine on the second ruled line in Silling's own book,"** he said. **"And that one is bare, and it is the last figure in that rack and I am not going to go round the room with it."**

**"You have not gone round the room with it on any of the ten mornings behind this one,"** Nia Vale said.

**"And I have not gone round the room with it because on the mornings when I have gone round the room with it somebody has been carrying water, or a load has been coming up the road, or a man has been putting a piece of iron in on purpose wrong, and this morning the yard is standing still and so am I."**

Sera Quill stood at the back of that and took her slate out and did not write anything on it, which is the first time in ten mornings she has stood in a group of people in this yard with a slate in her hand and a day on it that was not a day the cycle came round.

**"Then the slate is going back in my pocket,"** she said, **"and the two reckonings are the only things on it, and they are thirteen apart, and the second of them is still a figure with no rule behind it that I copied, and I am not going to write a third line under it on the first of anything."**

Harlan Vetch put the two stones of the third course back on Sunday afternoon, on a bed he had dug out four inches deeper than the one that was there, and he did it without a level and without a figure and told nobody he had done it until Monday.

The man of about fifty spent Sunday carrying the last of the water back from the well house and then stood at the gate for an hour with nothing to do, and the latch went up four times that day and he held it twice and did not need to twice, and at four he came past the nail in the one room door and stopped and said a thing out loud that he had not planned to say.

**"Six weeks is not very long,"** he said, to the nail. **"I said it on a Thursday a fortnight ago and I meant every word of it, and I have had a fortnight of it and I am not saying a different thing, I am saying that a fortnight is not very long and I have noticed that, and I would like somebody in this yard to notice it with me."**

Nobody answered him and he did not want them to and he went home, and the key was on the nail at dusk.

Marek did not say the two things on Sunday either, and he gave a reason for the first time in two weeks that was about himself and not about anything else in the yard.

**"I am not saying them because four people in this holding have read three figures out loud this week and I have not been one of them, and that is the right arrangement and I helped make it, and a man who has spent a week standing back on purpose does not get to come back at the end of the week and be the loudest thing in the yard."**

**"You have not been standing back,"** Tova Reed said. **"You have been on a plank with a file since six o'clock both days."**

**"I have been on a plank with a file,"** he said, **"and that is not the same as standing back and I know the difference and I have known it since Saturday and I am not going to be talked out of the other thing by a woman who has carried a pail nine hundred yards eleven times in a day."**

She said that was fair and went in.

There are three sheets on the long table. The first has lain face up for eighty-eight days. The second is folded. The third carries a name, a date, a signature and a seal, and nobody in this yard has ever found the thing wrong with it. Two hands of bare board stand either side of each sheet, and that is the most careful piece of work anybody does here, and in four books there is not a ruled column under any of them. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand in their two columns, and seven sessions are entered in this holding's book, and Sunday is not one of them.

The board in that room carries thirty-nine blanks and the rule standing open and empty underneath them, and a rule that comes back empty opens nothing.

Six ruled rows are set against what this holding does not know, and all of them are bare, and the paper has nothing cut for a seventh below them, and the four things a seventh row has been asked about are not spoken on this morning either. Four bare lines stand under two words in that corner, and a third column on the terms sheet is ruled and empty beneath a heading nobody has improved.

The compost line still reads paid against twenty-nine and has been discharged in nothing, and a Sunday is not an interval, and it will not move for twenty-six mornings. Fifteen lines are in the use log and the sixteenth is not written, and the barrow says eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor.

Nobody went for the man of about seventy this morning and nothing was put to him. Twenty-nine fetchings is where four years ago left him, and the seventh column of the door nine hundred yards off still carries sixty-six. It was not read today, and there is one reading left in it for the whole of what this holding has left to give, and nobody in this yard has claimed it.

No rung of that ladder was climbed today and the two people belonging to it go on carrying no figure at all. The offer lying on the low board has not been dated, read out, moved, taken up or taken back. Nothing stands in the ring of bare ground and no dimension has ever been given it, and what is standing on the low field is a different piece of ground and the two have never been added together.

The clerk turned the yard key at noon and at dusk, and the second key stayed on its nail in the one room door at both of those hours, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut on a Sunday in which the date was the first and every other figure in the holding said the same thing it had said the day before.

Sundays are the mornings this holding has never been different on, and the first of anything came round in the night the way the fourth morning comes round in the night, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden washed the three pale rings off the step in the afternoon and left the ones she could not get, and the tap ran all evening, and nobody in that yard wrote one word of any of it down.

# Chapter 623

## The Twenty-Sixth Of The Sixteenth, And A Tap That Came Up At Six With Half the Water It Ought

Tuesday was the first hard morning since the frost before the wet, and the tap at the back of the tap house was slow when the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden turned it on at six, and she stood and watched it for a while before she did anything about it.

**"That is a trickle and not a supply,"** she said, **"and a trickle on a Tuesday is going to be a trickle on a Saturday, and Saturday is the day I have a man taking a joint apart in front of me."**

She got the bucket and went round to the well house and filled it and carried it back and did it again four times before seven, and the yard came out and watched her do the fourth one and then two of them took a bucket each without being asked and she let them.

Soren Rill was taking the first barrow out at eight with the seed in it and stopped at the rack on his way past.

**"Will you read these three out for me,"** he said. **"I have got a barrow in my hands and I am not going to put it down on a morning like this, and I would rather ask now than stand here at noon and say I never got them."**

**"Give me the rack and keep the barrow,"** the clerk said.

**"You cannot reach over a barrow with a rack,"** Soren Rill said. **"I have been doing this eleven journeys a year for four years and I know exactly how much of this holding I can reach with a full barrow in front of me, and the answer is the top shelf and nothing else."**

So he read them himself, off the top shelf, with the barrow between him and the light.

**"Two hundred and eighty days on the register form,"** he said, and stopped, and had to get the barrow's tailboard off the doorframe before he could turn round, and the yard waited for him.

**"A hundred and eighty-six on the charter behind it. And two hundred and seventy-four where the second ruled line is in Silling's own book, and that one has never had anything on it and I have had a barrow in my hands every time anybody has ever read it to me."**

**"You have the first of them wrong,"** the woman of about thirty-eight said, from the well house steps.

**"I have not,"** Soren Rill said. **"Two hundred and eighty. That is two hundred and seventy-nine plus one and I have read it off the top shelf with a barrow in the way and I have got it right."**

**"You have got it right,"** she said, **"and I said you had it wrong on purpose to see whether you would argue with me about a figure you had not looked at twice, and I am not going to do that again in this yard."**

The wall was read at nine by Harlan Vetch, who did it on his way down to the north end and did it standing at an angle with his thumb on the wall for balance.

**One thousand and one hundred and ninety-three hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, and it is an even morning, and this is the three hundred and eighty-sixth of the run,"** he said. **"A hundred and eighty-two and a hundred and seventy-one. I have not added them. Three hundred and fifty-three, and if either of you got there first you have not told me and I would have liked to know."**

The four bodies of households stand at three hundred and eighty-four on the sheet on the long wall, and the clause in the smaller hand under it takes the three hundred and eighty-third out of three hundred and eighty-fifth. The board at the near end says seven hundred and seventy and the one at the far end says eight hundred and seventeen. The drawer behind the near one has stood shut at every hour of every morning in this run of mornings, and its key was on its nail in the door of the one room.

Sera Quill said the reason for the space in the morning out loud at the gate with her slate under her arm, and said it to a man who had not asked her and who was not reading the wall.

**"There is no pair on the wall because Renn reads it in the doorway at seven and it is an even morning and it is not read, and I have said this at the gate four times in a fortnight and I am going to go on saying it until somebody writes down that the reason is not interesting, and then I will stop."**

**"It is not interesting,"** said the man of about fifty.

**"Then it will be written down,"** she said, **"and you can watch me not say it again, and you will find that a gate with a reason written down is worse than a gate with a reason said at it."**

Marek had the taper in the vice on Tuesday morning and cut three threads on it and put three lengths of iron together dry and taken them apart again, and he had the bottle and the level out of the tool house because the frost had put a skin of ice in the butt and he wanted the butt's fall before Saturday.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning and I am not going to give you a reason that is about me,"** he said, to nobody, at eleven, with the level in his hand. **"I am not saying them because I have got a level and a bottle and three threads cut on a taper and there is not one person in this yard who could tell me the difference between the first fit of this joint and a good fit, and I am not going to explain a thing to a yard that cannot check it."**

**"I can check it,"** the woman of about thirty-eight said, from the butt, with her arm in to the elbow.

**"You can check whether the water stays in or it does not,"** Marek said, **"and that is the only check there is and I would rather have it than have a conversation about it, so on Saturday morning I will put this thing in on purpose wrong and you will tell me where the water goes and that is how I will know what I am looking at."**

**"On purpose wrong,"** she said.

**"On purpose wrong,"** he said, **"and I will tell everybody in this yard that I have done it on purpose before I do it, so that when it leaks nobody stands in the road and looks at it and thinks a man has cut a piece of iron badly."**

Tova Reed came out of the seed house at the hour the seed work goes out and found the woman of about thirty-eight drawing from the well house, and the two of them worked around each other's water for twenty minutes without either of them moving her own.

**"I can carry a bucket and I cannot hear the butt fill,"** Tova Reed said, when the woman of about thirty-eight came over with one. **"So I will carry what I can see and you will tell me when it is coming, and that is not a favor either way and I am not going to be thanked for it."**

On the long table there are three sheets and the first of them is eighty-three days old and face up. Either side of each one there is board about the width of a hand and a half, and nothing has been ruled underneath any of them in any of the books in this building. Fifty-three requests sit in one column and fifty-three section-nine notes in the other, and neither of the two has ever taken a sheet off anybody's table. This holding's book has seven entered sessions, and Tuesday was not one of them.

On the board in the one room there are thirty-nine blanks and the rule under them stands open and empty.

Six rows are ruled against what this holding does not know and there is nothing cut below the sixth, and nobody has cut one for the covenant or for the machine or for the schedule or for the confession, and that has been the standing of it since the sheet came. Four lines stand ruled under two words in that corner and have never taken anything at all, and beside them a third column on the terms sheet is ruled and empty under a title nobody has improved.

The compost line has the word paid in front of twenty-nine and has been discharged in nothing, and nothing will come out of it for twenty-six mornings. Fifteen lines are in the use log and no sixteenth has been written, and the barrow says eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor. The man of about seventy has not been fetched and nobody has asked him anything, and sixty-six in the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off was read on Monday by one man and is not being read again this week.

No rung of the ladder was used and neither of the two people belonging to it has any figure set against him. The offer on the low board is undated, untaken, and not taken back. The ring of bare ground has taken no boot and carries no figure, and what is standing on the low field is land of its own and does not join it.

Wrought was in the vice on the bench all afternoon with three threads on it and the file across the jaws, and by four o'clock there were two more beside it, and Marek put all five in a row and looked at them the way a man looks at five things that are not the same and not supposed to be, and did not number them, and did not write any of them down.

# Chapter 621

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Sixteenth, And A Slate With Two Figures And A Line Under Them

Sunday was the stillest morning the yard had had in a fortnight, and the cycle came round in the night the way it does, and the number in force was a hundred and thirty-five when Sennik Vaul brought the board out and propped it against the end of the bench at ten.

**"A hundred and thirty-five, with twenty-nine taken and a hundred and six not,"** he said. **"It came up by itself in the night, exactly as it does every fourth morning, and not one person in this holding did anything about it, and I want that said out loud before anybody in this yard starts working out whose morning it was."**

**"It is a Sunday and we are not going to blame anybody for a field that has not been taken,"** Marek said. **"I asked for a field on the twelfth and I was refused in two different sentences, and I have not asked since, and I am not going to start again on a morning when a number came up by itself."**

**"Then I will ask the yard something once and then never again,"** Sennik Vaul said, **"because it has been sitting on my shelf for a fortnight and it is a bigger question than a field. What is it for."**

Nobody in the yard answered him.

He looked at the board for a while and then turned round to it and put the flat of his hand on the top figure.

**"I know that,"** he said. **"I asked on a Sunday because a Sunday is the morning when eleven people have got nothing else to do with a silence, and I will ask it again in four mornings when the cycle comes round, and it will get the same silence, and I have decided that is allowed."**

Sera Quill had the two reckonings on the slate by nine, dated and signed at the top, and underneath the two figures she wrote a third line, which is the first time in nineteen years that she has put anything on that slate that is not one of the two.

It said: carried forward, no rule shown.

The clerk read it at ten and put his hand flat on the slate and did not move it for a while.

**"That is right,"** he said. **"The first of those two is a figure like any other and the second one is a figure I have watched come round four times now and it has moved up by one each time in a way that no printed rule in this building produces, and I have never been able to show anybody where it comes from, and neither can she, and neither of us is going to keep quietly copying a pair of numbers and calling it a rule."**

**"Thirteen apart, and the gap has not closed and I have not written that it closed,"** Sera Quill said. **"I have written that I copied it, so that if a man comes along in a year with a rule and says that is not how it goes, then there is a slate in this yard that says a woman knew she did not know."**

**"Then leave it on,"** the clerk said. **"Leave it on for as long as the pair keeps coming round with nothing under it."**

The wall was read at half past ten by the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who had asked for the book instead of the chalk because Sunday light comes in flat off the wall and she wanted the number out of a page.

**One thousand and one hundred and ninety hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, and it is an even morning, and it is the three hundred and eighty-fourth of the run,"** she said. **"A hundred and eighty-one against a hundred and seventy, and between them three hundred and fifty-one, and I want it noticed that I have not added them twice and that I have also not added them at all, which is the same as a thing I have never done."**

The four bodies of households have three hundred and eighty-two on the line of their sheet, and the small hand underneath sets out the three hundred and eighty-first out of three hundred and eighty-third, which is yesterday on one side and the day after on the other. The two boards at the ends of the wall read seven hundred and sixty-eight and eight hundred and fifteen, and the drawer behind the near one came back empty at every hour of Sunday, and the key to it stood on its nail in the one room door.

Soren Rill said the reason for the empty space on the wall, standing by the barrow with his hands full of the handle.

**"There is no pair on it today because Renn has it in the doorway at seven and it is an even morning and it is not read, and I am saying it at this gate because somebody will ask me before dark and I would rather they asked me than worked it out and thought I had been paid to keep it quiet."**

**"Nobody has paid you anything,"** the man of about fifty said from the bench.

**"No, and I said it badly, and I have been in this yard four years and I have never once got a sentence to land the first time,"** Soren Rill said.

The three ages went across the middle table on a piece of paper that had been a register form once and was not one any more, and the clerk wrote them on the back of it in the middle of the room and then tore it off along the fold and put it down in front of whoever wanted it.

**"Two hundred and seventy-eight days on the form this morning stands behind. A hundred and eighty-four on the charter. Two hundred and seventy-two on the second ruled line in Silling's own book, which is bare and stays bare."**

Nobody signed the back of it. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden took it, read it once, put it in her apron pocket and took it out and read it again, and then folded it in four and put it back.

**"If a thing is going to be paper then it can be a torn-off back of a form and I can put it in my pocket and be certain that nobody is going to come along in a year and say I copied it out of a book I had open on the bench,"** she said. **"That is the whole of why I am keeping it."**

Kellan Rusk came in through the gate at one with his coat buttoned and did not go near the long table, and Nia Vale came out of the seed house at the same moment and looked at the coat and looked away again, and neither of them said one word about the thing in the inside pocket of it, and it was still in there at dusk.

Harlan Vetch put the two feet of course back at the north end on Saturday afternoon and on Sunday he went along it with his thumb and found the joint where he had left it and left it where it was, and would not be given a figure for it and did not want one.

Marek did not come out of the tool house until two, and when he came out he stood in the doorway with the frame saw still in his hand and did not go to the wall at all.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, **"and I am not going to explain it to anybody who is disappointed, because this morning this yard measured a gap of thirteen of its own accord before I had my coat off, and I am not going to stand in a gap that the yard has already measured and put two large things into it."**

Three sheets lie at the far end of the long table, and the first of them has been face up since the morning it arrived. The middle one is creased the other way from the way a thing is folded when a person is thinking. The last one carries a name, a date, a signature and a seal, and is wrong in nothing that anybody in this holding is able to name. A strip of bare board about two hands wide is left either side of each, and no column has been ruled under any of them by anybody, in any of the four books here.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand in their two columns, and Sunday was not the morning a seventh session went into this holding's book. This holding's book carries seven entered sessions and Sunday is not among them. Thirty-nine blanks are ruled on the board in that room, and the rule under them came back with nothing on it, and it opened nothing.

What this holding does not know is written out in six ruled rows and every one of them is bare. Paper has been left below the sixth for a seventh and it has not been cut. In the same corner two words have four bare lines under them that have never taken anything, and beside them the terms sheet has a column ruled with a title over it that nobody has improved.

The man of about seventy was not fetched on Sunday and nothing was put to him, and the twenty-nine fetchings of him are standing where four years ago left them. In the seventh column of a door nine hundred yards off, sixty-six is scratched, and a man read that on Monday and told one person, and it is not read again on Sunday, and it does not come down.

Twenty-six mornings have to go by before the compost line moves at all, and the word in front of twenty-nine is paid, and nothing has been discharged in it. The use log has fifteen lines and the sixteenth is not written, and the barrow went back against the wall at its eleven journeys, and eleven is a floor, and a Sunday is no reason to move a floor.

No rung of the ladder was climbed and the two people who belong to it carry no figure of any kind in this holding. The offer lying on the low board has never been dated, never been read out, never been moved, and never been taken up or taken back. The ring of bare ground took no boot on this Sunday and has never been given a number, and the low field is a separate piece of ground and the two are never added.

The clerk turned the yard key at noon, at four and at dusk, and the second key on its nail in the one room door was on that nail at all three of them, and the drawer behind the near board did not open at any hour of a Sunday in which nothing in this holding was answered.

Brick was stacked against the north end of the wall in the afternoon and there were two of them out of a course that has wanted doing for three weeks, and neither of them had a figure against it, and the wall was no measurably different on Sunday night than it had been on Friday morning, and Harlan Vetch said that out loud to nobody and went home.

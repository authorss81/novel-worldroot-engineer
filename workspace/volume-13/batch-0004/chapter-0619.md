# Chapter 619

## The Twenty-Second Of The Sixteenth, And A Latch Lifted Twice With A Full Pail

Friday came in mild off the west and by ten past seven the yard had two things moving in it, the seed house door and a pail.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came down the middle road with a full pail in each hand and went straight at the gate, because on Thursday morning she had told Renn Ashby in front of nine people that she would lift that latch four times in four weeks with a full pail and that Friday was the first of them.

He was at the gate before she got to it. He had been at it since half past six and he had not said so and did not say so now.

**"Hold it, then,"** she said, and put her hip under the bar of it.

**"I am holding it,"** he said.

The first time was nothing. The latch came up about two inches, the pail went through, and the gate came to behind her without a sound, and she stood on the other side of it with two full pails and an expression that said she had expected worse.

**"That is not a lift,"** she said. **"That is a suggestion. I have had four months with the pin and I know what this gate costs to open and that just told me."**

The second time she came back at half past eight and did not go round. She put the pail down on the hard standing, took the bar in both hands and went up on it, and the latch lifted four inches and stayed lifted while she got a shoulder under it.

**"There,"** she said, from over the top of it. **"That is a lift. That is a thing I would have to plan a morning around."**

Renn Ashby had a hand on the back of the gate and the weight of it on his forearm and he did not take either away.

**"Then a morning gets planned around it,"** he said. **"I said I would be standing here and I am standing here, and the fourth time you do it you can do it without asking me, and I will still be standing here."**

She came down off it and put the pail on the barrow and looked at the gate the way she looks at a thing that has just cost her something it did not cost her before.

**"It is a different animal from the pin,"** she said. **"The pin was a thing you did with your thumb and this is a thing you do with your back, and I am going to be at this gate more than I was, and I would rather you heard that from me than worked it out on a morning you were not standing here."**

The wall was read at nine by the clerk, in a voice that had come most of the way back over the week and still did not carry past about thirty feet, and the yard had arranged itself on the strength of that by standing nearer than it used to.

**One thousand and one hundred and eighty-seven hundredweight.**

**"Eight on, and Friday is an even morning, and it is the three hundred and eighty-second figure of the run and the run has never come back with a number it had already used,"** he said. **"A hundred and eighty on one side of the chalk and a hundred and sixty-nine on the other, and between them three hundred and forty-nine."**

He put the chalk back in his pocket instead of leaving it on the wall, which is not what he does, and Harlan Vetch looked at the pocket and then at the wall and said nothing about it.

Against the long wall the sheet headed with the four bodies of households has three hundred and eighty on its line, and the small print under that line reads the three hundred and seventy-ninth out of three hundred and eighty-first. The board at the near end of the same wall shows seven hundred and sixty-six and the one at the far end shows eight hundred and thirteen. A drawer is let into the masonry behind the near board and it has not been opened, and the key to it is where it always is, on a nail in the frame of the one room door.

Hesta Lyle said out loud in the middle of the yard why there was no pair on the wall this morning, which is a thing she now does and which nobody has asked her to do.

**"It is not read on an even morning and I am not going to stand here and pretend I forgot a book that is on the shelf behind me. Renn has it in the doorway at seven and he stands there and says the reason, and I would rather that than a yard that hears a number on five mornings and invents the other five."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read the three ages out of the rack at ten, and read them in the order she lifted them, which was the wrong order, and made no attempt at all to hide that it was the wrong order.

**"A hundred and eighty-two days on the charter behind it. Two hundred and seventy where the second ruled line sits in Silling's own book, and there is nothing on that line and nothing due on it. And two hundred and seventy-six days on the register form, which is the one I nearly left off the bench because I had it in my hand and not in my eye."**

She put all three back on the rack in the right places and squared the charter to the edge of it.

**"Three correct figures in an order nobody asked for,"** she said. **"If a man in this holding writes them down in the wrong order he has still got three right numbers, and the yard can hear the difference between that and a man who has got one wrong."**

Marek came out of the tool house at eleven with the two pieces of iron from Wednesday's work under his arm and set them down on the bench outside the one room and squared them to each other, and the yard came and looked at them the way a yard does.

**"I am not saying the two things this morning,"** he said, before anybody had asked him. **"The key to that drawer is on a nail in a doorframe and the man who put it there has said his piece about it out loud in front of eleven people, and a man who has already said the one true thing about a key has nothing left to trade with and I am not going to try to make something out of that."**

Nia Vale came out with two trays and stopped, which is what she does when he has said it first.

**"That is a new reason,"** she said.

**"It is the same reason with a different thing in front of it,"** he said, **"and I would rather it was the same reason than a new one every morning, because a new one every morning is a man building a wall."**

Then the yard got on with Friday. Harlan Vetch took two feet of the third course out at the north end and put the stone back the way it came out, and would not be told a figure for it, and said so twice.

The man of about fifty stood at the gate at noon and watched the pail come through again for the third time that morning, and did not offer to hold it, and did not go inside either.

Sennik Vaul did not bring the board out, because the cycle had not come round, and the number in force was where it was and it did not move, and nobody in that yard found a morning of work to spend it on and nobody was blamed for that.

On the compost line the word paid stands in front of twenty-nine, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read that word out loud twice on Friday to convince herself she had read the figure under it. Twenty-six mornings have to go by before that line does anything, and no part of twenty-nine is owed to anybody. The use log on its shelf has fifteen lines on it and the paper under the fifteenth is clean, and the barrow was put back against the wall with eleven journeys in chalk down its side, and nobody in this holding has ever taken eleven for a number to improve on.

Kellan Rusk went past the long table at ten past eleven with the barrow and did not turn his head at it, which is the fourth morning running that a man has carried a barrow the length of this yard and not once looked at the three sheets lying at the end of it. There is a piece of board either side of each one about the width of two hands, and the first of them is seventy-nine days old and lying face up, and not one of the three has been answered, refused or moved since the day it came into this building.

Seven sessions are entered in this holding's book. None of them was entered on Friday, and the book stood open at that page all morning with the pen lying beside it and nobody reaching for it.

Against the corner of the one room, thirty-nine blanks are ruled on a board, and the rule immediately beneath them stands open with nothing on it whatsoever, and a rule that returns empty opens nothing at all. Four bare lines are ruled further along under a heading of two words, and beside them the terms sheet carries a third column with a title over it that nobody has taken a pen to. What this holding does not know is set out in six rows, all of them empty, and the paper has room below the sixth for a seventh that has not been cut.

Harlan Vetch went down the yard at four and passed the ladder on his way and did not look at it, and it stands at its bottom rung with nothing whatever written against the two people who belong to it. The low board carries an offer that has not been dated and has not been answered by anybody in four years, and the ring of bare ground beside the low field has never had a boot in it and has never been given a measurement, and what is coming up on the low field is a different parcel of land and the two are not added together.

At noon the clerk turned his own key and left the one room shut behind him, and at four he did it again, and the second key hanging by the doorframe of that room did not leave its nail at either hour, and neither did the drawer behind the near board at any point of a Friday when a woman tested a gate six times with a full pail.

Latch had taken the weight of that gate all morning and the bar had not dropped once, and by six the pail had gone through it six times and every one of the six had cost somebody their back, and at dusk the latch was still up two inches because nobody had pushed it down, and the gate stood that way through the dark and into the morning after.

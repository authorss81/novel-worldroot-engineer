# Chapter 181

## The Seed Side Goes On The Road

The sixth and the seventh of this month were a Thursday and a Friday, and the seed side of this holding went onto a road, and it was a working movement and it was not a departure, and about nine people were in the yard both days because the yard is where anything said out loud gets entered.

Two carts went out on the Thursday at about the seventh hour and came back on the Friday at about the eighth hour, and the count of the carts, the count of the sacks, and the count of the households' seed that went and the count that stayed was made in a yard on the Friday at about the fourth hour by four households in turns, the way a gate count is made, and the man of about fifty counted and did not ask anybody a question while he counted, and his initial went in against nothing afterward, **and the count of that request is forty this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.**

The Thursday was the working day and it was in the yard from the fifth hour, and there is not much to say about it that is worth saying, and the fen entered that as well.

A sack of a dry line is about two hundredweight and a sack of a line that has been in a shed through a wet month two months back is heavier than that by about the weight of the water in it, and there were nine sacks on the first cart on the Thursday morning and seven on the second, and the two that did not go were the two that had been nearest the wall, and the person who keeps the seed house opened both of them on the boards in the rain and looked in them and said nothing to anybody and closed them again. The engineer of record carried eleven sacks and got into the cart bed and put them where she pointed and came down and carried eleven more, and he was not asked to and he was not thanked and he was not needed, and the fen entered all four of those.

And the one thing that happened in the yard on the Thursday that was not a sack. The seedwright of the trust put her hand into the fourth sack on the first cart, which is a line she has carried for nine years and knows to the pound, and she said one word about it, which was wet, and the engineer of record said that it is two degrees above the seed house floor and about a hand's depth from the wall, and she said that is the wall's fault and not the road's, and he said he knows it is the wall's fault and is not going to argue with a woman about a wall, and that exchange is the whole of what two of them said to each other on the Thursday and about nine people in that yard heard it and nobody wrote it down until Friday and nobody improved on it when it was written down.

> Entered on the same line: **the seedwright of the trust said one word about the fourth sack on the first cart, being wet. The engineer of record gave a reason and did not argue about it. That exchange is entered entire and nobody thanked either of them and neither of them asked.**

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **two carts went out of this holding on the sixth of this month and came back on the seventh. The count of what went and what stayed is a count four households made in turns at a gate, with a tally, in about half an hour. It is a count of carts and of sacks. It is not a count of a decision and no decision was taken in the yard while it was being made.** The fen entered that this is the only count of its kind in this book's history and entered that it is a count of carts.

The four costs a road costs went in on four lines and were not folded into anything, and the woman who does the sums entered them in her own words and did not give them a total.

"**One. The cart, which is hired and is not ours, and the hire is at the rate in the cart book and it is in the cart book and not in this book. Two. The driver's day, which is a day out of a working week and is the driver's and not anybody's here. Three. The two days off four households, which is work that does not get done on this head because four people are standing in a yard counting sacks. Four. The four hundred yards a person has to walk to find out where a cart has got to, and I have got that one off a woman who has done it, and it is not on any line in any book and it is the one that costs the most.**"

"**I am not adding the four. A cost that is a hire and a cost that is a day and a cost that is four hundred yards are three different objects and adding them gives a number about money that is not a number about a road.**"

And then the money, and it was the trust's money, and the fen entered all three of those separately and read them back.

"**Every one of that was paid by the seed trust, at the trust's own cost, out of a fund this holding has never seen a figure from and is not entitled to. This holding did not pay any of it. This holding could not pay any of it. And this holding is not going to be thanked for the road, and I want that last one on the page, because in about four years somebody in this yard is going to describe a woman taking a cart and forty households' seed onto a road and somebody is going to say this holding helped, and the truth is that this holding could not have bought the cart.**"

> Entered on the same line: **the costs of the road were paid by the seed trust at its own cost. This holding did not pay any of it, could not pay any of it, and is not going to be thanked for the road. Nobody thanked anybody on either of those two days and the seedwright of the trust was not thanked and did not ask to be.**

Then the reason, and it was not the one anybody in that yard expected, and she gave it standing up in the ordinary voice in about five sentences, and about nine people heard all of it and the clerk was one of the nine.

"**A seed route is a licensed thing on a road that has been declared a licensed use. That is what the form on Monday said and it is correct and I am not arguing with the form. A trust with seed on a licensed road is a party to a licensed use. And a body that has been a party twice in a month is a category, and I have watched what a category is for, because I am the one who reads the returns of the people who make categories.**"

"**And I am not going to be a category with forty households' seed in a cart on a road with soldiers standing at the gate of the next place. I would rather move the seed on a road nobody has written a line about and be slow, and I would rather be slow.**"

Nobody argued with her. The man of the north row with the cough looked at the ground. The engineer of record did not answer her, the reader of this body did not answer her, the person who keeps the seed house did not answer her, and the fen entered that nobody answered her and entered that nobody was asked to.

And the reader of this body then did four things, and the fen entered all four separately because three of them are things a body that was losing would not have done.

"**I am not going to argue with that and I am not going to ask her to wait, and I am not going to offer a way of doing it that is better, and I am not going to thank her, and I want all four of those in the book in that order, and I would like the clerk to enter that I did not do any of the four and did not consider doing any of the four.**"

Then he said the one thing that is his whole position, once, and she did not answer it, and the fen entered that she did not answer it and did not improve on her not answering it.

"**Here is what I actually think and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it again in a month. A seed on a licensed road can be carried by a body that has refused to be a party. I have looked for one. There is not one in this district that I have ever heard of, and I have been looking for eleven years, and I have looked hardest in the four places where I would expect to find one, which are the places where somebody has already been punished for being a party.**"

"**And I am not going to pretend that this holding is one. I have no charter. I have sixteen households on one head. I have put myself in a class in my own hand on Tuesday in front of a man with a book and I did it because the alternative was worse, and I would do it again in the morning, and that is not a foundation anybody should be asked to build a seed route on.**"

And then the separation, in the ordinary way, and it took about four minutes and it was not a scene and nobody in that yard treated it as one.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a working arrangement: **the seed side of this holding takes the seed, the route and the record of the route, and the record of the route is the trust's and is kept by the trust. This holding keeps the water, the well, the leak, the beat, the door and the four lines. The separation is entered as a working arrangement and not as a quarrel. Neither of the two said the word and the fen entered that the word was not said and did not enter the word.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's own words, read back: the count of things this holding used to do with a person is now a count of things it does not do, and the two lists are on facing pages of this book, and they are not to be read as a grievance by anybody in four years.**

Then the cost of it, which is not a cost anybody can write on a line, and no name is in it at all and the fen entered that too.

The carts came back at about the eighth hour on the Friday and the sacks came off them in the order they went on, and one household of the four found that the two sacks that had not gone were the two it had wanted out of its own ground before the sixteenth, **and it asked the person who keeps the seed house, in that yard, in front of about nine people, whether the seed side was coming back for them. And the person who keeps the seed house said that a seed side is on a road and not on a calendar, and that she did not know, and that a household should not be given a day by a body that has not got one. And the household took the two sacks back into its own shed and said nothing more about it, and the fen entered the exchange entire and entered that it was the last business of that day and that nobody in that yard answered the household better than she did and that this holding could not have answered it better because it does not know.**

The seedwright of the trust asked one question in that yard before the carts were unloaded, and she asked it in the ordinary way, and about nine people heard it, and the reader of this body answered it in one sentence and did not build up to it.

"**What happens to the gate count of the four households on a seed side that is not on this head.**"

"**They keep it. It is their count and it was always their count and it is in their hands and not in this book, and this holding has been reading a copy of it for eleven years and the copy is not the count.**"

And then she said the second half of it, and it was the half about a book, and about nine people heard it and the fen entered that the reader of this body was in that yard and did not answer it.

"**And there is a room with the doors shut in this building with a ledger in it that has my hand in it for four years and about nine hundred entries, and the whole of what I am telling you this afternoon is that the ledger stays on this head and goes on a cart on Thursday and comes back on Friday, and the road is the road. A record that cannot go where the work goes is not a record. It is a place where somebody kept a copy.**"

> Entered on the same line: **the four households keep their own count of their own seed, at their own gate, in their own hands. This holding holds a copy and the copy is not the count and the fen entered that difference and entered that this holding has been printing a figure four times this month that belongs to somebody else's counting and did not know that until a woman asked it a question in a yard. The seed-route ledger stays on this head and travels with the carts; the fen entered the seedwright of the trust's own sentence on that and entered that the reader of this body was in that yard and did not answer it.**

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked on either of those two days, and the seed side went onto a road at about the seventh hour on the Thursday and came back at about the eighth hour on the Friday, and the seedwright of the trust came up the fen road on the Friday evening after the carts were unloaded and slept on the bench in the cart shed, which the man of the north row with the cough put there a month ago and told nobody about, **and the fen entered that nobody thanked him for the bench and that nobody was told in advance who was going to sleep on it, and that it is the third time that bench has had somebody on it this season.**

**And this holding is now a body that does water and a body that keeps a book and a body that used to do both with a woman in it, and the two of those are on facing pages, and the fourth cart is still not asked about, and the count of the things this body does not know stands at four and was four a month ago, and on the morning of the eighth there is a woman asleep in a cart shed nine hundred yards from a door this holding reads out in front of twenty-five people.**

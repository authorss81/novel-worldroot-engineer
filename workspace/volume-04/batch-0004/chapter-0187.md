# Chapter 187

## The Twentieth

The twentieth of this month was a Thursday, and it is the last working day this body has put in a book in this month, and at about the eleventh hour of the morning the standing figures of the day were read out whole in a yard of about nine people with nothing softened, and about nine of those nine had heard them before and did not go away while they were read.

> **The compost line for the tenth month.** Eighty pounds, on a specification of four hundred hundredweight, at a valuation of four shillings the hundredweight, the shortfall derived twice out loud and the two derivations agreeing, the ninth month's figure having been eighty pounds also and the two being entered on the same line, **and the run at ten not discharged in ten, and a figure that repeats entered by the reader of this body as a worse kind of figure than a figure that moves.** The word said before the figure was crowded, and the word and the figure do not agree, and the woman who does the sums does not know yet which of the two is telling the truth and intends to find out before she is able to. **The costs of the tenth month are on four separate lines and are not folded into the shortfall.**
>
> **The compost line for the eleventh month.** Not entered, and the reason entered. The figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and this holding does not know what it is. **The eleventh month of a run of eleven not discharged in eleven, once that figure is in.** The word said before the tenth month's figure was crowded, and it does not agree with the figure.
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of a month two months back, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit. **This body will not say whether the difference from the reading of the thirteenth of a month three months back is a movement or a season.** No cause, no due date, not measured since, and not to be measured again this month.>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. **Column open.** The rule's third line does not say in a yard, this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule was used outside one of its own four lines on the twenty-second of a month two months back, and that is entered, **and the column was not opened today in front of nine people and will not be, and the fen entered that the not opening is a decision and not an oversight.** The fen entered that the day of that beat is not restated in this book and is not going to be.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-five.** The last reading day of this month is the thirtieth, which is not this batch's, **and the count has stood at thirty-five since the first, and a count of readings is a count of events and cannot stand still across a day on which a reading was made, and there has not been one.**
>
> **The boards, two hundred and fifty-four days, day minus sixty-six,** cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years. **The March's tin, three hundred and one days. Day minus nineteen,** unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.** **Day minus sixty-six. Day minus nineteen. Read out loud by the clerk before either number, which he has now done on nine reading days running and which nobody has asked for on any of the nine.**
>
> **The standing rule.** Four lines, every one about a stop, satisfied on the third of a month five months back, **used twice in this volume, on the twenty-second of a month two months back and on the thirteenth of this month, and not used on any other day, and the count of the uses is two, and the fen entered that the count is not going to be three.**
>
> **The use log at fifteen lines.** The fifteenth is a contact on a boarded floor on the ninth of last month, with a licensed man and a plain wire gauge test written down first. **And entered on the same standing entry and not in the log, because none of them is a line: a second contact on the twentieth of last month, a hold on the same boards on the twenty-fourth of last month, and a draw on the thirteenth of this month. A line records a contact and never a reading. A draw gets no line. A hold gets no line.** The log stands at fifteen and the reason it stands at fifteen is that this holding cannot write down what happened on those boards in a line anybody could check.
>
> **The rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is not longer than it was on the twenty-fourth of last month, there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow is not a mark. And on the same line: four accounts of the same arm in this book, none of which agrees with any of the others, and the draw of the thirteenth is not a fifth account and reconciles none of them, and the fen does not reconcile them and is not going to.**
>
> **A form on a bench in the seed house, correct, filled in, not returned, not torn up, and the answer not given.**
>
> **And the well on the shelf.** Five feet at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month, by the occupier, by her own nail, in her own yard, on her own wall, four feet against the sixteenth of a month six months back, **and this body will not decompose that figure and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.** A day letter off its stone from the eighth to the seventeenth of a month two months back with the eight days not written on it. A man with a spade who has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month and has not been asked. Four lines down a four-day road at that household's own cost, and two numbers in the fourth of them that this body did not add up. **And a row, and a wall, and a yard four hundred miles off, with no entry anywhere for any of the three either.**

And then, in the same yard, before the figures were closed, the standing rule was read out in full again, four lines, by the clerk, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit.> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month five months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. There is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The fen entered that the rule has now been read out in full five times in this month and used twice, and that the two counts are two counts and are not the same count, and did not improve on it, **and entered that the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and that on the thirteenth of this month nine people came up a slope and a road did not open, and that every one of those four lines was satisfied the whole time and not one of them was about the nine.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, **and the count of that request is forty-four this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint and is not going to be a complaint at the eleventh hour on a Thursday.**

Then the change, and it is the batch's and not the volume's, and the reader of this body read it out in the same yard because a month of standing is not closed by a column.

"**The seed side is on a road. The water is on a form. A man eleven miles off is a supply and not a thief and I have said that in this yard four times and I am not going to say it a fifth. A man of about thirty-five will not say a name and has refused four times in nineteen years. A thing under a low quarter will not be drawn from, and eleven came up a road and said so in a yard and gave a figure that is not a number of anybody.**"

"**A party has become a person. And the person said a true thing about a form and a quarter, and this holding has spent a year arguing against guessing and cannot answer one word of it, and he said the method out loud after the true thing and not before it, and he was not thanked and nobody was forgiven anything.**"

"**And this holding has four figures it cannot check and a form it has not answered and a cart it will not ask about, and every one of those is entered, and none of them is thanked, and I have come to the end of that list and I am not going to add a fifth thing to it in a yard to see how it reads.**"

---

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the man of about seventy was fetched, the fifteenth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was given the figures and the counts in that order, and he was not thanked and was not explained.

He was given the compost line for the tenth month at eighty pounds and the run at ten not discharged in ten, and the compost line for the eleventh month not entered, the leak with no verdict, the beat with its column open and not opened today, the seventh column at thirty-five, the boards and the tin, the use log at fifteen lines and the reason it stands at fifteen lines, the rootmark and the four accounts of the arm, the form on a bench, **and the letter of the ninth of this month, and the man who came up the fen road on the tenth of this month on foot and was announced in a yard before he spoke, and what he said that was true, and what he said after it that was not, and the demand he made in a document that this holding can neither refuse nor accept.**

He asked for the last one to be repeated. The clerk repeated it and the reader of this body said it again in the same words, and he listened to it twice and said four words.

"**Ask who counts them.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that it does not know what they were about and has entered that it is not going to write and ask, and entered that in fifteen times it has never twice been about the same thing.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twentieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the man of about seventy was fetched for the fifteenth time in a season, was given the figures and the counts in that order, asked for the last one to be repeated, and said four words: ask who counts them. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about. The fen entered that it does not know and is not going to write and ask.**

And then at about the seventh hour of the evening, at the north gate, in about nine people, the handoff was read, and it is four sentences, and the day it names is not this book's.

"**The first of next month is a Saturday, and it is the day the compost line for the eleventh month falls on, and this holding does not know what that figure is, and the door has a column and a month and a figure, and this body has no standing on any of the three.**"

"**It did not write and ask. It is not going to. And the fen entered the day and not the figure, the way it entered the day and not the figure for a shelf four hundred miles off.**"

"**And that is the third clock this body owns that it can count and cannot price, and I am not going to make a column out of the three of them, and I am not going to say the word pattern out loud in this yard about any of them, and I would like the clerk to enter that I did not.**"

Nobody so much as one word was said. The man of about seventy went to the gate in his own time and the woman who does the sums went with him as far as the gate and not past it, and nobody has ever asked her why and the fen is not going to.Underneath the month's work, dated the twentieth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-eighth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A month that opened on a figure that came in the same twice in a row, with a word before it that does not agree with it, and a cost on four lines that does not go on the line of the shortfall. A form that says a migration along a road is a licensed use, and a rate of fourpence the hundredweight, and a class assigned by an office and not by a party, and this body putting itself in that class in its own hand before a man with a book because the alternative was worse. Soldiers on a wet road for an hour and a half at the fourth hour of a Tuesday, and a district officer's book read out in a yard, and this holding's own figures read out whole and unsoftened in front of him because it had them and nobody had ever asked, and one observation entered by the clerk and not explained by anybody. A seed side on a road at a trust's own cost, and a count of carts and not a decision, and two people who did not work together for the rest of the month and did not say the word and were not thanked.*

*A man who came up the fen road on foot and was announced before he spoke, and gave his name and his position in six sentences, and corrected this holding against his own party in the first ten minutes, and said a true thing about a form that this holding has spent a year arguing against and cannot answer, and said the method out loud after it and not before it, and was not thanked and nobody was forgiven anything. A dead man of thirty-one on a page in a party's own words and in nobody's scene, and a woman of the low acre who said both halves of what she came to say in the same breath, and the sentence that the accusation and the method are two things, said out loud in a room by a person who is neither the accused nor the accuser, and neither of the two was resolved. A draw at the half hour past the fourth hour on a Thursday, stopped at four feet out loud by a licensed man and entered at the top of a slope, every one of the four lines satisfied, and then the sentence said out loud in a yard of about nine that this body has written a rule about waiting and has called it a rule about care, and that any body can obey it perfectly for ever and never do a thing again and it will look exactly like a body that is being careful. A man of about thirty-one fetched for the fifteenth time, asking who counts them.*

*And on this holding's own side of it, a plate on an arm that got hot, a hand for a day, a headache that is not the mark, four feet, a figure of one pound for one day of a licensed man, and three pounds five shillings left in a drawer, and a gate count of two hundred and fourteen pails priced by a woman who said she was not glad about it and said the whole of it in one breath. Nobody in this book is thanked for being readable and cannot be.*

*This is what a month of a volume is.*

And so the eleventh month of this holding's book closed at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth, on a class this body put itself in, a form this body has not answered, a party that is a person, a man of thirty-one on a page, a rule that says nothing about what comes after the stop, a second man on a lane whose water nobody knows, and a well four hundred miles off that is five feet and is not coming back, **and every one of those went in with the hour on it and was read back out loud in front of the people it was about, and the first of next month belongs to somebody else, and this book entered the day and not the figure.**

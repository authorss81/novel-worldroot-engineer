# Chapter 185

## What Comes After The Stop

The thirteenth of this month was a Thursday, and at about the fourth hour of the morning, in the low ground four hundred yards below the north gate of this holding, at the boards over a shaft that came off on the twenty-first of a month three months back and was put back together on the twentieth of last month, a draw was begun under the four-line rule with a licensed man holding the book and the hour on it and the reader of this body at the top of the slope above with about nine people in it.

The first line was kept, and it was kept before anything else happened, and the fen entered that the first line was established before the draw was begun and not after it, and entered that this is the first time in the four years of that rule that the first line has been written down first.

And before any of that, in the yard at about the fourth hour, the rule was read out in full, four lines, in the ordinary voice, by the clerk, with about nine people standing in it, and it was read out for the third time this month, and the count of the times it has been read out in full in this month and the count of the times it has been used are two counts and are not the same count, **and the fen entered that and entered that a rule read out on the morning it is used is not a rule that is being kept and that this one is read out on the mornings it is not used as well and that the second of those is the one this body would have to give up first if it ever gave anything up.**

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month five months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the morning of the thirteenth of this month, in the low ground, by the licensed man, in his own hand, with the hour on it: **the return on this ground is carrying. It was read at the fifth hour of the evening of the twelfth, which is yesterday, and the reading of yesterday is on the page above this one and is the first entry of the four-line rule. The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying, and the return is carrying, and the first line is kept and is kept first.**

Nobody obstructed anybody, nothing was delayed, and no door was shut in a face. **The fen entered those three and entered that they are the correct conduct of a Thursday morning in this holding's own low ground and not a favour to anybody.**

The work was ordinary work for about forty minutes. Two men on the boards and a man on the boards with a shovel and the licensed man at the head of the shaft with the book open, and the level in the shaft was taken before and taken after and the two numbers were written down side by side and **no difference was written**, because that is what a licensed man does with two numbers and a night between them, and he has said so out loud in this book before.

There is a lamp on the housing and it was the fourth hour and it had not gone light yet, and the reader of this body was at the top of the slope with about nine people and did not go down, **and the fen entered that he did not go down and entered the reason he gave for it out loud on the slope before anybody asked him, which is that a body that stands over a crew is a body the crew talks to, and that this holding has four people on a floor and no custom for it, and that he had been told that on the nineteenth of last month and has not improved on it since.** The man of the north row with the cough was on the boards and went down the ladder with the shovel and came up it three times in forty minutes, and the reader of this body said out loud that he was not to be asked to do it again, and the man of the north row with the cough said nothing about that and did it anyway, **and the fen entered that he was asked not to and did it, and entered that a man who is told he is not to be asked and does the thing is not a man who has agreed to it and is not a man who has refused it either, and that the fen does not have a word for that and is not going to find one this month.**

And then the draw was begun at about half past four by the engineer of record, and it was a draw, and it was the second in this volume and the last, and the fen entered that before it was begun.

At four feet the licensed man called the stop out loud, in the ordinary voice, from the head of the shaft, to about four people on the boards, and the reader of this body heard it at the top of the slope with about nine people and did not go down, and the stop was entered by whoever held the book, which was the licensed man, in his own hand at the head of the shaft with the hour on it, and the clerk entered the same thing in this holding's book and read it back down the slope in the ordinary voice so that the four people on the boards heard it entered.

Two of the men on the boards stopped before the licensed man had finished the sentence, which the fen entered, because a stop is a stop and the half second in which it is not yet obeyed is not a failure of the rule and is not going to be written down as one.

**Four feet. The person who called the stop is not the person doing the work.** The stop was entered by whoever held the book, which was the clerk, who was at the top of the slope and wrote it in his own hand with the hour on it and read it back down the slope afterwards in the ordinary voice so that the four people on the boards heard it entered.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the morning of the thirteenth of this month, at the top of the slope, by the clerk, on the standing of a rule: **the draw begun this morning in the low ground of this holding at about the half hour past the fourth hour was stopped at four feet, out loud, by a licensed man, at the head of the shaft, and the stop was entered by whoever held the book, which was that licensed man, in his own hand with the hour on it, and the clerk entered the same thing in this holding's book and read it back down the slope to the boards. Every one of the four lines of the standing rule about the engineer of record was satisfied this morning: the return was carrying and that was established before the draw was begun and the first line was kept; the draw was stopped at four feet; the stop was called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book; and the person who called the stop is not the person doing the work. Nobody broke anything. Nobody has a defence. The fen entered that this is the second use of that rule in this volume and the last, and that the count of the uses is now two, and that it is not going to be three.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the draw got no line in the use log and the chapter says so on the page, and a draw gets no line. A line records a contact and never a reading. The use log stands at fifteen lines, the fifteenth being a contact on a boarded floor on the ninth of last month, and the two contacts since that day and the hold in between have got no line either, and the reason the log stands at fifteen is that this holding cannot write down what it did on the boards in a line anybody could check.**

Then the thing no rule in this book has ever been about, and the reader of this body said it at the top of the slope with about nine people standing in it, out loud and in the ordinary words, and he gave the reason first and the clerk was one of the nine.

"**Nine people came up that slope this morning at about the fourth hour to see whether a thing could be got through to a road. I have got their names in the book and I am not going to read them out and I am not going to count them, because a count would be a thing they did not come for.**"

"**They did not get it. The draw stopped at four feet, the four lines were satisfied, the book says what happened, and about four hundred yards of a road on the other side of that ground did not open. That is the whole of what the morning produced and every part of it was correct.**"

"**And here is what came after the stop, and it is a sentence I have been not saying since the day I wrote the rule. We have written a rule about waiting and we have called it a rule about care. Any body can obey it perfectly for ever and never do a thing again and it will look exactly like a body that is being careful.**"

"**That is the second time this book has said that sentence. The first time was in a room in this building with about eleven people in it, and I am not going to say the day out loud on this slope, and the clerk can enter that I did not say the day, and it is said now about a rule this body wrote and is still in force, and I am not going to soften it.**"

Nobody argued with him. The man of the north row with the cough was at the top of that slope and did not say anything, and the fen entered that he did not say anything and entered that he was the one who had carried the case up the slope on the twentieth of last month and that nobody thanked him for that either and the fen entered that.

The nine were held by what they had come for and the fen entered them in the book and not on a page in this building. A woman of a household in the low acre, who came because a pipe is being laid and her household's man had been told about it on the Sunday. A driver on the road the carts went onto, who had come up with them on the Friday and had been told there was a road and had come to see about it. A second driver's household, and a woman of a household in the low quarter who came because a seed gate and a pipe are the same argument. And the man of about fifty's household, which is one household of this holding and is at the top of that slope most days of its life and came up it anyway.

> Entered on the same line, in the clerk's hand: **nine people came up the slope at about the fourth hour of the morning of the thirteenth of this month to see whether a thing could be got through to a road. They are held by what they came for and are entered in the book by that and not by name, at the reader of this body's request. None of them asked for anything, and none of them was thanked, and none of them was given a figure.**

"**One of them has a fever. He is not one of the nine and he is a household in the low acre and I am not going to name him out loud on a slope because a fever is not a thing for a slope, and he is not the reason the nine came up. And one of the nine has lost a day of carrying that is not going to be repaid in anything, in money or in seed or in a week, and I have looked for about four minutes for a way to repay it and there is not one, and I would like the clerk to enter that I looked.**"

Then the arithmetic, said out loud on the slope and derived twice, and the two agree, and the examiner took the second one through a third time with her finger on the page after saying out loud that she keeps time and does not round.

"**One day of a licensed man this month, the thirteenth, at the rate in the hire book, which is a pound a day and has been a pound a day for two years. A pound is twenty shillings. One pound a day for one day is twenty shillings. That is one.**"

"**And what that leaves. Four pounds and fivepence is eighty-five shillings, and eighty-five less twenty is sixty-five, and sixty-five shillings is three pounds and five shillings. And in pence, because my father did pence and not shillings and I have never had a reason to stop. Four pounds five is a thousand and twenty pence. Twenty shillings is two hundred and forty pence. A thousand and twenty less two hundred and forty is seven hundred and eighty pence. Seven hundred and eighty pence is sixty-five shillings, which is three pounds and five shillings. The two agree.**"

"**And I am going to say the next part faster this month and I am not going to split it. A cost this holding can compute is a cost it can hand to an office. I said that in a room a month ago and I said it on the first of this month and I am saying it a third time, and I am not going to be glad about it and I am not going to be sorry about it, and nobody is going to thank me for it.**"

Nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be thanked and the reader of this body did not improve on it and was not asked to.

Then the cost, in the engineer's own hand, in a line of its own, with the hour on it, and the man wrote it in the cart shed at about the eighth hour with the door open and the clerk stood in it and did not leave.

> Entered at the ninth hour of the morning of the thirteenth of this month, in the cart shed, by the engineer of record, in his own hand, in a line of its own, and read back by the clerk and not improved:
>
> **The mark is hot. The hand from the wrist down was no use for about a day. A headache behind the eyes from about the seventh hour last night until about the fourth hour this morning, which is not the mark and is not a mark and is not to be entered as one. Four feet.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand: the four accounts of the arm in this book stand where they stood this morning. This is not a fifth account and it does not reconcile any of them, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it, and entered that a hold gets no use-log line and a draw gets no use-log line and this draw is in the book with the hour on it and not in the log because a line is a contact and not a reading.**

> Entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: **this is the second use of the standing rule in this volume and it was satisfied perfectly and nothing happened because of it. The rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and the four lines have never said it and no fifth line may be written, and the whole of what came after the stop this morning is nine people who came up a slope and a road that did not open and a fever in the low acre and a day of carrying that is not repaid in anything. The fen entered that and entered that this holding is not going to write a fifth line and is not going to improve on the four it has.**

And so the thirteenth of this month went down with a draw begun at the half hour past the fourth hour and stopped at four feet by a licensed man out loud and entered twice, once in the licensed man's hand at the head of the shaft and once in this holding's book at the top of the slope, **and every one of the four lines was satisfied the whole time and not one of them was about the nine people who came up to watch, and the count of the uses of that rule in this volume is two and is not going to be three, and the sentence about a rule about waiting was said for the second time in this book out loud on a slope with about nine people on a Thursday, and nobody thanked anybody.**

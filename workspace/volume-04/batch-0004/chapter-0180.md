# Chapter 180

## Soldiers On The Fen Road

The fourth of this month was a Tuesday, and at about the fourth hour of the morning an inspection came up the fen road in the rain and stopped at the gate, and there were soldiers on the road, and they were soldiers, and nobody in this yard asked them to be anything else.

They stood on the road in the rain for about an hour and a half and then two of them went as far as the gate and stood at the posts and did not come past them, and that is the whole of what soldiers did on the fen road on the fourth of this month. The engineer of record was in the low ground four hundred yards below the gate and did not come up and was not called. The licensed man's case was in the cart shed where it has been since the ninth of last month. Nothing on this holding was open that was not open every day of a year.

What came up the road was an officer of a district office with a book in a satchel and a clerk of a counter behind him with a flat case, and the officer was let in at the gate without a question and without a delay, and the man of about fifty got up off the bench before he got to it.

The officer asked two questions in that yard and both of them were about the yard and neither of them was about a person. He asked how many households are on this head, and the answer given to him was sixteen, and the man of about seventy was standing about nine feet away when it was given and the fen entered that he was standing there and entered that nobody looked at him when it was said. He asked whether there is a licence on this head, and the answer was that there is not and there has not been for two years, and the officer wrote both answers down in the ordinary way and did not ask a third question.

And then the licensed man's standing was said out loud, because the reader of this body asked for it, and the reason is on the page two paragraphs below and is the reason he gave.

"**Say your standing where nine people can hear it, and I will say why afterwards.**"

Nobody obstructed anybody. Nothing was delayed. No door in this holding was shut in an officer's face. **The fen entered all three of those separately, and entered that they are the correct conduct of this holding and not a favour to anybody, and entered that a body which is correct about this sort of thing does not get thanked for it by anybody and is not going to.**

The book was read out in the yard with about twenty-five people in it, and about eleven of those twenty-five had come out of the rain and were standing against the seed house wall with water coming off them onto the boards, and the officer read it himself and did not ask anybody to hold it.

> Entered by an officer of a district office at the gate on this day, in the office's own hand, on a form of the office, and the form is in order and nothing in it is a finding against any person:
>
> **One. The inspection of a road and of the works and parties upon it is made in the ordinary course, and is made at the hour and in the order set out in the schedule attached.**
>
> **Two. A body of households on one head that uses a road is a party to the use of that road and is to be returned under the class assigned to it, and the class assigned to it is migration, and the class was assigned by the office and not by the party.**
>
> **Three. No obstruction, delay or refusal is to be offered to an inspection, and an inspection may not be refused, and a body that has a question about a form is to put the question in the form and not in a yard.**
>
> **Four. This is made in the ordinary course and no part of it is a finding against any person. A party entered under a class is not a holder of anything by being entered under a class.**

Nobody said a word in that yard for about four seconds and the fen entered the four seconds and entered that it did not improve on them, and then the reader of this body stood up and said why this holding was going to put itself in a class in its own hand, and he said the reason before he said the thing.

"**I am going to put this holding in that class myself, in my own hand, this morning, and I am going to say why out loud in a yard of twenty-five people before I do it, and the reason is not that the office has told me to.**"

"**A body that does not put itself in a class is a body that somebody else puts in it. A class is a thing an office can use against a body in a room, in a letter, in a year, when there is nobody in the room who was in the yard. I have watched that done to a body of four houses on the other side of this district and I am not going to be put in one by a man with a book who has done nothing wrong.**"

"**And the third line of that form says a question about a form is to go in the form and not in a yard. That is a correct line and I am not going to argue with it. I want it entered that this holding has been putting its questions in yards for two months and that the office is telling it in writing that a yard is not a form, and that I have not got a form, and that I am not going to be embarrassed about that in front of the man who wrote the line.**"

Then the thing the chapter is for, and the reader of this body asked for it, and he asked for it out loud in the yard before the officer had finished putting the case down.

"**I would like the standing figures of this holding read out loud, in this yard, in front of that officer. All of them. Including the ones that are not entered.**"

The officer said yes in about four seconds and did not ask why, and the fen entered that he did not ask why and entered that he was not asked to.

And then the class was filled in, and the reader of this body filled it in himself, and the officer countersigned it and read it back, and the fen entered that the pen was this holding's and not the office's, and that a body which lets an office write its own class down is a body that has not understood what a class is.

"**Migration, says the box. And I am going to write it in the box with this holding's own hand and not have it written for me, and the reason is on the page already and I am not going to say it twice in one morning. There is no cart on this road of ours and there are no people walking on it and the box is not asking me whether there are. The box is asking me what I am, and I am, and it is going to have my answer in my own writing.**"

> Entered at the sixth hour of the morning of the fourth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a book: **the standing figures of this holding were read out whole and unsoftened in a yard of about twenty-five people, in front of an officer of a district office who came to check them, at the reader of this body's request and not at the officer's.** The compost line for the tenth month is eighty pounds on a specification of four hundred hundredweight and the run stands at ten not discharged in ten. The leak is three-quarters of an inch, no cause, no due date, not measured since the twenty-first of a month two months back. The beat is not logged since, cause not established, nothing underneath it, column open, and the column was not opened in that yard and is not going to be. The seventh column of the door is at thirty-five. The standing rule is four lines, every one about a stop, and the count of the uses is one. The use log is at fifteen lines. The rootmark is four inches and branches twice and there is no second mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this book and none of them agrees with any of the others. A form on a bench in the seed house is not answered, not returned and not torn up. The well on a shelf four hundred miles off is five feet against nine feet for the sixteenth of a month six months back and is not decomposed and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.
>
> **And entered on the same line: this is the first time in eleven years that the figures of this holding have been read out loud to a person who came to find out whether they were true. The fen entered that this body asked for it, and entered why, which is that a body that keeps a book anybody may read ought to be the first one to hand it over, and that the alternative is a body which only reads its book when there is nobody in the yard who can check it.**

And then the things that were not entered, and they were entered as not entered, and the reason is the same reason four times, and the officer did not ask for any of them.

> Entered on the same line: **not entered, and the reason is the same in each of the four. The form on the bench was not entered, because this holding has no standing on it and will not put its name to it and will not refuse to either. The column of the beat was not opened, because opening it in front of a man with a book would be a thing done for the man with the book. The fourth cart was not entered, because it went up a road in the eighth month and has not come down and this body has not written and asked and is not going to. The word a man of about thirty-five has refused for nineteen years was not entered, because it is not a name this holding has been given and is not a name this holding is going to supply.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the officer did not ask for any of the four. The fen entered that he did not ask and entered the reason this holding gives, which is that he is not the office and this is not an examination of a person.**

The officer wrote nothing down for about ten minutes and then wrote one line down, and read it back before he closed the case, and it is this.

> **Entered on the fourth of this month at the north gate in the ordinary course of the inspection: the figures of the body were read out in full in the yard at the body's own request. No obstruction and no delay. No finding.**

And then Dunmar Fitch entered the one observation he is allowed and nobody explained it and nobody asked.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the morning of the fourth of this month, by the clerk, on his own motion, unasked: **the figures of this holding were read out loud, in a yard, to a man who came to find out whether they were true.**

---

The fifth of this month was a Wednesday, and the figures of the day were read at the gate at about the eleventh hour in the ordinary way in a yard of about nine, and a licensed man was standing in it and had been since the fourth hour, **and he was not on this holding's time that day and did not charge for it and the fen entered that he was not on this holding's time and entered that he was in the yard because he had business of his own at the sixth hour down the road and had come up the slope to stand in the rain with about nine people rather than go on without saying it**, and the standing rule was read out in full again, four lines, in the same yard, before the figures were closed.

A licensed man on this fen road holds a licence on the water record and not on the rootwright roll, and the two rolls are different and have never once been run together in this book and are not going to be.

The licensed man said the small professional sentence, standing up, in the ordinary voice, and it is short and it was not asked for.

"**A man on the water record can be on this road and can look at a shaft and can say a thing about water and cannot lay a graft and cannot go near a living root, and if anybody in this yard has ever heard me say otherwise then I have never corrected it and I am correcting it now in a yard of nine people on a Wednesday morning.**"

"**And the other half, which is mine and not anybody's. There were soldiers on this road yesterday at the fourth hour and there were four of them and one of them was wet through to the shirt, and I stood in a gate for an hour and a half and watched water come off a man onto boards, and I have been doing this work for nineteen years and I have never once had a reason to be glad that a road had soldiers on it, and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this yard that I have one now.**"

Nobody thanked him and he did not ask to be thanked. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is thirty-nine this season, and the count is not a complaint.

The officer and the clerk of the counter went back down the fen road in the rain at about the tenth hour and stopped at the gate to write the time of their leaving on the back of their own sheet, which is their ordinary practice and which nobody in this yard had seen an officer do before, **and the fen entered that they did it at the gate and not in the office and entered that it is the same hand and the same kind of entry and that this holding has no opinion about it at all and is not going to develop one.**

And so the fourth and the fifth of this month went down with soldiers on a wet road and a correct book and four lines in an officer's own hand and one line in his hand at the end, **and this holding put itself in a class in its own writing before a man with a book, and read its own figures out loud to him in the rain because it had them and nobody had ever asked, and the only observation anybody made about any of it was three words long and is in the book and is not going to be explained.**

# Chapter 182

## In Front Of People

The eighth of this month was a Saturday, and at about the eleventh hour of the morning, in a full yard of about twenty-five people, the reader of this body said the thing he has been carrying since a room with the doors shut, and he said it standing up, and nobody made him.

He had told one person on the Thursday night that he was going to say a thing in that yard on the Saturday at the eleventh hour and had not said what it was, and the person he told it to asked him nothing about it, and the fen entered that she asked nothing and entered that a man who says out loud in a yard on a Thursday night that he is going to say something on a Saturday has given the yard the day and the hour and kept the thing, and that this holding has no custom for that and has it now.

There were about twenty-five people in that yard because it is a Saturday and because it had rained in the night and everybody who had anywhere to be was already in it, and the ordinary business of that hour was going on when he stood up, which is to say that two of them were arguing about a gate post and one of them was telling the man of about fifty that his cart shed roof was not going to last the month and the man of about fifty was saying that it was not his roof. None of that stopped. **The fen entered that none of it stopped and entered that this is the first time in this book's history that something has been said out loud in a yard of about twenty-five people and the ordinary business of a Saturday carried on around it at the same time, and entered that the reader of this body had asked for the eleventh hour specifically and not the fourth hour, and gave the reason, which is that at the fourth hour the yard is empty and at the eleventh hour it is full, and that a thing said to nine people is a thing said to a room and a thing said to twenty-five is a thing said to a yard.**

He gave the date of the room first, then the hour, then the number of people in it, and he gave them in that order, and the clerk was one of the four and confirmed the hour out loud in the ordinary voice from his own hand before the reader of this body got to the fourth sentence of it.

"**The twenty-fourth of a month two months back, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in a room with the doors shut, and there were four of us in it and one of us was the clerk.**"

"**I said it to one person and I would not write it down. The reason I gave at the time is that I could not defend it and I had not got a second reading. That reason was a good reason then and it is not a good reason today and I am not going to pretend to this yard that it is.**"

"**The date it named fell on a Sunday, the eleventh of last month, and nothing happened, and there was nothing to be honest about. The man who did it is the one who has to say that, and I said it in front of him and not behind his back, and I am saying it in front of nine hundred people this morning, and there is nothing on this page to be honest about, and I would like that entered.**"

Nobody said anything. The man of about fifty did not move. The man of about seventy was at the back of that yard with his back to the wall and had not come forward and the fen entered that he did not come forward and entered that nobody looked at him while it was being said.

Then the rest of it, in about four sentences, and he did not build up to it and he did not stop in the middle of it.

"**I did it to one person and not to a body. A thing that is in one room and not in a book is a thing that only one person can be asked to carry, and I made a person into a book, and I would not have done that to a stranger.**"

"**I want it entered that I would not have done it to a stranger, and that the fact that I would not have done it to a stranger is not a defence and is the reason it was worse. I have not got anybody to forgive me for it and I am not going to ask this yard for one and I am not going to be forgiven today, because it is not that kind of day.**"

Then the clerk entered it and read it back in the ordinary voice and did not improve it, and about twenty-five people heard a man read another man's apology back to him in a yard, and the fen entered that it was read back in the ordinary voice and entered why.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the eighth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a statement made out loud: **the reader of this body said, standing up in a yard of about twenty-five people, that on the twenty-fourth of a month two months back, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in a room with the doors shut with four people in it, of whom the clerk was one, he said a thing to one person and would not write it down; that the reason he gave at the time was that he could not defend it and had not got a second reading; that the date it named fell on a Sunday, the eleventh of last month, on which nothing happened that this body can see; that the man who did it is the one who has to say that; and that he said it in front of the man it concerned and not behind his back. He said he was saying it in front of nine hundred people and the fen entered that the number is not a count and did not correct it, and entered the reason, which is that a man making an apology is entitled to one wrong number about how many people are hearing him and that it is the only number in this book this holding is not going to check.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: he said he did it to one person and not to a body, that a thing which is in one room and not in a book is a thing only one person can be asked to carry, that he made a person into a book, that he would not have done that to a stranger, and that the fact that he would not have done it to a stranger is not a defence and is the reason it was worse. He asked for no forgiveness and said he would not be thanked and said it is not that kind of day. The clerk read the whole of it back in the ordinary voice and did not improve it.**

Nobody thanked him. Nobody said a word. The fen entered all three of those and entered the reason, and the reason is the whole of the yard and the whole of the book.

> Entered on the same line: **nobody in that yard of about twenty-five people thanked the reader of this body for saying it and nobody said anything else. The fen entered that nobody treated it as a thing between two people, and entered the reason, which is that about twenty-five people watched a man say a thing out loud in daylight, and a thing said out loud in a yard is a fact about a yard and not a thing two people have. The fen entered that the reader of this body was not thanked and that he was not thanked because he did not ask and could not have been, and the fen did not improve on that.**

Then she answered, and it was one sentence, in the ordinary voice, and it was not forgiveness and it was not a refusal, and about twenty-five people heard it and the clerk was one of them.

"**I am not going to say that makes it right and I am not going to say it does not, and I am going to go on the road on Monday, and the reason I am going is on a page with my name at the top of it and not on this one, and I would like it entered that I said the reason is on another page.**"

She said it standing at the wall side of that yard and she did not raise her voice to do it and she said it to about twenty-five people and not to him, and then she sat down on the bench in the cart shed and worked on a list until the yard emptied, and the fen entered that she sat down on that bench and worked on a list, and entered that the list was the trust's and not this holding's, and entered that nobody sat down with her.

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the eighth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of an answer: **the seedwright of the trust answered in one sentence and in the ordinary voice. She said she is not going to say that makes it right and is not going to say it does not. She said she is going on the road on Monday. She said the reason she is going is on a page with her name at the top of it and not on this one, and she asked for it to be entered that she said the reason is on another page. The clerk entered the request. Nobody in that yard treated the answer as forgiveness and the fen entered that nobody treated it as a refusal either, and entered that the fen is not going to improve on the answer and is not going to try to find out which of the two it was.**
>
> **She was not thanked. She was not forgiven on the page and she forgave nothing on the page. Nobody sat down with her on the bench in the cart shed and the fen entered that and entered that the man of the north row with the cough put that bench there a month ago and told nobody about it and has not been thanked for it since.**

Then, before the figures, the reader of this body said one more thing in that yard, and it was about himself and it was short, and about nine people were still in it.

"**I am not going to say that again this month and I would like the clerk to enter that I said that, and I am not saying it because it should not be said twice. A thing said once in a yard is a thing a man did. A thing said twice is a thing this holding does on a first of a month, and then it is a standing, and a standing is a thing this holding has to keep, and I have watched this holding get a standing it did not ask for out of a thing a man said once in a room, and I am not going to build one out of this.**"

> Entered on the same line: **the reader of this body said in that yard that he was not going to say the thing again this month, and gave the reason, which is that a thing said once in a yard is a thing a man did and a thing said twice is a standing this holding would have to keep. The fen entered that and did not improve on it. The man of about fifty said nothing and the fen entered that he said nothing.**

Then the figures of that Saturday went into the book in the ordinary way at about the sixth hour of the evening, and they were the figures of a day that was not a reading day and the reader of this body did not soften one of them. The leak at three-quarters of an inch with no verdict and not measured again. The beat not logged since with its column open. The seventh column at thirty-five. The boards and the March's tin, day minus sixty-six and day minus nineteen, read out loud by the clerk before either number. The standing rule, four lines, used once this season and not used this month. The use log at fifteen lines. The rootmark four inches and branching twice and no second mark, and four accounts of that arm that reconcile none of them. The form on a bench. The well on a shelf four hundred miles off at five feet and not coming back. **And a woman of thirty-one asleep in a cart shed nine hundred yards from a door this holding reads out in front of twenty-five people, entered as a fact and not as anything else.**

And then the question went into that yard at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and nobody took it, and the count did not move.

"**Whose order is it.**"

Nobody answered. About nine people were in that yard by then and the man of about fifty stood about four feet away with his hand flat on the top of a bench and the clerk did not answer and the reader of this body did not answer, and the fen entered that it is the third time this body has put that question into a yard and answered it never, and that the count of the times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is six and did not move.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the eighth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the reader of this body put the question whose order it is into a yard of about nine people and nobody took it. The count of the times this body has asked it is three and none has been answered. The count of the times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is six, and three of those six are this question, and both counts stand and neither has been added to the other. Nobody in that yard asked who told them and the fen entered that nobody asked.**

And so the eighth of this month went down in daylight in front of about twenty-five people, and a man said out loud that he had made a person into a book and that he would not have done it to a stranger and that this is not a defence, and a woman said one sentence back and it was not forgiveness and it was not a refusal and neither of them was thanked, **and the count of the questions this holding has available to a room and not taken is at six and has not moved since the first of this month, and the day is not finished, and a man said the thing in front of nine hundred people in a yard of about twenty-five, and the fen entered the number and did not correct it.**

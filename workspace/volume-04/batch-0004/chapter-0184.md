# Chapter 184

## What It Did, And What It Said

The eleventh of this month was a Tuesday, and a return came up the fen road on the ordinary post at about the tenth hour of the morning, and it was four lines, and it was correct, and it was the party's own return, and it reported a death in its own operation in its own words in the ordinary course.

> Return of an operation. Made on the tenth of this month by the Pale Orchard, a party, on that party's own paper, on the ordinary post, four lines, and the form is in order and nothing in it is softened:
>
> **One. On the eighth of this month, in the course of moving water into a quarter in which wells are off, one man died. He was thirty-one. He is named in the return of the district and not in this one.**
>
> **Two. The manner of it was a fall of about nine feet onto stone while a pipe was being laid. The party paid for the stone to be taken up and for the ground to be made good and it did not pay for the man and could not.**
>
> **Three. The party reports the death, and reports it against itself, and does not report a cause, and says in this line that it is not able to give a cause that anybody could check.**
>
> **Four. This return is made in the ordinary course and no part of it is a finding against any person. A party that reports a death in its own operation is a party and is entered as one.**

The person who keeps the seed house read it out in a yard of about eleven people and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and he read it back with the hour on it and the clerk entered it and nobody thanked anybody and **the reader of this body did not say one word about it in that yard that was not about what the sheet was, and the fen entered that and entered that it is the fourth time in two months that the reader of this body has received a piece of paper with a person in it and has not made the person into a subject of the yard.**

Then the arithmetic, and it was done in the yard because nobody would go back into the room, and the woman who does the sums did the two halves and put them on one line and did not put a figure at the end of the line.

"**What this holding can check. That sheet says one man, on the eighth, thirty-one, a fall of about nine feet onto stone while a pipe was being laid. That is three figures and a day and I can count those and anybody in this yard can count those and I am not going to do the counting out loud because it would take forty minutes and make a spectacle of a dead man.**"

"**What this holding cannot check. Whether the schedule put him there. Whether the count of who is in a quarter put him there. Whether the rate of fourpence the hundredweight and the class and the return made a man of thirty-one be on that road on that day with a pipe in his hands instead of somewhere else with a wage. I cannot check any of that and neither can anybody in this yard, and I am not going to pretend that a figure about a man on a page is a figure about why the man was on the page.**"

"**So the two are on one line and they are not added together, and I am going to say the sentence and somebody in this yard will remember that I have said it before, and they are right, and I am going to say it again anyway. A figure which is the sum of two things one of which was never measured is not a figure, and it is not going into this book, and I would like the clerk to enter that this is the sixth time in two months that this holding has been beaten home by somebody else's arithmetic, and that the count of six is entered, and that it is not going to stop being entered.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the eleventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a sum: **the number this holding can check in a return of a death and the number it cannot check are entered on the same line and are not added together. The woman who does the sums stated out loud, in a yard of about eleven people, that a figure which is the sum of two things one of which was never measured is not a figure, and that this is the sixth time in two months that this holding has been beaten home by somebody else's arithmetic, and the count of six is entered and is not going to stop being entered.** Nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be thanked and the reader of this body did not improve on it and was not asked to.

The death is on a page. There is no body in a room in this holding and there was not one in a yard either, and the fen entered that the reader of this body did not ask a single question about the man and entered why, which is one sentence: **a man is thirty-one and dead on a piece of paper and the only thing this holding could offer him is a question.**

---

And then in the evening of the same Tuesday, with the bay doors shut and about eleven people in the room, a woman of a household in the low acre came up the fen road and sat down, and she had not asked to be let in and she was let in at the gate, and she was not thanked and she did not ask to be thanked, and she said six sentences and she said them in the ordinary voice and the clerk was one of the eleven and wrote all of them.

She came in at about the seventh hour with wet boots and she did not take them off and nobody asked her to, and about eleven people were in that room and the seedwright of the trust was at the table with the trust's ledger shut in front of her and did not open it while that woman was talking, **and the fen entered that the ledger was shut and entered that it was shut by the person whose it is and not by anybody else, and entered that a woman who walks nine miles in wet boots to say six sentences and is owed something is not going to be made to sit through a book about anybody's seed.**

"**I am not going to talk about the man. I did not know him and I am not here about him and anybody in this room that thinks I came up nine miles to be sorry has misunderstood the whole of the evening.**"

"**I am here because the party is not wrong about the schedule and is wrong about the count, and I have been trying for a month to work out how those two can both be in the same sentence and I have worked it out on my own feet and I am going to say it in this room.**"

"**A count of who is in a quarter is a count of who can be reached. That is what a count is. You do not count a quarter so that you know the quarter. You count it so that you can get to it.**"

"**And my household was not in it. And my household's man is one of the people it did not reach, and he is in the low acre and not in the low quarter, and he has been on the same road as those men every day of his life, and he is not on any page, and nobody counted him because there was no reason to count him.**"

"**So both halves of that are true and I am saying both halves and I am not going to be asked to pick one. He is not counted and he is not reached and it is the same gap and it is not a different gap and it is not somebody else's bookkeeping, because the party is not a bad office. The party is four people who did exactly what I have just described, correctly, in the order they had to do it in, and got it wrong, and did it knowing they were getting it wrong, and wrote it on a page.**"

"**And I am not here to tell you what to do about it and I want that said in my house's name and not in mine, and I am not going to say a name of anybody in that yard in the low acre, because I said on the twenty-second of last month that I did not want a body that is not a quarter to come, and I have not changed my mind, and there is a difference between telling a thing in a book and bringing a body, and I am the one who has to carry my own door.**"

And then a person in that room who is not the accused and not the accuser said the sentence the volume needs said once, out loud, in the open, in about six words, and about eleven people heard it.

Anneke Roos said it standing up, in the ordinary voice, without building up to it.

"**The accusation and the method are two things, and we have been arguing with one of them for a month.**"

And then the reader of this body said the second half of it, which is the half he is going to be blamed for, and he gave the reason first and about eleven people were in that room and the clerk was one of the eleven.

"**A method that counts who can be reached is a method that will reach the people it did not count the next time, and I am going to say that out loud in this room, and the reason I am saying it here and not in a yard is that everybody in this room has already said it out loud to themselves, and there is no point in the ninth hour of the evening finding out who has been thinking it.**"

"**And the reason I am saying it out loud at all is that a thing said to yourself is a thing this body has a habit of, and it is not a good habit, and I have been in four rooms in two months where everybody knew what was happening and nobody had it written down, and I was in one of the four, and that is how a page about a man who is thirty-one and dead gets written and nobody ever has to answer for it.**"

Then the woman of the low acre asked the only question anybody asked in that room that evening, and she asked it in the open and in the ordinary voice and about eleven people heard it, and she did not ask it in a yard and she did not ask it twice.

"**Then what is this holding going to do about it.**"

And he gave the only honest sentence available to him and he gave it in about five sentences and he did not dress any of it up.

"**This holding has no standing, no charter, no form on which to put that number, and no second reading of anything, and the count of the things this body does not know is four and is not going to be five this month because a man said a hard thing in a room.**"

"**So it is going to do the two things it can do. It is going to write it down with the hour on it. And it is going to read it back out loud in front of the people it is about, which is going to be this room, and about nine people in a yard, and a door nine hundred yards off with a column on it.**"

"**And those two things are worth very little. I am not going to dress them up. A page is not a supply and a reading-out is not an answer, and the woman who asked the question is owed more than a page and this body cannot give her more than a page, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said that in front of her and not in a book she has not read.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The woman of the low acre did not ask to be thanked and the reader of this body was not thanked and Anneke Roos was not thanked and the fen entered all three and did not improve on any of them.

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the eleventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a limit: **a woman of a household in the low acre came up the fen road and said six sentences, and they are entered entire, and she said both halves of what she came to say in the same breath and was not asked to choose between them and nobody asked her to. She said a count of who is in a quarter is a count of who can be reached; that her household was not in it; that her household's man is one of the people it did not reach; and that the party is four people who did what they had to do correctly, in order, knowing they were getting it wrong, and wrote it on a page. Nobody in that room named a person in the low acre and the fen entered that nobody did.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: Anneke Roos said, standing up, in the ordinary voice, that the accusation and the method are two things and that this body has been arguing with one of them for a month. The reader of this body said the second half and said that a method that counts who can be reached will reach the people it did not count the next time, and gave the reason he said it out loud in a room, which is that everybody in that room has already said it to themselves and that a thing said to oneself is a habit this body has and does not want. The woman of the low acre then asked, in the open, what this holding is going to do about it. He said it has no standing, no charter, no form and no second reading, and that it is going to write it down with the hour on it and read it back out loud in front of the people it is about, and that those two things are worth very little and he was not going to dress them up, and that the woman who asked is owed more than a page.**

And so the eleventh of this month went down with a dead man on a page in a party's own words and a woman from the low acre saying both halves of the only thing anybody had, **and the reader of this body said out loud in a room with the doors shut that the accusation and the method are two things, and it did not resolve either of them, and the accusation is not the method and the method is not the accusation, and both of those are now on the page in a woman's mouth and not in this body's, and a body of sixteen households with no charter went home able to do two things and one of them is writing things down.**

# Chapter 183

## The Name Becomes A Person

The ninth of this month was a Sunday, and a letter came up the fen road on the ordinary post at about the third hour of the afternoon in a leather bag, and it was four lines, and it was correct, and it was on the party's own paper and not on an office's.

> Written and sent on the eighth of this month by the Pale Orchard, a party, on that party's own paper, on the ordinary post, four lines, and the form is in order and nothing in it is a finding against any person:
>
> **One. The supply of water at no price made into the low quarter between the second and the seventh of last month has not been resumed, and the reason is not given on this sheet and no reason is asked for.**
>
> **Two. The party will be in the district on the tenth of this month. A person will come to this holding, on foot, and will stand in whatever yard this holding keeps its book in.**
>
> **Three. That person is the person who signs these returns, and his name is at the foot of this sheet, and a party is a party and is not an office and has no standing over anybody.**
>
> **Four. This letter is made in the ordinary course. It asks this holding for no form, no certificate, no answer, and no water, and no part of it is a finding against any person.**

It is a letter and it is not a return, and the fen entered the difference on the same line, and the difference is that a return is a thing an office keeps and a letter is a thing a person sends, and that this holding has had four returns in two months and has never had a letter.

**The name at the foot of it had been four words in a return on the seventh of last month and it had been read out loud in a room of about nine people and the fen entered that a name in a return cannot be answered, and entered that a name in a letter can be, and entered that this holding has been saying for a month that a party is a party and has now got one standing in a yard with its hands at its sides.**

---

The tenth of this month was a Monday, and he came up the fen road on foot at about the second hour of the afternoon, and he was not announced before he came, and he was announced before he spoke.

The person who keeps the seed house stood up in the yard with about eleven people in it and said who was at the gate in the ordinary voice and before anybody had asked her anything.

"**A man has come up this road on foot and he is not of this body and he is not on anything and he is not a representative of anything, and I am saying that in a yard before he opens his mouth, and the same way I have said it about every man who has come up this road in two months, and I am not going to start doing it differently for him.**"

Then she said his name, and she said it off the sheet, and he said the rest himself.

"**My name is Tarin Callow. I am the person who signs the returns of the Pale Orchard. I held a post in the district water office at Fennmere for six years and I do not hold one now and I have not held one for eleven. I have no licence and no charter and no paper except that one. I am not a representative of anything and I am not anybody's guide.**"

About eleven people were in that yard and nobody thanked him and the clerk entered the six sentences and read them back and the reader of this body asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-one this season, and the count is not a complaint.

He was correct in every particular, and the fen entered that on the day and not afterwards, and the fen entered that a man being correct in every particular in a yard of about eleven people on a Monday afternoon is a harder thing to write down than a man being wrong, and that this holding has a great deal more practice at the second.

Then, in the first ten minutes, he corrected this holding once, and the correction was against the party and not against this holding, and about eleven people heard it.

"**Your book says a party supplied water at no price into that quarter for six days. I am going to correct your book against my own party and not against you, and I am going to do it in the first ten minutes so that nobody in this yard can think for the rest of the afternoon that I came here to be liked.**"

"**We were not giving it. The supply was already paid for. There is a line in our own accounts that says what it cost and I have not got it with me and I am not going to read you a figure I cannot show you, and the line says we bought it and gave it away, and there was nothing in it for anybody in that quarter. That is the correction. It is not a smaller thing than the one you have written. It is a different thing, and it is worse, because what you have written down is that we did something for them and what is true is that we moved our own water through their quarter.**"

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the tenth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a correction: **the person named in the letter of the ninth corrected this holding, in the first ten minutes, in a yard of about eleven people, against the party and not against this holding, and said that the six days of water at no price were supplied at no price because the supply was already paid for, and not because of anything to do with anybody in that quarter, and said he would not read out a figure he could not show, and said that the correction is not a smaller thing than the one in this book's book and is a different thing and is worse.** The fen entered the correction and entered that this is the first time in this book's history that a person has corrected this holding against his own interest, and entered that it does not settle anything.

And then the true thing, and it is true, and about eleven people in that yard heard it and this holding has spent a year arguing against guessing and cannot answer it.

"**A controlled system does not fail the way an uncontrolled one fails. That is the whole of what I have come to say and I am going to say it plainly because you will not be able to argue with a thing said plainly.**"

"**An uncontrolled system leaves people to find out. Your four wells went off on four different days in a month and every household in that lane worked out for itself what to do about it, and some of them found out before anybody else and some of them found out last, and a quarter of four hundred and forty-one people spent a month doing arithmetic about water with no instrument. That is what not having a form looks like from the inside of a district office, and I have read the returns and I know what it looks like from the outside, and from the outside it is forty-one houses each of them solving the same problem badly at a different speed.**"

"**A system with a schedule and a rate and a class of use and a form does not do that. A quarter that has a form on it is a quarter in which nobody has to guess where the water went. You can go and read it. So can I. So can the man with the cart. You do not have to trust anybody, because there is a page and the page is dated, and a page that is dated is worth more to a person with a fever than a well-informed neighbour.**"

Then the other half, and he did not soften it, and he said it in the same voice and at the same speed and about eleven people in that yard did not look away from him while he said it.

"**And the method, and the method is what I am actually here about and I am not going to put it after the true thing to get the true thing said first. The schedule is made. The class is chosen. And the people in the class are counted.**"

"**A count is made of who is in a quarter so that the quarter can be supplied. And a quarter that has been supplied on a schedule for a season has a supply that belongs to the supply, and not to the people in it, and when the schedule changes the people in it are not a people any more, they are a class that has moved or a class that has not. And I know what that sentence sounds like on the other side of a form, because I have written it in a letter this week and I had it not typed, and I want that on your page and I want it on your page in my words and not in a summary.**"

Nobody said anything. About nine of the eleven were still in that yard and two had gone, and the fen entered that two had gone and entered that nobody followed them and entered why, which is one sentence: **a man who has just said a true thing and then said the thing the true thing is built on does not get followed out of a yard by a body that has learned something this month about letting people be finished.**

Then the demand, and it was correct, and it is a document, and the reader of this body could not refuse it and could not accept it, and the fen entered both of those facts separately and entered the difference between them, which is the whole argument of this volume in one line.

> **Entered on the tenth of this month by the person named in the letter, in the yard, in the ordinary voice, and asked for as a document and not as a favour, and it is this: that this holding will state, in its own book and on a date, the number of people in the low quarter, and where they are, and how many of them there are.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the reader of this body cannot refuse this and cannot accept it and said so out loud in the yard in one sentence and did not improve on it. The fen entered that it cannot refuse it, because a body that refuses a question in the open has said the question, and it cannot accept it, because this holding does not know the answer and has said so for two months in a room and in a yard and on a bench where a form is sitting unanswered. And the fen entered the difference between those two facts, and entered that the difference is the whole argument of this volume in one line, and entered that nobody in that yard argued about it.**

Then the reader of this body asked him one question, in the open, in the yard, and he gave the reason first, and about eleven people heard the reason and the question and the four sentences of the answer.

"**I am going to ask you one question and I am going to say why out loud first, and the reason is that you have told this yard what you will not do today and I want it in the book in your own mouth before you go, and I would rather have it now in daylight than have somebody in four years ask me what you would not say and have me say I did not think to ask.**"

"**What the party is for.**"

He gave it in about four sentences and the first of them was the half that is checkable, and he did not number it and the fen did not number it for him.

"**A party is a set of people who have agreed to be answerable for one another's water. That is all it is. Four of us, at the start, in a wet spring, on a road out of a district where nobody was going to answer for anything, and we said we would each be the one who had to be asked. We wrote it down. We gave each other the right to be asked by a household that had nothing, and the household did not have to know which of us to ask, and that was the whole of the invention and it is not clever and it should not have taken a party to think of it.**"

"**What I am not going to tell this yard is why we are organized that way and what we are for beyond that, and I am not going to tell you because it is not checkable. I could say a great deal about who the four of us answer to and about what the party intends to be in five years and every word of it would be true and none of it could be checked by anybody standing in this yard, and this yard has had two months of a body that says it does not know what it does not know, and I am not going to come in here and hand it four months of things that sound like that and are not.**"

Nobody thanked him. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-two this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the tenth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **a person was asked one question in the open, in a yard of about eleven people, with the reason given first, and the question was what the party is for. He gave the half of it that is checkable, in about four sentences, being that a party is a set of people who have agreed to be answerable for one another's water, and that there were four of them at the start on a road out of a district, and that they wrote it down and gave each other the right to be asked by a household that had nothing. He said he is not going to say why they are organized that way or what they intend to be, and gave the reason, which is that it is not checkable and that anything else would be four months of words that sound like a body admitting what it does not know and is not.** The fen entered that the count of that refusal is one, and entered that the count of one is a count of this morning and not a count of a man, and entered that it is not going to be carried forward as a standing.

A name that was four words in a return a month ago stood in a yard of about eleven people at about the second hour of the afternoon and made a correction against his own party in the first ten minutes, **and he said a true thing this holding cannot answer and a method this holding cannot defend and a demand this holding can neither refuse nor accept, and he was not thanked, and nobody was forgiven anything, and the reader of this body did not shake a hand and did not offer a chair and did not say one word that was not asked for.**

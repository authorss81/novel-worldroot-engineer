# Chapter 178

## The First Of The Eleventh

The first of the eleventh month was a Saturday, and the compost line for the tenth month fell on it at about the eleventh hour in a yard with about twenty-five people in it, and the figure came in in a hand that is not the hand this book has been written in for eleven years, and the person who keeps the seed house read it out and the examiner read it back and then the woman who does the sums said the word before she said the number, which is her standing and the count of that standing is now in three volumes.

**Eighty pounds.**

**The specification is four hundred hundredweight. The tenth month of a run of ten not discharged in ten.**

She said the word first, and she said it in the room and not in the yard, and about eleven people had gone into the seed house with the doors shut at about the fourth hour because a figure that has come in the same twice in a row is not a thing a yard is the right place to find out about, and she said it before anybody asked her a question.

"**The word is crowded.**"

"**And I want the two halves of that on separate lines, because they do not agree with each other and I have not got time today to work out which one of them is telling the truth. The figure is the same as last month's. The word is not. Last month I said emptier and I meant it and I could not defend it in a yard, and this month the figure has not moved one penny and the word has moved a long way, and I am not going to stand in a room with eleven people in it and say which of those two I believe, and I intend to find out before I am able to.**"

Then she did the figure, and she did it twice, and the two agree, and the examiner went through the second one a third time with her finger on the paper after saying out loud in front of everybody that she keeps time and does not round.

"**Four hundred hundredweight short of the specification, and the valuation is four shillings the hundredweight, and that is the compost valuation and it is not anybody's day rate. Four hundred at four shillings is one thousand six hundred shillings. One thousand six hundred shillings is eighty pounds. That is the first time.**"

"**And the other way, because I have done sums one way since I was a girl and a figure that only goes one way is a figure I have not really done. Four shillings is forty-eight pence. Four hundred hundredweight at forty-eight pence is nineteen thousand two hundred pence. A pound is two hundred and forty pence. Nineteen thousand two hundred pence is two hundred and forty pence, eighty times over. Eighty is eighty pounds. That is the second time, and the two agree.**"

"**Eighty pounds, and it is the same eighty pounds as the ninth month, and I am going to say that out loud in a yard on Monday and I am not going to say it with any pleasure in it, because a woman who is pleased about a figure that has not moved is a woman who has stopped looking at the figure.**"

Then, before anybody left the room, the reader of this body asked the question this holding asks on the first of a month and has not had to ask since the fourth line went onto the standing list, which is whether anybody in that room wanted to change a word of it. It is a standing and not a courtesy. The first three lines of that list have been in the book since the first year and the fourth has been in it since the ninth of a month two months back, and on a first of a month anybody may stand up and say a word of it is wrong, and the word goes in or the standing is a different standing.

**Nobody in that room said a word, and the count of that is one, and the fen entered that the count is one and is not going to become two without somebody putting their hand up.**

Then the reader of this body entered the finding, and he entered it standing up, and about twenty-five people were in the yard and the clerk was one of them.

"**Two months in a row at the same figure is a coincidence until it is not one, and this holding has one month of evidence and is not going to call a pattern, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said one month of evidence and not two, and that a month of evidence is a month of evidence and not the start of a line.**"

"**And the other half, which is mine and not the book's. A figure that repeats is a worse kind of figure than a figure that moves. A figure that moves can be argued about. A figure that repeats can only be waited on, and a body that has got nothing to do but wait is a body that will do the next thing that comes up a road and call it a job.**"

Nobody said no to anybody in that yard that morning and nobody said yes. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, **and the count of that request is thirty-eight this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that a count of this kind is not a complaint and is not going to be a complaint at eight in the morning on a first of a month.**

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure: **the compost line for the tenth month is eighty pounds. The specification is four hundred hundredweight and the valuation is four shillings the hundredweight. The shortfall is four hundred hundredweight, which at that valuation is one thousand six hundred shillings, which is eighty pounds, and the figure was derived twice out loud in a room with about eleven people in it and the two derivations agree. The ninth month's figure was also eighty pounds and the two are entered on the same line, and the count of months in a run is ten and the run has not been discharged in ten.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the person who does the sums said the word crowded before she said the figure, in a room and not in a yard, and entered that the word and the figure do not agree with each other and that she does not know yet which of the two is telling the truth, and that she intends to find out before she is able to. Two months at the same figure is entered as a coincidence until it is not one. This holding has one month of evidence and is not calling a pattern.**

Then the cost, and the cost goes in separately and is not folded into the shortfall, and the reason is not the reason from last month and the fen entered that it is not.

"**Three days of a licensed man in the tenth month, the ninth, the twentieth and the twenty-fourth, at the rate in the hire book, which is a pound a day and has been a pound a day for two years. That is three pounds.**"

"**A woman of fifty-six at no fee, and the fen entered on the sixth that this holding could not have paid her and entered the count of the times it has been beaten home by somebody else's expense, and I am not going to price her at nothing and call that an entry. She came nine miles on her own money and there is no line in this book for what that cost and there is going to be no line.**"

"**The cart. And the bench in the cart shed, which the man of the north row with the cough put there a month ago and told nobody about, and which is now a thing four people have slept on and I am not going to put a rent on it in this entry.**"

"**And the reason the four go on four lines and are not added to the shortfall, and it is not last month's reason. A shortfall is a thing owed to a specification and a specification does not care what it cost to get within a hundred yards of it. If the costs go on the same line then in about four years somebody is going to read that line and see a smaller number than the truth, and the smaller number is going to be the one they quote.**"

"**And the last of it, and it is the half I have never had a column for. Every one of those four is a figure this holding can hand to an office, and I said that in a room a month ago and I am saying it again because a thing that has been said once is a thing that has been said, and a thing that has been said once has to be said again in a different room before it is a standing. I said it in the last month of a season and I am saying it in the first of a month and neither of those rooms is this one, and the figure is still in a drawer and it is still four pounds and fivepence.**"

> Entered on the same line: **the costs of the tenth month's work are entered separately and are not folded into the shortfall. Three licensed-man days at the rate in the hire book, being one pound a day, being three pounds. A woman of fifty-six at no fee, and the fen entered that this holding could not have paid her. The cart. The bench in a cart shed. The reason, in her own words, is that a shortfall is owed to a specification and a specification does not care what it cost to get near it, and that a book which puts the two kinds of thing in one column is a book that shows a smaller number than the truth in about four years.**

And then the fourth hour of the same morning, four hundred miles off, where nothing in this book is going to happen, and the fen entered the day.

**The next ordinary reading of the well on the shelf is the fourth hour on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and this holding is not in it, and this holding did not write and ask, and it entered the day and not the figure.** It is the third time this body has entered a day and not a figure for that shelf and the fen entered that the count is three and that a thing done three times in eleven years is a practice and not a habit, and entered that the difference between those two is a thing a body writes down and does not explain.

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the first of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a clock: **the next ordinary reading of a well on a shelf four hundred miles off is the fourth hour on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and is not in this book. The fen has no standing on it, did not write and ask and is not going to. The day and not the figure has been entered, and the day is the first of next month, and no figure has been entered against it and none is to be. The count of the times this body has entered a day and not a figure for that shelf is three, and the fen entered that three is a practice and not a habit.**

Then at the tenth hour of that morning the door at nine hundred yards off was read out whole in the full yard, by the steward, with the reader of this body on one side of her and the clerk on the other, and **the count of the readings of the seventh column goes to thirty-five**, and the next reading day in this month is the thirtieth, which is not the day in hand, and the fen entered that the door is read on the first and the thirtieth of a month and has been for four years, and that the three readings in the last month that were not on those two days were made on days when a man was standing at the door for another reason, and that this holding has not decided whether to call those reading days and is not going to decide it in a yard.

It is nine hundred yards from the gate and the whole of the yard had to stand in the cold for it, which is the custom and is the reason a reading day is the only day of a month that this holding puts in front of twenty-five people instead of nine, and the steward read it standing up with her finger on the line and did not skip a column and did not hurry the sixth line, and the sixth line is the one this holding has had the longest argument about.

The leak at three-quarters of an inch, no cause, no due date, **not measured since the twenty-first of a month two months back, and this body will not say whether the difference between that and the reading of the thirteenth of a month three months back is a movement or a season.** The beat not logged since, cause not established, nothing underneath it, column open, and the column was not opened in that yard of twenty-five and is not going to be. The boards at two hundred and thirty-five days, day minus sixty-six, and the March's tin at two hundred and eighty-two days, day minus nineteen, **both read out loud by the clerk before either number, which he has now done on seven reading days running and which nobody has asked for on any of the seven.** The standing rule, four lines, every one about a stop. The use log at fifteen lines, and the reason it stands at fifteen lines, which is that a line records a contact and never a reading and a draw gets no line. The rootmark four inches and branching twice and no second mark, and four accounts of that arm in this book none of which agrees with any of the others. A form on a bench with the answer not given. The pail gone and the measure back. And the well on the shelf at five feet, four feet against the sixteenth of a month six months back, not decomposed, and no entry anywhere for that well coming back.

**And then the standing rule was read out in full, four lines, before the door was closed, and about sixteen of the twenty-five had heard them before.**

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month five months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called, and there is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The fen entered that the count of the uses is one and that this month it goes to two on the thirteenth and not before, **and that the third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years, and that is entered, and the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop and this holding is not going to pretend that it does.**

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked. And so the eleventh month opened on a Saturday at about the eleventh hour on a figure that has now come in the same twice in a row, **and the word before the figure was crowded and does not agree with the figure, and the fen entered that it does not know which of the two is telling the truth and is not going to find out by asking anybody, and the whole of what this holding knows about the shape of a month is a specification, a valuation and a run of ten.**

# Chapter 179

## A Licensed Use

The second of this month was a Sunday, and this holding did almost nothing on it, and the fen entered that as a fact and not as a virtue.

What it did was load two carts in the yard, which are not this holding's carts and are not going anywhere near this holding's work, and about nine people were in the seed house with the bay doors shut and a woman of thirty-one going down a list against a sack with a thumb in it, and the reader of this body was not in that room and has not been asked what was said in it and did not write and ask.

The seed side of this holding goes on a road on Thursday and Friday and it is a working movement and not a departure, and the count of what goes and what stays is going to be made in a yard on the Friday and entered, and the man of about fifty said out loud on the Sunday that he would get his initial put against nothing on the Friday whether the count was a good one or not, and nobody thanked him for saying it and he did not do it for anybody.

The third of this month was a Monday, and at about the tenth hour of the morning the ordinary post came up the fen road in a leather bag with a carrier in it who went on up the road afterward and is not in this book's book, and about eleven people were in the yard when it was opened, and what was in the bag was four lines on one sheet in a clerk's square hand, and it was correct, and it is the frame this volume has been standing in for two months and had never before been handed to it in writing.

> Entered by the desk at the sub-office at Fennmere, on the ordinary post, on this day, four lines, at the foot of a printed sheet with a date under a heading, and the form is in order and nothing in it is a finding against any person:
>
> **One. A migration along a road is a licensed use of that road, with a rate and a class, and a licensed use is made under the schedule and is returned on the form provided.**
>
> **Two. The rate for a use under the first line is fourpence the hundredweight of goods carried, and the class of use is a class of migration and is assigned by the office and not by the party.**
>
> **Three. The parties to a use under the first line are to be identified. A body of households on one head is a party to a licensed use in the same way a holder of a well is a party, and a party has the standing of a party and not of the standing of a holder and not of the standing of an office.**
>
> **Four. This is made in the ordinary course. No part of it states that any person has done anything, and no part of it is a finding against any person, and a form not returned is a form outstanding and an outstanding form is a matter for the office and not for a third party.**

The person who keeps the seed house read it out in the yard with about eleven people in it, and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty, and he read it back out of the bag in the ordinary voice with the hour on it, and the clerk entered it, and nobody thanked anybody.

"**It is a form. It is four lines and it is correct and it names nothing and names everybody, and the third line is the one that is going to be on this holding's page in about four years, so I am going to say what it does before anybody can make it into a fight.**"

"**A migration along a road is a licensed thing. That is not an opinion. That is the same sentence, word for word, as the third line of the return of a month ago about a party that supplied water at no price into that quarter. A party is a party. It is not a holder and it is not an office. And a body which has now been a party twice in a month has stopped being a name and has started being a category, and I would like the clerk to enter that sentence in the words I said it in and not in better words.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute. The fen entered the minute and entered that it did not improve on it, and the man of the north row with the cough was at the back of that yard with his back against the wall and did not say anything, and the fen entered that too.

And then a woman of about sixty at the front of that yard, who has four pails and has walked four miles to a gate twice this season, asked the question that about nine people in that yard were holding and nobody had put out, and she asked it in the plainest words available to her and she asked it once.

"**Which road.**"

And the person who keeps the seed house answered it in one sentence, out loud, in the yard, before the reader of this body could answer it, and the fen entered that she answered it and entered that the reader of this body did not take it off her.

"**The road out of this district, south, the one the drivers are on, and the one you have seen the carts on, and it is not the fen road and it is not the road to the low quarter, and the office has not written one road on that sheet and that is not an excuse. It is a form and it is for any road that is a licensed road, and the road they are on is one.**"

Nobody said anything after that either, and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that a woman asked one question and got one sentence and did not ask a second one, **and entered that this holding has eleven people in a yard and one of the eleven asked the question the other ten were thinking, and that the person who answered it was not the engineer of record and was not the man whose book it is.**

Then the reader of this body said the frame out loud, standing up, before anybody could turn the form into a fight, and he gave the reason first, and about eleven people heard it.

"**An office is not a villain. I have said that in this book's history once, in a room of about four people, and I am saying it now in a yard of about eleven, and it is going to keep needing to be said every time a piece of paper comes up this road with a word on it that I do not like.**"

"**Nobody in that office has lied to this holding. Nobody in that office has obstructed a document, and nobody in that office has delayed anything, and I have had four pieces of paper out of it in two months and every one of them was correct and one of them told us something we did not want, and I have written the wanting down four times and I have not once written the office down. That is not because the office is good. That is because the office is not a person, and a person you can be angry at, and a structure you can only be angry inside of, and being angry inside a structure is how a body ends up doing nothing in a yard and calling it patience.**"

Then the fourth line, read out twice, in the ordinary voice, and the count of the two readings is entered.

"**No part of it states that any person has done anything. I want that in a yard of eleven people on a Monday morning and I want it in the book before anybody in this yard has finished being angry, because the sentence has come up a road before in a different form and every one of the four times it has cost this holding something and not once of the four was the office's fault.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the third of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a form: **a form came up the fen road on the ordinary post from the sub-office at Fennmere, four lines, correct, in the office's hand, and the fourth line states that no part of it is a finding against any person and that nothing in it states that any person has done anything. The fourth line was read out twice in a yard of about eleven people. The count of the things this holding has been told no by this season stands at six and nothing in that yard was a no and nothing in that yard was a yes, and the fen entered that a thing a form says that this body does not want is not a refusal and must not be counted as one.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the reader of this body said in the yard that an office is not a villain, and gave the reason, which is that nobody in that office has lied to this holding or obstructed a document or delayed anything, and that the office is not a person and a structure cannot be argued with from outside, and that being angry inside a structure is how a body ends up doing nothing in a yard and calling it patience. He said the sentence has been said once in this book's history, in a room of about four people, and that it is going to keep needing to be said.**

Then the sums, done in the yard because nobody would go back into the room, and the woman who does the sums put them on the back of the sheet the man of about fifty had read it out on, and she said the word before the figure the way she says it now, which is that she is going to put one number against another number and that neither of them is hers.

"**The rate on that form is fourpence the hundredweight. I have got a fourpence. I have had it since the nineteenth of a month two months back and I have never once been able to work out where it came from, because I put it in a line and I did not put the working in the line.**"

"**So here is the working, and I am doing it now in a yard because I am not going to be the only person in this book who finds out a figure a month late. One thousand and forty-five hundredweight went up that road over eleven days at a shilling and fourpence, which is sixteen pence, and the quarter paid fifty-two pounds five shillings. Sixteen pence on one thousand and forty-five is sixteen thousand seven hundred and twenty pence, which is one thousand three hundred and ninety-three shillings and fourpence, which is sixty-nine pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence. Sixty-nine pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence less fifty-two pounds five shillings is seventeen pounds eight shillings and fourpence.**"

"**And the other way, which is a shilling and a fourpence and not a sixteen. A shilling a hundredweight on one thousand and forty-five is twelve thousand five hundred and forty pence. Seventeen pounds eight shillings and fourpence is four thousand one hundred and eighty pence. Twelve thousand five hundred and forty and four thousand one hundred and eighty is sixteen thousand seven hundred and twenty. Same. And four thousand one hundred and eighty pence on one thousand and forty-five hundredweight is four pence, and I have got that division twice, once forward and once back, and the two agree.**"

"**And now the half of it that I am not going to be glad about. That is a rate I derived, out of two numbers this holding took itself, and the form says the same rate, and that is the first time in two months that a number on a piece of paper has agreed with a number I worked out myself, and I am not glad about it, and I would like the clerk to enter why, and it is this: I have spent two months arguing that a figure you can check is worth more than a figure you cannot, and this week a figure I could check came down a road in somebody else's hand and told me nothing I did not already have.**"

Nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be thanked. The reader of this body did not improve on it and was not asked to.

"**Fourpence the hundredweight is the price of eleven miles, and it is in nobody's pocket, and it is the same fourpence whether the water in the cart or the seed in the cart, and that is the sentence this form says in the only words it uses. It does not say anybody is doing anything. It says a road has a price and a class and a schedule, and that a person walking on it is a party to it whether they have a cart or not.**"

Then the last of that Monday, and the reader of this body said it in the yard with the sheet still on the wall where the reader of it had left it, and he said the reason first.

"**We cannot name a single household in that migration. I have said that this holding cannot name a single party to a licensed supply in that quarter and I have said the reason, and the reason has not changed, and there are four hundred and forty-one people in that quarter and this body does not know which of them bought a pail and which of them was given one.**"

"**And I am not going to start naming households on a road. The difference between not knowing and refusing has been the whole argument of this book for a year and it is not going to be collapsed a second time because a piece of paper came in the post, and the third line of this form is not going to be the thing that does it, and I would like the clerk to enter that the sentence in the third line is the same sentence as the fourth line of a return a month ago and that I said so before anybody could turn it into a fight.**"

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the third of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a limit: **a form of a licensed use of a road has been entered and the fen entered that this holding cannot name a single household in a migration and will not try, and entered the reason, which is that there are four hundred and forty-one people in the low quarter and this body does not know which of them bought a pail and which of them was given one, and that the difference between not knowing and refusing has been the whole argument of this book for a year and is not going to be collapsed a second time because a piece of paper came in the post.**

The form went up on the wall of the yard at about the twelfth hour of the noon and it stayed up, **and this holding is a category, and it has known that since about the fourth hour of that Monday morning, and nobody was thanked, and the count of the things it has been told no by stands at six and did not move, and the arithmetic came out at fourpence twice and the woman who did it said out loud that she was not glad about that.**

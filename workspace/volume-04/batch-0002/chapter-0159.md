# Chapter 159

## The First Of The Tenth

The first of the tenth month was a Thursday, and the ninth month's compost line fell on it at about the eleventh hour in a yard with about twenty people in it, and the two of those facts are on the same day and this holding is not going to make anything of them.

It has said out loud, in a yard, on five separate days in the ninth month, that it does not know what the figure is going to be. It said it on the eleventh with about eleven people there, and on the sixteenth in a full yard of about twenty-five, and on the twenty-first in the same yard, and on the twenty-ninth with about eleven, and on the thirtieth in a full yard of about twenty-five. The count of the five is entered and it is not a complaint, and the figure arrived this morning in a hand that is not the hand that book has been written in for eleven years, and it was no worse than the month before it, and the person who does the sums said the word for what it actually was before she said the number.

Gael Senn read the whole of it out loud, the figures first and then the words around them, which is her practice, and the reader appointed for it was the man of about fifty and not the steward, and the reason for that is four months old and is hers. Dunmar Fitch entered the hour and read the entry back before he initialled it. Nobody thanked her.

> Return main, the ninth month. Entered on the first of the tenth month by the examiner, in the steward's book, in her own hand, which is not the steward's hand. Nothing put in. None available. **Specification four hundred hundredweight.**
>
> **Against the specification, the going: nothing.** No journey is charged to this month. **The reason is entered, and the reason is that this holding hired nobody in the ninth month and paid off nobody hired in it, and the last man hired in the eighth month was paid off inside that month.** The examiner stated that she has been in the hiring book twice and that she did not believe herself the first time.
>
> **Shortfall four hundred hundredweight, eighty pounds, at four shillings the hundredweight, which is the compost valuation and is not anybody's day rate.** Carried forward and not discharged.
>
> **The run of it is nine months and it has not been discharged in nine.**
>
> And against the same line, separately, in the same hand, and entered as three things and not as part of the shortfall, because they are not a shortfall of compost and they are the cost of doing the work and **a book that puts the two in one column is a book that cannot be read in four years:**
>
> **One. A balance of twenty-eight shillings not discharged, against a man hired on the fourth of a month two months back. His pay-off day was never entered against him, and the fen has entered that it was not and is not going to invent one.** The person who keeps the money entered on the same line that she has carried that balance for two months and has not moved it and is not going to fold it into a shortfall of compost to make a line smaller.
>
> **Two. Three appointments of a licensed man at the fourth hour, and one at the eighth hour of the same day, being the twenty-first, the twenty-second and the twenty-eighth, and the four hours of the twenty-eighth are the four hours this holding gave away on the twenty-sixth with no promise and a time.**
>
> **Three. A wash of a pail on the Saturday morning, entered because the last thing that pail carried was a man's fever, and a man of seventy-one brought out for the twelfth time in a season, who said three words and is not to be explained.**

Anneke Roos said the word before the figure, and she has done that three times in this book and this is the fourth.

"**It is emptier. I am saying that before the number, and I am saying it without any pleasure in it whatever, and I would like the clerk to enter the second half of that sentence as well as the first, because the second half is the reason the first one is the word.**"

"**It is the first month in nine that has not gone up. And it has not gone up because there was nothing in it to charge, and a book that lets anybody find that out off a slate is a book that gets read the wrong way in four years, and this book has been read the right way by about nine people in eleven years and I am not going to be the tenth thing that gets it wrong.**"

"**The specification is four hundred hundredweight and it has been four hundred since the flood and it is not going to be four hundred and fifty because a man is expensive. The going is nothing. No journeys, no hired man, no six days of a woman's keep over a fortnight, no second blanket. That is what the word emptier means and I want it in the book in my own words because in four years somebody is going to see eighty pounds and think this body had a good month, and this body has not had a good month. This body had nothing to do.**"

Then she did the two sums, out loud, in the ordinary way, and she did not average them and she did not put them in one column.

"**First sum, at the valuation, which is what goes on the line. Four hundred hundredweight at four shillings is one thousand six hundred shillings, which is eighty pounds. That is the figure on the sheet and the sheet is right.**"

"**Second sum, at the price at a counting house nine hundred miles off, which is three shillings and elevenpence the hundredweight, being forty-seven pence. Four hundred hundredweight at forty-seven pence is eighteen thousand eight hundred pence. Eighteen thousand eight hundred pence is one thousand five hundred and sixty-six shillings and eightpence, which is seventy-eight pounds, six shillings and eightpence.**"

"**The difference between those two numbers is one pound, thirteen shillings and fourpence, and the whole of that difference is a penny that came down by itself on a sheet with a firm's name at the head of it on the first of last month, and this holding did not cause that and did not write and ask about it, and that is eight months of a run and this is the ninth.**"

"And here is the sentence I have been getting to since I read the sheet this morning. **A body that puts that penny on the same line as a hundredweight specification is a book that has stopped being a book.** One of those two numbers is a thing somebody did and the other one is a thing that happened to a firm nine hundred miles off, and they are not the same kind of number, and the only thing that joins them is that a woman standing in a yard multiplied one by the other. I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be asked to be and I have said that before I was thanked."

Nobody thanked her. About twenty people were in that yard and the reader read it back with the hour on it and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and the count of that request is twenty this season.

Then the reader of this body stood up and did the thing he does, which is put the whole of a month on a page in front of the people who are standing in it, and he did it from the sheet and not from memory, and the numbers went the way they are on the sheet.

"**The run, in the order it came, and the two numbers either side of each difference, because the person who keeps the money has been saying that for two years and I am not going to be the one who breaks it. The sixth month of the year was ninety pounds eight. The seventh was eighty pounds eight. The eighth was ninety-five pounds. This is eighty pounds. The eighth to this is a fall of fifteen pounds, and I am not going to stand in a yard and let anybody hear that as good news, and I would like it read back that way, because it went up by fourteen pounds twelve shillings a month ago and this is the same line and not a different one.**"

"And the cause is the second figure in the entry, which is **nothing**, and the going on this line does not go up when people are hired and down when they are not, and this holding did not have a good month and did not have a bad one. **We had a month in which the compost we did not have was the same as it always is, and I would like somebody in four years to find that sentence and decide whether it is a comfort.**"

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the first of the tenth month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the money: **the ninth month's compost line fell at about the eleventh hour, in a yard of about twenty, in a hand that is not the steward's. The specification is four hundred hundredweight. The going is nothing and the reason is entered. The shortfall is four hundred hundredweight, eighty pounds, at four shillings the hundredweight. The run of it is nine months and it has not been discharged in nine.** The person who keeps the money said before the figure that the month was emptier and not better, **and stated that the price at the counting house is three shillings and elevenpence the hundredweight, being forty-seven pence, on which four hundred hundredweight is seventy-eight pounds, six shillings and eightpence, and that the difference of one pound, thirteen shillings and fourpence is a penny that came down on a sheet with a firm's name at the head of it on the first of last month, and that a body that puts that penny on the same line as a hundredweight specification is a book that has stopped being a book.** The reader of this body read the run aloud from the sheet and entered that a fall of fifteen pounds from the month before is not good news and is the same line.

---

And then the yard emptied down to about nine and did not go inside, because it was a Thursday in the first of a month and there was nothing else in it, and the reader of this body said a date out loud.

He said it in the ordinary voice, standing at the wall side of the gate, and about twenty people were still in that yard and heard it, and he did not say whose it was and he did not say what it was about.

"**The eleventh.**"

Gael Senn said the next thing before anybody had written anything down, which is her practice and which the fen has entered four times in a season.

"**I keep time and I do not round and I say so in front of whoever is standing here. From the first of a month to the eleventh of that month is ten days and it is not three, and whoever said a number just now said three.**"

The reader of this body said yes.

"**Then enter both figures and do not improve on them. I said three and she said ten and I have not got a reason for the three and I am not going to make one up in a yard, and the reason I said it out loud at all is that it is a date in this book's book with nothing against it and it is three days off the day I said three and ten days off the day she said ten, and either way it is soon, and there is nobody in this yard who can be asked what it is about because I am not going to ask them and because two of the people I would ask are nine miles off and one of them is asleep at this hour.**"

Nobody answered him. Nobody was asked to answer him, and the fen entered that nobody was asked and entered that the man of about thirty-five is not in this yard and has not been since the twenty-fifth of last month and has said he will not be asked again by this body.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the first of the tenth month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a date: **a date was said out loud in a yard of about twenty people by the reader of this body. He said three days. The examiner said ten days, and said before anything was written down that she keeps time and does not round, and he agreed with her. Both figures are entered and neither has been improved on.** He did not say whose the date is and did not say what it is about. **He did not ask the engineer of record what it is about and did not ask anybody else, and the fen entered that he did not ask, and entered the reason, which is that it is his standing and has never once cost anybody anything.**
>
> **A date with nothing against it is in this book's book. It was said out loud in a yard of about twenty people on the first of the tenth month and about twenty people heard it and nobody took it, and the count of the questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is now six, the fifth having been put in a yard of about twenty-five on the thirtieth of last month, and the count is entered and it is not a complaint.**

And then the man of about seventy was not fetched and was not sent for and was in that yard at the wall, and the fen entered that it did not fetch him, that this body has brought him out for a thing twelve times in a season, and that the twelfth was on the twenty-eighth of last month, and that today was not a bringing out at all, because the first of a month is read in a yard and he is one of the twenty. He was given the two sums, the two figures and the difference, in that order, and **he said nothing, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be**, and the fen entered that he said nothing and entered that it did not ask him whether he had anything to say, which is his standing and which has never once cost anybody anything.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the first of the tenth month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **a man of seventy-one stood at the wall side of a yard on the first of a month and was not fetched, and was given the two sums, the two figures and the difference, and said nothing.** He was not thanked and did not ask to be. **The reader of this body did not ask him whether he had anything to say, and the fen entered that he did not, and entered the reason, which is that it is his standing and has never once cost anybody anything, and entered that a man who is brought out twelve times in a season and says nothing on the thirteenth occasion has still been brought out twelve times.**

And so the first of the tenth month closed a line that has been open for nine months on a figure that is the smallest in the run, and a woman who does the sums has said out loud that it is emptier and not better, and a date went out into a yard of about twenty people and came back with nobody holding it, and this holding is now a body that has a name, a book, a rule of four lines, five things it is looking after and cannot reach, a form it cannot fill, a woman it has not paid, and a date in a month ahead of it that it cannot defend and has now said out loud, and **the whole of what it has against any of that is that it wrote down that it did not know, and the only one of those things it can still keep is the leak, because the leak is three-quarters of an inch and has no verdict on it and nobody has asked it for one.**

# Chapter 158

## The Thirtieth

The hour on the twenty-eighth of this month has run. A licensed man was at the fourth hour and at the eighth, as he said he would be, and he wrote both down in his own hand with the hour on it, and he did not write down what happened between them and has not been asked to and is not going to be. What was on the boards at the eighth hour was the measure. The pail was not on the boards at the eighth hour and was not on the boards this morning. And no person was with either of them.

That is the whole of it and it is entered first and in the plainest words this book has, because it is a fact about a floor and about a yard and not about a man, and because a month that closed on anything else would be a month that has been tidied, and this one has not been tidied.

Coll Renner said it at about the eighth hour on the twenty-ninth, which was a Tuesday, at the north gate, in a yard of about eleven, standing up, and he gave the hour before the finding and the finding before anything else, and about eleven people heard all of it.

"**I was there at the fourth hour and I was there at the eighth hour and I have written both down. Between the two of them there was a measure on the boards and a plain wire gauge and me, and I did not see the pail at either hour and I am not going to say that I went looking for it at the eighth because I did not go looking for it, and I am not going to say I did not go looking for it either, because I have not got a column for that and neither has anybody else.**"

"**So: the measure came back. The pail did not. And nobody came with either, which I have put in the same line as the two of them because it is the same fact, and because if you put it in a different line you are saying a person was expected.**"

Nobody answered him. The reader of this body asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is nineteen this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the ninth hour of the morning of the twenty-ninth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of an hour this holding gave away: **a licensed man was at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth and at the eighth hour and wrote both down in his own hand with the hour on it. A measure and a plain wire gauge were on the boards at the eighth hour. The pail was not there at the eighth hour and has not been. No person was with either.** The pail was given away on the twenty-sixth with no promise and a time, and **this holding entered on the twenty-sixth that the pail may not come back and has not been given a promise about it and is not going to be given one now that it has not come back.**

Then the reader of this body stood up and said the half of it that nobody else in that yard was going to say, and he said the reason for saying it before he said it, which is his practice, and about eleven people were in that yard and one of them was a woman of a household in the low quarter who had come up the fen road on the morning of the twenty-fourth and had not been announced because she is in the book and nobody has sent her home.

"**Nobody in this yard can say what the hour was for and nobody is going to find out, and I want that entered in that order, because the two halves of that sentence are not the same fact and one of them is a decision.**"

"**The first half is this. A man of about thirty-five asked this holding for three things on the twenty-fourth, a pail, a measure and an hour, and he said the price of them out loud before anybody asked him for it. This holding gave him the three things on the twenty-sixth in a yard of about thirty and has not been told what the hour was for, and the man who would tell us has said in a room of about nine people that he is not going to be asked again by this body, and that if he is asked again by a person in an office he will say no in one word.**"

"**And the second half is this, and I would like the clerk to enter it as a decision and not as a difficulty. The fen has decided this morning not to ask him and not to write to anybody about it and not to have it asked in a yard by anybody else, and this body has no standing to write to anybody about it in any case. The first half is that we do not know. Those have been two different things in this book for two volumes and I am not going to have them run together on the last day of a month.**"

---

And then the thirtieth came, which was a Wednesday and the last day of the ninth month, and the door was read out whole at the tenth hour in a full yard of about twenty-five, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-two, and none of the figures was softened.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the thirtieth of this month, in a full yard of about twenty-five. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month three months back, the fifth read twice. **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-two.**
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Thursday, and this holding does not know what it is.** That is the ninth month of a run of eight that has not been discharged in eight, and this holding has said that figure out loud on the eleventh, the sixteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth of this month, and the count of the five is entered and is not a complaint.
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit. The reading of the thirteenth of a month two months back is five-eighths. **This body will not say whether the difference is a movement or a season.** No cause has been looked for and none is entered, no due date is invented, and there is none, and it has not been measured since the twenty-first and is not to be measured again this month.
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing written underneath it. **Column open.** The rule's third line does not say in a yard, **this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule was used outside one of its own four lines on the twenty-second, and that is entered.**
>
> **The boards. Two hundred and four days.** Day minus sixty-six, the two hundred and seventieth day of this season less sixty-six, cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years and said out loud in this yard.
>
> **The March's tin. Two hundred and fifty-one days.** Day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**

The reader of this body made the clerk read the day-minus rule out loud before he read either number, which he has now done on four reading days running and which nobody has asked for and which he says he is going to keep doing, and the steward read it because it is her book and it is her rule and she has let him have it for four months and the fen entered that she has let him have it and entered that nobody asked her to.

And then the standing rule about the engineer of record was read out again, in full, four lines, in the same yard, before the door was closed, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month three months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called, and there is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The rule was used once, on the twenty-second of this month, and the rule permitted it, and it was used with no yard called and outside one of its own four lines, and **the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop. No draw has been attempted since and none is proposed. The rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice, and it is not longer this morning than it was on the eleventh of this month, and there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow on the twenty-second is not a mark.**

Then the reader of this body asked the question that was available in that yard, and he said the reason for asking it first, and about twenty-five people were in that yard and the gate was open behind them.

"**Was the hour.**"

He stood there after he said it and nobody took it.

"**I am going to say why I am asking and then I am going to stand here. There were about thirty people in this yard on the twenty-sixth when the three things were read out, and about nine in it yesterday when the licensed man told us the measure came back and the pail did not. In yards of that size there was a person who had been on the ground that hour or had been under it or had spoken to somebody who had. I do not know who they were. That is not a complaint and it is not an accusation and it is not about any of those people.**"

"**It is about this body. This holding has put three things out of its yard and got half of one of them back and it has no standing to ask after the other half and has not tried, and that is correct, and this morning I asked the question in a full yard in the ordinary voice because that is what the question is for, and nobody took it, and I am not going to ask it twice.**"

"**Enter the count of the questions that have been available to a room in this holding and not taken. It is five. The first was in a yard in a month two months back and the fourth was in a low ground on the twenty-third of this month, and this is the fifth and it is the first one I have asked out loud.**"

> Entered at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the thirtieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **a question was asked out loud in a yard of about twenty-five and was not answered and no person in that yard was asked to answer it. The question was: was the hour. The count of the questions available to a room in this holding and not taken is five, and the first was in a month two months back and the fifth is this, and the reader of this body stated that he is not going to ask it twice and entered the reason, which is that he has no standing to ask after it and that the asking is the whole of the asking.**

And then the steward asked who loses, and it is her question and she has asked it three times in a season, and the answer was the third this holding has given that it could not finish, and the difference between it and the other two is on the page and nobody improved on it.

Lissa Vale said it standing, with the door shut behind her and about nine people still in the seed house out of the twenty-five.

"**The first time I was asked that in this book I could not tell them what four carts cost anybody. That was on the seventh of last month. The second time I could not tell them what four hours cost, and that was on the twenty-sixth, and I said the words *we cannot tell* and I meant them.**"

"**This is the third and it is a different thing and I have been three days getting to the difference, and here it is. The first was about a thing this holding had not decided to do. The second was about a thing this holding had decided to do and had done. This one is about a thing this holding cannot ask anybody about, and I want that in the book in those words, because it is a third kind and this book has only had two and both of them were about a decision of ours.**"
"**And I am going to say the last of it and it is the whole of what I have. A body that has been refused stands where it stood, and it knows what it wanted and it has been told the price of it, and it can go and do the work again. **A body that has given away a thing and got half of it back is not in the same position as a body that was refused**, because it has had a hand put in it and the hand is holding nothing and the body cannot get the hand back by asking. There is no sentence for that in this book's vocabulary and I have looked for one for three days and I am not going to invent one in a yard and be asked in four years what I meant by it.**"

"**So enter that I could not finish it a third time and enter the reason, and enter that the reason is a different reason from the other two, and enter that I am not going to try again until somebody in this yard gives me a word for it that is not mine.**"

Nobody answered her. Nobody was asked to answer her, and the fen entered that nobody was asked and that the person who keeps the seed house was not thanked and did not ask to be.

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the thirtieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a cost: **the third answer this holding has given to the question of who loses, and the third time it could not finish it. The first was the four carts, the second was the four hours, and the third is a thing this holding cannot ask anybody about. The person who keeps the seed house stated the difference between the third and the other two in her own words, and stated that a body that has given away a thing and got half of it back is not in the same position as a body that was refused, and that there is no sentence for that in this book's vocabulary and that she is not going to invent one.**

And so the ninth month of this holding's book closed at about the second hour of the afternoon of the thirtieth on a pail that is gone, a measure that came back, four hours of a licensed man's time that went out with no promise attached to them, a form returned from the sub-office on the eighteenth of this month that asked this body to name the parties to a licensed supply in a quarter of four hundred and forty-one people and that has not been returned and cannot be, **a name at the foot of a sheet for a holder of a well eleven miles off with no address on the sheet**, a beat with its column open, a leak at three-quarters of an inch with nothing said about whether it has moved, and a woman of a household in the low quarter sitting at the wall side of a yard who has been in this book three times now and has not been asked anything today. And the only protection this holding has against any of it is that it gave a promise away without giving a promise, and the fen entered that on the twenty-sixth, and the fen entered this morning that a promise that was not given cannot be broken and cannot be called, and that this is the whole of what it has, and that **the protection is thin, and that a book which has nothing but that in it is a book about a body that has been careful.**

# Chapter 161

## She Came On The Fourth

The fourth of this month was a Sunday, and at about the second hour of the afternoon a woman of fifty-six came up the fen road in the rain on foot and stood at the gate, and she had not been announced, and the reason she was not announced is that she is already in this book's book.

That is the whole of the first fact and the rest of it took an hour. She was not announced because there is nobody in this fen who does not know that her name is on a certificate in a room in this building as the first of three, and because a body that announces at its own gate a person who is already on a page in its own book has admitted that it does not know its own book, and this body has never once been able to be accused of that.

She was let in at the gate by the young man of twenty with the dog, who said nothing to anybody about her and was not asked to and told nobody, and the fen entered that he told nobody and entered that nobody asked him and entered that he was right not to have said anything, which was a thing he could not have known at the gate and worked out in a yard about an hour later.

She was wet through to the shoulder and had nine miles of road behind her and the fen entered that as a fact and did not ask her about it. She had come by the ordinary post to Fennmere, which is nine miles, and walked the last nine, and **four days down the plateau road is a different woman entirely and the fen entered that it has not seen the woman who came down that road and is not going to be told about her by anybody.**

She gave her name and her position in the first minute, in a form that could be read back out loud without being improved on, and she gave it standing up, in the yard, to about nine people.

"**Sera Quill. I am a root engineer. I am retired. I have held no licence for nineteen years and I hold none now. I am the first of the three names on the certificate in the room behind you, the second is the examiner and the third is your engineer of record, and none of the three of us put the other two there.**"

"**I have come on my own money and I am not charging this body a fee, and I am saying that first and not later, and I would like the clerk to enter the reason I am saying it first and the reason is this: a fee is the only thing a person in my position can charge, and I have not charged it, and I would like that written down in the order I said it in.**"

Then she said the third thing, and it is the thing she came for, and she said it in about six sentences without stopping.

"**And the reason I did not charge it is that I have read that a class of your records has been held to be unfit for evidentiary use, and I know what happens to a body after that, and it is not what an office does to it.** An office writes a line and goes and does the next thing. A body of sixteen households on one head reads a line like that and thinks about the next line, and about four months later it has a second book. Not a copy. A second book, in an engineer's own hand, with no reading day on it and no hour on it, kept in a tool case, **and that is the whole of what I know about what you are about to do and I have come nine miles to say it in a yard.**"

Nobody thanked her. She said before she was thanked that she was not going to be thanked, and the fen entered that and entered the count, which is twelve in a season.

> Entered at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a person received: **a retired root engineer of fifty-six, the first of the three names on the certificate in this building, came up the fen road on foot in the rain on the fourth of this month at about the second hour of the afternoon and was let in at the gate and was not announced, the reason being that she is already in this book's book.** She stated her name and her position in the first minute in a form that could be read back. **She stated, before anybody offered it, that no fee is charged. The fen entered that it has not paid her, that it cannot pay her, and that it would not have been able to, and entered that this is the reason she came, and entered the reason in her own words and did not improve it.** Nobody thanked her. She said before she was thanked that she was not going to be thanked, and the count of that standing in a season is twelve.

And then the reader of this body asked her the question that nobody else in that yard could ask, and he gave the reason for asking it before he asked it, and about nine people were in the seed house and the clerk was one of the nine.

"**I am going to ask you one question and I am going to say why I am asking it first, and then I am not going to ask you anything else today.**"

"**There are two things in this building you could have come about and I want to know which one it is before anybody in this room decides how to treat you. One of them is a class of record. The other one is a name at the foot of a sheet for a holder of a well eleven miles off.**"

"Are you here because of a declaration or because of a name."

"**Neither.** And I am going to say the third thing and then I am going to sit down, because I have walked nine miles and I am fifty-six and I am not going to be able to do this standing up twice."

"**A body that has been told its record cannot be used is a body that will start keeping another kind. The other kind does not have a reading day. It does not have an hour on it. It is kept in a man's own book and it is the only one of the three that anybody in the yard actually reads.**"

"**I have watched two bodies do that in nineteen years. One of them was a good body. The other one is the reason I am standing in yours.**"

And then she said the thing that is on the page for the rest of this book, and she said it sitting down, and she said it to nine people.

"**I am not going to be forgiven and I have not come to be. I have not come to be thanked and I said so before I was thanked. I have not come to be given a room or a bed or a figure, and I have come on my own money, and if anybody in this yard wants to put something in a book about the fact that I came at my own expense then put in that it was at my own expense and not that it was given.**"

Nobody answered that. Nobody was asked to answer it, and the fen entered that nobody was asked and entered that she was not thanked and was not forgiven and that the count of the first of those two is not a figure this holding keeps.

---

And then at about the ninth hour of the evening the steward said one sentence at the door of the seed house, and it is the whole of what happened between the two women that day, and it is one sentence and it is correct.

Lissa Vale had the door in her hand and about nine people were still in the room and the retired root engineer was standing up with her wet coat over her arm.

"**A person who is not of this body does not sleep in the seed house.**"

And she gave the reason in the same breath, and the reason was not suspicion, and the fen entered the reason as the reason.

"**And I want the clerk to enter why, so that nobody in four years reads it as the other thing. It is not that I do not trust you. It is that a person who is not of this body does not sleep in the seed house, and if I made an exception for you this week then I have made an exception, and the next one is easier, and the one after that is a custom, and a seed house is not a place to put a custom in. I would have given the same sentence this evening to a licensed man off an office who had come up the fen road in the rain with a form in his bag.**"

She accepted it in about four seconds.

That is a figure and the fen counted it, because the fen counts everything, and four seconds is what it took, and **she said nothing at all about it, and she did not say one word about it for the rest of the evening and the next day and the day after that, and the fen entered that she said nothing about it and entered that the fen did not ask her whether she minded and did not write and ask.**

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **a person who is not of this body was refused the use of the seed house for a night, in one sentence, by the person who keeps it.** The reason was entered by the person who keeps it and was not suspicion, **and was that an exception made this week is an exception made, and the next one is easier, and a seed house is not a place to put a custom in, and the same sentence would have been given to a licensed man off an office who had come up the fen road in the rain with a form in his bag.** The person refused accepted it in about four seconds and said nothing about it for the rest of the evening and was not thanked and did not ask to be. **The instrument was not asked for and not refused. The fieldbook was not asked for by anybody and did not come down four hundred miles of road and is not coming down that road.**
>
> **Entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand: the count of times a refusal in this building has been given in one sentence for a reason that is not suspicion is one, and the count of times it has been given for a reason that is suspicion is one, and the two are not to be added together and are not to be read as one kind of thing.**

And the man of the north row with the cough put a bench in the cart shed and a lamp on it and told nobody he had done it, and the fen entered that he did it and entered that nobody thanked him for it and did not ask.

And then, at about the eleventh hour of the same night, the reader of this body was not in the room, and Lissa Vale said one thing to a clerk who was writing, and the clerk wrote it down and read it back and did not improve it.

"**On the twenty-seventh of last month he stood in a yard of about nine and said that a man who cannot defend a thing and holds it anyway has not kept a private record, he has started one, and that a body with a name can hold a thing it cannot defend, and that is how a name becomes a party.**"

"**And on the fourth of this month a woman walked nine miles in the rain to tell us what happens to a body after a line goes into a book, and she did not know he had said it, and he was not in the room when she was asked to say her piece first, and the two of them have not compared notes and are not going to in this building as far as I am able to stand in a yard and stop it.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the evening of the fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **a person came nine miles on her own money and was refused a bed in one sentence, and the reason she came is the same shape of thing as a thing the reader of this body said out loud in a yard of about nine on the twenty-seventh of last month, and the two have not compared notes and have not been told of each other, and the fen entered that it did not tell one of them about the other and is not going to.**

And then the fifth of this month came, which was a Monday, and the standing figures of the day were read out at the gate in the ordinary way with a stranger in the yard, and the stranger did the thing that strangers do, which is that she stood at the wall side and asked for the sheet.

That is entered because it is a fact about a yard and it is the first time in eleven years that a person has stood at that wall and asked for a page.

"**May I have the page.**"

Lissa Vale gave her the page. She gave it to her without asking the reader and without asking the clerk and without a word, and the reader of this body entered that afterwards and entered that he had not been asked and had not offered and that the person who keeps the seed house did not consult him about a page in her own book in her own building in front of nine people.

"**That is my whole trade and I have not done it in nineteen years and I am not going to start pretending I have.** Three people in a yard this month have said a number out loud. Four households at the bottom of that road sent up a bill on a miller's tally and counted pails in turns at a gate. A miller nine miles off writes on a board every milling day and has done for thirty-one years and sent us three figures. **Four sources, four hands, and not one of them is a clerk, and I have watched this holding be proud of that for eleven years and it is the single most useful thing I have ever seen a body do.**"

"**And the sheet that came on the eighteenth of last month is the first one in three years that anybody has put in front of this holding that is in a different hand, and it is the one that has cost this holding the most, and I have got nothing against it and I am not going to be thanked for saying that, and I have said that before I was thanked.**"

The reader of this body did not say anything to that and the fen entered that he did not and entered that he has not said a word in that yard about a page given away by the person who keeps the book, **and the fen entered that he has not written and asked why, and entered the reason, which is that it is her book and her page and that a man who has been handed a page he did not ask for does not get to say what was on it.**

And so the second day of this movement of this book had in it a woman in a wet coat at a gate, a fee that was not charged, a class of record named in a yard by the person it concerns, a bench in a cart shed put there by a man nobody thanked, and one sentence at a door, **and the whole of what the fen got for the nine miles was the professional reason for a thing the reader of this body had already said out loud in front of nine people a week before, and neither of them knew the other had said it, and the fen entered that it is not going to tell them.**

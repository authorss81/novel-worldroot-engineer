# Chapter 167

## Entered, In Her Own Name

The fourteenth of this month was a Wednesday, and at about the second hour of the afternoon the longest single entry in the history of this holding's book was read out whole in the seed house with the bay doors shut, in a room with about eleven people in it, in the words it is written in, and it is four lines long, and the reader of this body read it back out of the book twice and did not ask one question about it.

> Entered on the sixth of this month at the tenth hour of the morning, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a statement, in the words of the person who made it:
>
> **I designed a safety relay for a flow in a dry country nineteen years ago. The relay could have isolated a pulse that was going out where it should not have gone. I took the red line out of it. I changed the log entry from what it was to a line that said routine recalibration. I signed a report that put the cause down to salt creep and weather. The pulse killed four thousand two hundred people in the first winter and moved nine thousand more. The place was called ______________________ and I said that name once, in this room, on the sixth, and I am not going to say it again.**
>
> **Sera Quill. I have asked to be on this page as the person who said it. I have not asked for anything else and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be forgiven for it, because it is not that kind of day.**

**There is a blank in the second line of that entry and it has been in the book since the sixth of this month and the clerk put it there on the sixth and has not filled it in since, and about eleven people in that room read four lines with a blank in the middle of one of them and nobody asked about the blank, and the fen entered that nobody asked about it and entered the reason, which is that the woman who wrote that line asked for the name to be said once and said it once and this holding does not put a name on a page twice.**

And then he said why he had not asked, and he said it before he read it back, and about eleven people were in that room.

"**I did not ask one question about that page and I am going to say why in this room and not in the book, and then I am going to read it back twice.** A thing in this book that a person said out loud in a room is not evidence. It is not going to become evidence by being asked about in a yard. **And the asking is for the asker and not for the page, and I have wanted to ask one question about that page every day for eight days and I have not asked it, and the question is not about her, it is about what a body does with a word like that in it.**"

"**So I read it back twice and I asked nothing and the clerk entered that I asked nothing.**"

> Entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: **the reader read the entry back twice and asked no question about it, and stated the reason, which is that a thing in this book that a person said out loud in a room is not evidence and is not going to become evidence by being asked about in a yard, and that the asking is for the asker and not for the page. The fen entered that the reader had wanted to ask one question every day for eight days and had not asked it and entered that the question is not about the person who made the statement.**

And then the fen entered the cost, and it entered it as a cost and not as a sacrifice, and about eleven people were in that room and none of them said anything while it was read.

> Entered on the same line: **this holding has no charter, a standing list of four lines, five things it is looking after and cannot reach, four pounds and fivepence, a pail it has lost, a form on a bench with the answer not given, a party named on a return it cannot check, and a woman's name against a thing nobody in this district can corroborate. The request has been granted and the grant is entered, and the cost of the grant is this holding's own exposure, and it is not hers, and it went in this holding's book and not in hers.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's own words, which he said twice and did not improve: this holding has been a name for a month and has now entered a woman's name against a thing it cannot check, and has made itself a party. On the eleventh of last month a man of about fifty said in a yard that a name is a thing other people can spend. On the twenty-seventh of last month the same man said in a yard that a body with a name can hold a thing it cannot defend and that this is how a name becomes a party. He did not think about that for a day at the time and he has thought about it for two days since and is not going to improve on it in a room with about eleven people in it.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand, read back and not improved: she is on this page once. She did not ask to be on it. The fen entered that she did not ask to be on it and entered that she was not asked whether she wanted it and would have accepted it either way, and the clerk entered that and read it back and did not improve on it.**

And then Dunmar Fitch entered the one observation he is allowed and nobody explained it and nobody asked.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the fourteenth of this month, by the clerk, on his own motion, unasked: **the book cannot check her.**

---

And on the fifteenth, which was a Thursday, the figures of the month were read in a yard of about nine at about the eleventh hour in the ordinary way, and none of them was softened, and the reader of the body read them and the clerk entered them and the man of about fifty said nothing that was not on the list.

> **The compost line for the tenth month.** Not entered. The figure falls on the first of next month, a Saturday, and this holding does not know what it is. **The tenth month of a run of nine that has not been discharged in nine.**
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of last month, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit. The reading of the thirteenth of a month two months back is five-eighths. **This body will not say whether the difference is a movement or a season.** No cause, no due date, **and it has not been measured since the twenty-first of last month and is not to be measured again this month.**
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. **Column open.** The rule's third line does not say in a yard, **this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule was used outside one of its own four lines on the twenty-second of last month, and that is entered.**
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-two.** The last reading day of this month is the thirtieth. The boards, **two hundred and nineteen days**, day minus sixty-six, cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years and said out loud in this yard.
>
> **The March's tin. Two hundred and sixty-six days.** Day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**
>
> **The standing rule.** Four lines, every one about a stop, satisfied on the third of a month four months back, **used once, on the twenty-second of last month, and used outside one of its own four lines, and no use in this month at all.**
>
> **The use log at fifteen lines.** The fifteenth is a contact on a boarded floor at the fourth hour of the ninth, with a licensed man, a plain wire gauge test written down first, the mark hot for a day and the hand no use for about a day, and the case carried up the slope by the man from the north row. **The rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is not longer than it was on the eleventh of last month, there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow is not a mark.**
>
> **A form on a bench in the seed house, correct, filled in, not returned, not torn up, and the answer not given.**
>
> **The pail gone and the measure back.**
>
> **And the well on the shelf.** Five feet and one inch at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month, by the occupier, by her own nail, in her own yard, three feet and eleven inches against the ninth of a month four months back, **and this body will not decompose that figure and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.** A day letter off its stone from the eighth to the seventeenth of last month and the eight days not written on it. A green in a row nobody has read: three of four looked, one did not and said he is not going to, nobody dug, nobody weighed it, nobody named it. **And a row, and a wall, and a yard four hundred miles off, with no entry anywhere for any of the three either.**

And then the standing rule about the engineer of record was read out again, in full, four lines, in the same yard of about nine, before the figures were closed, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit, and the fen entered that it has now been read out in full three times in twenty-four days and used once and **the count of the uses is one.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is twenty-eight this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

And then, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in the same yard of about nine, he asked the fen what it would like to happen tomorrow, which is his standing and the count of that standing is four in a season, and the answer was six words and it is not a figure and there is nothing on a page that can be got wrong about it.

"**I want somebody to say no.**"

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the fifteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a question: **the reader of this body asked this body what it would like to happen tomorrow, which is his standing, and the fen answered in six words: I want somebody to say no. The clerk did not read it back a second time. The fen entered why, and the reason is that a thing read back twice is a thing that is going to be improved on, and that six words is not a standing and cannot be made into one, and that this holding has six things it has been told no by this season and has not been told no by anybody since the eighteenth of last month and has entered that it does not know which of those two facts it would rather be in.**

And then, at about the sixth hour of the evening, at the north gate, in about nine people, the handoff was read, and it is four sentences, and it is the day after tomorrow's and not this book's.

"**The fourth hour on the sixteenth of this month, four hundred miles off, a nail and a string and a woman of fifty-four's own hand, in her own yard, on her own wall.** This holding has no standing on it, has never had, did not write and ask about the last one and is not going to write and ask about this one. **The reading is not in this book and is not going to be predicted in this book.**"

"**And the fen entered the day and not the figure. The day is the sixteenth. The count of days between the end of this book and the fourth hour of that morning is one, and the fen entered that instead, because a day that can be counted is the only one of the three things this body is able to put in a book.**"

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the fifteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a clock: **the next ordinary reading of the well on a shelf four hundred miles off is the fourth hour on the sixteenth of this month and is not in this book. The fen has no standing on it and did not write and ask and is not going to. The day and not the figure has been entered, and the day is the sixteenth, and the count of one day has been entered, and no figure has been entered against it and none is to be.**

Underneath the day's work, dated the fifteenth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-fifth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A month turned in the middle of it and the ninth month's line came in at eighty pounds and the woman who does the sums said it was emptier and not better, and the price came down a penny on a sheet with a firm's name on it and nobody wrote and asked. A pail that went out and a measure that came back and nobody with either, and a question asked in a full yard of about twenty-five that nobody took, and the count of the questions available to a room and not taken at six. A schedule out of a building this body has never been in giving one thousand five hundred and twenty hundredweight over sixteen days, and a sum that closes at twenty-five pounds, six shillings and eightpence, and a road, and four hundred and forty-one people at the end of it, and a body that can do the whole of the sum and cannot write the first half of the sentence. A woman of fifty-six up nine miles in the rain on her own money saying the fee before anybody offered it, refused a bed in one sentence for a reason that was not suspicion, and four decisions about a relay made nineteen years ago and a number and a name said once out of her own mouth in a room with the doors shut, entered on a page anybody may walk up to and read, uncorroborated, at the cost of this holding's own exposure and not hers. A holder of record of a well eleven miles off who is correct in every particular and has corrected this holding twice against himself, and a form on a bench with the answer not given, and a licensed man at a shaft and four lines out of an instrument and the second one about a hand and not about a person. A party on a return that supplied water at no price and named itself because a document is the only place a body can be a party, and four people in a quarter who have stopped saying a thing they were saying. A date with nothing against it that fell on the eleventh and a Sunday on which nothing happened that this body can see, and a man of about thirty-five down a slope saying a thing under a floor is not late. Five things against four lines and no fifth line written. And six words at a gate, and one day, and a wall four hundred miles off with a nail and a string on it and nothing entered against either.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

And so the tenth month of this holding's book closed at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fifteenth, on a form on a bench, a woman on a page, a party on a return, a prediction that came and went with nothing attached to it, a refusal from a person who has refused five things in eleven years, a count of the not-known at four against a standing list of four lines and five things, **and the whole of what it has is that every one of those went in with the hour on it and was read back out loud in front of the people it was about, and that is nine days of a volume and it is the only part of it anybody is going to be able to check, and the fen entered that it knows that and is not going to do anything about it.**

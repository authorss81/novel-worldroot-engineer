# Chapter 162

## The Name In Her Own Mouth

The sixth of this month was a Tuesday, and at about the tenth hour of the morning a room in the seed house with the bay doors shut had about eleven people in it, and a retired root engineer of fifty-six sat at the table on the side of it where the light is worst, and the fen closed the doors because a definition is read in a room and this is worse than a definition and there is no form on which anybody may read it in the open.

The man of about seventy was not in the room. He was asked to be and was not fetched, and the fen entered that it did not fetch him, that this body has brought him out for a thing twelve times in a season and that the thirteenth was owed and was not taken today, **and entered that it did not take it because it has brought that man out once in nine days and once in two days and is not going to do it a third time to find out what he would say about a word he has never heard.**

She gave her name and her position again in the first minute, in the same form as the day before, and the clerk read it back, and then she said that she was going to say four things and then a number and then one word, and that she was going to say them in that order, and that the clerk was to write them in that order, and that she was not going to say any of them twice.

Then she said them, and the clerk wrote them, and about eleven people heard all of it, and the clerk did not improve one word of it afterwards.

"**One. Nineteen years ago I designed a safety relay for a flow in a dry country. It was not a large work. It was a relay with a red line in it, and the red line meant that if the flow went out where it should not have gone, the relay was to shut itself and say so.**"

"**Two. The relay could have done that. That is the part that matters and I am putting it before the part that does not. The relay worked. It was sound. It would have shut itself.**"

"**Three. I took the red line out of it.**"

"**Four. And then I changed the log entry from what it was to a line that said routine recalibration, and I signed a report that put the cause down to salt creep and weather, and both of those are my hand and my name on them, and there is a copy of the report in a room in another district in a country nine hundred miles from here and it is a true copy.**"

And then she gave the number, and the number is on this page and is not a figure this holding can check, and she said it the way a person says a thing they have carried a long time and have stopped being able to make smaller.

"**Four thousand two hundred people in the first winter. Nine thousand more moved. The pulse is what the people who were in it called it and I have never used the word myself and I am not going to start this morning.**"

And then she said the one word, out of her own mouth, once, in a room with about eleven people in it, and it was a place and it was not an office and it was not a date.

"**Brinewake.**"

Nobody said anything. She did not say it again. The clerk wrote it once and did not write it twice, and the fen entered that he did not write it twice, and **the word is on this page once and it is on no other page of this book, and that is a fact about this book's own page and not an accident, and a person in four years can go and look and will find it here and nowhere.**

And then the reader of this body asked her one more question before he asked the one that was his to ask, and he said the reason for that order, and about eleven people were in that room and nobody else was going to ask it.

"**Before I ask the question that is mine, I am going to ask you the one that is yours, and I am going to ask it in front of them so that the answer goes in with the rest.**"

"**What do you want this body to do with a number like that.**"

"**Nothing.** That is the answer and I want it written down first, because everything else in that room has been about what people are going to do. I do not want it used. I do not want it printed. I do not want it in a yard in nine miles and I do not want it in a yard here.** I have come nine miles to have my name on a page, and the name is the whole of it, and if that number is on a page in this building then somebody in nine miles with a form and a standing can pick it up, and I would then be a woman in a room in four years instead of a woman in a room in nineteen, and that is not what I have come for.**"

"**I have had one of these. I am not going to have a second one that I chose.**"

Nobody said the next part right. The reader read it back into a room of about eleven and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and the count of that request is twenty-two this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint, and entered that this holding has no use for the number and has entered that it has no use for it and is not going to acquire one.

Then he asked the only question in that room that was his to ask, and he gave the reason for asking it first, and the clerk was one of the eleven.

"**What you have said in that room is four decisions and a number and a name, and I am going to say the whole of what this holding can do with it, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said this in front of her and not behind her back.**"

"**We cannot check one word of it. Not one. I am not saying that as a complaint and I am not saying it as a way of not being in the room, because I have been in the room since the ninth of last month and this holding wrote down that it did not know something out loud in front of nine people and it was four words long and it was the best thing it has ever done.**"

"**There are two things in the world that can carry what she has just said. One of them is her memory. The other one is a record of compensation that is sealed, and that record is produced to the person named in it and on that person's own request, and no request has been made, and nobody has been named in it, and nothing in it has been opened, and it is not going to be opened in this room.**"

"**Enter that. Not as a reason against her and not as a reason for her. Enter that the only two doors onto nineteen years of this are a woman's memory and a piece of paper this body has no standing to ask for, and that this body is not going to open either, and that if a person in four years reads this page and asks which of the two came out, the answer is one and the other one is a door with a rule on it.**"

The seedwright of the trust said it once and she did not soften it and she did not number it, and about eleven people were in that room and one of them was a woman who had walked nine miles in the rain two days before.

"**A mechanism is not a verification and a soundness is not a hand, and this holding is about to do something it has not done in eleven years, which is to enter the soundness of a woman into its own book and call the book a record.**"

Nobody thanked her. She was not thanked and had said before she was thanked that she was not going to be, and the count of that standing in a season is thirteen, and the fen entered the count.

---

And then she asked for the thing she came for, and it was not money, and the room went quiet in a way the fen entered.

"**I have one request and it is small and it is the whole of why I came and it is not for me.**"

"**Put my name against it. In your own book. Under my own name, with the date, as the person who said it. That is all. I am not asking for the record. I am not asking to see what you have written. I am asking to be on the page, because in four years there is going to be one person in the world who can say that she said it, and if she is not on the page then the page says a thing happened to a room.**"

The reader of this body stood up and put the two halves of it in front of about eleven people, and he named the wrong half as the wrong half before he put the other one down, and that is his practice and the fen entered it as a practice and not as a virtue.

"**First the one that is wrong, and I am naming it as wrong before I say it because if I say it second it will sound like the sensible one.**"

"**If this holding writes it down, an office can list it. That is not a threat and it is not a guess, it is the sixth thing this holding has been told no by this season and the sixth was a form in the ordinary course with no part of it a finding against anybody. A thing that is in a list is a thing that has been had. Her name, her hand, a date and a number, in a book anybody may walk up to and read, in a fen with no charter and four pounds and fivepence, and an office in nine miles that has four of this holding's records in a class of its own and cannot get at a fifth.**"

"**And the half that is right. If this holding does not write it down, then a woman said a thing in a room and it is gone, and in four years she will be the only person who ever said it, and this book will be the proof that nobody was listening. That is worse than the list. I do not know how it is worse and I do not need to know, because a list is a thing you can fight and a room nobody was in is a thing you cannot.**"

"**So this holding has a choice between being listed and being empty, and I have been in this book for nine years and I have never had to put that in a yard, and I am putting it in one, and the count of that is one.**"

He looked round the room and nobody said anything and then he said the sentence that the clerk entered whole, and the fen entered that he said it with his hand flat on the bench, which he does.

"**Enter it. Her name, the date, the four decisions, the number and the word, in her words and not in mine, and read it back to her, and let an office list it. And enter the whole of what that costs, which is this body's exposure and not her absolution, and enter that it went in this holding's book and not in hers.**"

"**And enter the other half, which is the one I have had to sit down for. On the eleventh of last month I said in a yard that a name is a thing other people can spend, and I said a body with a name has somebody to blame. On the twenty-seventh of last month I said that a body with a name can hold a thing it cannot defend and that is how a name becomes a party. This body has been a name for a month and it has just made itself a party, and I said the sentence about how that happens eight days before a woman walked nine miles to hand it to us, and I did not think about it for a day then, and I have thought about it for two days since, and I am not going to improve on it in a room with eleven people in it.**"

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the sixth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a statement: **a retired root engineer of fifty-six, the first of the three names on the certificate in this building, was heard in a room with the bay doors shut and about eleven people in it. She gave her name and her position. She said that she designed a safety relay for a flow in a dry country nineteen years ago; that the relay could have isolated a pulse that was going out where it should not have gone; that she took a red line out of it; that she changed the log entry from what it was to a line that said routine recalibration; and that she signed a report putting the cause down to salt creep and weather. She gave the number of that first winter, four thousand two hundred dead and nine thousand moved. She said one name of a place, once, and did not say it again. The clerk wrote it once and did not write it twice.**
>
> **Entered on the same line: the statement is unverified and cannot be verified by this body. The record of compensation is sealed and is produced to the person named in it on that person's own request; no request has been made, nobody has been named, nothing has been opened, and this body has no standing to become a person who could. The two things that can carry what she said are her memory and that record, and the fen has not opened the second and is not going to.**
>
> **Entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: the reader said that if this holding writes it down an office can list it, and named that as the wrong half, and said that if this holding does not write it down then a woman said a thing in a room and it is gone and in four years she will be the only person who ever said it. The fen entered the request and entered the answer, and entered that the answer was given by the reader of this body standing up with his hand on the bench.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand, read back and not improved: this holding has been a name for a month and has entered a woman's name against a thing nobody can corroborate. The payment for it is this body's exposure and it is not hers, and it went in this holding's book and not in hers, and she was not thanked and was not forgiven and did not ask to be either.**

And then the clerk read the whole of it back to her, all of it, the four decisions and the number and the word, in the order she gave them, and she sat and listened to all of it and corrected one figure, which was the number, and the correction was that the nine thousand had moved and not died, which was in the record already, and the clerk entered that she corrected it and entered that the correction was the same as what she had said.

Nobody thanked anybody. About eleven people were in that room and one of them had been in a room like it before, four hundred miles off, in a yard with a nail and a string and eight readings on a wall, and the fen entered that she has not asked for one word of this back and has not offered to take any of it out, and has not been thanked, and the count of her not asking is entered and is not a figure anybody in that room was going to put a pound sign against.

And so the sixth of this month put a word into a book that anybody may walk up to and read, on the say-so of one woman, in a building with no charter, in a fen with four pounds and fivepence in a tin, **and there is not one other person in the world this body could go to and say: check it, and it is not going to be checked, and that is not a failure of this book and it is the condition this book has been written in for two volumes.**

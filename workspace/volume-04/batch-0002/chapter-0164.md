# Chapter 164

## A Holder In A Yard

The ninth of this month was a Friday, and at about the second hour of the afternoon a man came up the fen road in the rain with a schedule in a leather bag and a man with him, and he was let in at the gate and announced, because he had not been in this yard in his life and this holding has no custom for a charter holder and had to invent one at the gate in about four seconds and the clerk entered that it invented one.

Garrow Nye, holder of record of the Pans well, of no address this office holds, which is a sentence that has been in this book's book since the second of last month and which is now a man standing in a yard saying his own name out loud.

Nobody thanked him and nobody refused him and about twenty-five people were in that yard inside ten minutes, which is more than this holding has had on a working day since the twenty-sixth.

He was not a liar. The reader of this body entered that at the fourth sentence and entered that it stands, and **he corrected this holding twice in his first ten minutes and both of the two were against himself, and the reader entered the count of that and entered that the count is two and that the two are entered and are not to be improved on by anybody who was not in that yard.**

Then he said what he had come to say, and he said it in about five sentences, and he did not say it in a low voice and he did not say it to one man.

"**The Pans well stopped at the fourth hour of the morning of the twentieth of last month. Not the afternoon. The fourth hour. I know the day and the hour and I have had both in a book since the morning it happened and the book is at home and it is anybody's book to read who comes eleven miles.**"

"**The licensed draw on that well in the last twelve months is one hundred and ninety thousand hundredweight, and the specification on my own charter is one hundred and seventy-four thousand. I have put the two figures in a room together and I have looked at them. That is the first time. That is the first time in nineteen years and I am telling you before I tell you the rest.**"

"**The flow under the low quarter and the flow under the Pans well are the same flow.** I do not know how a body of sixteen households finds that out and I have not asked how, and I would rather say that than have somebody work it out in front of me and be polite about it."

"**The man with the cart is not the cause of anything. I am.**"

"**And I have come into this yard to say that out loud, and I want the last part in. A form would have said it wrong and a form would have said it late. I have had four days to write it down and I have not written it down, and it is in here instead, and a yard is nine miles from a counter and this body has no charter, so if you want to make a thing of it you are going to have to make a thing of it the hard way, and I would rather that.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody was asked to answer him and about twenty-five people were in that yard and the reader of this body did not say one word that was not on the list, and he put the second correction in himself before the first one was out of the room.

"**The second one, and it is smaller than the first and it is the reason I came. A man with a cart has been hauling water out of that well into a quarter where wells are off for a season, at a shilling a pail, and about forty people in that quarter have said he is a thief, and this holding has written a great deal about it in a book and has not put the word *licensed* next to his name once.**"

"**He is licensed to carry under my charter. I did not know that. I asked this morning before I came up the road and I have been told so in a room in eleven miles by a man who has no reason to say it and every reason not to, and it is in the schedule in this bag, and I have not got a copy for you and I have not got a copy for myself and I am not going to be the one who gives it out, because a schedule is a holder's document and the holder is me and a man in a yard is not the office.**"

"**So: two corrections, both against me, both entered, and I would like the clerk to enter the count and enter that neither of them makes the water cheaper.**"

Then the reader of this body said the one distinction this book has been keeping for a month, out loud, in a yard of about twenty-five, standing up, and gave the reason for saying it first, and about twenty-five people heard it.

"**A charter holder is a supply. A charter holder is not a thief. I have said that in this yard twice in a month and it is in this book's book in my own words and it is going to be in the book again this afternoon because that man has just said a sentence in this yard that would take the distinction out of it in about four years if nobody puts it back.**"

"**A man emptying a well and charging by the pail is a supply, and a supply is a thing the law has an opinion about, and the law is nine miles away in a building and does not live in a yard. He has a licence. So he is a supply with a licence on it and a road of eleven miles between him and the water, and the difference between his rate and the price of a pail over that month is twenty-five pounds, six shillings and eightpence, and it is in nobody's pocket.**"

"**And I am not going to have this holding confused in four years because a holder of record stood in a yard and said the words *I am*. He is not a thief. He is the man whose water the quarter was standing in and he is the man with a form in his bag, and both of those are true at once and this book has been written for a year on the two of them being true at once.**"

Nobody answered that either. The fen entered that nobody in that yard said the word *thief* about anybody during the ten minutes that man was speaking, and entered that the fen did not find that out by asking and entered that the fen did not ask, and entered the reason, which is that it does not ask a yard what it did not hear.

Then he asked for the thing, and the thing is the day, and he said it flatly and in the ordinary voice and he did not build up to it.

"**I want this holding to certify, on a form, that a flow is broken.**"

And he held up the form while he said it, so that the whole of that yard could see the shape of it, and it is the ordinary form and it is correct and it has four lines and it is a form anybody may fill in and it is filled in with a date and a well and a licensed man and a number.

"**And I know what the form does. If that is certified the licensed draw at the Pans well closes, because a licensed supply out of a broken flow is not a supply, it is a theft, and I am not going to stand in a yard and let anybody think I do not know that.**"

"**The licensed draw is the only supply that quarter has got. The water a party put in there at no price on the second to the seventh has stopped. Nobody in this fen knows where that water came from, nobody in this fen knows where it went, and I have not asked and I am not going to, because I have a well to think about.**"

"**So I know that this form would close the only supply to four hundred and forty-one people and I have brought it in the rain anyway. And here is the reason and I would like the clerk to write it before I go on, because I have said four things in a yard this morning and three of them were about myself.**"

> **A holder whose well is broken and who does not say so is a holder whose break is somebody else's water. I have read four holders' books in nineteen years and I can tell which of them were lying and which of them were not, and I do not think I am one of the ones that were, and I would like that thought about with a form on it in front of twenty-five people, because a thought about in a yard is worth nothing in four years and a form is worth something in four years.**

---

And then, because the man of about fifty had asked at the gate whether anybody was going down to the floor and had been told that a licensed man was already on the boards, at about the fourth hour, at the shaft in this holding's own low ground, the instrument was laid on the boards and the reading of the test was written down before it went near anything.

The use log's fifteenth line was written first and read back first by the person who keeps the seed house, and she read it back and entered that she read it and entered nothing else, and gave the reason in one sentence, which is the reason she has given every time for eleven years: **there is nothing in this that a man with a rule can check, and a line in a book that cannot be checked is a line somebody will use.**

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in the low ground, by the clerk, on the standing of the use log: **the fifteenth line. Date: the ninth of this month. Instrument: the case, at the boards over the shaft in the low ground. Person holding it: C. Renner, licensed. Purpose: contact, at the boards, on a shaft that came off on the twenty-first of a month two months back. Licensed person present: the holder. Contact tested with a plain wire gauge and the reading of the test written down before the instrument was laid on the boards. Out. Back: the same day, the eighth hour.**

The instrument came out of the case and three of the four lines came out of it, and the licensed man said out loud, before he read them, that they are the instrument's and not a person's, and that the second one is about a hand and not about a person, and that no document in this book is going to quote them as an instruction or as a reason.

> The mouth is not the same twice.
> A flow that is broken shows the break and not the hand that made it.
> A person who says it out loud is not a finding.
> Ask what it is carrying and not who is holding it.

He read them out once, in the ordinary voice, at about the fourth hour and a half, with about four people on the boards and about twenty-five in the yard above, and then he put the instrument away and could not get the latch with two fingers, and the man of the north row with the cough carried the case up the slope with him because he asked and because nobody else offered in time. **The mark on his forearm was hot for the rest of that day and the hand from the wrist down was no use for about a day afterward, and neither of those is a mark and neither is a new one, and the rootmark is four inches and branching twice and is not longer this afternoon than it was on the eleventh of last month, and there is no second mark and the burn that ran past it into the elbow on the twenty-second of last month is not a mark.**

And then the licensed man said what the contact does and does not do, in that order, standing in about four feet of grass at the fifth hour, and about twenty-five people in the yard heard all of it.

"**What I can defend. That floor is dry and there is water under it, and I did not put a rule to it and I did not dig, and there is a shaft about two hundred years old with a boarded top on it. That is a fact about a floor and a fact about a date and it is not a fact about a cause.**"

"**And what I cannot defend, and I am going to say it up here and not down there, because up here there are twenty-five people and one of them has come eleven miles in the rain. This contact says nothing whatsoever about the Pans well. Not one word of it. The Pans well is eleven miles off on a road this body did not lay, this body has no standing on that road, this body has no crew, and a man with a schedule in a bag is not a crew.**"

"**And the only man in this district who could tell me what is carrying under that floor is a man of about thirty-five who has said he will not be asked again by this body, and he is not in this yard, and I am not going to ask him, and if somebody in this yard asks him on my behalf I will say no in one word and it will be in the book.**"

---

And then the reader of this body had to decide, in about twenty-five people, what this holding could put its name to, and it is the whole of the day and it took him until the seventh hour and the fen entered the hour.

"**The standing rule's first line is a question about what a return is carrying. I read it out in a yard of twenty-five on the thirtieth of last month and I said then that nobody in this yard could answer it, and that has not changed and the man of about thirty-five is not in this yard.**"

"**The only man in this district who can answer it is standing in my yard, on his own money, in the rain, and he is not a licensed man and he has no book and he is not a party to this body, and he has come here to be asked a question, and I have to decide in front of twenty-five people whether this holding can put its name to the asking.**"

Then he said what he decided, and the clerk took it down in the order he said it, and the reader of this body did not improve it afterwards and read it back out of the book twice.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the ninth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a form and a decision: **a holder of record of a well eleven miles off has asked this holding to certify on a form that a flow is broken. The form is the ordinary form, it is correct, it is filled in with a date, a well, a licensed man and a number, and anybody may fill in one. This holding will not put its name to it.**
>
> **And this holding will not refuse to either.** The form is on the bench in the seed house. It has not been returned and it has not been torn up. **The entry is that the form is on the bench and the answer has not been given.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the day the answer is given, a holder of record will have an answer from a body of sixteen households with no charter, and this body has known that since the eighteenth of last month and has not done one thing about it, and the reason it has not done one thing about it is that it has no standing, and the reason it has no standing is on a sheet written by an office nine miles off, and the office is right, and this has been entered four times in a season and is entered a fifth time here because a form is on a bench.**

Nobody thanked anybody. About twenty-five people were in that yard and the holder of record stood at the wall side of it and did not say one word about the answer he had not got, and the fen entered that he did not and entered that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and the count of the reader of this body's requests for his initial against nothing is twenty-four this season, and the count is entered and the count is not a complaint.

And so a mechanism was named in a yard on the ninth of this month by the man it damages, in about five sentences, in front of about twenty-five people, and it was correct, and a form is on a bench in a seed house with the answer not given, **and this holding has found out one new thing about itself, which is that it does not have a way of saying no to a person who is telling the truth, and it has had a way of saying no to a man with a cart for a month and never once used it, and the difference between those two men is a form and eleven miles and the direction of the water.**

# Chapter 176

## Four Of Them And One Of Them

The twenty-eighth of this month was a Wednesday, and the man of the north row with the cough went out to the low quarter at about the fifth hour of the morning and came back at about the seventh hour of the evening, and he came into a yard of about nine people at the north gate without sitting down, and he had two things, and he asked nobody anything about either of them, and the fen entered that he did not and entered that nobody tried to make him.

He had gone out with a seed bag and a hooked stick and come back with the seed bag, and the bag was lighter than it had gone out with and the fen entered that and entered that it was lighter because he had left a paper of turnip on a gate on the way down and had not taken it back, and that he has sold seed on that lane for eleven years and has never once left a paper and come back for it, and that this holding does not know why he did it and did not write and ask.

The first thing was about four houses on that row, and it is a thing that has been in this book for a month, and the reader of this body asked for it in the ordinary way and was given it in about six sentences.

"**The four that stopped saying it have not started it again. That is the first thing and I want it said first, because I have walked the whole of that lane twice this week looking for a sign that they had and I have not found one.**"

"**And one of the four has said a new thing instead, and the new thing is that they would like to know who is paying for the water.**"

Nobody said anything about that for a moment, and the fen entered the moment and entered that the reader of this body did not answer it and entered that about nine people in that yard did not answer it either, and entered that a licensed supply is a licensed supply, and that the house asking was not asking this body a question and did not want an answer and said so before she left the lane.

"**That is what I have got. It is not a complaint. It is not the old sentence. The old sentence was a sentence about a man with a cart and I have not heard it in a month, and this is a different one, and this holding does not know what to do with a sentence it has not heard before, and I have not written and asked anybody what it means and I am not going to.**"

And then the second thing, which is smaller, and he gave it in about four sentences before anybody had got the words out for the first one.

"**A fifth house on that row has stopped asking. Not the four. A fifth one. Somebody used to ask about the water twice a week in that lane and has not asked in about a fortnight, and I stood in that gate on Tuesday and I did not ask him why, and I am going to say why I did not ask him why and then I am going to stop talking about it.**"

"**It was not my row. I sell them seed and I have no business on that lane and I have had that business for eleven years and I know what a gate is for. And I have been asked in this yard to write things down, and I write things down, and a man who writes things down is not a man who goes and stands in gates asking people what they have stopped doing. If I asked him that I would be a second thing this body has sent up that road with no standing, and one of the two is enough.**"

Nobody said anything to that either, and the man of the north row with the cough went home, and nobody offered him anything and he did not ask for anything, and the fen entered that he was not thanked and entered the count of that standing, which is a man's refusal to do a thing this body would have liked done, and the count of those in this fen in a season is one, and the fen entered that it is the first of them and did not improve on it.

---

And then the arithmetic, and it happened in the seed house with the bay doors shut and about eleven people in it on the evening of the twenty-eighth, and nobody had asked for it, and the woman who does the sums put it on the table herself and said the word before the figure, which is her standing.

"**I am going to do two sums and I am going to say first that I have the number of things I cannot do up to four, and I would rather do the two I can and say that out loud than do neither and say nothing about it.**"

"**One. The licensed man's time. There have been three days of him in this month, the ninth, the twentieth and the twenty-fourth, and the hire book is a pound a day and has been a pound a day for two years, so three days at a pound is three pounds. And I am going to do it the other way, because I have been doing sums one way since I was a girl. Three pounds is sixty shillings, and sixty shillings is three days at twenty shillings, and that is the same three days and the same twenty shillings.**"

"**Two. What that leaves. Four pounds and fivepence is eighty-five shillings, and eighty-five shillings less sixty shillings is twenty-five shillings, and twenty-five shillings is two pounds and five shillings. And in pence, because my father did pence and not shillings and I have never had a reason to stop. Four pounds five is a thousand and twenty pence. Three pounds is seven hundred and twenty pence. A thousand and twenty less seven hundred and twenty is three hundred pence. Three hundred pence is twenty-five shillings. That is two pounds and five shillings.**"

"**The two agree, and that is the whole of what I have got, and it took about four minutes, and I am going to say the next part out loud before anybody in this room thanks me for doing two sums in four minutes.**"

"**A figure that a body cannot check is not a protection. It is a debt. And this holding has four of those now and I am going to name all four, because a woman who says a number of things without saying which things is the reason the number is no use to anybody.**"

"**One, what that quarter drinks now. Two, the strip about two paces wide in front of four houses and what comes up out of it. Three, the eleven a man gave in this yard on Tuesday, which is a number of the ones who came to a decision and not a number of anybody. Four, what a man spent on his own ground eleven miles off and has not written down and is not going to.**"

"**And the reason there are four of them and not one is that a quarter with water in it again has two things under the same ground and a gate on neither of them. The number of pails a quarter bought in a month was a count four households could make at a gate with a tally and a child. The number of pails a quarter is drinking is not a thing anybody can count, and the two cannot be added and the two cannot be divided, and a figure that is the sum of two things one of which was never measured is not a figure, and I am not entering it, and I have entered the count of four and I have not made a column.**"

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a sum: **the woman who does the sums derived the licensed man's time for this month twice, being the ninth, the twentieth and the twenty-fourth, at the rate in the hire book, being one pound a day, and the two derivations agree at three pounds. She derived what is left in hand twice, being four pounds and fivepence less three pounds, and the two derivations agree at two pounds and five shillings. The four things this holding cannot check are entered separately and are not folded into anything. The fen entered the count of four and did not make a column, and entered that the count of four figures and the count of the four things this body does not know are two different counts and are not to be run together, and did not improve on that.**

And then she said the last of it, in one sentence, and about eleven people were in that room and the clerk was one of the eleven and wrote all of it.

"**And here is the half I have not got a column for. A figure that can be checked is a figure this holding can hand to an office, and that used to be a complaint and is now a thing that travels, and I am glad about it and I am not glad about it, and I am not going to split it into two sentences, because splitting it would let me pick one.**"

Nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be thanked, and the reader of this body did not improve on it and was not asked to.

---

And on the twenty-ninth, which was a Thursday, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in a yard of about nine, the reader of this body was asked a question by somebody who had not asked it before, and the question was about a cart, and he gave the only honest sentence he had.

"**Is anybody going to say anything about the cart that went up the plateau road.**"

"**It is the second of the four things this body does not know. I have not written and asked about it and I am not going to this month, and the count of the not-known stands at four and was four a month ago, and I would like the clerk to enter that I am not going to make it five this month, and entered that I did not promise anybody that I would not.**"

"**And I would like the second thing on the same line, which is that a month ago somebody in this yard worked out that the not asking was a month old, and nobody said anything then either, and the not asking is a month old and not a day, and I am not going to convert it into a day this month to make it look like progress.**"

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-ninth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing not known: **the fourth cart went up the plateau road on the seventh of a month two months back and has not come down. Nobody asked. The not asking is a month old and is entered as a month and not as a day. The waggon is accounted for and the fourth cart is not, and the two are different vehicles, and the fen entered that difference again and entered that this is the second of the four things this body does not know, and entered that the count stands at four and was four a month ago, and that the reader of this body is not going to make it five this month and did not promise anybody that he would not.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: nobody in that yard said a word about it. Not one of the about nine. The fen entered that nobody said a word about it, and entered that the man who asked had not been asked to ask by anybody, and that he had been in this yard about four years, and that he had worked the count out for himself, and that he said it out loud anyway, and the fen entered that it is not going to thank him for it and did not.**

The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, **and the count of that request is thirty-six this season, which is entered, and the count is not a complaint.**

And then, at about the eighth hour of that evening at the north gate, in about nine people, the reader of this body said out loud how many days were left in the month, and he said eight, and about four of the nine looked at the gate and none of them said anything, and the fen entered that nobody corrected him for about a minute.

The woman who keeps time corrected him before anything was written down, and she said out loud before she started that she keeps time and does not round, and he agreed with her.

"**It is one day. It is the twenty-ninth and the month is thirty days and there is one day left in it, and I said eight, and I have not got a reason for the eight and I am not going to make one up in a yard.**"

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-ninth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the reader of this body said in a yard of about nine that there were eight days left in the month. The woman who keeps time said there was one, before anything was written down, and said out loud that she keeps time and does not round, and he agreed with her. Both figures are entered and neither has been improved on. The fen entered that he has not got a reason for the eight, and entered that this is the second wrong count this body has made in a yard in front of nine people or twenty, the first of them a month ago in a yard of about twenty, and that the voice is the same both times and the mistake is the same shape both times, and entered that a body which corrects its own clock out loud in a yard is a body whose clock can be believed, and did not improve on that.**

And so the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth of this month went down, and four people in a quarter have stopped saying the thing they were saying and one of them has asked a new question that nobody in this fen can answer, and a fifth has stopped asking and nobody knows why, and a man walked nine miles on Tuesday and said a thing under that ground is not to be drawn from and gave a figure that is not a number of anybody, **and this holding entered all of it, and has four figures it cannot check, and a form on a bench, and a cart it will not ask about, and about nine people in a yard who said nothing at all about the cart, and it thanked none of them, and it did not improve on one word of the day, and the month closes tomorrow and this book does not know what is on the first of next month and has entered the day and not the figure.**

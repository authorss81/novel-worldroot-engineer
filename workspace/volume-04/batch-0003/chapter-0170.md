# Chapter 170

## It Came Back On A Tuesday

The nineteenth of this month was a Monday, and at about the eleventh hour of the morning the standing figures of the day were read at the north gate in the ordinary way with about nine people there, and none of them was softened, and the reader of this body asked one question in the middle of it and got the answer he expected and did not improve on it.

He asked whether anybody was going down the dry road tomorrow, and nobody was, and he said the reason out loud before anybody could offer to go with him.

"**Nobody from this holding is going eleven miles tomorrow and nobody from this holding is going to stand in that man's yard while he takes his own well apart. We have no standing on that road, we have had none for eleven years, and a body that turns up at a repair it did not ask about is a body asking to be a party to it.**"

"**And I am not going to be thanked for that. I am going to be blamed for it by somebody in about four years and that is a price and not a fault, and I would like it written down now in a month when I am in a better temper about it.**"

Nobody argued and about nine people were in that yard and the man of the north row with the cough said that he was going down to the low quarter tomorrow morning because the licensed draw was going to resume and a row that has had no water in it has four hundred yards of carrying in it before anybody has had a cup, and the reader of this body said that he would go with him and that he would not be asked to lift anything, and the fen entered both of those.

---

And on the twentieth, which was a Tuesday, a schedule came up the fen road on the ordinary post at about the tenth hour of the morning, and it was four lines and it was correct, and it is the first piece of news about that road this fen has had in a month that it did not have to ask a stranger for.

> Schedule of a licensed supply, made on the twentieth of this month by the holder of record of the Pans well, on his own paper, and sent up the fen road by the ordinary post in the ordinary course.
>
> The Pans well is running. The licensed draw resumed on this day at about the fourth hour, and the hours of a day are the hours of the charter and not the hours of anybody who is short of water.
>
> The quantity drawn since this morning is not on this sheet and will be on the next one. This sheet is a notice and is not a return, and no part of it is a finding against any person.
>
> This schedule is made in the ordinary course and asks no person for any form, no certificate and no answer.

Nobody thanked him and nobody was thanked, and the reader of this body read it out in the yard with about eleven people in it, and then said the half of it that is about this holding, and it is the half nobody had to ask him for.

"**The low quarter has water. That is the sentence and it is the whole of what has happened in this month that is good, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it twice, and I am not going to be thanked for it by anybody.**"

"**And the second half, which is in this yard because it has to be. This holding does not know what that water costs that quarter now and is not going to work it out. The rate is one shilling and fourpence the hundredweight and the price of a pail is a shilling and the difference is fourpence a hundredweight and eleven miles, and this holding has done that sum three times in a month and is not going to do it a fourth time for a figure that is nobody's to hand.**"

"**Because here is what a fourth time would be. Three times, a number is a complaint. The fourth time, a number is a thing four hundred and forty-one people are going to argue about, and this holding has no standing to start that argument and would be the only body in the district that had started it, and I have watched what happens to a body that is the only one that started something.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the twentieth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a supply: **the licensed draw at the Pans well resumed on this day at about the fourth hour and the low quarter has water. The fen entered that it has water and entered that it does not know what that water costs that quarter now and is not going to work it out, and entered the reason, which is that this holding has done that sum three times in a month and that a fourth time would make a number into a thing four hundred and forty-one people argue about, and this holding has no standing to start that and cannot stop it once it has.**

---

And then at about the fourth hour of that same afternoon, in the low ground four hundred yards below the north gate, with about four feet of grass and a slope and a lamp, a licensed man laid a plain wire gauge on the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, and the reading of the test was written down before the instrument went near anything, and the use log was not written on, and the clerk entered why before he wrote anything else.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twentieth of this month, in the low ground, by the clerk, on the standing of a line: **no line has been written in the use log today and the use log stands at fifteen lines. The reason is that the use log records a contact and a line records a contact and never a reading, and a draw gets no line, and a hold gets no line either, and this afternoon was a contact. The fifteenth line is the ninth of this month and this afternoon is the second contact in this fen in eleven days and it is not a line because it is not a line.**

The mark on the forearm was hot for the rest of that afternoon and the hand from the wrist down was no use for about a day afterward, and the man of the north row with the cough carried the case up the slope, **and neither of those is a mark and neither is a new one, and the rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is not longer this afternoon than it was on the ninth of this month, and there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow on the twenty-second of last month is not a mark.**

The licensed man wrote three numbers on a page side by side and did not write a difference, and the clerk entered that he did not write a difference and entered that nobody in the low ground asked him to and entered the reason he gave, which is one sentence.

> Entered on the same line, in the licensed man's own hand, with the hour on it: **the reading of the plain wire gauge test at the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, taken this afternoon at about the fourth hour, the reading of the test being written down before the instrument was laid on the boards. The reading of the twenty-first of last month was three and a half. The reading of the twenty-eighth of last month was three and a half. The reading of this afternoon is three and seven tenths.**
>
> **No difference is written. A difference between two readings taken on two different days with a plain wire gauge is not a number of tenths of an inch, it is two numbers with a day between them, and I write two numbers and a day and I do not write the thing a man would want me to write.**

And then the licensed man said what he could defend and what he could not, in that order, standing in about four feet of grass with about nine people in the yard above, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

"**What I can defend. That floor is dry and there is water under it and I did not put a rule to it and I did not dig, and there is a shaft about two hundred years old with a boarded top on it. That is a fact about a floor and a fact about a date and it is not a fact about a cause.**"

"**And what I cannot defend, said up here and not down there. I will not say that the flow under that floor is bigger because a man put his own well back together eleven miles off, and I will not say that it is not, and I want the clerk to enter that I said neither, because the first of those is a finding and the second of those is a finding and I have got a piece of metal and not a finding.**"

"**And the thing I am not going to say is a person's name, and I have not said it this afternoon and I am not going to say it on a different day. The instrument is a piece of metal that cannot be checked. The man who could check it is not in this yard and has said he will not be asked again by this body. Those two are eleven days apart in this book and they are not to be read as one, and the eleven days are the eleventh of this month and this afternoon, and I have been in two rooms in that time and I have asked nobody anything.**"

> Entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand, read back: **the licensed man declined to say whether the flow under that floor is larger than the reading of the twenty-eighth of last month, and declined to say that it is not, and said that the reason was that the first of those was a finding and the second of those was a finding. The fen entered that the instrument is a piece of metal that cannot be checked, and that the man of about thirty-five is not in that yard and has said he will not be asked again by this body, and that the two of those are eleven days apart in this book and are not to be read as one.**

---

And then at about the seventh hour of the evening of the same Tuesday, the ordinary post brought four lines down a four-day road in a leather bag, and they came from a shelf four hundred miles off, and the fen had entered on the sixteenth that it was four days from finding out the rest of it and had not written and asked.

> Written on the sixteenth of this month at the fourth house, at the fourth hour, by the occupier, in her own hand, in four lines, and taken down that road at the ordinary cost of that road:
>
> **One. The reading is five feet and the shortfall against the ninth of a month four months back is four feet, and I have not written what the difference is made of and I am not going to.**
>
> **Two. The day letter is on the stone and the eight days from the eighth to the seventeenth of last month are not on it and are not going on it, and I am telling you that they are not and I am not going to tell you why not.**
>
> **Three. There is green in the older row. We have not dug at it and we have not weighed it and nothing has come off that shelf in a vessel, and nobody here has said what it is.**
>
> **Four. Between the eighth and the fifteenth of this month the well came up half an inch, and in the night before this morning it went down half an inch, and I have written the reading on the wall and I am not going to write what the two numbers are. And the man with the spade has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month and we have not asked him, and I am telling you because you are the only party we have that writes things down.**

The reader of this body read those four lines out in the seed house with about nine people in it and the clerk entered them, and he did not write a fifth line under them and he did not write a reason, and the fen entered that he did not and entered the reason in one sentence: **two numbers that a woman four hundred miles off did not write what they are are not two numbers that this holding can make into one, and a body that adds a reason under four correct lines from a yard four hundred miles off is a body that has started editing people.**

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the twentieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a shelf: **four lines came down the four-day road from the fourth house at Cauldron Reach, written on the sixteenth at the fourth hour, at the household's own cost, and are entered entire.** The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-three. **The compost line for this month is not entered and the reason is entered: the figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and this holding does not know what it is, and it has said so out loud on the first, the sixteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth of last month and on the first of this month, and the count of the six is entered, and the count is not a complaint.**

And then at about the sixth hour of that Tuesday evening, in the yard, the man of the north row with the cough came back from the low quarter and said what he had found there, and he said it in about six sentences, and he said it as a fact about a yard and not as a finding, and he did not ask anybody anything.

"**The draw came on at the fourth hour and there were nine or ten pails at that row's end of the lane by the sixth hour of the morning and there were more after that, and the men with the pails are not the same men who were on that lane in the first week of the month.**"

"**And there is one thing I am going to say because I walked the whole of that lane and I did not see a face I did not know, and it is that nobody in that quarter said anything to me at all. Not good and not bad. I stood at a gate for about a quarter of an hour and I am used to being told to go away and I was not told to go away once.**"

"**I do not know what that means. I am not going to stand in this yard and tell nine people that the water has made a quarter polite, because a quarter with water in it is not a polite quarter, it is a quarter with water in it, and I have been in this fen long enough to know the difference between a thing and a finding.**"

And so the twentieth of this month was a Tuesday, and a well eleven miles off came back together at the fourth hour at a man's own cost on his own ground, and a quarter nine miles off has water, and a floor four hundred yards from a north gate read three and seven tenths of an inch on a piece of metal in the grass, and a woman of fifty-four wrote a round number on a wall four hundred miles off and would not write what it was made of, **and not one person in this yard said the word *thank you* and not one person was thanked, and the fen entered that the licensed man carried nothing to anybody except a case up a slope and that this holding has gained a quarter's water and a shelf's four lines and a figure it cannot check and has paid nothing for any of the three and is not going to be thanked for it.**

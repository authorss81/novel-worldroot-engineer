### Chapter 168 — *The Sixteenth* — day 286, the sixteenth, a Friday

**Goal:** the volume's second clock, read from outside, at the fourth hour, by a woman with a nail and a string, and the figure is a round one, and nobody on that shelf writes down what a round figure means.

**Must happen.** **The fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month, four hundred miles off, in the yard of the fourth house at Cauldron Reach, a woman of fifty-four reads her own well with her own nail and her own string and writes it on the wall in her own hand, and it is the ninth reading on that wall and the count of the series is settled and may not be re-dated.** The reading: **five feet.** She says it in the plainest words available to her and the three other people standing in that yard hear all of it and one of them has written it down since. **The ninth line of the wall block goes on the page, in wall order, the whole series, and the shortfall is four feet against the ninth of a month four months back, and it is not decomposed and the half inch is not carried forward as a figure.** And the reason a round figure is a thing she is going to say out loud: **nine feet, and five, and the four in between has been every figure there is, and now it is a number a person says out loud in a yard, and she is going to write it on a wall and is not going to write what it is.**

**And the rest of that yard, in about six hundred words, because this chapter is the volume's outside eye and it may not be a page of figures.** The grass cut every ten days by the man with the spade. The stack against the north wall and the place in it that the four of them have not agreed a name for. ### Chapter 169 — *What A Holder May Do* — days 287–288, the seventeenth and the eighteenth, a Saturday and a Sunday

**Goal:** a charter holder does the one thing a charter holder can do without a body of sixteen households, and it is correct, and this fen cannot stop him and cannot thank him, and the form on the bench is still on the bench.

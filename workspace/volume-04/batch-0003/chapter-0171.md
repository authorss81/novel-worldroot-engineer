# Chapter 171

## The Word, Used

The twenty-first of this month was a Wednesday, and at about the tenth hour of the morning the reader of this body read the standing list of four lines and the door at nine hundred yards off in the ordinary way, in a full yard with about twenty-five people in it, and the clerk entered the hour and the count of the readings of the seventh column, which is thirty-three, and nobody thanked anybody and the count of times a reading day has been read in a full yard in this month is now four.

> **What this body holds. What this body is owed. What this body does not know. What this body is looking after and cannot reach.**
>
> **Four lines. The first three have been in this book since the first year. The fourth names no person and no thing and no number of things, and it is a line and not a sentence, and it has not been improved on and is not to be. What this body does not know is four and it was four a month ago and it is not going to be three this month.**

The man of about thirty-five was at the wall side of that yard and had been since about the ninth hour, and he was not standing with anybody and he had not asked for anything, and the man from the north row with the cough was beside him and had been beside him for about a minute.

The figures of the day came after the list and before the word, and about twenty-five people stood in a yard in the ordinary way while a woman who keeps time read them out and said out loud before she began that she keeps time and does not round. The leak at three-quarters of an inch, measured once, on the twenty-first of last month, no cause, no due date, and not measured since. The beat not logged since, cause not established, nothing underneath it, column open. The seventh column at thirty-three. The standing rule, four lines, every one about a stop, used once on the twenty-second of last month, used outside one of its own four lines, and not used in this month. The use log at fifteen lines. The rootmark four inches and branching twice and no second mark. A form on a bench with the answer not given. The pail gone and the measure back. And the boards at two hundred and twenty-five days and the March's tin at two hundred and seventy-two days, the day-minus rule read out loud first, which the clerk has now done on five reading days running and which nobody has asked for.

**The compost line for this month was not entered and the reason was entered, and the reason is the same one it has been all month, which is that the figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and this holding does not know what it is.**

And then, and then, in the middle of it, in about the middle of the reading of the fourth line, one of about twenty-five people said the word.

He said it once. He said it into the air in front of him the way a man says a thing he has been carrying and did not mean to put down, **and it was not said by anybody who has read any ground in his life, and the fen entered that on the page without softening it and entered the name of the function and not the name of the man, because this holding does not give names to people who say a word in a yard by accident.**

The man of about thirty-five was four feet away and heard it and did not pretend he had not, and about twenty-five people watched a man decide, in about two seconds, not to pretend.

He did not shout and he did not build up to it. He said it in the ordinary voice, at the wall side of a yard of about twenty-five people, and he said the second half before anybody could get to the first half, and the clerk was one of the twenty-five and wrote it by the light of the lamp on the housing in his own hand afterward and read it back and did not improve it.

"**A man has said a word in this yard and I am not going to stand here and let twenty-five people go home with it. I am not going to tell you what it is. I am going to tell you how I know it is wrong, and that is a different thing and it is the only thing I have got.**"

"**I have read four pieces of ground. Nineteen years, and not nine, and I said nine to a man on this road in the middle of last month because nine is the answer that makes a man stop asking, and I have been carrying that for a month and I am going to correct it in a yard. Three of the four were crews with a licence and a schedule. And in none of the three did the schedule say that what was being held was being held because somebody was doing work on it every day. The fourth had nobody in it, it is down the coast road, I have not been back, and I am not going to say what I found there.**"

"**I am not going to tell you what it is and you are not going to get it out of me, and this is the third time I have said that and it is the same answer.**"

And then he said the other half, and this is the half he has given twice and gave a third time in a yard of about twenty-five people, and about nine of those twenty-five had heard the first two and the other sixteen had not.

"**And here is what I will say, and it is the whole of what I have got, and it is checkable and any one of you can go and check it or not check it. A nest has one thing in it, and one thing in it has a number, and a thing with a number in it is finished. The thing under that floor is not finished and it does not stop. It does not go where the ground goes. It is doing work, and the work is not water and it is not seeds, and it is the sort of work that stops if nobody does it.**"

"**And it has grown since the twenty-second of last month and I did not have anything to do with that and I was not asked and I came and said it anyway. And the pail you gave me has not come back and I have not been paid, and I am not asking, and I am not going to be thanked.**"

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was thanked, about twenty-five people were in that yard, and the man from the north row with the cough said nothing at all after he had said the word, and the fen entered that he said nothing and entered that nobody in that yard took the word off him and entered that nobody was asked to.

Then one of the twenty-five asked him afterwards, in the ordinary way, on the way to the gate, what the word was, and he said no in one word, and the count of that is three.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the twenty-first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **about twenty-five people heard a man refuse a word and a man of about thirty-five gave the method and not a definition. One of the twenty-five asked him afterwards, in the ordinary way, what the word was, and he said no in one word. The count of that refusal is three. The first of the three was on the twenty-third of last month at the wall side of this holding's own low ground and this holding was in the room for it. The second is a person this body has never met and is not going to write to. The third was this morning. Two of the three are not in this book and the fen entered that and is not going to make them three.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the word was used once this morning in this yard by a person who has read no ground, and the fen has entered the use and the refusal on the same line and has entered that the word is still not a name for anything on any page of this book, and that a word said in a yard by a man who did not mean to say it is a word somebody has, and not a word anybody has got a right to.**

Then the reader of this body asked the one question in that yard that nobody else could ask, and he gave the reason for it before he asked it, which is that he did not ask it in a book and did not ask it in a room with the doors shut.

"**Is the refusal about the word, or is it about the counting. And I am asking that in front of twenty-five people on purpose, because a man who will not name a thing may be protecting that thing, or he may be protecting himself, and this holding has to know which of those two it is before it writes his refusal down in its own book as a virtue.**"

And the man of about thirty-five answered in about four sentences, standing at the wall side, and the second sentence is the whole of what the answer is.

"**Both, and I will not separate them for you, and you may write down that I would not separate them, which is the only part of it that is about me.**"

"**If it is about the counting then I am a man with a method and you have got something to argue with and you will be arguing with me in about four years and I will still be here. If it is about the thing then I am a man who has decided that a body of sixteen households on one head is not the place a name goes, and that is a decision and you are entitled to have it against me and you are not entitled to have it as my good side.**"

"**And I have done both of those at different times on different ground, and I am not going to hand either of you a clean one this morning because you asked in front of twenty-five people.**"

And then the fourth man in that yard that day was a man who had come up the fen road on foot and had not come for the reading, and he had been at the wall side on the other side of the yard for about an hour, and he said it once, and it was about nine sentences, and he said the first of it before he said his name.

"**I am nobody's guide and I am not here as a representative of anything and I have no paper, and I would like the clerk to enter that in a yard of twenty-five people rather than in a book four years on, because in about four years somebody is going to write down that a man from up the coast road was in a yard and everybody in this room will read that as somebody sent.**"

"**There are Assemblies in the Thornwild. That is what they are called and it is not a rank and none of them has ever been given one, and I have been in two of them and I have been turned out of one, and I am telling you that they exist because about four years ago a body like yours would have written down the word wild and left it at that.**"

"**And I heard the word that was said this morning and I am not going to give it to you either, and my reason is not his reason and I am not going to pretend it is. He has read four pieces of ground. I have never read one. If I say the word, I am a man in a yard using a word a man got off a man, and that is worse than saying nothing, and I would rather be a man in a yard who has said nothing.**"

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the twenty-first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **two men in one yard refused the same word on the same day, and neither of them gave the word, and the reasons are not the same and the refusals are the same. This is the first time in this book that two people have independently arrived at a refusal about the same thing, and the fen entered that and entered that the man of about thirty-five was asked whether his refusal is about the word or about the counting and answered that it is both and that he would not separate them and that the clerk might write down that he would not separate them, which is the only part of it that is about him.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: a man of the coast road named the Assemblies of the Thornwild in this yard for the first time in this book's history and said that none of them is a rank and that he has been in two and turned out of one and has no paper and is nobody's representative. He was not thanked and did not ask to be. Neither man was thanked. The word is not on any page of this book and nothing in this book is going to put it there.**

And then, at about the first hour of the afternoon, in the same yard, with about twenty-five people in it and two men of about thirty-five gone off the road in two directions and about nine people left, the reader of this body asked the count out loud, and nobody took it, and the fen entered that nobody took it and entered why.

"**There is a count in this book of the questions that have been put in a room and not taken, and it is at six, and it has been at six since the first of this month, and it goes up when a question is put and not when a figure goes on a table, and I am putting one in this yard in about four minutes and I would like somebody to have it.**"

"**Whose order is it.**"

Nobody answered him. About nine people were in that yard and about sixteen had gone, and the man of about fifty stood about four feet away with his hand on the back of the bench, and the fen entered that he did not answer and entered that the clerk did not answer and entered that the man of the north row with the cough, who had said a word in that yard an hour before, did not answer either.

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the reader of this body asked the count of the questions available to a room in this holding and not taken to go up in a yard of about twenty-five, and put the question whose order it is. Nobody took it. The count is at six and it was at six on the first of this month and it is at six this afternoon, and it is one count and it is not two, and the fen entered that the reader asked for somebody to have it and nobody had it and entered that the man who had said a word in that yard an hour before did not take it either.**

And so the twenty-first of this month went down with the word in a yard and off the page, and a man of about thirty-five said the checkable half of what he has got for a third time in front of sixteen people who had not heard it twice, and a man from the coast road said a thing about Assemblies that nobody in this holding has ever had to hold before, and nobody in that yard of about twenty-five thanked anybody, **and the fen entered on the afternoon of the twenty-first that a man who is getting more willing is not becoming tame, and that the difference between those two things is the whole of what a holding like this one is going to have to learn how to do without him, and that the fen is not going to make a column out of that and is not going to improve on it.**

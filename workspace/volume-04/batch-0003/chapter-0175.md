# Chapter 175

## They Do Not Want It Drawn

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Tuesday, and a man came up the fen road from the low quarter on foot at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and he did not stop at the gate, and about nine people were in the seed house yard because it is a Tuesday and the yard is where anything said out loud gets entered, and the fen entered the hour before anything else was entered on that day.

He was let in by the man of about fifty, who did not ask him a single question on the road and did not walk the two hundred yards with him and did not say anything at all on the way up, and the fen entered the not asking and entered the not walking, and entered that on the twenty-second of this month the same man walked the same two hundred yards with a woman from that quarter, and entered that he gave no reason today and was not asked for one.

The person who keeps the seed house said what he was, standing up, before anybody read anything and before anybody asked him anything, in about two sentences, and she said it in the ordinary voice and did not say his name and there was no name to say.

"**This man has come up nine miles on foot to say a thing and he is not of this body and he is not on anything and he is not a representative of anything, and I am saying that in a yard before he opens his mouth so that nobody in this yard has to be told it afterward.**"

And the man said the same thing himself, in his first four words, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it, and the clerk entered the four words and entered that he had said them before anybody had put anything to him.

"**I speak for it and for nobody here.**"

Nobody asked him anything else for about a minute, and then the man of about fifty read the standing figures of the day in the ordinary way with the hour on them and did not soften one of them, and the leak at three-quarters of an inch and the beat not logged since with the column open and the seventh column at thirty-three and the use log at fifteen lines and the form on a bench with the answer not given were all read out, and the man stood at the wall side and let it be read.

Then he said it, and it is about five sentences, and it is the first refusal in this book's history that has come from the thing itself, and it came in the ordinary voice and at no point did he raise it.

"**We know what you did on the floor over the shaft. We know the man came down on the twenty-eighth of last month and we know the pail went out and we know it did not come back, and we are not writing to you about it and this is not a complaint. We are here about the water under the row, and the answer is that it is not to be drawn from, and that is the whole of the answer and it is not going to be longer.**"

"**It is not water and it is not seeds and it is the sort of work that stops if nobody does it, and you have a man who has said that out loud three times and he is right, and the drawing is the stopping.**"

"**We are not asking you for anything. We are telling you, so that the telling is ours and not yours, and we would like the clerk to enter that we came.**"

About nine people were in that yard and nobody said anything for about four seconds and the fen entered the four seconds and entered that it did not improve on them, and then the reader of this body asked the one question in that yard that nobody else could ask, and he gave the reason for it first, and he gave it in the open and in daylight and in front of the man it was aimed at.

"**How many of you.** And I am asking that out loud, and I am telling you first why I am asking it, which is that this holding is sixteen households on one head and has no way of finding out anything about that quarter at all, and a refusal that comes up a road with no number on it is the one kind of thing this book has never been able to hold, and I would rather ask a man to his face in my own yard than write to an office about you and be told I have no standing.**"

"**And I am not going to ask it twice and I am not going to ask it in a room, and if you would rather give it to the clerk than to me then the clerk is standing about four feet away with a page and you are welcome to do that instead.**"

And the man gave a figure, in his own words, and he gave it in about five sentences, and it was not a count of houses and it was not a count of people and it was not anything this holding has a column for.

"**Eleven. And that is the number of us who came to it, and I am not going to tell you the number of us, and I am not going to tell you the number of houses on that row, and I am not going to tell you how many of us there are the way you would count it.**"

"**If you write eleven in that book then in about four years somebody is going to go down that road to find eleven people, and there are not eleven people to find, because eleven is not a number of people. It is a number of the ones who came to the decision. I do not know the rest and I am not the one who counts the rest and nobody is.**"

"**You can check it. You cannot check it now. Those are two different sentences and I have watched a body like yours go wrong on one of them about twice in my life, and both times it was the second one it went wrong on.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is thirty-five this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **a man came up the fen road from the low quarter on foot and did not stop at the gate. The person who keeps the seed house said in the yard, before he spoke, that he is not of this body and is not a representative of anything. He said in his first four words: I speak for it and for nobody here. The clerk entered the four words and entered that he said them before anybody had put anything to him. He was not thanked and did not ask to be.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the refusal. The water under the row is not to be drawn from. It is not water and it is not seeds and it is the sort of work that stops if nobody does it, and a man of about thirty-five has said that out loud three times, and the drawing is the stopping. It is not a complaint. It is not a request. He asked that the clerk enter that he came, and the clerk entered that he came.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the figure given is eleven, in the man's own words, and it is the number of the ones who came to the decision. It is not a count of houses, not a count of people, and not a count of anything this body has a column for. The reader of this body asked the only question in that yard that nobody else could ask and gave the reason for it first, out loud, in daylight, in front of the man. The fen entered the figure and entered that it is a figure told to a yard and not counted by this body, and entered that those two are not the same and that this holding is not going to pretend that they are.**

And then, and this is the whole of that afternoon, this holding could not do one single thing about any of it, and the fen entered the five of them separately and gave the reason for the fifth.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing it cannot do: **one, this holding cannot ask anybody about that refusal. Two, it cannot write to anybody about it. Three, it has no standing in that quarter and has never had and has no form on which a quarter can be asked anything. Four, the office nine miles off has a form for a licensed supply and does not have a form for a thing that will not be drawn from, and this body has looked for one twice this month and did not find it and did not write and ask.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, the fifth: the man of about thirty-five is up a slope or nine miles off and is not a party to a quarter, and has said he will not be asked again by this body, and this holding has kept that. The holder of record of the Pans well is eleven miles off and is not a party to a quarter and does not come to one. And this body has no instrument in a shed that can be put on the thing under that row, and a man who can read that ground without one, and has not put the two together.**

And then the reader of this body said the thing about the form on the bench, and he said it in the open, and he said it because the man in that yard had said the drawing is the stopping and there was a piece of paper in the seed house on a bench that would also stop a drawing, and about nine people were in the yard and it was not said quietly.

"**There is a form on a bench in that room. It is a correct form. A holder of record carried it up this road in the rain a month ago and asked this body to certify on it that a flow is broken, and if this body puts its name to it then the licensed draw at that well stops, and that draw is the only water going into that quarter.**"

"**And I do not know what the draw at that well has to do with a thing under a row of houses nine miles off. I have not worked that out. I am not going to work it out in a yard this afternoon with a man standing in it who has just told us a thing is not to be drawn from, because if I start now I will be doing it to a man and not to a question.**"

"**So the form is on the bench and the answer is not given and it is not going to be given this month. The day it is given, a holder of record will have an answer from a body of sixteen households with no charter, and he will be able to take that answer to an office, and I have thought about that about four times in a month and I have not stopped him, and I would like the clerk to enter that I have not stopped him.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The man at the wall side did not go back down the road until about the seventh hour and stood at the gate for a while and did not say anything else, and the fen entered that he said nothing else and entered that nobody followed him to the gate and entered why, which is one sentence: **a man who has said a thing is not to be drawn from and has come nine miles to say it does not get walked to the gate by a yard that has learned something about consent this month.**

And then there was a question in that yard that nobody took, and the fen entered it, and the fen did not put it in, and nobody in that yard was asked whether they had an answer to it.

"**Whose order is it.**"

Nobody answered. About nine people were in that yard and the man of the north row with the cough was at the back of it and the man of about fifty was about four feet away with his hand on the bench and the clerk did not answer and the man who had said that question in a yard of about twenty-five on the twenty-first, four days ago, was not in it, and the fen entered that he was not in it and entered that the fen did not write to him about it and entered that this is not a fifth not-asking, because a not-asking is a thing this body could have done and did not do, and this body cannot ask anybody at all, which is a different trouble and is entered as a different trouble.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the reader of this body put the question whose order it is into a yard of about nine people on the twenty-seventh of this month, and nobody took it, and he did not ask it twice. The count of the times this body has asked it is two and neither has been answered. The count of the times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is six, and three of those six are this question, and both counts stand and neither has been added to the other.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: nobody in that yard asked who told them. The fen entered that nobody asked and entered the reason, which is that the question has been asked twice and answered never, and that asking it a third time in front of a man who has just said no to something would be a way of not having heard him, and that this holding would rather be recorded as a body that did not hear him properly than as a body that heard him and then went on with its own question.**

And so the twenty-seventh of this month went down, and a man walked nine miles to say that a thing under a row of houses is not to be drawn from, and gave a figure that is not a number of anybody, and the fen entered it, and **could not ask one question about it, could not write to one person about it, could not answer it and could not put the form on the bench, and the count of the things this body does not know is four and was four a month ago and this holding has learned a fifth thing it cannot do and is not going to call it a fifth of those, because the four are things it does not know and this one is a thing it cannot ask about, and those are two different troubles and a body that puts them in one column is a body that will solve the wrong one in about four years.**

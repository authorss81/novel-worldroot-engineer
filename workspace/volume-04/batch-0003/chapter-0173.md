# Chapter 173

## Four Pairs Of Hands

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Saturday, and at about the fourth hour of the morning, in the low ground four hundred yards below the north gate, with a lamp on the housing and about four feet of grass and a slope, a crew of four went onto the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground with a licensed man holding the book, and the engineer of record went onto the boards with them, and the reader of this body watched from the top of the slope and was not on the boards and was not asked to be.

The work was ordinary work. The boards were to come up and the shaft under them is about two hundred years old and there is silt in the bottom of it that has not been moved in a working lifetime, and the four of them were going to shore two timbers and clear the silt to a depth that a man had written on a piece of paper on the nineteenth, and the licensed man held the book and the hour on it and did not write down what any of them said to each other between the fourth hour and the sixth.

And at about half past four, the engineer of record held a pattern for the four of them for about two hours.

For about three-quarters of an hour before that, the four of them had been at it one at a time, and the man of the north row with the cough was on the boards and the young man of twenty with his dog was on the boards and a woman of a household in this fen was on the boards and the man from the second house was on the boards, and the licensed man stood at the head of the shaft with the book open and the hour on it, and the reader of this body stood at the top of the slope and could hear all four of them and could not see one of them, and the reason he went up the slope instead of down is that he had been told on the nineteenth that a body that stands over a crew is a body the crew talks to, and this holding has four people on a floor and no custom for it.

The three-quarters of an hour was not bad work and it was not good work. It was one man in a hole with a shovel and three people on the boards passing a bucket, and the bucket went up and came down and went up and came down, and the licensed man wrote the hour and did not write the number of buckets, because a bucket is not a unit and the licensed man has said so out loud in this book before.

Nobody in this book has the name for what happened at about half past four, and the reader of this body asked the licensed man for one on the twentieth and the licensed man said that there was a word in a book four hundred miles off in a room with a lock and that the word was not coming down that road, and the fen entered both of those on the twentieth and did not improve on either.

It was not a draw. It is not a return. It is a hold, and the fen entered the three of those separately, in that order, in a book, at about the seventh hour, with about nine people on the slope.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a work: **a hold was held at about the half hour past the fourth hour of this morning at the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, by the engineer of record, for a crew of four, for about two hours, with a licensed man holding the book and the hour on it.** No water was moved out of anywhere. The level in the shaft before and after differs by about the width of a hand, **and that figure is entered because it was taken and not because it is worth knowing, and the fen entered that the licensed man took it and entered that the fen does not know what it is worth and has not asked.**
>
> **A hold is not a draw and a hold is not a return. A hold is a thing a man does with his own body while four other people work.**

And then the line that had to be said, and the reader of this body said it at the top of the slope at about the fifth hour with about nine people there, and he said the second half of it before anybody could get to the first half.

"**It is not a graft. It is not an anchor. There is no anchor in this building and there has not been one since the last volume and the case is closed on that and I am not going to have it opened again this month by anybody, including me.**"

"**There is no link and no union and no return. Nothing living was touched. No seed, no scion, no bead, nothing anybody in a book four hundred miles off would call a thing that goes into the ground. I put a shape of ordinary work into my own body and I held it there while four other people did that work in step instead of one at a time, and the only part of this that is not a thing a man could do with his voice is the part where four people could not get out of step.**"

"**And I am going to say that before somebody asks me how long it lasts, and it is a morning, and it was two hours, and it cost me a day and a night, and if anybody in this yard wants to know whether it is worth it they should ask the four men on those boards and not me.**"

And then it was said again afterward, at about the seventh hour, in a yard of about nine, and the fen entered that it was said twice and entered that the second time was shorter and that a thing said shorter the second time is a thing the speaker is not sure of.

The four of them came up the slope at about the sixth hour, and two of them said out loud, in the ordinary way, that it was the difference between working and not working, and one of them used the phrase four times, and two of them said nothing at all about it and were not asked and did not volunteer it.

The man of about fifty was sitting on the bench at the top of the slope when they came up and he got up off it before any of them reached him, which is a thing he does and which he has never once been thanked for, and the fen entered that he got up off it and entered that the count of times he has got up off that bench for a crew coming off a floor is now, by the fen's own count, one more than the number of times anybody has noticed.

> Entered on the same line, in the clerk's own hand, read back: **two of the crew of four said out loud that the work went four times as fast with the pattern held as it had gone at the fourth hour without it. Two of them said nothing.** The fen entered that the two who said so were the two who had done the most and were not the two who were the strongest, and entered that it did not improve on that observation and is not going to, **and entered that a man of about fifty watched all four of them come up a slope and could not tell from the slope which two had done the most, and entered that he has watched this crew for eleven years and could not tell before today either, and entered that he does not know what that means and did not write and ask.**

And then, in the seed house, at about the seventh hour, the person who keeps the seed house asked who loses, and it is her standing and the count of that standing is five in a season, and the man of about fifty did not answer it and the fen entered that he did not answer it and entered that nobody else answered it.

"**I am not going to answer that one and I am going to say why in the room, because I have had three in a year and I could not finish any of the three, and I would rather be quiet on the fourth than finish it badly.**"

"**What I can say is that four men got off a floor two hours earlier than they would have and one man went to bed at four in the afternoon, and both of those are in this book, and I do not know which of the two is the cost, and I am not going to stand in a room with eleven people in it and pick one out of the two so that the room can go home.**"

Nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be, and the count of that standing in a season is fifteen, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint and is not going to be read as a fault.

---

And then the cost, and it went into the book in a man's own hand in a line of its own, and it is the first time in three volumes that this holding has had a name for this cost and the man gave the name himself.

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the engineer of record, in his own hand, in a line of its own, and read back by the clerk and not improved:
>
> **A headache behind the eyes, from about the sixth hour this morning until about the fourth hour this afternoon, which is not the mark and is not a mark and is not to be entered as one. A bad night, of which I have had three in nine years. And a temper.**
>
> **The temper. Between the sixth hour and the seventh hour this evening I was short with three people in this room and it was not about any of them. I have had this three times in three volumes and I have not given it a name until this month, and I am writing it down because a cost with no name in a book is a cost the next man pays in a room he does not know he is standing in. Nobody in that room did anything wrong. I have not been able to tell anybody in that room which of them it was about, and I have stopped trying, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to be talked out of it.**
>
> **The hand from the wrist down was not much use for the rest of that day. That is the fourth account of this arm in this book and it does not agree with the other three and it does not reconcile them, and the fen entered all four and entered that it is not going to reconcile them.**

> Entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: **the temper happened in a room and was not about anybody in the room. This is the third cost of that kind in three volumes and the first one this holding has had a name for, and the name is in the engineer's own hand and not in mine, and I have not improved on it and am not going to.**

And then, at about the ninth hour of the evening, in a yard of about nine, the question was asked, and it is the question this movement turns on, and nobody in that yard could answer it except one man, and the man who answered it had known the answer since the second hour and had worked for two hours anyway.

Somebody asked it. It was asked by the man of about fifty, in a yard of about nine, in the ordinary voice, and it is one sentence, and the fen entered that he asked it and entered that he had not written it down first and entered the reason he gave for that, which is that a question he has written down is a question he has decided to ask and he had not decided to ask it.

"**Who else on this head can do that.**"

"**Nobody.**"

"**And I have known that since the second hour this morning and I have been working for two hours anyway, and I would like the clerk to enter that in that order and not the other way round, because the other way round makes me into a man who found out at six o'clock and that is not what happened and this book has had enough of me finding things out at six o'clock.**"

"**I am not going to put that in a book tonight. I am going to put it in a book on the twenty-sixth, in a yard, with the reader of this body standing in it, and you are all going to be in it, and I am telling you the day now so that none of you can have it later.**"

Nobody thanked him and nobody was thanked, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is thirty-three this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the tenth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a stage: **the engineer of record of this holding held a pattern for four other people for about two hours this morning, and it is the first time he has done that, and the question of who else on this head can do it was asked in a yard of about nine and was answered in one word.** The fen entered the answer and entered that the man who gave it had known it since the second hour of the morning and worked for two hours anyway, and entered that he asked for the order to be put in as the second hour and not the sixth and that the request was granted.
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's own words, read back: this is a stage and it is a stage about a body and not about an instrument, and the fen is entering it in this book as a defect. **A man who is the only person who can do a thing is not a better man than he was on Friday. He is a man with one more thing that stops working on Saturday, and this holding has no schedule and no rule about what happens on the Sunday, and the whole of what this holding has got is one man and a pattern and a case in a cart shed.** Nobody thanked anybody and the fen entered that the count of times a thing has been said in a yard and not been thanked in this month is now four, and the fen does not know what four is and did not write and ask.**

And so the twenty-fourth of this month was a Saturday, and a crew of four cleared silt out of a two-hundred-year-old shaft at four times the rate they had done it at, and a man paid for it with a day of his hand and a night of no sleep and a temper he has named in his own hand in a book, and the answer to the only question anybody asked about it was one word long, and that word was **nobody**, and the fen entered it as a defect and not as an achievement, and did not improve on it, and did not add a fifth line.

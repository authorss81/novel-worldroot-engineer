# Chapter 174

## A Bottleneck Is Not A System

The twenty-fifth of this month was a Sunday, and the seed house had the bay doors shut and about nine people in it, and the man of about fifty came down at about the tenth hour of the morning to write the entry for the next day, and he did not ask anybody anything while he wrote it.

The engineer of record was in the chair by the south wall with his left forearm on the table and his right hand in a cloth that had been a clean shirt on Friday, and the hand from the wrist down had not been much use for the rest of the twenty-fourth and was better on the twenty-fifth and worse again in the evening, and the man of about fifty looked at it once and then wrote his entry and did not look at it again. He was asked about it afterward, in the ordinary way, in front of about nine people, and he said that he had nothing to add to what was on the page, and he was not asked a second time, and the clerk entered that and entered the reason, which is one sentence: **a man who has put a thing in a book four times does not owe a fifth answer to a room, and a room that keeps asking is a room that is looking for something to be able to use.**

The seedwright of the trust was in that room and had come up the fen road on the Saturday, and she said in about four sentences why, which was that she had a fortnight in the district and had spent two days of it at Fennmere with nobody's book open. She slept on the bench in the cart shed, which the man of the north row with the cough had put there a month ago and told nobody about, and the fen entered that nobody thanked him for it and that he was not told who was going to sleep on it.

**And the ask was not made on the Sunday.** It was made on the twenty-sixth, in a yard, in front of about nine people, and the fen entered that the reader of this body has asked four questions in rooms with the doors shut in this book's history and three of the four were about figures, and that he was not going to ask the fifth in a room with the doors shut.

---

The twenty-sixth of this month was a Monday, and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, which is the hour the work was done on the Saturday, and he came at that hour and had not been asked to come at that hour and came at it anyway, about nine people were in the yard at the north gate, and the reader of this body read the entry out of the book in the ordinary voice and the clerk entered the hour and nobody was asked to stop him.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the engineer of record, in his own hand, in a line of its own, and read back by the clerk and not improved: **a pattern was held for a crew of four at the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, at about the half hour past the fourth hour of that morning, for about two hours, with a licensed man holding the book. No water was moved out of anywhere. The level in the shaft before and after differs by about the width of a hand. A hold is not a draw and a hold is not a return.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: who else on this head can do it was asked in a yard of about nine and was answered in one word, and the answer was nobody, and the man who gave it had known it since the second hour of the morning and worked for two hours anyway, and asked that the order be put in as the second hour and not the sixth, and the request was granted.**
>
> Entered by the clerk on the same day and read back: **the use log stands at fifteen lines and stands at fifteen lines because a line records a contact and never a reading, a draw gets no line, a hold gets no line either, and this holding cannot write down what happened on the twenty-fourth in a line that anybody could check.**

And then the reader of this body said the sentence, standing up in a yard of about nine people, in the ordinary words, and he did not build up to it, and the clerk was one of the nine and wrote it and read it back and did not improve it.

"**A man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system.** I am going to say the engineering half of that in this yard and I am not going to say the other half, and I want the clerk to enter that I said so, because a number put against a feeling is how a book ends up with a figure in it that somebody in four years will read as a measurement.**"

"**Here is the half I will say. A bottleneck is a man. A system is four people and a schedule and a rule about what happens when the man is asleep. This head has got a man and a pattern and no schedule and no rule. And the difference between those two things is not a matter of character, and anybody in this yard who thinks it is a character has not been up a slope at six in the morning.**"

"**The difference is four more people who can be taught, and one day of not being able to, and a day of not being able to is a day this head has never had and has now had once, and on that day the four men on those boards had a licensed man and a book and a man with a hand that could not hold anything.**"

And then the reader of this body entered the whole of it, and entered it on purpose and said so, and about nine people were in that yard while he did it.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a work: **a man held a pattern for four other people on the twenty-fourth of this month for about two hours at about the half hour past the fourth hour, at the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, and it is the first time he has done that. Nobody else on this head can do it. The count of people in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is one.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: a system is four people and a schedule and a rule about what happens when the man is asleep, and this head has a man and a pattern and no schedule and no rule, and the difference between those two is not a matter of character. And the fen entered that he was asked, on the twenty-second of last month, by a person in a room, to be told what a thing cannot be checked against, and that he said he had nothing yet, and that a body of sixteen households with no charter has no rule about the morning after a night like Saturday's, and that the whole of that rule would be one line, and that this holding has not written it and is not going to be pushed into writing it by a defect.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is thirty-four this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

Then the ask, and it was made in the ordinary way and without any preamble at all, and it was made of the seedwright of the trust, and she was standing at the wall side of that yard, and about nine people heard it.

"**Will you be the person the engineer of record comes to when he cannot hold it alone and has not got anybody else.**"

And she refused in one sentence, in the ordinary voice, without softening it and without a preamble of her own, and she did not number it.

"**No, and I will not be the person a man comes to when he cannot do a thing, because a person you come to when you cannot do a thing is not a person you come to, it is the last place a thing goes.**"

Nobody said anything. The man of the north row with the cough looked at the ground. The engineer of record did not answer her, the reader of this body did not answer her, the person who keeps the seed house did not answer her, and about nine people stood in a yard while a refusal stood in it.

And then she said the half of it that she had thought about before she came up the road, and she said it before anybody thanked her for anything, and the fen entered that she said it before she was thanked.

"**You are not going to thank me for this and I would like it entered that I said so before I was thanked, because in about four years somebody is going to read that page and see a woman refusing a man in a yard and start putting a shape on it, and there is no shape on it. I said no because the job is wrong. I would say no on a Tuesday with nobody in the yard and I would say it faster.**"

"**And I am not going to be the anchor for anybody, and I would like the clerk to enter that I used that word in a yard of nine people and that I have not been asked about it by anybody in this building in eleven years, and that nobody in this yard is going to be asked about it today either.**"

And the clerk entered both of those and read them back and did not improve them, and the fen entered the whole of what she said on one line, and entered on the same line what was not in it.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a refusal: **the seedwright of the trust was asked, in the ordinary way and without preamble, to be the person the engineer of record comes to when he cannot hold it alone and has not got anybody else, and refused in one sentence. She was not thanked and she said before she was thanked that she was not going to be.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: this is a refusal and not an arrangement. Nothing was set up. No anchor was offered, no anchor was accepted, and nothing was prepared. This holding has no anchor and has not had one since the last volume and did not have one in a yard where somebody was asked whether they would be one. The count of that standing in this building in a season is six, of which five were about figures. The fen entered that she did not number it and has not numbered it for her.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the reader of this body's words, read back: the engineer of record did not answer the refusal, nobody else answered it, and the time between the question and the end of the yard was not long. The fen did not put a number on it and entered why: a number is a thing a person can be defended with later, and this one is not that, and the last time this holding put a number on a silence it was nine seconds and the number did not do one thing for anybody who heard it.**

And then the reader of this body asked her one more question, and it was not about the rule and not about the anchor, and he gave the reason for asking it before he asked it.

"**Is there anybody in this fen who could be taught. And I am asking that in a yard and not in a room, and I am asking it in front of nine people on purpose, because the answer is either yes or no and both of them are better in the open.**"

She said yes, and she gave one condition, and the condition was not a favour, and the fen entered that it was a second refusal and entered the count of the two.

"**Yes, and here is the condition and it is not a favour and it is not a favour for me either. Whoever it is has to be asked by somebody who is not you, in a yard, out loud, on a day of their own choosing, and has to be allowed to say no in front of the same nine people, and the first time and the second time. And you do not get to be in the room when it is asked, and you do not get to be told the answer before the asking is finished.**"

"**That is the whole of what I have got. I am not going to name them in this yard and I would like the clerk to enter that I was not asked to and that I would have said no to naming them anyway, and that a person is not a resource in a book of this kind and I have watched a book of this kind be wrong about that twice.**"

Nobody answered that either. The man of about fifty put his hand flat on the top of a bench he had got up off four days ago, and the fen entered that nobody saw him do it.

And then the fen entered the thing the chapter is about, and it entered it on purpose, and it entered the person who wrote the rule first and the person being asked second, because the other order is the order that makes it look like a coincidence.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-sixth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a rule: **the standing rule about the engineer of record is four lines and every one of the four is about a stop. It was written into the seed-route ledger on the fourteenth of a month four months back by the seedwright of the trust, in the ordinary words, and it is the first rule this body has ever written about the engineer of record instead of about the ground. The person who wrote it is the person who was asked today whether she would be the one a bottleneck comes to.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: this is the fifth time this holding has been told something by a rule of its own. It is the first time it has been told by a rule it wrote about a man who was standing in the yard. The reader of this body is the reason the appointment on the twenty-sixth is in a book at all, because a man said in a yard on the twenty-fourth that he would put it in a book on a day he named out loud, and a day that is named out loud in a yard is a day this body cannot now move. The fen entered that it would have been easier in every way if the man had not said it, and entered that a body which is easier to run is not the same as a body that is being run properly.**

And then the change, and it is the change and not an improvement, and the fen entered it and entered that the fen had not seen it coming.

> Entered on the same line: **the fault line in this body used to be between two people and it was about trust. It is between a body and a job, and it is about authority, and it is open in a yard of about nine people in daylight instead of in a room with the doors shut. The refusal bought nothing and stopped nothing. It is the standing and not an exception to it, and a man who is the only person in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is going to be that at four in the morning on a day nobody in this body can name, and there is nothing in this room that touches that.**

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked. And so the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth went down, and a man with a hand in a cloth was asked about the hand once and gave nothing, and a rule written by a woman on the fourteenth of a month four months back was read back against her in a yard of nine by the man who writes things down, and she refused in one sentence and gave a condition that is a second refusal and refused to name a person, and the engineer of record did not answer her, **and the fen entered that a system is a thing that is still in the book on the first day of next month, and this body has one man and a pattern and a bench in a cart shed and nothing else.**

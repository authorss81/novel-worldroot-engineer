# Chapter 168

## The Sixteenth

The fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month, which was a Friday, four hundred miles off, on a shelf above a road that this holding has never laid, in the yard of the fourth house at Cauldron Reach, a woman of fifty-four read her own well with her own nail and her own string and wrote it on a wall in her own hand, and it is the ninth reading on that wall, and the count of that series is settled and this body is not going to re-date one line of it.

**Five feet.**

It is in the plainest words anybody has used about it and it is how she said it and not how this book would.

"**Five feet. Not five feet and an inch and not five feet and a half. Five feet, on the string, this morning, at the fourth hour, and the chalk is the same chalk.**"

"**I have read this well the same way since the flood and I am not going to start reading it a different way because the number has come out round. A round number is still a number that a nail and a string put there, and a man who wants an inch in it is a man who wants a reason on my wall.**"

And then she said the second half, and she said it standing at the wall with the chalk in her hand, and the second half is the reason she is going to say it out loud in a yard where three other people are standing.

"**Nine feet, and five, and the four in between has been every figure there is. I have had four inches and I have had a half and I have had a half again and I have had nothing at all, and every one of them was a number a person says out loud in a yard and then stops.**"

"**And this morning it is a round one, and I am going to write it on a wall, and I am not going to write what it is.**"

Nobody answered her. The three of them did the ordinary things that four people do in a yard at the fourth hour on a Friday, and those things are the whole of the rest of that day on that shelf, and one of the three has written all of it down since and two of them have not and neither of those two is a criticism.

The string goes down the shaft on a nail and comes up wet on a nail, and it has been wet at four in the morning on that shelf for as long as there has been a fourth house on it, and she wrings it out over the bucket and puts the bucket outside the wall and comes back in and does not look at the other three while she does it. **There is a mark on the wall at about the height of her hand where the bucket has been set down every morning for thirty-one years, and the mark is not in any book, and she has never once scraped it out.**

The one who has written it down is the one at the second house, and he has a sheet and he wrote it at about the fifth hour while the string was still drying, and he wrote what she said and not what he thought of it, and the two who have not written anything down have not written anything down for eleven years between them, and one of those two does not write and one of those two does not read, and both of those are standing.

Then the man with the spade put the spade against the south wall and said the only thing he said that morning, which was that the grass was cut, and the occupier said that it was always cut on the sixteenth, and neither of them said anything else and neither of the other two said anything either, and that is the whole of the conversation of that yard on that Friday and it is not entered here as a thing because it is not a thing.

The wall is on the north side of the yard under the eaves, and it is a wall of coursed stone about eight feet long, and the writing on it is chalk, and it has been washed off twice in thirty-one years and rewritten both times, and it is in the hand of the occupier and it is the only record of that well that has ever existed on that shelf and it is not in anybody's book. There is one more line on it this month than there was on it last month, and the line is this one.

> Copied from a wall at Cauldron Reach on the sixteenth of this month by the occupier, at her own hand, in the order it is on the wall, which is the order the days came. The middle of it is written the way that yard has always written it and the way that yard's hand has always written it, and the words *the month after* and *of that month* are the occupier's and not this book's:
>
> **Sixteenth of a month four months back. Nine feet.**
> **Fourteenth of the month after. Five feet and a half.**
> **Sixteenth of the month after. Five feet five inches.**
> **Twentieth of that month. Five feet four inches.**
> **Thirtieth of that month. Five feet two inches.**
> **Sixteenth of the month after. Five feet three inches.**
> **Sixteenth of that month. Five feet and two inches.**
> **Sixteenth of last month. Five feet and one inch.**
> **Sixteenth of this month. Five feet.**
>
> **Under the last line, in the same chalk and the same hand, and the only sentence on that wall that is not a figure: I do not write what it is made of. A. Ollvar.**

Nine feet less five feet is four feet, and that is what a person writes on a wall and nothing else, and there is no entry anywhere on that wall or in any book in this world for that well coming back, and the half inch that stands in a month of last month is on the wall and is not carried forward into this morning's figure and is not to be added to it or taken off it or made to do any arithmetic at all, **and the fen entered on this body that it is not going to decompose four feet and has not decomposed it and is not going to.**

The grass along the south wall was cut on the sixteenth, the same as it is cut every ten days, and it was cut by the man with the spade who has used that tool on that ground for twenty-six years. The west wall had a stone put back into it in the middle of the month and nothing was done to it on the sixteenth. And there is a place in the stack against the north wall where a thing stood for thirty-one years and does not stand now, and the four of them have not agreed on a name for the place and have not agreed since the month before, and none of the four will say what the thing was, and this body has not asked and is not going to.

In the first row, which is the row of eleven faces, out of the collars of the trees that were let go on the tenth of a month two months back, the green that was cut off with a sickle on the twenty-fifth of that month and left lying on the ground where it was left was still lying on the ground, and there is a stake in the ground at each of two places in that row, because nine of the eleven faces in it were read with a rule and a lens on the twenty-fifth of that month and two were not read and were not guessed at, and the two stakes are where the two are.

And in the next row, which is the older row and the row nobody has read with a rule and a lens, there is green coming up out of the collars.

Three of the four have walked out and looked at it since the twenty-fifth of a month two months back. The fourth has not looked at it and said, once, in the plainest words available to him, that he was not going to look at it, and the three who did look did not say anything to him about that, and the fen does not know whether they said anything to him about it at all and is not going to write and ask.

**Nobody dug at it. Nobody weighed it. Nothing has come off that shelf in a vessel. Nobody on that shelf has said what it is, and nobody has said that the telling is still going on, and nobody has said that it has stopped, and the two of those are the only two sentences anybody on that shelf could have said that would have been a finding, and neither of them was said and neither of them is going to be.**

The day letter was on its stone on the sixteenth, and it is a thing four households use in turn and it goes back on the stone at the end of a day, and it is a stone by the well at the fourth house, and it had been on that stone since the evening of the seventeenth of last month, folded, with the chalk in the fold, and the eight days between the eighth and the seventeenth of last month are still not written on it, and one of the four reasons given for that has not been given to anybody since and the fourth reason was the man with the spade's and the fourth reason was three words and he would say nothing else about it and one of the four did not ask him twice.

That man had not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month, and nobody in that yard had asked him, and the fen has no way of checking one word of it because it is four hundred miles off and the thing was never in this book's book in the first place, because the letter is a thing the four of them have between them and not a thing anybody else may read on a day.

And there is a fourth line of that yard's account, and it is new this month, and this fen has not been told it and is four days late about it, and it is going down the four-day road in a bag with a sheet nobody has opened.

> Written on the sixteenth of this month at the fourth house, at the fourth hour, by the occupier, in her own hand, in four lines, and taken down that road at the ordinary cost of that road, which is the cost of a house with four people in it and is not anybody else's:
>
> **One. The reading is five feet and the shortfall against the ninth of a month four months back is four feet, and I have not written what the difference is made of and I am not going to.**
>
> **Two. The day letter is on the stone and the eight days from the eighth to the seventeenth of last month are not on it and are not going on it, and I am telling you that they are not and I am not going to tell you why not.**
>
> **Three. There is green in the older row. We have not dug at it and we have not weighed it and nothing has come off this shelf in a vessel, and nobody here has said what it is.**
>
> **Four. Between the eighth and the fifteenth of this month the well came up half an inch, and in the night before this morning it went down half an inch, and I have written the reading on the wall and I am not going to write what the two numbers are. And the man with the spade has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month and we have not asked him, and I am telling you because you are the only party we have that writes things down.**

Nobody on that shelf was thanked for any of it and nobody asked to be, and a house of four people that has put a woman of fifty-four and a nail and a wall in the ordinary course of a Friday morning is not a party to anything and does not want to be one, and the fen entered that and entered that it has looked at the word party on that sheet about nine times in a month and is not going to say the word back to them.

**And this holding last knew what happened on that shelf out of a sheet, and it was four days late then, and on the morning of the sixteenth it knew nothing at all of the four lines above, and it entered that it does not know what is on that shelf and does not know what it does not know, and the fen entered that it is four days late and has not written and asked and is not going to.**

---

And then at about the eleventh hour of the same day, at the north gate, in about nine people, the reader of this body said the handoff out loud, and it is four sentences and it is the only time in this batch that a thing four hundred miles off is named in a yard by a person who is not on that shelf, and the clerk entered the two dates and entered no figure against either of them.

"**The fourth hour on the sixteenth of this month, four hundred miles off, a nail and a string and a woman of fifty-four's own hand, in her own yard, on her own wall. That was a reading and the reading is in a book four hundred miles off and it is not in this one, and this holding has no standing on it, has never had, did not write and ask about the last one and is not going to write and ask about this one.**"

"**And the first of next month is a Saturday, and it is not this book's, and the fen entered that day because a day that can be counted is the only one of the two things this body is able to put in a book, and the other thing is a figure and the figure is four hundred miles off.**"

"**So there are two dates and no figure, and a body that has entered a date with nothing against it four times this season did it once on purpose in a room with four people in it and got told what a concealment is worth, and I am not going to enter a fifth one from a gate, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said that.**"

Nobody answered him. About nine people were in that yard and nobody was asked to answer him and the fen entered that nobody was asked, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is twenty-nine this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the twelfth hour of the noon of the sixteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a clock: **the ordinary reading of a well on a shelf four hundred miles off is taken by the occupier at the fourth hour on the sixteenth of every month and is not in this book. The reading of the sixteenth of this month is five feet. The shortfall against the ninth of a month four months back is four feet, and is not decomposed, and the half inch of the sixteenth of last month is on the wall and is not carried forward as a figure.** The next date this holding has entered for that shelf is the first of next month, which is a Saturday, **which is not this book's day, and the fen entered the day and not the figure, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and none is to be.**

And so the sixteenth of this month went the way the four hundred miles off let it go, at the fourth hour, with a nail and a string and a round number and a wall, **and there is no entry anywhere in this book for that well coming back, and there is a woman four hundred miles off who has told three people in a yard that she read it and wrote it down and does not know what it is for, and this holding is four days from finding out the rest of it, and it is not going to write and ask, and the fen entered that it is not going to and did not improve on it.**

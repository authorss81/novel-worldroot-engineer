# Chapter 177

## The Thirtieth

The thirtieth of this month was a Friday, and it is the last reading day of the tenth month, and at about the tenth hour of the morning the person who keeps the seed house read the door out whole at nine hundred yards off in a full yard of about twenty-five people, with the reader of this body on one side of her and the clerk on the other, and she did not read it fast and she did not skip a column, and the count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-four.

Nobody thanked anybody. A reading day has been read in a full yard five times this month, and the figures of a day that was not a reading day were read once in a yard of about nine, on the twenty-seventh, when a man came up nine miles with a refusal in it, and the fen entered that he did not ask for it to stop.

> **The compost line for the tenth month.** Not entered. The figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Saturday, and this holding does not know what it is. **The tenth month of a run of nine not discharged in nine.**
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of last month, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit. The reading of the thirteenth of a month two months back is five-eighths. **This body will not say whether the difference is a movement or a season.** No cause, no due date, and not measured since the twenty-first of last month and not to be measured again this month.
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. **Column open.** The rule's third line does not say in a yard, **this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule was used outside one of its own four lines on the twenty-second of last month, and that is entered, and the column was not opened today in front of twenty-five people and will not be.** The fen entered that the not opening is a decision and not an oversight.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-four.** The last reading day of this month is the thirtieth.
>
> **The boards, two hundred and thirty-four days, day minus sixty-six,** cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years. **The March's tin, two hundred and eighty-one days, day minus nineteen,** unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.** **Day minus sixty-six. Day minus nineteen. Read out loud by the clerk before either number, which he has now done on six reading days running and which nobody has asked for on any of the six.**
>
> **The standing rule.** Four lines, every one about a stop, satisfied on the third of a month four months back, **used once, on the twenty-second of last month, and not used once in this month.**
>
> **The use log at fifteen lines.** The fifteenth is the contact on the ninth of this month, with a licensed man, a plain wire gauge test written down first, the mark hot for a day and the hand no use for about a day. **And entered on the same standing entry and not in the log, because neither of them is a line: the gauge test on the boards on the twentieth, and the hold on the same boards on the twenty-fourth.** A line records a contact and never a reading. A draw gets no line. A hold gets no line. **The log stands at fifteen lines and the reason is that this holding cannot write down what happened on the twenty-fourth in a line that anybody could check.**
>
> **The rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is not longer than it was on the twenty-fourth of this month, there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow on the twenty-second of last month is not a mark. And on the same line: four accounts of the same arm in this book, none of which agrees with any of the others, and the fen does not reconcile them and is not going to.**
>
> **A form on a bench in the seed house, correct, filled in, not returned, not torn up, and the answer not given.**
>
> **And the well on the shelf.** Five feet at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month, by the occupier, by her own nail, in her own yard, on her own wall, four feet against the sixteenth of a month five months back, **and this body will not decompose that figure and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.** A day letter off its stone from the eighth to the seventeenth of last month with the eight days not written on it. A man with a spade who has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month and has not been asked. Four lines down a four-day road at that household's own cost, and two numbers in the fourth of them that this body did not add up. **And a row, and a wall, and a yard four hundred miles off, with no entry anywhere for any of the three either.**

And then, in the same yard, before the door was closed, the standing rule about the engineer of record was read out again, in full, four lines, by the clerk, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit, and about sixteen of the twenty-five had heard them before.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month four months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called, and there is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The fen entered that the rule was satisfied and that satisfaction is not permission, and that it has now been read out in full four times in this month and **the count of the uses is one**, and that the third line does not say in a yard, **that this body read it as though it did for two years, and that is entered, and the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and on Saturday a man held a pattern for four other people for two hours and every one of those four lines was satisfied the whole time and not one of them was about what he was doing.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing, the count of that request is thirty-seven this season, and the count is not a complaint.

And then the rest of the month, which is not on the door and is in the book, and the reader of this body read it out in the same yard, because a month is not closed by a column.

"**The things that came up a road this month. A man walked nine miles on Tuesday and said in this yard that a thing under a row of houses nine miles off is not to be drawn from, and that the drawing is the stopping, and that it is not a complaint and not a request, and he asked the clerk to enter that he came, and the clerk entered that he came. He gave a figure and it is eleven, and it is not a number of people and not a number of houses, and he said so himself.**"

"**A food, then. A woman walked nine miles on Thursday and said six sentences, and four houses on a row have been eating something since about the tenth of this month that they did not plant and did not buy and did not find, and one of them has been feeding it to a child, and it comes up in a strip about two paces wide, and nobody has been told they may not, and this holding did not go and look and entered four answers in a household's own hand at that household's cost and did not ask the price.**"

"**A man who is the only person in this fen who can hold a pattern for four. He said that out loud in a yard on Saturday on a day he named in advance, and the woman who wrote the four lines of the rule on the fourteenth of a month two months back was asked on Monday in a yard of nine whether she would be the person he comes to when he cannot hold it alone, and she refused in one sentence and gave a condition that is a second refusal, and he did not answer her and nobody else did.**"

"**A word used in a yard of about twenty-five on the twenty-first by a person who has read no ground in his life, and refused twice in the same yard by two men for two different reasons, and the word is not on any page of this book and is not going to be. And a man of about thirty-five who has refused four times in nineteen years and was not thanked for any of them.**"

"**And a name at the foot of a sheet in eleven miles, correct, and no address for the name, and this holding read it out loud in a yard once this month and did not ask the man whether he wanted it read out loud. And a refusal from a person who has refused five things in eleven years, and a form on a bench not answered, not returned and not torn up, and four pounds and fivepence in a drawer not to be opened this month.**"

Nobody thanked anybody for the reading of a month, and the person who keeps the seed house closed the door.

---

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon the man of about seventy was fetched, the fourteenth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was given the figures and the counts in that order, and he was not asked a question and was not thanked and was not explained.

He was given the compost line not entered, the leak with no verdict, the beat with its column open and not opened today, the seventh column at thirty-four, the boards and the tin, the use log at fifteen lines, the rootmark and the four accounts of the arm, the form on a bench, the well on the shelf at five feet, **and the name at the foot of a sheet in eleven miles with no address for it.**

He asked for the last one to be repeated. The clerk repeated it and the reader of this body said it again in the same words, and he listened to it twice and said four words.

"**Ask who is paying.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that it does not know what they were about and has entered that it is not going to write and ask, and entered that in fourteen times it has never twice been about the same thing.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the thirtieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the man of about seventy was fetched for the fourteenth time in a season, was given the figures and the counts in that order, asked for the last one to be repeated, and said four words: ask who is paying. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about. The fen entered that it does not know and is not going to write and ask.**

And then, at about the seventh hour of the evening, at the north gate, in about nine people, the handoff was read, and the day it names is not this book's.

"**The first of next month is a Saturday, and it is the day this holding's compost line for the tenth month falls on, and this holding does not know what that figure is, and the door has a column and a month and a figure and this body has no standing on any of the three.**"

"**The fen entered the day and not the figure, the way it entered the day and not the figure for a well four hundred miles off on the sixteenth, and the two entries are the same entry with two different things on the wrong side of it, and the fen entered that it is getting good at this and did not say so in a yard.**"

"**It did not write and ask. It is not going to. And the day is the first, and there is nothing else in this yard tonight that anybody is going to be able to carry, and I would like the clerk to enter that nine people heard that and that none of them said so much as one word.**"

Nobody said so much as one word. The man of about seventy went to the gate in his own time and the woman who does the sums went with him as far as the gate and not past it, and nobody has ever asked her why and the fen is not going to.

Underneath the month's work, dated the thirtieth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-sixth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Fifteen days, and a well on the dry road put back together at a man's own cost on his own ground under his own charter, and four correct lines, and the low quarter has water in it again and this holding does not know what it costs that quarter now. A nail and a string and a wall four hundred miles off, and a round number, and a woman who has said she read it and will not write what it is made of, and a fourth line on that wall that came four days late down a road nobody wrote and asked about. A word used once in a yard and refused twice in the same yard by two men, and not on any page of this book. Six sentences from a household's mouth and four answers on the back of a tally at that household's cost, about a strip two paces wide in front of four houses, and a child. Nine words about a return, and a refusal of the rest, and four in nineteen years, and not thanked.*

*And on Saturday a man held a shape of ordinary work in his own body for about two hours so that four other people could do four times what they had been doing, and it cost him a headache that is not the mark, a night of no sleep, and a temper he has named in his own hand in this book in a line of its own with the hour on it. And on Monday the woman who wrote the four lines of the rule was asked in a yard of nine whether she would be the person he comes to when he cannot hold it alone, and she said no in one sentence, and he did not answer her, and this holding has entered that a man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system. And on Tuesday a man walked nine miles to say that the drawing is the stopping, and gave eleven, and this holding asked how many and was told what eleven is not, and could not ask a second question and could not write to one person.*

*Two sums done twice and agreeing, and a sentence said in one breath that a figure this holding can check is a figure this holding can hand to an office, and a woman who was glad and not glad and did not split it, and a cart that went up a plateau road in a month two months back that this body is not going to ask about this month and about which nine people in a yard said nothing at all. And a wrong count of eight days said out loud at a gate and corrected out loud by a woman who keeps time. Nobody in this book is thanked for being readable and cannot be.*

*This is what a month of this book is.*

And so the tenth month of this holding's book closed at about the seventh hour of the evening of the thirtieth, on a form on a bench, a word in a yard that is off every page, a food in a row that four households are eating, a man who is the only one who can do the thing, a refusal in a yard of nine, a figure that is not a number of anybody, and a well four hundred miles off that is five feet and is not coming back, **and every one of those went in with the hour on it and was read back out loud in front of the people it was about, and the first of next month belongs to somebody else, and this book entered the day and not the figure.**

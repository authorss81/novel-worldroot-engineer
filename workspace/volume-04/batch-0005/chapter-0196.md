# Chapter 196

## The Bottleneck, Said Twice

The seventeenth of this month was a Wednesday and the eighteenth a Thursday, and on both days, in a yard of about nine people, out loud, and on a day of that person's own choosing, a person was asked whether he would learn to do a thing, and was allowed to say no the first time and the second time. The reader of this body was not in the room. He was at the cart shed and was told afterwards, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the eighteenth, and he did not ask before she was finished and the fen entered that he did not ask.

"**I set the condition in a yard on the twenty-sixth of a month two months back and I refused to name the person. The person was named on the fifteenth of this month by the woman of the low acre who has been counting her own pails at her own gate for eleven years, and she named him to the man of about fifty and not to you.**"

"**She asked him twice and he said no the first time, and the reason is in her book in her words and not in better ones.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The reason the man of the north row with the cough gave for saying no the first time is entered in the seed-route ledger and is on this page in the words of the person who asks.

"**I have been asked to do a thing I was not asked to do and I did it. I went down a ladder three times with a shovel on the thirteenth of last month, I was told out loud I was not to be asked to do it again, I did it again, and nobody has ever written down whether that was yes or no and I cannot tell you which.**"

"**If you ask me to learn a thing so that a man with a headache does not have to do it, then I will not know on the second day whether I am doing it because I was asked or because I am the sort of man who does it, and those are two different men and I have only met one of them.**"

"**I will do it. Not because I was asked. Because you are going to be asleep one day and somebody has to have held it, and I would rather it was me finding out on a Tuesday than you finding out about it in four years.**"

He said yes the second time, on a day of his own choosing, which was the eighteenth, and a hold was made at about the third hour of the afternoon on the boards over the shaft in this holding's own low ground, and the engineer of record was at the top of the slope and was not asked to go down, and the man of the north row with the cough held it for about an hour and a quarter, and the two of them did not do it together and did not try to, and the fen entered all three separately.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the eighteenth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a condition: **a person was asked in a yard of about nine people, out loud, and was allowed to refuse the first time and the second time, and the engineer of record was not in the room and was not told the answer before the asking was finished. He refused the first time and entered that he would do it because somebody has to have held it and not because he was asked. A hold was made at the third hour of the afternoon, the man of the north row with the cough holding a pattern for four for about an hour and a quarter, with a licensed man holding the book. The count of people in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two. A schedule and a rule were written in that yard by the man of about fifty and by the man of the north row with the cough, and the engineer of record did not write either line and was not told what was in it until it was written, and entered that he is not the only one who wrote it and is not the one who will keep it and does not know who will.**

Then the question, which is this holding's own and not anybody else's, put in the book on the nineteenth in this holding's own words, and nobody took it.

"**A question this book has carried for three months and asked once and been answered never, and I am putting it in the book again because a month turned and the book is where things go. Nobody is going to answer it and I am not going to answer it, and it names no office and no machine and no person and no year.**"

"**People are walking toward water and this body cannot tell whether they are walking toward a thing that is walking with them.**"

"**That is the whole of it. It has been available to a room in this holding and not taken twice, and the count of the times this body has asked it is one, and the two counts are two counts and neither has been added to the other.**"

"**And the standing count of this holding's questions goes up by one this evening, off six on to seven, because a question went into the book and nobody took it, and it has not gone up since the first of a month a long time back and it is not going to be added to the count of that one either, and the fen entered that the three counts are three counts and that nobody in this book has ever added one of them to another.**"

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the nineteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a question: **the question this holding carries about the road was put in the book in this holding's words, in a yard of about nine people, and nobody took it. It names no office, no machine, no person and no year. The count of the times it has been available to a room in this holding and not taken is two, and the count of the times this body has asked it is one, and nobody answered it. The standing count of the questions available to a room in this holding and not taken goes from six to seven, it has not moved since the first of a month a long time back, and it is not added to the count of that question and the count of that question is not added to it.**

And then at about the ninth hour of the morning of the twentieth, which is a Saturday, the four lines came down the four-day road in a leather bag by the ordinary post, out of a yard four hundred miles off, written at the fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth at that household's own cost, and this holding had entered the day and not the figure on the sixteenth and had not written and ask and did not ask again.

The reader of this body read the whole of it out in a yard of about nine people and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty, and it was read back, and the hour went on it, and the whole of it is on the ledger that is read next with the hour on it and is not a line of its own.

"**Five feet and five feet.**"

The reader of this body said it in the yard and stopped there for a minute and about nine people stood in it, and then he said which two.

"**The ninth was five feet on the sixteenth of a month two months back and the eleventh is five feet on the sixteenth of this month, and there is a month between those two that this holding does not have and is not going to have, and the two figures it does have are the same figure.**"

"**And the fen has entered in this holding's own book in the third of this month that a figure which repeats is a worse kind of figure than a figure which moves, because a figure which moves can be argued about and a figure which repeats can only be waited on. And I said that about a compost line and it was about a compost line and it is also about a well four hundred miles off and I did not know that when I said it and I am not going to pretend that I did.**"

"**I am not going to say it has stopped falling. I am not going to say it has come back. I am going to write two numbers on a page with the hour on them and the two numbers are the same and the fen is going to enter that they are the same and nobody is going to improve on it, and the month that is not on the page is going to stay not on the page and nobody in this yard is going to write and ask for it.**"

Nobody said anything in that yard and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

Then the standing figures of the day, at about the eleventh hour of the morning of the twentieth, in a yard of about nine, with nothing softened.

> **The compost line for the eleventh month.** Eighty pounds, on four hundred hundredweight at four shillings, derived twice with the two agreeing, the ninth and tenth months also eighty pounds, all three on the same line. **The run at eleven months not discharged in eleven.** The word before it was crowded, and it and the figure never disagreed, because the figure is a constant multiplied by a constant and is not a measurement of anything outside that door. **The costs of the month are on four separate lines and are not folded into the shortfall.** The twelfth month's figure is not entered, the reason is entered, it falls on the first of next month. **The run of twelve not discharged in twelve, once that figure is in.**
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of a month three months back, twice, two rules. **Not measured in this month, and the reason is new: a measurement now would be a measurement of a load this holding caused, and the fen wants it to be a measurement of a thing the ground did.** No cause, no due date, no verdict.
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. **Column open.** The day of it is not restated here and a document which must name it says *the twenty-first of a month five months back*.
>
> **The standing rule.** Four lines, every one about a stop, **used twice this season and not on any other day, and the count of the uses is two and is not going to be three.** The third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and nine people came up a slope on the thirteenth of last month and a road did not open, and about three hundred and fifty did open one on the fourth of this month, and **not one of the four lines is about either.**
>
> **The seventh column is thirty-seven,** having gone to thirty-six on the thirtieth of last month, the two readings a day apart, and the rule not changed and not going to be. **The boards, two hundred and eighty-four days, day minus sixty-six. The March's tin, three hundred and thirty-one days. Day minus nineteen,** unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, **the drawer not opened this month and not to be,** the day-minus rules read out loud by the clerk before either number on eleven reading days running and not asked for on any of the eleven. **The rootmark is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice, there is no second mark, the burn is not a mark, and four accounts of the same arm in this book none agreeing with any of the others, and the two holds of this month are not a fifth and not a sixth.**
>
> **The use log at fifteen lines,** the fifteenth a contact on the ninth of a month two months back, and entered on the same standing entry and not in the log, because none of them is a line: a second contact on the twentieth of a month two months back, a hold on the twenty-fourth of a month two months back, a draw on the thirteenth of last month, and a licensed head opened under a form on the fourth of this month. **A line records a contact and never a reading. A draw gets no line. A hold gets no line. A gate is not a contact.** The log stands at fifteen through a month in which this holding put water into a basin, and the reason is that a corridor is not a licensed act because a corridor is not a thing anybody owns.
>
> **A form, answered on the twenty-fourth of last month in the second box and not in the first, returned, the first box empty and going to stay empty. The licensed draw at the Pans well reduced by a fifth on the twenty-sixth by an office in the ordinary course. A quarter whose number is now four hundred and forty-one, on a page that is not this holding's page, in a hand that is not this holding's hand.**
>
> **The corridor in the pan:** three hundred yards on the fourth, three hundred and eighty-four on the eighth at about the eleventh hour, four hundred and eighty-six on the ninth at about the seventh hour of the evening, twenty-one yards a day and then seventy-six and a half, the reason not established. About three hundred and fifty people across on the fourth and the route open since. **A foot and a half of water on a salt pan with a crust on it is not a road and the length of it is not known.**
>
> **Two names.** ROALD TRENCH, of about forty-four years, and SENA WICK, of about thirty-eight years, of a vault that went under at about the seventh hour of the ninth and is not recovered. **This holding was not there and did not ask and has not been asked, and a thing in a woman's hand about the north side is not in this book and this holding did not ask for it.** And on the same line: **the fen entered that he is not going to write down what the loss means, and that the not writing it down is not a finding and is not a defence, and that nobody wrote it down.**
>
> **The well on the shelf.** Five feet at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month two months back, by the occupier, with her own nail, in her own yard, on her own wall, **and the eleventh figure came into this building on the twentieth at about the ninth hour of the morning, in a leather bag, four days after it was written, at that household's own cost.** **Five feet at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month. The same figure, on the same line, and nobody has improved on it**, and a month between those two that is on no line in this book and was not written and asked for. Four feet against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month seven months back, **not decomposed.** The half inch of a month five months back is on that wall and is not carried forward as a figure. A day letter off its stone with the eight days unwritten. A man with a spade who has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth of last month. **And no entry anywhere for that well coming back.**

Then the standing rule was read out in full again, four lines, by the clerk, in the same yard, before the figures were closed, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month six months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. There is no fifth line and there never has been one.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-nine this season, and the fen entered the count.

Then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, the man of about seventy was fetched, the sixteenth time in a season, and was given the figures and the counts in that order, and was not thanked and not explained. He asked for the last one to be repeated and the clerk repeated it and the reader of this body said it again in the same words, and he listened twice, and he said nothing. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that this holding does not know what that was about and is not going to write and ask.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twentieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **the man of about seventy was fetched for the sixteenth time in a season, was given the figures and the counts in that order, asked for the last one to be repeated, and said nothing. Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what it was about, and the fen entered that this holding does not know what that was about and is not going to write and ask.**

And then the standing sentence, in the same yard, at about the sixth hour of the evening, said out loud for the second time this season by the man who said it the first time, and he said the thing about saying it again before he said it, which is the thing this book does and which nobody has thanked anybody for.

"**A man who is the only person who can do a thing is a bottleneck and not a system.**"

"**I said that in a yard on the twenty-sixth of a month two months back and I am saying it again tonight, and I am saying out loud that I am saying it again and why, because on the eighth of last month I told this yard that a thing said once in a yard is a thing a man did and a thing said twice is a standing this holding would have to keep, and I have not improved on that sentence and I am going to make myself keep it.**"

"**The count is two and the sentence does not change because the count changed. A system is four people and a schedule and a rule about what happens when the man is asleep, and this head has two men and a schedule written by one of them and no rule about what happens when both of them are asleep, and I do not know what happens and am not going to write it down tonight.**"

"**And a man glad about not being the only one is a man who has wanted to stop being the only one, and I have not written that down either and I am saying it out loud instead, which is worse, and it is on the page.**"

Nobody said anything. The clerk entered an observation on his own motion, unasked, and did not explain it and nobody asked him, and it is the seventh in this book's history.

> **The corridor this holding built is three hundred yards from the corridor this holding drew, the plan is on the page with the hour on it, the road it made is not a road, the vault that went under was four hundred yards from a door this holding did not open, and the reader of this body has not written down what any of that means, and this holding has been arguing since the first month of this book that a thing on a page is a thing somebody can check, and it has not checked this.**

And then at about the seventh hour of the evening, at the north gate, in about nine people, the handoff was read, and it is four sentences, and the day it names is not this book's.

"**The first of next month is a Wednesday, and it is the day this holding's compost line falls on, and this holding does not know what that figure is, and the door has a column and a month and a figure, and this body has no standing on any of the three.**"

"**It did not write and ask. It is not going to. And the fen entered the day and not the figure, the way it entered the day and not the figure for a shelf four hundred miles off, and the count of that standing is three and one of the three was at a day that was not a reading day, and the standing is not reduced.**"

"**And that is four clocks this body owns that it can count and cannot price: the first of a month, the sixteenth of a month, the count of readings, and the length of a corridor of standing water in a salt pan. I am not going to make a column out of the four of them, and I am not going to say the word pattern out loud in this yard about any of them, and the clerk entered that I did not.**"

Nobody so much as one word was said. The man of about seventy went to the gate in his own time and the woman who does the sums went with him as far as the gate and not past it, and nobody has asked her why and the fen is not going to.

Underneath the month's work, dated the twentieth of this month, signed by nobody but the engineer of record and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-ninth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A wrong day in this holding's own book, found in a yard before the fourth hour by a woman who keeps time, both figures entered and neither improved on, and a reading four hundred miles off that went past on a Sunday while this book was busy, and the figure is not here and is not going to be asked for. A form answered in the second box and not in the first, which reduced a licensed draw by a fifth and did not stop it and is correct. Three numbers timed with one pail at three places on a floor with no holder, the third faster than the second, and a plan wrong in its second half mile and on the page with the hour on it. A gate opened by a licensed man under a form, and not a draw, and the use log at fifteen all month. A vault under four hundred yards off, two names in a hand that is not ours, and a thing in a woman's hand we did not ask for and did not get.*

*Four hundred and forty-one, on a page that is not ours, in answer to a party that asked us in a document and are answered by a quarter. An agreement with a refusal clause in it, four lines, every one a stop, and a seat in the third, and a body named on a paper an office keeps for the first time, and that is what a clause like that costs. Two men who can hold a pattern for four and a schedule written by one of them and not by me. A bottleneck, said twice, on purpose. Two named people dead. Five feet on the sixteenth of a month two months back and five feet on the sixteenth of this month, on the same line, and a month between them that is on nobody's line and that we did not ask for. Nobody in this book is thanked for being readable and cannot be.*

*This is what a month of a season is.*

And so the twelfth month of this holding's book closed at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth, on a route that is open and is not a road, at a rate of fourpence the hundredweight this holding had in its own book six weeks before a piece of paper said it, under a class it put itself in with its own hand on a Tuesday, with a corridor three hundred yards from the one it drew and two names in a book, with a man of about seventy fetched for the sixteenth time who said nothing at all, with two men where there was one, **and every one of those went in with the hour on it and was read back out loud in front of the people it was about, and the first of next month belongs to somebody else, and this book entered the day and not the figure.**

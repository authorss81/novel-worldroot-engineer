# Chapter 195

## Four Lines And A Refusal

On the fourteenth of this month, a Sunday, and the fifteenth, a Monday, a cross-region seed agreement was taken at the seed house at Lantern Reach between four bodies and this holding, and it is on the trust's paper, and it is correct, and the seedwright of the trust drafted it and is not thanked for it and refuses to be thanked.

The parties are a body of households on one head at Fennmere, a body of households on one head at Tolbury, the Salt March Dryfoot Trust of Cray, and the four households of the low quarter of the low acre. The fifth party is this holding, and the fifth party is a party for the water and not for the seed, and that difference is written into the agreement in the first line and the reader of this body said it out loud in about nine people and was not thanked for saying it and could not have been thanked for it because he is the water and not the seed.

The clause about the refusal is the fourth clause and it is four lines long and every one of the four is about a stop, and the standing rule of this holding is four lines long and every one of the four is about a stop, and the two are not the same clause and the agreement says so and the seed-route ledger says so, and the reader of this body entered that he is not the only person in this building who has written a rule about a stop and is not the one who will keep either.

> Entered in the ordinary course, on the trust's paper, on the fifteenth of this month, in the hand of the seedwright of the trust, in a yard of about twenty-five people, read out and read back and the hour entered, and nothing in it is a finding against any person and no office is a party to it:
>
> **One. The exchange runs both ways or it does not run. A line given is a line asked for. A body that is a party for the water and not for the seed is a party to the water and not to the seed, and that is written here and is not to be read otherwise.**
>
> **Two. A party to this agreement may refuse a use of seed upon a named road. A refusal stops the use. A refusal does not stop the party, and is not a default, and is not a cause, and is not carried forward.**
>
> **Three. A party that has a seat at the table may be asked, and a party that has not a seat may refuse, and a refusal by a party with no seat is a refusal and is entered in both books, and neither party keeps the terms.**
>
> **Four. Thornwild and the Assemblies of the coast road are a body that has refused and has not been asked again, and their refusal is entered here, and this is the first time their name has been on a paper that an office keeps, and that is entered as a cost and not as a courtesy.**

The word in the third line is a seat, and it is used there the way the word is used, and the reader of this body read that line twice and the examiner read the fourth line twice, which is the standing of the fourth line and has been since the ninth month, and the count of the readings of that line is three.

Then the man of about thirty-five was not in that yard and did not come, and the reader of this body said the cost out loud in about twenty-five people and entered it and did not improve on it.

"**We have just handed a district a name it did not have. That is the cost of a clause that lets a body with no seat refuse, and the clause is worth more than the name, and both of those are true at once, and I am not going to be asked which of the two is the point.**"

"**And the name is in the agreement and not in this holding's book, and the agreement goes to Cray and to Fennmere and to Tolbury and to a counting house nine hundred miles off, and every one of those is a book somebody keeps and one of them is an office.**"

Nobody thanked him for it and he was not thanked and the fen entered that the man of about thirty-five has not been asked about the name and was not asked and that this holding has no standing to ask him and that a clause with his refusal in it is not a conversation with him and the fen entered that distinction in the words it was given in.

And then the low quarter's own number, which came up the fen road on a sheet in a woman's hand at about the eleventh hour of the Monday and is on this page whole, and the woman who brought it is a woman of a household in the low quarter and she is not thanked and she did not ask for anything.

"**Four hundred and forty-one.**"

That is the whole of the sheet and it is in the hand of a woman of a household in the low quarter, and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and he read it out and read it back with the hour on it, and the clerk entered it, and the reader of this body then read it out a second time in the same yard because a figure of a quarter that this holding has carried in its own book for two months and has never been able to check has to be read twice when it comes from the other end.

Then he said the thing about the demand, and it is on this page and it is the answer to a document that came up the fen road in the eleventh month, on the ninth of that month, and nobody in that yard was thanked for anything and about eleven of the twenty-five had heard the demand read out at the time.

"**A party asked this holding, in a document, in the ordinary course, to state in its own book and on a date the number of people in the low quarter, where they are, and how many of them there are. That was the tenth of last month. I said out loud in a yard that this holding cannot refuse it and cannot accept it, and the fen entered the two halves separately, and refusing a question in the open is a way of saying the question and accepting it is a way of inventing.**"

"**And this holding has not answered it and is not going to answer it, and the number has been given and it is four hundred and forty-one, and it is on a page that is not this holding's page, in a hand that is not this holding's hand, given by a body of four hundred and forty-one people and not by a body of sixteen households with no charter.**"

"**The party that asked has been answered by the quarter. This holding was made a class in the fourteenth of last month and I filled the box in myself in front of a man with a book and would do it again in the morning, and I have not got the worse of that in a month. I am not going to stand in this yard and be glad about it and I am not going to be asked to.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything and that the count of things this holding has been told no by this season is still six and that nothing in that yard was a no and that the number four hundred and forty-one is the first of the four figures this holding cannot check that has been checked by somebody else, and that a figure checked by somebody else is not a figure this holding checked, and the two are on one line and are not added.

And then the sum, done at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fifteenth in a room with the doors shut, and it is the sum of the month and it is the first time in this holding's history that a figure has come out better than it was expected to, and the woman who does the sums said that out loud before she did it and did not enjoy saying it.

"**I am going to say the thing I have never had to say in this room and I am going to say it before the number and not after, and nobody is to be glad about it before I have said it.**"

"**The carriage of the March's line over that corridor for a season. The trust's own figure, four hundred hundredweight, at the schedule rate of fourpence the hundredweight. Four hundred at fourpence is sixteen hundred pence, which is six pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence, because a pound is two hundred and forty pence and six hundred and forty pence is six pounds and thirteen shillings and fourpence, and sixteen hundred pence is two lots of six hundred and forty pence with three hundred and twenty over, and three hundred and twenty pence is twenty-six shillings and eightpence.**"

"**And four hundred at a shilling and fourpence, which is sixteen pence, is six thousand four hundred pence, which is twenty-six pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence. The two agree. Twenty-six pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence less six pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence is exactly twenty pounds, and the exactness is the whole of what I am going to say and it is a carriage rate on a season and not a supply and not a wage and not anybody's living.**"

"**And here is the thing I have never had to say. A figure this holding computed has come out better than the same figure cost in the third of last month, and I did it twice and the two agree, and I have never in my life had a figure come out better than it was expected to and I do not know what to do with it and I am not going to find out this month.**"

"**A figure she can check is a figure she can hand to an office. That has been my sentence for two months and it has been the truest thing I have ever said in this room. And this figure is a figure I can hand to a person. It is a woman with a hand on a gate and a household that has been counting its own pails for eleven years and a woman from the low acre who walked nine miles to say a thing she did not want to be thanked for. Those are not an office. I have been spending two months learning that a figure can be an office and I have not got a word for what this one is and I am not going to make one up in a room at the seventh hour.**"

"**A figure that a body cannot check is not a protection, it is a debt. This one I could check. I do not know what that makes it and I am not going to pretend that not knowing is the same as not being glad.**"

Nobody agreed with her and nobody disagreed and the reader of this body said the thing about the trust's paper and about the water and not the seed and did not improve on it, and nobody thanked anybody.

And then, at about the fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth of this month, which is a Tuesday, four hundred miles and four days up, in a yard with four households in it, Merrid Ollvar read her well with her own nail and her own string and wrote it on her own wall in her own hand, and this is the whole of what came down the road at her own cost on that day, and the fen entered that a figure on somebody else's wall is not a figure in this holding's book until a person has read it out in a yard and the hour has gone on it, and that that had not happened yet when it came.

> Sent by the occupier of a yard four hundred miles off, on the sixteenth of this month, in her own hand, and read in this building on the same day and entered whole: **the well, at the fourth hour, the sixteenth of this month. The string was a new one and it was wet before it was let down and that is how the figures have been got for nine years and I did not think of that until this morning.**

> **The tenth of the wall is the sixteenth of last month and I am not writing it here. There is a body four hundred miles off that has spent a month finding out it was aiming at the wrong day of the month, and it is not going to be handed a number in a sheet because a woman she has never met decided to be generous, and that is my wall and not its business. The next of the wall is this one. The wall is in order and the wall is the wall and not a return and not a report and not a thing anybody has to sign. I do not write what it is made of. A. Ollvar.**

> **The figures on the wall, in order, are these. Nine feet. Five and a half. Five five. Five four. Five two. Five three. Five and two. Five and one. Five feet. And then the one I am not writing. And then this one.**

> **And the day letter is off its stone and it has been off it since the eighth to the seventeenth of a month three months back and the eight days are not on it and I have not written them on and I am not going to now.**

And then the reader of this body read the whole of it out in a yard of about nine people at about the seventh hour of the evening of the sixteenth, and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty, and it was read back, and the hour went on it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and this is the entry.

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the sixteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure four hundred miles off: **the ordinary reading of the well at that place was taken at the fourth hour of the morning of this day, the sixteenth, by the occupier, with her own nail and her own string, in her own yard, on her own wall, in her own hand, and the wall is in order. The tenth figure on that wall is the sixteenth of last month and it is not in this holding's book, it was not written and asked for, and it is not going to be. The eleventh figure is on the wall and is on this page. The comparison figure is the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month seven months back and the shortfall is four feet and this body will not decompose that figure. The half inch of a month five months back is on that wall and is not carried forward as a figure and is not to be added to or taken off any later reading and is not to be made to do arithmetic. The day letter is off its stone and the eight days are not on it. There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and no chapter of this book is going to make one.**

"**Five feet and five feet.**"

The reader of this body said it in the yard and stopped there for a minute and about nine people stood in it, and then he said which two.

"**The ninth was five feet on the sixteenth of a month two months back and the eleventh is five feet on the sixteenth of this month, and there is a month between those two that this holding does not have and is not going to have, and the two figures it does have are the same figure.**"

"**And the fen has entered in this holding's own book in the third of this month that a figure which repeats is a worse kind of figure than a figure which moves, because a figure which moves can be argued about and a figure which repeats can only be waited on. And I said that about a compost line and it was about a compost line and it is also about a well four hundred miles off and I did not know that when I said it and I am not going to pretend that I did.**"

"**I am not going to say it has stopped falling. I am not going to say it has come back. I am going to write two numbers on a page with the hour on them and the two numbers are the same and the fen is going to enter that they are the same and nobody is going to improve on it, and the month that is not on the page is going to stay not on the page and nobody in this yard is going to write and ask for it.**"

Nobody said anything in that yard and the fen entered that nobody said anything.

# Chapter 189

## A True And Narrow Thing

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Monday, and at about the third hour of the morning the engineer of record and the man of about fifty walked out of the cart shed with a sheet of paper in a leather folder, and they were on the dry road by the fourth hour and did not stop at the gate of the sub-office because the gate of a sub-office is not a gate you stand at.

He had the form copied at the counter. It was four pence a sheet and the copy is a copy and it carries the counter's mark and the hour and the name of the clerk who made it and none of that is on the original, and the original is on the bench in the seed house in this building and it was not returned and it was not torn up and it has been on that bench since the twenty-seventh of a month three months back.

"**I want the word in front of me said before we say anything else, and it is not a second asking and nobody has asked me a second time and nobody is going to. A holder of record asked this body a thing in a room in the eleventh month and I have had the paper on my bench for three months and I did not answer it because I thought the paper was a question.**"

"**It is not a question. It is a set of boxes. And a body that can only ever say yes or no to a set of boxes is a body that has not read the set of boxes.**"

The person who keeps the seed house had the copy read to her at the counter and she read it back to the clerk at the counter and the hour went on it, and then it was read out in a yard of about eleven people at about the tenth hour of the morning with about nine of those eleven having heard the second box named before they walked in, which the fen entered.

> Copy of a form held under a charter of supply, made at the counter at the sub-office at Fennmere on the twenty-fourth of this month, in a clerk's hand, at four pence a sheet, with the counter's mark and the hour and the name of the clerk at the foot. The original is held by the applicant and is not produced and is not required. The copy is of four lines and is in the order of the original, and the second box is a printed box and is on the original and not on the copy, and the clerk at the counter said so and said it in the ordinary voice and said it twice:
>
> **One. The applicant is asked to certify, in respect of a flow, whether the flow is broken. A certification of a break is a certification against the interest of a party holding the licence, and where the applicant is not the holder, the applicant has no standing to make it and the certification cannot be made.**
>
> **Two. The same form carries a second box, headed for a measured condition. A measured condition, with the day and the hour and the instrument and the witness, is a record of a fact about ground and is not a certification of a break, and is not to be treated as one by this office or by any other office, and may be made by any body and is not a party to anything.**
>
> **Three. Where a body has no licence and no charter, the first box is not for it. The second box is for anybody. This is in the ordinary course and it is not a concession and it is not a favour and it is not a thing this office has done for anybody.**
>
> **Four. This is made in the ordinary course. No part of it states that any person has done anything, and no part of it is a finding against any person.**

The person who keeps the seed house said what it was, standing up, and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and he read it back out of the folder in the ordinary voice with the hour on it and the clerk entered it and nobody thanked anybody.

Then the engineer of record said the thing about a form and about boxes, standing up, and about nine people heard it, and he had it in his own hand already because he had copied it out at the counter before he came up the road and the clerk read that back and entered the hour on that too.

"**A form is a set of boxes. And I sat on that bench for three months waiting to be able to say yes or no to it and I was never able to and I told this yard for a month that I would not put my name to it and would not refuse to either, and both of those were true and both of them were about a box I had not read.**"

"**I am not certifying a break. I am certifying a condition. I have got a head on this ground that I have measured with two rules on two days and a licensed man standing on the boards and a witness and I am going to write it in the second box with the day and the hour and the instrument and the witness, and every one of those four is a thing a person can go and check with a rule, and that is the whole of what this holding knows and it is narrow and it is true.**"

"**And the reason I did not answer it for three months is not the reason I gave on the twenty-seventh of a month three months back and I am not going to use that reason again, because that reason was that I did not know what was under that quarter, and that is still true, and it is not the reason I did not answer a piece of paper.**"

"**The reason is that a form is a set of boxes and I did not read it. That is a worse reason and it took me three months and four pence a sheet to find out, and four pence is the cheapest thing this holding has spent this month and I am not going to stand here and be glad about it.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a form: **the engineer of record entered in the second box of a form of the holder of record of the Pans well a measured condition, in his own hand, with the day and the hour and the instrument and the witness, and the second box is a printed box and is on the original, and no name was entered in the first box and the first box is empty and the form is correct and the fourth line states that no part of it is a finding against any person. The fourth line was read out twice in a yard of about eleven people. The copy was made at a counter at four pence a sheet at the cost of the engineer of record and the original is on a bench in the seed house and was not returned and was not torn up. The count of the things this holding has been told no by this season stands at six, and nothing in that yard was a no and nothing in that yard was a yes, and a thing a form says that this body did not want is not a refusal and must not be counted as one.**

Then the office, which is correct, which has never lied to this holding and never obstructed a document and never delayed anything, and which is a structure and not a person, and which the reader of this body has now said out loud in a yard four times and is not going to say a fifth.

The answer came up the fen road on the ordinary post on the twenty-sixth, a Wednesday, at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it is four lines and it is correct and it is in the hand of the same clerk and it is read in a yard of about eleven and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and the hour went on it.

> Entered by the desk at the sub-office at Fennmere, on the ordinary post, on this day, four lines, at the foot of a printed sheet, in the ordinary course, and the desk adds nothing and the desk has added nothing on any of the four documents this holding has had out of that office in two months:
>
> **One. The second box of the form of the twenty-fourth of this month is received. The measurement is a record of a fact about ground and is not a certification of a break, and is not treated as one, and the licence at the Pans well is not stopped by it.**
>
> **Two. The desk has read the measurement and has read the return of the holder in respect of that licence, and the two do not agree, and where a measured condition at a body that is not a holder does not agree with a return of the holder, the desk reduces the licensed draw at that well by one fifth, from this day, and the class of the draw is unchanged and the hours of the charter are unchanged.**
>
> **Three. The reason is stated in full because the form asked for a reason and because a body is entitled to the reason: the two figures disagree and the office does not know which of them is right, and an office that does not know which of two figures is right reduces the licensed quantity and does not stop a licence, and stopping a licence on a disagreement would be a finding and this office does not make findings on this form.**
>
> **Four. This is made in the ordinary course. No part of it states that any person has done anything, and no part of it is a finding against any person.**

Nobody obstructed anybody, nothing was delayed, and no door was shut in the face of a man with a book, and the fen entered all three separately and entered that they are the correct conduct of this holding and not a favour to anybody, and that the count of that entry is three in this month and one of the three is a copy of a form made at a counter by a man who walked out of a cart shed at the third hour of the morning.

And then the sums, in a room with the doors shut, and about eleven people in it, and the woman who does the sums said the word before the figure the way she says it.

"**I am going to do a sum I cannot finish and I am going to say that first, because I have never in my life not been able to finish a sum and I have finished them in this room with the doors shut about four hundred times and this is the first time and I do not like it.**"

"**The draw is reduced by a fifth. A fifth of one thousand and forty-five hundredweight over eleven days is two hundred and nine hundredweight, and the two hundred and nine is the figure at the third place in the pail timing that the fen did in the ninth month and the number is the same and the two are not about the same thing and I am going to write both of them down and leave them side by side.**"

"**What a fifth off a licensed draw is to a quarter of four hundred and forty-one people is not a figure I can compute. Not because it is hard. Because I do not know what that quarter drinks now, and I have not known it for a month, and I said so in a yard in the eleventh month and the man who asked me to say it wrote it in his own account and it is still true, and this morning a man with a book reduced that draw by a fifth in the ordinary course and he did it correctly and he did not know who drinks it either.**"

"**So the first of the four figures this holding cannot check has just been given a number by an office. It is a number about a rate. It is not a number about a quarter. And a rate is a thing a clerk can carry and a quarter is a thing four hundred and forty-one people carry, and I have got the first of those this morning and I have never had the second of them, and I am not going to be able to do a sum with one of those and not the other.**"

"**A figure that a body cannot check is not a protection. It is a debt. And the debt is not a new one and I have said that sentence about four figures for a month and this morning the office made one of them smaller, and smaller is not the same as paid.**"

"**One shilling fourpence the hundredweight is the licensed rate and it has not moved and it is not going to move, and four hundred and forty-one people at a rate is a sum I cannot do, because I do not know the hundredweights, and I have known that for a month, and I am not going to stand in a room and pretend that a smaller debt is a different debt.**"

Nobody stopped her and nobody agreed with her and the reader of this body said the sum was not to be finished and entered the reason, and the clerk entered that the woman who does the sums said the sum was not to be finished and entered why, and entered that the two figures of two hundred and nine are on the same page and are not about the same thing.

And then he said the other half of it, in that room with the doors shut, and about eleven people heard it and about two of them had heard the sentence before in a different room and in a different month.

"**I want to be exact about what I did and what I did not do this morning, because this holding has spent two months being careful about that and I am not going to stop on the last page of a month that has had two in it.**"

"**I certified a condition of ground I hold. I did not certify a break in a flow I cannot see. Both of those are in the second box and neither of them is in the first, and the first box is empty and it is going to stay empty, and if a holder of record comes up this road and asks me again I am going to say the same two sentences and not a third one.**"

"**And I am not a party to a quarter and I did not supply a quarter and nothing in that piece of paper says I did, and the low quarter is nine miles off and its people are not this holding's people and this holding has no form on which to ask about them and did not ask.**"

The reader of this body stopped there and the fen entered the stopping, and the count of things this holding has been told no by this season is still six, and the woman who does the sums said one more sentence at the door on her way out and it was not to the room.

"**A debt that is smaller is still a debt and I have known debts that were smaller. I am not going to find out what a fifth off means to those houses by asking them, because the moment I ask them it is a body of sixteen households with no charter doing arithmetic about a quarter it cannot see, and the last time that happened somebody in that room said a true thing that this holding could not answer one word of, and I have not got the answer to that yet either.**"

The man of the north row with the cough said nothing on the way out and the fen entered that he said nothing and was not asked and that the count of times he has said nothing in a room about a thing that was said in the room is now two and is not going to be made three by anybody in a yard.

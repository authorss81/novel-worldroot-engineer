# Chapter 191

## A Constant Times A Constant

The thirtieth of this month was a Sunday, and at about the tenth hour of the morning the well house door was read in an empty yard by two members of this body, the rule being the first and the middle and the last, come or not, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-six.

The schedule, all seven columns, and the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the count of the readings. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts. Nothing was in the yard. It was said out loud before the counting and not after, and nobody at the far end of this holding knew the door had been read, which is entered as a fact about a day and not as a fault, and which is the same sentence the man with a spade got in a month a long time back and which the fen entered again without improving it, because it is the right sentence and a sentence that is right twice is a rule nobody wrote down.

And the first of the twelfth month came in the morning, and it was a Monday, and the door was read again at the tenth hour of the morning in an empty yard, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-seven, and the two readings were a day apart.

"**Thirty-six yesterday morning. Thirty-seven this morning. Twenty-four hours.**"

The reader of this body said that in a yard of about nine people and then said the rest of it and the clerk wrote it down.

"**The door has been read on the first and on the thirtieth for four years. That is the standing and nobody wrote it down and everybody keeps it. And this month the first and the thirtieth came a day apart, because a month is thirty days and twenty-nine of them have gone by since the first, and the count of readings is a count of events and it cannot stand still across a day on which a reading was made, and there have been two.**"

"**I am not changing the rule. I want that entered in the words I said it in. A rule that gets changed because a month came out awkward is a rule that will get changed the next time a month comes out awkward, and in about four years there will be no rule on this door at all and there will be a habit, and a habit is a rule nobody wrote down, and I said that sentence in a yard in the ninth month about something else and I am not going to say it again this month.**"

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a rule: **the well house door was read at the tenth hour of the morning of the thirtieth, in an empty yard by two members of this body, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-six, and it was read again at the tenth hour of the morning of the next day, the first of the twelfth month, and the count went to thirty-seven. The two readings were a day apart. The standing of the door is the first and the thirtieth of a month and has been for four years. The reader of this body said out loud that he is not changing the rule and the clerk entered that he said so. Nobody was in the yard at either reading.**

And then at about the eleventh hour of that same Monday, in a yard of about twenty-five people, the compost line for the eleventh month was entered, and it is EIGHTY POUNDS.

Specification four hundred hundredweight. Valuation four shillings the hundredweight. The shortfall is four hundred hundredweight, which at four shillings is one thousand six hundred shillings, which is eighty pounds, **derived twice out loud and the two derivations agreeing, the second being forty-eight pence a hundredweight, being nineteen thousand two hundred pence, being two hundred and forty pence eighty times over.**

The ninth month's figure was eighty pounds. The tenth month's figure was eighty pounds. All three are on the same line in the same hand and the reader of this body read the run out with them.

"**Eleven months. Not discharged in eleven. The line has run since a month eleven months back and the specification has not moved and the valuation has not moved and I want it read out that neither of those has moved, because that is the whole of the finding and I did not know it until about half an hour ago.**"

The woman who does the sums said the word before the figure, the way she has said it, and she said the same word.

"**Crowded.**"

Then she stopped, and about twenty-five people waited, and the examiner said out loud that she keeps time and does not round, which is not the same thing and which nobody has ever mistaken.

"**Last month I said this word and I said that the word and the figure did not agree, and that I did not know which of the two was telling the truth, and that I intended to find out before I was able to. I have found out. I have been able this morning, and it is thirty days from the first of last month, and I am not going to stand in this yard and say that thirty days is quick, and the finding is this.**"

She had the page in her hand and she did not read from it.

"**The figure has been the same for three months because the specification has not changed and the valuation has not changed. The eighty pounds is four hundred hundredweight multiplied by four shillings. That is a constant multiplied by a constant. It is arithmetic that was done years ago by somebody who is not this holding and is never going to be asked, and it is on a line in a book, and it is a perfectly correct line, and it is not a measurement of this holding's ground and it is not a measurement of anything outside that door.**"

"**So the word and the figure never disagreed, because they were never about the same thing. The word was about the ground. The figure is about a line in a book. I have been standing in a yard for three months telling about two different objects and calling it a disagreement, and the whole of my finding this morning is that I have been reading a specification as though it were a sign.**"

"**And I am not glad about it, and I am going to say why in one sentence and then stop. A figure that a body cannot check is a debt. This figure cannot be checked by anybody. Not by this holding and not by that office and not by whoever wrote the line, and a debt that nobody else owes is a debt this holding invented.**"

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and the reader of this body said four words standing up, and they were about the bed and not about the yard.

"**Three and a half degrees. Since the twenty-fourth of a month five months back. The line says nothing about that and it never will and that is what the line is for.**"

The month's costs went on four separate lines and were not folded into the shortfall: three licensed-man days at the rate in the hire book, one pound a day, being three pounds; the cart, which is hired and is not ours; the bench in a cart shed, which is this holding's; and a woman of fifty-six at no fee, and the fen entered that this holding could not have paid her and entered that there is going to be no line for what it cost.

Then the standing rule was read out in full, four lines, in the same yard, before the figures were closed, and the clerk read it and the reader of this body did not read it because he has not read it in six weeks and says the reason out loud.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month six months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. There is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The fen entered that the rule has now been read out in full six times across two months, five of them in last month and this one, and used twice in this volume, and that the two counts are two counts and are not the same count, and did not improve on it, **and entered that the third line does not say in a yard and this body read it as though it did for two years, and that a rule read out on the morning it is used is not a rule that is being kept, and that the mornings it is not used are the ones this body would have to give up first.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in and the count of that request is forty-six this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint and has never been a complaint.

And then the handoff, at about the ninth hour of the evening, at the north gate, in about nine people, and it is four sentences and the day it names is this book's.

"**The sixteenth of this month is a Tuesday. That is the day the well on the shelf is read, at the fourth hour, four hundred miles off, in her yard, with her nail, and this holding has no figure for the last one and did not write and ask and is not going to.**"

"**The first of this month is a Monday and it is the day this holding's compost line falls on, and the figure is on the page, and it is eighty pounds, and the run is eleven months not discharged in eleven, and I have not entered a column for the three of them and I am not going to.**"

"**And the day I entered three times in last month for that well was a Saturday, and the first of a month is a Monday, and the sixteenth of a month is the day she reads it, and the first of a month is not.**"

Nobody so much as one word was said, and the man of about fifty went to the gate in his own time and the woman who does the sums went with him as far as the gate and not past it, and nobody has ever asked her why and the fen is not going to.

The second of the month was a Tuesday and the yard was full by the seventh hour because a month turned on Monday and brings every household on the head in through that gate inside two days to look at a number that has not moved in three, and the fen entered that a body of sixteen households came to look at a figure that was the same figure it had looked at in a month three months back and in the two months before that, and that nobody said anything about it, and that the not saying was a fact about that yard and not a virtue.

The four lines on the door were read out in that yard, because the door is read on the first and the thirtieth and the four lines are read when the door is read, and about twenty-five people heard them and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and he read them back in the ordinary voice with the hour on it.

> **Four lines, on the notice, read with the door on the first of a month as they have been read since a month eleven months back, in the words of the notice and not improved:**
>
> **The return on the compost line is made on the first of the month and is not made on any other day.**
>
> **The return is a figure of the specification against the going, and is not a finding against any person.**
>
> **A household may read the return at the seed house at any hour and may copy it.**
>
> **No office is bound by the figure in this return and no office is the body that keeps it.**

The examiner read the fourth line twice, because she read it twice in the ninth month, and the fen entered that the count of the readings of that line is two and that it is read twice because nobody has ever been able to hear it once, and that this holding does not know why and has entered that it does not know.

And then the steward said one thing in the yard that was not asked for and was not about the compost line, and the clerk entered it and read it back.

"**The seed goes out on Thursday and Friday. It goes at the trust's cost. It goes in two carts and the seed-route ledger travels with the carts and the ledger is the trust's and is kept by the trust and comes back on the Friday and does not come into this building.**"

"**I am telling that out loud because a month turned this morning and a door was read twice in twenty-four hours, and in about four years somebody is going to read this month's figures and decide that the water and the seed belong to the same body, and they do not, and the page that says so is not going to be this one.**"

Nobody thanked her for it and she was not thanked and the fen entered that she said it to the yard and not to the engineer of record and that the engineer of record was about eleven feet away and did not take it off her and has entered that he did not take it off her three times this season.

The man of the north row with the cough was in that yard on the Tuesday and he had come back from the pan at about the fifth hour and he was carrying the rod because he had not put it down, and the reader of this body said the thing about the rod out loud to about nine people and it was the third time in two days and it was the last time.

"**Put the rod down and go home, and come back Thursday if you are coming back Thursday, and if you are not coming back Thursday then do not come back on Tuesday and stand in a yard holding a thing you have already brought back.**"

And the man of the north row with the cough said the thing that has been on the page about a man and a work for eleven years and is not going to be improved on by a woman with a rod in her hand.

"**I will put it down when I have put it down. I will not be told what to hold on a Tuesday by a man who did not go down a ladder on the thirteenth of last month and has said so in a yard ever since.**"

Nobody said anything after that and the fen entered that nobody said anything and that the two of them had said two sentences each and that the count of sentences in that exchange is four and that the reader of this body did not ask the last one and has entered that he wanted to and did not and that the not asking is a decision and is not a virtue and is the third of those this month.

# Chapter 193

## Roald Trench And Sena Wick

The sixth of this month was a Saturday, and the seed came out of the vault at the head of the flats in carts, at the trust's cost, and about nine people were in that yard at about the seventh hour and the count of what came out was made in that yard by four households in turns, the way a gate count is made, and the man of about fifty wrote it down and did not round.

Eleven carts' worth. Nine went south on that morning to a body in Cray that is nine hundred miles from here and the seed-route ledger travelled with them and the ledger is the trust's and is kept by the trust. Two did not go, and those two were the two nearest the wall, and the woman of that household said out loud in that yard that they were not going because they were not going to be needed and she was asked by nobody why and did not say.

The vault is a stone-and-turf store with a slate roof and a door of two boards and a lock that is not a lock. It was built by forty households of the migration at their own cost in a month three months back, and it holds the wet-line reserve of a season and the seed of a road, and there is no holder of it and no charter of it and no form on which anybody can be asked about it, and it is on ground the district calls Crown-held and has never held. It is four hundred yards from the north edge of the pan.

The two people in charge of it are Roald Trench, a man of about forty-four, and Sena Wick, a woman of about thirty-eight, and neither of them is of this holding and neither of them is a member of anything, and they are named on this page because the vault is named on this page and there is a difference between the two and this holding has been writing that difference down since the first month of this book and has never once been asked to act on it before today.

Roald Trench said the thing about the vault out loud in that yard on the Saturday at about the eighth hour, to about nine people, and he did not say it to the engineer of record.

"**We are not moving the last of it today. We moved what we could move in three days and this is what is left and it is two carts' worth and it is the wet-line, and the wet-line does not go on a cart that might not come back.**"

"**There is nine hundred yards of dry salt between this door and a road and about four hundred people on the other side of it that are not going to be on this side by Friday, and I have been watching that water come down since Thursday and I am telling you what I am going to do about it and I am not asking you for anything.**"

The reader of this body said one sentence back to him and it was in the ordinary voice and it is on this page and it was the first time in four months that the two of them had spoken.

"**You are eleven miles from a body that owns a licensed head and four pence a sheet of paper and you have told me what you are going to do and you did not ask me. I am going to write that down and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to thank you for it.**"

Nobody thanked anybody.

On the eighth, a Monday, and the ninth, a Tuesday, the corridor moved. The engineer of record was on the slope on the Monday morning and the licensed man had the head on a third turn, under the same form and at the same rate, and the water went south-west again and went further than it went on Thursday, and at about the eleventh hour of the Monday the man of about fifty walked out to the north edge of the pan at the request of nobody and came back and said the number of yards.

"**It is got to about three hundred and eighty-four. It was three hundred on Thursday and it has not done much over the weekend. It is coming down the line the water found and not the line on the sheet and the vault is at four hundred, and it is sixteen yards short of it.**"

And on the Tuesday, at about the seventh hour of the morning, the water reached the vault.

Nobody in this book put a person in a room about it and nobody in this book watched it. The water came up over the door boards at about the seventh hour on the Tuesday and the vault is nine inches above the pan floor and the pan floor at that end is a foot and a half below the middle, and a foot and a half of water over a floor is a foot and three quarters inside it, and the two boards of the door are two inches thick and they are the door and they are not a wall.

The man of about fifty went out at about the ninth hour with two men of the crew and the licensed man and got the door off its hinges from the outside at about the tenth hour, and by the time the door was off the water inside was at the level of the water outside and there was no air under the roof and there was no way in and no way out from the north side and nobody went round to the south side because the south side is four hundred yards of water that a man cannot cross in a coat.

What came down the road to this gate at about the second hour of the afternoon of the Tuesday, on a sheet, in a hand, at a household's own cost, was this. It was brought by a person not named in this book's book and it went up the fen road and into the seed house and the person who keeps the seed house read it and did not come out of the building with it, and the man of about fifty read it in a yard of about nine people and read it back out loud, and the hour went on it, and the clerk entered it.

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a sheet carried up the fen road at a household's own cost and not produced by this holding: **the vault of the forty households at the head of the flats went under on the seventh hour of this morning. Two were in it. Their names are ROALD TRENCH, of about forty-four years, and SENA WICK, of about thirty-eight years. They were taken out on the tenth hour and they are dead. This sheet says in its own words that it is written by a household and not by this body and that this body was not there and has not been asked and has not asked. The vault is not recovered.**

Nobody said anything in that yard for longer than a minute and the fen entered the minute and entered that it did not improve on it.

Then the reader of this body said four sentences, standing up, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and they are on this page and they are not a sentence about what it means.

"**I did not know either of their names four days ago.**"

"**I have not written and asked and I am not going to, and I am not going to say that the not asking is a rule or a principle or a care, because I do not know what it is and I have said in a yard in a month four months back that a man who writes things down is not a man who goes and stands in gates asking people what they have stopped doing, and I wrote that down, and I have not found a better sentence since and I am not going to look for one today.**"

"**A name entered on a page is not a person carried. That has been on this page in one form or another since the first month of this book and I have never had to say it in a yard about two people who are dead and I am saying it now and it does not do one thing for them.**"

"**And I am not going to write down what this means. That is not a finding and it is not a defence and it is not care, and if anybody in this yard wants to write it down they are welcome to and I am not going to stop them and I am not going to read it out.**"

Nobody wrote it down.

And then the man of about thirty-five came up the dry road on foot on the Tuesday at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, unannounced, and he was announced in a yard before he spoke, standing up, by the person who keeps the seed house, and about eleven people were in that yard, and she said that he is not of this body and is not on anything and is not a representative of anything, and that she is not going to start doing it differently for him.

He gave his name in one sentence and his position in about four and neither of them was a request.

"**Soren Rill. I am a guide and I am not of this holding and I am nobody's guide today and I have not been paid and I am not asking.**"

"**I came because I read on the eleventh of last month that the ground of the Pan softens under about nine inches and goes hard again at about a foot and a half, and I have read that four times in nineteen years and three of them were crews with a licence, and I am telling you where the rest of it is because there is a hundred and eighty yards of it below the third place and nobody has put a rod in it.**"

"**It will carry until the water is off it and it will not carry a cart after that and I have not been asked when that is and neither has anybody else and that is the whole of what I have.**"

Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked and he was not asked to stay and he did not stay, and the fen entered all three of those separately, and entered that the count of his refusals in nineteen years stands at four and is not five, and that he has not refused anything in this yard, and that a man who has not refused anything is not a man who has been made tame and the difference is the whole of what this book is arguing about with him.

And at about the seventh hour of the evening of the ninth, the reader of this body went out to the north edge of the pan with a rod and a line by himself, which is a thing he told nobody he was going to do and about four people saw him do, and he came back with water to the knee and stood in the yard at the wall side and said the number out loud to nobody in particular.

"**Four hundred and eighty-six yards. It was three hundred on Thursday and it was three hundred and eighty-four yesterday at about the eleventh hour and it is four hundred and eighty-six now, and it has passed the vault by about eighty-six, and it went about a yard an hour over the weekend and about seven yards an hour since Monday morning and I do not know which of those two numbers is the water and which of them is the ground, and I am not going to guess at it in a yard, and I am going to go out there tomorrow with a rod and find out and put the number on the page with the hour on it.**"

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the ninth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of the loss of a thing this body was not in: **the vault of the forty households at the head of the flats went under at about the seventh hour of the ninth of this month and two were in it and they are dead and their names are Roald Trench and Sena Wick, and the vault is not recovered and this body was not there and did not ask and has not been asked. The corridor of water in the pan measured three hundred yards on the fourth of this month at about the third hundred and eighty-four yards on the eighth of this morning and four hundred and eighty-six yards at about the seventh hour of this evening, and the reason it went at those two speeds is not established. The engineer of record said out loud that he is not going to write down what the loss means and that the not writing it down is not a finding and is not a defence, and nobody in that yard wrote it down.**

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything about what the loss was worth, and the man of the north row with the cough was not in that yard and nobody sent for him.

The man of the north row with the cough came up the road on the Tuesday evening at about the ninth hour with about two hundred people behind him on the road, and he had been at the pan since the second hour of the morning and he had not slept and he had not asked anybody for anything, and he said the one thing he had come to say to the reader of this body in the ordinary voice at the wall side of that yard and to the reader of this body and not to anybody else.

"**I got the door off the hinges at the tenth hour on the Tuesday with a crowbar and there was a man with me whose name I have never had and there is a woman on the north side who says she saw two of them go under the north side at about half past eight in the morning and I have written that down and I have not read it to anybody and it is in my hand and not in this book.**"

"**I am not asking you to put it in this book. I am telling you that it is in my hand and that it is in a woman's hand, and that if it stays in my hand then in about four years there will be two people in this fen who know it and one of them will be dead.**"

The reader of this body did not take it and did not refuse it and said the only sentence that was available to him, and it is on this page.

"**Then it is in your hand and I am not going to ask you for it, and I am not going to pretend that the reason I am not going to ask you for it is respect. It is that a thing in a man's hand is a thing in a man's hand and this book has spent four months arguing with offices about the difference between a thing on a page and a thing in the world, and I have just been shown the difference by a man with a crowbar and I do not have an answer to it this morning.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of the north row with the cough did not give it up and the reader of this body did not ask again, and the fen entered that neither of them asked twice and entered that the count of the asking between those two men in this month is two and both of them were the other one, and that is a thing this holding has never had on the page before and does not know what to do with and is going to write down anyway.

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the ninth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing held in a hand and not in a book: **a person states that he was at the vault at the tenth hour of the ninth of this month and that a person is in a woman's hand who says she saw two of them go under the north side at about the half hour past the eighth hour of the morning, and that the statement is in his hand and not in this book, and that he was not asked for it and did not give it up. The engineer of record was not asked for it and did not ask again. The fen entered that neither of them asked twice and that the count of the asking between those two men in this month is two and both of them were the other one, and entered that this holding does not know what to do with that and is writing it down anyway.**

# Chapter 188

## The Wrong Day In The Book

The twenty-first of this month was a Friday, and the reader of this body could not sleep past the second hour, and at about the third hour he went down to the seed house and took the book off the table and read the standing figures of the last month off the page he had read them off a month ago, and then he did a thing he had not done in eleven years, which was to open the second book and find the page in it.

The second book is the copy of the schedule of readings. It is a certified copy in a clerk's hand in a file four hundred miles and four days off, and the copy was brought up the fen road in a leather bag in a month four months back and put on the table in the seed house and has been on the table ever since, and in eleven years nobody has opened it at the hour of the day it was made for.

The examiner found the page on the Thursday and she sent word up the road by a boy at about the sixth hour of the evening and what she sent was not a question. It was **the sixteenth**, and the day of the month, and a page number, and nothing else, and the boy did not know what it was about and did not ask and got his supper for it.

So on the Friday morning, at about the fourth hour, in a yard of about eleven people, the reader of this body read out of this holding's own book the day of the next ordinary reading of the well on the shelf, and it was the first of next month.

"**The first of next month, which is a Saturday.**"

He had read it out in the same words three times in a month and it was in the book in his own hand in three places, and the yard heard it, and the examiner said the thing she says.

"**That is a Monday and not a Saturday, and I keep time and I do not round.**"

She said it standing up and she said it before anything was written down, which is her standing and has been for eleven years, and the reader of this body agreed with her in the yard and in front of about eleven people and did not ask how she knew, and the clerk wrote both figures down and read them back and the reader of this body said that he had that day out of his own head and that the head was two days out, and that the day number was the first of next month and the day number was not in question and the day of the week was.

"**Then it goes in as it is and neither one of them is improved on.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute and the fen entered the minute.

And then he said the second half of it, which is the half nobody in that yard was expecting, and which he had worked out at about the third hour of the morning with a lamp and the certified copy on the table.

"**She reads it on the sixteenth.**"

The reader of this body held the second book up so that the yard could see the page and did not read from it and said the rest from where he stood.

"**Not on the first of a month. On the sixteenth of every month, at the fourth hour, with her own nail and her own string, in her own yard, and she has done it for thirty-one years and there is a figure on that wall for every one of them. My book has said the first of next month three times in a month and the first of a month is not a reading day and never was one and I did not know that until about ten minutes ago.**"

Nobody in that yard said a word and the fen entered that nobody said a word.

"**Which means the reading was on the sixteenth of this month, and the sixteenth of this month was a Sunday, and it was five days ago, and it was taken.**"

He put the second book down on the trestle and kept his hand on it.

"**She took it. At the fourth hour on the Sunday, in her yard, with her nail. Nobody in this fen has to know and nobody in this fen has to be told and nobody in this fen had to ask her for it, and I want that said in this yard because I have spent a month saying that this holding does not write and ask, and there is a thing on that ground that I did not have to ask for and I have not got.**"

The clerk entered it and read it back and did not improve it.

"**The four lines went down the four-day road at that household's own cost, the way they went for the last one, and four days up and four days down is eight, and the reading was on the Sunday and it is the Wednesday, and they came in some time yesterday or the day before and there is nobody in this fen on that road to carry them, and a carrier on that road does not come here first, and this holding has no address on that road and did not write and ask and is not going to.**"

The reader of this body stopped there and the fen entered the stopping.

"**So there is a figure on a wall four hundred miles off that I do not have, and there always was going to be a figure on that wall that I do not have, and that is not a new thing and it is not a fifth thing to be added to the four.**"

"**A figure this holding was never given is not a thing this holding does not know about the world. It is a hole in this holding's own book. Those are two different shapes and they are not to be run together, and I have said that sentence about the two counts of four twice in a month and I am saying it a third time because this is the first time it has caught me out.**"

> Entered at the fourth hour of the morning of the twenty-first of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of a wrong figure in this holding's own book: **the day of the next ordinary reading of the well on a shelf four hundred miles off was entered in this book on three occasions in last month as the first of next month, which is a Saturday. The day of the week is a Monday. The day number is the first of next month and is not in question. The examiner kept time in the yard before anything was written down and said she does not round, and the reader of this body agreed with her, and BOTH FIGURES ARE ENTERED AND NEITHER HAS BEEN IMPROVED ON. The day of the month is the sixteenth and not the first, the well at that place is read on the sixteenth of every month at the fourth hour by the occupier with her own nail, and this holding has kept the date of it out of its own head for eleven years and had the page in its own book the whole time and had not opened it. The reading of the sixteenth of this month was taken. The four lines went down the four-day road at that household's own cost. This holding does not have that figure, did not write and ask, and is not going to. The count of the standing of the day and not the figure stands at three and is not reduced, and the fen entered that one of the three was at a day that was not a reading day and that this is not the same sentence as the other two and is not going to be made the same sentence as the other two.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The reader of this body said the count of his own requests for an initial against nothing was forty-five this season and did not elaborate and the clerk entered the count.

Then, at about the seventh hour, with about nine people in the yard and the light coming sideways off the cart shed, the engineer of record put the other thing on the trestle beside the second book, and it was a sheet of his own paper with a plan on it, and it was the same sheet he had carried for a month and had not shown anybody.

The pan is eleven miles south on the dry road, past the Pans well, and it is about nine hundred yards by two hundred at the low place, and it is a floor of salt and clay and it has not held water since before the four households on that road were born, and there is no holder of it and no charter of it and no form on which anybody can be asked about it, and the office nine miles off has a form for a licensed supply and does not have a form for a dry pan, and this holding has looked for one twice this month and did not find it and did not write and ask.

The plan on the sheet is a corridor. It is about two inches deep and forty yards wide and it is drawn in pencil and it is ruled, and it is dated the twenty-ninth of a month two months back with the hour on it, and the hour is the ninth hour of the evening, and the reader of this body said out loud that he had written it down on purpose so that a person in about four years could check whether it was right.

"**This is the plan. It is in this holding's book with the hour on it and I wrote the hour on it myself at about the ninth hour of the evening because a plan nobody can check is a complaint and a plan anybody can check is a thing.**"

He put his thumb on the line.

"**And there is nothing on that ground to carry it. Nobody has ever dug it. The salt is nine inches to a foot and a half over clay and it will not take a channel cut in it in a wet month and it will not take one in a dry one either, and I have known that since the second day of the month that has gone and I have been carrying a plan for a corridor that is not there since the twenty-ninth of the month before it.**"

"**Which is not a complaint about this holding. It is a plan. And the thing about a plan is that the day a body writes a plan down is the day it stops being able to say it did not know.**"

Then he said the reason for the plan, and he gave it as arithmetic and not as a reason, and about nine people heard all of it.

"**There are four hundred and forty-one people in a quarter nine miles off this gate with four wells off since a month three months back and about two hundred and thirty of them under the age where you stop counting them as children, and the licensed draw at the Pans well is the only water going into that quarter, and the holder of it is eleven miles off and is not a party to a quarter and does not come to one.**"

"**And there are people on the coast road behind them who want to be in front of them, and the only road in front of them goes south over nine hundred yards of salt that is dry now and was not dry in the year my mother was born.**"

"**So somebody is going to put water into that pan, and it is going to be somebody with a licensed head and a licensed man, and the question in this holding's book is whether it is going to be water somebody chose or water the ground found. That is the whole of the plan and the plan is on the sheet.**"

The woman of about sixty with four pails who asked which road in a yard four weeks ago was at the front of that yard and she said one sentence into the middle of it without being asked and she was not thanked.

"**It is the road they are on. I have seen the carts. Nobody has told us anything and we are not going to be told anything and we are on it.**"

The reader of this body did not take it off her and the fen entered that he did not take it off her.

Then the part that is his own hand and the part that is not, and the man of the north row with the cough was at the back of that yard with his back against the wall throughout and said one sentence at the end of it.

"**I will tell you the part that is mine and you can leave it out of the book if you want. I did the arithmetic on that sheet at the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-ninth of the month two months back, on this head, and the arithmetic said the ground does not take a cut, and I wrote the corridor on the sheet anyway, and I have carried it for a month, and I did not tell anybody that the arithmetic had already said no.**"

"**That is not a thing about a plan. That is a thing about a man with a sheet of paper, and this holding has spent a year writing down every time it did not know something and I have just gone a month with the one I did know in my pocket.**"

Nobody thanked him for that either and he was not thanked and he was not excused and the fen entered all three separately.

"**Then it is not a corridor you have drawn, it is a corridor you would like, and a floor of salt does not know the difference, and if you put water in the wrong place you will find out where the right one was.**"

The engineer of record read the second book off the trestle, shut it, and put it back on the table on top of where it had been sitting for four months, and the fen entered that the copy is on the table and not in the case and that this holding's own record of a thing four hundred miles off has been on the table the whole time under about four inches of seed list and a lamp.

"**Then I will find out where the right one is, and I am not going to find out by drawing it, because I have proved that I can draw.**"

The man of the north row with the cough was not thanked and was not asked to be in this yard again and the fen entered that the man has been in this yard four times this month and this holding did not ask him for any of them and does not know why he comes.

# Chapter 192

## The Water Goes Where The Ground Says

The third of this month was a Wednesday and about four hundred and eleven people were camped on the north side of the pan and about four hundred and forty-one were nine miles off in a quarter with four wells off, and the two numbers are not the same people and this holding does not know how many of the four hundred and eleven are from the quarter and has not written and asked and is not going to.

The fourth was a Thursday, and at about the fourth hour of the morning a licensed man opened the head of this holding's own ground under a form and at a rate and wrote the hour and the figure in his own book, and the fen was at the top of the slope and did not go down, and the reason was given out loud before anybody asked and it is the reason from the twenty-seventh of a month three months back and the count of times he has given it is three.

"**A body that stands over a crew is a body the crew talks to. This holding has four people on a floor and no custom for it and I was told that on the nineteenth of a month two months back and I have not improved on it since and I am not going to start with a head.**"

The licensed man's name is Coll Renner and he is on the rootwright roll and he is not of this body and he is not a member of anything that meets, and he came out at about the third hour and had the case with him and did not open it, and the clerk was not on the slope at all and the hour went in the licensed man's own book and not in this holding's, and the man of about fifty read the hour back off the licensed man's book aloud on the slope so that the four people on the boards heard a number said.

Then the engineer of record said the thing about the log, in the ordinary voice, with about nine people on the slope, before the head was opened, and it is the sentence the whole of the last three months has been walking toward without knowing it.

"**Nothing that happens today goes in the use log. A gate is not a contact. A draw gets no line and a hold gets no line and a contact gets a line, and what I am about to do is open a licensed head with a licensed man under a form at a rate, and that is a gate, and the log stands at fifteen lines.**"

"**The fifteenth is a contact on a boarded floor on the ninth of a month two months back and it is the last line, and there have been three things since then that got no line, and this is the fourth, and the reason the log stands at fifteen through a month in which this holding put water into a basin is that a gate is a licensed act and a corridor is not a licensed act, because a corridor is not a thing anybody owns.**"

"**I have spent two months writing forms into a book to stop offices doing things, and the thing I am doing today at about the fourth hour of a Thursday is the first licensed act anybody in this holding has committed in a year, and the log does not have a line for it, and that is not a hole in the log. That is what the log is for.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything and the fen entered the minute and entered that it did not improve on it.

The head came up at about the seventh hour. The licensed man had the gate on a quarter turn and the water came out of the head of this holding's own ground into a channel that had been cut on the Friday before, four feet wide, by four men, in two days, at about one in fifty-five, and it did not go where the plan said.

It went south-west.

The engineer of record was on the slope when it happened and he said the sentence out loud to about nine people and then said it again because he wanted it heard twice, the way he had said the third pail time twice a week before.

"**It is going the wrong way. It is three hundred yards south of the plan from the second place and the plan says north-east and it is going south-west, and there is no channel cut in that direction because there is no channel, because I did not cut one, because I did not know there was one.**"

"**A pan with a crust on it does not have a channel in it and I said that on the Friday in a yard and I said the man with a spade proved it in a month three months back and I wrote it down. And I was wrong about the second half of it. A pan with a crust on it has got a soft place in it, and the soft place is where the water has been, and I did not know where that was and I did not ask the man who cut a sod off it and took it home.**"

Then the hold, and it is entered and it is the fourth thing in this volume that this holding's own body has done that it cannot put in a book.

The engineer of record held a pattern for a crew of four for about two hours and a half, on the boards at the head of the cut, with the licensed man holding the book and the hour on it, and the man of about fifty on the slope with the book and about nine people in a yard at the top who came up to see whether a road could be got through and were not on the slope and were not asked to be.

Two of the four said out loud that the work went faster and two said nothing, and the fen entered that the two who said so were not the two who were strongest, and did not improve on that, and that the count of that observation across this holding's book is now two and both times it was the two who had done the most.

And the cost, in the engineer's own hand, in a line of its own, at the seventh hour of the evening with the door of the cart shed open and the clerk standing in it.

> **THE MARK IS HOT. THE HAND FROM THE WRIST DOWN WAS NO USE FOR ABOUT A DAY. A HEADACHE BEHIND THE EYES FROM ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON UNTIL ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR THIS MORNING, WHICH IS NOT THE MARK AND IS NOT A MARK AND IS NOT TO BE ENTERED AS ONE. TWO HOURS AND A HALF FOR FOUR PEOPLE, WHICH IS LONGER THAN THE LAST TIME BY HALF AN HOUR AND THE LAST TIME WAS TOO LONG.**

> And in the clerk's hand on the same line, and it is the clerk's hand and not the engineer's: **the four accounts of the arm in this book stand where they stood. THIS IS NOT A FIFTH ACCOUNT AND IT DOES NOT RECONCILE ANY OF THEM, and the fen entered that and did not improve on it.**

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-seven this season.

Coll Renner said the professional thing at about the third hour of the afternoon, on the boards, in the ordinary voice, to the four men and to the engineer of record and to nobody else, and he said it before he packed the case and he said it once.

"**A hold is not a draw and it is not a return and it is not a graft and it is not an anchor, and there is no form for it and there is no line in a book for it anywhere in this district, and the reason there is no form for it is that nobody has ever had to write one.**"

"**And I want to say the other half of that out loud because I have written a method down in my own book under my own licence for eleven years and I have never had to write this one. I do not know what it costs the man. He has written down a headache and a hand and a mark that is hot and a fourth account of an arm, and every one of those is a thing that happened to him, and not one of them is a thing that happened to the four men on this floor.**"

"**That is not a complaint and it is not a finding. It is a licensed man saying that there is a piece of work in this district that no licence covers and no book has a line for, and that the day somebody finds that out the first question anybody will ask is whose fault, and I would like it written down today that it is nobody's and that it was a Tuesday in a salt pan with four men who came because they were asked.**"

The engineer of record did not answer it and the fen entered that he did not answer it and did not write and ask about it afterward and that the not writing and asking is the fourth of those this month and that the count of the not askings is four and it is a month count and not a season count and he is not going to make it five in a yard.

Then the nine people who came up that slope at about the fourth hour to see whether a road could be got through, which is the same nine and about the same nine as came up a slope in the thirteenth of last month for a reason that did not come, and they were entered by what they came for and not by name, because a count would be a thing they did not come for.

A woman of a household in the low acre whose household's man was told on a Sunday. A driver on the road the carts went onto. A second driver's household. A woman of a household in the low quarter who has not walked up this road since a month four months back. The man of about fifty's household, which is at the top of that slope most days of its life. And a family of four from the camp on the north side of the pan, which is a thing the reader of this body had not thought to enter and entered afterwards, at the ninth hour, with the hour on it.

And one of the nine has a fever and he is not one of the nine and he is a household in the low acre, and one of the nine has lost a day of carrying that is not going to be repaid in anything, in money or in seed or in a week, and the reader of this body looked for about four minutes for a way to repay it and there is not one, and the fen entered that he looked, and that is the second time he has looked and the count of that is two.

And at about the eighth hour of the evening the man of the north row with the cough came up out of the pan with water to his thigh and did not come to the yard and went home, and the reader of this body did not send anybody after him and entered that he did not send anybody after him and that the reason is not that the man is owed something and is not that the man is not owed something.

And at about the ninth hour of the evening the man of about fifty came up the road from the pan and said one thing in the yard at about the wall side, to about nine people and not to the engineer of record, and it was this.

"**It is a foot deep at the second place and a foot and a half at the third, and the third is where the water went, and about three hundred and fifty people went across it between the second hour and the ninth hour today on foot and with carts, and the carts went through at the third place and not at the second, and the water at the second place is a foot and the water at the third is a foot and a half, and a cart does not care which is deeper, a cart cares which is firmer.**"

"**The whole of the plan was about where the water is deepest and the whole of the use is about where the ground is firmest and those are not the same place and nobody in this yard has ever written that down because nobody in this yard has ever had both numbers at once.**"

The engineer of record said one sentence back to that and it was in the ordinary voice and it is on this page.

"**Then the sheet is wrong in its second half mile and the corridor is three hundred yards south of where I drew it and the plan is on the page with the hour on it and it is not torn up and I wrote the hour on it myself at the ninth hour of the evening of the twenty-ninth of a month three months back, and a person in four years can look at the hour and the ground and see that I was wrong at that hour about that ground, and if I had not written the hour down there would be nothing to be wrong about except me.**"

"**And a foot and a half of water in a salt pan with a crust on it is not a road. I do not know how long it lasts. I have never known how long it lasts and it is the second thing on that sheet I could not finish, and the finishing of it is somebody else's job and I am going to write that down and let it be somebody's.**"

And so the fourth of this month, a Thursday, went down with a licensed head opened by a licensed man under a form and a rate, a use log that did not move and the reason it did not move, a pattern held for four people for two hours and a half, a mark that is hot, a hand for a day, a headache that is not the mark, the fourth account of the same arm in a book that has four accounts that reconcile none of one another, three hundred and fifty people across about nine hundred yards of water that is not a road, a corridor three hundred yards south of the plan, a plan on the page with the hour on it that is not torn up, and a sentence said out loud on a slope by a man who has been given no thanks for anything in this book and cannot be, and the man of about thirty-five was not on that slope and was not asked and did not come.

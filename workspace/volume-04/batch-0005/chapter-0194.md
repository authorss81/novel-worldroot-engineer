# Chapter 194

## Soldiers On The Dry Road

The eleventh of this month was a Thursday, and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon an inspection came up the dry road in the rain with four soldiers on it, and the road was held for two days.

They stopped at the gate posts of the seed house and they did not come past them, and the engineer of record was on the pan and did not come up and was not called, and the licensed man's case was in the cart shed, and nothing on this holding was open that was not open every day of a year. The officer of the district carried a book and a schedule of dates went with it and the book is correct and was read out in full, in a yard of about eleven people, and the reader appointed was the man of about fifty and he read it back out loud with the hour on it and the clerk entered it and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the desk at the sub-office at Fennmere, on the ordinary post, on the eleventh of this month, in a clerk's hand, four lines, with a schedule of dates at the foot, and it is made in the ordinary course and nothing in it is a finding against any person:
>
> **One. A use of a road that has been declared a licensed use is returned under the class assigned to it and at the rate in this schedule, and the class of a corridor of standing water across ground that is not a road is migration, and the class of migration is assigned by this office and not by the party.**
>
> **Two. The rate for a use under the first line is fourpence the hundredweight of goods carried. The rate is not a charge for the use of the ground and is not a charge for the water and is not a licence, and is not discharged by any return of water.**
>
> **Three. Persons conducting a use under the first line are stopped and counted at such places and at such times as are set out in the schedule, and are not hindered, delayed or put off by any person other than this office, and the office's men are posted to count and not to keep.**
>
> **Four. This is made in the ordinary course. No part of it states that any person has done anything, and no part of it is a finding against any person, and a party entered under a class is not a holder of anything by being entered under a class.**

Then the reader of this body put his hand on the third line and said the sentence that two months of arithmetic had earned him, out loud, in about eleven people, and gave the hour and the finding before the thing, the way he does.

"**A man on this road on the fourth of last month stood at these gate posts for an hour and a half with two men wet through to the shirt and I entered that a licensed man in this fen had never had a reason to be glad that a road had soldiers on it and did not pretend to anybody in that yard that he had one now.**"

"**I have one now. I am not going to be able to do anything about the rate on the fourth line in about a fortnight, and these four men have held a road for two days so that somebody in a county can write down how many carts went across a piece of salt with water on it, and one of those things is a wrong and one of those things is a correct, and I have had both of them in one week and I wrote them both down.**"

Nobody said anything after that and the fen entered that nobody said anything and that the officer did not answer it, and the officer did not answer it, and the fen entered that too and entered the reason, which is that the officer is not a person and the sentence was not a question and an office does not answer a question it was not asked.

Then the officer did the only thing an office does with a party that is a class, which is to say what the class costs, and he said it in the ordinary voice with the book open.

"**Fourpence the hundredweight. On about six hundred hundredweight a week going south over that corridor, that is twenty-four hundred pence, which is ten pounds, and the class is migration, and the class was assigned by this office in the eleventh month of last year and is not assigned by any party and will not be re-assigned because a party has become a party twice.**"

And then the reader of this body said the sentence, and it is the second time in this volume that a figure of this holding's has come back on a piece of paper, and the first time was in the third of last month.

"**Fourpence the hundredweight. I derived that figure out of my own two numbers in a yard about six weeks before this office said it.**"

He said it in the ordinary voice and did not say it to the officer and he did not say it to the reader of this body either, he said it to about eleven people who had all heard the two numbers.

"**One thousand and forty-five hundredweight over eleven days at a shilling and fourpence, which is sixteen pence, is sixteen thousand seven hundred and twenty pence, which is sixty-nine pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence, and the quarter paid fifty-two pounds five shillings, and the difference is seventeen pounds eight shillings and fourpence, and four thousand one hundred and eighty pence on one thousand and forty-five hundredweight is fourpence. The two agree and the two agreed in the third of last month in a yard and I read that out then.**"

"**And a woman in this building did that sum and said at the time that a figure she could check had come down a road in somebody else's hand and told her nothing she did not already have. I am not being reminded of that and I am not thanking anybody. I am saying that the figure on this page was on that page first, by six weeks, and that a body that has spent two months being put in classes and rates and forms is the body that had the rate in its own book the whole time, and neither of those facts is a defence of anything and both of them are on this page.**"

Nobody thanked anybody.

Then the sums, out loud, in the yard, in the rain, and the woman who does the sums did them and said the word before the figure and the word was the one she has been saying, and she did not enjoy it.

"**Six hundred hundredweight a week at fourpence the hundredweight. Six hundred at fourpence is twenty-four hundred pence. Twenty-four hundred pence is two pounds.**"

She stopped there, in the rain, with about eleven people standing in it.

"**A pound is twenty shillings and a shilling is twelve pence, so a pound is two hundred and forty pence, and two pounds is four hundred and eighty pence, and twenty-four hundred pence is not four hundred and eighty pence, it is five lots of four hundred and eighty, and five lots of four hundred and eighty is two thousand and four hundred, and that is ten pounds. I said two pounds a minute ago and that is wrong, and I am saying it is wrong in a yard in the rain rather than at a table on Thursday, and the officer of the district said ten pounds before I did and did not look at me when I got it wrong, and an office does not put a party right on a question the party has not asked it. The count of times this holding has got a sum wrong in public is two, and the first one was eight days in a month at a gate.**"

"**Four pounds is nine hundred and sixty pence and five lots of four hundred and eighty is two thousand and four hundred, which is twenty-four hundred pence, which is ten pounds. The two agree and the first one did not and I have entered the first one.**"

"**Now the other way, and then the first way again, and they are going to agree, because that is what this holding does.**"

"**Six hundred hundredweight at a shilling and fourpence, which is sixteen pence, is nine thousand six hundred pence, which is forty pounds, because forty pounds is nine thousand six hundred pence. Forty pounds less ten pounds is thirty pounds. And thirty pounds is six hundred hundredweight at twelve pence, which is seven thousand two hundred pence, which is thirty pounds, because thirty pounds is seven thousand two hundred pence. So the schedule rate costs ten pounds a week where the licensed rate would have cost forty, and the difference is thirty pounds a week, and the three of those agree.**"

"**Thirty pounds a week is a shilling on every hundredweight anybody moves over that corridor, and this holding has had that difference in a book since the third of last month as seventeen pounds eight shillings and fourpence on eleven days of a different season with a different number of hundredweight on it, and the two are the same arithmetic on two different sums and I am not going to put them on the same line.**"

And then the cost, said out loud in the ordinary voice by the reader of this body, and nobody in that yard argued with him and about two of them thought it was a speech and it was not a speech and he said so.

"**Ten pounds a week is the trust's and not this holding's. The seed on the other side of that corridor is the trust's and the ledger is the trust's and the carts are hired and the driver is paid and the fourpence a hundredweight is a rate on goods and the goods are the trust's. This holding is not going to be thanked for the corridor and could not have paid the rate and is not going to be asked to.**"

"**And the two days the road was held cost about four hundred people a day of carrying each, and that is eight hundred days of carrying, and eight hundred days of carrying is not going to be repaid in money or in seed or in a week, and I looked for about four minutes on Thursday for a way to repay one day's carrying and there is not one, and the count of that looking is now three and it is a month count.**"

Then the officer said the thing about the road, and it is the last thing an office says and it is correct.

"**The road is held from the fourth hour of this day to the fourth hour of the day after tomorrow and the holding of it is in the schedule and is not discretionary. The class is migration and the rate is fourpence the hundredweight of goods carried and the goods carried over that corridor in the last six days is a figure this office does not have and will ask a party for.**"

"**This office will ask a party for a figure it does not have, because that is what a form is for, and a party that does not have the figure writes down that it does not have the figure, and that is a complete answer, and this office will accept it, and it is not a refusal and it is not a statement and it is not a finding against any person.**"

Nobody obstructed anybody, nothing was delayed, and no door was shut in the face of a man with a book, and the fen entered all three separately and entered that they are the correct conduct of this holding and not a favour to anybody and that the count of that entry is four in this month and two of the four were on a road and two were at a counter, and that a body of sixteen households on one head has now entered that it did not obstruct anybody four times in eleven days and that four is a habit and that the fen entered that it is a habit and did not improve on it.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the eleventh of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, on the standing of an inspection: **an inspection came up the dry road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of this day with four soldiers on it, stopped at the gate posts and did not come past them, held the road for two days under a schedule, and read a book of four lines with a schedule of dates. The class is migration, assigned by the office and not by the party. The rate is fourpence the hundredweight of goods carried. Nobody obstructed anybody, nothing was delayed, and no door was shut in a face, entered separately as the correct conduct of this holding. The rate of fourpence the hundredweight was derived by the engineer of record in a yard six weeks before this office stated it, out of his own two figures, derived twice with the two agreeing, and that derivation is on this page already and was not improved on. The office will ask a party for a figure it does not have and a party that does not have it writes down that it does not have it. The seed is the trust's and the rate is the trust's and this holding could not have paid it and is not going to be thanked for the corridor.**

And so the eleventh, a Thursday, and the twelfth and the thirteenth went down with a road held for two days by four men whose business was counting, a class assigned in a county in the eleventh month of last year, a rate of fourpence the hundredweight that this holding had in its own book for six weeks before the paper said it, about six hundred hundredweight a week, twenty-four hundred pence that came to ten pounds three times, a first wrong sum of that woman in two months said out loud in a yard in the rain, thirty pounds a week of difference against a licensed rate, and two named people dead in a vault four hundred yards off, and about four hundred people on the north side of a corridor that is not a road, and nobody thanked anybody, and the road opens at the fourth hour on the day after tomorrow whether the figure is in or not.

The figure was not in. The office asked for it at the fourth hour on the day after tomorrow, in the ordinary course, on a form, and the man of about fifty wrote on the form that this holding does not have the figure of the goods carried over that corridor and cannot make it and has not been given it, and read it back, and the officer wrote one line under it and read that back too, and nobody was charged with anything and nobody was accused of anything and the road was open by the seventh hour.

"**That is a complete answer and it cost nothing and it took a man about half an hour, and it is the first document this holding has ever returned that was true and did not answer the question, and I have spent four months being unable to fill in a box, and it turns out the box has a line in it for not knowing, and that line is in the fourth part of the form and the fourth part of the form is the only part of it written by a person who thought about not knowing.**"

The reader of this body said that in a yard of about nine on the fifteenth and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is forty-eight this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that a body which has asked for an initial against nothing forty-eight times in a season is a body that has not found anything to put one against, and that is the whole of what the count means and it is not a complaint.

# Chapter 154

## A Place Can Be Dry

The fourth hour of the morning of the twenty-first of this month, which was a Monday, in the low ground four hundred yards below the north gate of this holding, at a boarded floor over a shaft that came off on the twenty-first of a month two months back and has not come back since, the instrument was laid on the boards by a licensed man and the reading of the test was written down before he went on with it.

The fieldbook is not in the case. The fieldbook is four hundred miles off in a room with a lock and it is not coming down that road and nobody in that yard was told anything else about it, and the clerk entered that the instrument is not the book and the book is not the instrument, and that the two have been confused in a room in this book before and that the confusion is entered so that a person in four years does not repeat it.

Coll Renner wrote the use-log line first, on the page, in his own hand, and read it back before the instrument went near anything, and Lissa Vale read it back again and entered that she read it and entered nothing else, and gave the reason in one sentence, which is the reason she gave in a year ago for the same thing: **there is nothing in this that a man with a rule can check, and a line in a book that cannot be checked is a line somebody will use.**

> Entered at the fifth hour of the morning of the twenty-first of this month, in the low ground, by the clerk, on the standing of the use log: **the fourteenth line. Date: the twenty-first of this month. Instrument: the case, at the boards over the shaft in the low ground. Person holding it: C. Renner, licensed. Purpose: contact, at the boards, on a shaft that came off on the twenty-first of a month two months back. Licensed person present: the holder. Contact tested with a plain wire gauge and the reading of the test written down before the instrument was laid on the boards. Out. Back: the same day, the seventh hour.**

The instrument came out of the case, and the three lines came out of it, and they are the instrument's and not a person's, and no document in this book quotes them as an instruction or as a reason, and the man who laid it on the boards said out loud that a floor is a floor and that this instrument reads a node and has never read a page and is not going to start because a man in a yard is in a hurry.

> This is not the same as the mouth.
>
> A place can be dry and not be empty.
>
> What is under a floor is not under your hand.
>
> Ground that is held is ground somebody is working.

He read them out once, in the ordinary voice, at about the fourth hour and a half, with four people standing on the boards above him, and then he put the instrument away and could not get the latch with two fingers, and the man of the north row with the cough carried the case up the slope with him because he asked and because nobody else offered in time, **and the mark on his forearm was hot for the rest of that day and his hand from the wrist down was no use for about a day afterward, and neither of those is entered as a mark and neither is entered as a new one, and the rootmark is four inches and branching twice and it is not longer this morning than it was on the eleventh.**

Then he said what the reading does and what it does not do, in that order, standing in about four feet of grass at the fourth hour and three quarters, which is his practice and which the fen entered as a practice and not as a virtue.

"**What I can defend. That floor is dry and there is water under it, and I did not put a rule to it and I did not dig and there is a shaft two hundred years old or near it with a boarded top and no bucket in it for four months. That is a fact about a floor and a fact about a date and it is not a fact about a cause.**"

"**And what I cannot defend. The third line says ground that is held is ground somebody is working, and I have got a floor that is held, or that the instrument says is held, and the instrument is a piece of metal that eleven seconds ago told me something I cannot check, and I have not got a man in this yard who can check it, and I have not got a shape of ground in this book that anybody has ever entered in a column.**"

"**And the thing I am not going to say. I am not going to say anybody is working down there. A thing being held is a fact about the thing and not about a person, and a man of about thirty-five told me in a yard on the fifteenth that he has read four pieces of ground in nine years and would not put a year on any of them, and he is not in this yard this morning, and I am not going to fill in a person where an instrument gave me a floor.**"

---

And at about the sixth hour, while nine of them were still on the boards, a man came up the fen road with a cart and did not go to the gate, and stopped at the low ground, and said four things in the order they are written here, and he was a man of about forty and he was not of this holding and his name is not in this book's and is not going to be.

"**The Pans well stopped at the fourth hour yesterday morning. Not yesterday afternoon. The fourth hour.**"

"**I was there at the fifth and I put a pail down and I pulled it up and I did it four times and there was nothing in the bucket that was not in the bottom of the bucket, and I am a man and not a clerk and I have hauled water off that well for two years and I have never once had it do that.**"

"**The holder's man is there. He came out at the seventh hour and he has a book and he has not said anything to me that I could repeat and I have not asked him any and I am not going to.**"

"**And that is all of it and I am telling you and not the district, and you can do what you like with it and I have not come nine miles for a form.**"

The reader of this body asked the question, and gave the reason first, which is his practice, and about eleven people were in that yard by then.

"**Say what you are, so that the clerk can enter it as a function.**"

"**I haul. I am not the holder and I am not the holder's man and I have never been anything but a man with a cart, and I am not going to be asked to be anything else, because the last time somebody asked me to be something else I said yes and then I could not stop saying it.**"

"So, in the yard, in front of about eleven people, and I am going to say the two halves and I am going to say the bad one first."

"**The bad one: this holding has a well four hundred yards below this gate that has been off for two months and a floor over it that this morning's instrument says is being held by somebody, and a man of about thirty-five told us on the fifteenth that he will not have a crew across it, and this body has no crew, no charter, no licensed man under a retainer, and no standing on the road where the water is. There is nothing this holding can legally do to a shaft it does not hold.**"

"**And the other one, which is worse, and which I am saying because I would rather say it than not. This holding has a head nine hundred yards from this yard with a leak of five-eighths of an inch, a beat in it that has not been logged since, and a draw out of it on the twelfth of last month that this holding is still paying for in sixteen households' water, and it has thirty households on it, and there are four hundred and forty-one people nine miles off with four wells off, and a well on a dry road eleven miles off that stopped at the fourth hour yesterday morning.**"

"**I am not going to say what any of those three is the beginning of. I am going to say that a body that has been a name for nine days has three facts in front of it today and that all three of them are about water, and that the ordinary thing a named body does with three facts about water is to go and get some, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said that before anybody else in this yard did.**"

---

And then the door was read out whole at the tenth hour in a full yard of about twenty-five, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-one, and none of the figures was softened.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the twenty-first of this month, in a full yard of about twenty-five. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month five months back, the fifth read twice. **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty-one.**
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.** The ninth month of a run of eight that has not been discharged in eight.
>
> **The leak.** Read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of this month, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up, at the six o'clock corner. **Three-quarters of an inch.** The reading of the thirteenth of a month two months back is five-eighths of an inch and the two figures are in this book and **this body will not say whether the difference between them is a movement or a season and will not be asked.** No cause has been looked for and none is entered, no due date is invented, and there is none.
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing written underneath it. **Column open.** And the fen has not opened it with about twenty-five people standing in a yard, and the column is a column of the rule, and the rule's third line says the stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book, and it does not say in a yard, and this body has read that line as though it did, out loud, in this yard, for two years, and it was never in the four lines. **And there was a draw on the twelfth of last month at the fourth hour of the morning that this holding is still paying for in sixteen households' water, and that is entered here because it is in every line of the rule.**
>
> **The boards. One hundred and ninety-five days.** Day minus sixty-six, the two hundred and sixty-first day of this season less sixty-six, cut into both this morning and said out loud.
>
> **The March's tin. Two hundred and forty-two days.** Day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**

And then the standing rule about the engineer of record was read out again, in full, four lines, in a yard of about twenty-five, because a rule read only when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of a month five months back, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called, and there is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The rule was used once, on the twelfth of a month two months back, and the rule permitted it, and the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop. **It is proposed that it be used again on the morning of the twenty-second, on a shaft in this holding's own low ground, and the first line of it is the line that decides whether it may be used at all.**

And the reader of this body put his hand flat on the bench and said the last thing of that yard.

"**Then I am going to say the part nobody in this yard wants said, and I am going to say it slowly, and the clerk is to enter it and I am not going to soften it in the book afterwards.**"

"**The first line says the draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying. I have read that line out loud in this yard about nine times in two years. I have never once had to stop and work out what the words mean, and this morning I have had to, twice, and both times I have got the same answer.**"

"**And the answer is: nobody in this yard can say whether the return under that floor is carrying. The instrument said the ground is held. The man of about thirty-five says a held ground is ground somebody is working, and he is not in this yard, and he is not a licensed man, and he has no book, and he is not a party to this body and cannot be asked in a room and be made to give an answer that goes in a book.**"

"So the rule cannot be satisfied tomorrow morning, and the rule has got no clause for a rule that cannot be satisfied, **and I have read every line of it twice this afternoon and there is no fifth line and there never has been one, and the man who wrote it wrote four lines on purpose and one of them is the first one and the first one is the one that stops us.**"

"And I am going to say the other thing, and it is the reason I read the rule out again today and it is not a good reason. **The rule was written to stop a man doing this on his own. A rule that stops him by asking a question only one stranger can answer is a rule that has handed him the whole of it because a stranger is nine miles away and does not read our book.**"

"Enter that. Do not improve on it. **Enter that a man of about fifty read a rule of four lines out loud in a yard of about twenty-five people and told them it was the worst-written rule he has ever read and that it is the worst-written rule in this book and that it is his own.**"

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a rule and of a limit: **the standing rule was read out in full and it is proposed that it be used on the morning of the twenty-second on a shaft in this holding's own low ground. Its first line requires that the draw not be begun on ground whose return is not carrying. The reader of this body entered that nobody in this yard can say whether the return under that floor is carrying, that the instrument and a man of about thirty-five are the only two sources in this book that have anything to say about it, that one of them is a piece of metal that cannot be checked and the other is nine miles away and is not a party and cannot be asked in a room, and that the rule therefore cannot be satisfied. The rule has no clause for that. The reader entered that the rule has handed its whole of itself to a man because the only person who can answer its first line is a stranger.**

# Chapter 151

## The Sixteenth

The fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth of the ninth month, which was a Wednesday, on a shelf four hundred miles off, in the yard of the fourth house at Cauldron Reach, a woman of fifty-four read her own well with her own nail and her own string and wrote it on the wall in her own hand, and this is the fourth of the month and the fourth month of a run and it is the only thing anybody on that shelf did that was measured.

**Five feet and one inch.**

It is in the plainest words anybody has used about it and it is how she said it and not how this book would.

She said the whole thing in about a minute and a half to the three other people standing in that yard, and the three of them heard all of it, and one of them has written it down since and two of them have not and neither of those two is a criticism.

"**Five feet and one inch. That is what the nail says and that is what the string says and I have read it the same way since the flood.**"

"**Now the other half, because I write both halves and I have written both halves on that wall since the first month I came up here, and the first half is the reading and the second half is what it is a reading of.** The sixteenth of a month four months back was nine feet. The sixteenth of the month after that was five feet and five inches. The fourteenth of this month two months back was five feet and a half, and the twentieth of it was five feet and four inches, and the thirtieth of that same month was five feet and two inches. The sixteenth of last month was five feet and three inches. **And this morning it is five feet and one inch.**"

"**Nine feet less five feet and one inch is three feet and eleven inches, and that is what a person writes on the wall and nothing else, and I have not written on that wall what the three feet and eleven inches is made of, because I do not know and because the last woman who told me she knew was telling me a thing she had heard.**"

"And the reason I do not write it is not modesty. **It is that a wall is for ever and a woman in a yard is not, and if I put a reason on that wall then in four years there will be a reason on that wall and a nail and a string, and a person will come along and read the reason and skip the nail.** That has happened in a book I have read. I am fifty-four and I have been on this shelf thirty-one years and I know what a reason on a wall costs."

Then she said the other thing, and she said it standing at the wall with the chalk in her hand, and the three of them heard it and one of them has written it down since.

"**There is no entry on that wall for this well coming back and there is not going to be one from me, and I have been asked three times in a season by men off the low road whether I think it is coming back and I have said no three times and I am going to say it a fourth time for the wall.**"

"A well is a nail and a string. I have got a rule for that yard and the rule is that I read it and I write it and I do not tell anybody what it is for. **Nobody on this shelf has ever needed me to tell them what a well is for. If you have needed me to tell you what a well is for then you have come to the wrong yard and I would rather you found out here than four miles down the road.**"

Nobody answered her, and the three of them did the ordinary things that four people do in a yard at the fourth hour on a Wednesday, and those things are the whole of the rest of that day on that shelf.

The wall is on the north side of the yard under the eaves, and it is a wall of coursed stone about eight feet long, and the writing on it is chalk, and it has been washed off twice in thirty-one years and rewritten both times, and it is in the hand of the occupier and it is the only record of the well that has ever existed on that shelf and it is not in anybody's book.

> Copied from a wall at Cauldron Reach on the sixteenth of this month by the occupier, at her own hand, in the order it is on the wall, which is the order the days came:
>
> **Sixteenth of a month four months back. Nine feet.**
> **Fourteenth of the month after. Five feet and a half.**
> **Sixteenth of the month after. Five feet five inches.**
> **Twentieth of that month. Five feet four inches.**
> **Thirtieth of that month. Five feet two inches.**
> **Sixteenth of a month two months back. Five feet three inches.**
> **Sixteenth of last month. Five feet and two inches.**
> **Sixteenth of this month. Five feet and one inch.**
>
> **Under the last line, in the same chalk and the same hand, and the only sentence on that wall that is not a figure: I do not write what it is made of. A. Ollvar.**

"She will not have it out," said the man with the spade, when a person asked him about it once, which was one time. "**She washed it off twice in thirty-one years and put it back both times and it is her wall and it is her well and there is a difference between a thing a man keeps and a thing four people keep, and the difference is that a thing four people keep has to be legible at four in the morning to somebody who is frightened.**"

The grass along the south wall was cut on the sixteenth, the same as it is cut every ten days, and it was cut by the man with the spade who has used it on that ground for twenty-six years. The west wall had a stone put back into it on the fourteenth and nothing was done to it on the sixteenth. **And there is a place in the stack against the north wall where a thing stood for thirty-one years and does not stand now, and the four of them have not agreed on a name for the place and have not agreed since the month before, and none of the four will say what the thing was, and this body has not asked and is not going to.**

And in the first row, which is the row of eleven faces, out of the collars of the trees that were let go on the tenth of a month two months back, the green that was cut back off with a sickle on the twenty-fifth of that month and left lying on the ground where it was left was still lying on the ground. There is a stake in the ground at each of two places in that row, because nine of the eleven faces in it were read with a rule and a lens on the twenty-fifth of that month and two were not read and were not guessed at, and the two stakes are where the two are.

And in the next row, which is the older row and the row nobody has read with a rule and a lens, there is green coming up out of the collars.

It is in the same words the four of them would use. It is in the next row and not in the first, and the first row is the row that was read, and the first row is bare collars with a sickle's worth of cuttings lying along it. It is not in the grass. It is not at the wall. It is along the older row and it is in the collars and that is the whole of where it is.

One of the four asked the occupier about it at about the sixth hour on the sixteenth, in the yard, and she answered him in about four sentences and she answered him the same way she answers a person who wants her to put a reason on the wall.

"**You want me to say it is in the next row and the next row is the one nobody read. I will say that much because it is a place and I have been in that row a hundred times. What you want is the other half and the other half is not mine.**"

"**Nine of them were read with a rule and two of them were not, and the two of them have a stake in the ground, and I did not put the stakes there and I have not looked at what is under the two stakes. And the green is in the row that was not read and not in the row that was, and I have thought about that for about two hours and I have got one thing out of it and it is a small thing and I am going to say it and then I am going to cut the grass.**"

"**A thing comes up where nobody is doing anything to it. That is not a finding. That is the difference between a thing and a finding and I have watched two men in my life not know it.**"

Three of the four have walked out and looked at it. The fourth has not looked at it and said, once, in the plainest words available to him, that he was not going to look at it, and the three who did look did not say anything to him about that and the fen does not know whether they said anything to him about it at all.

**Nobody dug at it. Nobody weighed it. Nothing came off that shelf in a vessel. Nobody on that shelf has said what it is, and nobody has said that the telling is still going on, and nobody has said that it has stopped, and the two of those are the only two sentences anybody on that shelf could have said that would have been a finding, and neither of them was said and neither of them is going to be.**

And this holding does not know any of it. **This holding last knew what happened on that shelf on the seventh of last month, out of a sheet, and it was four days late then, and it is going to be four days late again, and the fen entered on the sixth of this month that it does not know what is on that shelf and does not know what it does not know, and that entry has not been improved on.**

And then, at about the ninth hour of the same morning, at the fourth house, a thing happened that the four of them argued about for two days and that is the reason this holding is going to be four days late.

**The day letter was not on its stone.** It had been on a stone by the well at the fourth house for as long as there had been a well at the fourth house and it was a thing four households used in turn, and it was off the stone, and it had been off the stone since the morning of the eighth of this month, and it was still off the stone on the sixteenth, which is eight days.

Nobody had taken it. All four of them said so and all four of them were believed and none of them was thanked for saying it, and the fen has no way of checking one word of that because it is four hundred miles off and the thing was never in this book's book in the first place, because the letter is a thing the four of them have between them and not a thing anybody else may read on a day.

The four of them gave four reasons and the four reasons are not the same reason and the fen has three of the four and not the fourth.

"**One. I did not take it and I do not know who did.**"

"**Two. I have not been near that stone since the eighth and I am not going to be near it now and I do not care whose fault it is.**"

"**Three. It was not my turn and I have said so in this yard before and I will say it again: it was not my turn on the ninth, or the tenth, or the eleventh, or the twelfth, or the thirteenth, or the fourteenth, or the fifteenth, and I have said that in this yard and nobody has written it down and I am saying it again in front of you.**"

"**Four. I took it.**" The man with the spade said that and would say nothing else about it, and the three of them did not ask him again, and one of them said afterwards that he was not going to ask him because he had been in this yard for six years and he was not going to start, **and the fen entered that one of the four said that in his own words about himself and that it is a fact about a yard and not about a letter.**

The letter was found on the seventeenth, on the shelf above the fire in the fourth house, folded, with the chalk in the fold, and it had eight days of nothing in it, and the eighth day is the day it went off the stone, and it was found by the man with the spade, and he has not said in that yard how a stone came to be a shelf above a fire, and one of the four asked him once and did not ask him twice.

**Nobody on that shelf entered the eight days. The four of them wrote the sixteenth on it and put it back on the stone on the evening of the seventeenth, and what the four of them did about the eight days between the eighth and the sixteenth is their own business and has not been told to this fen and was not told to this fen for four days and this fen has not written and asked.**

And the green in the older row was not mentioned to anybody outside that yard in the same week, and the only person on that shelf who has ever written a sentence about it has not written one yet, and the fen has entered that it does not know whether the telling is still going on and has entered that it struck that question out of its own mouth once already this season and is not going to put it back in.

# Chapter 148

## The Eleventh

The eleventh of the ninth month was a Friday and the fen read nothing out in it, and the reason it read nothing out is that the ninth month's compost line does not fall until the first of the tenth, which is a Thursday, and this holding does not know what that line is and has said so out loud on the first, the sixteenth, the twenty-first and the thirtieth of last month and is going to say it again on the first of the tenth.

It said it on the eleventh anyway, in a yard of about eleven people, in the ordinary way, with the hour on it.

> Entered at about the eighth hour of the morning of the eleventh of the ninth month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the compost line: **the compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.** That is the ninth month of a run of eight that has not been discharged in eight. **The figure on that line last month was four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight and ninety-five pounds, entered in a hand that is not the steward's hand, and the price of a hundredweight at Cray is three shillings and elevenpence and this body has not written and asked why it came down.**
>
> **Two things on this holding's own figures do not move today and are moved by nobody.** The boards in the bay, **day minus sixty-six: one hundred and eighty-five days**, the two hundred and fifty-first day of this season less sixty-six, cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years. The March's tin, **day minus nineteen: two hundred and thirty-two days**, on a bed at three and a half degrees, unmoved, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty and the next reading day is the twenty-first.**

The reader of this body made the clerk read the rule out loud before he read either number, which he has done now on three reading days running and which nobody has asked for and which he says he is going to keep doing.

Then, at about the tenth hour, the man of about fifty stood at the wall side of the gate with his own book shut under his arm and did an arithmetic that is not about the compost line, and about nine of the eleven people in that yard stayed where they were and listened to it, and two of them did not and went home.

"**The waggon.** It went on up the plateau road on the seventh of last month at about the sixth hour in the afternoon, in the rain, at the back of three carts and behind a tarpaulin, and this body entered on the seventh that it does not know where it went and did not ask and that asking would have meant deciding something. That entry stands and I am not taking it back and I am not going to improve on it tonight."

"**The road is four days up and four days down and a man is four shillings a day and those are the two figures this book has been carrying since the flood.** Four days up from the seventh at the sixth hour is the eleventh at about the sixth hour in the evening, which is tonight, and it is a Wednesday on the shelf and there is nobody in this fen who can see that. Four days down from the eleventh is the fifteenth. So if the waggon comes down it can be in this yard on the fifteenth and not one day sooner, and if it does not come down then it was never coming down, and **this body has no standing on that road, has never had, and is not going to acquire one tonight by doing sums about it.**"

"And then the part I actually want said, in a yard, and I would like the clerk to enter the reason before I say it. **The arithmetic is about a road and the road is the only part of it I can check. I have got four days up and four days down and a shilling and I have not got one thing else about that waggon: not what is on it, not who is on it, not whether it is drawn or harnessed to nothing.** So the first thing I want entered is that this holding can compute a date and cannot compute a cargo, and the two facts are about the same vehicle and one of them is worth nothing."

Nobody said the first thing right. The reader asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is fifteen this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the eleventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure and a limit: **the waggon that left this yard on the seventh at about the sixth hour in the afternoon may be at the top of the plateau road tonight, on four days up, and may be in this yard on the fifteenth, on four days down, and this body has entered both figures and has entered that it has no standing on that road.** The person who gave the arithmetic stated the reason for it first and it is entered: **this holding can compute a date and cannot compute a cargo, and the two are about the same vehicle and one of them is worth nothing.**

---

And then, at about the second hour of the afternoon, a woman of a household in the low quarter came up the fen road on foot and stood at the gate and held out a sheet, and the sheet was written on the back of a miller's tally and it was not a complaint and it was not a request and it was a bill, and she said so in the first four words she said.

"**This is a bill.** It is not a letter and there is nothing in it I want. I have brought it up because there are four of us who have been doing the same arithmetic since the seventh and we would rather all be wrong together than be right separately, and one of us is a woman of about sixty and she does not come up a road."

Dunmar Fitch read it out whole in the yard with the reader and the clerk and about eleven people there, and it is four lines and one of them is a figure and the clerk entered the four lines and entered that this body has received it and is not able to do anything with it.

> From four households of the low quarter, on the eleventh of this month, on the back of a miller's tally, in a hand that is not anybody's in this book, and the woman who brought it said four words before anybody read it and the four words were that it is a bill:
>
> **One. There are four wells off in our quarter and there have been four wells off since the twenty-first of a month and there are forty-one houses.**
>
> **Two. A man hauls water into it at a shilling the pail and he has hauled ninety-five a day since the first of this month and not one day less, and we have the number because we have been counting at the gate of the yard he uses, one of us, in turns, and it is not his number and it is ours.**
>
> **Three. Eleven days at ninety-five is one thousand and forty-five pails, and one thousand and forty-five shillings is fifty-two pounds and five shillings, and the same quarter had nine hundred and ten pails bought in in the whole of last month at the same shilling, which is forty-five pounds and ten shillings, and we have both numbers off the same two books.**
>
> **Four. We are not asking this body for anything. We are telling it the number because the number is going to be in somebody's book this month and we would rather it was this body's number and got it from us than be a number somebody else made up about us.**

Anneke Roos did the sum out loud twice, in the ordinary way, and did it again with the two halves kept apart, and about nine people heard her do it three times.

"**Ninety-five a day for eleven days is one thousand and forty-five, and the four women and the four men who counted it at a gate did it in turns and their count is not a measurement, it is a count, and I am going to say that now before anybody puts it in a column with a pound sign against it.**"

"**One thousand and forty-five shillings is fifty-two pounds and five shillings. Nine hundred and ten last month is forty-five pounds and ten. So the same quarter, at the same shilling, has gone from forty-five pounds ten to fifty-two pounds five in eleven days, and it has not gone up by the price of anything, because the price is the same shilling it was in the middle of last month, and the only reason the figure moved is that the number of days is smaller and the number of pails is not.**"

"And here is the other half and I am going to say it slower. **This holding has four pounds and fivepence in a tin. The line on that tin is ninety-five pounds a month. The bill in front of me is fifty-two pounds and five shillings for eleven days and if it went on at that rate for the rest of the month it would be two thousand eight hundred and fifty pails, which is one hundred and forty-two pounds and ten shillings**, against a hundredweight specification of four hundred, and **I am not going to add those three numbers together and neither is anybody else in this yard.** A tin is a tin. A compost line is a shortfall of compost. A bill for water in a quarter nine miles off is a thing this body does not hold and cannot check and has no column for."

Then the man of about fifty said the sentence, and he said that he had been carrying it since the seventh and had not said it in a yard because there had not been a number to say it about.

"**There is a thing that has happened to this holding in the last month that nobody in this yard did.** Four carts and a waggon came up a road this body did not lay, and a miller nine miles off wrote down that there are four hundred and forty-one people at the bottom of a quarter with four wells off in it, and every one of us in this yard has read that number, and every one of us has been asked since then what this holding is going to do about the people, and not one of us has noticed that the same number has a price and the price is going up while we were talking."

"So here is what a body that has become a name actually is. **A name is a thing other people can spend. They can point at it in a room and say, that is the one, they did it, and then they do not have to carry the thing themselves.** A hundred and forty-one people have come into this district and every one of them has asked somebody for water, and a number of them have said a name out loud, and I do not know which name and I am not going to guess, **and if I find out I am going to put it in this book and I am going to put the date on it and I am going to read it back.**"

"**And I want the clerk to enter the other half of that, which is that a body which is spent is a body that has been useful to somebody and is finished, and a body which is a name is a body that has stopped being able to say no, because a thing that has a name has somebody to blame and a thing that has not got a name has to go and do the work.** We have a name now. I have watched it arrive in about nine days and I have not watched it arrive anywhere in eleven years of this book, and I would like somebody in four years to find this page and decide whether I was right about the week it came in or the day.**"

"**And I want the clerk to enter the other half of that, which is that a body which is spent is a body that has been useful to somebody and is finished, and a body which is a name is a body that has stopped being able to say no.**"

Nobody said the second thing right either. The reader read the second half back into a yard of about eleven and asked for his initial against nothing, and about nine people were in that yard and one of them was a woman of a household from the low quarter who had not said one word since the four lines were read, and the fen entered that she was in the yard and that nobody asked her anything and that she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the eleventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure this body does not hold: **one thousand and forty-five pails at a shilling, fifty-two pounds and five shillings, for eleven days, counted in turns by four households of the low quarter at a gate, and the last month's figure for the same quarter is nine hundred and ten pails, forty-five pounds and ten shillings.** The person who keeps the money entered the arithmetic three times in the open and entered that the three figures on this page are three kinds of number and are not added: **a tin at four pounds and fivepence, a compost line at ninety-five pounds a month, and a water bill for a quarter nine miles off.**
>
> **And entered under that, in the reader of this body's own words, read back: a name is a thing other people can spend, and this holding has become one in about nine days, and a body that has a name has somebody to blame and a body that has not got a name has to go and do the work.**

And on the twelfth, which was a Saturday, nothing was read out and about four people were in the seed house and the door at nine hundred yards off had thirty readings on its seventh column and no more, and the waggon was somewhere on a road four hundred miles long with a tarpaulin on it and this body had a date for it and no cargo, and the two figures were on a page together and one of them was worth nothing.

And in the evening of the twelfth the man of about seventy was not fetched and was not sent for and was not in the yard, and the fen entered that it did not fetch him, that this is the twelfth month of a body that has been keeping a book since the first year, and that the count of times it has brought him out to a thing is eleven as of the tenth of last month and has not gone up since, **and that this body entered on the tenth of last month that it does not know what his four words meant and is not going to find out this season and has not found out.**

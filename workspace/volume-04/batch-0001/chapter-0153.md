# Chapter 153

## A Return In The Ordinary Course

The sheet came on the ordinary post on the morning of the nineteenth of this month from the sub-office at Fennmere, and it is four lines and it is signed at the foot by the desk and not by a person, and it is correct, and it is the third-shortest document this holding has received in two years and it is the one that has put the word *licensed* into a yard of about eleven people for the first time.

> From the sub-office at Fennmere, on the ordinary post, on the eighteenth of this month, four lines, at the foot of a printed sheet with a date under a heading:
>
> **One. A licensed holder of a well may supply water to a quarter in which wells are off, at the tariff and under the conditions of the holder's charter, and a supply so made is a licensed use and is entered as one.**
>
> **Two. The rate for a supply under the first line is one shilling and fourpence the hundredweight, of which seven pence is the tariff and nine pence is the holder's.**
>
> **Three. The parties to a supply under the first line are to be identified, and are to be returned on the form provided, and a form not returned is a form outstanding and an outstanding form is a matter for the office and not for a third party.**
>
> **Four. This return is made in the ordinary course and no part of it is a finding against any person. Nothing in this line states that any water has been taken by anybody who was not entitled to take it.**

Lissa Vale read it out whole in the seed house with the bay doors shut, because a definition is read in a room and a return is worse than a definition when it has your quarter on it, and about nine people were in the room and the reader of this body read it back out of the book with the hour on it and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

And then the woman who keeps the money did the arithmetic, and she did it before she read the fourth line out, and she said so that she was doing it.

"**One thousand and forty-five pails for eleven days, carried by this body as one thousand and forty-five hundredweight, and I am going to say the carrying first because it is the part that is not a fact. A pail is not a hundredweight. The man who hauls says a pail is a hundredweight and the man who hauls is not a clerk and is not wrong about his own pail. This body has carried the two as one because carrying them as two would mean carrying a number it cannot get, and that carrying is a practice and not a fact and it is entered as a practice.**"

"**At a shilling and fourpence, which is sixteen pence: one thousand and forty-five hundredweight is sixteen thousand seven hundred and twenty pence. Sixteen thousand seven hundred and twenty pence is one thousand three hundred and ninety-three shillings and fourpence, which is sixty-nine pounds and thirteen shillings and fourpence.**"

"**And they paid fifty-two pounds and five shillings. So the difference is sixty-nine pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence against fifty-two pounds and five shillings, which is seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence.**"

"**And I am going to say that second time a different way, because I got it wrong the first way and I am not going to pretend I did not. Fourpence a hundredweight on one thousand and forty-five hundredweight is four thousand one hundred and eighty pence. Four thousand one hundred and eighty pence is three hundred and forty-eight shillings and fourpence, which is seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence.** Same. **Seventeen pounds. Eight. And four.**"

"**And that is not a fraud. That is the road. That is fourpence a hundredweight of carriage, and if you are hauling water eleven miles you may have it, and the office has written a sheet saying you may have it, and the man has charged a shilling, and the whole of the difference between those two numbers is a road and it is in nobody's pocket and it is the price of eleven miles.**"

Then she read the fourth line out, twice, in the ordinary voice, and the reader of this body read it back after her and the count of the readings is entered.

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the nineteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **the fourth line of the return of the eighteenth was read out twice in a room of about nine people and read back once, and the arithmetic of the difference between the licensed rate and the price of a pail was done out loud three times in the room and is entered as seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence over eleven days, and the person who keeps the money stated before the fourth line was read that she was doing the sum first, and stated that the difference is a road and not a fraud.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the same hand: a pail is not a hundredweight, the two have been carried as one by this body for a month, and that carrying is entered as a practice and not as a fact, and this body can check neither of them.**

Then the reader of this body said what the sheet does, and he said it standing, and he gave the reason before he said it, which is his practice.

"**Nobody in this room has been accused of anything. I want that said first and I want it said in a yard tomorrow as well, because in about four days there is going to be a person on this fen road who says that this holding took water that was not its own, and that person will be quoting this sheet, and he will be quoting it correctly.**"

"**So here is what the sheet does. A licensed holder of a well may supply a quarter in which wells are off. That is a good sentence. I have read it eleven times this morning and I have not found a thing in it I would write differently, and I have been looking for one since the eleventh of this month because I have wanted a bad one and I have not got it.**"

"**And it does this: it makes the water in that quarter a licensed thing, and it makes the people in that quarter the households served by a licensed well, and it asks for the parties to a licensed supply to be identified and returned on a form.**"

"And this body is a party. Not a holder. A party. **We hold no charter and we serve no household under a charter and that has been our standing for two years and it is the standing we are proud of in this yard on the first of a month, and it is the standing that makes this form ours and there is no version of the eleventh of this month in which the bill that came up that gate was anybody else's.**"

"So the count of things this holding has been told no by this season is **six**, and I am entering the sixth with the reason, because the other five were entered with reasons and this one is the reason the whole of this volume is about. **We cannot name a single party to a licensed supply in the low quarter and we will not try, and the reason is that there are four hundred and forty-one people in that quarter and we do not know which of them bought a pail and which of them was given one, and a form that asks us to say would make us the people who know, and we are not, and a body that puts its name to a form it cannot fill is a body that has begun inventing.**"

"Enter it as that. Do not enter it as a refusal of the office, because it is not one. **The office has asked a question and the answer is that we do not know, and the difference between not knowing and refusing has been the whole argument of this book for a year and I am not going to have it collapsed in a month because a piece of paper came in the post.**"

And then, at about the fifth hour of the evening, the reader of this body asked who loses, and it is his question and he gave the reason for asking it first, and the answer came from the man of the north row with the cough and it was not the steward's answer and she let him give it.

"**The low quarter loses. Not this body, not yet, and I am not going to say that in a way that is anybody's comfort. The low quarter loses four hundred and forty-one people the price of a road, every day, and it has been losing it since the twenty-first of a month, and the only reason it is a road and not a robbery is that a man with a cart and no licence is a supply and is not a thief, and about forty people in that quarter do not know that and four of them have said the other thing out loud and this body has heard it four times in two days.**"

"So: **one, the low quarter. Two, the hundred and forty-five households on this road that have not moved, who are about to be asked to fill in a form about somebody else's water. And three, and I am the man who has to say it and I have thought about it for a day: the man with the cart, who is losing nothing at all and is the only one of the three who is winning, and who does not know that he is in a book.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. About nine people were in that yard and the man of about thirty-five was at the wall side of it and had been since about the second hour of the afternoon and had not spoken for two hours, and the reader of this body did not ask him anything and entered that he did not ask him anything and entered that he had not decided whether he was allowed to.

---

And on the twentieth, which was a Sunday, a sheet came down the four-day road in a hired man's satchel, and it was written on the morning of the sixteenth at the fourth house, in the occupier's own hand, and it cost that household a rider and the rider's hire and this body did not pay it and did not know it had been paid and has entered that it did not ask the price, **which is the first time in a season that this body has been beaten home by a household's own expense and the count of that is now four and it is entered and is not going to stop being entered.**

Four lines, and the reader of this body read the whole of it out in the seed house on the Sunday with about nine people in the room.

> From the fourth house at Cauldron Reach, on the sixteenth of this month, down the four-day road, four lines, in the occupier's hand:
>
> **One. The well read five feet and one inch at the fourth hour of the sixteenth. I write it on the wall under the last one. The wall says nine feet for the sixteenth of a month four months back and the difference is three feet and eleven inches and I have not written what the difference is made of and I am not going to.**
>
> **Two. The day letter was not on its stone from the eighth to the sixteenth. It was found on the seventeenth on a shelf above a fire in the fourth house. We have written the sixteenth on it and put it back on the stone. We have not written the eight days and I am telling you that we have not and I am not going to tell you why not.**
>
> **Three. There is a green coming up out of the collars in the older row. Three of us have looked at it. One of us has not and has said he is not going to. We have not dug at it and we have not weighed it and nothing has come off this shelf in a vessel. Nobody here has said what it is.**
>
> **Four. The man with the spade has not spoken to the other three since the seventeenth. We have not asked him and we are telling you because you are the only party we have that writes things down.**

Nobody answered the fourth line. The reader of this body read it back into a room of about nine people and then he said the thing that is the whole of what this holding can do with it, and he said it in the ordinary voice.

"**We cannot write to them. Not because of the money. We have no charter, we have no standing on that ground, we have no party, and we have told you four times this month that we are a body of sixteen households on one head and a reader of a book anybody may walk up to.** There is no form on which this body can put a line about a man who has not spoken to three people in a yard four hundred miles off, and there is no address on the sheet for the person who would receive one, and **I have looked and there is not one and I am not going to invent a way of writing to four households we do not know in order to have the feeling of having written.**"

"So the fourth line is on the page and the fourth line is a fact about a yard and not about this holding and there is nothing this holding can do about it, **and I would like the clerk to enter the word nothing, and to enter it four times, because there are four lines on that sheet and this body can do nothing about three of them and the fourth one is the worst.**"

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **nothing.** Four lines from a shelf four hundred miles off, and this holding can do nothing about the first, the second or the third, and the fourth, which is that a man has not spoken to three people in a yard since the seventeenth, is a fact about that yard and there is no form on which this body could enter it and no address to write to. The count of things this holding has been told no by this season is six, and the count of times it has been beaten home by a household's own expense is four, and both counts are entered and neither is a complaint.

And the engineer of record said one thing that night, in a room with about four people in it, and it is the second time in five days he was asked for a figure and gave one, and the reader of this body was not in that room and has not been told what the figure was, **and the fen entered that the engineer of record was asked for a figure on the fifteenth and gave one and was asked a second question about it and gave a second answer, and that on the twentieth he gave a third answer to a fourth person, and that this body has not got the twentieth one and is not going to ask for it until somebody asks him in a yard where the clerk is standing.**

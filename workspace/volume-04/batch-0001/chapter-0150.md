# Chapter 150

## Four Days Down

The waggon was at the north gate at about the seventh hour on the fifteenth of the ninth month, which was a Tuesday, and it came down the plateau road in the rain and it came down with a tarpaulin on it and it came down four days, and the reader of this body was standing at the wall side of the gate when it came in and he had said the fifteenth out loud in a yard four days earlier and about nine people heard him say it.

He said it out loud again, before anybody had got out of the cart, and he said it to about eleven people and not to the waggon.

"**Four days down, and it is the fifteenth, and I said the fifteenth.** I am going to say the rest of it in the same breath and I would like the clerk to enter the whole sentence. **I have got a road and a date and I have got nothing else, and being right about a road does not make a man right, and if anybody in this yard wants to give me a nod for the fifteenth I would rather they waited until I have been wrong about something in public.**"

Then he asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and it went in, and the count of that request is sixteen this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

> Entered at the eighth hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure this holding gave in advance: **the waggon that left this yard on the seventh came into this yard on the fifteenth, on four days down, and the date was given in this yard on the eleventh and entered on the eleventh and read back on the eleventh, and the person who gave it asked for it to be entered that being right about a road does not make a man right.**

There were nine people on it and a seed chest, and the chest was lashed with a rope that had been tied and untied and tied again by somebody who was in a hurry about it, and the nine got down and stood in the yard in the rain and nobody told them to come in and nobody asked them a question for about a quarter of an hour.

The tenth person was not one of the nine, and he said so himself before anybody asked, in the first minute, in a form that could be read back.

"**Soren Rill. I am a guide and I am not of the nine. I came down with the waggon because I was going to the bottom of the road anyway, and I am not going to the fen, and I would like that entered first and before anything else I say, because everything else I am going to say in this yard sounds like a reason for staying and it is not one.**"

"**I am of an assembly of people who live in the old wood up the coast road, and I am not here as a representative of anybody and I have not got a paper that says so, and if anybody in this yard wants to write down that a man from the wood said a thing, I would like the words *said a thing* to be in the writing, and not the word *sent*.**"

And then, before the cloth came off the chest, before the nine had said where they had come from, before anybody in that yard had put a cup in a hand, he said the other thing, and he said that he had thought about the order of the day and had decided the order himself and that he was telling the yard he had done that.

"**There is a living root in the low ground at the bottom of this road, and I will not have a crew across it. I have not been asked. Nobody in this yard has asked me about a crew and I have said the sentence anyway, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said it first and that I have not been asked and that I would rather have said it in the first hour than on the fourth day, and that this is not an accusation against anybody in this yard and I have not made one.**"

About eleven people were in that yard. Nobody had said a word. The reader of this body read the sentence back into the rain, once, and then he asked the question that nobody else in that yard could have asked, and he gave the reason for asking it first, which is his practice.

"**Say what a living root is and say how a man would know.** That is not a challenge. I am asking it because you have used a word in my yard and I have to put it in this book and I am not going to put a word in this book that four people in it cannot check, and there are not four people in this yard who can check a graft and there is one of me and he is a licensed contractor with nine men and not enough work."

Soren Rill looked at the ground for a moment, and then he said it, and he said it slowly, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has said before and been argued with.

"**I will not give you a definition, because a definition would be a thing you could put in a book and check, and what I have got is not that. What I have got is this. I read ground. It is not a trade anybody has a licence for and I do not want one. You go along a place and you look at the shape of it, and the shape is the shape of water and of what water has been made to do, and there are shapes that mean a flow is being held and there are shapes that mean a flow is dead, and the two are not the same and they do not look the same to a man who has looked at a lot of it, and they look the same to everybody else, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is worth nothing in a hearing.**"

"**Now the part you can check, and I am giving it to you for that reason and not out of anything else. This ground has been in that shape longer than the charter on the well at the bottom of the other road, and I have put a year on that from the shape and not from a document, so do not put a year on it in your book. I have read four pieces of ground in nine years that were in that shape. Three of the four were crews with a licence and a schedule and a bill of work, and in all three of them the thing that was being held was being held because somebody was doing work on it every day, and in none of the three did the schedule say so.**"

"And I will tell you the fourth, because you will ask me about it in about four days and I would rather say it now. **The fourth had nobody in it. It is down the coast road and I have not been back to it and I am not going to, and I am not going to tell you what I found there either, and if you write that down as a refusal to be asked a question, that is the wrong entry and I will correct it in the room.**"

Dunmar Fitch entered all of it, and entered that the word *living root* is not defined by anybody in the room and was not defined by the person who used it and said that he could not have been, and entered that a shape of ground is not a measurement and cannot be entered in a column and has been entered in the margin and not in the body of the book, **and entered that the count of things this holding has been told no by this season is now five, and that the fifth is not a body and has no ledger and cannot be written to, and that the fen is entering the count anyway and is not going to stop entering it.**

Then the cloth came off the chest, and the woman carrying it said where they had come from and where they were going, and she said it in about nine sentences and she said it to the yard and not to anybody in it, and about four of the nine were still standing in the rain.

"**We came off the dry road eleven days ago with twenty-six trays and nine of us, and there were thirty-one of us at Tolbury and twenty-six trays, and we did not leave anybody. We have four days of water and we have had four days of water for six days. We are going down the coast road and not up this fen road, and the reason we are going down the coast road is that the coast road has a bridge at the third crossing and the fen road has a bridge at the first crossing and the fen road is eleven miles longer and there is no second bridge anywhere between here and the water.**"

"**We are not asking you for anything. I have said that four times on this road and I am saying it a fifth time because it is about to look like an ask. We have seed in a chest and a rope on it and nine of us and four days of water, and we are going to go down your gate and along your road and we are not going to stop and we are not going to come in.**"

And the reader of this body said the thing that had to be said, out loud, in front of nine people and about eleven of his own, and he said the reason for saying it before he said it.

"**I am going to say the true thing about this yard now and I am going to say it to them and not to us, because they have four days of water and we have a tin.** This holding has four pounds and fivepence in it. We have a compost line of ninety-five pounds a month that has not been discharged in eight months. **We have thirty of our own households on this head and we have not carried a day of anybody's water in eleven years, and the reason we have not is not generosity. The reason is that we have never had any.**"

"So: **nothing. There is nothing in this body for you and I am not going to pretend otherwise in front of nine people at a gate, and if any man in this yard offers you a bed tonight he is speaking for himself and not for this holding and I would like the clerk to enter the difference.**"

And the engineer of record went out of the gate at about the ninth hour and down the fen road four hundred yards to where the ground stops being this holding's and started being somebody's, and he was gone about fifty minutes, and about four people watched him go and one of them was the man of about thirty-five and he watched him go and did not follow him and the fen entered that he was not asked to and did not.

He came back with a rule in his hand and a wet coat and he wrote a figure in the back of his own book at the gate bench and closed the book, and the reader of this body was still in the yard and asked for it, and gave the reason for asking first.

"**You have been down there fifty minutes with a rule and you have written something and you have closed the book. I am going to ask you for the figure and I am not asking you for the reason, and I would like the clerk to enter which of the two I asked for.**"

"**Nine feet two inches.**"

"**Enter it.** And after it had been entered he asked the second question, which was the one he had said in the rain he was going to have to think about whether he was allowed to ask, and he thought about it for about nine seconds and then asked it in front of about four people."

"**Is that the figure.**"

"**It is a figure.**"

"**Enter that as well, and enter the two answers in the order they were given, and do not ask me which of them is the one I would have got if I had asked the second one first.**"

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the fifteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a figure and of two answers: **the engineer of record returned from four hundred yards below this gate with a rule and entered nine feet two inches in his own book and read out that figure when he was asked for it. Asked whether that was the figure, he answered that it was a figure.** Both answers are entered in the order they were given. **The reader of this body did not ask which of the two he would have got if he had asked the second question first, and entered that he did not ask, and did not say why.**

Nobody offered them a bed. The nine went down the fen road in the rain at about the ninth hour and about twenty-five people from this head were in that yard by then and the fen entered that twenty-five people were in a yard and offered nothing and were not asked to and that the count of households on this fen road that have not moved and are not going to and have said so out loud is still two hundred and eighty, and entered that this is the second time in a fortnight that this body has been in a yard while something went through it, **and that the first time was four carts and a waggon and the second time is nine people and a chest, and that the fen does not know which of the two was the movement and is not going to decide.**

And the man of about thirty-five was still in the yard after the nine had gone, because he had said he was not going to the fen and then had not gone, and about four people were still in that yard and the rain had not stopped and the reader of this body said the one question that was left in it.

"**You said you were going to the bottom of the road anyway.**"

"**I was.**"

"**And you are still standing in this yard.**"

"**I am.**"

"**Enter that, and enter the hour, and do not ask him the next question, because the next question is mine and I am going to have to think about whether I am allowed to ask it and I am not going to do that standing up in the rain in front of a yard.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **nine people and a seed chest of twenty-six trays came through this gate and went down this road in the rain and were offered nothing by this holding and did not ask.** Four days of water, six days carried, thirty-one at a place called Tolbury and not one left there, and the destination is the coast road and the reason is a bridge and eleven miles. **And a man who said he was not going to the fen and had not gone was still in this yard at the eleventh hour, and the reader of this body entered that he was not going to ask him the next question in the rain and entered that he had not decided whether he was allowed to ask it.**

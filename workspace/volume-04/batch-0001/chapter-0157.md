# Chapter 157

## The Hour

The twenty-sixth was a Saturday, and at about the third hour of the afternoon, in a yard with about thirty people in it, which is the largest this holding has had on a working day since the flood, this body gave away a pail, a measure, and four hours of a licensed man's time, and entered all three, and the hour was fixed for the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth to the eighth hour of it, and that is two days off and is in this book with the hour on it.

The reader of this body put the three things as three things and would not have them put as a gift, and said the reason before he read them.

"**One, and I am reading this as a cost and not as a favour. This holding has two pails and has had two pails for eleven years and nobody in this yard knew that until Friday morning, when a stranger asked for one. I want that entered as a fault and not as a lesson.**"

"**Two. A pail, a measure that will hold an exact amount and not a bucket, and four hours of a licensed man's time on the twenty-eighth, from the fourth hour to the eighth. The pail is the boards pail, washed on Saturday morning, and the wash is entered because the last thing it carried was a man's fever and the man does not want it recorded and it is recorded.**"

"**Three. The pail may not come back. He said so on Thursday in front of four people, the steward asked him who would bring it back and he said not him, and nobody in this yard has been given a promise and has been given a time.**"

Then the steward asked who loses, and the answer was the second this holding has ever given that it could not finish, and the first was on the seventh of last month about four carts.

"**We cannot tell.** And it is not a shrug and I am not tired."

"**On the seventh of last month I could not tell you what four carts cost anybody. Today I cannot tell you what a held ground costs, what nine people and four days of water cost, what twenty-six trays cost a woman who carried them eleven miles, what seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence of road costs four hundred and forty-one people, or what a man with a cart will do on the twenty-eighth with a pail and an hour.**"

"And the price of a migration is not a figure this holding is entitled to and I said that in this yard three weeks ago and I am not going to unsay it. **What I have changed is that this holding is now a party to one thing it cannot price, and that is new, and it is the first time in eleven years I have had to stand in a yard and admit that a thing I have done has a price I have not got.**"

"I am recommending nothing and I have got nothing to recommend anybody with, and I am not going to be thanked, and I would like it entered that I was thanked by nobody and did not ask."

Nobody thanked her. The reader read it back into a yard of about thirty and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and the count of that request is eighteen in a season, and the fen entered the count and entered that it is not a complaint.

And the licensed man was asked whether he was going with it, and said no, and gave the reason before the no.

"**No, and the reason is not that I will not go. A licensed man under a ground is a thing that can be written about afterwards, and I would rather there were four people in this yard who can say afterwards that there was no licensed man under that ground at any hour on the twenty-eighth.** I will be here at the fourth hour and at the eighth and I will write down both, and I will not write down what happened in between, and if anybody asks me in four years I will say that I was not there and that I did not ask."

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body has done: **a pail, a measure that will hold an exact amount, and four hours of a licensed man's time, from the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth to the eighth hour of it.** The pail may not come back. No promise was given and a time was given. The person who keeps the seed house asked who loses and entered that this body cannot tell, and that this holding is now a party to one thing it cannot price, which is the first time in eleven years. **A licensed man entered that he will not go, gave the reason, and entered that he will be at both hours and will not write down what happened between them and has not asked.**
>
> **The count of things offered in this holding and not had is three. The count of questions asked in a yard and not got an answer is two. The count of things this holding has been told no by this season is six, and the sixth was a form in the ordinary course.**

---

And on the twenty-seventh, a Sunday, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, in the seed house with the bay doors shut and about nine people in it, the reader of this body asked the engineer of record a question, and gave the reason for asking it first, and the clerk was one of the nine.

"**Is there anything in this holding's book that you know and that is not in this holding's book.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Enter it as that, and enter the hour, and do not ask him what it is, and enter that I decided that before he answered and not after.**"

"**Do not decide I have asked a clever question. I have asked the only question in this room, and it is the one anybody would have asked if anybody here had been thinking about anything except the pail, and there has been a pail in this book for two days and a man in a low ground on Thursday night.**"

And the engineer of record gave the count and the day and did not give the thing, and the reader of this body did not ask, and the clerk entered both answers in the order they were given.

"**On the fourteenth I gave the seedwright of the trust a date instead of a figure and she asked for it on the sixteenth. On the fifteenth I gave a figure in a yard and was asked a second question and gave a second answer. On the twentieth I gave a third answer to a fourth person. On the twenty-fourth I told her that the ground under the low quarter is going to move before the end of next month and I said the eleventh and I cannot defend it and I have not got a second reading.**"

"So: **four answers, and the count is entered, and the count of the four that are in this book's book is one.** I am not going to say what the fourth one is, and I am not going to be thanked for saying a count, because saying a count is not a thing anybody gets thanked for."

Then the reader of this body said the thing that is the whole of what he had come to say, and he said it to nine people and not to one.

"**Then you have told me a number and not the number, and the number you have not told me is the eleventh of next month, and I know it is the eleventh of next month because it is in this book, written on Saturday morning by the clerk as a date with nothing against it, and the clerk read it back to me on Thursday evening and did not say who said it, and I did not ask, and I am not proud of it this afternoon.**"

"**And the reason you are not going to tell me is that you would have to defend it, and you cannot, and you have not got a second reading. That is going to be a good reason and I want to say the other half of it first.**"

"**A man who cannot defend a thing and holds it anyway has not kept a private record. He has started one. And this holding spent two volumes and about eleven years getting to the place where it could say that it did not know something, out loud, in a room, with the hour on it, and it did that on the ninth of last month with about eleven people in it, and it was the best thing this body has ever done, and it was four words long.**"

"And I am not going to say the rest. **The rest is that a body that has a name can hold a thing it cannot defend, and that is how a name becomes a party, and I said in a yard on the eleventh of this month that a name is a thing other people can spend, and I did not think about that for nine days and I have thought about it for one, and I would like somebody in four years to find the two pages and read them side by side.**"

Nobody answered him. Nobody was asked to answer him.

---

And the twenty-eighth was a Monday, and the figures of the month were read out in the ordinary way in a yard of about twenty, none of them softened, and neither the reader nor the clerk said one word that was not on the list.

> **The compost line for this month.** Not entered. The figure falls on the first of next month, a Thursday, and this holding does not know what it is. **The ninth month of a run of eight not discharged in eight.**
>
> **The leak.** Three-quarters of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the twenty-first, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit. The reading of the thirteenth of a month two months back is five-eighths. **This body will not say whether the difference is a movement or a season.** No cause, no due date.
>
> **The beat.** Not logged since. Cause not established. Nothing underneath it. **Column open.** The rule's third line does not say in a yard, **this body read it as though it did for two years, and the rule was used outside one of its four lines on the twenty-second, and that is entered.**
>
> **The March's tin. Two hundred and forty-nine days.** Day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, both undertakings standing, **the drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column is thirty-one and the last reading day of this month is the thirtieth. The boards, two hundred and two days**, day minus sixty-six, cut into both this morning.
>
> **The standing rule.** Four lines. Used once, on the twenty-second, and used outside one of them.
>
> **A contact on the boards over the shaft in the low ground, on the twenty-first, at the fourth hour, with a licensed man, the use log's fourteenth line, and three lines out of the instrument. The mark hot for a day and the hand no use for two. The rootmark is four inches and branching twice and is not longer than it was on the eleventh, there is no second mark, and the burn that ran past it into the elbow is not a mark.**
>
> **And the well on the shelf.** Five feet and one inch at the fourth hour of the sixteenth, by the occupier, by her own nail, in her own yard. **Three feet and eleven inches against the nine feet of the sixteenth of a month four months back, and this body will not decompose that figure, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.** A day letter off its stone from the eighth to the seventeenth, the eight days not written on it. A green in a row nobody has read: three of four looked, one not, nobody dug, nobody weighed it, nobody named it. **And a fourth line saying a man has not spoken to three people since the seventeenth, with no form on which this body could enter it, and the word nothing entered four times on that page and four times here.**
>
> **And a pail, a measure, and four hours, given away on the twenty-sixth, with no promise and a time, and the pail may not come back.**

And then the man of about seventy was fetched on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth, the twelfth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and was given the three counts, the two sums, the three answers of the engineer of record, the date with nothing against it, and the pail and the measure and the hour, and he asked for the last count to be repeated and said three words.

"Write it twice."

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **a man of seventy-one was brought out for the twelfth time in a season, was given the three counts, the two sums, the three answers of the engineer of record, the date with nothing against it, and the pail and the measure and the hour, and said: write it twice.** Nobody explained it, he was not asked what it was about and was not going to be, **and the fen does not know what it means and has entered that it does not know.**

And then, in the same room, at about the fifth hour, the question was put in the fen's own words. It is a third question and it is not the first two, and the difference is on the page and the fen is careful about it.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a question, in this body's own words:
>
> **This body has carried two questions from a shelf four hundred miles off and can answer neither. The first is whose order it is. The second is whether the telling is still going on, and this body struck the second out of its own mouth in front of witnesses a month two months back and entered that it was the worse of the two.**
>
> **The third is not one of those two, and this body wants the difference in its own words, because a book that puts three questions in one column is a book about a body and not about three things.**
>
> **People are walking toward water. Nine of them went down this fen road on the fifteenth with a chest of twenty-six trays and four days of water, a hundred and forty-one more are at the bottom of a quarter nine miles off, and a man with a cart is filling pails from a well eleven miles off, and this body cannot tell whether they are walking toward a thing that is walking with them.**
>
> **The first question asks who gave an order. The third does not ask that, and nobody in this book could answer it, because an order has a person at the end of it and this body has not got one and has looked and not found one.**
>
> **This body asked the third question once, in a yard of about thirty on the twenty-sixth, and nobody in that yard took it. The count of the times it has been asked is one and the count of the times it has been available to a room and not been taken is one. This body is not going to find out. This body is going to keep asking. And a book that says that in its own hand is a book a person in four years can hold against it.**

Nobody answered it and nobody was asked to. The reader read it out of the book twice and did not ask who had said the thing it was about, and the fen entered that the two counts on that page are not to be added to the count of the first question, which stands at two asked and three available and not taken.

Underneath the day's work, dated the twenty-eighth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-fourth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and it says so on its face.

*A bill this holding cannot pay, and four hours it has given away. Nine people and a chest of twenty-six trays through a gate that gave them nothing, and a man of thirty-five who would not have a crew across a living root, on the ground of a shape that cannot be entered in a column. Three counts of one pail at three places, ninety-four, a hundred and fifty-one, two hundred and nine, kept by the examiner and not rounded. An office, four correct lines, the word licensed put into a yard, seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence of road over eleven days, named as a road and not a fraud. A use log's fourteenth line, and a word of four inches that did not grow. A draw at twenty past four in the morning with four people on the boards and no yard called, the rule used outside one of its four lines, the stop called at four feet by a licensed man who is not the man who did the work, and a well nine miles off that went off the next morning and has not come back. A man who refused the word nest and gave the reason, would not say what is under the ground, and asked for a pail, a measure, and an hour. Four answers from the engineer of record and one of them in this book, dated the eleventh of next month with nothing against it. A third question, not taken by anybody in a yard of about thirty. A man of seventy-one, saying three words. A fourth cart that went up the plateau road on the seventh and has not come down.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

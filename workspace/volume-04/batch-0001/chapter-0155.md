# Chapter 155

## Four Feet

The draw was begun at the fourth hour and twenty minutes of the morning of the twenty-second of this month, which was a Tuesday, in the low ground four hundred yards below the north gate of this holding, at a boarded floor over a shaft, and it was begun by the engineer of record, and **it was not begun in front of a yard, and no yard had been called, and the rule says the stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book, and it does not say a yard has to be called, and about four people were on the boards and none of them were a yard.**

Coll Renner had said the night before, in a room with about nine people in it, that he would call the stop at four feet and would call it wherever he was standing and would enter it himself, and the clerk entered that sentence the same night and read it back, and the reader of this body read it back and did not ask whether four feet was going to be enough, **and the fen entered that nobody asked him that and that the count of times the reader of this body has not asked that question is now two in a season and that the second one was the night before a draw.**

And then, at about the fourth hour of the morning of the twenty-second, in the same room and the same night, the reader of this body said one more thing and entered that he said it after the others had gone, and that about four people were in the room and that one of them was the licensed man, and that the count of people in that room is entered because a thing said to one man is a different thing from a thing said to a yard.

"**The third line says the stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book. It does not say in a yard. For two years this yard has read that line as though it said in a yard, including me, including the clerk, and I have entered the reading as though it were the line.** So: **the stop tomorrow morning is not going to be called in a yard, because there is not going to be a yard, and the rule has no line about that and there is no fifth line and there never has been one, and I am not going to write a fifth line tonight.**"

"**Enter the rule used as it is written, and enter that it was used outside one of its four lines because this holding built a custom of calling a yard and put the custom in its own mouth and never in its own book, and that the cost of that this morning is four feet taken out of a floor with four people on it and about nine people asleep a quarter of a mile away, and the cost of it is not a figure and I have got no figure and I am not going to get one.**"

Nobody said a word. The licensed man wrote the entry in his own hand and read it back and asked for nothing to be put against it, and the fen entered that the count of the times a rule of this body has been used outside one of its own lines is one, and that the count is one and it is not going to be improved on by anybody in this room.

It went to four feet at about the fifth hour and forty minutes, and the stop was called out loud at that hour by a licensed man, and it was entered by the licensed man, in his own hand, with the hour on it, and nobody in that low ground argued about the figure because the figure was in the rule.

Then it took about four minutes to come back up to where it had been, and it did, and the level of the shaft before and after is in this book at a hand's width of difference and the fen entered that a hand's width is a figure about a pail and is not a figure about a shaft, and that it is entered because it was taken and not because it is worth knowing.

And then the engineer of record sat down on the boards with his back against the pump housing and could not get up for about nine minutes, and the first of the four was not the hand.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the morning of the twenty-second of this month, in the low ground, by the clerk, on the standing of the rule: **the standing rule was used. It was used once. The draw was begun at the fourth hour and twenty minutes and was stopped at the fourth hour and forty minutes, at four feet, by a licensed man, in a low ground with about four people in it and no yard called. The line of the low ground is open in this column and nothing has been written underneath it and the fen has not opened it with about four people standing in it.**
>
> **The person who called the stop is not the person who did the work. That is the fourth line and it was kept, and it was kept by C. Renner and not by the engineer of record, and the licensed man entered it himself and read it back in the room that night before the draw and not in the yard after it, and the fen entered the difference and entered the reason the reader of this body gave for it, which is that a rule read after the thing it governs is a report and a rule read before is a rule.**

Nobody thanked anybody. Coll Renner was not thanked and said before he was thanked that he was not going to be, and the count of times he has said that in a season is eleven, and the fen entered the count.

---

The burn came up the arm in about half an hour and it went past the four inches and into the elbow, and it is not a mark, and it is not a second mark, and the mark itself is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and branches twice and is not longer this morning than it was on the eleventh of this month and will not be.

There were two accounts of that arm in this book in a year ago and they did not agree then and they do not agree now, and this body entered both of them again and entered that it does not reconcile them and has never reconciled them, and the two accounts are the reader of this body's and the engineer of record's, and the first says the hand was no use to the wrist down for a day and a half and the second says it was no use for two, and there is an hour at about the fourth that is in neither.

What it took, on the page, in the plainest words the yard could manage on the twenty-second and the twenty-third:

**Darkened veins, past the mark and into the elbow, on the left arm, from about the fifth hour of the twenty-second until about the third hour of the twenty-fourth. Fever, in the night, twice, and the engineer of record was not alone in the house on either night and has entered that he did not ask for company and did not send anybody away. Numbness in three fingers, the two middle ones and the ring one, and the third finger is the one he rules with. Loss of taste, from about the eleventh hour of the twenty-second until about the ninth hour of the twenty-third, and he ate a piece of bread on the twenty-third at about the fourth hour because somebody put it in his hand and he has entered that he does not know whether he tasted it.**

And a hand, and the hand is the part this book cares about, because a hand is a thing somebody else has to take.

**The hand was no use from the wrist down for two days. The man of the north row with the cough carried the rule and the case. The examiner carried the pail. The engineer of record did not hold a pail, did not hold a rule, and did not go down a slope without being told that the boards were wet, and the reader of this body entered that he was told once and not twice, and that the count of times he was told in two days is one, and that the count of times he needed to be told is not entered and cannot be entered by anybody but him.**

And then one more instrument, and it is nine miles off, and it belongs to a man who is not of this holding and is not a member of anything and is held by function in this book, and it is a chalk tally board across a mill race that the same man has read every milling day for thirty-one years.

> From a miller at Fennmere, on the twenty-third of this month, on the ordinary post, three lines, in his own hand, which is the same hand as the last four figures this holding has had from him:
>
> **Sixteenth: two hundred and forty-four.**
> **Twenty-second, the morning, before anything: two hundred and forty-four.**
> **Twenty-third, the morning: two hundred and thirty-three.**
>
> **And a fourth line with no figure on it, which is the first such line in nineteen years of that board: the wheel was not turned by the water on the morning of the twenty-second and I turned it by my own arm for a count of ninety and I have written what I did because I am a miller and not a clerk and a number without what I did to make it is a thing somebody will use against me in a year.**

Eleven. That is the whole of it, and the reader of this body entered it as a difference and not as a cause, and entered that the number on that board has been read by the same man for thirty-one years and that this body has been able to check it four times in two years and can check it now, **and that eleven on a board that read two hundred and forty-four four days running is a figure about a race and not a figure about a draw, and the fen does not know that the two are the same thing and is not going to find out by putting them next to each other in a book.**

Then the thing that is on the page with people attached to it, and the fen is not going to soften it and does not have a figure for it either.

At about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-third, a woman of a household in the low quarter came up the fen road on foot and did not stop at the gate and came the last four hundred yards down into the low ground, and she was the second of the four who had written the bill on the eleventh, and she said four things and the fourth of them was not about herself.

"**The well we were drawing off is off. It went at about the second hour this morning. It was off before that at the first hour of the month and it came on on the nineteenth and it has been on four days.**"

"**We do not know that it is you. I want that said in this yard and I want it said first, because I have had four men say the other thing at me in four days and I am not one of them and I am not going to be the fifth.**"

"**But I timed it. I have timed a pail ever since the seventeenth, in turns, at that well, and I have got one hundred and fifty-one and I have got it every day since, and this morning I could not get a pail of it and I stood in that yard for about an hour and a half and I timed it four times and there was nothing to time.**"

"And the fourth, which is the one I walked nine miles for and I am going to say it slowly."

"**There were five houses on our row that had water on the fourteenth. There are four. The one that went is not ours. And the man who has been pulling water into that quarter with a cart has been in that yard since the fifth hour this morning and he has not said where the water is coming from and I did not ask him, because he is a man with a cart and I am a woman with a pail, and that is how it is done down there and it has always been done that way and it is not going to be me who changes it this week.**"

The reader of this body asked who loses, and it is his question, and this time the answer came from the engineer of record, who was standing up by then and who had not said anything for about a day and a half, and the fen entered that he was asked the question and answered it and that nobody else was asked it, and that the count of times he has been asked it in a season is two.

"**The low quarter.** Five houses instead of four. One pail of water a house a day, if the water is bought, and at a shilling a pail that is five shillings a day across five houses, **and I am not going to do that sum out loud in this yard because I have not got a figure for how many days and a figure for how many days is the only thing that turns this into a number, and I have not got it and I am not going to make it up at half past four in the afternoon with a fever on me.**"

"And I am going to say the second half and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have and I am not going to be thanked for it, because the second half is mine and the first half is not."

"**I did it. I began it at twenty past four in the morning on a floor over a shaft with four people on the boards and no yard called, and I did not tell anybody in this holding that I was going to do it, and I told them afterwards in this yard at about the fifth hour this morning and about nine people were standing there, and I have not said why I did not tell them and I am going to say it now and I am not going to have it improved on.**"

"**I did not tell them because I did not want a yard. That is the whole of it and it took me a day and a half to get to the words.** I did not want about twenty-five people and a rule and a licensed man and a clerk standing on a floor while I did it, because I knew what the rule's first line said and I knew I could not satisfy it, and the only way I could get past that without breaking the rule in front of anybody was to do it when there was nobody there to break it in front of.** **And that is a thing you do when you have decided you are going to do a thing, and not a thing you do when you have decided the right thing.**"

Nobody answered him. Nobody was asked to answer him, and the fen entered that nobody was asked and that nobody answered, **and that the count of things this body has been told no by this season is six, and that this is not one of them, and that the fen entered that in a separate line on purpose, because a thing a man does to himself is not a refusal and must not be counted as one.**

Then the man of about thirty-five, who had been at the wall side of the low ground for the whole of it and had not said one word for a day and a half, said one sentence, and then he did not say anything else that day.

"**A held ground and a dry ground are not the same and I told you that on the fifteenth and you have been down there twice since and asked a piece of metal instead of asking me, and I am not going to be angry in a yard about it because I have better things to be angry about and you will find out what they are.**"

"**But I am going to say one thing, and it is a thing about a floor and not about a man, and then I am going up the slope.**"

"**You took four feet out of a place that was holding. I have read ground for nineteen years and a held ground does not empty in a morning and does not come back in an afternoon, and if a well nine miles off went off the same day you took four feet, then that well was not on the same ground, and you have got a floor under a shaft and a well nine miles off and a road eleven miles off and you have put them in one book and one of them is not yours.**"

And then he went up the slope, and the reader of this body did not call him back and did not ask him the next question, and the fen entered that he did not, and entered that the next question was the one about the flow, and that it was not asked, and that the count of questions available in that yard and not taken is now four, and the first was in a month two months back.

# Chapter 149

## A Party And Not A Holder

The sheet came down the March road with the bill and was read on the thirteenth, which was a Sunday, in the seed house with the bay doors shut and about nine people in the room, and the reason it was read on a Sunday and not a Monday is that a definition is read in a room and this was a guarantee, and a guarantee is a thing people sign, and nobody in that room was going to stand in a yard on a Sunday and be asked whether they wanted it.

It had come on the ordinary post. It was written on the second of this month, which is nine days back on a road this holding has never been on, and it is four clauses and a schedule and the schedule is a column of eleven numbers with a heading at the top, and the heading at the top is not a name.

> From the Salt March Dryfoot Trust, of Cray, on the second of this month, on the March road, four clauses and a schedule:
>
> **One. The trust stands behind the seed named in the schedule and behind the cost of it at the price named in the schedule, and it will stand behind that seed being planted in ground with a named return.**
>
> **Two. The guarantee is given to the holder of the well named at the foot of this sheet, for the households served by that well, and to no other person and to no other body.**
>
> **Three. Where there is no holder, the trust's duty is to the seed and not to the water, and the trust will deliver the seed to a road and will not deliver it into a ground whose return this office cannot name.**
>
> **Four. The trust asks for a receipt for the seed and not a receipt for the water, and the receipt is to be given by the person who plants and not by the holder of the well.**
>
> **At the foot, under a line: the Pans well, on the dry road, eleven miles. The holder of record: Garrow Nye, of no address that this office holds.**

Lissa Vale read the whole of it out, the four clauses and then the eleven numbers, and the clerk read it back out of the book with the hour on it, and the man of about fifty read it back after him and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and about nine people were in that room and nobody thanked anybody.

Then the reader of this body read the second clause out by itself, twice, with about a minute between the two readings and nothing in his face at all.

"**The guarantee is given to the holder of the well named at the foot of this sheet, for the households served by that well, and to no other person and to no other body.** I have read that eleven times this morning and I am going to read it out twice more in this room and I would like the clerk to enter the count and enter the hour, and then I am going to say what it does, in the ordinary words."

He read it out. Twice more.

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the thirteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body: **the second clause of the guarantee was read out in a room of about nine people, twice, on its own, and the reading is entered as three readings in all, and the count is entered because a body that says a thing three times in one day in front of the same people is making a point and not making a finding.**

---

And then he asked the question nobody in that room could have asked, and he gave the reason for asking it first, which is his practice.

"**Who is Garrow Nye.** That is the whole of what I have got out of that sheet and it is a name at the foot of a page and there is no address against it, and **this holding has had a name in its yard for nine days that it did not ask for and now it has a second name in a sheet and the two names are about the same water and this body is in neither of them.**"

"So I am going to say the thing about the sheet and I am going to be exact, because the sheet is not against anybody and it is not the kind of paper anybody in this room is used to. **This is the most ordinary piece of paper that has come into this building in a year.** No office has written a word of it. No form has been abused. Nobody has lied on it. Every clause of it is a thing a person who has nine thousand people behind them and not one well would write, and I have read it three times and I cannot find the sentence I would change and I have been looking for it since the thirteenth."

"**And it does not apply to us.** That is what it does. A guarantee of seed, given to a holder of a well, for the households that well serves, and this body holds no well under a charter and serves no household under a charter and has not held either since the eighteenth of a month two years back. **So the correct reading of that sheet is that a body of sixteen households on one head is not a party to it, and the reason is on the face of the sheet, in the second clause, in the ordinary way, and nobody in this room has to be told anything by anybody.**"

"And here is the sentence I have been looking for and I could not find until the third reading. **The trust is offering seed to a holder and asking for a receipt from a person, and the water is nowhere in the document except in the name at the foot.** I have had four months of a book in which water is the first figure on every page, and I have just been handed four clauses in which a body with nine thousand people behind it has written *the trust's duty is to the seed and not to the water* as a line, and I would like somebody in four years to tell me whether that was arrogance or the only way it can be written."

Tova Reed stood up, and it is her standing and has been for eleven years, and she said the thing she has refused to say four times in this book's history and said the first part before the second, which is the order she says things in.

"**I am not going to stand in this room and say that the trust cannot run a guarantee to a cooperative and I am not going to have the clerk write it, and I am going to say the reason in the form I say reasons in, which is that the reason is a form.**"

"**A thing that one person can do is not a thing a body can be asked to do.** That is the fifth time I have said that sentence in a room and it is not going to get better by being said a sixth time and the clerk is not to enter it as a refusal of anything, because it is not a refusal, it is a piece of arithmetic with a person's name in it."

"**And the reason the guarantee is given to a holder and not to a holder's district is not distrust and it is not law.** The trust sells seed. The trust does not sell water and has never claimed to and is not going to. **A guarantee of seed given to a body that cannot hold water is a promise about somebody else's water, made by people who cannot be there when it is not there, and if that promise fails the trust will hear about it nine days later on a road and the households will have heard about it in a yard the same afternoon.** Those are two different distances and a document is the only place a distance can be written down before somebody has to live inside it."

Nobody said anything kind about her. Dunmar Fitch entered that the person who said it was not thanked and had said so before she was thanked, and the fen entered that the count of that standing in a season is five and that no person in this holding has conceded any part of it to any other person.

Then the seedwright gave the one number she had, and she gave it before it was asked for, and she said that she had brought it nine hundred miles on the same road as the sheet and that it was the only thing she had brought that nobody at Cray had asked her for.

"**Cray's price on the second, for what is not seed: thirty-one shillings and fourpence a hundredweight. The price of the same hundredweight of the same bean in a yard at Fennmere on the ninth of this month: eleven shillings.** Twenty shillings and fourpence a hundredweight, which is nearly three times the local price, and **the March road is nine days and the carriage is in the same figure and is not in it separately because a body that is quoted two prices knows that.**"

"And I am going to say the thing about that number that nobody has said and I am not going to be thanked for it. **Twenty shillings and fourpence a hundredweight is the price of a road and not the price of hunger, and there is a difference, and the difference is that a road price can be refused by a person who has seed in her own ground and a hunger price cannot.** Nine thousand people in the March have seed and no water. They are not going to plant at thirty-one and fourpence. **They are going to walk, and they are going to walk toward something, and the only thing anybody has told me that any of them is walking toward is a road with a well at the bottom of it that is somebody's.**"

"I am not going to say who told them. I have not asked and I am not going to ask and I am going to enter that I did not ask, and I would like the count of that not asking entered beside the count of this body's not asking on the seventh, because **a body and a trust and a seedwright have now all three declined to write to anybody in the same week, and I would like a person in four years to look at the three of them together and decide whether that is a practice or a habit.**"

---

And then the steward asked the one question in that room that nobody else had standing to ask, and she is not thanked and is not forgiven and she is the woman who keeps the seed house and this is her office and not a courtesy.

"**Clause four. The receipt is to be given by the person who plants and not by the holder of the well.** That is a strange sentence and it is the only sentence in four clauses that anybody in this room has not read twice, and I have read it three times and I am not going to pretend to you that I know why it is there, and I know exactly what it does."

"**It means a person can be given seed and be given no water and still be owed a receipt, and the receipt is theirs and not the holder's, and a body that has a receipt has a piece of paper that says a person planted something in a place and did not have to be a charter holder to do it.** That is not nothing. In eleven years of this book that is the first time anybody has written down that a grower is a party in their own right and does not have to hold a well to be one, and **it is on the second page of a guarantee that was addressed to a man in a well this body has never met, and it arrived here by accident, on a Sunday, in the rain.**"

"So my question is the plain one and it is the only one I have. **The trust will put seed into a ground whose return somebody can name. This holding has exactly one ground whose return somebody can name and it is one head with a leak at the six o'clock corner, a beat that has not been logged since, and a draw on the twelfth of last month that this holding is still paying for in sixteen households' water, and I am not going to sign a sheet putting nine thousand people's seed into it.** Enter that I was asked nothing and that I said this and that I am not going to be thanked and that this is my office and not a favour."

Nobody thanked her. The clerk entered the four clauses and the schedule of eleven numbers and the refusal and the standing, and entered that **eleven of the eleven numbers in the schedule are the number of trays in the trust's own line and that this body can check none of them and has not asked to and that the one number it can put next to them is four pounds and fivepence in a tin**, and read it back.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the thirteenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body cannot be a party to: **a guarantee of eleven trays of seed, given to the holder of record of the Pans well, eleven miles on the dry road, and to no other person and no other body, of which the holder of record is Garrow Nye, of no address this office holds.** This body holds no charter, serves no household under a charter, and is not a party to the guarantee. The seedwright of the trust stated that the guarantee's being given to a holder is not distrust and is not law, and gave the reason, and the price of the same hundredweight on the March road on the second was thirty-one shillings and fourpence against eleven shillings in a yard at Fennmere on the ninth, and the difference of twenty shillings and fourpence was named as the price of a road and not the price of hunger.
>
> **The steward entered that the trust's fourth clause gives a receipt to the person who plants and not to the holder of the well, that this is the first instrument in eleven years of this book in which a grower is a party without holding a well, and that this holding will not put the trust's seed into its own ground and gave the state of that ground as a leak, a beat not logged since, and a draw on the twelfth of last month that this holding is still paying for in sixteen households' water.**

And on the fourteenth of this month, which was a Monday, about nine people were in the yard and nothing was read out, and the engineer of record and the seedwright of the trust stood at the gate for about a quarter of an hour on the business of a figure and not on anything else, and about four other people were in that yard and heard all of it.

"**Give me the number on the sixteenth.**"

"**I will have it for you on the twenty-first.**"

"**You will have it on the sixteenth or you will tell me on the sixteenth that you have not got it, and one of those two is worth something to me and the other one is worth nothing, and I have been in this book long enough to know which of them I am being offered.**"

"**You will have it on the twenty-first.**"

"**Enter it as the twenty-first.**" And she said that, and the clerk entered it, and the fen entered that the date was given twice in the same form by the person who had asked for it and by nobody else, **and that this body has been given a date to bring a figure and that a date is not a figure, and that the count of figures this holding has been given a date for and not received is one, and that the count is entered before the figure rather than after it.**

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the fourteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing owed: **the engineer of record is to bring a figure to the seedwright of the trust on the twenty-first of this month. He said the twenty-first when he was asked for the sixteenth. The date was given twice in the same form and was not read back by the reader of this body and the clerk was not asked to read it back and the person who asked for the figure did not ask him to.**

# Chapter 122

## The Relay

She wrote it for four days and read it out on the seventeenth, and the seventeenth was a Sunday, and about eleven people were in a room with the bay doors shut, and none of them had asked to be there.

She came in at about the tenth hour with the pages folded in four and gave them to Lissa Vale, and the steward took them and did not read them and stood with them in her hand for the whole of the time it took the room to fill, which was about six minutes, and about eleven people came into that room because a seedwright's door was shut and everybody in nine miles knows what a shut door in a seed house means.

Two witnesses were appointed standing up, before anything was read, and they were Jonas Byre and Gael Senn, and neither of them was asked whether they wanted it. Byre said he was in it because he is forty-eight and has been the person who remembers a practice in this holding for eleven years, and a thing said once in a room is a practice. Gael Senn said she was in it because the examiner keeps the books and there is a standing in this fen that the person who keeps the books hears a document before it goes in, and that the standing has never once been exercised and this is not the place to start it.

And Coll Renner put his own book on the bench at the head of the table and wrote the date on a fresh page and nothing else, and he said out loud, before anybody started, that he was there to be a witness and not to be a reader and was not going to write one word of what was said into his own book, and the clerk entered that he said so, and the man of about fifty read that back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

---

> Written by **Sera Quill**, in her own hand, on the seventeenth of this month, at about the tenth hour, in the seed house at Lantern Reach, with the bay doors shut. The steward held the page. A licensed man was present with his own book. Two witnesses. About nine other people in the room.
>
> Read out whole in the room. Read back to her in the room. The figures read back as figures.
>
> **This is a statement of records and not of events. I was not present at any of the following. I did not see a collapse and I did not see a body. Everything below is a thing I did. The arithmetic is mine and the arithmetic was mine then.**
>
> **One.** Nineteen years ago I was thirty-seven. I held a post in the **Continuity Office**, which is the office named at the head of the district's index of relay designs. **The name is on a paper in front of me. I am not going to say it again and I am not going to say what it means, and I would like the clerk to enter that I was asked nothing about it and that I would have answered nothing.**
>
> **Two.** The work was a Crown safety relay of my own design, to the western salt corridor. A relay of that kind is not a valve and it is not a pump. It sits at a collar, it watches the flow going past it, and it has one job, which is to say out loud, at a fixed point in the middle of a run of country, that the flow has gone above a line it is not to go above.
>
> **Three.** It could have isolated a pruning pulse before the pulse reached the three heartroots on that corridor. That is what it was for and that is the whole of what it was for. It was designed, built, tested and signed by me, and it worked. **I have no doubt in the world that it worked. I have tested nothing since and I was not asked to and I will not do it.**
>
> **Four.** **The red-line warning was removed by my hand.** On the fourteenth of that month, at about the second hour, alone, in a room with a hand lamp in it. I took the bridge out and I took the fuse out and I put the cover back on. It took eleven minutes and there is nobody in the world who saw me do it.
>
> **Five.** The maintenance log for the western salt corridor, for that season and for the one before it, was altered in the same hand I have always used, on the sixteenth and again on the twenty-ninth, and the words put in the place of the words that were there were **routine recalibration.**
>
> **Six.** I signed a report against salt creep and weather. **The report is a real report and every figure in it is a true figure about salt and about weather, and it is not about the corridor, and the reason it is not about the corridor is that I wrote it that way.**
>
> **Seven.** **Four thousand two hundred people died in the first winter. Nine thousand were displaced.** I am not going to be asked to add those two figures together and I would like it entered that I said so before anybody asked. They are not the same kind of thing and whoever puts them in one column should not have.
>
> **Eight.** What I have is the design, the alteration, the log, the signature and the arithmetic. I kept no copy of any of it. There is no extract, no note, no private ledger and no letter to anybody. **The only thing I have in the world is a piece of the machine and it is not a document and it is not paper.** I said on the thirteenth of this month that I had kept nothing and I am saying it again on the day I am saying the rest of it.
>
> **Nine.** **I was not at the Quiet Break.** It is a hundred and twenty-one years old. I was not born. I have never claimed to have been at it and I am not claiming it now, and any person in any room who sets that beside the four above should take it out again. I will say so once and only once.

---

And the room was very quiet for about as long as it takes to read a page twice, and the man of about fifty read the whole of it back, and the figures he read back as figures, and he read the four thousand two hundred and the nine thousand and the eleven minutes and the three heartroots and the hundred and twenty-one years separately and said the number out loud each time before he said the words around it, which is the standing of this yard.

And nobody in that room breathed out loud at the ninth line, and the fen entered that afterwards as a fact about a room and not as a judgment about anybody, and Coll Renner wrote the date on the page in his own book and turned the book round so that the room could see that there was nothing on the page but the date.

And Dunmar Fitch entered the hour, which was the tenth hour of the seventeenth, and read the entry back before he initialled it, and Lissa Vale read it back after him, and there was no note under section nine for the seventeenth and the reason is that a document is not an argument.

And one man in that room asked one question, and it was the engineer of record, and he asked it at about the eleventh hour with the page still in the steward's hand and his own arm not in a sleeve.

"How long did it take you to decide."

And she did not answer him.

And the fen entered that the question is in the book and that the answer is not going to be asked for, and entered that the reason she would not give it is not entered and is not going to be asked for either, and entered that the question was available to anybody in that room on a Sunday with the doors shut and was taken by one person, and that the reason it was taken by that person and not by somebody else is not in the book.

Then the man of about fifty said one thing about it and it is in the book and it is the only thing anybody said in that room about the question.

"Twenty-two years," he said. "A licensed man in this fen has been licensed twenty-two years and he wrote *not answering for a draw* in his own hand on the strength of one bad afternoon. **I am not going to stand in a room and tell that woman that the number of minutes it takes a person to decide is a small thing. It is a figure and I have not got it and this body is never going to have it, and that is the first figure in two volumes that a body of this kind has wanted more than anything and cannot have.**"

And then he said the half that is his standing, and he said it to the room and not to her, and she did not look up.

"And I am not going to ask you a second question to make up for the one you did not answer, and I am not going to leave the room. **A thing a woman says in a room that a man cannot check is not safer for being unread. It is only quieter, and quiet is not the same as sound.** I have said it in front of two witnesses so that it is in the book twice."

---

And then the cost of it, and the fen entered it in the ordinary words at the fourth hour of the afternoon with about nine people still in the room and none of them leaving.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the seventeenth of this month, in the seed house, in the ordinary words, on the standing of the money, which is the standing of Anneke Roos and is that a cost is entered as a cost and not as a fault and not as a virtue:
>
> **A woman of fifty-six came into this yard on the eleventh of this month at the ninth hour, on foot, off the Glassward road. This is the seventh day. A woman of fifty-six has given six days of a fortnight's living to a room in a fen and has asked for nothing and is not to be thanked and cannot be.**
>
> The keep is entered at three shillings and eightpence for the fortnight and eleven shillings for the licensed man, the second half of the fortnight is entered on the twenty-fifth, the second blanket at twopence is entered already, and the hire of the man who went up on the twelfth is entered in two parts, four shillings on the twelfth and twenty-eight on the sixteenth, and the whole of it is thirty-two shillings for eight days of a road that is four days, and **this holding did not have thirty-two shillings and took them out of that line anyway**, and he is paid for a sheet of paper with one figure on it, and that figure is not in this book.
>
> **She asked for no fee and the fen entered no fee and paid it anyway, and the two halves of that are on the same line of the same book and the fen does not get to be only one of them.** That sentence was entered on the eleventh by the woman who writes the money in it, and it is repeated here on the day of the statement because a body that has entered it once may be found out having learned nothing.

And then the man of about seventy, who had been at the back with the blanket over his knees for the whole of it and had not said one word, asked for the page to be given to him for about a minute, and gave it back, and asked that four words go in beside the four thousand two hundred.

"It is not *killed*," he said. "Put in *died in the first winter*, in her words, and put it beside the four thousand two hundred and do not let anybody shorten it for want of a line. **I have watched men shorten a number into a word and the word has gone into a return and the number has gone into nobody.** Read that back and put my initial against nothing, which is my standing, and do not thank anybody in this room, because not one person in it has done anything that can be thanked and the woman least of all."

---

And on the eighteenth, which was a Monday, the fen did the thing it does with anything a woman says in a room, and it did it standing up in a yard at about the ninth hour with about eleven people in it, and nobody thanked anybody.

"It is in the book," said Dunmar Fitch. "All nine lines, the hour, the room, the number of people in it, the two witnesses, the name of the office on a paper in front of her, and the fact that she was asked nothing about it. **And the fen entered this morning that it holds a statement it cannot corroborate, and it entered the reason, and the reason is that a thing a woman says in a room cannot be checked by an office in nine miles because there is no paper, and cannot be checked by a licensed man because a reading is not a witness, and cannot be checked by her.**"

"That is the entry, and it is not a finding. Nothing in this fortnight is a finding, and anybody in nine miles who wants a finding out of this body can have it when somebody produces a document, and the woman who could have produced it chose not to, nineteen years before she came down this road."

And then the man of about fifty asked the question he asks after anything, standing in the yard, with his hat off, and it is the standing of this holding and it has been asked nine times this season.

"Name me one thing in this holding that the statement changed," he said. "Not one thing she said. One thing we do."

And Lissa Vale read the ledger on the bench of the seed house in the open and counted, out loud, in front of about eleven people, and did it twice, and got the same answer twice, which is her practice and which she has done with figures for four years.

"The route is written as the second of this month wrote it, six clauses, the fifth of them unchanged. The door is the door. The books are the books and the shut drawer is shut and the fieldbook is four hundred miles off in a locked room. The eleven cuts are eleven cuts, nine read, and the two that are not read are not read. The stack is a stack and the bevel is in two places. The compost line's next figure is not known. The well is five feet and three inches as of the sixteenth and is a household's problem on a shelf. The beat is logged once, is not logged since, and the column is open and we have not opened it. The leak is five-eighths and has not been measured since the first of this month. The second draw is on a table and has not been decided."

"**Not one of them moved. And every one of them is on this yard's list because this yard publishes things.** I want that read back, because in three years somebody in nine miles is going to find that page and think this statement was important, and it was important to about eleven people in a room, and it changed nothing on that list, and the reason is that **the list was the work and the statement was never going to be the work.**"

Underneath the day's work, dated the eighteenth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-third note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Nine lines in a woman's own hand, read out whole on a Sunday with the bay doors shut, a steward holding the page and a licensed man present with his own book and nothing on the page but a date. A records witness saying on her own first line that she was not present for any of it, and on her ninth line that she was not at a thing a hundred and twenty-one years old. A red-line warning taken out by one hand in eleven minutes. A log altered in the same hand twice and the words put in were *routine recalibration*. A report signed against salt creep and weather, every figure in it true and the corridor not mentioned. Four thousand two hundred who died in the first winter and nine thousand who were displaced, with the reader reading both numbers out loud and the seventy-year-old man having *died in the first winter* put in beside the first of them. A question asked in a room by the engineer of record and not answered, and the man of about fifty saying that it is the first figure in two volumes this body has wanted more than anything and cannot have. A cost entered as a cost: six days of a fortnight's living, no fee asked for and none entered and paid anyway, on one line. And on the eighteenth a fen standing in a yard saying that it holds a statement it cannot corroborate, and why, and that nothing in this fortnight is a finding, and then naming every figure on its own list and being told that not one of them moved.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

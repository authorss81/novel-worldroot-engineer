# Chapter 127

## Where It Lives

Lissa Vale said it in one sentence on the twenty-seventh of this month at about the ninth hour in the yard, standing at the gate with the ledger shut under her arm, and it is her sentence and not anybody else's, and she is not a lawyer and has never been one and does not claim to be, and the book has entered that twice this season and that the second time is because it stopped being a compliment and became a standing.

"The fifth clause of the second of this month says the record of this route is one record and no person holding any part of it keeps a private copy of any part of it. **A collar section is not the record of the route.**"

And that is the whole of it, and it took the rest of the morning to find out what it means, and what it means is that this body has just acquired a thing it cannot put anywhere, and has known that it cannot put it anywhere since the nineteenth of this month, and has spent eight days not saying so.

---

Because there are four places a record goes in this holding and there has never been a fifth.

It goes in the seed house book, which is the fen's own record and is the only one anybody in nine miles could ask to read. It goes on a return of inventories, which is a list and not a copy and which has six lines and a district holding number at the foot of it. It goes to Cray on the ordinary post, which is the other copy of the same record. And it goes to a place, and there are four places in this district that will hold a thing for nothing, forever, and one of them offered to this body in writing on a rider's letterhead and was declined in writing in one line on the fifteenth of last month.

**None of the four will take a piece of brass with a cut in it.**

"It is not a record and it is not a copy of a record," said Dunmar Fitch. "It is not a list and it is not on a list, because a return asks what part a record falls in and there is no part for this, and the office's own line on the fifth of this month says that a holder who states a part has stated a fact about the office's classification and not about the record. So I cannot put it on the return. I can put *held* against it and *held* is a fact about us and not about it, and if I put *held* against it then in four years there is a line in an office in nine miles that says this body holds a thing, and the thing is a piece of a machine, and an officer is entitled to ask what it is."

"And Cray," said Marek Vale. "Cray is the second copy of the same record and it goes on the post when a person goes and not before. I could put a piece of brass in an envelope and walk it down there myself and it would be the safest thing in this fen, because Cray is a building and a building is a place and a place can be found. **And I am not going to do it, and the reason is in the fifth clause and the reason is that the second copy of the record of this route is the copy that lives at Cray, and this is not the second copy of the record of this route, it is a fifth thing, and the day a body decides it can put any shape it likes into any envelope it likes is the day the envelope stops meaning anything.**"

"And the offer," said the man of about fifty, from the wall. "The free safekeeping. The district will hold copies of anything a holder places with it, on the district's storage, in perpetuity, at no charge, and they will not produce it and no officer may be asked for it, and we refused it in one line on the fifteenth of last month and I would like it read out, because it is now going to be the reason for the fifth time we have kept a thing on a shape and a body that forgets why it refused a free offer in one line is a body that is about to accept one."

And it was read out, in one line, in the fen's own hand, and the reason was in it and the good reason was left out, and nobody improved on it.

---

And then the man of about seventy said the sentence, from the wall, with the blanket over his knees, and it is the seventh time in a season he has been brought out to a thing, and he gave the two sentences he has given in this holding and then gave the new one, and nobody in that yard thanked him for any of them and the fen entered that nobody did.

"I gave four words on the fifth of last month at a reading in this yard and the four words were *write down what you did not find*, and that is the whole of what a licensed man is for and I have still not got a better one. I gave eleven words at a hard standing by a pump last month and the eleven words were that a rule a person has to remember is not a rule, it is a habit with a number in it. **And I am going to give a third one and it is the same mistake again in a different noun, so I am not going to pretend I have had a new thought.**

"**The second copy of anything has to be a person. A person is not a place and a person cannot be put on a return.** And I know what that sounds like coming off a wall, and I know what I am, and I am not going anywhere this week. But a drawer in a building that anybody may walk into at any hour of the day is not a second copy of anything, it is a hiding place with a lid, and we have had one of those in this fen before and it is four hundred miles off in a locked room and nobody in this holding is going to see into it again this season. **So the second copy is a woman, or a man, and it is not a drawer, and it is not an office, and it is not on a return, and the reason it has to be a person is that a person can be asked and a drawer cannot.**"

And the man of about fifty read that back, and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and the fen entered that the second copy of this body's record is now to be a person and that a person is not a place, and that the fen does not have a form for a person and is not going to invent one at about the eleventh hour on a Wednesday.

---

And Tova Reed said the thing that connects the two decisions, and she said it at about the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth, in a room with the bay doors open, standing at the bench, and she was not asked to and she had not offered and she said the first part of it before the second part of it and the order is the order she says things in and it is not a courtesy.

"Two things have happened in this fen in four weeks and they are one thing and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have got, and I am not going to be asked to, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would like the man of about fifty to read this back and I would like him to read it back twice.

"On the fifteenth of last month, in a yard, four of us stood around a woman's refusal and the fen entered that the question of where a thing came from was available to anybody in that yard and was taken by nobody, and that the fen has decided it will not be taken, and that it is available again tomorrow. **I was the one who was refusing. I am still refusing. I have a thing I have carried since before this fen had a door and it has no number on it and no date on it and no place on it and no name on it and no source on it, and I am not going to give it any of those today and I am not going to give it any of them in a year, and the question of where it came from has been available to me personally for about two years and I have not taken it either.**

"And this week this body has had to put a second copy of a thing in a person, and it has to be a person, and the only person anybody in this yard has ever proposed for a second copy of anything is a woman who has never signed a piece of paper in her life and signed one on the seventeenth of last month on a shelf four hundred miles off, and a licensed man inside a coat, and a clerk at a counter who read a thing he was not entitled to refuse.

"**The two decisions are the same decision and neither of them is about the other.** One of them is a piece of brass and one of them is a piece of paper and neither of them is a record and neither of them can be found by anybody who is not one of us. And I am not going to stand here and make anything of that, and I would like it entered that I did not make anything of it in a room with nine people in it, and that if I had been going to I would have done it on a day when nobody was asking me about a collar."

---

And she named the person, and the naming is in the book, and the name is not in the book.

"She is not of this fen and she is not a member of anything and she does not know you and she is not going to know you, and I have not asked her, and if you ask me whether I have asked her I will tell you that I have not, and if you ask me why I have not I will tell you that a woman does not become a safe place because somebody has said a sentence to her in a yard, and **that is the only condition and it is not a small one.**

And I am going to say the name, once, in this room, and the clerk is going to write *a person named, name not entered, at the seedwright's own asking and not at the clerk's*, and he is going to read that back, and the man of about fifty is going to read it back after him, and **the name is not going to be said a second time in this room or in any other room in this holding, and it is not going to be in this body's book, and if it ever is in this body's book then somebody in this holding has broken the fifth clause and taken a copy of a thing that was not paper.**"

She said it. It took about a second and a half. And the clerk wrote what he had been told to write, and read it back, and the man of about fifty read it after him and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and asked for it to be read a third time, which he has never done, and which he gave a reason for, which is that he wanted to be certain the page was clean.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, and read back three times:
>
> A description of a collar section, being a work, in the fen's own hand, **naming nothing and naming no one, and addressed to nobody, and with no part stated and no district holding number.** The piece itself is in the keeping of the engineer of record, whose name stands second on the page on which the first name stands, and the reason is entered and it is the standing that a licensed engineer's name on a thing is a promise that the thing is somebody's to answer for.
>
> **First place: a drawer in the well house.** It is the one room in this holding that a stranger may walk into at any hour, and the reason it was chosen is the reason it is the public face, and the fen has entered that a drawer in a public room is a hiding place with a lid and that it has been given that and not the other thing because the other thing is worse.
>
> **Second place: a person, named once in a room on the twenty-eighth and not entered.** The name is not in this body's book and is not going to be. **A copy held by a person whom nobody can name is not a copy anybody can find, and the fen has entered that it wanted a thing it could find in nine years' time and has decided that it would rather have one it cannot.**

And the man of about seventy asked for his initial against nothing, which is his standing, and said one thing.

"That is the fifth time. Four before it and this one, in three years, and every one of them was a place an instrument could not reach. **Say the count out loud in a yard on the last day of this month and do not call it a victory, because the day four places are not enough is coming and the day one person is not enough came about two weeks ago.**"

Underneath the day's work, dated the twenty-eighth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-fifth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Four places a record can go in this holding, and a piece of brass with a cut in it that will go in none of them: not the seed house book, because it is not a record; not a return of inventories, because there is no part for it and the office's own line says *held* is a fact about a holder; not Cray, because Cray is the second copy of the record of this route and this is a fifth thing; and not the free safekeeping, whose offer was declined in one line on the fifteenth of last month and whose one line was read out again in a yard on the twenty-seventh because a body that forgets why it refused is a body that is about to accept. A steward's one sentence, and she is not a lawyer and has said so twice. A man of about seventy giving three sentences in two months and saying that the third is the same mistake in a different noun, and giving the third, which is that the second copy of anything has to be a person, and that a person is not a place and cannot be put on a return. A seedwright connecting a decision she made in a yard on the fifteenth of last month to a decision this body has been forced into this week, and saying that the two are the same decision and that neither is about the other, and refusing in a room with nine people in it to make anything of it. A name given once in a room and not entered, and the name not said a second time, and a person who has not been asked, on the grounds that a woman does not become a safe place because somebody has said a sentence to her in a yard. A drawer in the public room and a person who cannot be found, and the count of five, said out loud, and not called a victory.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

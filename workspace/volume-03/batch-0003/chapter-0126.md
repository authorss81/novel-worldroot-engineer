# Chapter 126

## Necessary

They put it in front of her on the twenty-fifth of this month at about the ninth hour in the seed house, as a fact and not as a question, and the putting of it was arranged in about six minutes and the six minutes are in the book, and the arrangement was Lissa Vale's and she did it alone and she told nobody she had done it.

There is no fieldbook in this building. The book is in a locked room at the first house on a shelf four hundred miles off, key on somebody's ring, and it may not be opened again in this season by anybody including the person who locked it, and the only person who asked to see into it was refused in one word in front of eight people on the sixth of this month and did not ask again.

What there is, is four lines in a steward's hand, written out on the sixteenth of last month in a stone barn on a plateau, in front of eleven people, by a steward who is not a lawyer and who copied them out of a dead man's book because a licensed man asked her to and because the alternative was that the only record of the four lines in the world would be inside a locked room in another district.

And beside them, in her hand, the things a person needs in order to look at a sheet of paper: what it is made of, what is printed at the head of it, what year it is dated, and what the writing on it does.

> Copied out on the sixteenth of last month at about the fourth hour, from a sheet of three, by the steward of this body, in the presence of a licensed man with his own book and about nine other people, in a stone barn on another district's ground. **This is a copy of four lines and not a copy of a page, and the page itself is in a book in a locked room four hundred miles off and is not coming down that road and is not coming down that road this season.**
>
> Third week, Thursday. Take the branch at the upper collar, the whole of it, and dress. Flow at the head is not what the board says and the board is not mine. **Necessary.** Do not enter this sheet against the node. Enter it against me.
>
> Entered beside it, in the same hand, on the same day, and not read out to the room: **the paper is a district paper of a shape not used in this district for a long time. There is an office's name at the head of it and the office is not named on this page. The year on the sheet is the year of a shaft. The four lines are in one hand and one of the words in the middle of them carries more weight than the other three. The hand makes the letter *W* in a way that a licensed man in this district has seen once.**

---

And Lissa Vale wrote the four lines out again on the morning of the twenty-fifth in her own hand on a clean sheet, because the copy of the sixteenth of last month is a copy of a copy and this body does not put a copy of a copy in front of a person, and she put it face up on the bench at the head of the table, and she said what it was and she said nothing else.

"Sera Quill. There are four lines on that paper and the office's name is at the head of it and the year is the year of a shaft. That is all it is. I am not asking you what it is. I am not asking you whose it is. **It is on the table and you can read it or not read it and both of those are the same to this body and I have said so before you pick it up.**"

The room was quiet. There were about nine people in it. There was a steward at the bench and a clerk at the gate end of the room and a reader and a licensed man with his own book and a man of about fifty and the man of about seventy against the wall and a woman of about fifty-four's fruit in four crates in a corner of a yard four hundred miles off, and none of those people said anything.

Sera Quill read it. She read it twice, and the second time she put her finger on the middle of the third line and stayed there for about two seconds, and she did not say the word out loud and she did not touch the ink.

The first time took her about four seconds and the second one took about nine, and about halfway through the second one she stopped and went back to the first line and read that again, and the fen entered that she went back to the first line, and did not enter why, and entered that she read all four lines and not the copy beside it, which is the handwriting, and that a person who has been shown a description of a page and a copy of four lines chose the four lines.

And then she put both hands flat on the table on either side of the paper, which is not touching it, and she said one thing.

"Paper like that. Three sheets to a set, that size, that weight, that ruled line across the head. **Yes. I have seen paper like that.** I am not going to say where, and I am not going to say when, and I am not going to say the name of the office at the head of it, and I would like the clerk to enter that I have said all three of those refusals in the order I said them and that they are not one refusal and are three, and that I have not been asked for any of them."

And nobody looked at the steward, and the steward stood at the bench with her hands behind her back for about four minutes afterwards and did not say one word, and the fen entered that nobody looked at her and that she saw nobody look at her and that she has not asked anybody to enter it and that it is entered on the steward's own motion, at the fourth hour, in her own hand, and it is the only entry in this body's book that a person has written about herself while standing in the room where it happened.

---

And Coll Renner said one thing about the hand, and he said it standing, and he said it to the page and not to anybody in it, and it is the third time in two materials that he has refused the same thing in the same terms.

"I will say the one thing I can defend and I am going to say it before anybody decides I have been silent on purpose.

"**A hand can be described. A hand cannot be dated by anybody who has not got the original in front of him, and I have not got the original in front of me, I have got four lines in another person's hand, and this is the fourth page of a book in a locked room and I have not seen the paper and I have not seen the ink.** So I will tell you what the description says and I will tell you what a description of a hand is worth, and it is worth this much: that the person who wrote it is not a person who writes in a book, because a man who writes in a book does not tell a clerk what to enter about a node, and that is a habit of a trade and not of a character.

"**And I am not going any further than that, and I would like the word *trade* entered in my own hand and not in anybody's, and I would like it read back by a man who is not a licensed man, because it is not a licensed man's word and it is mine.**"

---

And then the man of about fifty stood up, because he has a standing, and it is the standing of a man who asks questions, and he asked none.

"Entered at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of this month, in the seed house, in the ordinary words, by the clerk, and read back before it was initialled:

"**Nobody asked her what it is. Nobody asked her whose it is. Nobody asked her when it was written or who wrote it or what the word means or whether the word was right.**

"The question was available to anybody in that room at every hour of that morning. It was available to the reader of this body, who has asked four questions in a yard this season that nobody in this holding wanted to answer. It was available to the seedwright. It was available to the licensed man, who is the only man in the room who could have put a date to a hand and who has never once in twenty-two years put a date to a hand in a room. It was available to the engineer of record, whose father's hand it is.

"**It was taken by nobody and it is not going to be taken. This is the third time this fen has entered that sentence in three weeks and the count is three and the count is not going to be entered as a thing anybody should be proud of.**"

And nobody in that room breathed out loud, and Coll Renner shut his own book, which he had not opened, and the man of about seventy put his hand up once and put it down again and did not say anything, and the fen entered all of that.

And then the reader of this body stood up and read the refusals back into the room in the ordinary words, and the clerk wrote them, and the room did not go quiet again for about four minutes.

"**Refused: where. Refused: when. Refused: the name of the office at the head of the sheet.** Three refusals, given in that order, not asked for, and the woman gave the order before the reasons. Nothing was said about the hand, nothing was said about the writing, and nothing was said about the year, and I want it entered that the year is on a page in a room four hundred miles off and that nobody in this room has ever seen the sheet and that a year is the one thing on that page that this body could check if anybody brought the page down, and that nobody is going to bring the page down, and that the word on the sheet is therefore standing in this body's book on the word of a woman who has seen paper like it and on the word of a licensed man who has seen one hand and not the page.

"Read that back. And read back that there is no fee in it and that she is not to be thanked, and that if anybody in this holding says one word of thanks to that woman about any part of this fortnight, **I will enter the thanks and I will enter who said it, and I will not be the man who is the only one who has to.**"

And nobody said anything, and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and that is the fourth sentence of this chapter that has been entered about somebody not saying something.

---

And then Marek Vale said the thing that is the position of this volume and he said it at about the eleventh hour to about nine people and he did not ask for it to be read back and it was read back.

"On the eighteenth of last month I wrote in this body's book that I am not going to be able to find out whether my father was right, and I have read it back since and I am not going to unsay it and I am not going to soften it into a question I am working on.

"The room is shut. The key is on somebody's ring. The book is not coming down that road and is not coming down that road this season, and the one person in the world who asked to see into it was refused in one word in front of eight people and did not ask again, and I am not going to be the man who makes her hear it a second time.

"So here is what I have instead, and I am going to say all three of them and then I am going to stop. **I have a sequence. I have a word. And I have a woman who has seen paper like that.** That is the whole of it. It is three things and none of them is a reason, and I would like them entered in the same line so that a person in four years does not have to sort them out.

"And I am not going to be told what it means. I want that in the book too, in my own hand, because the man of about seventy said a thing to me in this room a month ago that I did not answer and I am answering it now in a different room about a different man and the sentence is the same one.

"That is not him. Let it be entered that that is not him and that I am not asking anybody to tell me that it is. **A body that cannot tell a father from a piece of paper will be told what it wants by the first man who comes up a road with a bag. That man came down that road four days ago. He came with nothing in a bag. And the sentence still stands and it stands harder than it did a month ago and I am not going to have it softened by anybody in this room including the man of about fifty, who is not to be thanked, and including me.**"

---

And then the second week of the keep, which Anneke Roos entered on the morning of the twenty-fifth at about the eighth hour before any of this happened, and which nobody in that room voted on, and which is three shillings and eightpence, and which is the smallest decision the fen has made in a season and the only one it has made without a vote and without anybody in it having to be told that a stranger is being paid for.

"Second week," she said, at the bench, with the pen. "Same figure. Not voted on. Nobody asked for it and nobody refused it, and I am entering that there was no vote and that nobody proposed one, and that the reason there was no vote is that there is nothing to vote about. It is a bed and a room and a stove at the end of a corridor and there is no argument in any of it.

And I am going to say the thing I have said twice this fortnight and I am not going to say it a third time, and the reader can read it back or not read it back: **that woman asked for no fee and this body is paying her anyway, and both halves of that are in the same book, and the fen does not get to be only one of them.**"

---

And the last of the twenty-sixth, which was a Tuesday, and it is a Tuesday that is not warm, and there is no note under section nine for the twenty-fifth or the twenty-sixth and the reason for that is entered underneath the twenty-fifth and the reason is that a refusal entered as a fact is not an argument.

Ilsa Kern cut one hundred and forty into both boards at about the seventh hour on the morning of the twenty-sixth and said the number out loud in the yard, which is the seventh time in eleven years that anybody in this holding has said that number in front of another person, and the man of about seventy was at the wall and heard it and said one thing and it is the same thing he said on the thirtieth of last month.

"It is the only figure in this holding nobody has ever argued about," he said, "and the reason is that **nobody can make a number go down**, and I would like somebody in four years to come back to that board and see whether it is still going up. That is all I want out of eleven years of a man cutting wood twice a day."

And the reader of this body read the entry of the twenty-fifth back in the yard at about the eleventh hour, and it is a refusal and it is three refusals and it is a page of four lines on a table with two hands flat on either side of it, and nobody thanked anybody, and the woman who said the word is not going to be forgiven and has not asked to be and cannot be.

# Chapter 128

## The Thirtieth

A body that has spent twenty days reading out a woman's account of a thing that happened four hundred miles off and nineteen years back has to close a month with its own figures, and it did, and the figures are the figures, and the last reading day of the seventh month went the way the last reading day has gone every month since the flood.

The door was read out whole at the tenth hour of the thirtieth in a yard with about twenty-five people in it, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk and a licensed man at the back, and a woman who had been in this fen since the eleventh of this month standing at the wall side of the gate where anybody could see her, and nobody in that yard had asked her to stand there and nobody in that yard asked her to move.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the thirtieth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of this month, the fifth read twice.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-six.**
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered tonight, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.** This is the sixth month of a run that has not been discharged in six. The seventh is a figure nobody in this yard can say, and this body will not enter a figure it does not know, and it will not guess at one on the last day of a month in front of twenty-five people either, which is the day it would be easiest and which is the day the practice exists for.
>
> **The confession is not on this door and this door is not about the confession, and the count of the readings of the seventh column has gone from twenty-four to twenty-six in twenty-nine days and not one of those two was caused by anything said in a room.**

And then the standing figures, in the ordinary words, and none of them softened, and the reader of this body read them out and the clerk wrote them and neither of them said one word that was not on the list.

> **The leak.** Five-eighths of an inch, twice, two rules, the second of them the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up. Read at the seventh hour of the first of this month in a full yard, with a reader appointed and the count of mornings read out with the figure, being the forty-third day since the last entry in the book and forty-two mornings in between, of which the number that were measured cannot be given and is not given. **It has not been measured since. It has no due date and none is to be invented, and a due date on a thing that has been done on most mornings since before the charter would be a way of saying it was not being done.** Nobody has looked for a cause and none is entered.
>
> **The beat.** The fifteenth, at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of last month, nine in the quarter hour with one run of three, at the rail. Cause not established. Licensed person present, none. Nothing written underneath it. **It has not been logged since and the column is open and this fen has not opened it, and the fen entered tonight that it has not opened it now, with a witness in the yard and about twenty-five people in it.**
>
> **The boards.** One hundred and forty-four days, cut into both of them this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years, said out loud in a yard, and the number is still going up and nobody can make it go down.
>
> **The March's tin.** **One hundred and ninety-one days**, day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, with both undertakings standing: the seed house's that it will not be moved, and the trust's that it will not be asked. Neither has been touched in this month and neither was asked anything this month and the drawer it is in was not opened this month and is not to be.
>
> **The standing rule about the engineer of record.** Satisfied on the third of this month, in a room, on a day, with a licensed man writing it down. Two accounts of the same arm in this book that do not agree and were not reconciled. **The rule is in force and it says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and a second draw is on a table and this body has not decided it, and has entered that it has not decided it for twenty-five days.**
>
> **The well on the shelf.** Five feet and three inches at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month, by the occupier, by her own hand and by her own nail, in her own yard, said out loud before it was written down. Nine feet at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month three months back. The shortfall three feet and nine inches, of which **three and a half feet is the draw of the thirteenth of last month and is entered against a man, and half an inch is the ground's and is entered against nobody**, in two hands in the same ledger on the same day. Four houses on a day letter, one drawing each in turn, the first house a Tuesday, since the sixth of this month, and the rise of an inch in sixteen days is a household's decision and not a figure. **There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and this body will not be predicting one.**
>
> **The man at the second house.** In this yard since the fifteenth of this month. Not a member of this body. **This fen does not know what he is doing here and has not asked and is not going to.**

And Anneke Roos initialled the last of that as an opinion in four words, and the four words have never been explained to anybody in eleven years and are not going to be, and they were **falls on the first**, and the reader of this body read them back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing and did not ask what they meant, because he has never once asked.

---

And then the handoff, said plainly, in the same yard, at about the eleventh hour, because this fen does its own handoffs and has never in eleven years sent one to anybody.

"Two things fall on the first of next month and they fall outside today and this body cannot do either of them today, and I am saying that out loud at a gate with twenty-five people in it so that nobody can say afterwards that it was kept quiet."

"**One. The seventh month's compost line.** It falls on the first, it is not in this book, and this holding does not know what it is, and I have written that in the ledger in four words and the man of about fifty has read it back and has not asked me what the four words mean. It will be entered in whatever hand it is in and the reader for it will be the man of about fifty and not me, and it will be the seventh month of a run of six that has not been discharged in six, and **nobody in this yard can say what it is and I will not have a figure guessed at on the strength of a month I have read out in a yard.**"

"**Two. The day letter.** Four houses on a shelf four hundred miles off, one drawing each in turn, the first house a Tuesday, since the sixth of this month. **It runs a month and the households named the first of next month to look at it, and they will do it in daylight at the well with whoever is standing there, and they decided that and not this body, and this body was not asked and was not going to be.** There will be people on that shelf on the first who can read a figure and there may be a person from this fen standing at that well, and whether there will be is not decided and I am not going to decide it in a yard, and that is the steward's question and I have answered it in front of about twenty-five people."

"And three, which is not a date and is a fact, and the man of about fifty has read this one back twice."

"**The man at the second house is in this yard and has been since the fifteenth of this month. This fen does not know what he is doing here and has not asked and is not going to.** He came down a road of nine days in nine days, and somebody in that yard paid for it or he paid for it himself and this body has entered that it does not know which and has decided not to ask, and he has been in this yard for fifteen days and he has not asked this body for one thing, and **a man who has not asked for one thing in a fortnight is not a thing this body has got a form for, and the form does not exist and is not going to be invented at about the eleventh hour on the last day of a month.** Read that back twice, please, and then we will do the last of the day."

---

And the last of the thirtieth, and it is a Saturday, and the man at the second house read his own last statement in this fen in a yard at about the sixth hour and about nine people heard it, and the woman who came down the road on the eleventh went on the afternoon cart and nobody paid for her place and nobody thanked her for any part of it.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the thirtieth of this month, at the bench, by Anneke Roos, on the standing of the money, which is that a cost is entered as a cost, and read back by the man of about fifty and initialled by nobody:
>
> **The keep of a woman of fifty-six, closed on the day she went.** Three shillings and eightpence for a fortnight, entered in two halves, and eleven shillings for the licensed man, and twopence for a second blanket, and the second week entered on the twenty-fifth at about the eighth hour and not voted on.
>
> **She was in this yard from the eleventh of this month to the thirtieth of this month, which is six days over a fortnight, and no line in that book said so until this one.** Those six days are one shilling and sevenpence at her figure of three shillings and eightpence for fourteen, because a day out of that fortnight is a little over three pence and this body will not enter a fraction and rounds a person up.
>
> **And the reason they were not entered on the twenty-fifth, when the second half of that fortnight went in, is that on the twenty-fifth this body did not know she would still be in this yard on the thirtieth, and that reason is entered as well, and I do not know what to do with it.**

"Then here is the whole of it," he said, standing against the wall side of the gate with his hat in his hands. "I have been in this yard fifteen days and I came here to be angry at a man about a bucket and I have been angry at him on the fifteenth in about nine words and I have been in this yard every day since doing nothing in particular, and I have read your door twice and I have counted a column of your numbers off a board with my finger, and I have watched a woman of fifty-six say nine things in a room and refuse three things at a gate."

"I do not know what I am doing here. **I have not asked anybody and nobody has asked me and I am not going to, and I would like the clerk to write that down and I would like the man of about fifty to read it back, which he has done twice already and can do it a third time.** And I am going to say the other half, because I am not a man who leaves a thing half said in a yard."

"You gave me four entries and a fortnight. The four entries are in your book and I have read them and **I did not accept them and I did not refuse them and both of those are in there in the order they were said.** The bucket is three and a half feet shorter than it was and it is going to be three and a half feet shorter on the first of next month, and the first of next month is a day I put in your book in a barn four hundred miles off before I came down here, and **I have not moved that day, and I am not going to move it now that I know more, and knowing more is not a reason to move a day.**"

That is all. That is the whole of what I came for and about a foot of it is still not answered and I am not going to chase it. **I would like the fen to understand that a man can be answered and not accept it, and go on being in the yard, and that this is not a threat and is not an opening.** It is a bucket. It is a bucket and a well at the fourth house and a stone windlass and a woman of fifty-four who read a nail out loud in front of strangers because a man from nine miles off told her to."

And the reader of this body read that back twice, and the clerk entered the hour, and nobody thanked anybody, and the man of about seventy asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and the fen entered that he asked and did not enter what it was about.

Underneath the day's work, dated the thirtieth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-sixth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A month closed with its own figures and not with the fortnight's. A door read whole at the tenth hour in a yard of about twenty-five, the seventh column at twenty-six, and the count moved by two in twenty-nine days and neither of the two had anything to do with a room. The seventh month's compost line not entered, and the figure not in the book, and the holding not knowing it, and six months of a run not discharged in six, on the last day of a month, in front of twenty-five people, which is the day it would be easiest and the day the practice is for. A leak with no due date and no cause entered, a beat logged once and not since with the column open and a witness in the yard, a board at one hundred and forty-four, a tin at one hundred and ninety-one days unmoved with both undertakings standing, a rule in force with a second draw on a table undecided for twenty-five days, and a well three and a half feet down against a man and half an inch down against the ground. A handoff said out loud at a gate: a compost line on the first of next month and a day letter looked at in daylight at a well with whoever is standing there, and a third thing that is not a date, which is a man who has been in this yard since the fifteenth and has not asked for one thing and has not been asked what he is here for, and that sentence read back twice. And a man at a gate in a hat saying that a man can be answered and not accept it, and go on being in a yard, and that a bucket is a bucket.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

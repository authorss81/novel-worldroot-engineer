# Chapter 121

## The Ninth Day

He came into the yard at Lantern Reach on the fifteenth of this month at about the tenth hour in the morning, on foot, off the plateau road and down the fen road, and he came the last of it alone, and he was nine days getting here from the sixth, and the road is four days.

Nobody asked him about the other five. Dunmar Fitch entered the fact on the page with the hour, and entered the reason, and the reason is in the words of the thing itself and is not improved on.

> Entered at the tenth hour of the fifteenth of this month: the man at the second house is in this yard. He left the shelf on the sixth of this month. The road is four days and it is nine days that he has taken.
>
> **The fare for it was not eleven shillings a day and was not paid out of this body's compost line, and the fen does not know who paid it. Somebody in that yard paid for it, or he paid for it himself, and this body has decided not to ask, and the decision is entered with the hour.**
>
> He is not a member of this body. He has not been asked to be one. He is not to be made one.

He stood in the yard where he had stood for a fortnight of a season four hundred miles off, and he was fifty-one or thereabouts and he had a coat he did not need and a hat in his hand, and about fourteen people came into that yard inside twenty minutes because word goes two hundred yards on this ground faster than a letter goes nine miles.

And he was angry, and it went in with the hour, and it is a fact and not a scene, and nobody in that yard asked him to explain himself a second time and nobody asked him to leave.

"You spent a bucket," he said. "That is all I have come to say and I have said it, and I am not going to say it again today, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said it once and that I am not going to say it again today."

> Entered at the tenth hour of the fifteenth of this month, at the north gate, in the yard, by the clerk: **you spent a bucket.**
>
> Said once. Not said again on that day. **The anger is entered as a fact and is not softened, and no reason was asked for and none is entered, and no person in that yard asked the man to explain himself a second time and no person asked him to leave.**

And Marek Vale gave him the answer, and it is not an apology, and it was given in the shape of one, and he did not use the word and could not.

He got the seed-route ledger off the bench and put it on the wall side of the gate where the light was and did not open it and did not turn to a page.

"Four entries," he said. "They are the whole of what I have got and they are the whole of what I am going to say, and I am going to say them in the order they were made and not in the order they help me.

"The first is mine and it is in my own hand. The draw of the thirteenth of last month, water and heat and rootlight together through a scion three-tenths of an inch a day, over about four feet, for about four hours, in the lee of a north wall with a licensed man holding a lamp. And the cost, which is not the four inches and is not the mark: fever inside two hours, three fingers of my left hand gone from the second joint until the second morning, my taste gone entire and sour not back on the twentieth and nobody in this holding has asked me since and I will not promise it.

"The second is Coll Renner's, in his own book, in his own hand, and it is not mine and I cannot amend it. **Not answering for a draw.** Twenty-two years licensed and he wrote it and he wrote it before I did the thing, and it is the one piece of paper in this whole business that was written by a man who had nothing to gain by writing it.

"The third is the one you should have had first and I did not have ready, and it is a matter of record and not of memory. On the twelfth of last month, at about the sixth hour, standing in a barn in front of eleven people, I gave that away before I did it. In those words: if the well two hundred yards down your slope is shallower on the fourteenth than it was on the sixteenth of a month three months back, then it is entered as my cost and not as the weather's, and it is entered in the seed-route ledger and not in a book anybody can lose, and it is read out loud to that household and to the three others within a fortnight, whatever I say at the time and whatever I am like at the time. Tova Reed wrote it. The clerk read it back on the fourteenth at the ninth hour in a yard with nine people in it. **Nobody in this yard was in that yard. You were.**

"And the fourth is the one I am going to have to hold on to, and it is two figures and they are in the same ledger on the same day in two hands. On the fourteenth of last month the shortfall was entered against the draw and not against the weather. And on the thirtieth of last month, out loud, in a yard, in front of twenty people: three and a half feet is mine and the other half inch is not. **Half an inch of that bucket is the ground's and not mine and it is going to stay the ground's in every book it goes into and I am not going to be permitted to be right about the first one and I am not going to be permitted to be right about the second one either, and that is a fair outcome.**"

And the man at the second house stood and listened to all of it with his hat in his hands.

And Dunmar Fitch read the four of them back out of the book with the hour on them, one at a time, and the man of about fifty read them back after him, and nobody softened a figure and nobody thanked anybody.

Then the man at the second house said the thing the four households had said, and he said it in the plain words of a man repeating a decision he did not make.

"The decision was theirs," he said. "I have said that to a man in this yard once and I will say it to him twice. Four houses, one drawing each in turn, on a day letter, the first house a Tuesday, for a month, and it began on the sixth of this month. And they will look at it on the first of next month, in daylight, at the well, with whoever is standing there. **Nobody in this fen has voted on that and nobody in this fen is going to be asked to.** It was put to them and they took it and I am the one standing here repeating it because I am the one who can read."

And then he said the rest of it, and it is in the book, and he said it to Marek Vale and not to the yard.

"You have not asked me for anything. I want that said, because in nine days I expected a man to ask me for a thing and hand me a page to sign, and you have not, and I do not know what to do with a person who gives it away and then does not come and ask whether it was accepted.

"So I am going to say the two halves and you are not to pick one. **I did not accept what you have just read to me. I did not refuse it either.** Both of those are in your book in the order they were said in and I would like them written in the order they were said in and not in the order a person would prefer them in.

"And I have nine days' worth of something in me that I told a woman in a barn I would have on the fifteenth, and it has come out this morning, and I would like it entered that it came out at the gate at the tenth hour and not anywhere else, and I am going to go and sit on your wall now and I am not going to be spoken to for an hour."

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the fifteenth of this month, on the same line of the same book and beneath the four entries: **did not accept. Did not refuse.** Both in the order they were said in. The man did not take the second hour of that morning and said so before he sat down.

---

And then the sixteenth, and it is a Saturday, and two things fell due on it and neither of them moved.

The well on the shelf was read at the fourth hour by the woman who owns it, on her own rule, by her own hand and her own nail, in the yard of the fourth house with about six people standing round the windlass, and she said it out loud before she wrote it down, and the fen did not have the figure for four days and did not guess at it.

> Well, Cauldron Reach. Read at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month by the occupier, by her own hand and by her own nail, and said out loud in her own yard before it was written down, which is not her rule and is this fen's rule and she has taken it off this fen without being asked: **five feet and three inches.**
>
> The same well at the fourth hour of the thirtieth of last month: five feet and two inches. The same well at the fourth hour of the twentieth of last month: five feet and four inches. The same well at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month: five feet five inches. The same well at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of last month, an extra reading asked for by a man in front of witnesses: five feet and a half. The same well at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month three months back: **nine feet.**
>
> **It has come up an inch in sixteen days.** The four houses have been drawing one in four since the sixth of this month, and one inch is a household's decision and not a figure and no person in that yard proposed it.
>
> The shortfall is **three feet and nine inches.** Three and a half feet of that is the draw of the thirteenth of last month and is entered against a man. **Half an inch of that is the ground's and is not entered against anybody.** Two figures, two hands, one ledger, one day.
>
> **There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and this body will not be predicting one.**

---

And at the tenth hour in the fen, the door was read out whole, in a yard with about twenty people in it, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk and a licensed man at the back, and **the count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-five.**

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the sixteenth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of this month, the fifth read twice. **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, which is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.**
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-five.**
>
> **This is the first middle reading of this door in this season that was made with the body of this holding standing in its own yard, and with a person in that yard who is not of this fen and is not a member of anything.** The count is a number like any other number. **A count made in front of a stranger can be checked by a stranger, and this is the first time in eleven years that has been true of this body.**

And it was checked, and the check was not arranged by anybody, and the yard did it inside about a minute and a half.

The seventh column on that door is a ruled list with a line for every reading made since the flood and a date against every line, and a number at the head of the column that a clerk writes in once a year. It has been there four years and about forty people in this district could read it.

Sera Quill was at the back of the yard, and she had not come to the reading, and she came to the back of it and stood four rows off and looked at the board for a while without saying anything to anybody.

"How many," said Dunmar Fitch.

"Twenty-five," she said. "And the head of the column says twenty-four, which means somebody has written the count at the head of a column and has not carried it down. **That is not a fault. That is a practice, and it is the first one I have seen in six years and I am not going to say whose it is.**"

And the man at the second house, who had been sitting on the wall for an hour and had not been spoken to, stood up and came and read the whole of the column off the board, line by line, out loud, in the order it was on the board, with his finger under the date.

"Twenty-five," he said, to her, and he did not know her name and did not ask it. "**Twenty-five. And you counted it from the back of a yard and I have counted it with a finger, and we have got the same number, and neither of us is a clerk.**"

And about twenty people in that yard watched a stranger and a man from a shelf four hundred miles off agree on a number in front of the well house door, and nobody said anything about it, and the reader of this body read that back in the book afterwards and the clerk entered the hour, and the fen entered that the number had been checked and not arranged, and entered that **a man who has come to be angry read a figure out loud to a woman he does not know, in a yard, and neither of them knew the other's name, and that is the whole of what happened and it is not going to be improved on in the writing.**

And a man was hired at the change of horses on the twelfth of this month, four days before the figure he was sent for existed, at four shillings a day, to go up the four days and back down them for a sheet of paper with one figure on it in a woman's own hand, and the four shillings for the day he was hired went into the compost line at Lantern Reach in Anneke Roos's ledger, and she entered that the road is four days and a round trip is eight days of it and that the whole of the hire is thirty-two shillings, and that twenty-four of them were not entered until the man was paid off, and that this holding did not have thirty-two shillings and took them out of that line anyway, and that the figure the sheet is for is one that this holding has not got and is not going to guess at for four days, and that a body which reads a figure out at a gate is not a body that can live four days without one.

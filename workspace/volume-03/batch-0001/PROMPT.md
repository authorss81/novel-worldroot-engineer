## House style, which is not optional and is not a preference

Look at Chapters 89–98 and copy them.

- Paragraphs run **long**. Two to six sentences is the general rule and a great many of this manuscript's paragraphs are eight or twelve. The close of a chapter is usually a long paragraph with a figure or a named person in it and no ornament.
- A document is set as a block quote, in the fen's hand, unnumbered, in the words of the office or the person who wrote it. **The document is correct.** Nobody lies in a document.
- After the document, somebody says what it is, in a long speech, standing up, and a named person is appointed as the **reader**, and the reading is read back, and the hour goes on it, and somebody enters an **observation** which is four words and is never explained, and nobody thanks anybody.

## The ten cards

### Chapter 99 — *A New Writing* — days 151–152, Wednesday the first and Thursday the second

**Goal:** the fen puts its own fifth month on the page and decides, with no charter and no vote, whether the road keeps running.

**Must happen, in this order.** Lissa Vale enters the fifth month's compost line on the morning of the first: nothing put into the return main, none available, the specification four hundred hundredweight, the shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, **fifth month**, carried forward and not discharged — **and the fen does not know whether she will write the same words or different ones, and nobody asks her, and Anneke Roos wants to ask her and does not.** The sowing closed yesterday, which is the end of the arrangement's term, and the question is a new writing or a stop, and a stop is not a failure. **The road goes on.** Draft and read aloud at the well house door, **one page, shorter than the five clauses, and it has one clause the old one did not have**: the record of the route goes to Cray **and to a second address**, and **no person holding any part of it keeps a private copy of any part of it.** That is the volume's relationship milestone planted as a clause in a fen document, and it must be argued, not announced — the man of about fifty argues against it and is outvoted four to three in daylight with the book shut, and he asks for his vote against to be recorded with his reason and no date on it, which is his standing. The seventh column is read at the tenth hour and the count of the readings goes to **twenty-one**. The examination is kept **with no officer and no form, by choice**, and Gael Senn keeps it and writes nothing in advance. And at about the fourth hour a woman comes up the fen road on foot with a slat crate, and she is from the Glassward, and she has walked down off the plateau, and she is not stopped at the gate before she has said what she wants, because Lissa Vale's standing is that anybody may say what they came for at the gate.

**Resistance:** the new writing is easier to sign than to argue about, and the clause about private copies is the one that costs somebody something. **The refusal clause from Volume 02 stays on the door untouched and is read as part of the door, not discussed.**

**Change:** the fen is now running a road to a second address it has never run a road to, and there is a person in the yard from a place the fen has no record of.

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** the **twenty-second** note under section nine, dated the second, and there is no charter and the note says so on its face.

### Chapter 100 — *A Crate Off The Plateau* — days 153–154, Friday the third and Saturday the fourth

**Goal:** find out what she has come with, before anybody in the yard forms an opinion about it.

**Merrid Ollvar, about fifty-four, of Cauldron Reach, in the Glassward high country.** Four houses, eleven acres of old pear and plum on a south-facing shelf, thirty-one years on the shelf, eleven acres and no help. **She is not a member of the cooperative, she is not a villain, and she is not redeemed.** She came down because nobody answered three letters.

**Must happen.** The crate is opened in the yard in daylight, standing, and **the fruit is real and it is last season's**: thirty-one pears off a row of pears that has not fruited since the same month last year, picked on a date, kept cold in a stone room, and now off the same trees again, eleven weeks after the blossom should have been over. **Nobody in the yard says anything clever for a while.** Tova Reed asks the first question and it is the only question that matters in the chapter: *what do you want.* The answer is one sentence and it is arithmetic — a buyer has offered a price per hundredweight for four consecutive seasons and has never been told why the fruit is unusual, and the price is more money than that holding has seen in four years, and **she wants somebody to tell her whether it is a harvest or a fault.** The name on the buying paper is not a villain and gets no more than four words. Anneke Roos does the arithmetic in the open on the fen's own paper and the sum is embarrassing. Lissa Vale asks the question that becomes the batch: **who is going to be asked what it means.** Coll Renner is in the yard and says the only useful thing anybody says all day, and it is nine words, and it is *a thing like that needs a licensed man and not a guess.*

**Resistance:** every explanation available in the yard is wrong, and the two most attractive ones — a graft, and a trick by a buyer — are both wrong, and the fen wants one of them to be right because it would be simpler.

**Change:** the fen has agreed in daylight to go four hundred miles to look at a cut in a root, and the cost of the going is counted in Ch 102.

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** none. **The stage-nine note count does not rise for a chapter with no new document in it.**

### Chapter 101 — *The Second Reading* — day 155, Sunday the fifth

**Goal:** establish, by instrument and not by opinion, that the fruit is not a graft artifact, a trick, or a fault in her keeping.

**Must happen.** Coll Renner tests the ring and the collar before the instrument goes near anything, **writes the test down first, fits his own clip**, and reads the root collar of one of her own trees, in the yard, on a Sunday, with no charge for the day and a note entered that he charged nothing and why. **The Fieldbook panel is spent in this chapter and nowhere else in the batch:** three plain lines, the instrument's own words, about a root collar and a season's growth, **not a finding, not a verdict, and quoted by nobody in any document for the rest of the book.** The reading takes seconds and it is not cheap for Marek: the four inches of the rootmark are hot before it is over, and his hand is no use from the wrist down, and **this is Stage 2 work and it must be entered as exactly that and not as more.**

Then the mechanism, stated as engineering and not as wonder, and it is Marek who states it and it takes him about a minute and a half: **the tree is not remembering. It is keeping order.** A graft carries the thing put in and loses the thing that was there — that is Volume 02's finding and it holds here and Tova says so before he does. What the pear is doing is producing in the order it was told, the way a mill produces the same cut of flour twice, and the question is not what the tree knows. **The question is what it was told, and by whom, and whether the telling is still going on.**

**Resistance:** a panel that says three plain things and not the answer is not a way for a man to be sure, and Marek wants to be sure, and refuses to write anything he cannot check with a rule.

**Change:** the fen has a method instead of a wonder, and the method is a sequence, and a sequence implies an order, and an order implies somebody.

**Notes:** none.

### Chapter 103 — *Eleven Acres On A Shelf* — days 158–159, Wednesday the eighth and Thursday the ninth

**Goal:** arrive and see it, and let the place be a place.

**Staging — this chapter is the volume's first sustained scene off the fen and it must feel like a different country.** Nine days is too many for one chapter, so the chapter opens on the **plateau**, and the going is one paragraph in Lissa's hand in the ledger. The Glassward: exposed rootwood standing out of the ground in the open, old observatories, sealed deep mines, wind off the ice, a south-facing shelf with a stone wall built by somebody who is not in any record Merrid has. **Cauldron Reach:** four houses, eleven acres, a well two hundred yards downslope, and against the north wall of the yard a stack of pressed rootwood in bales, rotted to a grey pulp and stacked higher than a man, **which has been there longer than Merrid can date and which she has never opened because there is no reason to open it.**

The first sight of the cut: on a row of pears along the wall, a deliberate cut, dressed, and healed over, and **a man with a rule can date it.** Marek dates it. He does it with a rule and a lens and a method he explains out loud, and the method is unglamorous: **count the healed growth, divide by what this ground grows in a year, and that is how many seasons ago.** Tova asks the question that is the batch's whole shape: **why would a man cut a live branch and dress the wound?** Two answers are available. A bad cut. A good cut with a reason. **Nobody in this chapter chooses, and the chapter ends without choosing, and the fen is on a plateau with a crate of pears and a row of healed cuts and no reason on the page.**

**Resistance:** the plateau is not grateful to them, the wind is against the instruments, and a local man at the second house says the flat thing that the fen is not the first to come up here and that the last ones did not pay.

**Change:** the sequence is confirmed to be deliberate. The fen is now committed.

**Panel:** none — it was spent in Ch 101. **Notes:** none.

### Chapter 104 — *What A Cut Is* — days 160–161, Friday the tenth and Saturday the eleventh

**Goal:** read the face, and let the reading mean two things at once.

**Must happen.** The cut face is cleaned and read in daylight with the wind dropped, and the sequence is counted and dated and **it is an order of operations and not a scar:** a branch is taken, the wound is dressed, the wound is allowed to close, the next is taken, the wound is dressed. **Eleven of them, in one row, in one season, at intervals a man can count.** Marek says the sentence that turns the chapter and it is a working sentence and not a speech: *that is not damage, that is a method.* The **tool-mark** is a smooth face and a bevel on one side only, and **no tool in this district owns that bevel**, and the district's own hire list is produced and does not have it, and the sub-office at the plateau foot says in writing that it does not know what made it and is not required to.

**Then the argument, and it is the batch's real midpoint and it is a real disagreement, not a mood.** Marek reads the eleven cuts as the work of somebody in a hurry with a season to save. Tova Reed reads them as a decision, taken once, in advance, with a list. **They do not agree and neither of them softens it and the clerk cannot enter it as a finding because it is two findings.** Anneke Roos asks the question that reframes it: *which of the two would you rather it was,* and Marek's answer is the wrong one on purpose and he knows it and enters that he knows it.

**And the money, hard, in the same chapter.** A figure per hundredweight on paper, four seasons, and the standing of it, and the arithmetic done in the open by a woman who is not of the fen and does not have to be careful with them: **this crop is worth more than Lantern Reach has taken in in four years, and it is the only money any of these four houses will see, and the man who cut those branches is dead and does not have a claim on it.** And **Marek does not tell them to give it up, and that restraint is the point of the chapter**, and it costs him, and the cost is entered by the reader and not by him.

**Resistance:** the money is real, it is theirs, and the honest answer to *should we keep it* is not available to a man who came four hundred miles to look at a cut.

**Change:** the sequence is confirmed as an order, not a wound, and the fen is out of time on the buyer's date.

**Notes:** the **twenty-fourth** note, dated the eleventh, **carrying the standing sentence**, because it is an argument: that a person who has come to look at a cut is not a person who gets to say what a crop is worth to the people standing in it, and that the fen entered this against its own interest on the day it read it.

### Chapter 105 — *The Graft* — day 162, Sunday the twelfth

**Goal:** keep a dying root alive long enough to read the cut under it, and find out what it costs in links.

**Must happen.** The pear row's third tree is failing — a graft at the collar from four seasons ago, a bad union, and the root below the cut is dying — and the cut they want to read is **under** it. Marek makes a small graft. **Stage 2 work, and the whole chapter is about the ceiling of it:** two active links already, and the third is what he is attempting, and **a fourth is known to fail and is not attempted and is named as a thing that will not be attempted.** Tova records it and reads it back. Renner witnesses and writes four lines and the hour in his own book. **The graft works and it does not work well, and the difference is entered.** The fen is now two links down on a plateau with one licensed man and a rented room, and **the number of links a man can keep without help is three and the fen has entered that it has two and cannot get a third back.**

**And the wrong decision, which is not stupid.** Marek has done the arithmetic and it is in front of him: the tree will not hold through the afternoon, and the face under it will close before it is read, and the reading is what they came for. **He proposes to hold the tree's flow with a draw.** He has never drawn. **Nobody in that room has ever seen him draw, and Renner says the honest thing, which is that a man who has never done it should not do it on a tree four houses drink from.**

**Resistance:** the temptation is specific and correct — the thing they came for is on the other side of a closing wound.

**Change:** a decision to attempt Stage 3 is taken in daylight, in front of witnesses, with the objection recorded, and it is not heroic and it is not approved.

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** none.

### Chapter 106 — *The Draw* — day 163, Monday the thirteenth

**Goal:** pay for the stage in the same chapter it arrives, and put the cost on other people's ground.

**This is the first draw in the book. It is written as work, at length, in the light, and it is not a light.**

**Must happen.** He holds the flow through one afternoon so the face will not close. **What moves is water, heat and rootlight, and it moves through a living path, and it comes back the way it came.** The physical account is specific and unflattering: the four inches of the rootmark go hot, and then past the four inches, **darkened veins running into the elbow and past it**, fever inside of two hours, **three fingers of the left hand gone**, and **taste, entirely, for the rest of the day and the day after and, in the way he can defend today, not certainly back.** He loses an hour he does not account for. He cannot stop it by wanting to stop it. **The instrument reads it and Renner reads it out and does not soften a figure.** The panel is not spent here and the Fieldbook is not open.

**And the downstream debt, on the page, with people against it, entered in the ordinary words.** The well two hundred yards downslope, which four houses drink from, is **nine feet on the sixteenth of last month by Merrid's own rule and is five and a half at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of this month by the same rule, and it does not come back, and nobody on that shelf can say that it will.** Tova puts the cost in a ledger line in the fen's own book, in the seed-route ledger, because the seed-route ledger is the book anybody may ask to have any line of read aloud. **The cost is not refunded in this volume. It is entered and it is read aloud and the man of about fifty is not there to hear it and will hear it in four chapters' time and the whole of Volume 04 is the consequence.**

**Resistance:** the recovery does not happen in this chapter. He is worse at the end of the day than at the start and he says so.

**Change:** **Marek Vale is Stage 3, Drawwright, and the cost is on the page, and the stage is not a promotion.** Anneke Roos is not on the plateau and the arithmetic of it gets entered by Tova in four words and no more.

**Notes:** none. **The beat is not logged on the thirteenth because Gael Senn is at Lantern Reach and the fourteenth is logged there on the twelfth.**

### Chapter 107 — *The Inventory* — days 164–165, Tuesday the fourteenth and Wednesday the fifteenth

**Goal:** have the Crown's answer to all of this arrive as a form, and be correct.

**Must happen.** A form comes up the plateau road, and it is a **schedule of records held** and a **category** of historical rootwork record and a power to declare a category of it **unfit for evidentiary use**, in the ordinary course, on an inventory, by an officer, without a hearing — because a record that is unfit is not evidence of anything, and a body may not cite it, and the people who made it may not be asked about it. **This is Volume 02's mechanism exactly: a class, a form, and a power, and nobody in the document lies.** **Sabin Roell**, records officer at the district office at the foot of the plateau, about thirty-four, comes up himself, gives her name once, is entered by the clerk, is correct, and **is not a member of the cooperative and is not a villain and is not made one.** The head of the column carries a name that is **not** explained, and it is the name on the letterhead of the district's index, and Tova Reed reads it and **does not say anything about it, and Marek notices her not saying it, and neither of them says a word to each other about it on the page.**

**And the offer, which is the real thing and is free.** The office will hold copies of everything — the schedule, the ledger, the reading of the cut, the whole of it — at Cauldron Reach, on the district's storage, in perpetuity, at no charge, **under a signature.** Tova Reed says the whole of it in one sentence: **she has read the shape of a free offer with a signature attached before, and she is not going to take it, and she is not going to say where she read it.** **She is not asked. Nobody asks her twice. The man of about fifty's standing does not exist on this plateau and the fen does the asking itself, in one question, and gets one answer, and stops.** — **This is the Volume 01 Chapter 4 plant paying off. It may not be given a number, a date, a place, a name, or a source. Do not restore a number to it.**

**Resistance:** the offer is genuinely free and storing a record at no charge is the safest thing anybody has offered this fen in two volumes, and refusing it costs something real, which is that a copy held at the district's storage is a copy the district can declare unfit.

**Change:** the record has to survive somewhere that is not the district, and the fen has to decide now, and the answer is copies and not one better copy.

**Notes:** the **twenty-fifth** note, dated the fifteenth, and **this one is a record and the standing sentence is OFF it, with the reason written underneath so the chain cannot be seen to have dropped one.** The reason is that the note is the record of a refusal and a refusal is not an argument.

### Chapter 108 — *The Fieldbook* — days 166–170, Thursday the sixteenth through Monday the twentieth

**Goal:** open the one book in this story that has never been opened, find the record, and get copies out — and pay for it.

**Must happen, in this order.**

1. **On the sixteenth, at Lantern Reach, four hundred miles off, Ilsa Kern and Jonas Byre read the well house door out in an empty yard at the tenth hour because the fen's rule is the first, the middle and the last, whether anybody comes or not. The count of the readings of the seventh column goes to twenty-two. Nobody in that yard can check the count at the other end and nobody at the other end knows it was read.** Entered as a fact, by Dunmar Fitch, at both ends.
2. **On the seventeenth Lissa Vale opens the fieldbook.** In a stone barn at Cauldron Reach, on a trestle, with the front board, the living root bead, and **the handful of pages that are all of it.** **She opens it because it is hers and nobody asks her to and Marek does not ask.** Coll Renner is the licensed reader and he uses the copper clip and **he does not touch the bead and he says so in his own book.**
3. **The use-log line is not written.** **There has never been one and there is not going to be and nobody says why and the whole room knows it.** The use log is twelve lines and records contacts and never readings and it stays twelve lines. This is the beat of the chapter and it must not be explained.
4. **What is on the pages, and no more:** Aldren's last line of figures — *Seventeen. Set in the low. Eleven. Watch the fourth beat in the small hours* — the column head and figure, the impossible year, **and a work order.** The work order is in his hand, on a form from the old observatories, and it says a branch is to be taken at a named place on a named day, and the word at the bottom is **necessary**, and there is **no reason on the page.** **Marek sees a record suggesting his father chose to cut a branch during a real emergency.** He does not read a memory. He cannot. Nobody in that barn sees a vision and nobody speaks to a dead man.
5. **The copying, which is the point of the whole volume and is done badly and fast and in four hands:** four copies, two roads, one to Cray, one down the fen road, one in Merrid Ollvar's own hand because she can write and has never signed anything else, and one **not** for this fen, which goes with Renner because he is licensed and will be believed. **The count of the readings of the seventh column is not one of them and the seventh column's own line is copied as a line.**
6. **The cost, entered, and it is Lissa's decision and it is not a favour to Marek:** the fieldbook is closed and **may not be opened again this season**, and she enters that with her reason, and her reason is one sentence and it is *a book that has been open twice this month is a book that somebody can say was altered between the openings.* And Marek's taste has still not come back on the twentieth and the darkened veins have not gone above the elbow, and Renner reads both figures and does not soften them.
7. **On the twentieth, the number cut into the two boards at Lantern Reach is one hundred and four and Ilsa Kern cut it and nobody told anybody.**
8. **Closing image of the batch:** the arrangement runs, the shilling is paid, the measured figure is six and the declared is thirty and both are on the door, the fruit is in the barn and unowned and selling or not selling is not decided, **and the question from the last volume is on the table in a stone barn and has acquired one more shape without one more answer: eleven cuts, in a row, in a season, by a man who dressed every wound and wrote the word necessary and is dead.**

**Resistance:** the temptation to make the fieldbook a vision. **It may not be one.** It is a fragment with a front board and a handful of pages and a man who wrote *necessary* and gave no reason, and the whole of Marek's reaction is that he cannot find out whether his father was right and has to keep working before he can stand to ask.

**Change:** the batch's evidence exists in four places, the fieldbook is closed for the season, and the volume's hinge is a dead man's unexplained order with the word *necessary* at the bottom of it.

**Panel:** none — spent in Ch 101. **Notes:** the **twenty-sixth** note, dated the twentieth, **carrying the standing sentence**, because it is an argument: that a record which exists in one place is a record somebody owns, and that four copies is not confidence but arithmetic.

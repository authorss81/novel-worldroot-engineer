# Chapter 99

## A New Writing

The sowing closed at dark on the thirtieth and nobody in that yard watched it close, because there is no hour at which sowing closes. It stops being a thing anybody is doing somewhere between the second hour of the afternoon and the light going, and the books know it stopped because the books have a day on them and the day ended.

So the first of this month came up on a Wednesday and the first thing in the yard was the compost line, and Lissa Vale wrote it standing at the north end of the seed house bench with the ledger open in front of her and the clerk behind her shoulder and the bay doors open behind both of them, and she wrote it in the ordinary words, and she did not hurry it, and about nine people watched her do it.

> Return main, this month: nothing put in. None available. Specification four hundred hundredweight. Shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings. Carried forward and not discharged. Fifth month.

The clerk read it back and she initialled it and the hour went on it and it went into the fen's own book and into the seed-route ledger, which is a book anybody may ask to have any line of read aloud and which has nineteen pages and is now, on the first of this month, twenty-one.

And then nobody moved, and the reason nobody moved was standing at the bench with the pen in her hand, and Anneke Roos wanted to ask and did not ask, and the wanting is a thing a body can see.

"You are going to write the same words," said Anneke Roos.

"No," said Lissa Vale.

"Then I will hold my tongue, and I will say that I am holding it, and I will put my initial against nothing, and neither of those two things is the same as not wanting to know."

"Enter all three," said Lissa Vale. "And do not ask me on the second or the third, because you will want to on the second worse than on the first, and by the third you will be asking because a thing you have not been told is a thing you can still be given, and I have watched a body do that to itself twice this season and I am not doing it a third time in front of nine people."

The clerk entered all three. The man of about fifty read the entry back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and which cost the holding nothing.

---

The arrangement's term ended with the closing of the sowing, and the closing of the sowing was the day before, and so the whole of the question of the road had been sitting in that yard for a night and a day without anybody saying it, and it was said at about the eighth hour of the morning by the man of about fifty, standing by the gate with his hand on it.

"The term ended last night."

"It ended at the closing of the sowing," said Tova Reed, from the bench, without looking up. "Which was yesterday afternoon, and there is no hour at which sowing closes, and the clerk has entered that, and so the term ended yesterday afternoon, and it does not matter which hour and I would like it entered that it does not matter which hour, because if it matters which hour then somebody in nine miles can pick one."

"And so it is a stop or a new writing, and there is no third thing, and I have thought about it since the second hour and I have got the same answer four times and I would like somebody else to have it before I say mine."

Marek Vale drafted it at the seed house bench in about forty minutes and brought it to the well house door, and it is one page, and it is shorter than the five clauses it replaces, and there is no heading over it, because the five clauses had a heading and the heading had a small lie in it and the small lie is the only thing in the arrangement anybody ever had to be careful about and this page does not have it.

> One. What runs. The route as it is on the schedule on this door, from the first end to the low dry, and not beyond, and not to the second break. The third part of the district's terms is honored as written, the declared capacity kept as declared, the rate fourpence the crate on thirty, one shilling a month, and the record of the route produced to any person at any hour.
>
> Two. The condition. The measured capacity is measured every month and entered on that sheet whatever it is, and is not left off and is not moved off and is not taken off the door.
>
> Three. Stopping. Either end may stop the route on a day's notice by writing it in the book. No reason is required and none is to be entered. A route that stops is not a route that has failed.
>
> Four. A copy. A copy of the schedule and of the ledger may be taken by anybody at this end who asks for one, and the reading of any entry in either is a right and not a favor. The copy for the far end goes on the post when a person goes, and not before.
>
> Five. The record of this route is one record. It is kept at this end, and a copy of it is sent to Cray on the ordinary post, and a copy of it is sent to Cauldron Reach in the Glassward when there is anything in it to send, and **no person holding any part of it keeps a private copy of any part of it, and there is no part of it that is anybody's own.**
>
> Six. The term. To the last day of the sowing again, and then a new writing or a stop.

Six clauses and it is one page and the drafting of it took forty minutes, and the fifth is the only new thing in it, and the rest of the morning and most of the afternoon went on two votes and a yard.

---

The vote was not needed. Nothing in this holding requires a vote, and the fen entered that, and the man of about fifty asked for one anyway, which he does, and Lissa Vale told him he could have one on the fifth clause and not on the rest, and he took it.

"You are voting on the fifth clause."

"I am voting on the fifth clause," he said, "and I am going to say why in front of my reason and not in front of my feeling, because I have watched this yard do that twice. Put my vote against first and read it back after."

The clerk entered the vote against and the hour and read it back, and the man of about fifty gave his reason standing at the gate with his hand still on it, and it is the fourth time he has spoken at that gate and it is the third time he has voted against a thing this body wanted.

"I am against the fifth clause," he said, "and I have said in the book in the spring that how a body is known is not how it is honest, it is how big it is, and I have said it about the ledger and I meant it. And I am against this because it is the same sentence again with a new road on it. You have found a thing worth copying and your first thought is to make a rule about who does the copying, and a rule about who does the copying is a rule about who is trusted, and this yard has been trusted by nobody in a long while and has not once been caught at it.

"And there is a worse thing and I have thought about it since the second hour and this is the fourth time I have had this thought and it is the same thought. If nobody keeps a copy, then there is one place where the whole of it is, and if that place is a road, or a fire, or a man, or a district office, then the whole of it goes at once and it goes in a day. I know that. I know that four copies in four counties is four chances to be wrong and four chances to be read by somebody with a lever. I have had a vote on this body four times and I have been against twice and for four times and I am going to be against this and I would like it entered that I thought about it until the fourth hour and I did not have a better reason than the one I have got."

And then the man of about seventy said the thing that made the vote four to three, and he said it from against the well house wall where he sits, and it is nine words.

"A copy is a person." And he asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

And then Anneke Roos said the other nine words, and she said them in the steward's way, which is that she asked one question about who loses.

"You have written *no person holding any part of it keeps a private copy*," she said, "and it is right, and it is the first rule anybody in this yard has written that is against us, and I am for it, and here is the whole of my reason and it is his reason answered and not a better one. He is afraid of the one place. I am afraid of the man. Those are different fears and they do not both get to be in a rule, and if you write a rule against the man then the man is the one you have decided about, and he is not going to be the only one.

"And I will say the other thing because I am the last word on money in this yard for a fortnight and I am using it. A private copy is a thing a person can hold at a kitchen table with nobody else in the room, and read, and be the only one who has read it, and be afraid of somebody, and sell, or lend, or leave, and no body in this holding has ever had one and this holding has got its house and its books out of that by accident and not by a rule, and I would like a rule for it while there is still nobody to catch."

Four to three, in daylight, with the book shut, and the clerk entered the count and the hour and the standing of the vote, which is that a vote is what you take when you are not sure, and that this body has taken one on this clause and not on the other five, and that both halves of that are now in the book.

Lissa Vale read the whole of the six clauses back in the yard at about the fourth hour, and the man of about fifty read the fifth clause back after her, and nobody thanked anybody, and the standing is that nobody in that yard is thanked for being readable and cannot be.

---

The schedule went back on the door on the same four nails and in the same four holes, with the new writing under it and the refusal clause still under the seven columns where it has been since the seventeenth of last month, and the four sheets of class 4 still nailed on the left under the notice of the second, and the holes still show.

At the tenth hour, in the ordinary way, the door was read out. The schedule, all seven columns, and the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line, and the count of the readings. The notice, all four lines. The four sheets, all four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, in the fen's own hand, read out from the north gate to make a person read it standing at a distance. And the new writing, six clauses, with the fifth clause read twice because a man asked for it and the request was granted and the reason was that a clause nobody asks for twice is a clause nobody has agreed to.

The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-one in the clerk's hand under the form, and the fen entered that the rule is a decision and not a mood, and that it has been a decision since the second of last month, and that the thirtieth of last month and the first of this one came on consecutive days, which is a day closer than the rule is set, and that this holding was therefore one day out of step with its own rule for the third time this season and has entered all three.

And the examination was kept at the eleventh hour with no officer and no form, by choice, and Gael Senn kept it and wrote nothing in advance, and there is nothing in this holding now that requires it, and she kept it anyway, and the standing reason was entered by her own hand and is four words and has never been explained to anybody.

And the two boards over the bay had eighty-five days cut into both of them, and Ilsa Kern said the number out loud on her way past and did not stop, and the number was still going up.

---

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, with the light off the door and the six clauses lying flat on the bench, somebody came up the fen road on foot.

She was about fifty-four and she was carrying a slat crate in both hands and she had come a long way and it showed in the way she was standing at the gate, which is the standing of a person who has decided where they are going to stop.

Lissa Vale went out to her, because that is what Lissa Vale does, and she did not ask her name first.

"Say what you came for," she said, "and then I will ask you everything else, in that order, and the first one is not a formality because anybody who walks into this yard has a thing they came for and half of them will not say it until they have been asked twice."

The woman put the crate down on the flat by the gate where anybody coming up the road can see it, which is a thing a person does who has thought about being seen.

"I have come about my pears," she said, "and I have come about eleven weeks, and I have a crate of them at your gate, and I want somebody in this yard to tell me whether they are a harvest or a fault, and I have walked down off the plateau to ask it and I would like it before the buyer comes, and the buyer comes on the seventh of next month by the look of your dates, which I have been reading off your door since I came up the road."

Nobody in that yard said anything for a moment, and then the man of about fifty said the thing that is the last thing anybody says that day, and it is the thing this holding has been trying to get to since the sowing closed.

"Then somebody had better go and look," he said.

Underneath the day's work, dated the second, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the twenty-second note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Nothing put into the return main, none available, the specification four hundred hundredweight, the shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, fifth month, carried forward and not discharged, written in the same words as the four before it, and the person who wrote them has told this body she does not know whether she will write them again, and that sentence was asked for by a member of this body and not given. A road whose term ended with the closing of the sowing and did not end. Six clauses in one page, shorter than the five they replace, with a fifth clause that no person holding any part of the record keeps a private copy of any part of it, carried on four to three in daylight with the book shut, against the vote of a member who gave his reason and asked that it be entered with no date on it, which is his standing. A copy to a second address, in a county this holding has never sent a copy to anything in. A door read out whole, the count of the readings of the seventh column at twenty-one, and an examination kept with no officer and no form and no charter and by choice. And a woman at the gate at the fourth hour with a crate of pears and a question about the seventh of next month.*

*This is a record and not an argument, and the sentence is off this note for that reason, and the reason is written here so that the chain cannot be seen to have dropped one.*

# Chapter 113

## The Thirtieth

They came off the plateau road on the thirtieth at about the sixth hour in the morning, and the figures were read out in the yard before anybody had said good morning, because the man at the second house had asked for exactly that on the twenty-eighth in a barn and had said why.

He had said: I would like it read out on the day you arrive, in the yard, with the figures, before anybody has said good morning. And Lissa Vale had written it into the seed-route ledger in her own hand with the hour on it, and the reader for it was the man of about fifty, and it is in the book, and it happened.

> Going up, nine days, ninety-nine shillings at eleven a day of horse and man. Board at both ends taken by the occupier and refused. A change of horses at a place with a name not entered.
>
> **Coming down, four days, a hundred and four shillings**, the trestle and the bench and the case and a ledger four hundred miles each way, and the last two days of a descent being the first two days of somebody else's ascent and the fare not being the same.
>
> The shortfall in the compost line is **not three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight** this month and is not going to be three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight next month, and no figure was guessed at on the road, and the figure for the first of next month is not in this book and this body does not know what it is.
>
> The well at Cauldron Reach, entered against the draw of the thirteenth of this month: **three and a half feet**, and it is not entered against the weather.
>
> A row of eleven cuts, dated to this season, with a bevel that no tool on the district's hire sheet carries. A stack of pressed rootwood against the north wall that nobody on this shelf has ever opened. **A man who cannot taste a lemon and an hour at about the fifth on the thirteenth that is not there.**

And then Lissa Vale said the thing that she had come down the road to say, in a yard, standing up, in daylight, to about twenty people, and it is nine sentences and it is the whole of what this yard has for an account of itself.

"That is the four of us," she said. "Nine days up and four down, one hundred and four shillings of horse against a holding that has four pounds five in it, thirteen weeks of a compost line entered as a cost and not as a fault, a well three and a half feet down that four houses drink from, a locked room on this shelf with a book in it that is not coming down that road, and eleven cuts in a row with a bevel on them. **I would like the yard to say the number of things that are better now than they were on the sixth of this month, and I would like it said in the same breath.** I have been on the road for thirteen days and I can do the sum and I am not going to do it out loud because I do not trust my own arithmetic on a subject like this one, and because somebody in this yard will do it in about an hour with a pen."

And nobody in that yard answered her, and nobody answered her on the twenty-eighth in the fen either, and the clerk entered at Fennmere on the first of next month, from the ledger, that the question was available to anybody in two yards and was taken by nobody in either, and that the fen had entered that it would not be taken, and that a question nobody takes is not a question the body has failed to answer. It is the fourth time this season.

---

The well had already been read that morning, at the fourth hour, before any of them was on the shelf, and the reading was in the yard in front of about nine people because Merrid Ollvar read it out loud before she wrote it down, which is not her rule and is this fen's rule and she took it off this fen three weeks ago without being asked.

> Well, Cauldron Reach, read at the fourth hour of the thirtieth of this month by the occupier, by her own hand and her own nail: **five feet and two inches.**
>
> The same well, at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month, by the same rule and the same nail: **nine feet.** The same well at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month: **five feet five inches.** The same well at the fourth hour of the twentieth of this month: **five feet and four inches.**
>
> **There has been no rain worth the name on this shelf in twenty-five days.** The figure on the thirtieth is worse than the figure on the twentieth, and the figure on the twentieth was worse than the figure on the sixteenth, and the shortfall against the sixteenth of last month is **three feet and ten inches.**
>
> The shortfall is entered against the draw of the thirteenth of this month and **it is not entered against the weather, and the two entries are in the same ledger on the same day in different hands and they are not the same entry.** The draw is three and a half feet. The other half inch came off it in a month with no rain in it worth the name and the ground did not put it back. That is a fact about this shelf and not about a man from nine miles down a road, and it was entered by the person who lives in the fourth house, and she has not spoken to the engineer of record since the fourteenth of this month and she read the figure out loud in a yard this morning with him standing in it.

Nobody argued about the figure. The man of about fifty read it back and did not soften it, and then Marek Vale said the only two sentences he says in that yard, and they were the figures, and the man at the second house had told him nine days ago on a plateau what he was going to be asked in this yard and he was not going to be asked in this yard.

"Three and a half is mine and the other half inch is not, and I would like them kept as two figures in every book they go into, and I am not going to be permitted to be right about the first one and I am not going to be permitted to be right about the second one either, and that is a fair outcome."

Nobody thanked anybody, and the man at the second house was not looking at him and did not look at him for the rest of the morning, and there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back. Not on the shelf, not in the ledger, not in a book four hundred miles off, and the fen has entered that it will not be predicting one, and that a body which has begun writing down the month a well will be shallow in has begun keeping a second kind of book, and that the second kind is the kind an office is good at.

---

The three options were put in the open at about the ninth hour by Lissa Vale, standing up in the yard with the reader at the end of the row, and she did not recommend one, and the reason she did not recommend one is that the question she asks is who loses, and there was a person attached to each of the three.

"There is a wet place at the foot of the slope, about a mile round and a mile back by the track, and it is a trough in a seep and not a well, and it is there in the spring and gone by the last week of the summer, and the water in it is the temperature of the ground, and a woman of about sixty at my house carried a pail of it in the bad year and had to sit down twice on the way up."

"There is a house at the top of the track, below the shelf, with a well in it, four hundred yards, and I do not know the name of anybody in it because I have not been down there and I am not sending anybody down there this week to find out. **That is a debt and it goes in the ledger as a debt and not as a favor, and the man at the second house is to hear me use that word in that order.**"

"And there is a well at the second house. It is older than mine, it is in ground a yard off from this one, it was dry in the summer of the bad year, it has not been looked at since, and looking at it is two men and a day. It is his father's well and it is on his ground and the asking is his to refuse and it is not the fen's to ask."

"That is the three of them. I am recommending none of them. Whoever loses on any of the three is in this yard, and I would like that said before anybody says a word in favor of anything, and I would like the man of about fifty to read the three of them back at the north gate."

And the man of about seventy said the thing that was going to be the fourth option whether anybody put it there or not.

"You have not put a fourth," he said. "I have been waiting a day for somebody to put it and nobody has. The fourth is that four houses are going to draw from one well three and a half feet shallower than it was, which is arithmetic and not a decision, and every one of those four houses is going to be short about a quarter of what it took in the spring, and there is nobody anywhere on this shelf to be short at, and no ledger on earth that can put the quarter back. **I am not asking you to decide it. I am asking you to write it down while you are still choosing.**"

"And I will say the other thing, which is that I have now been the fourth person in eleven years to say that a thing nobody wanted in a book has to go in a book anyway, and I am the only one of the four of us who does not have to be listened to, and that is why I am the one who is allowed to say it and none of you are."

---

The door was read out whole on the thirtieth at the tenth hour, in a yard with about twenty people in it, and Lissa Vale read it and the man of about fifty read the figures back after her, and **the count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-three.**

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the thirtieth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of this month, the fifth read twice. **The compost line for this month, not entered tonight,** and the reason entered, which is that the figure is a large one and this holding does not know what it is and will not enter a figure it does not know.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-three.**
>
> **The steward was standing in front of it.** That is the first reading of this door in this season with the steward in the yard, and the first in eleven years with the steward, the reader, the clerk and twenty people in front of it at once, and it is entered as a fact about a day and not as a moment. The count on the sixteenth of this month was made in an empty yard by two men while this body was four hundred miles off and the two ends of it could not have checked each other. The number this morning is the same number. The difference is everything else about it.

And then Ilsa Kern cut one hundred and fourteen into both boards over the bay at the north end of the yard, and she said the number out loud, and it is the first time in eleven years that the number cut into the boards has been said out loud in a yard with people in it.

He had asked her to, and she had said she would, and the cutting of it has never been asked of her by anybody in eleven years.

"Stop at the boards and say it," the man of about fifty had said to her that morning, "because you have been saying it to an empty yard since the flood, and you are the only person in this holding doing a practice to nobody, and you are no worse at it than the seventh hour is, and neither of you is to blame. And I would like a yard to hear that number once in front of me before I am sixty."

And the man of about seventy said the one thing anybody said about it.

"That is the only figure in this holding nobody has ever argued about, and the reason is that **nobody can make it go down.** Not one man in nine miles can make a number go down, and a body that keeps a figure which only goes up is a body with one thing it does not have to be right about, and I would like somebody in four years to be able to come back to that board and see whether it is still going up. That is all I want out of eleven years of a man cutting wood twice a day."

Underneath the day's work, dated the thirtieth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the twenty-ninth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A month read out whole in a full yard on the last day of the month for the first time in eleven years: the schedule, the notice, the four sheets, the refusal clause from the gate, six clauses with the fifth read twice, and the count of the readings of the seventh column at twenty-three, with the steward standing in front of it, entered as a fact about a day and not as a moment. A well read by its occupier on her own rule and her own nail at the fourth hour: five feet and two inches, three feet and ten inches against the sixteenth of last month, no rain worth the name in twenty-five days, and the draw's three and a half feet entered against the draw and the other half inch entered against the ground, in a different hand, in the same ledger, on the same day. A steward in a yard with twenty people in it reading four hundred miles of arithmetic out of a ledger she carried down a road, and asking for the number of things that are better now than on the sixth of this month, and nobody in either yard taking the question. Three options for four houses, each with a person in it, entered and none recommended, and a fourth thing that is not a choice, and a month that is over. The compost line for this month not entered tonight, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the holding does not know the figure. And one hundred and fourteen days cut into two boards and said out loud in a yard for the first time in eleven years.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

# Chapter 112

## The Return

The form came down the plateau road with the party and it was filled in four hundred miles off it, and neither of those two facts is in the form.

The four of them left the shelf on the twenty-seventh at about the sixth hour in the morning, four days down with one change of horses, and the going cost money, and the money is in the seed-route ledger in Lissa Vale's hand with the hour on it, and it is larger than the going up, and the reason it is larger is entered with it and nobody in the fen found it funny.

Nine days up was ninety-nine shillings at eleven a day of horse and man. Four days down is forty-four, and the forty-four is not the whole of it. The going up was arranged by a woman who has kept a ledger of her own for thirty-one years and who knows what a horse costs on that road and in what order the changes are; the coming down was arranged at the foot of the plateau in about an hour by four men who did not know the road, and the first two of the four days of it are the last two days of somebody else's going up, **and the fare for a man going up a shelf is not the fare for a man coming off one**, and the reason is that a man going up books it four days ahead and a man coming off it books it at a gate at seven in the morning. On top of that came the trestle, which is the licensed man's and goes back to him, and the bench, which is hired and does not, and the case and the clip, and the seed-route ledger itself going four hundred miles and coming four hundred miles, and a hired trestle is two shillings a day and a bench is one and eightpence and this holding is not a rich one.

So the cost of coming down this shelf is a hundred and four shillings against ninety-nine, and Anneke Roos's standing for this fortnight is the last word on money in this yard and she is not in this yard, and so the figure was read out of the ledger in a barn on the shelf on the evening of the twenty-seventh by Lissa Vale, with the hour on it, and the man of about fifty read the figure back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and the fen entered that a body which has spent a season publishing every figure it has has just had a figure it could not check read to it by a woman four hundred miles off, and that the two things are the same thing and that the fen is not going to be pleased about the second one.

---

And the going itself took twenty minutes and nobody narrated it afterward except the ledger, and the ledger is a ledger and not a story, and the four days down are four lines in it.

What happened at the gate at about the sixth hour in the morning on the twenty-seventh was that the man of about fifty read the fifth clause of the second of this month out loud, from the page in his own hand, to four people and a cart, which is what he did on the seventh when they went the other way, and which is his standing, and which nobody in either yard has ever once tried to talk him out of.

Then he read the entry of the twenty-eighth of last month out after it, which is not a clause and is not on the door and is the only thing in this body's book that anybody here has said was a mistake, and the clerk entered that the reader of this body has now read that entry at a gate twice, at both ends of the same road, in front of the same four people and a cart, and that he gave the reason the second time and not the first, and that the reason was that a gate is the one place on a road where anybody going can hear a thing being read, and that the number of people who heard it going up was four and the number who will hear it coming down is whoever is standing at the other gate.

And the locked room at the first house on that shelf was not opened and the key did not change hands, and the book in it is not coming down this road. Merrid Ollvar keeps the key and it is on her ring and she said so at the gate without being asked, and Lissa Vale entered it in the ledger with the hour on it, and entered that the person who locked it said the sentence about it and that nobody thanked her for saying it.

---

The form is the fourth part of the schedule of records held, and it came up the plateau road on a cart on the fourteenth of this month with three sheets and a covering letter, and it is a form with six lines and a number at the head of four digits and a number at the foot of six, and a box headed for the district holding number, and the box has a line in it that the fen's own water uses a class of use on, which nobody in this yard has had time to be funny about.

The person who had to fill it in was Gael Senn, and the reason is in the book and the reason is not a compliment.

She is the examiner and she is keeping the books, and the party was two days down a road, and **the only person who may write in this fen's book is the clerk, and the clerk was on the cart with a ledger and a seal and forty miles of fen road still in front of him.** There is no standing in eleven years that lets a person put a figure into this body's book because nobody better is available. The clerk read it back before it was initialled and the man of about fifty read it back after the clerk, and that is the mechanism, and the mechanism does not care that four people were in the same yard on the shelf on the twenty-fourth and did not see any of it.

And the six lines are here entire, and they are as they went, and the word on the fifth of them is the word of a form and not the word of a body.

> **One.** The schedule on the well house door and the six clauses of the second of this month, being a work and its reading. **Part two.**
>
> **Two.** The seed-route ledger, being a work and its reading. **Part two.** And it is entered here which of two things the count of readings standing under the seventh column is: **a reading.** A body that cannot say is a body whose record is part three, and this body can say, and it says it is a reading, and the person who said it is the person who filled in this form, and a person in four years may read that line and decide she was wrong, and that is a thing she can be asked about and not a thing she is going to be protected from.
>
> **Three.** The three books on the table in the seed house, being the old man's fragment, the use log, and the seed-route ledger, and being a work and its reading. **Part two.** There is a fourth thing in a shut drawer in that building. It is not listed. It is not asked about. Nobody asked about it.
>
> **Four.** A reading of the root collar of a pear tree, made on the seventeenth of this month with a licensed reader and a witness, on a hired trestle in a stone barn, on ground in another district, the holder of it being a person of about fifty-four at Cauldron Reach who is not a member of this body. **Part two.** And the whole of what this form says about that item is true: it was a reading, it was of a collar, it was on the seventeenth, it was made with a licensed man. **And it is not the whole of it, and this body knows what it is leaving off, and it is leaving it off on purpose and not because it does not know.**
>
> **Five.** A copy of that reading in the hand of a licensed man, carried by him in his own name with his own license on it. **Not this holder's record and not produced by this holder.** A licensed man's own record of his own work is the licensed man's. It is not a record of a holder and it cannot be listed as one, and it is entered here so that a person in four years does not read this return and think this body has hidden a thing.
>
> **Six.** The form is not listed. It is not a record of this holder. It is a page in a book that is not this body's, and a return of inventories is a list of this holder's records and not a list of what other people have written inside a thing this body was lent for nine days and handed back.

And that went on the post at the change of horses on the twenty-eighth, in an envelope with no number on it, because the fifth clause of the second of this month says that no person holding any part of the record keeps a private copy of any part of it, and a copy of a return is a copy of the record, and a body that has just written the fifth clause is not going to be the body that breaks it in an envelope.

---

The argument about line four took the whole of the twenty-eighth and it is in the book whole, and it is the fourth time this season that the man of about fifty has stood up and said the sentence that reorganises everybody else's position, and the reason it reorganised it is that he did not make an argument.

"They have not asked for a copy," he said. "I have said that in this yard before and I am going to say it again because it is the whole of what that instrument is. They have asked for a list, and they have written down on their own sheet that a list is not a copy, and they have been careful about that on purpose, and everything we have done for a year has been careful about it on purpose too, and I do not think that was a coincidence in either direction.

"So on line four they have asked whether we hold a reading and what part it falls in, and they have not asked whether the reading has a copy in a house in another district in a woman's own hand. And the answer we are going to give is yes, and a woman of fifty-four who has never signed a piece of paper in her life has signed one, and the fen entered that on the seventeenth of this month and said it was doing it on purpose.

"That is correct. **I want it to be correct and I want it to go on the paper anyway, and I want the reason written on the same line.** Because if we leave it off then a second copy of a reading held by a stranger is a copy we cannot point at, and a copy you cannot point at is a copy that does not exist for any purpose except the one it was made for, and the whole of the fifth clause is a refusal to have a record only in the place it started. So it goes on the return, with the district, the day and the kind of holder, and not the person's name, and Lissa Vale writes the name on the fen's copy and not on the one that goes to the office, and the reader reads that back before it is initialled."

And the man of about seventy said one thing about it, from the wall, and it went in the way things from the wall go in.

"You are telling an office where to write to," he said. "That is what you have just agreed to do, and I am not against it, because a body that has just spent a fortnight hiding what it has got will be found out in a year and the finding out will be the end of it, and I have seen a body end that way. **Say where it is. Do not put her name on it. And understand what you are doing, which is that you have decided this body's record is worth more than a woman's morning.** She offered on the seventeenth and it was a signature and not a question, and I would like the offer entered as a signature and not as a consent to the fifth of this line."

And the fen entered that, and the hour, and the reader read it back, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

And the last of the twenty-eighth was the arithmetic, which is Anneke Roos's standing and which is the fourth time this building has done a sum in a room and got worse each time, and it is the fourth sum to be read out of a ledger to a yard that is not the one that did it.

Lissa Vale read out of the seed-route ledger, in a barn on the shelf, at about the ninth hour on the twenty-eighth, the entry of the tenth of this month, which is a sum of the whole of the pear crop on that shelf at the buyer's price, done standing, in a yard, in daylight, and entered twice.

*Eleven acres. A hundred and forty-one trees. The whole of it at that price is a figure I am not going to say out loud in a yard with a stranger standing in it, because it is her money and not ours and it is not my place to make a woman of fifty-four hear her own figure said in front of six people. Here is what I will say: the whole of it, at the figure she gave me, is more than this holding has taken in since the charter was made. Not more than the repair cost. More than everything. I have the fen's own book here and I have gone back four years in it and it is not a close thing, and I am going to say one more sentence and then I am going to stop talking about money, which is my fortnight's standing and I am spending it on a stranger, and I would like that entered.*

*It is more than the repair cost, and the repair is standing, and the two things are four hundred miles apart and they are not in the same account and anybody who puts them in the same account is doing it on purpose.*

And then the twenty-ninth of this month, entered in the same ledger on the same evening by the same hand, from four hundred miles off, and it is the second half of the same sum and it is the half that is not on this shelf.

*The going up, ninety-nine shillings, and the coming down, a hundred and four, because the last two days of a descent are the first two days of somebody else's ascent and the fare is not the same. The trestle and the bench and the case and a ledger going four hundred miles and coming four hundred miles. And the shortfall in the compost line, which is not three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight this month and is not going to be three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight next month, and the man of about fifty read that figure back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.*

And the man at the second house, who has been in that barn every evening for three weeks and brought a stool for an old woman on the third evening and has never once been asked to be there, said the thing that the whole of the next week turns on, and he said it in the plainest words anybody has used on that shelf and did not perform about it.

"So it is cheaper for you to have gone up and come back than it is to keep the four of you in the fen, and the well is three and a half feet down, and the buyer is on the seventh of next month," he said. "I am not saying that to be unkind. I am saying it because you have all been very careful with me for three weeks and I would rather be spoken to straight. **I would like it read out on the day you arrive, in the yard, with the figures, before anybody has said good morning.**"

## The batch's first job, and it is not optional

**An earlier draft of this section said the leak went unmeasured on the fourteenth because the person who measures it went nine days up a road. All three halves of that are false and the batch must not inherit them.** **Gael Senn measures the leak. She did not go up the road — she is the one who stayed and kept the books.** **And it is not a monthly figure.** She measures it at the seventh hour, twice, with two rules, the second of them the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, into the housing and not into the pit: at the seventh hour of the first of a month two months back, and again on the fifteenth and the eighteenth of that same month, and **at the seventh hour on most mornings for two volumes, and nobody has ever asked her to.**

**So here is the real position on the twenty-first of this month, day 171.** Four people left on the seventh and the yard has been empty at the seventh hour every morning since. **The leak has not been measured in fourteen days.** It stands at five-eighths as at the seventh hour of the eighteenth of a month two months back, and it has not been measured since, and **the last figure in the book is a fortnight old and is being carried forward as though it were current.**

**Ch 109 measures it. Not the first, the middle and the last — those are the *door*'s days. The leak has never had a rule and that is the finding.** She measures it at the seventh hour in a yard with three people in it, it is five-eighths, the second rule reads smaller, the pit is not up, and **the number is the number, and the yard has not seen a number stand still in front of it in two volumes.**

**What is not permitted is silence.** The leak is standing unsolved engineering and not a mystery and not a plot, and a reader who has been told for ninety-eight chapters that a woman measures it at the seventh hour without being asked must be told what happened for a fortnight. **And the fortnight must not be written as an oversight: it stopped because it was one person's practice and she is twenty-four and four hundred miles of road took the rest of the yard out of the morning, and nobody decided anything.**

**And the pay-off is in Ch 114, on the first of next month, day 181, in a full yard, where the leak is measured with a reader appointed and the count said out loud** — the same beat as the boards being read in public in Ch 113, and the same argument underneath it, and the fen has just written a clause about records.

## The ten cards

### Chapter 109 — *The Seventh Hour* — days 171–172, the twenty-first and the twenty-second of this month

**Goal:** the first business of a month in a fen that is not whole, and the finding that the fen has been running a rule it cannot keep.

**Must happen.** **The twenty-first of this month, at about the seventh hour, in the fen, with three people in the yard.** Gael Senn has kept the books alone since the seventh and four people are four hundred miles off on a plateau, and **there is no monthly return due in this chapter** — the examination and the compost line fall on the first of next month and are in Ch 114. **What is due in this chapter is the leak, and the beat, and the discovery that a practice can stop without anybody deciding to stop it.**

**The leak.** She measures it at the seventh hour, twice, with two rules, the second of them the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, into the housing and not into the pit. **Five-eighths. The second rule smaller. The pit is not up.** And it is the first measurement in fourteen days, and the last entry in the book about it is the seventh hour of the eighteenth of a month two months back, and **the clerk enters that the figure in the book was a fortnight old and has been carried forward as though it were current, and that nobody in this yard decided to carry it forward.** Nobody was in the yard at the seventh hour. That is the whole of the reason and it is not an excuse and it is not a fault, and **the fen enters the difference between those two things because it has spent a season learning that they are not the same.**

**Then the beat, the fifteenth, at the seventh hour of this same day, cause not established, licensed person present none, nothing written underneath it, and the four-word decision underneath that as her standing.**

**And the thing the chapter is actually about.** Gael Senn has been keeping seven practices for two months with nobody in the yard to keep them with, and one of the seven is a measurement at the seventh hour on most mornings that **no body has ever written down as a practice, because it is one person's habit and has been since before the charter.** It is the oldest thing in the fen and it is the only one that was never voted on, and **the man of about seventy asks who owns it**, and the answer is that nobody knew it needed an owner until the morning it did not happen.

**Resistance:** a body that keeps rules for a person who is not in the room will find out what it has become. **The man of about seventy says the sentence, and it is the batch's hinge: a rule that has to be remembered by the person it was made for is not a rule, it is a habit with a number in it.** And the answer is not to drop the rule. The answer is worse.

**Change:** the fen has entered a fortnight with a hole in it, and has entered that the hole is a shape it built and not a thing that happened to it, and **it has entered that the oldest practice in the building was never anybody's decision.**

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** the **twenty-seventh** note, dated **the twenty-second**, **carrying the standing sentence**, because it is an argument: that a body with no charter has kept seven practices by choice, has now found out that a choice has a cost, and that the cost is not a vote and was not proposed by anybody.

### Chapter 110 — *What Is In The Stack* — days 173–174, the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth

**Goal:** open the stack, by the person who owns it, and find out what a family stacks against a north wall for two generations without knowing.

**Must happen.** Merrid Ollvar opens it. **Not Marek, not Lissa, not Renner, and the chapter must make the refusal to open it a decision with reasons in it and not a gesture.** She is asked by three people to let somebody else do it and she says no three times with three different reasons, and the third is that *it is my father's and his father's and it is in my yard and if a man from nine miles down the road opens it then in thirty years there will be a story in this valley about the day the valley let a stranger open the stack*, and **that is the best argument in the chapter and it is not an engineering argument at all.**

She opens it herself, with a knife, on the twenty-third, and it takes most of a day because it has been pressed for a long time and the bales have fused, and **the yard is quiet and it is a working scene and nothing supernatural happens in it.**

What is in it: **pressed rootwood, rotted at the outside to a gray pulp, and in the middle of the bales, where the press never got to it, the wood is sound and it is cut in short lengths of a uniform length, and it is cut with a bevel.**

**Marek is not allowed to touch it and does not.** He is asked to look and he looks and he says the one sentence he is allowed to say, which is that the cutting is done and the pressing is done by two different people or in two different trades, because a press that good is not a family press.

**And Lissa Vale's refusal from Ch 103 is honored and paid off:** she said she would not open it, and she was right, and now somebody else has opened it and she is the one who has to stand in a yard and hear about it. **Say that. It is the whole of her in this chapter.**

**Resistance:** the stack is a family's and it is not evidence until somebody says it is, and the moment the fen says it is, the family's thirty-one years becomes a thing in a ledger.

**Change:** the bevel is in a second place. **That is the batch's turn and it is the whole of it: the same cut, in the same country, made by the same kind of tool, in a bale of pressed rootwood that has been in a yard for longer than anybody can date.**

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** none.

### Chapter 111 — *The Maintenance Sequence* — days 175–176, the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth

**Goal:** read the stack as engineering, and find the first hard object of Movement 2.

**Must happen.** The sound wood is dated and read with the same method as the cuts — a rule, a lens, a year measured off the ground — and it is dated, and it comes out where nobody expected it. **In living rootwood, the sound lengths in the middle of the bales were cut from wood that was alive when it was cut and it was cut in a sequence, and the sequence is a maintenance order, and the order has steps in it, and the steps are written in the wood the way the eleven cuts are written in the row.**

Not a vision. **A hand's method, dated.** Somebody pressed rootwood for a living, and the pressing was not the point, and the point was that the lengths came off in a particular order because the wood had to come off in that order.

**And then the object, and it is the batch's climax-in-substance.** In three of the bales, at the core, where the press did not reach, **there is a graft.** A living graft, cut and fitted by hand, in rootwood that was stacked as waste, on a shelf four hundred miles from any nursery, in a yard with no charter, by a family that has never signed a piece of paper and does not know what a graft is.

**Renner's standing is what the chapter is for: he has to say whether it is a graft, and he says yes, and then he says the sentence that turns the volume: *somebody made this on purpose, they made it to keep, and they put it in with the waste, and that is not how a man keeps a thing he means to use. That is how a man hides it.***

Nobody in that yard says *hiding* out loud and one of them says it and is not answered.

**Resistance:** the fen wants this to be a record and it may be a hobby, and a man who grafts for pleasure is not a conspirator, and the chapter must keep that door open without leaning on it.

**Change:** a maintenance sequence and a hand-made graft in a stack of waste, made by somebody who expected to come back and did not say so.

**Panel:** none. **Notes:** the **twenty-eighth** note, dated **the twenty-sixth**, **a record, and the standing sentence is off it, with the reason underneath** — the note is the record of a reading and a reading is not an argument.

**Goal:** the first of a month in a full yard, in somebody else's hand, and then the discovery that a copy is a thing that gets read.

**Must happen, first half — the first of next month, at the eleventh hour, in a full yard.**

The pit is not up.** And **a reader is appointed and the number is said out loud and the count of how many mornings in a fortnight it went unmeasured is read with it, and the fen does not move the figure and does not move the count.** The number is the number and the room is different, and the difference is the whole of what the fen learned in the last three months, and **nobody in the yard says that sentence out loud because it is Ilsa Kern's argument and she is in a barn four hundred miles off making it to an empty yard.**

The sub-office copy, at Fennmere, has been read by a clerk who has done nothing wrong and who has done the ordinary thing: a clerk reads what comes across his counter.

**And Perrin Osse has to decide what to do about it, and the decision is correct and it costs the fen something.** He does not hide it and he does not send it back and he **enters the fact of the reading in the register in the ordinary words with the hour**, because he is the clerk at that counter and his standing is the standing of this fen's clerk, which is that he enters what happened and reads it back.

**The consequence is immediate and it is not a catastrophe and that is the point:** the reading is a reading of a fieldbook, and a fieldbook is an instrument record, and **the record of an instrument is not the instrument**, and the office can hold the copy and can ask about the copy and **cannot get at the book, and the reason it cannot get at the book is a clause on a sheet of fen paper that names nobody and requires no signature.** The Volume 02 refusal clause is what saves this, and **it saves this because it cannot be enforced and does not need to be**, and the chapter must make that connection without a speech about it.

**And Renner is in the wrong light and knows it.** His copy is the safe one because it is his, and the safe one is the one that is now the only one nobody can rely on, because a licensed man's copy is a licensed man's and a man can lose a coat. **He says so himself, in one sentence, and it is the most uncomfortable thing anybody says in this batch.**

**Resistance:** the chapter must not be a scene about a leak in a plan. It is a scene about a month turning in a yard and a fact of ordinary office life, and the second half is about a clause that happened to be the right shape.

**Change:** the fen learns what a copy actually is, which is that a copy is a thing that gets read, and that the question is never where a record is but **who has had it in their hands.** And a figure goes into the compost line in a hand that is not the steward's, on the first of a month, in public.

### Chapter 116 — *The Household On The Shelf* — days 185–186, the fifth and the sixth of next month

**Goal:** the shallow well becomes a household decision with four households in it, and the batch pays the rootburn's ecological debt in people rather than in a number.

**Must happen.** **The part three return comes back on this chapter's first day, sent on the eighth and returned in a fortnight, and it is a line in a book and it is correct, and nothing in it is anybody's fault.** Read it out, enter it, read it back. **The office has written *held* against six items and has classified none of them, and a line saying *held* is a fact about a holder and not about a record, and the fen has just spent a chapter being told exactly that by an officer who was not trying to tell it.**

**And then the well, and the resolution of the well question, or the beginning of it.** Three options and each has a person attached to it: go down the slope to a neighbour's well, which means a debt to a household that has one; go to the well at the second house, which means the man with the spade and his family carry it; or dig, which is a hundred and forty feet of pale rootwood and a winter and money none of four houses has.

**The argument is not about water. ### Chapter 117 — *The Buyer Comes* — days 187–188, the seventh and the eighth of next month

**Goal:** the day on the buying paper arrives, and the fen is not asked for an opinion and gives one anyway.

### Chapter 118 — *The Fourth Copy* — days 189–190, the ninth and the tenth of next month

**Goal:** the batch climax. The record goes where the office cannot follow it, and the fen finds out that a copy is a thing that gets read.

**Must happen.** The part three return came back on the fifth of next month, in Ch 116, and it is a line in a book that has been in the fen's hands since. **The line is correct.** Somewhere in this batch the fen has to face the fact that the office is not a villain and has never been one, and that a correct answer to a correct form is a wall.

**And the answer is the fourth copy and the shape of it.** Not a better copy. **A copy in a shape the schedule has no part for**, which is the same trick the refusal clause played in Volume 02 and the fen knows it and says so, and Tova Reed refuses to be proud of it: *we have done this before and we know what it is and I am not going to be pleased about it in front of these people.*

**What the fourth copy is:** not a reading. **A sequence.** The eleven cuts, dated; the bevel; the sound lengths in the bales; the graft in the waste; the work order with the word *necessary*; the two different dates. **It is a list of things a man did, in order, and it has no person in it, and a direction given to another person is a record in which somebody is told what to do by somebody who was not the one who did the work, and there is no direction in this and no person in it, and the schedule has no part for a thing that is only a sequence.**

**And that is the batch's win and it is narrow and it is arguable and the fen knows it is arguable.**

**Then the cost of the batch, in the ordinary words, on the tenth of next month.** The tin at **one hundred and seventy-one days**, unmoved. The leak, with whatever Ch 109 entered. The beat, the fifteenth. The boards, one hundred and twenty-four. The well. **And the standing rule, still in force, with a second draw undecided and the fen not having decided.**

**And the question, said once, plainly, at the end, in a yard or a barn, with no history in it: the man who cut eleven branches and dressed every wound and pressed rootwood into bales and put a graft in with the waste, made a list before he started — and it is still going on, or it is not. Which.**

**Resistance:** the temptation is to have a character say *it is still going on.* **Nobody may say that. Not in this batch, and not until the volume's climax, and not as a fact.** A person may say what evidence there is and what there is not. Nobody may say the answer.

**Panel:** none — the Ch 101 panel is Batch 0001's and is spent; **Batch 0002 has one available and should not spend it on a sequence of dates.** **Notes:** the **thirty-first** note, dated **the tenth of next month**, **carrying the standing sentence.**

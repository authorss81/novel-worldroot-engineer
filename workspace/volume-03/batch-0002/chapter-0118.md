# Chapter 118

## The Fourth Copy

The line that came back on the fifth of this month is correct, and it has been in the fen's own book since the eleventh hour of that day, and it is correct in the way a form is correct, which is to say that nobody in nine miles has done anything wrong and nothing can be done about it.

The fourth copy is the answer, and it is not a better copy.

The thing the fen had to face in the six days between the fifth and the ninth is that the office has never been a villain. The records officer came up the road herself and said three things in the order she said them and looked twice on purpose for a power she expected to find and did not find, and said so first. The answer is a line in a book, and it says *held*, six times, and says in its own second line that *held* is a fact about a holder and not about a record, and that a holder has not said anything about the record at all. **And that is a wall, and it is built out of correctness, and nobody on either side of it has done a single thing wrong, and the fen will not be able to point at anybody in four years and it is going to have to be able to point at somebody in four years or it is not a body.**

The answer is a copy in a shape the schedule has no part for, and the fen worked out which shape for two days, and what it came to is a sequence. Not a reading: a list of things a man did, in order, with the dates on them, and no person in it, in the fen's own hand, and it is going into four hands.

> **The order of the work, as it is dated, and as nothing else.**
>
> **One.** A branch taken from the upper limb of a pear in a row of a hundred and forty-one, and dressed, in one week. The row carries eleven faces. Nine have been read with a rule and a lens; two have not and have not been guessed at. The faces were cut in this season: the healed growth over the first is a shade under four tenths of an inch, and this ground puts down four tenths on a limb in a year.
>
> **Two.** The face. Flat across the whole of the cut. A single bevel, about an inch and a half, on one edge, and on the same edge on all nine. There is no chisel and no draw-knife and no shoulder-blade on the district's hire sheet at the change of horses, or on the district's own sheet at the foot of the plateau.
>
> **Three.** The wounds closed in one season and the limb is carrying, and the growth below a face is five tenths and above it four.
>
> **Four.** Rootwood cut in lengths of about a foot and a half, every length in a pile of sixty-one bales within an inch of every other, cut from a seam, in a maintenance order. Outside first, then the wet, then the core. Twenty-eight layers going back without a break, and four bands in them where the layer is thicker and the wood is different, at intervals of seven years, seven years, and six years, the last of them about six years back, and six or seven bare years between them in which nothing came off that seam at all.
>
> **Five.** The same bevel, on one edge, on the end of every one of them.
>
> **Six.** Pressed hot, at a mill, and the mill is not a family press, and a family did the cutting or a household did.
>
> **Seven.** Three pieces, in three bales, at the core of the pile where the press did not reach. Cut to cut, fitted, and bound in bast. One living seam joined to another. Put in with the waste.
>
> **Eight.** A sheet of district paper, a sheet of three, with the name of an office at the head and a signature at the foot and four lines in the middle, of which one word is heavier than the others. The year on it is the year of a shaft. It is not the year on the second page of the book it was in, and both years are here, and neither of them is this sheet's fault.
>
> That is the whole of it, on two pages, and it names nothing and no one.

The argument about whether that can be held is the whole of the ninth of next month and it took a day, and the man of about fifty made the last three of the arguments and the man of about seventy made the fourth and it was not his.

"Part one is a work and its result. Part two is a work and its reading. Part three is a work, its reading, and a direction given to another person, and a direction is a record in which somebody is told what to do by somebody who was not the one who did the work," said Tova Reed, standing at the bench, at about the fourth hour on the ninth, with the ledger open and about eleven people in the room.

"**So take it part by part. It is a work and it is not a result, because a result is what the work came to and this is what was done and in what order. It is not a reading, because nobody read a tree in it; a date is not a reading and a date is in it. And it is not part three, because there is no direction in it.** Doing a thing is not telling a person what to do, and the sheet in the eighth is described and not reproduced, and the four lines that tell a clerk what to enter are not in it and are not going to be in it.

"And somebody will say the word *necessary* is a direction, and it is not. **A reason is a thing a man writes for himself at four in the morning and does not send to anybody.** There is no *you* in it. The man who did all of it is not the man who will read it, and the two are not connected by any telling, and the test is passed by not carrying the direction and not carrying the name."

And the man of about seventy said the fourth thing, and he was the fourth person to make an argument that day and he made it in three sentences and then asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

"And the form says a holder who cannot classify a record may state so, and the statement is itself a classification, and a classification is part three, and part three exists and does not count. So we cannot say we cannot classify it, and we cannot send it, and we cannot mention that it exists. **The return that went on the twenty-eighth of last month is complete and correct, and this is not on it, and nobody in nine miles can find out from that paper that there is anything in this building except a door and a seed house and a man with a bad arm.**"

---

And it was not settled there, and the man of about fifty spent the rest of the ninth and most of the tenth making the fen be honest about what it had just agreed to, and he is right, and it is in the book.

"**We have left a record off a return.** That is the sentence and I have voted against this body three times and I am not going to discover a fourth thing in a comfortable way. We sent six lines to nine miles on the twenty-eighth of last month and there is a seventh thing in that building and it is not on the six, and a return of inventories is a document and a document is a statement.

"The reason is good, and it is the same reason as the refusal clause on the door in the spring and the form's own second line, and a shape the instrument has no part for is the only place a thing can live, and this is the second time we have used it.

"And it is the fourth time in three years that this body has kept a thing on a shape. **A body which gets a good answer out of a form by finding the form cannot hold it has not beaten the form. It has found a place the form cannot reach, and the shape is ours, and in four years somebody in nine miles is going to work out how to ask for a shape.** Write down, on the day we do it, that this is the second time, and that a person in four years may find both, and that we did not do it in a hurry and we did not do it in a yard with nobody in it."

And Lissa Vale entered that, and the clerk entered the hour, and the reader asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing.

And then Tova Reed said the honest end of it, standing up, and she would not be pleased about it in front of those people and she was not.

"**We have done this before and we know what it is, and I am not going to be pleased about it in front of these people.** A shape an instrument cannot reach. I have said the sentence about a free offer with a signature attached and did not say where I read it, and about a post on a fence, and about a man's coat. Four times in three years, and every time it was the right answer, and every time it was a room where a thing could not be got at.

"And that is what it is, and it is not a victory, and I would like it written in the same book as the eleventh cut and the two that were not read. **Every one of the four is a place where something was put that an office could not reach. That is a good way to live and it is not a way to win, and the day comes when four places are not enough and the thing in them is worth more than the four places, and on that day this body is going to have to say so out loud to whoever it has been keeping it from.**"

The four copies went out on the tenth, in four hands: the ordinary post, down the fen road, up the plateau road, and inside a licensed man's coat. None of them is headed by a holder, none carries a part, and none carries a name, and the seventh column was not copied into it because the sequence has no columns, and the fen entered that as a loss.

---

And then the tenth, at about the fourth hour, in the seed house, with the bay doors open and about nine people in the room, and the reader was the man of about fifty and the clerk was at the gate, and nothing in it was softened. The standing figures of this holding, read out in the ordinary way, and none of them entered anywhere else.
>
> **The compost line.** Sixth month, entered on the first by the examiner in the steward's book, in a hand that is not the steward's, by an arrangement four months old. Nothing put in, none available, specification four hundred hundredweight, **shortfall four hundred and fifty-two hundredweight, ninety pounds eight at four shillings**, carried forward and not discharged, and the run of it six months and not discharged in six. The month now begun is the seventh, its line falls on the first of next month, and the figure is not in this book and this body does not know what it is.
>
> **The leak.** Five-eighths of an inch, twice, two rules, the second the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up. Read at the seventh hour of the first of this month in a full yard, with the reader appointed and the count read out with the figure, being the forty-third day since the last entry in the book and forty-two mornings in between, of which the number that were measured cannot be given and is not given. **It has not been measured since and there is no due date and none is to be invented, and a due date on a thing that has been done on most mornings since before the charter would be a way of saying it was not being done.**
>
> **The beat.** The fifteenth, at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of last month, nine in the quarter hour with one run of three, at the rail. Cause not established. Licensed person present, none. Nothing written underneath it. **It has not been logged since and the column is open and the fen has not opened it.**
>
> **The boards.** One hundred and twenty-four days, cut into both of them this morning, said out loud in a yard, and the number is still going up.
>
> **The well on the shelf.** Five feet and two inches on the thirtieth of last month, by the occupier, on her own rule and her own nail, three feet and ten inches against the sixteenth of a month three months back. Three and a half feet of that entered against the draw of the thirteenth of last month and the other half inch against the ground. Four houses on a day letter, one drawing each in turn, entered on the sixth of this month, **and no entry anywhere for that well coming back, and the fen has decided it will not be predicting one.**
>
> **The March's tin.** One hundred and seventy-one days, day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, with both undertakings standing: the seed house's that it will not be moved, and the trust's that it will not be asked.
>
> **The standing rule about the engineer of record.** Satisfied on the third of this month, in a room, on a day, with a licensed man writing it down, and two accounts of the same arm in the book that do not agree and were not reconciled. **The rule is in force and it says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and a second draw is on a table and the fen has not decided it, and has entered that it has not decided it for five days.**

And the man of about seventy said the only thing anybody said about the last line, and it has not been explained and is not going to be.

"That is the longest list this body has ever read out in a yard, and every figure on it is worse than the one above it, except two, and I would like somebody to look at the two."

---

And then the barn, at about the sixth hour of the tenth of next month, with the lamp lit and about nine people in it.

Tova Reed asked it once, and she asked it plainly, and she said that she was asking it for herself and not for the body, and then she asked it.

"A man cut eleven branches off one row of pears in one week in this season and dressed every one of them. A man cut rootwood out of a seam in a particular order for four years over about twenty-eight years and pressed it and put a graft in with the waste. A man wrote one word on a sheet and hid the sheet from the node.

"**He made a list before he started, and the list is not on anything, and it is in eleven trees and in a heap of waste and in a sheet of paper in a locked room. And nobody knows whether it is still going on, or it is not.** Which is it."

And nine people in a barn did not answer her, and nobody in that barn knew, and one of the people in that barn had spent three weeks and four hundred miles of road and a locked book and a well to be able to ask the question, and one of the people in that barn had a word on a sheet and could not hear the question, and the question was not asked of him.

Underneath the day's work, dated the tenth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-first note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A line in a book, correct, in the fen's own hands since the fifth of this month, and the office that sent it never having been a villain and the answer being a wall built out of correctness. A seventh thing left off a return of six, by a body that cannot say it cannot classify it, the reader of this body entering that this is the second time and that a person in four years may find both, and the seedwright saying that a shape an instrument cannot reach is a good way to live and is not a way to win. Two pages with nothing named and nobody addressed, four copies into four hands, and the seventh column not in them, entered as a loss. Standing figures on the tenth, read out in the ordinary way, none of them softened: the compost line, the leak, the beat, the boards, the well, the tin, and a rule satisfied that says nothing about what comes after it, and a second draw on a table this body has not decided. And a question asked once, plainly, in a barn, with the lamp lit, and nobody in the room knowing, and nobody in the room answering, and the question not asked of the one man in it who is dead.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

# Chapter 115

## The Asking

The rule was satisfied on the third of next month, at about the eleventh hour, in the seed house, with the bay doors open, on a Sunday, and about fourteen people in the room, and the clerk was there and the reader was there and the licensed man was there with his own book, and it was not a form.

Nobody had set it up as anything. There was no notice on the door, there was no business in the day's list, and the standing of it in this holding is that a practice with no day and no due date has to be given a day by somebody, and on the second of next month the man of about fifty said, in the ordinary way at the bench, that the rule of the fourteenth of last month was now four weeks old and that four weeks was long enough for a body to be comfortable, and that the day had better be a day somebody could name afterwards, and everybody in the room agreed, and the man of about fifty named the next day.

Tova Reed asked the question.

She did it standing, and she did it in about nine words, and she did not put her hand on anything, and she did not soften it, and she said before she asked it who she was asking on behalf of, because she is a person who does that.

"I am asking for this body," she said. "I am not asking for you and I am not asking on my own account and there is nothing in this question that is between you and me. **What is the matter with your arm.**"

And he answered her in the plainest words he had, and they were not the words of the twentieth of last month.

"It is nothing that stops me working," he said. "The four inches are the mark and the mark is where it was and it does not hurt. What I have is that the arm is not right above the elbow, and it is not right in a way that comes and goes, and I cannot tell you when it comes. It is a heavy feeling and a heat and it does not stay where it is put."

"And I can tell you what I can do with it. I can hold a rule and I can read a rule and I can do a graft, and it takes me about four minutes to change hands and I have got used to changing hands and I have not told anybody that. And I could not carry a pail up from the second house's well with my left hand on the second of this month. I carried half of it and I changed hands twice and I did not say so then and I am saying so now, and I would like it written that I said it now and not then, and that the reason is that nobody had asked me, and that is the entire reason and I am not going to improve on it."

And then he said the thing that made the two accounts not join up, and he said it flatly and without any performance about it, the way a man says a thing he has thought about for three weeks.

"On the twentieth of last month Coll Renner wrote in his book that I was not fit for a draw and that he would not answer for one, and I read it and I have thought about it every day since, and he is right and he is not right, and both of those are in the same answer and I cannot get them apart. **I do not know whether I could do it. I did not say no. I said a man who cannot do a thing is not the same as a man who has not tried it, and that is not the same sentence and it is the only one I have.** If the question had been put to me as *will you* I would not have known how to answer it, and if it is put to me as *will you* I still do not know, and I am telling you the truth about the not knowing because you have all been told the truth about everything else on this ground for a season and I am not going to be the first place you have to say that a thing cannot be done."

---

And both of them went in, and the fen did not try to make them one, and that decision is in the book with the hour on it and it is the reason this entry is worth more than a clean one.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the third of next month, in the seed house, with the bay doors open, fourteen people in the room, the clerk present and the reader present and a licensed man present with his own book, on a Sunday, and no form and no notice on the door and the day named on the second by a member of this body.
>
> Question, asked by the seedwright on behalf of this body: **what is the matter with your arm.** Answer, in his own words, and read back by the reader before it was initialled: the four inches are the mark and it is where it was and it does not hurt; the arm is not right above the elbow; a heavy feeling and a heat that does not stay where it is put; four minutes to change hands; a pail carried on the second of this month with two changes of hand and not reported then and reported now; and **that he does not know whether he could do it, that he did not say no, and that not knowing is the whole of the answer.**
>
> **The entry of the twentieth of last month stands entire and is not altered.** It is not withdrawn, it is not qualified, and it is not reconciled with the above, and the fen entered that a man asked twice about his own arm in two rooms has given two accounts and both of them are his, and that the licensed man's is a licensed man's finding and this one is a man's answer to a person, and that the fen's book holds both and does not choose, and that choosing would be the fen deciding which of two honest accounts of the same arm it prefers, and the fen does not do that and did not do it.
>
> The asking is done. **The rule of the fourteenth of last month is satisfied and nothing in it is suspended and nothing in it is spent, and it is entered that a rule about a stop, once the stop has been asked after and answered, says nothing whatever about what happens next.**

And Coll Renner wrote the second reading in his own book, standing, with the bay doors open and the wind off the door, and he read it out whole, and it is longer than the one of the twentieth of last month and the difference is entered as a difference and not as a decline.

> Arm, left. Inside and outside, from the four inches of the mark to above the elbow, and **on this occasion to the top of the shoulder, which it was not on the twentieth of last month.** There is a place on the inside of the forearm at about the middle where the skin is not the color of the skin either side of it and did not take the color back. It is not the mark and the mark is where it was, four inches, branching twice.
>
> Sensation: three fingers tested, present, and he told me the middle of the forearm is different today from yesterday and I have not found a reason for that and I have not guessed at one. Sour tested against sweet at his asking and not at mine, because on the twentieth I put it in a book of mine at his word and I am not going to do it twice on a different basis.
>
> **It is longer than on the twentieth of last month. It is not better. It is longer, and I am writing it as a length and not as a decline, because I have been licensed twenty-two years and I do not know what a decline of this is and I will not put a word in my own book that I cannot defend in a room.**
>
> The hour at about the fifth on the thirteenth is not accounted for and he does not intend to account for it and I have asked him about it once and I am not going to ask him again.
>
> Recommendation in my own hand: **fit for ordinary work. Fit for a scion and a graft. Not answering for a draw.** I will not change that line on the strength of an answer he gave to a person in a room with fourteen people in it, and I will say why on the page, because a page is what I have. **A man in a room with other people in it will tell you what he can do. A man with a licensed man's book in front of him will tell you what he will answer for. Those are two different questions and I am the man who gets asked the second one and this body has arranged, correctly, to ask the first one as well, and the two answers are both in that room and they do not agree and it is not my job to make them.**

And the man of about seventy was in the room and said nothing at all during any of it, and read the whole of it back afterwards from about eleven feet with his hand up, which is his standing, and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and did not comment on any of it, and the fen entered that he did not comment on any of it.

---

And then the argument, on the fourth, at about the seventh hour, in the yard, and it is the argument the whole of the standing rule turns on, and it took about an hour and it was not settled and it is in the book whole.

"It is done," said the man of about fifty, standing at the gate. "The asking is done. So what is the rule now. **Read it out. Read the rule out as it stands, and tell me what it says, and if it says nothing then say it says nothing in front of the clerk.**"

And Dunmar Fitch read it out, because he reads things out, and it is four lines and it is the same four lines.

> Nothing is to be attempted again by the engineer of record until a person has asked him in a room, on a day, what is the matter with his arm, and he has said so in a room, on a day, and a licensed man has written it down.
>
> A number of days is not entered. A number of days would be a promise and a promise is the thing this fen has learned to write only against itself.

"That is four lines and every one of them is about a stop," said the man of about fifty, "and the stop is now finished, and there is no fifth line, and there is no line that says what happens after the fourth line. **We have written a rule about waiting and called it a rule about care, and any body can obey it perfectly for ever and never do a thing again, and it will look exactly like a body that is being careful.** I am not saying that is what anybody here wants. I am saying it is what the paper says, and this body has spent a season insisting that the paper is the thing, and if we are going to start obeying a paper instead of reading it then I would like to know the day."

And then Marek Vale said the position he is going to hold for the rest of the volume, and he said it in a yard to about nine people and he did not argue it and he did not soften it, and it is nine sentences and it is in the book.

"I am not going to use it," he said. "I want to say that first, because everything else I am about to say sounds like a man lining up to use it. **There is a well on a shelf four hundred miles off that is five feet and two inches and four houses drink from it, and a man with a spade and four households are about to decide in daylight this week what to do about it, and the one thing I could offer that would change that household's morning is the one thing the rule of the fourteenth of last month does not forbid, because the rule is satisfied.** And I am not going to offer it, and the reason is not the rule and it is not the burn."

"**The reason is that the man at the second house has not been angry with me yet.** On the fourteenth of last month a woman in this yard said in front of nine people that I would be angry about in about nine days, and she was right about the day and wrong about the nine, and the man at the second house has not spoken to me since the fourteenth of this month and I have been off the shelf for a week. If I go back up that road with a thing in my hands that moves water, then whatever this household decides this week they will decide it against the thing I brought, and there is no version of that in which the four of them are choosing, and there is no version of that in which anybody in this yard is not choosing either, and I would rather the four of them chose badly on Wednesday with a whole bucket than well on Thursday with a man in it."

And the man of about seventy said the thing that was the whole of the answer, and he said it to Marek Vale, and it is the sentence of the twenty-second of last month given back to the man it was said about, and Marek Vale did not answer it and did not try.

"**A rule that has to be remembered by the person it was made for is not a rule. It is a habit with a number in it.** I said that about a leak at a hard standing with three people in a yard, and I have thought about nothing else since, and you have had a month to see whether it applies to a rule that four people can all remember, and it does, and I am not going to pretend that a man with a licence and a clerk and a reader in a room cannot be the one who remembers it, because he will be, and on a wet afternoon in a year he will be the one who says not yet, and he will be doing it for the sake of a word in a book and the day will be the fourteenth of the month and the rule will be four lines long and there will be nothing in it about this."

And nobody answered that, and the man of about seventy asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and the clerk entered that the argument was not settled and that no vote was taken and that nobody in that yard had asked for one, and that a body which cannot make a rule about what comes after a stop has found the second half of the thing it set out to find, four days after a household on a shelf was told in public that its bucket is three and a half feet shorter than it was, and that the fen has entered both halves on the same page and has not put them in the order anybody wanted them in.

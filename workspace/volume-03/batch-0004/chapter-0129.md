# Chapter 129

## The First Of The Eighth

The seventh month's compost line fell on the first of the eighth month, and it fell at about the eleventh hour in a yard with about twenty people in it, and it was written in a hand that is not the hand that book has been written in for eleven years, and the reader appointed for it was the man of about fifty and not the steward, and the reason for that is four months old and is hers and was hers before anybody thought it would matter, **and it is that a figure read out of a yard is a figure and not an instruction, and she had it said in a yard four months before there was a figure in this holding that anybody in another office could have asked a question about.**

> Return main, the seventh month. Entered on the first of the eighth month by the examiner, in the steward's book, in her own hand, which is not the steward's hand. Nothing put in. None available. Specification four hundred hundredweight. **Shortfall four hundred and two hundredweight, eighty pounds eight, at four shillings the hundredweight, which is the compost valuation and is not anybody's day rate.** Carried forward and not discharged.
>
> **The run of it is seven months and it has not been discharged in seven.**
>
> And against the same line, separately, in the same hand, and entered as a separate thing and not as part of the shortfall: **the hire of a man who went up the plateau road on the twelfth of the seventh month, thirty-two shillings, being four shillings a day for eight days of a road that is four days. Entered four shillings on the twelfth and twenty-eight shillings on the sixteenth.** Four shillings the hundredweight and four shillings the day are two different figures and this body has put them in one book and has entered that they are two figures.
>
> And on a line of its own, entered as a figure and not as a sentence:
>
> **Zero.** A hundred and forty-one trees on a shelf four hundred miles off, read and cleaned and dated. **Not one hundredweight of anything has ever come back to this holding.** That is the first zero that has been entered in this body's history and it has been entered as a figure.

Gael Senn read the whole of it out loud, the figures first and then the words around them, which is her practice, and the man of about fifty read it back out of the book after her with the hour on it, and he asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and Dunmar Fitch entered the hour and read the entry back before he initialled it.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody has thanked her for the compost line in seven months and she has not asked and the fen has entered that she has not asked, and the reason she writes it in another hand is the first of last month and has not moved since, and it is her own: **a book that has one page in another hand in it is a book anybody can check, and a book that has every page in one hand in it is a book that can only be checked by one person.**

---

And then about ten people stood in that yard looking at the bottom of the page, because a zero is a figure and figures in this body get read out, and nobody had ever seen one on this page before and the man of about fifty asked what it was standing up with his hat in his hands.

"It is a hundred and forty-one trees," said Anneke Roos, "and it is not a figure of anything of ours, and I am going to say the three things about it and then stop."

"One. It is not a loss. **A loss is a thing this holding had and does not have, and this holding never had a hundred and forty-one trees and is not going to get any.** Two. It is not a fault. Nobody on that shelf has done this body a harm and nobody on that shelf owes this body a hundredweight, and the fen has no standing over a hundred and forty-one trees and has never had and is not going to ask for any. Three. It is a zero because there has never been anything else, and the reason there has never been anything else is that a thing comes back to this holding on a cart or in a barrow or over a plank, and a pear does not come back over a plank."

"And the fourth thing, which is the one the reader is going to want," she said. "**In eleven years not one hundredweight of anything has come back to this holding off that shelf. Nothing. Not a crate, not a basket, not a stone of fruit.** And the flock went up the fen road on the tenth of a month eight months back and has not come down it, and the line of the compost is short by four hundred and two hundredweight a month, and **the two facts are on the same page today and they are not the same fact and I am not going to let anybody in a yard put them in one column in a year.**"

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the first of this month, by the clerk, on the standing of the money and against the line above it: **zero.** Not one hundredweight of anything has ever come back to this holding. **It is entered as a figure and it is not a claim, and no person in this holding has standing over any part of it, and the person who keeps the money has said so out loud in a yard and it is entered that she said so.**

---

And at about the same hour, in the seed house, with the bay doors open behind the bench and about nine people in the room, the examination was kept, and it is kept on the first of every month, and it is kept by choice and there is nothing in this holding now that requires it.

> Kept at the eleventh hour of the first of this month, with no officer and no form, by the person who keeps it, by choice. **Nothing found.** Licensed person present, none. The column for a cause is left as it is left and no cause is entered, and it is not to be read as a finding that there is no cause.

**Nothing found, and not *all well*,** and the difference between those two phrases is the standing of the whole of this arrangement and it is in the book and it has been in the book for a long time. She wrote nothing in advance, as is her practice, and she wrote it after, and the clerk entered the hour.

---

And then the woman who does the sums stood in the yard and did the one she has to do, and the sum does not get worse, and she said so out loud, and she said it the way she says things, which is without any pleasure in it in front of anybody.

"Fifty hundredweight better than the sixth month," Anneke Roos said, with her own ledger open on the wall side of the gate and the slate under it. "Four hundred and two against four hundred and fifty-two. **Eighty pounds eight against ninety pounds eight, and I am going to say that number out loud in a yard because that is what this book is for, and I am not going to be pleased about it in front of you and I would like it entered that I said I would not be and then did not.**"

"And here is why, and the why is the whole of the figure, and it is not a virtue. **Two long journeys are not in this month. Thirteen weeks of horse is twenty-six hundredweight at four. The coming down the plateau road on the twenty-seventh of a month three months back is twenty-six hundredweight at four. That is fifty-two hundredweight of shortfall that belongs to two months ago and is not in this one.** And the next one will have a journey in it, because a man is going up that road again this week and again the week after, and every one of them is four shillings the day and not four shillings the hundredweight, and I would like somebody in four years to be able to tell the difference between those two numbers by looking at this page."

"So the line is better and it is not better. **It is a month in which nothing came back.** That is twice now and it was once, and a zero is the first one this body has ever entered and I have written it as a figure because it is a figure and because I do not intend to write a sentence about a shelf four hundred miles off that is not my ground and that a hundred and forty-one trees stand on."

"And the last of it, and I have been asked for it and I am giving it. **The eighth month's figure is not in this book and this holding does not know what it is, and it is the first day of a month and the first day is exactly when it does not know.** The seventh month of a run is entered and the run goes on. The figure that would go beside it is not in this book and I am not going to guess at one on the first of a month in front of twenty people, and let the clerk enter that I was asked for it and gave it and that the answer was that I have not got it."

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the first of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the money: **the figure of this month's compost line was asked for and was not held.** The person who keeps the money in this body was asked for it in a yard and gave the answer she has. **The run of the shortfall is seven months and it has not been discharged in seven. The month now begun is the eighth and its line falls on the first of next month, which is a Tuesday.**

---

And on the same day, four hundred miles off, in daylight, four households looked at a letter.

It is on a stone at a well at the fourth house and it has been on that stone since the sixth of last month and it is a day letter, which means four houses draw one in four on a day each and the drawing is written on the stone by whoever has the pail, and the first house is a Tuesday because a Tuesday is the worst of the days, there are four of them in it and the oldest of them is sixty-one and the pail is heavy in the heat.

They looked at it at about the fourth hour on the first, in daylight, at the well, with the occupier of the fourth house and the man of the second house and a third person who is not of the fen and had come up the plateau road two days before with a rule and a chain and no letter and had not been asked by anybody in that yard what he was.

The sheet is Merrid Ollvar's hand, and it is the month, and it is the whole of the month, and it says what a month of one household drawing in turn has done.

> Days, twenty-eight. Houses, four, in turn, first house a Tuesday. **Pails carried up in the month, counted by each house in its own pail: two thousand and ninety-six.**
>
> That is the whole of it. The four pails are not the same pail.

And the figure on it is a household's figure. **Four pails are four pails and a pail is not a measure, and the fen that has spent eleven years putting a rule to everything it has ever touched cannot check that number and cannot reduce it and will not pretend to, and the number is on a stone four hundred miles off and it is true and it is not verifiable and those two things have never once stopped being in the same sentence on this page.**

The third person asked the woman who keeps the letter how a pail could not be a measure, and she told him, and it took her about nine words and she did not soften them.

"Because a pail is what a house has got," she said. "Mine is deeper than a narrow one and I have had it eleven years and the hoop is out at the middle, and you would fill that to a hand's breadth less than you would fill a narrow one, and a man standing at a well in the middle of a month cannot know which pail the number is in. **So the number is right and it is not a figure of anything but four households and three pails, and I have written it down in a hand you can read and I will not have anybody at nine miles reduce it to gallons.**"

And he wrote that down where he could, which is a piece of paper in a coat, and the fen has entered that the sheet is a household's and the man who took a copy of it was not of the fen and was not asked to and was not thanked and is not on any page of this body's book.

And the man of the fen was not there. **Nobody from Lantern Reach was at that well on the first of this month.** The man who came down the four-day road on the sixth of last month and was in a yard at Lantern Reach from the fifteenth of that month to the thirtieth and went on an afternoon cart is not one of the three people standing at that well, and this body does not know where he is and has not asked and is not going to.

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the first of this month, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body has done and not done:
>
> **This body was not at the well at Cauldron Reach on the first of this month.** It was four hundred miles off, in a yard, entering a figure it did not have, at about the eleventh hour, and the two things fell on the same day and one of them is a figure and the other one is a household.
>
> **The choice was the compost line over the shelf, and the choice was not made in a yard.** It was made in a book on the thirtieth of last month, in the ordinary course, by a woman who had a line to close and did not have a road, and the reason is entered and it is in the fen's own words and it is not a defense: **a body cannot be in two places on the first of a month and the seventh month of a run does not wait for a road.**
>
> The fen entered that it had four hundred miles of road in front of it on the thirtieth of last month and a figure to enter on the first and did not write to a household, and did not send a man, and did not ask. **The reason it did not write is entered and it is the reason above.** It is not entered as correct and it is not entered as a fault. It is entered as what this body did with a day it only had one of.

And then nothing else happened on the first of this month, and about twenty people went home, and the yard was empty by the second hour, and the man of about fifty said one thing at the gate to nobody in particular and the clerk entered it because it was said in a yard and it was about a thing.

"That is the first month this body has been in the wrong place, and it was in the wrong place on purpose, and the reason is arithmetic, and I would like somebody in four years to come back and tell me whether I was right."

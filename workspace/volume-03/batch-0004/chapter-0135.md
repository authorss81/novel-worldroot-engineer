# Chapter 135

## The Head

He attempted it on the twelfth of this month, at a hard standing in the fen, in this holding's own low ground, and he began at about the second hour of the afternoon and it was over at about the sixth, with about nine people in that yard the whole of the time.

In the yard: **a licensed man with a lamp, a steward with a page, the reader of this body, the clerk at the gate, a licensed man with his own book who was not going to answer for any of it, and about nine people, who came because this fen does not keep a thing like this a secret and has entered that it does not intend to start.**

And he said the method out loud before he used it, which is his standing, and he said it to the yard and not to the two licensed men.

"I am going to say what I am about to do and then I will do it. **Water, heat and rootlight together, over about four feet, for about four hours, through a living path, and returned.** That is the whole of it. There is no second thing this afternoon and nothing else anybody in this yard will be asked to do."

"And I am going to say the other half, because I say both halves or I say nothing. **I have two links and both of them are on a shelf four hundred miles off in a woman's hand, and there is no link of my own in this yard, and the path I am going to use is this fen's.** That is the whole of the difference between this afternoon and the one on a plateau, and the whole of the cost, and nobody in this yard is going to improve on it afterwards, including me."

---

And then the rule, in force, four lines, satisfied on the third of last month with a licensed man writing it down, and every one of the four is about a stop.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of last month, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called.** The fen entered on the third of last month that there is no fifth line and there never has been one, and entered that again on the twelfth with about nine people in the yard, **and entered that the rule was satisfied and that satisfaction is not permission, and that the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop.**

And he used the hole in it and said out loud that he was using it, and the fen entered that he used it, and entered that the fen had known since the second of this month and had entered that too and had not voted on it, because there was nothing to vote on.

"I am not breaking a rule," he said, at the edge of the hard standing. "**The rule stops a man at four feet and I am not going past four feet. It says a draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying, this return is carrying, and the meter is in this book's at ten past one this afternoon. It says a stop is called by a licensed man, and it was called at about the sixth hour, and Coll Renner called it, and I did not call it and I would not have been allowed to.** And after the sixth hour this afternoon the rule does not say one word. It was written to be four lines long. It is four lines long. I would like the reader to read them back out loud in this yard so that everybody here has heard them and not only me."

And the man of about fifty read them back out loud in a yard with nine people in it, and nobody said a word, and the clerk entered the hour.

---

And Coll Renner was in that yard the whole of the four hours with his own book open on the wall side of the hard standing, and he did not answer for it, and he said why before anybody asked him, which is his practice and has been all season.

"I wrote *not answering for a draw* in this book on the twentieth of a month three months back and I wrote it before he did the thing, and I have not taken it back. **A man in a room with other people in it will tell you what he can do. A man with a licensed man's book in front of him will tell you what he will answer for. Those two answers are in this yard at the same time and they are going to be about a foot apart and they are both true and it is not my job to make them agree, and I have been licensed twenty-two years and I have never once made two men agree with me in a room.**"

And the page, in his own hand, in his own book.

> Taken by me, at the hard standing, on the twelfth of this month, from the second hour of the afternoon to about the sixth, in a yard with nine people in it, with a lamp and a page held by the steward.
>
> **Account given by the engineer of record, and I wrote it down as he gave it.** Water, heat and rootlight together, four feet, about four hours, through a living path in this holding's own low ground, and returned. He named the two links, said both are on a shelf four hundred miles off, and that there is no link of his own in this yard.
>
> **Account taken. The mark is unchanged: four inches along the inside of the left forearm, branching twice, the color of a healed burn and not a bruise. Not longer, no second mark, and no burn running past it.**
>
> **The account is longer than the one I took on the third of last month and it is not better.** His words were shorter then and the numbers were more. **The two accounts of this arm in this holding's book do not agree and have not agreed since the third of last month, and this one does not reconcile them and I am not going to try.**
>
> **There is an hour at about the fourth that is not in the account. I have asked him about it once, standing, in the yard, and he has not answered it, and I am not going to ask him again, because a man who asks twice is a man asking to be answered.**
>
> **He is not fit for a second one inside a week. I have written that in this book before and I am writing it again and it is not a new opinion and I am not softening it.**

And about the cost, from him, in the yard, at about the seventh hour, to nine people and to nobody in particular.

"Taste is gone. **Three fingers of the left hand gone from the second joint, the same as the first time, back by morning, and I am not going to make a virtue of it.** Sour has not come back since the second hour and I am not going to promise you that it will. Nobody in this holding has asked me a third time. Enter that nobody has, and I would like the man of about seventy to hear that I noticed."

---

And then the head, in the book in two places. That is the cost, and it is not the arm.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the afternoon of the twelfth of this month, by the clerk, in the water record and against the compost line of this month, on the standing of a cost, which is that a cost is entered as a cost and not as a fault and not as a virtue: **the head this holding draws off is down four inches and has not come back.** Sixteen households and this body are on that head. **Four inches is not a quarter of an inch, and a quarter of an inch is where a licensed man stops calling it a weep, and nobody in this yard is going to call this one.**

And then the woman who does the sums, in a yard, on the morning of the thirteenth, with about twenty people in it, because a thing that takes a head down four inches is not a thing anybody is going to hear about secondhand.

"Right," said Anneke Roos. "**I have got a hard number and a soft one and I am saying the hard one first.**"

"The head this holding draws off carries sixteen households and this body, and four inches off it is four inches off every one of them. A household of four in a dry week is about **twenty-eight pails.** Sixteen households is **four hundred and forty-eight pails.** Water brought in this district is **a shilling a pail**, a carting man's figure from a yard in the low acre two years ago, unchanged, and I have not gone and checked it this week and I am saying that on the page."

"**Four hundred and forty-eight pails at a shilling a pail is four hundred and forty-eight shillings. That is twenty-two pounds and fourpence for one dry week.** A month is thirty days, which is four weeks and two days, and four dry weeks and two days of that is four hundred and forty-eight pails taken thirty days over seven, which is **one thousand nine hundred and twenty shillings. That is ninety-six pounds.**"

"And the compost line is **eighty pounds eight** for a month. **Ninety-six pounds is worse than eighty pounds eight. I am going to say that out loud in a yard in front of twenty people because that is what this yard is for, and I am not going to enjoy it and I am not going to pretend to.**"

"And now the half that makes it a lie. **I do not know how many dry weeks there are in a year and neither does anybody in this yard, and I am not going to invent a multiplier and call it a year.** So the honest form of it is: **one dry week costs the sixteen households of this holding more than a month of the compost line costs this holding, and four dry weeks and two days costs ninety-six pounds against eighty pounds eight, and the fen cannot say how many of those weeks there are in a year and will not put a year on it.**"

"And the other half, and I want it read back. **Four inches of head is not a thing a household can argue with. Sixteen households and this body are on one head, the four inches came off it this afternoon because a man in this yard did it, and there is nothing on that face a household can be asked about and nothing it can be told to do.**"

> Entered at the ninth hour of the morning of the thirteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the money: **one dry week: four hundred and forty-eight pails, four hundred and forty-eight shillings, twenty-two pounds four. Four weeks and two days: one thousand nine hundred and twenty shillings, ninety-six pounds, against a compost line of eighty pounds eight.** The person who keeps the money stated before the sum that the number of dry weeks in a year is not known, **and that one dry week costs the households more than a month of the compost line costs this holding.**

---

And then the leak, on the morning of the thirteenth, at the seventh hour, at the hard standing, in public, with the reader appointed standing up before the pump was touched, and he gave his reason for taking it and it is the reason he gave in a month three months back.

"Thirty-one years of counting a mill wheel in the voice of a man reciting a habit," said Jonas Byre. "**If a figure is going to be said out in this yard with twenty people standing round it then the man who counts out loud is the one who does it, and that is me, and I have not had to ask for it once.**"

> Read at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of this month, at the hard standing, in a yard of about twenty, by the reader, twice, with two rules, the second of them the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up: **five-eighths.**
>
> The reader read the figure back into the yard and the examiner read the whole of it back after him, and neither was thanked and neither can be.
>
> **The figure is entered as the figure. The fen entered it and entered nothing about whether it has moved, and entered no cause, and entered no interval and did not offer one.** The practice has no due date and none is to be invented. **This body will not say whether that figure is the figure of the first of this month or is not, and nobody in this yard has asked it and nobody is going to.**

---

And the man of about seventy was fetched on the afternoon of the thirteenth, the eighth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was told the four lines of the rule, the figure off the head, the reading of the leak and the hour at about the fourth, in that order, and he asked for the hour again and then for the four lines.

"You have done the thing the rule was written to stop you doing," he said, "and the rule did not stop you, and I said on the fourth of last month that it would not, and I am seventy-one and I have been right about the small things for a long time. **And I would like somebody in this yard to be careful about how much comfort they take from that.**"

He put his hand on his knee and said nothing for about half a minute, and the yard waited.

"It is not a comfort. **A rule a man can get round by reading it once and finding the gap in it was never a rule, and the man who wrote those four lines knew that and wrote them anyway, and the man who used the gap knew it too, and neither of them is a fool.** And that is the whole of what a yard has to say about it and I am not going to say it again this month."

"And the other thing, which is what I was brought out for. **Ninety-six pounds against eighty pounds eight, four hundred and forty-eight pails in a dry week, nobody in that yard knows how many dry weeks there are, and the woman who did it said so before she said the number.** In eleven years I have watched about forty people in this yard say a number and not one of them said the half of it first. **It is not a finding and it is not an argument and it is a better practice than any other in this book, and I would like my initial put against nothing.**"

And the reader read it back, the clerk entered the hour, and nobody thanked anybody.

Underneath the day's work, dated the thirteenth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-seventh note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Four hours on a living path in this holding's own low ground, a licensed man holding a lamp, a licensed man eight feet off with his own book open who does not answer for it, about nine people in a yard the whole of it. Two links, both on a shelf four hundred miles off, and no link of his own in this yard. A rule of four lines, satisfied on the third of last month, every line about a stop, no fifth line, the gap in it used on purpose, and the four lines read back out loud so that everybody there had heard them. Taste gone, three fingers gone from the second joint and back by morning, sour not back, and nobody in this holding has asked him a third time and the man noticed that nobody had. A licensed man's account longer than the one he took on the third of last month and not better, two accounts of the same arm that do not agree, and an hour at about the fourth that is in neither, asked about once and not again. A head down four inches and not come back, sixteen households and this body on it, one dry week at four hundred and forty-eight pails and a shilling a pail against a compost line of eighty pounds eight, the number of dry weeks in a year entered as not known and no year put on the figure. A leak read at the seventh hour in a yard of about twenty, five-eighths, entered as the figure, with nothing entered about whether it has moved. And a man of seventy-one saying that a rule with a gap in it was never a rule, and that forty people in a yard have said numbers in eleven years and not one said the half of it first.*

*This is a record and not an argument, and the sentence is off this note for that reason, and the reason is written here so that the chain cannot be seen to have dropped one.*

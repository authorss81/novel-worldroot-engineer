# Chapter 130

## Served, Not Answered

She was not at a gate. She was at the foot of the plateau road on the second of this month at about the eleventh hour, on the flat ground where the road comes up out of the low country and turns into the cutting, with a satchel open on a stone and four sheets of paper on the stone under a stone, and she was working.

Marek Vale walked down to her because there was nobody else to walk down, and she looked up, and he said who he was, and she said her name once, and he did not write it down.

"You had it four months ago," she said. "I came up your fen road and gave it at the gate and your clerk put it in a book. **I am not going to say it a second time for a man to take it down, and if your clerk wants it he can have it off the page he wrote it on and that is the proper way and it does not need me to stand in a road saying it again.**"

Then she went back to the stone, and she worked, and he stood in the road about four minutes and then asked whether she would walk up with him, and she said she would finish the four sheets first because they go on the ordinary post at noon and the noon post does not wait.

So this body has a records officer of the district standing in a yard at about the first hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, and **she was not announced and she was not entered by the clerk, because she is already in the book, and the fen entered that she was not entered a second time and entered the reason she gave for it, which is the reason above, and the reason is correct and this body did not think of it.**

She is about thirty-four. She has a coat for the road and boots with the mud of four days on them and a satchel she has carried a long way and she did not sit down while she was in this yard.

And she said three things, and the order she said them in is the shape of the day, and the fen entered the order and did not improve on it.

---

"First, and it is the form and it is the only reason I am up this road," said Sabin Roell. "**The fourth part of the schedule of records held, served on the holder in person, and the holder is to produce the records within this body's own keeping for classification at the office at the foot of the plateau on the fourteenth of this month, and to state in writing, for each one produced, the part it falls in.** That is a document and I have it in the satchel and it is signed and it is dated and there is nothing wrong with it."

She put it face up on the bench at the north gate and squared it with two fingers, which is a thing a person does with a paper they have carried a long way.

"Second, and I am telling you this before you have read it, because you will read it in about a minute and I would rather you read it knowing it was coming." She put the second sheet on top of the first. "**And where the holder does not state a part within the time stated, the record is held to be in such class as this office determines, and that class is entered against the record and not against the holder, and the holder is notified of it after it has been entered.**"

She stepped back from the bench and let the man of about fifty read both of them, and he read them twice, and he read the second one three times, and Dunmar Fitch read them back out of the book afterwards with the hour on them.

"Third," she said. "**I cannot take that line off it.** I have read the fourth part before today. I have read it four times this year for other holders and I know what that line does and I know that the office that wrote it does not intend anything by it that it has not written down. **And I am not able to remove it, and I am not going to pretend I do not know what I am handing you, and if you want that in your book you can have it in those words, which are mine and not the office's.**"

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, at the north gate of Lantern Reach, by the clerk, in the words of the person who served it: **the line cannot be taken off. I am not able to remove it. I am not going to pretend I do not know what I am handing you.**
>
> The line is a line of the office and the words are a woman's and the two have been entered separately and are not the same thing, and the fen has entered that they are not the same thing, because a body that puts an officer's opinion in an officer's mouth in a book is a body that will be found out having done it in about four years.

And then the door behind her. She stood at the gate for the best part of an hour while this body argued with itself in public, and at the end of it she was at the north gate with the door behind her and the two served sheets on the bench in front of her, and **the door was behind her because she had finished being in a room with this body and she had not been asked one question and had answered one question that nobody asked.**

> Served at the north gate of Lantern Reach on the second of this month, in person, by a records officer of the district, on the fourth part of the schedule of records held.
>
> The holder is to **produce the records within this body's own keeping for classification**, at the office at the foot of the plateau, **on the fourteenth of this month**, and to state in writing, for each one produced, the part it falls in.
>
> **And where the holder does not state a part within the time stated, the record is held to be in such class as this office determines, and that class is entered against the record and not against the holder, and the holder is notified of it after it has been entered.**

---

And the man of about fifty read it out in the ordinary way, standing, with his hat off, and the yard had about twenty people in it by then, and this is what he said and it is the worst of the instrument and it is correct.

"**We answered this form in seven days in a month three months back. We put *held* against six items and we classified nothing, and we classified nothing on purpose, and the office wrote on its own sheet in its own hand that the word *held* is a fact about a holder and not about a record, and that a holder who has not stated a part has not said anything about the record at all.** That answer was correct. It was complete. It took a records officer a fortnight of a district's time to tell us in writing that we had not touched anything.

"And that answer is now the answer that puts a class on our records without a word from anybody. **We said nothing and the nothing was correct and the nothing has been sitting in a book for three months waiting for a line to tell it what it was.** Silence was never a refusal. Nobody in this yard ever thought it was. **Silence was never anything at all until a line came along and said what it is.**"

He put his hand flat on the second sheet.

"And I am not going to stand in a yard and say that anybody in nine miles has done this on purpose, because I have been in this yard all season long and I have learned that an office answers correctly in seven days and does not have to be believed. **What I am going to say is this. There is a thing coming the fourteenth and it is coming because we did the right thing, and there is no answer to it that anybody in this yard can write, and I would like that entered before somebody in this yard stands up and says we have been let down.**"

And then the question, which is the question this body asks about everything, and it is the steward's and not his.

---

"Who loses," said Lissa Vale.

She said it the way she has said it every day of this season, standing, with her hands behind her back, and about twenty people were in that yard and none of them knew in advance that she was going to ask it and the reader of this body had not asked it and the clerk had not asked it.

"**Two.** And I have found two and I have gone through the yard twice and I would like the clerk to enter that I went through it twice.

"**One is this body.** We are going to have to produce what we hold and state a part for each of the things we produce, and there are six things on a return and the fen has not decided what part any of them falls in, and the line on that sheet says that if we do not say then the office says, and the office will say it correctly and in seven days and we will be told afterwards. That is one and it is us.

"**And the other one is a woman on a shelf four hundred miles off whose ledger is a record.** Merrid Ollvar has a ledger. It is a book of what a household has and what it has done and what it has had, and it goes back years, and it is a record within any meaning of that word, and **it is a record of a household, and it is the only record of that household that anybody has, and there is not one person in this fen who has standing to state a part for it and not one person in this fen who can be asked whether a part has been stated for it.** And in four years a clerk in nine miles may hold a class against it that a woman of fifty-four never saw and cannot answer, on the ground that a body four hundred miles off did not write a letter."

Nobody in that yard said anything for about four seconds.

"**We are not going to tell her. I want that written down in the ordinary words this morning and not at the end of the month.** We are not going to write to her and we are not going to send a man up that road to warn her and we are not going to send anything at all, because a warning is a document and a document about a household's ledger is a thing an office can hold, and because she has a buyer she is not going to have and four houses that draw one in four and a well that is three and a half feet shorter than it was, **and she has enough of other people's documents in her yard.**

"And the clerk is to enter that this body did not write to her and entered that it decided not to, and the two are separate lines and the second one is the one that matters, and I am not going to be thanked for either of them because I would rather somebody looked at them hard than be kind to me about them."

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk: **two lose.** The first is this body. The second is a woman on a shelf four hundred miles off whose ledger is a record of a household and is the only one there is, and this body has no standing over it and no power to state a part for it and no power to ask whether a part has been stated.
>
> **This body did not write to her. This body decided not to.** Both are entered and they are two lines and the second line is the decision and the decision was taken in a yard in front of about twenty people and was not voted on and there was nothing to vote about.

---

And then the reader for the answer was appointed standing up on the second of this month, at about the third hour, and the appointment was made out loud before the sheets were put in a drawer, and it is in the book and the man appointed was the man of about fifty, and he read the two sheets back out of the book whole.

And **the answer was not given on the second.** It could not have been, and the fen entered that it could not have been and entered the reason, which is that there are six items and a shut drawer and a sequence of two pages that has no part in any schedule, and that a body which decides in an hour what it will say in twelve days has not decided anything.

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, by the clerk: **the reader for the answer is the man of about fifty.** The answer is not given on this day.
>
> **The day of production is the fourteenth of this month. This holding has twelve days and has not decided what it is going to say in them, and the fen has entered that it has not decided, and that is a fact about a day and not a finding, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in.**

And Sabin Roell took her two served sheets off the bench, squared them against each other, put them in the satchel, and went down the plateau road on foot in the same boots, and the fen entered that she did not say goodnight to anybody and that nobody said anything to her and that **neither of those is a fault and the fen entered that neither is a fault**, because a records officer who has done her work is not a person anybody has to be courteous to at the end of a road, and a body that has begun expecting courtesies from officers is a body that is about to be disappointed in a very small way on a very ordinary day.

At the gate, with the door behind her, she stopped and said one more thing and it was not one of her three and it was not written down, and the clerk entered it and the man of about fifty read it back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

"Nobody has asked me whether I have read the fourth part before," she said. "**I have been in this district four years and nobody has asked me one question about one of these papers, and I have come up a road with a satchel twice this year and both times it was to hand a sheet to a man who was not in a hurry.** Read that back and put it in the book as it stands, and do not put anything after it, because if I am wrong about it then the book is wrong and not me."

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the second of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, in the words of the person who served the instrument and with the door behind her: **nobody has asked me whether I have read the fourth part before.** Entered as it stands, with nothing after it, and the reason, which is hers: if it is wrong then the book is wrong and not the woman.

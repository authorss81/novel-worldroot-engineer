# Chapter 136

## The Day Of Production

They went up the plateau road on the morning of the fourteenth at about the seventh hour, three of them in a cart, and got there at about the eleventh, and it is four days up and four days down and nobody said the number out loud and everybody in the cart knew it.

Three went: **Marek Vale as engineer of record, the man of about fifty as reader, and Dunmar Fitch as clerk.** The clerk entered the hour of everything that was said and read it back, and about six other people were in the room, and they waited behind a man with a form case for about nine minutes and did not go round.

And then the office asked, in the ordinary way, in the ordinary voice, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the answer is the answer of the sixth line of a return of three months back and it went in the book of this body in the ordinary words, and the reader of this body said the six words of it himself without being asked.

---

The six things were produced, and each one was produced with a part stated against it, and the parts were part two, six times.

> Produced at the office at the foot of the plateau on the fourteenth of this month, in person, at the fourth part of the schedule of records held.
>
> **One. The door, being the schedule, the notice, the four sheets and the refusal clause, and the six clauses of the second of a month three months back. Part two.**
> **Two. The seed-route ledger, the whole of it, and with it a count of the readings entered under the seventh column, given as a reading and not as a figure off a board: twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-six, twenty-seven, in the order they were made, with the day of each. Part two.**
> **Three. The three books, being the log, the work book and the acceptance column, and in the same bundle a fourth thing which is in a shut drawer in the well house, which is a description of a piece of a machine, and which is not listed on any return and is not stated to be part of anything. Part two for the three books, and the fourth thing was produced and not classified.**
> **Four. A reading of an instrument, taken by a licensed reader on a hired trestle on another district's ground, in a stone barn, in front of about eleven people, on the seventeenth of a month four months back. Part two. The instrument itself is not produced. It is not this body's, it is in a locked room four hundred miles off, and it is not coming down that road this season.**
> **Five. A copy of the fourth above, in the keeping of a licensed man, being a licensed man's own copy and not this holder's record. Entered here on purpose, so that a person reading this return in four years does not come to the conclusion that this body has kept something back.**
> **Six. The compost lines, six months of them, entered on the first of each month, in two hands, the first page of the first of them by the person who keeps the books of this body and the rest by another person. Part two.**

And the seventh thing was not produced, and nothing was said about it, and it is a sequence of two pages with nothing named in it and no part in any schedule in this district.

"A sequence is not a direction," said the reader of this body, to nobody, at the counter, before they went in. "A part three is a direction given to another person. **A shape is not a part. And if this body stands in that room today and says it cannot classify the sequence, then the statement is itself a classification, and a classification is part three, and part three exists and does not count.** So there is no version of the next ten minutes in which this body says that sentence, and we are not going to say it, and I would like it read back in the cart on the way down."

It was read back in the cart on the way down, by the clerk, and the engineer of record read it after him and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

---

And the one thing that took longest was a figure, and it is in the book because it is the only figure this body has handed over in a shape instead of as a number, and the officer asked for it a second time and got the same answer.

"You have written twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-six, twenty-seven against a column," he said. "**I want a number.**"

"**You have one.** Twenty-seven, at the fourth hour of this morning, in a yard, with about twenty people in it, by the person who keeps the books, and it is the twenty-seventh reading entered under that column since the flood and it is a count of readings and not a count of anything else."

"That is not what I asked for and you know it is not." The officer put the pen down. "**I asked whether the twenty-seventh agrees with the twenty-sixth and the twenty-fifth, and I am asking because a column of readings is a column somebody has counted, and a count that nobody has checked is a rumour with a number at the top of it.**"

"Then the answer is yes and the fen does not care whether you believe it, and I am going to give you the shape of it in three lines." The reader of this body did not raise his voice. "**Twenty-four on the first of last month. Twenty-five on the sixteenth of last month, in a full yard, with a stranger in it. Twenty-six on the thirtieth of last month, in a yard of about twenty-five. Twenty-seven on the sixteenth of this month, in a yard of about twenty. Four readings in forty-five days, and none of them moved because of anything said in a room, and one of them moved because a stranger stood at the back of a yard with her finger on a board.**"

"That is a reading and not a number, and a person in four years can take that sheet and count the column on it and find this body's count is wrong. **That is the reason it is in this shape and not in a figure. A number off a board cannot be disagreed with. It can only be believed or not believed, and this holding has spent eleven years finding out what that costs.**"

The officer wrote it down, all of it, and at the end he said, without any emphasis at all, that it was the first time in a long while that a holder had given him a figure he could check.

---

And then the ordinary question, asked in the ordinary voice, by an officer of that office, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, with about six other people in the room and nobody looking up.

"Does the holder hold any other record."

And the man of about fifty answered it. It is the answer of the sixth line of a return of three months back and he said the six words of it himself.

"**The form is not a record of this holder. A return is a list of this holder's records and not a list of what other people have written inside a thing this body was lent.**"

The officer wrote it down. He was not satisfied, and he said so, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has a right to say.

"I am not satisfied with that," he said, "and I want it in the room that I am not satisfied, and I am not going to pretend to you that it satisfies me. **I have a form in front of me with six lines on it and I have a person in front of me who has been extremely careful, and the two of those are not the same thing and I am the officer of this office and it is my whole afternoon.**"

"**You are right to say it and I am obliged to let you say it, and I would like it entered in my own book that you said it and that nobody in this room stopped you.**"

"**I am doing my job,"** said the officer. "That is all I am doing. There is no more of it than that and there is not going to be any more of it than that today, and nobody in this room is going to be obstructed by me and if anybody tells you afterward that anybody was, you come back with the date and I will answer for it.**"

And the clerk entered that, and the reader read it back, and nobody thanked the officer and the officer did not expect to be thanked and the fen entered that he did not expect to be thanked, because a man who expects to be thanked is a man who is doing something else.

---

And what came back, and it is not an answer to the return, and it is a note, and it is correct, and it is four lines in the office's hand on a slip the size of half a sheet, and it was handed across the counter by a clerk who was not asked to hand it across and who did.

> **Noted: the holder produced six items and was asked whether it held any other record, and answered that it did not say. No classification is entered against any item by this note. The office notes the answer for its own use. The holder is not required to answer further and the office has not required it.**

The man of about fifty read that out twice in the office's own room, standing at a counter with a queue behind him, and then he asked for it to be read back to him, and Dunmar Fitch read it back to him out of the book with the hour on it, and then he asked for it a second time.

"Read it again, please," he said, "and I will tell you why and I am not going to pretend the reason is a good one."

"**Because in four years somebody is going to come to that return and read the sixth line and think there is a seventh thing, and they will be right to think it, and I have wanted this body to be the kind of body that is caught out by its own page and not by a stranger's guess. And I have no idea whether I am being careful or frightened and I have entered that I do not know which, and I would like the clerk to enter that too.**"

"And I will say the other half. **Nobody in this room knows anything about a wall four hundred miles off, or about a drawer, or about a man in a coat, or about a woman of fifty-four and a ledger, and every word of that is true and none of it is on this counter.**"

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the fourteenth of this month, in the office at the foot of the plateau, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing entered without being asked: **the reason a slip was read back twice.** The person who asked stated the reason, and stated that he does not know whether he is being careful or frightened, **and that nobody in that room knew anything about a wall four hundred miles off and that every word of that is true.**

---

And then who loses, and it is the steward's, and she asked it in the yard at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fourteenth, in front of about twenty people, and the whole of it is one sentence and she does not recommend anything.

"**Who loses is this body, and what it loses is not the records. Those are copies and they were copies before anybody came up the road with a form case.**"

She stood at the wall side of the gate with her hands behind her back.

"What this body has done today is tell an office, in writing, on a form, that it holds something it will not name. **And the nothing is now a line in a register in nine miles with an hour on it, and that is not going to be taken back, and there is no version of the next four years in which it comes out of that register.** The reader read it twice and asked for it a third time, and I want it said in this yard that a man can read a thing three times and it will not come out of the book."

"And we have paid for it in the compost line, at four shillings the hundredweight, and the whole of this month's line is not written until the first of next month. **We have not paid for it in anything else and I do not know what we have paid for it in and I am not going to find out this month, and I would rather say that in a yard of twenty people than have it found out in a year by somebody else.**"

---

And then, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the fourteenth, a man came off the change of horses with a sheet in a satchel, four days after he went up, and he was paid four shillings on the tenth when he was hired and the whole of his hire is thirty-two shillings, **and the remaining twenty-eight of them are in the ledger and are not discharged, and the line they fall in is the line of this month, and the line of this month is not written until the first of next month and is outside this book.**

And the sheet was four lines, in the occupier's hand, and it went in with the hour and the man of about fifty read it and asked for his initial to be put against nothing.

> From the fourth house, on the fourteenth of this month, down the four-day road, four lines, in the occupier's hand, on the back of a sheet that has thirty-one years of the same practice on it. **Read out in a yard at Lantern Reach at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the fourteenth, by the reader of this body, with the clerk present.**
>
> **One. The rows are let go. No feeding, no pruning, no selling. A hundred and forty-one trees of bearing left standing.**
>
> **Two. We worked them a long time. Eleven years and four harvests and a buyer for all four.**
>
> **Three. We were told a record of this could be read. We have not been able to find out by what.**
>
> **Four. You did not ask us before we did it and we would have liked you to and we are not going to make a thing of that, and you are four hundred miles off and you could not have come, and we know that, and it is written down here so that you cannot say afterward that nobody told you we were angry.**

And the fourth line was read twice, and nobody in that yard said one word about it, and the fen entered that nobody said one word, and entered that the fourth line is in the book in the households' own words and not in a clerk's, and that the households put it there before this body knew the answer and not after, and that the fen did not ask for it and did not send for it and did not pay for the second half of that conversation and has not been given it.

---

And the fifteenth of this month was a Sunday, and almost nothing happened on it, and the fen put the Sunday in the book because it is the standing of this holding not to leave a day out of a book on the ground that nothing occurred on it.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the fifteenth of this month, by the clerk, on the standing of a day with nothing in it: **a Sunday, and nothing done.** The four lines from the shelf were copied into this body's own book in the steward's hand and read out once in the seed house with the bay doors open and about nine people in it. **Nobody said one word. The reader asked for his initial to be put against nothing. The man of about seventy was not fetched and was not sent for and the fen entered that it did not send for him on a Sunday for a document and that it will not.**

> The four lines are in this body's book in a second hand, a woman's hand from four hundred miles off, **and the fen entered that this is the second time on purpose that a hand which is not the steward's has gone into this book, and that in one of them the reason was a check and in this one the reason is that the words are hers, and that words of hers in a clerk's hand would be a different thing.** The reader read them back after the clerk and nobody said anything about the fourth one, and the fen entered that nobody did and that it has been read out twice in two days and not answered once.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the fourteenth of this month, by the clerk: **four houses have given up a year of fruit on eleven acres of their own ground, in daylight, in a yard, on the tenth of this month, and this body did not know until this afternoon and did not ask and was not told and did not send for it.** The fourth line of their sheet is in this body's book in their own words. **This body has not answered the fourth line and has not decided whether it is going to and has entered that it has not decided, and the reader read that last sentence back.**

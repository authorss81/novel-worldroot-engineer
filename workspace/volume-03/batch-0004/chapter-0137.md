# Chapter 137

## Unfit

The door was read out whole at the tenth hour of the sixteenth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and it is the door and the schedule and the notice and the four sheets and the refusal clause and the six clauses, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-seven, and the compost line for this month was not entered and the reason was entered.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the sixteenth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month three months back, the fifth read twice.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-seven.**
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.** That is the eighth month of a run of seven that has not been discharged in seven. **It is a figure nobody in this yard can say and this body will not enter a figure it does not know, and the first of next month is a Tuesday and this holding will stand in a yard on a Tuesday and say out loud that it has nothing.**

And the reader read the whole of it back and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and the clerk entered the hour, and about twenty-five people were in that yard, and a clerk from the counter at Fennmere stood at the back of it with a bundle of returns under his arm, and nobody had announced him and nobody asked him anything, and he counted the column off the board with his finger and said twenty-seven, and the reader of this body heard him say it and entered that he heard it and entered that nobody arranged it and that this is the second time a figure on that door has been checked by somebody outside this holding.

---

And the sheet came on the ordinary post on the same morning, from the sub-office at Fennmere, and it is a declaration and it is correct and it is four lines long and it names nothing.

> Records of an experimental character, made in a working district, not confirmed by a second reading at the time of making, **are held to be unfit for evidentiary use.** Two records of this holder are within the class. A record so held may be produced by a holder, and is not to be produced by this office, and no officer of this district may be cited as a holder of one. **Neither record is named on this sheet.**

And the man of about fifty read it out twice and then stopped in the middle of a yard with about twenty-five people in it and said the sentence that the rest of the day was made of.

"**Two.** It is two. And nobody in this fen knows which two, and that is not a figure of danger, that is a figure of a hole. **The class reaches four things this holding has and the sheet names none of them, and a body that cannot tell which two of its own records are gone is a body that has lost two things and does not know what it is looking for.**

"So somebody in this yard has to say which two they would hand over if they were asked, and I am going to say it, and I am going to say it in front of twenty-five people, and I would like the clerk to enter it before I do and I would like my initial to be put against it and not against nothing for once."

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the sixteenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, in advance, at the request of the reader of this body: **two records of this holding are, in the opinion of the reader of this body, the two most likely to be within the class declared this morning.** One, a reading taken by a licensed reader on a hired trestle in a stone barn on another district's ground, on the seventeenth of a month four months back, and which was produced at an office at the foot of the plateau on the fourteenth of this month and stated as part two. Two, a copy of a page of a book in a locked room four hundred miles off. **The reader stated that he is not a clerk of that office and cannot know, and that the two are his opinion and not a finding, and that it is entered because a body that has to guess should be found guessing on the page and not in a fortnight in a room in nine miles.**

And then Lissa Vale said one thing about the sheet and she said it standing up and she did not recommend anything, and it is the only thing anybody said about the paper itself.

"**I have been reading that sentence since the seventh hour and I have got to the end of it and there is nothing wrong with it.** Four lines, a class, a procedure, and a statement that the word is not defined. **An office has written four lines that can take two things out of this holding's hands and has told us in the same four lines that it will not do it by argument.** I have voted against this body three times and I have never once been able to say that an office in nine miles was doing a thing to us that it ought not to do, and I cannot say it this morning either, and I am not going to try, and I would like the clerk to enter that the steward read a sheet that takes two of her body's records out of its hands and found nothing to say about it and has entered that and will not be thanked for it."

And then the clerk entered an observation of his own, which is his standing, unasked, and the man of about fifty read it back and did not ask him what it meant, because he has never once asked.

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the sixteenth of this month, by the clerk, on his own motion, unasked: **the office is not a villain.** Not explained and not to be explained, and no person in this holding has been asked for an explanation of it and the clerk has not offered one, and the three previous times this fen has entered that sentence it has entered it the other way round.

And Coll Renner said the other half of it from the wall, and he said it flatly, and he had his own book shut under his arm.

"No," he said. "**It is not one of them. A class of record of an experimental character is held against the record and not against the holder, and a licensed man's copy is not a holder's record at all. I have said that in an office in a small square hand and it is in a register in nine miles, and I am saying it again in a yard because this morning somebody handed this body a sheet that can take a class of record away without a holder being cited, and the only copy that sheet cannot touch is the one in my coat.**"

And the man of about fifty said the sentence, and it is the sentence and it is in the book, and he did not enjoy it.

"**That is the one I was going to hand over.** I had the two written in my head an hour ago and one of them was your coat, and you have just made it the one I cannot, in front of the same twenty-five people, and you did not do it to make a point and I am not going to treat it as though you had."

"Then I will say the useful half," said Coll Renner. "**You have two names on a page and one of them is a piece of a machine in a book four hundred miles off and the other one is a reading that this body produced four days ago with a part stated against it in front of an officer. Neither of those is the worst of the two you could have said, and both of them are the kind of thing a person in four years can look at and say yes.** And I would like the clerk to enter that I said that and that nobody thanked me for it, because it is my standing and I would like it on the page on the day and not remembered."

---

And then the well, on the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month, four hundred miles off, in daylight, in the yard of the fourth house, by the occupier, on her own rule, by her own hand and by her own nail, and she said it out loud before she wrote it down, and the fen did not have the figure for four days and did not guess at it.

> Well, Cauldron Reach. Read at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month by the occupier, by her own hand and by her own nail, and said out loud in her own yard before it was written down, which is not her rule and is this fen's rule and she has taken it off this fen without being asked: **five feet and two inches.**
>
> The same well at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month: five feet and three inches. The same well at the fourth hour of the thirtieth of a month three months back: five feet and two inches. The same well at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month four months back: nine feet.
>
> **The shortfall is three feet and ten inches.** Three and a half feet of that is the draw of the thirteenth of last month and is entered against a man. **Four inches of that is the ground's and is entered against nobody.** On the sixteenth of last month the ground's share was half an inch. **The half inch was never the engineer's and the four inches are not the engineer's either, and a body cannot move a figure about a stranger's ground by wanting it.**
>
> **There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and this body will not be predicting one.**

And a man was hired at the change of horses on the sixteenth of this month at four shillings a day, to go up the four days and back down them for that sheet, and four shillings went into the ledger that day against the line of this month, and the whole of the hire is thirty-two shillings, and **the remaining twenty-eight are not paid and are not entered as paid, because he has not come back.**

And with him went a sheet with the count on it, which is the count the fen cannot check, because four houses have been on a day letter since the sixth of last month and they looked at it on the first of this month in daylight at the well, and the figures on it are counted in four pails and **a pail is not a measure and this holding cannot reduce that sheet to anything it has ever put in a book and is not going to.**

---

And then the man of about seventy, on the afternoon of the seventeenth of this month, and it was the ninth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was told the sheet and the two names and the coat and the count and the well, in that order, and he asked for the two names to be repeated and then he sat against the wall with the blanket over his knees for about a minute.

"Four," he said. "**That is four in three weeks and every one of them was correct, and I have been the fourth person in this yard in eleven years to say that a thing nobody wanted in a book has to go in a book anyway, and I have not been right about as much as I have been right about, and this is one of the times.**

"And I want somebody to notice what has happened, and nobody is going to notice it, so I am going to say it out loud and then nobody is going to answer me, and both of those are in the book.

"**Every one of these four has come from the other side of that gate.** A part three condition from a schedule. A shape from a form that has no part for shapes. A sealed record from an office nineteen years back. And this morning, a class, from a sheet, with two records in it and neither of them named. **Not one of the four came from us. A wall we built ourselves we can take down. Those four we cannot take down at all and we cannot see the top of and we are not going to be told by anybody.**

"And I would like it entered that I said that in a yard on the sixteenth of a month, and that nobody answered me, **because I am seventy-one and I have learned what that means.**"

And the reader of this body read it back, and the clerk entered the hour, and the fen entered that nobody answered it, and the man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the seventeenth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk: **the question of the four walls was available to anybody in that yard and was taken by one person, who is seventy-one, and it was not answered by anybody.** It is entered because a body that enters the count of its own silences has entered five of them in a month and the count is entered for the sixth time, **and because the person who took it said beforehand that nobody was going to answer it and said so out loud and was not thanked for saying it and the fen entered that he was not thanked.**

Underneath the day's work, dated the seventeenth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-eighth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A door read whole at the tenth hour in a full yard of about twenty-five, a clerk from the counter at Fennmere at the back of it with a bundle of returns who counted the column off the board with his finger and said twenty-seven and was not announced and was not asked anything, the seventh column at twenty-seven, and the compost line for this month not entered with the figure not in the book and the first of next month a Tuesday. A sheet from a sub-office, four lines, correct, declaring two records of this holder within a class of experimental character and not named. The reader of this body entering two names in advance, in a yard, as his opinion and not a finding, and asking for his initial against it. A licensed man saying that a class of record held against the record does not reach a person's coat, and the reader of this body saying that the coat was the one he was going to hand over and that the licensed man had just made it the one he could not. A well four hundred miles off read at the fourth hour by its occupier on her own nail and said out loud before it was written down: five feet and two inches, the shortfall three feet and ten inches, three and a half against a man and four inches against nobody, the ground's share half an inch a month ago, and no entry anywhere for that well coming back. A man hired at the change of horses at four shillings a day, four entered, the whole of the hire thirty-two and the rest not paid and not entered as paid. A day letter looked at on the first of this month in four pails and a pail not being a measure. And a man of seventy-one, ninth in a season, saying that all four walls have come from the other side of the gate and that nobody answered him and that he is seventy-one and has learned what that means.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

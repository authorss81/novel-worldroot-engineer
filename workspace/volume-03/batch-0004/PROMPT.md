### Chapter 132 — *The Category* — days 216–217, the sixth and the seventh

**Goal:** the name of the thing this volume is about, written on an office's own sheet in the ordinary course, and the fen's honest count of the four walls it has built.

**Must happen.** **The class is named.** It comes on a sheet with a heading and a definition and it is four lines long and it is correct, and **the definition reaches a reading made by a licensed man on a hired trestle in a stone barn on another district's ground, and a maintenance order copied out by a woman of about fifty-four in her own bad ledger, and a copy of a page inside a book that is in a locked room four hundred miles off.** The office has not seen any of those things and does not need to have; the class is a class and it is applied in the ordinary course by an officer with no power to do anything else.

> **Records of an experimental character, made in a working district, not confirmed by a second reading at the time of making, are held to be unfit for evidentiary use.** A record so held may be produced by a holder, and is not to be produced by this office, and no officer of this district may be cited as a holder of one.
>
> **The word *experimental* is not defined on this sheet and the office has stated that it does not define it, and that a holder who considers a record experimental may say so, and that a statement so made is itself a classification of the record and is entered against the record.**

**And that last line is the whole chapter.** The man of about fifty: **so the way to keep a record out of that class is to declare it experimental, and the way to declare it experimental is to make a statement that is entered against it, and the way to make no statement is to have it put in by somebody else.** We have built a door and the door is a trapdoor and it was built by an office that is right, and the rightness is not going to be argued with by us in a yard.

**And the fen's count, and it is now four.** The part three condition. The shape the schedule has no part for. The sealed record nobody can produce. And this. **Four walls in three weeks, every one of them correct, and the fen has entered the count and has entered that it is not proud of the count and that the count is the only thing it can do with them.**

**And Coll Renner's problem, and he brings it himself and he does not want to be asked for it.** A licensed man's copy is his and a class of record that is experimental does not reach a person's coat; **so the one copy no office can reach is the one a man can lose, and he has had the coat on the fen road for a season and he has thought about it for three weeks and he has made no decision and he is going to say that out loud in a yard and he would like the clerk to enter that he said it and to enter that nobody thanked him for saying it.**

Resistance: the steward asks who loses again, and the answer is a licensed man and a child of his and about nine months of the work of the most careful record in this district, and **she writes that down in her own hand and does not recommend anything.**

# Chapter 138

## What Came Up Through Them

On the eighteenth of this month, on a shelf four hundred miles off, in the yard of the first house and along the first row and in four other yards, there came up out of the collars of the trees that were let go eight days before a green of a kind nobody on that shelf has seen.

It is in the plainest words anybody has used about it and it is how the four of them told it and not how this fen would have told it.

It is coming up out of the collars, which is to say out of the ground at the foot of a tree, and it is not a shoot off the trunk and it is not moss and it is not anything one of the four has planted. It is in the first row first. **The first row is the row of eleven faces.** It is not in the next row, and the next row is the older row, and the older row is the one that was not read with a rule and a lens and has two faces in it that nobody has ever looked at. It is not in the grass. It is not at the wall. It is along the row and it is in the collars and that is the whole of where it is.

Nobody on that shelf knows what it is. Three of the four have said out loud, in the same words and not because anybody asked them, that they have never seen the like of it, and the fourth did not say it and said nothing at all, and one of the four is sixty-one and one of them is a man with a spade who has used it on that ground for twenty-six years and he put it down in a yard on the eighteenth and picked it up again and did not say the three words with the rest of them.

And nobody in that yard said what it meant, and nobody in that yard was asked what it meant, and **the fen did not send anybody to ask, and the fen did not write, and the fen has entered that the not-asking was correct and has entered that the fen did not know it had a choice until the sheet came down, and that a body four hundred miles off which found out four days afterwards that something had happened on a shelf it has no standing over is not a body that kept out of it. It is a body that was not there.**

---

And the man who was hired at the change of horses on the sixteenth of this month, four shillings a day and four shillings into the ledger that day and the whole of the hire thirty-two shillings of which **the remaining twenty-eight are in the ledger and are not discharged, and the line they fall in is the first of next month, which is day 241 and is outside this book**, came down the four-day road and was in this fen at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth, which is the last hour this holding has for this month.

He had two sheets. One is in the occupier's hand and has been in this body's book since the fourteenth in the same woman's words, and the other is a page in a licensed man's own hand, and Coll Renner had written it on the seventeenth of this month and put it into the satchel with his own hands before the man went, **because a licensed man's standing on what can and cannot be said about a row of trees ought to be in the same satchel as the row and not in a house four hundred miles off, and he said so in a yard and the clerk entered it and nobody thanked him for it.**

And the sheet is four lines and a figure, and there is no handwriting on it of a man of seventy-one or of any other man in this fen, and the reader of this body read it out in a yard with about twenty people in it and the clerk entered the hour.

> From the fourth house, on the twentieth of this month, down the four-day road, four lines and a figure, in the occupier's hand. **Read out in the yard at Lantern Reach at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth, by the reader of this body, with the clerk present and about twenty people in the yard.**
>
> **On the eighteenth, in the yard of the first house and along the first row and in four other yards, there came up out of the collars of the trees we let go a green none of us has seen.**
>
> It is in the first row first and the first row is the row of eleven faces. It is not in the next row and the next row is the older row and nobody has read it.
>
> **Figure: eight days, from the tenth to the eighteenth.**
>
> That is the whole of it and we are not saying what it is. Three of us have said out loud that we have never seen the like of it and one of us did not say it and has not been asked twice.

---

And then the work, and the work is two things and neither of them is a reading.

Marek Vale read it and then he said what he can defend and what he cannot, in that order, standing in a yard with about twenty people in it, and he did not do it in the other order and the clerk entered that he did not.

"**What I can defend.** A row of a hundred and forty-one trees was not worked for eight days and something came up out of the collars along one row and did not come up along the next one, and that is a fact about eight days and about a row and it is not a fact about a cause. The first row is the row I have read. The next row is the row nobody has read. I am not going to go up that road and look at it, and I have no standing to ask anybody to, and I would not know what I was looking at if I did."

"**And what I cannot defend.** I cannot tell, from a page, with four lines and a figure on it, whether what came up through that row is a thing somebody told the row to do or a thing the row did because nobody did anything to it."

"And I have said that sentence before. **I said it in a barn four hundred miles off about eleven cuts, in a month now three months back, to about nine people, and nobody in that barn answered it, and it is the same sentence and I have not improved on it in three months and I am not going to try to this evening.** The clerk is to enter that I said it twice. Twice is the count and the count is the point."

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth of this month, in the yard, by the clerk, at the request of the engineer of record: **the sentence was said twice, three months apart, in two rooms, to about nine people in the first and about twenty in the second, and neither time was it answered.** On the fifteenth of last month he gave this body four entries in a yard and did not accept them and did not refuse them. **He did not say that sentence then and has not been asked to.**

---

And then the question, and it is the volume's question, and it was asked out loud in a yard for the first time by somebody who is not of this fen, and nobody answered it, and it is in the book whole.

"**Whose order is it.**"

Coll Renner said it standing at the wall side of the gate with his own book shut under his arm, in the plainest words he has, and about twenty people were in that yard and every one of them heard him and not one of them answered.

And then he said the other three sentences before anybody could answer, which is his practice.

"**I am not asking her. I am not going to ask her, and I do not want it read back as though I had, and if the clerk has entered that I asked it he is to enter that I withdrew it in the same breath, and both of those are true and one of them is a fact about a person.** A licensed man in this fen has been licensed twenty-two years and has four facts and no finding in a book about a piece of metal and he said then that he could not tell anybody who held the blade, and he is saying now that he cannot tell anybody whose order it is, and **the distance between those two sentences is this volume and nobody in this yard is going to close it and I am not going to try.**"

"And I am going to say the half that is mine. **The question was available to twenty people in this yard tonight and it was taken by one of them, and the one it was taken by is a licensed man who has a book and no interest, and that is the best available answer to who took it and it is not an answer to what it was.**"

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twentieth of this month, by the clerk, on the standing of a question: **whose order is it.** Asked in a yard with about twenty people in it, by a licensed man, standing up, in the plainest words. **The question was available to anybody in that yard and was taken by one person and was not answered by anybody.** It is the second time this question has been taken by one person in this body and **neither of the two has been answered, and the count is entered and the count is two.**
>
> And entered on the same line: **the man at the second house is not in that yard and is not available to anybody in it. This body did not look for him. Looking would have meant deciding something, and the fen has entered that it did not decide it and has entered the reason, which is that a body which sets out to find a man has to be ready for the answer and this body is not, and has not said so out loud, and is saying so now.**

---

And then the figures of the month, read out in the ordinary way in the same yard, none of them softened, and the reader of this body read them and the clerk wrote them and neither said one word that was not on the list.

> **The compost line for this month.** Not entered. The figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Tuesday, and this holding does not know what it is. The eighth month of a run of seven that has not been discharged in seven.
>
> **The leak.** Five-eighths of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of this month, twice, two rules, the second of them the one that reads smaller on that corner, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up. Read in a yard of about twenty. **It has not been measured since. It has no due date and none is to be invented, and nobody has looked for a cause and none is entered, and this body will not say whether the figure of the thirteenth is the figure of the first of this month or is not.**
>
> **The beat.** The fifteenth, at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of last month, nine in the quarter hour with one run of three, at the rail. Cause not established. Licensed person present, none. Nothing written underneath it. **It has not been logged since and the column is open, and this fen has not opened it and has entered that it has not opened it now with about twenty people standing in a yard.**
>
> **The boards.** **One hundred and sixty-four days**, cut into both of them this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years, said out loud in a yard, day minus sixty-six, and the number is still going up and nobody can make it go down.
>
> **The March's tin.** **Two hundred and eleven days**, day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, with both undertakings standing: the seed house's that it will not be moved, and the trust's that it will not be asked. **Neither has been touched in this month, neither was asked anything this month, and the drawer it is in was not opened this month and is not to be.**
>
> **The standing rule about the engineer of record.** In force. Satisfied on the third of last month, in a room, on a day, with a licensed man writing it down. Two accounts of the same arm in this book that do not agree and were not reconciled. **It is four lines and every one of them is about a stop, and it was used on the twelfth of this month, and the fen entered on the twelfth that it was used and has entered it again tonight.**
>
> **And a draw is on a table. It is not forbidden. The fen has entered that it is not forbidden and that it is not going to decide it this month either, and that a man who has done one of them in a fortnight is not a man anybody is going to be glad about on the twentieth of a month.**
>
> **The well on the shelf.** Five feet and two inches at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of this month, by the occupier, by her own hand and by her own nail, in her own yard. Three and a half feet of the shortfall against a man and four inches against nobody. **There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back.**
>
> **And a hundred and forty-one trees on the same shelf are coming up out of the ground along one row and not along the next one, and it is eight days old, and this body has no standing over that ground, no claim on those trees and no person in it. There is no entry anywhere for that row either, and this body will not be predicting one.**

And nobody thanked anybody, and the fen entered that a woman of fifty-four's orchard is coming up out of the ground four hundred miles off, that there is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and no entry anywhere for that row, and that this body is not going to predict one, **and that predicting one would be the first time in eleven years that this holding has entered a thing it has not got, on the strength of a figure, on the last day of a month, in a yard, to about twenty people who had all been told about it in the spring.**

Underneath the day's work, dated the twentieth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirty-ninth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Eight days of a row left unworked, and a green out of the collars along one row and not the next, told in the plainest words four households have and not in this body's words, with three of them saying out loud that they have never seen the like of it and one of them not saying it. A man hired on the sixteenth, four shillings in that day, the whole of the hire thirty-two and the remaining twenty-eight not discharged, and the line they fall in on the first of next month, which is outside this book. A sheet of four lines and a figure, read out in a yard of about twenty, and a page in a licensed man's own hand in the same satchel because a licensed man's standing on a row ought to travel with the row. An engineer of record who said what he can defend before what he cannot, and what he cannot is a sentence he said three months ago in a barn about eleven cuts, and the count of the times he has said it is entered and is two. A licensed man who asked whose order it is in a yard full of people, in the plainest words, and then said three sentences before anybody could answer, of which the first is that he is not asking her and does not want it read back as though he had, and the second is that a piece of metal in a locked room is four hundred miles off and cannot tell anybody who held the blade. The question available to twenty people and taken by one and answered by nobody, and the count of that now two. The man at the second house not in that yard and not looked for, and looking entered as a decision this body did not take. And the figures of the month in the ordinary way, none of them softened: no compost line, a leak with no due date and no cause and nothing said about whether it has moved, a beat logged once and not since with the column open, boards at one hundred and sixty-four, the tin at two hundred and eleven days unmoved with both undertakings standing, a rule of four lines that has now been used once, a draw on a table that is not forbidden and will not be decided this month, a well three and a half feet down against a man and four inches against nobody, and a hundred and forty-one trees four hundred miles off with no entry anywhere for the row either.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

### Chapter 139 — *The Twenty-First* — days 231–232, the twenty-first and the twenty-second, a Saturday and a Sunday

**Goal:** a month opens with a count this holding can check from outside, and a man tells a yard what he is going to do about the two records an office has taken, which is nothing, and says why, and the fen is right and it does not help.

**Must happen.** **The door read out whole at the tenth hour of the twenty-first in a full yard, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and the count of the readings of the seventh column goes to twenty-eight. The compost line for this month is not entered and the reason is entered.** Nobody from the fen asks anybody anything and the door is read in the ordinary way.

**And the answer, and it is nothing, and the man of about fifty says it in a yard and does not recommend it and is not thanked for it.** A declaration has said that two records of this body are within a class and has named neither. **The reader of this body says what he is going to do about it, which is: nothing, for four days, for four reasons, and the fourth is that anything he does is a thing an office can read as a thing he did because he was told to.** He says the cost out loud too, and it is the whole of the chapter: **a body that cannot tell which two of its own records are gone is a body that has lost two things and does not know what it is looking for, and the only honest thing to do about that is to be wrong in the open and to be found out having been wrong in the open.** He does not propose the copies. He does not say *we should send more copies*, because the copies went on the fourth of last month and he knows what they cost and the fen knows what they cost and the whole of the movement has been about that cost.

**And from outside, four hundred miles off, on the twenty-first: the green in the first row is five days old and nobody has cut it and nobody has dug about it and three of the four households have walked out and looked at it and come back.** Narrate it in the plainest words. **No person reads it, no person names it, and one of the four says out loud that he has stopped saying what he thinks it is and a second of them says that is the same thing said twice.**

Resistance: the reader of this body has a standing that is to ask the question nobody wanted answered, and **the question this month is not available in a form anybody can ask.** A class that names nothing cannot be argued with, because there is nothing on the page to argue with, and that is not a trap this body set and it is not a trap the office set either. **A body that publishes everything has run out of things to publish about its own trouble, and that is a first.**

### Chapter 141 — *A Working Record* — days 235–236, the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth

**Goal:** the orchard is a working record and not a monument, and the households say so, and the fen is not on the shelf and cannot be told to agree.

### Chapter 142 — *Unsafe* — days 237–238, the twenty-seventh and the twenty-eighth

**Goal:** the declaration, and it is the shortest document in the volume and the worst thing in the book, and nobody obstructs it and nobody is a villain.

**Must happen.** **On the twenty-seventh, from the sub-office at Fennmere, on the ordinary post, a sheet. It is four lines and it is a declaration and it is correct.**

> **Records of this holder are of an experimental character and are declared unsafe for evidentiary use.** A record so declared is not to be relied upon by this office, is not to be produced by an officer of this district, and is not to be entered in any proceeding in which this office has a part. **The declaration is made in the ordinary course and no part of it is a finding against the holder. Nothing in this line states that anything the holder has said is untrue.** The holder is notified. The holder may produce any record declared unsafe and may not be prevented from doing so.

**And the thing that makes it the worst document in the book is in the second line and is read out twice:** **nothing in this line states that anything the holder has said is untrue.** A body that has spent a season telling the truth in a yard has just been told by an office, in writing, on its own letterhead, in the ordinary course, that the truth of what it says is not in question. **And the man of about fifty has to stand up in a yard and say what that does to a body whose only defence has been that it publishes everything, and the answer is that the declaration is the reason a body cannot use its own record, and it is also the reason nobody has to answer it, and a body that is relieved of having to answer anything is not the same body as one that was never asked.**

**And the officer who sent it is not a villain and says so in the room if anybody puts it to him, which nobody does.** Nothing obstructs. **Nobody in this batch may have an officer lie, obstruct, delay a document, or be anybody but correct.**

Resistance: the steward's question. **Who loses.** And the answer is that this body loses its record and the sixteen households lose nothing today and the four houses on a shelf lose nothing today either, **and that the man of about seventy is the one who has to be asked, because he has been the fourth person in this yard in eleven years to say that a thing nobody wanted in a book has to go in a book anyway, and a book that cannot be used is not a book anybody can put a thing in.**

Change: the declaration is made, it is correct, it is four lines, and the fen's best quality — publishing everything — has been made worthless in writing by an office that has not lied once.

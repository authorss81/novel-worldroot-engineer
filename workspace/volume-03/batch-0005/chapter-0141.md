# Chapter 141

## A Working Record

On the twenty-fifth of this month, in daylight, four hundred miles off, four households did four ordinary working things to eleven acres that are not being sold and not being fed for fruit, and this fen was not there, was not asked, did not know, and has still not been told.

The first thing took most of the morning. A row was lifted, which is a matter of a spade and an hour, and the eleven faces of it were read again with a rule and a lens by two men and a woman, and **nine of the eleven were read and two of the eleven were not read and were not guessed at and were marked as not read on a stake, so that anybody coming up that row in a year can see which two were not looked at and does not have to take anybody's word for it.** That is what a rule and a lens are for and it is a working thing and not a service to anybody.

Reading a face is slow and it is not a mystery. You put the rule on the collar where a branch was dressed and you read the width of the roll that has come back over it, and you read it against the width of the roll below it, and the roll tells you about a year and a season and about whether the ground under that tree was carrying when the wound closed. Nine of the eleven were read on the twenty-fifth and the figures were written on a board in the occupier's hand and the board is a working board and has a nail in it and is not a record and is not going anywhere. **The tenth face had a roll that could be read two ways and the eleventh had a roll that could not be read at all, and neither of them was guessed at, and a stake went into the ground at each with the number of the face cut into it and nothing else cut into it.** Nobody on that shelf has ever pretended to have read eleven, and the two that were not read are still not read this season and will not be read this season and the fen has entered that it cannot go four hundred miles to read two faces and would not know more than the four of them by the time it got there.

The second was the grass, cut, because the grass under the rows is what holds the slope and it is cut twice a year and this is the half of the year it is cut in.

The third was a wall, on the west end, where a stone had come out of the face in the winter and had not been put back since. It took one man most of an afternoon and it is a wall and it is a wall of that field and it has nothing to do with anything that happened in this volume.

And the fourth was the green, and the fourth is the chapter, and it was done in one afternoon, and it was done by the man with the spade, and it was done with a sickle, and the green was cut back off the collars of the trees along the first row and **left on the ground.**

It took from about the second hour of the afternoon until the light went. The green was not in a mat and it did not come away in one piece. It came off in pieces, at the collar and below the collar and in some places above it, and it took about nine strokes for a big tree and rather more than that for a small one, and the man with the spade did the big ones and the two men did the small ones and the woman went along the line with a basket and put the cuttings in and tipped them out on the grass at the end of the row, and the grass is what holds the slope and the cuttings went onto the grass and stayed there.

Nobody dug. That is the part the fen would have wanted to write about and cannot, because it did not happen: **nobody put a spade in beside the green and nobody turned a clod over and nobody took a root up to look at it.** One of the four wanted to and did not, and said so afterwards in his own yard, in about nine words, and the words were that if you want to know what a thing is in the ground then you have to take it out of the ground, and taking it out of the ground on a shelf in the eighth month of a year with no water in the low quarter of a fen four hundred miles off is a thing you do with two men and a day and not with a sickle in an afternoon.

Nobody read it either. **Nobody weighed it, nobody counted it, nobody took a piece of it into a room, and nothing came off that shelf in a vessel.** A green came out of the collars on the sixteenth of this month and a green was cut back off the collars on the twenty-fifth and the whole of what is known about the two events is in four hundred yards of a yard on a shelf and in the four words of a woman in the same yard on the twenty-first, and this fen has neither the yard nor the four words and has not asked for them.

> Written on the back of a stake at the eleventh tree, in the occupier's hand, on the twenty-fifth of this month, and not sent to anybody: **the green along the first row was cut off the collars with a sickle on the twenty-fifth and the cuttings were left on the ground. Four houses. Nine faces read, two not read and not guessed at. The row was lifted, the grass was cut, the stone went back into the west wall.**
>
> **This sheet was not sent down the road and this holding was not asked for it and does not have it.**

And the reason was given out loud, in a yard, before the sickle came out, and it is about six sentences and it is not a virtue, and it is the reason and not a story about a reason.

"The ground is ours," said Merrid Ollvar. "**The green is not. A thing that comes up out of the collar of a tree is not part of the tree the way fruit is, and the man who cut the eleven branches understood that and I have understood it since I saw the row. If we leave it up it will be a thing we have to decide about every year, and deciding about it every year is a worse thing than deciding about it once. That is the whole of the reason and I am not going to dress it up as anything else.**"

And then she said the other half, which is the part that is in the book, and she said it slower.

"**And we are not going to pretend we know what it is, and we are not going to leave it up because it is interesting.** Those are two different reasons and I am giving you both. The first one is about a field. The second one is about the four of us, and the second one is the reason we would have got it wrong inside a month, because a thing you have decided not to look at is a thing you start making up, and there are four houses on this shelf and three of us have got the same imagination."

Nobody in that yard said anything for a while, and a woman of the four said the last of it, and it was about a year and not about anything else.

"**In a year somebody is going to have to decide what to call it.** It came up in an afternoon and it will either be a thing you can point at in a year or a thing you cannot, and I am not going to be the one who says which, and cutting it off the collars does not settle that and does not pretend to settle that, and we worked out on the twenty-first that we can cut it and still be here in a year looking at where it was."

---

And on the twenty-sixth of this month, at about the second hour of the afternoon, in the seed house at Lantern Reach, with the bay doors open and about nine people in the room, the fen entered a thing with four blanks in it, and entered it before it knew what was in the blanks, and said out loud that it was doing that and why.

"**I am going to write it with the blanks in and I am going to leave the blanks in while I say it, and I would like the reader to read it back with the blanks in and not fill them.** It is the third time this body has entered that shape of sentence and the first two were on the fourteenth and the twentieth of this month, four days each, and this one is being entered on the twenty-sixth for something that happened on the twenty-fifth, and if I entered it on the twenty-ninth it would still be four days and it would be four days of a body that knew."

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, read back by the reader of this body with the blanks in and not filled, and initialed against nothing:
>
> **Four households have been told a record of them can be read and have decided what they want read, and on the twenty-fifth of this month, four hundred miles off, they ____________________**
>
> **This body does not know.** The sheet that will tell it was written on the morning of the twenty-fifth at the fourth house, on the day, and cannot be in this yard before the twenty-ninth, and the four days are the same four days as on the fourteenth and the twentieth and the count of the three is entered. **The blanks are to be filled on the twenty-ninth from the sheet and not from anybody's memory of a conversation, and nothing is to be written in them that is not on that sheet.**
>
> **A body that fills blanks in afterwards can be suspected of having fitted them. This body has entered that it would rather have four blanks on a page for three days than four sentences that a person in four years cannot tell were written on the same side of the event as the event.**

And then the man of about fifty asked the question, and it is the only question in that room that nobody else could have asked, and he asked it standing, and he gave the reason for asking it first, which is his practice.

"**Did anybody tell them to cut it.**"

"I am going to say why I am asking it in a yard in front of nine people and it is not to catch anybody. It is that in four years there is going to be a page in this book with a thing cut off a row of trees on it, and the first question anybody will ask about that page is who said so, and **the honest answer to that question is going to depend entirely on whether this body said so on the twenty-fifth, and the only person who can settle it is this body, and this body is the only one in this room that has anything to do with it.**"

"And the answer is **no.** This body sent one sheet up the plateau road on the ninth of this month and it carried one question and no advice, and this body has not written to that household since and has not sent a man and has not asked them a second question about anything at all. **And that is all I can tell you, and I would like it read back that that is all I can tell you, because I cannot tell you what four houses did yesterday and I am not going to be asked to.**"

Nobody thanked him and he said, before anybody had, that he was not going to be thanked for it, and that he had said it in a yard on purpose and that this was the second time in a season he had asked a question in a yard in order to hear a no.

And then somebody asked the obvious thing in that room, which is what a room is, and it was Jonas Byre and he asked it flatly.

"Could we send a man up tonight."

"No," said Lissa Vale.

"Right," he said, and he stopped, and about a second afterwards he said the other half of it himself, which he has done twice this season and which is the reason he does the counting out loud. "**A man up the plateau road is four days and the hired rate is four shillings the day and a whole hire is thirty-two, and we have got a line of compost to write on Tuesday that nobody in this room can say, and I would be spending thirty-two shillings to arrive on the thirty-first in a yard with four people in it and learn what four people decided on the twenty-fifth.** And I would not have asked the question if I had not said the other half out loud first, and I have said it out loud first twice this season and I am not going to be thanked for it either because I am the man who counts out loud and that is the whole of my standing and it is not a virtue."

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **a man was not hired on the twenty-sixth of this month, and thirty-two shillings were not spent on a four-day road, and this body will learn what four households did on the twenty-fifth from a sheet on the twenty-ninth and from nothing else.** The person who asked was answered in one word and said the other half of his own question out loud before anybody answered it, **and entered that he has now done that twice this season and is not going to be thanked for it and does not think it is a virtue, and the fen has entered that it agrees and has entered why.**

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body, on the standing of a question asked and answered:
>
> **Did anybody tell them to cut it. No.** The one sheet this body has sent to that household in this season carried one question and no advice and went on the ninth of this month, and this body has not written to that household since, has not sent a man, and has not asked them a second question about anything.
>
> **And entered on the same line: the answer is a fact about this body's own outgoing book and about nothing else, and this body cannot say what four households did on the twenty-fifth of this month and has not been told and did not ask. The person who asked said that he asked the question in a yard in front of about nine people in order to hear a no, and said before he was thanked that he was not going to be thanked for it, and he was not, and the fen has entered that he was not.**

And nothing in those two days moved, and the class of record is a class of record, and two of this holding's records are in it and neither is named, and the door is not read again until the thirtieth because this body reads a door on a day it has appointed and had appointed none, and on the twenty-sixth of this month at the second hour of the afternoon a seed house in a fen had four blanks in a book and about nine people in it and a man of seventy-one at a window two hundred yards off who was not fetched and was not sent for and does not speak in chapters and has not been asked, and the fen entered that it did not fetch him, for the fourth time in a fortnight, and that it is the same reason each time, which is that he is not to be produced in a room with a blank in it.

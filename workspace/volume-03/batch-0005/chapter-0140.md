# Chapter 140

## Nine Hundred Miles

Nine hundred miles from the fen road there is a counting house, and the counting house has a room with a long table in it, and on the twenty-third of this month, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, there were about nine people in the room and one question on the table.

The counting house is Cray. **Four sets of this body's records are in that building and have been for years, and a hand goes into it, and nobody at a bench in a fen nine hundred miles off has ever been told whose hand it is. There is a fifth set in that room that a person put there, and this body has not asked who, and the name is not in this book's, and it is not going to be, and a person who is not named is not a place and cannot be put on a return.**

And the question was not asked by a holder. It was asked by a party, and the difference is the whole of the chapter.

> Put on the twenty-third of this month, in a room at Cray with about nine people in it, by the steward of the Salt March Dryfoot Trust, of Cray, **and asked by a party and not by a holder**:
>
> **Is a record of an experimental character, made in a working district and not confirmed by a second reading at the time of making, produced to a party to the seed arrangements of a node, on the asking of that party.**
>
> The trust is a party to the seed arrangements at Lantern Reach. **This holding is not a party to anything.** The trust has asked the question in its own name and not in the name of any other person, and the trust has not asked anybody else to put it, and no holder was asked to put it and no holder has been told that it was going to be asked, and the trust entered both of those in the room and would not have the question stand without them.

Tova Reed is the steward of that trust and has been for eleven years, and she has been the steward of a seed house in a fen for the same eleven years, and she gave the figure before anybody asked for it, which is the thing she does, and the clerk of a fen nine hundred miles off did not have that to hand and entered it after the fact from a copy of a sheet.

And the reason it was asked by her and not by this body is a difference this fen entered on the second of this month in a yard, in two lines, and has not stopped thinking about since, and neither has the reader of this body and the man of about seventy and anybody else in that yard.

"**A party is named in somebody else's arrangement and a holder is named in its own.** A party can ask a question about a record in a room because the room is going to answer it to somebody. A holder can ask a question about a record in a room because the room has to answer it to the person the record is about. **Those are two different doors and they are in the same wall and this body has been standing at the wrong one for eleven years and did not know there were two until an office in nine miles wrote a line that said which one a record goes through.**"

"And the fen is not a party to anything. The fen is a holder of six things and two of them are in a class it cannot use, and **the fen has no standing in that room at Cray and it could not have asked that question if it had wanted to, and it did not want to, and it was not asked to, and it has entered all three of those and would like the third one read back.**"

---

And there is a rule in that house and it is a rule of the house and not of any office, and it is why the question went on the twenty-third and not earlier, and the trust's steward put it in the room herself in one sentence.

"**A question about a record made four hundred miles off this house is not put before it before the ninth day of a month.** That is the rule of the house and it has been the rule of the house for longer than anybody in that room has been at it, and it is not on any form and there is no paper with it on. **The twenty-third is after the ninth, so the question is in order, and I want the room to notice that I waited for it to be in order instead of putting it in on the twenty-second because I could have.**"

"And I am going to say the other half and I am not going to be thanked for it. **A holder of the record would have had to ask before the ninth, and could not have asked at all, and the second of those is not a matter of days.** So this is not a body arranging its own paper through a friendly office. **It is a party with a standing of its own putting a question it is entitled to put, and if the answer is no then the answer is no and it will be no on its own letterhead in the ordinary course and nobody in that room is going to be obstructed by anybody.**"

Nobody in that room was thanked. The reader of this fen's book does not know that; he is nine hundred miles off and this body has not written to him about it and is not going to, on the ground that a trust putting a question in a room in a counting house is not an act of this body and must not be entered as one.

And one of the nine said the thing about the fifth set out loud, and it was said in the ordinary voice and it was correct, and the trust's steward let it stand.

"**There are five sets in this room and the fifth was not sent by anybody in that fen.**"

"**No,"** said Tova Reed. "**And I am not going to say who put it there, and nobody in this room is going to ask me in a way that makes me say, and if anybody does I will say no and it will go in the minutes as no.** I have put a thing of mine into a room this year and I have refused to say where it came from in front of nine people, and I am not going to do the other thing in a room that is not mine. **And the two are not the same decision and I am not going to be told they are the same decision by a person who wants a good paragraph.**"

> Entered in a book nine hundred miles off, in the room with about nine people in it, and copied into this body's book on the twenty-fourth because a person in that room sent the copy on the ordinary post and did not charge for it: **there are five sets of this body's records in that room and the fifth was not sent by anybody in this fen, and the trust's steward declined to say who put it there and declined in a form that could be read back.**
>
> **The copy came down on the ordinary post at a shilling and it is entered, because a thing a body cannot check is a thing a body has to enter anyway, and the price of it is a shilling and the fen has four pounds and fivepence in the tin and the tin does not get any easier and the shilling came out of it.**

And the answer is on a road. The March road is nine days. The question went on the ordinary post in the afternoon of the twenty-third and the answer will be in this fen on the second of next month, which is a Tuesday, and the clerk wrote the day down twice because it is a figure and figures in this body get read out.

> Entered at the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-third of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do:
>
> **A question was put on the twenty-third of this month, at Cray, in a room with about nine people in it, about a class of record, by the steward of the Salt March Dryfoot Trust, of Cray, who is a party to the seed arrangements at Lantern Reach and is not of this holding and has not been made of this holding.** The answer is on the March road, which is nine days, and will be in this yard on the second of next month.
>
> **This body did not ask the question, did not suggest it, did not know it was going to be asked until the day it was asked, and did not pay for it.** The trust paid for it out of its own office and the trust's own post and the trust did not tell this body the price and this body has not asked and has entered that it is not going to ask, on the ground that a price this holding did not incur is not a price this holding is owed an account of.
>
> And entered on the same page, on the standing of a thing this body has entered before and is entering again: **if the answer is that a record of that kind is produced only to the person named in it, then the request that would produce it is not a thing a body can make and is not a thing a party can make and is not a thing an office can make, and it is a thing one person can make.** **The only person this fen has entered as being able to make such a request is not on this fen, is not in this yard, and is not anybody this body has standing over, and this body has entered that it cannot become that person and is not going to try, and it entered that on the twenty-third of this month, eight days before there is an answer, because a body that enters it afterwards can be suspected of having fitted it.**
>
> And entered on the same line: **the fen is not a party to anything, and this is the second time in nine days it has entered that sentence, and the first time was about a ledger four hundred miles off and the reason is the same both times, which is that a warning is a document and a document about another household's ground or another body's record is a thing an office can hold. And entered under both of those, in a different hand, the man of about seventy's rule that the second copy of anything has to be a person, given in a yard four hundred miles off on the twenty-eighth of last month, read back and not improved on: this is the first time that rule has had to be applied to a request instead of to a piece of paper, it does not work any better, and it was not supposed to.**

---

And then the twenty-fourth, at the north gate, with about twenty people in the yard, and the reader of this body asked who loses, and asked it himself, which he does about once a month, and the steward did not ask it and the clerk did not ask it and nobody was surprised.

"**Who loses.** I will give you four and I am not going to make them the same size, because they are not the same size and the whole of this yard's trouble for a season has been putting things of different sizes in one column."

"**One, and it is the largest: this body waits nine days.** For eleven years this body has got a figure out of a room by standing in the room. That is the whole of its method and it is a good method and it has worked on a well house, a gate, a barn door and a form case. **It does not work on a counting house nine hundred miles off and it has never once tried to and I am not going to start on the twenty-fourth.**"

"**Two: whoever put the fifth set of this body's records in that room.** I have known about that set since the fourth of this month, when we counted our four places out loud in this room with fourteen people in it, and I have not asked who it is and I am not going to ask, and the reason I am not going to ask is that we counted four places and named them and the fifth was not in the count, and **a body that finds out about a fifth after it has counted four has to decide whether to be glad or frightened and I have not got time this month and I have said so out loud.**"

"**Three: the four households on a shelf four hundred miles off**, who are not in this and about whom nobody in that room said one word, and who would not be helped by a word, and whose day letter is on a stone and whose rows are let go."

"And **four, and this is the one nobody is going to enjoy: nobody.** Nobody in that room did anything wrong. **A trust with a standing put a question it was entitled to put into a room that will answer it in the ordinary course, on its own letterhead, correctly, and there is nothing in that to complain of and I am not going to stand here and pretend there is.** And this body has entered that the answer is nine days off and that the nine days are going to be about the worst nine days of this season and that **the worst of the nine days is not the answer. The worst of the nine days is that this body has a question in a room nine hundred miles off and can do nothing at all about it and has not got a single thing to enter against anybody while it waits.**"

Nobody answered that and he did not want them to. He read it back out of the book anyway, and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and it went in, and the yard went quiet in the ordinary way.

And then one of the sixteen households said a thing at the gate that has not been put in a book and that Dunmar Fitch wrote down anyway, in his own hand, without being asked, which is his standing and is not the same standing as the four-word observations and he has never once pretended it is.

"He has said nine days like it is a figure of money and it is not," the man said. "**Nine days is a fortnight less five and there is a man on this road with a sick leg who does not have a fortnight less five.**"

"Enter that," said the reader of this body, "**and do not improve on it, and do not let anybody in four years find that entry and think it was a finding about the road. It is one man's leg and one man in a yard and it is the only thing said in this yard on the twenty-fourth of this month that I have not got an answer to, and I have had it on the book about a minute and I have not stopped thinking about it.**"

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body and with the speaker's own name not taken, on the standing of a cost: **nine days is a fortnight less five, and there is a man on this road whose leg is not that.** **The reader read it back and stated that he has not got an answer to it, and entered that he has not got an answer to it, and the man who said it is one of sixteen households on this head and is not of the fen and is not on any other page of this book.**

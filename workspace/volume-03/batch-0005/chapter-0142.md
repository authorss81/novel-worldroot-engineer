# Chapter 142

## Unsafe

The sheet came on the ordinary post on the morning of the twenty-seventh of this month, from the sub-office at Fennmere, and it is four lines long, and it is a declaration, and it is correct, and it is the shortest document this body has been sent in a year and the worst thing in this book's history.

> **Records of this holder are of an experimental character and are declared unsafe for evidentiary use.**
>
> A record so declared is not to be relied upon by this office, is not to be produced by an officer of this district, and is not to be entered in any proceeding in which this office has a part.
>
> **The declaration is made in the ordinary course and no part of it is a finding against the holder. Nothing in this line states that anything the holder has said is untrue.**
>
> The holder is notified. The holder may produce any record declared unsafe and may not be prevented from doing so.

It has the office's heading at the top and a date under it and nothing at the foot. Lissa Vale read it out whole in the seed house with the bay doors shut, because a definition is read in a room and a declaration is worse than a definition. Dunmar Fitch read it back out of the book with the hour on it. The man of about fifty read it back after him and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and nobody thanked anybody.

And then the reader read the third line out again by itself, and only that line, twice, with about nine people in the room and about a minute between the two readings and nothing in his face at all.

"**Nothing in this line states that anything the holder has said is untrue.** I have read that eleven times this morning and I am going to read it out twice more in a yard and I would like the clerk to enter the count and enter the hour, and then I am going to say what it does, and I am going to say it in the ordinary words and not in any other words."

"So read it out," said Lissa Vale.

He read it out. Twice more, in a yard of about twenty at the north gate, at about the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the same voice both times, to about twenty people who had all heard it once already that morning.

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body: **the third line of the declaration of this date was read out in a yard of about twenty, twice, on its own, and the reading is entered as three readings in all, and the count is entered because a body that says a thing three times in a day in front of the same people is making a point and not making a finding.**

---

And then he said what it does, and he said it standing, and this is the whole of the chapter and it is not a lesson and it is not a speech and it took him about four minutes.

"**For eleven years the only defense this body has had is that it publishes everything, and the whole of that defense is one sentence, and the sentence is that if a thing is true and it is in a book that anybody may read, then a person who wants to know whether it is true can go and look at it.** That is not a law and it is not a privilege and it is not a charter, and it is not in the arrangement and it is not in the six clauses and it is not on the door. **It is a practice and it has been the practice of this holding for eleven years and it has cost this holding money and time and about forty arguments in this yard and it is the only reason anybody in nine miles has ever had to take this body seriously.**"

"And a declaration that says a record of this holder is unsafe for evidentiary use is not an attack on that practice. **A practice is not a record. You cannot classify a practice. What is classified here is a record, and what is excluded is the use of a record in a proceeding in which this office has a part, and this body has no part in any proceeding and has not been in one since a year this body would rather forget.**"

"Here is what it does, and I am going to say it as one sentence and then I am going to stop talking for a while. **The declaration is the reason this body cannot use its own record, and it is also the reason nobody has to answer it.** And a body that is relieved of having to answer anything is not the same body as one that was never asked, and I have been the reader of this body for four years and I can tell the difference between those two on the page, and **every person in this yard can tell the difference between those two in the room.**"

He put the sheet face up on the bench without touching the ink.

"And I want the other half said in the same yard on the same day by somebody who is not me. **For eleven years this body has been right out loud, every time, in front of whoever came.** And an office in nine miles has now put on its own letterhead, in the ordinary course, in a form with a date under it, that the truth of what this body says is not in question. **Not in question. That is the words. Not doubted, not suspected, not to be looked into. Not in question.** And I have been standing in this yard for a season waiting for somebody to say that, and it has been said, and it is on a piece of paper four lines long that took a district a shilling to send, **and I would like somebody in four years to sit down and decide for themselves whether I should be glad or whether I have been sold.**"

"And I am not going to say which one it is today, in this yard, in front of twenty people, and I would like the clerk to enter that I did not say it, because a man who says it in a yard has said it and cannot take it back and I have not got the rest of it yet."

---

And the officer came on the twenty-seventh, at about the fourth hour, on foot, in the same boots she was in on the second of this month, nine miles down the fen road, and she was let in at the gate and she was not announced, and the reason she was not announced is the reason she gave on the second of this month, which is that she is already in the book.

She put the sheet on the bench and squared it with two fingers, which is a thing a person does with a paper they have carried nine miles, and she said the four things she was there to say and they were all short.

"**That is the whole of it and it went on the ordinary post and it was not held.** It is made in the ordinary course, it names no record, it makes no finding against anybody, and I want the fourth line read at somebody in this yard because I think about half the people who will be told about this sheet in the next ten days will stop at the second line and never get to it. **A holder may produce any record declared unsafe and may not be prevented from doing so. That is yours and it does not expire.**"

"**I did not write it, I have no part in making it, and I have not been asked to explain it and I am not able to.** If anybody in this yard wants to put something to me about it they may do it now and I will answer in the room, and I say that as an officer and not as a favour, and I would like the clerk to enter that I offered and that it is my office to offer and not my kindness to offer."

Nobody put anything to her. About nine people were in that yard and about twenty-five were in the seed house and neither number of them said one word, and she stood at the gate for about a quarter of an hour and then went back down the fen road on foot in the same boots, and nobody said goodnight to her and she did not expect it and the fen entered that she did not expect it, **because an officer who has done her work is not a person anybody has to be courteous to at the end of a road, and a body that has begun expecting courtesies from officers is a body that is about to be disappointed in a very small way on a very ordinary day.**

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **nothing was put to an officer of a district office in this yard on the twenty-seventh of this month, and an officer offered to be asked a question in the room and was not asked one, and the fen entered that the offer is the office's ordinary practice and is not a courtesy, and that the fen has entered that it declined the courtesy as well as the question, and does not know which of the two it declined first and has not decided to work out.**
>
> **No officer of any district in this batch lies, obstructs, delays a document, or is anybody but correct, and this is entered as a fact about a day and not as a finding about anybody.**

---

And then the steward asked who loses, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh, in the same yard, with the sheet on the bench and about twenty people in it, and she recommended nothing, and what she found was three and the third one was a man two hundred yards off.

"**One. This body loses its record.** Not the books. The books are here and the books are in five places and two of those five are nine hundred miles off and one of them is a man's coat. What this body loses is the right to put any of it in front of anybody in a room where it counts. **And I am going to be exact about that because a person in four years is going to read the word *loses* and think somebody has been robbed, and nobody has been robbed. A thing nobody can use is not a thing anybody has taken.**"

"**Two. The sixteen households on this head lose nothing today.** Not a pail, not a shilling, not an hour. Their water is the same water it was on the twenty-sixth and it will be the same water on the twenty-eighth. **And I am putting that in the book in those words so that nobody in this yard can use the fourth line of that sheet to frighten them with, because that is exactly what a sheet like that is for and it will be tried.**"

"And **three, and it is not a loss either, and it is the one I cannot finish: four households four hundred miles off lose nothing today either, and I have no standing over them and I did not write to them and I am not going to, and the number of people in that yard today is four and the number of them this fen can account for is four and every one of them is a person with a ledger and a day letter and a wall and a row of a hundred and forty-one trees, and a piece of paper four lines long has not touched one of them today and will not.**"

"So there is one body and two thousands and a half of people and one of them is going to wake up tomorrow and find that the only thing it has ever been able to do is not available to it, and the other two thousand and a half are going to get up and do what they do. **And I have been asking who loses in this yard for a season and this is the first time the answer has been that everybody is fine, and I would like somebody in four years to read that I said it and that I did not enjoy it and that I am the one who sends a page to a counting house every month and this is the first month I have not known whether it arrived.**"

"And then there is a man I have to go and ask, and I am going to go and ask him, and it is the fourth time in eleven years that anybody in this yard has asked that man the same question, and I would like it entered that I am not asking him for an opinion."

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a cost, which is that a cost is entered as a cost and not as a fault and not as a virtue: **who loses. One, and it is this body. Two, and it is nobody on this head, and the number of households and the number of pails are entered as unchanged. Three, and it is nobody four hundred miles off, and the number of people in that yard is four and this body can account for four and has standing over none of them.**
>
> **And entered on the same line, in the steward's own hand and read back by her: a body that publishes everything has been told in writing that the truth of what it says is not in question, and this body does not know whether it has been given something or sold something, and the steward entered that she does not enjoy saying so and is not going to soften it in a book.**

---

And the man of about seventy was fetched on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth of this month, the tenth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was given the four lines in the order they are written in, and the three readings of the third one, and the man of about fifty's sentence about a body relieved of having to answer anything, and the three things the steward found, in that order, and he did not say one word for about a minute and a half.

"Ten," he said. "**And I have said nine times in this yard that a thing nobody wanted in a book has to go in a book anyway, and I have been right nine times, and this morning an office has gone and written down in the ordinary course that this holding's records are not fit to be used, and it is correct, and it is four lines, and there is not one word of it I would take out if I could put it back.**"

"Here is what I want said, and I am going to say it and then nobody is going to answer me, and both of those go in the book because they always do. **A book that cannot be used is not a book anybody can put a thing in.** And that is the whole of the tenth time, and it is the same mistake for the tenth time, in a different noun, and I am seventy-one and I have learned what that means and I have stopped expecting anybody to answer me, and I would like it entered that I said that in a yard on the twenty-eighth of this month and that nobody answered me and that I did not expect it this time either, which is the new thing, and the new thing is not a good thing and I have written it down anyway."

And the reader of this body read it back, and the clerk entered the hour, and the fen entered that nobody answered it, and the reader asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and nobody thanked anybody, and the sheet went in a drawer in the seed house and the drawer is not locked and the sheet is not a secret and the fen entered that the sheet is not a secret and that a thing four lines long that is not a secret is the reason this body cannot do anything about it.

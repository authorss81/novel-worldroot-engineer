# Chapter 139

## The Twenty-First

The door was read out whole at the tenth hour of the twenty-first of this month, in a full yard, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and there was about twenty-five people in it, and nobody from the fen asked anybody anything that day.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the twenty-first of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month three months back, the fifth read twice.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-eight.**
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Tuesday, and this holding does not know what it is.** That is the eighth month of a run of seven that has not been discharged in seven. **It is a figure nobody in this yard can say and this body will not enter a figure it does not know, and this is the second time in nine days that it has stood in a yard and said that out loud, and the count of the two is entered.**

The reader read the whole of it back out of the book and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and the clerk entered the hour, and nobody thanked anybody, and the steward did not go back inside for about an hour and a half.

---

And then he stood up at the wall side of the gate and said what this body is going to do about the sheet that came on the sixteenth, and what it is going to do is nothing, and he said the word nothing first, before anybody had asked him, and he gave the reason he gives when he is about to be argued with, which is that he is going to say the whole of it before anybody can take any part of it.

"I am going to do nothing," he said, "**for four days, and I have got four reasons and I am going to give all four in the order I have got them, and then I am going to say what it costs, and then this yard can say whatever it likes and I will not answer any of it.**"

"**One. The office has named neither of the two.** We put two names in this book on the sixteenth as my opinion and not as a finding, and the two names in that entry are the two I would hand over, and the office has told us it holds two and has not told us which. **A body that cannot say which two of its own records are gone has nothing to answer.** Every question anybody is going to ask me in the next four days has the same answer and the answer is that I do not know what the question is about."

"**Two. A class of record of an experimental character is entered against a record and not against a holder, and a decision is a record.** Everything this body does in the next four days is going into a book, and it is going into the same book, and if any of it is made in a hurry and in a yard then it is a record of an experimental character made in a working district and not confirmed by a second reading at the time of making. **I am not going to hand an office a third thing because I did not want to be on my toes for four days.**"

"**Three. I put two names in a book on the sixteenth and I put them there because I could not stand having to guess in private.** And the next thing I do is the thing people will read as the reason I had the opinion. If I write a letter this week then in four years somebody is going to come here and read the two names and the letter together, and the letter is going to look like the opinion and the opinion is going to look like the letter, **and I would rather have a gap on the page than two things on it that make one shape.**"

"And **four**, and this is the one I would like written down, and I am giving it as the fourth because I have tried the other three and this is the one that is actually holding me. **Anything this body does in the next four days is a thing an office in nine miles can read as a thing it did because it was told to.** Not as a thing it chose. **As a thing it was told to.** And that reading is available to anybody, it costs an office nothing, it requires no finding and no hearing, and I cannot see a single way of stopping it except by not doing anything at all in the days when it is cheapest for somebody to say so."

He stood there and let about four seconds go by, which he has done before.

"Now the cost, and I am going to say it as one sentence because it is one sentence and I have had it in my head since Friday."

"**A body that cannot tell which two of its own records are gone is a body that has lost two things and does not know what it is looking for. The only honest thing to do about that is to be wrong in the open and to be found out having been wrong in the open, and the four days are the price of being wrong in the open on purpose instead of by accident.** That is all of it. **And I am not going to stand here and tell anybody we should send more copies.** We have got four places and a table and I have said out loud in this yard that the reason we have got them is that we are afraid, and a man who is afraid is not a man who gets to be clever, and **I have got nothing new to add to four places and I have thought about it for two days and there is nothing new.**"

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a decision, read back by the reader of this body and initialed against nothing:
>
> **This body will do nothing about the declaration of the sixteenth until the twenty-fifth of this month.** Four reasons were given in a yard in the order they were given in and the fourth was that anything this body does in the next four days is a thing an office in nine miles can read as a thing it did because it was told to. **The cost was stated in one sentence and the sentence is: a body that cannot tell which two of its own records are gone is a body that has lost two things and does not know what it is looking for, and the only honest thing to do about that is to be wrong in the open and to be found out having been wrong in the open.**
>
> **No further copies were proposed. The reader of this body stated that he had thought about it for two days and had nothing new, and the count of places is four and has not been enlarged and the fen is not nine places.**

---

And then Coll Renner asked the only question in that yard that was going to be asked, and he asked it standing at the back with his own book shut under his arm, and he said the first half of it before the second half, which is his practice and has been all season.

"What happens on the twenty-fifth."

"**I have not decided that either, and I am not going to decide it today, and I would like the clerk to enter that the man asked and that I did not answer and why.** Deciding on the twenty-fifth is not the same as deciding today, and the difference between those two things is four days and it is the whole of what this body has got. If I decide today then the four days were a form and not a wait. **I have been in this yard for eleven years and I have watched two bodies decide things early so that they could be seen to have decided them early, and one of them got its records classified and the other one did not, and neither of them can tell anybody which one it was.**"

"And the other half, said first, which is the half that matters: **you did not have to ask that in a yard and you asked it in a yard, and that is your standing and not mine, and I am not going to take it off you.**"

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, by the clerk, on his own motion, unasked, and read back by the reader of this body, who did not ask what it meant because he has never once asked: **the question was available to twenty-five people in that yard and was taken by one person, and the answer given was that the person who asked did not have to.**
>
> The reader of this body answered the first half and did not answer the second half, and gave the reason for not answering the second half, and the reason was entered and is that the second half is a standing and a standing cannot be given away by a man in a yard in front of twenty-five people. **Nobody thanked the licensed man and he entered that he was not thanked, which is now the fourth time in a season that he has asked for it and the first time that the fen has entered that a standing was not taken off anybody.**

And then the steward went inside and was in the seed house for about an hour and a half and came out and did not say one word about it, and the fen entered the hour and did not enter why, and the reason it did not ask is that she is the steward of this body and it is eleven years of her standing that she is not asked.

---

And on the same day, four hundred miles off, in daylight, on a shelf, something five days old was looked at by four people and not one of them read it.

It is the same green and it is five days old. Nobody has cut it. Nobody has dug about it. Nobody has put a spade in the row since the tenth, and the row is the row, and the grass under it has not been cut either, because the grass is cut twice a year and this is not the half of the year it is cut in.

Three of the four had walked out of their own yards to look at it and had come back. That is the whole of the traffic. **Three people looked at a thing and came back and did not do anything, and one of them is sixty-one and one of them is a man with a spade and one of them is not of the four at all and was asked to look and looked.**

The man with the spade said the thing out loud, in his own yard, to nobody in particular, on the twenty-first, and the three of them who were within hearing heard it.

"**I have stopped saying what I think it is.** I said it in the first two days and I have said it to about four people and one of them was a stranger with a chain, and I am not going to say it again, and I am not going to say it is a growth and I am not going to say it is a message and I am not going to say it is a fault in the ground, because I do not know one of those and I have said two of them and a man with a spade does not get to keep saying things he cannot do."

And the second of the four said, in the same yard, within about a minute, and she said it flatly and she was not looking at anybody:

"**That is the same thing said twice.**"

Nobody wrote either of them down. There was nobody on that shelf with a book at the end of the twenty-first except the day letter on the stone at the well, which says a day and a house and a pail, and it does not say any of this and it is not for saying any of this.

And one of the four, the one who was not asked to look and looked, said the other thing that was said out loud on that shelf that day, and he said it to a wall.

"**It is going to be a year.** Nobody in this yard has said that word today and nobody has said it in five days. A green that came up in one afternoon is going to be a thing you can point at in a year or it is going to be a thing you cannot point at in a year, and I do not know which of those it is and I am not going to be the man who says it out loud first."

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-first of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do and has not done since the tenth of this month:
>
> **This body did not send anybody to a shelf four hundred miles off, did not write, did not hire a man, and did not ask a person a question about the row or about what came up out of it.** Nobody from this holding is on that shelf. The last thing this body knows about that row is a sheet written on the sixteenth and read in this yard on the twentieth, and the shape of it has not changed since and this body has not gone to find out whether it has.
>
> **The fen has entered that the not-asking is the same not-asking it entered on the second of this month about a ledger, and the fen has entered that the count of that decision is two and the reason is the same both times, which is that a warning is a document and a document about another household's ground is a thing an office can hold.** And entered on the same line: **on the twenty-first of this month a man with a spade said out loud in his own yard that he has stopped saying what he thinks the green is, and a woman in the same yard said that that was the same thing said twice, and this body has neither sentence, and did not ask for it, and will not be sent it, and is not going to describe either of them in this book as a finding about a row of trees.**

---

And the twenty-second was a Sunday and almost nothing happened on it and the fen put the Sunday in the book, because it is this holding's standing not to leave a day out on the ground that nothing occurred on it, and the reader of this body did not fetch the man of about seventy and did not send for him, and the fen entered that it does not send for a man on a Sunday for a decision it has already made.

> Entered at the eleventh hour of the morning of the twenty-second of this month, by the clerk, on the standing of a day with nothing in it: **a Sunday, and nothing done.** The four days this body has said it will do nothing for are the twenty-first, the twenty-second, the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth, and on the twenty-fifth the fen will either do something or enter that it has decided again not to, and the reader of this body has said out loud in a yard that he has not got anything new and is not going to be helped by anybody producing something.
>
> **And entered on the same page, because a body that waits four days has to say what it is doing with them: this body is doing nothing with them on purpose, it has not filled them with work, and it has entered that the four days are the price of being wrong in the open on purpose, and the cost of the four days is the four days and not one shilling and not one hundredweight, and this is the first time in eleven years that a thing this body has decided cost it nothing to say out loud.**

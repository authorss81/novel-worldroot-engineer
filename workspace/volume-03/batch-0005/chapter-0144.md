# Chapter 144

## The First Of The Ninth

The eighth month's compost line fell on the first of the ninth month at about the eleventh hour in a yard with about twenty people in it, and it was written in a hand that is not the hand that book has been written in for eleven years, and the reader appointed for it was the man of about fifty and not the steward, and the reason for that is four months old and is hers.

> Return main, the eighth month. Entered on the first of the ninth month by the examiner, in the steward's book, in her own hand, which is not the steward's hand. Nothing put in. None available. **Specification four hundred hundredweight.**
>
> **Against the specification, the going.** Three journeys are charged to this month, being three men hired in it, at twenty-five hundredweight each, which is the figure journeys have carried since the sixth month: **seventy-five hundredweight.**
>
> **Shortfall four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight, ninety-five pounds, at four shillings the hundredweight, which is the compost valuation and is not anybody's day rate.** Carried forward and not discharged.
>
> **The run of it is eight months and it has not been discharged in eight.**
>
> And against the same line, separately, in the same hand, and entered as four separate things and not as part of the shortfall, because they are not a shortfall of compost and they are the cost of doing the work and **a book that puts the two in one column is a book that cannot be read in four years:**
>
> **One. A man hired on the fourth of this month whose balance of twenty-eight shillings is not discharged. His pay-off day was never entered against him, and the fen has entered that it was not and is not going to invent one.**
> **Two. Two men hired in this month and paid off inside it, the ninth on the seventeenth and the twelfth on the twentieth, four shillings on the day each was hired and the balance on the day he was paid off.**
> **Three. Six days of a woman's keep over a fortnight, at one shilling and sevenpence for the six days.** **Four. A second blanket at twopence.** Both entered against the seventh month's line on the thirtieth and the eleventh of that month and entered here again, **because the person who keeps the money will not have a figure about a woman go out of a book by being a month old.**

Gael Senn read the whole of it out loud, the figures first and then the words around them, which is her practice, and the man of about fifty read it back with the hour on it and asked for his initial against nothing, and Dunmar Fitch entered the hour and read the entry back before he initialled it. Nobody thanked her.

---

And then the woman who does the sums said the word before the figure, and she has done that twice in this book and this is the third time.

"It is worse," said Anneke Roos. "**I am saying that before the number, because the number is four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight and ninety-five pounds and it is the biggest line this holding has entered, and I am not going to stand in a yard and let anybody find that out off a slate.**"

"**Four hundred is the specification and it has been four hundred since the flood and it is not going to be four hundred and fifty because a man is expensive.** Seventy-five hundredweight on top of it: three journeys up the plateau road, a journey is twenty-five hundredweight, and I have carried that figure since the sixth month and checked it this morning."

"So: four hundred and seventy-five at four shillings is one thousand nine hundred shillings, which is **ninety-five pounds.** The seventh month was eighty pounds eight. The sixth was ninety pounds eight. **Ninety-five is worse than both, and worse than the seventh by fourteen pounds and twelve shillings, and I have never in eleven years let a difference out of this yard without the two numbers either side of it.** And **the run is eight months and it has not been discharged in eight, and this is the first month in the run that the figure has gone up and the specification has not, and that is not a thing to be pleased with on a Tuesday morning and I am not.**"

And then she picked up a second sheet, and what was on it was the one time in eight months a figure on this line had moved in this holding's favour.

"**A penny, and I am going to say the whole of it and I am not going to be pleased about it in a book and I want it entered that I said I would not be and then did not.** A sheet has come to the counting house at Cray with a firm's name at the head of it, and the price of a hundredweight of compost has come down by a penny, from four shillings to three shillings and eleven pence, **and that is all I have got and there is nothing else on the sheet and I have looked.**"

"So the amount it moves this line is one penny on four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight, which is **four hundred and seventy-five pence, which is one pound, nineteen shillings and sevenpence.** That is the whole of it, and **it is the first time in eight months any figure on this line has moved in this body's favour, and the line is ninety-five pounds.**"

"And here is the half that makes it worse than nothing. **It is not a gift and it is not a kindness and nobody has told this body why it happened.** A price comes down because something came down, or because somebody wanted it to, or because the counting house is nine hundred miles off and this fen is one account, and **this body does not know which of the three, and has entered that it has not written and asked, the first time in eleven years it has not written and asked about a price.**"

> Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the first of the ninth month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the money: **the price of a hundredweight of compost at the counting house at Cray has come down by a penny, from four shillings to three shillings and eleven pence, on a sheet with a firm's name at the head of it. The amount it moves the shortfall of this line is one pound, nineteen shillings and sevenpence.** The person who keeps the money stated before the figure that she was not going to be pleased about it, **and entered that the reason it came down is not known, that this body does not know which of three explanations it is, and that it has not written and asked.**

---

And then there was a man in a yard at Fennmere with a thing to sell, and he is not of this holding and is not a member of anything and is not named in this book, and he gave his figure before he was asked for it, and he is not a liar and believes every word of it.

Four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight of compost, **three shillings and ninepence the hundredweight**, delivered to the gate, **not tested, and he cannot say what is in it, and it was made up in a yard behind a shed and the yard is being drained and cannot make it again.** The money in his hand on the day.

Anneke Roos did the sum with it in a yard of about twenty, both halves out loud, and added nothing.

"**His figure, four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight at three shillings and ninepence, is eighty-nine pounds, one shilling and threepence.** **Cray's figure, the same four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight at three shillings and eleven pence, is ninety-three pounds and five pence. The difference between the cheapest figure anybody has offered this holding and the price at a counting house nine hundred miles off is three pounds, nineteen shillings and twopence.** And this holding has **four pounds and fivepence in the tin, and the difference against it leaves one shilling and threepence.** And I did the difference first, and I have been doing that all season, so I am going to say the other thing: **that is not the point, and anybody in this yard who thinks it is the point stopped listening four sentences ago.**"

"The point is that a heap made up in a yard is a heap for a month and a drained yard cannot make another, and if this holding spends four pounds and fivepence on a year's shortfall in one purchase then **in three months it has no yard, no money and no line, and the line is a hundredweight a month for the rest of the season with nothing behind it.** That is worse in a way with no pound sign in it at all."

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the first of the ninth month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of the money, in two halves, and **the two halves were not added, not averaged, and not presented as one figure**:
>
> **First half, offered in a yard in nine miles on ordinary trade terms, not tested: four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight at three shillings and ninepence, eighty-nine pounds, one shilling and threepence.**
> **Second half, a sheet from a counting house nine hundred miles off: the same four hundred and seventy-five hundredweight at three shillings and eleven pence, ninety-three pounds and five pence.**
> **The difference between the two is three pounds, nineteen shillings and twopence, and this holding has four pounds and fivepence in the tin, and the difference against it leaves one shilling and threepence.** The person who keeps the money said before the difference that the difference was not the point, **and said that a heap made up in a yard is a heap for a month, a yard that is being drained cannot make it again, and buying a year's shortfall in one purchase would leave this holding in three months with no yard, no money and no line.**

---

And then the man of about fifty said the sentence the second sheet earns a body, standing, and he gave the reason for saying it first.

"**A compost line that improves for a reason nobody can find out is worse for this body than a compost line that does not improve at all.** In four years somebody is going to find that a price came down a penny and think this holding had a good month, **and there has been one movement in this line in eight months and it went the wrong way by fourteen pounds twelve shillings, and the other went the right way by one pound nineteen shillings and sevenpence for reasons nobody will tell us.** I am not going to say which of those two numbers frightens me more, and I am not going to be thanked, and the reader is to read that last sentence back."

The reader read it back and asked for his initial against nothing.

And the man of about seventy was in that yard at the wall and was not fetched and was not sent for. **The fen entered that it did not fetch him, that this is the tenth time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing and that the tenth was on the twenty-seventh of last month, and that today was not a bringing out at all, because the first of a month is read in a yard and he is one of the twenty, and he spoke and nobody asked him to.** He had been given the four lines, the two sheets, the two figures and the difference, in that order, and he asked for the difference to be repeated, then said the other half, and it is not a comfort.

"**A thing that comes down a penny and nobody can say why is not a gift. A gift is a thing somebody gives you and you can shake hands about. This is a figure that has come in from somewhere and this body cannot see where it came from and cannot see who moved it, and a body that puts a figure like that in a book has to be able to sleep on it.** I am seventy-one and the small thing today is that **a body that cannot account for a pound is a body that cannot account for the ninety-five either, and it has to stop finding out.** I am not going to be thanked, and if he reads it back I want him to go on not asking me what it was about, which is his standing and has never once cost anybody anything."

The man of about fifty read it back into a yard of about twenty and **did not ask what it was about**, and Dunmar Fitch entered that he did not ask, and nobody thanked anybody.

---

And on the second of the ninth month, a Tuesday, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, nine days after a question was put in a room nine hundred miles off, a form came down the March road and was read out whole in the seed house with the bay doors shut and about nine people in it, and it is four lines, and it is correct, and it names nobody, and nothing in it was opened.

> Records sealed under the schedule of compensation are produced to the person named in them and to no other person, and are produced to that person on that person's own request and not on the request of a holder, of a party, of an office, or of any other person.
>
> A request made by any person other than the person named has no effect and is not entered.
>
> **The holder is not a party to any such record and has no standing to request one. A party has none.**
>
> **The office has entered that no such request has been received and does not intend to solicit one.**

Dunmar Fitch read it back with the hour on it and the man of about fifty read it back after him and asked for his initial against nothing, and about nine people were in that room and none was named on the page.

"That is the most defensible instrument anybody has ever written," he said, in the ordinary voice, without irony and without pleasure. "**I have said in this yard for a season that an office is right and that the rightness is not going to be argued with, and this is the same rightness with a person in it. Nobody is accused of anything. Nobody's word is doubted today. The holder may produce the record and may not be prevented. And there is a door in it four feet wide, and one person in the world can walk through it, and that person is not in this room and was not written to and will not be.**"

And Tova Reed stood up, and it is her standing and has been for eleven years, and she said the thing she has refused to say three times in a season, and said the first part before the second, and the order is the order she says things in.

"I am not going to stand in this room and say that I am the only person in the world who can make that request. **And the clerk is not to write it, and I am not going to be asked to confirm it, and the reader is not to read it back, and if either of you does it I will say no and the no will go in the book.**" "**A thing that one person can do is not a thing a body can be asked to do. I have said that about a free offer, about a shape and about a mechanism, and this is the fourth time in a season and I am not going to make it into anything by saying it again.** And I am not going to be thanked for it, and I have said that before it is said, and if somebody in this room thanks me they will be wrong in the room and in the book."

Nobody thanked her, and Dunmar Fitch wrote *a person who could make the request is not named in this entry, has not been asked, and has not been written to*, and the man of about fifty read that back and **did not ask who**, which is his standing, and the fen entered that he did not ask.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the second of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **a request was not made.** The form states that such a record is produced to the person named in it and on that person's own request, that a request by any other person has no effect and is not entered, that a holder and a party both have no standing to request one, and that the office has entered that no such request has been received and does not intend to solicit one. **Nothing was opened. Nobody was named. No request was made by anybody, and this body cannot make one and has no standing to become a person who could.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the instrument is correct, and the man of about fifty said in a room with about nine people in it that it is the most defensible instrument anybody has ever written, and said it in the ordinary voice, and the count of the times the reader has said a thing an office has done is unanswerable is two.**

And the third of the ninth month was a Thursday and a dozen people were in the seed house and nothing was read out. A cart came up the fen road on the seventh, and on the thirtieth of last month this body entered that it did not know what was on it, and had not asked.

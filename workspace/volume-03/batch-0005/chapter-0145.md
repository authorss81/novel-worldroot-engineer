# Chapter 145

## Copies And A Ledger

The seed-route ledger was copied entire on the fourth of the ninth month, page by page, in two hands, and the copy began at about the seventh hour of the morning in the seed house with the bay doors open and about nine people in the room, and it was finished at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and nobody in that room read a page of it.

Dunmar Fitch copied it. Gael Senn checked the copy against the original, page by page, in a hand that is not his and is not the steward's, and **at the end of the afternoon she signed the last page and put the number of pages against her name and did not sign for the contents**, and the fen entered that she has never signed for the contents of anything in eleven years and has signed for the number of pages about forty times.

**It is not the four of the fourth of last month.** Those four are a different thing in a different bundle, and the count of that is four and has not been enlarged and is not going to be, and the fen entered that it is entering the two counts on the same page on purpose so that nobody in four years can add them.

And the count of this one is **five**, and the reader of this body said it out loud with the names on it, standing at the bench with the ledger shut, and about nine people heard it.

"**One. Cray**, a counting house, nine hundred miles, on the ordinary post, and a hand goes into it and nobody at this bench has ever been told whose."

"**Two. The sub-office at Fennmere**, a counter, nine miles, and a clerk with a tray, and the tray is in a building."

"**Three. Inside a licensed man's coat**, going up the plateau road on Tuesday, and I am not going to count the coat and the shelf as two places and I am not going to count them as one, and the clerk is to write that down as it stands and I will not argue it."

"**Four. A table in the yard at the seed house**, and the copy on it does not go under a cloth and does not go in a box."

"And **five. The well house door.** And the door is not a copy of this ledger and I am not going to pretend it is, and it is the only one of the five that is not a copy of anything: **it is a wall in a fen with a column of readings on it and a notice in four parts and a refusal clause in this body's own hand, and any person on this earth can walk up to it at any hour of the day and read a figure off it and be told what the figure is and be told the hour it was read at.**"

And then he said the two halves, and he said the second one first, and the clerk entered both in one line and the fen entered that he entered them in one line.

"**A table in a yard is not a safe place. It is the best place this holding has.** A table in a yard can be carried off in a cart in an hour by anybody who wants it and there is no lock on a yard and no lock on a table and anybody who has ever wanted to be sure of a thing has known that for longer than this body has been a body. **And it is the best place this holding has, because it is the only one of the five that this holding can check, and a copy this body cannot read is a copy this body has to take on the word of a post and a counter and a man in a coat.** Enter both halves in the same line and do not improve on either of them."

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the fourth of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a count: **five places, for a copy of the seed-route ledger made entire on this day in two hands.** Cray; the sub-office at Fennmere; inside a licensed man's coat, and the coat and the shelf it is going to are entered as one and not as two and the reason was given and not argued; a table in the yard at the seed house; the well house door, which is not a copy and is entered as a place a person can read this body's figures in.
>
> **And on the same line, in the reader of this body's own words: a table in a yard is not a safe place, and it is the best place this holding has.** The clerk entered both halves in the same line as he was asked to and improved on neither.
>
> **And entered under that: this count is five and is the count of a copy made today. The count of the four places made on the fourth of last month is four, is a different thing, and has not been enlarged. The two counts are on one page and are not to be added and the fen has entered that it has put them on one page on purpose.**

---

And then the man of about fifty stood up at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fourth of this month with the ledger shut under his arm and made the argument, and it is the last argument he makes in this volume, and it is the same shape as the one he made on the fourth of last month and it has grown a limb.

"We are not protecting a record any more," he said. "**We are spreading a rumour of one.** That was true three weeks ago and it is truer now and I am not going to say it gently to anybody because I said it gently three weeks ago and I have had three weeks to think about it and I have not got a better sentence. **A rumour that is true and a rumour that is not true cannot be told apart from outside a yard, and a body that is in five places is a body with five things that have to be right, and we can read one of the five and the one we can read is a table.**"

"And here is the limb, and it is the only new thing I have got, and it has been in my head since the twenty-seventh of last month and I have not said it out loud to a person until now. **The office has told us in writing that what we say is not in question.** Four lines, on letterhead, in the ordinary course, and a sentence that says the truth of what this holding says is not in question, and every one of us in this yard has read it and I do not think one of us has slept properly since."

"**A body that has been declared unsafe for evidentiary use can still be published.** That is the whole of it and I have turned it over about nine times. Being unsafe is a fact about an office and not about a page. It is a fact about what an office may do with a page in a proceeding it has a part in, and it is not a fact about the page, and **the whole of this holding's book has been written for eleven years by people who did not have an office and would not have wanted one.** And the day a copy of it is standing in five places, the office can list the copies. **It can list them. It cannot unwrite them.** That is not a victory. **It is a thing an office cannot do, and I have been in this yard all season watching four things an office could do to us and this is the first one it cannot do, and I have not got a name for it and I am not going to give it one in a yard.**"

"So. **I do not know whether that is a victory and I am not going to call it one in a yard, and if anybody in this room says victory tonight I would like the clerk to enter that I said no to it.** And I am going to say the two sentences, and I am going to say them slowly, and I would like the reader to read them back twice and to put my initial against them and not against nothing, because I want them to be mine."

"**A thing which survives in more places than one office can hold is not safe. And it is not lost. And those are two different sentences, and this body is going to have to be able to say both of them out loud for the rest of its life, and on the day it can only say one of them it will have stopped being a body and started being a party.**"

Dunmar Fitch read both sentences back twice. Nobody thanked him and he said before he was thanked that he was not going to be thanked, and the clerk entered that he had asked for his initial against them and it went in against them and not against nothing.

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the fourth of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body, initialed against the entry and not against nothing:
>
> **A thing which survives in more places than one office can hold is not safe. And it is not lost. Those are two different sentences, and this body is going to have to be able to say both of them out loud for the rest of its life, and on the day it can only say one of them it will have stopped being a body and started being a party.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the reader of this body stated that he does not know whether that is a victory, said that he would not call it one in a yard, and asked the clerk to enter that he said no to it if anybody in the room said it. Nobody said it. The clerk entered that nobody said it.**

---

And then Tova Reed said the thing, once, in a room with about nine people in it, and she said that she had said it three times in a season and was going to say it a fourth, and she said that first.

"A mechanism is not a verification," she said. "**Soundness is not a hand.** I said that in this yard in a month two months back, standing up, and I have said it twice since, and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to number it and I am not going to make it into anything by being careful about the tone."

"**The distance between a mechanism and a hand is the whole of this volume.** Not this year. This volume. Nine months of a year in which a man read a row of eleven dressed cuts with a rule and a lens and put the wrong question back into his own mouth in front of witnesses, and a woman stood in a yard four hundred miles off and said that a thing that came down a penny would be a gift if anybody could say why, and a man of seventy-one said that a book that cannot be used is not a book anybody can put a thing in, and an office in nine miles wrote four lines that were correct, and the four households cut something off a row with a sickle and left it on the ground. **That is the volume. And the distance between a mechanism and a hand is still exactly where it was on the third of this month, and it is going to be exactly where it is on the tenth, and there is nobody in this room who has closed it and nobody in this room who can.**"

"And the volume is ending and the distance is still there, **and I am not going to be sad about that in a room with nine people in it, because the distance being there on the last day is the correct condition and a book that closed it would be a worse book and I would know it in about four years.** And I would like the reader to read that last sentence back and I would like him to read it back twice, and I am not going to be thanked, and everybody in this room who is about to say something kind about me is about to be wrong in the room as well as in the book."

Nobody said anything kind about her. The man of about fifty read the whole of it back twice and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, which is his standing, and about nine people were in that room and none of them improved on a word of it.

> Entered at the ninth hour of the evening of the fourth of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **the distance between a mechanism and a hand is the whole of this volume, and the volume is ending and the distance is still there, and the person who said it said that the distance being there on the last day is the correct condition and that a book which closed it would be a worse book.** Read back twice and not improved on. **Nobody was thanked and nobody improved on it, and the fen entered that this is the fourth time in a season the same standing has been taken in the same room and that the count is four and that no person in this holding has conceded any part of it to any other person.**

And then the fen entered the other thing, at about the tenth hour, and it entered it on its own motion, unasked, and it is the fourth of the four-word observations and it was never explained and nobody asked.

> Entered at the tenth hour of the evening of the fourth of the ninth month, by the clerk, on his own motion, unasked: **which of the two he believes.** The person who said the two sentences has voted against this body three times in a season and has said the best argument anybody in this fen has made for it and the worst sentence anybody in this fen has said about it inside nine days, **and the fen has entered that it does not know which of the two he believes, and has entered that it never will, and has not asked him, and is not going to.**

---

And on the fifth of the ninth month the copies went out on the ordinary business of the places they were going to, and the coat went on Tuesday with a licensed man in it, and the fen read out the count of the copies it is able to check, which is one, and the number of copies it is not, which is four, and the difference between those two numbers, which is the whole of the method and the whole of the defect, and the same thing.

"The table in the seed house yard is this body's own table and I can walk out of this room and put my hand on it," said Marek Vale, who had not spoken for about an hour. "**And Cray is nine hundred miles and Fennmere is nine and the coat is on a man walking to a plateau in four days, and the door is nine hundred yards off and I can read it, and it is not a copy.** So the count of copies this body can check today is **two**, the table and the door, and the count of copies it cannot is **three**, and there is no way in this world to make those two numbers the same, **and the man of about fifty has just stood in this yard and told us that a thing which survives in more places than one office can hold is not safe and is not lost, and I want to say the engineering half of that, because he gave the other half and nobody else did.**"

"**A copy is a thing that can be out of date.** That is not a feeling, it is what a copy is. A book that is in five places is five books, and four of them are books that this body cannot correct, and in four years one of them will be wrong in a way nobody finds out for a year. **That is the cost of the five places and it does not go away because the five places are good, and the only thing I can offer this yard is that the copy on the table is the one that gets corrected, and it gets corrected every time we open the book, and the other four have to live with whatever we did last.** And I would like somebody in four years to count how many times the copy on the table and the copy in Cray say the same thing about the seventh column, **and I would like that count to be small, and I am not going to bet anything on it and neither is anybody else in this room.**"

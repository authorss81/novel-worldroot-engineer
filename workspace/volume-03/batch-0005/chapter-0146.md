# Chapter 146

## A Movement And Not A Flight

Between the sixth and the seventh of the ninth month four carts and a hired waggon left Fennmere and came up the fen road, and on the seventh, at about the second hour of the afternoon, they were in this yard, and nobody in this yard sent for them and nobody in this yard knew what was on them.

They came up slowly and they came up in the rain and they came up with the second one behind the third, and the waggon was at the back with a tarpaulin on it and it went on in the afternoon, and **this body entered on the seventh that it does not know where it went and did not ask and that asking would have meant deciding something.**

The figures are in this book's because a miller nine miles off put them in a letter, and the fen entered the letter and not the man.

> From a miller at Fennmere, on the sixth of this month, on the ordinary post, four lines:
>
> **The low quarter has four wells off in it and has had for the season. Houses in it, forty-one, which is what it was.**
> **People at the bottom of it, four hundred and forty-one. The same quarter held three hundred on the eleventh of a month five months back.**
> **Water brought into it this month by cart, at a shilling a pail, by a man who is not a miller and whose name is not in this book: nine hundred and ten pails.**
> **The waggon that came up your fen road yesterday is not mine and I did not hire it and I do not know whose it is.**

Lissa Vale read that out whole in a yard of about twenty and Dunmar Fitch entered the four lines and entered that the fen entered the letter and not the man, **and entered that this holding cannot check one word of it, has standing over none of it, and did not write to ask, and the count of that not asking is now four in a season and the fen has entered the count and does not intend to stop entering it.**

"**Four hundred and forty-one people, and a hundred and forty-one more than were there five months ago, in a quarter with four wells off and nine hundred and ten pails bought in at a shilling the pail.** That is nine hundred and ten shillings. That is forty-five pounds and ten shillings, and it went into a quarter in a month on carts, and **the miller has told us that and not the quarter and not a person in it, and a man in this yard is going to say the word thousand and get it wrong by a factor of two, and I am not going to be one of the people who does that.**"

And then nobody in that yard was a hero and nobody in it was a refugee, and the man of about fifty said the two things in the wrong order on purpose and said so.

"I am going to say the second one first," he said, "**and I am going to say that I am doing it in the wrong order on purpose, and I would like the clerk to enter the reason before I say either of them, which is that if I say the first one first this yard will hear a noble thing and about nine people in it will be standing straighter by the second hour of the afternoon than they were at the first.**"

"**Second. A body that calls this a movement has found a way of being proud of a thing it did not do.** Four carts came up a road this holding did not lay and this body did not ask for and did not write to and has no standing to stop, and the seedwright in this room is not of this body and the man with the spade is four hundred miles off and the miller is nine miles off and not a member of anything, and this holding is a body of sixteen households on one head with a leak and a beat and a line of compost that came down by nothing this month. **There is nothing in this yard to be proud of and I would like it entered that I looked for something and there was not one.**"

"And **first, which is the one I would rather not say: a body that calls this a flight has found a way of being pleased about somebody else's cost.** A flight is somebody running. **A person is not running if they have a cart and a date and somebody to go to, and the difference between a flight and a movement is not courage and it is not anybody's character.** It is a word, and the word is the only difference, and whoever gets there first with the word gets to be the one who was right and the other one gets to be the one who should have stayed. **I have watched that happen in this book twice and I have watched it happen in a yard, and I am not going to let it happen in this yard while there are twenty people standing in it.**"

And then he said the third thing, and he said that it was the one the whole of that afternoon was for, and he said it slowly, and nobody in that yard interrupted him.

"**There are two hundred and eighty households on this fen road that have not moved and are not going to and have said so out loud.**"

"**And the fact that they have said so is not loyalty.** It is not loyalty to me and it is not loyalty to this holding and there is not one of the two hundred and eighty who owes this body a thing, and if a hundred and forty-one of them said it because of what a man in this yard said then that would be a different fact and it is not the fact. **It is not obstinacy**, because obstinacy is a thing a person does against somebody and two hundred and eighty households cannot be obstinate against a road. And **it is not evidence that this holding is worth anything.** They have not read our door. Most of them cannot. **They have not stayed because of us and they are not going to be counted as though they had, and if a person in four years finds the number two hundred and eighty in this book next to the four carts, they are going to be wrong to read it as a thing this holding did.**"

"So: **a book that puts the ones who went and the ones who stayed in one column is a book about a body and not about two hundred and eighty households**, and the count of households that went is not entered in this book because this body does not have it, and the number on the miller's sheet is people and not households, **and those are two numbers and they are not the same and this yard is not going to be told they are the same by anybody, including me.**"

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a count and of a refusal: **two hundred and eighty households on this fen road have not moved, have said so out loud, and are not moving. The count of households that have moved is not held by this body and was not asked for.** The person who said it stated that the fact of their staying **is not loyalty, is not obstinacy, and is not evidence that this holding is worth anything**, and that a book which puts the ones who went and the ones who stayed in one column is a book about a body and not about two hundred and eighty households.

---

And then the steward asked who loses, and it is her question and it is the one she has asked every day of this season, and the answer was the first one this holding has ever given to it that it could not finish.

"Who loses," said Lissa Vale. "**I have been at this four days and I have got a man who cannot say it, and I am going to ask him in front of twenty people and I am not going to let him have the easy version.**"

"**We cannot tell.** That is the answer and it is not a shrug and it is not a way of not doing the sum. **This holding has published every figure it has for eleven years and it cannot tell you what four carts up the fen road cost anybody, and it cannot tell you what a hundred and forty-one people in a quarter with four wells off cost, and it cannot tell you what nine hundred and ten pails at a shilling is to a household that has to buy every one of them, and it cannot tell you what two hundred and eighty households who stayed will be doing in a month.**"

"**And the price of a migration is not a figure this holding is entitled to and this holding is not going to produce it.** We have one account, of our own water, on one head, in one yard, and every figure in this book is a figure about something in this yard. **There is no column for that and I am not going to make one, and a body that makes a column for a thing it cannot see is a body that has started putting its arithmetic on other people's lives.** Enter that I asked and that the answer was that we cannot tell, and enter it in my hand, and **no recommendation is made and I have got nothing to recommend anybody with.**"

And then she asked for her initial to be put against nothing.

"**Enter that and do not put it, because it is not my standing to ask for.** It is the man of about fifty's standing, it is his, it has been his for four years, and I have used it twice this season when I was tired and I am using it now because I cannot stand what is happening and because a woman who cannot stand a thing asks for a piece of paper to stand on. **Enter that the man of about fifty's standing was used by somebody else in a yard today and that nobody corrected it.**"

Dunmar Fitch entered it. The man of about fifty did not correct it and was asked to read it back and read it back, and nobody said a word about it to her, and the fen entered that nobody did and entered that she was thanked by nobody and asked for nothing.

> Entered at the fifth hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **who loses. The answer given by the steward, in her own hand and read back by her: this body cannot tell.** The count of households that have moved is not held, the cost to any household is not held, the cost to the two hundred and eighty who stayed is not held, **and the price of a migration is not a figure this holding is entitled to and is not going to be produced by it. No recommendation was made and the steward has nothing to recommend anybody with.**
>
> **And entered on the same line: the steward asked that her initial be put against nothing. That is the reader of this body's standing and not hers. The clerk entered that the standing was used by somebody else in a yard on this day, that nobody corrected it, and that the reader of this body read the entry back and said nothing to her about it, and that nobody thanked her and that she asked for nothing.**

---

And then the destinations, which are three and one, and the one is going up a road this body has no standing on, and about eleven people were in that yard by then and the rain had stopped.

"**Three of the four carts have said where they are going and they have said it out loud in this yard and I have written it down and I am not going to argue with any of the three.** A woman of a household with the shilling has a sister nine miles past the second bridge and there is a bed there and there is not a field season there. A man with a spade and his brother have ground that is not this holding's and a room in it that is not paid for. And the second cart is going nowhere in particular and is going at the speed of a cart that has not decided, and its driver said so, and I wrote that down too."

"**And the fourth has not said.** It is going up the plateau road and it is the one that came in behind the third and it had nothing written on it that anybody in this yard could read, **and nobody in this yard has asked it where, and the fen entered that it did not ask, and the reason is that asking would have meant deciding something.**"

"And I want that reason written down in my own words because I have thought about it for an hour and I do not think it is a good enough reason. **The reason is that this body has spent a season finding out that it does not have standing over a household's ledger, a household's rows, a household's ground and a household's woman, and it has not stopped finding that out, and it has got into the way of asking anybody a question at all. That is not prudence. It is a body that has been told no four times and has stopped opening its mouth.** And I would like somebody in four years to look at the four times and decide whether the four times were right, **and I am not going to decide it myself in the last hour of an afternoon with the rain coming back.**"

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the seventh of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a thing this body did not do: **the fourth cart was not asked where it was going.** Three of the four have said and the three are entered. The fourth is going up the plateau road and this body has no standing on that road and did not ask and did not write. **The reason was given twice, in two wordings, and both wordings are entered: asking would have meant deciding something, and this body has been told no four times this season by four different people and has stopped opening its mouth, and the person who said it said that is not prudence and did not say what it is and did not know.**

Underneath the day's work, dated the seventh of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-first note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Four carts and a hired waggon up the fen road in the rain between the sixth and the seventh of this month, and this body sent for none of them, knew of none of them, and has no standing to stop any of them, and the waggon went on in the afternoon and this body does not know where and did not ask. A miller's letter of four lines, entered, and the fen entered the letter and not the man, and the count of things this holding has been told no by this season is four and it is entered and it is not going to stop being entered. Four wells off, forty-one houses, four hundred and forty-one people, three hundred five months back, nine hundred and ten pails bought in at a shilling, and the arithmetic of the pails done out loud and the word thousand refused by the woman who did it. A man of about fifty saying the second of two things before the first, on purpose, with his reason entered first, and then the third thing, which is that there are two hundred and eighty households on this fen road that have not moved and have said so out loud, and that the fact of their staying is not loyalty, is not obstinacy, and is not evidence that this holding is worth anything, and that a book putting the ones who went and the ones who stayed in one column is a book about a body. A steward who asked who loses and got the first answer this holding has ever given that it could not finish, and entered that the price of a migration is not a figure this holding is entitled to and is not going to be produced by it, and used a standing that is not hers and had it entered that nobody corrected it. Three destinations said out loud and one not asked about, and the reason for not asking given twice, once as a decision and once as a thing this body is not sure about.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

# Chapter 143

## The Thirtieth

The twenty-ninth of this month was a Sunday, and on the Sunday a sheet came down the four-day road and four blanks in a book were filled in, and that is the only thing that happened on the Sunday.

The sheet was written on the morning of the twenty-fifth at the fourth house, on the day, by the occupier, at her own cost, on a rider she hired at the change of horses, and it was in this yard at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-ninth, four days after the day it was written on, and **this body did not pay for it, did not arrange it, and has entered that it did not ask the price of it, and that it is the third time in a month it has been beaten home by a household's own expense and that it is not going to ask this time either and that the not-asking is now a practice and not a mood.**

> From the fourth house, on the twenty-fifth of this month, down the four-day road, four lines, in the occupier's hand. **Read out in the seed house at Lantern Reach at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-ninth, by the reader of this body, with the clerk present and about nine people in the room.**
>
> **One. The row was lifted on the twenty-fifth and nine of the eleven faces were read with a rule and a lens and two were not read and were not guessed at, and a stake is in the ground at each of the two.**
>
> **Two. The green along the first row was cut back off the collars with a sickle on the twenty-fifth, in one afternoon, by the man with the spade, and the cuttings were left on the ground.**
>
> **Three. The reason is that the ground is ours and the green is not, and that we are not going to pretend we know what it is and we are not going to leave it up because it is interesting, and those are two different reasons and we are giving you both.**
>
> **Four. Nobody told us to cut it. We have been asked that twice in a fortnight by two different men who were not of us and neither of you had told us anything and both of you wrote it down after you had.**

The reader read the fourth line out twice and did not answer it, and the fen entered that it did not answer it, and entered that it had already answered the same question out loud in a yard on the twenty-sixth from its own outgoing book, and **that it is going to let two answers to the same question stand in this book on two pages nearly five thousand words apart and is not going to make them agree, because one of them is a fact about what this body sent and the other one is a fact about what four households did and this body can only ever know the first of the two.**

And then he filled the four blanks, and Dunmar Fitch wrote them in, and the man of about fifty read the page back with the four blanks in it and read the same page back with the blanks filled in, and the fen entered both.

> Entered at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-ninth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **the four blanks entered in advance on the twenty-sixth were filled on the twenty-ninth from a sheet and from nothing else.** The page exists in this book in two states and both states are in this book and the reader read both back, **and the fen has entered that the blanks are closed and that closing a blank is not closing a question, and that the third of the four lines on the sheet is a reason given by the people who own the ground and not a finding and not to be improved on by anybody in this yard including the reader of this body.**

---

And the thirtieth was the last reading day of the eighth month, and it was a Monday, and the door was read out whole at the tenth hour in a full yard by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and there were about twenty-five people in it, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-nine.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the thirtieth of this month, in a full yard, by the steward. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month three months back, the fifth read twice.
>
> **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty-nine.** It was twenty-eight on the twenty-first of this month and twenty-seven on the sixteenth of it, and the count was checked by a clerk from the counter at Fennmere on the sixteenth and by nobody at all today, and the fen has entered that it is the third time in eleven years a count on that door has been read in a full yard without anybody from outside this holding in it, and that the fen does not mind, and that this is the first of the three that it does not mind.
>
> **The compost line for this month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month, which is a Tuesday, and this holding does not know what it is.** That is the eighth month of a run of seven that has not been discharged in seven, and it is the eighth month, and it is the last hour of it.

And then the two numbers that are cut, and the rule is that both of them are a day and a number and the number comes out of the day, and the man of about fifty made the clerk read the rule out loud before he read either figure, which is new, and which nobody asked for.

"The boards. **Day minus sixty-six.** The thirtieth of the eighth month is the two hundred and fortieth day of this season and two hundred and forty less sixty-six is one hundred and seventy-four, and the board says one hundred and seventy-four, and it said one hundred and sixty-four on the twentieth of this month and it is the same rule and it has been the same rule for eleven years. Cut into both this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years, in front of nobody, and then said out loud in this yard because that is the standing and not because anybody made it one."

"And the tin. **Day minus nineteen.** Two hundred and forty less nineteen is two hundred and twenty-one days, and the tin says two hundred and twenty-one days, and the tin is in a shut drawer in a press in this building under a ledger and it has not been moved and it is not to be moved, and the seed house's undertaking that it will not be moved stands and the trust's undertaking that it will not be asked stands, and neither has been asked anything this month and neither will be. **The drawer was not opened this month. The drawer is not to be.**"

And then the rule about the engineer of record, read out again in full, four lines, in a yard of about twenty-five, because a rule that is only read out when it is used is a habit and this one is not a habit.

> **The standing rule about the engineer of record. In force. Satisfied on the third of last month, in a room, with a licensed man writing it down, entered and read back.**
>
> **One.** The draw is not begun on ground whose return is not carrying.
> **Two.** It is to be stopped at four feet.
> **Three.** The stop is to be called out loud by a licensed man and entered by whoever holds the book.
> **Four.** The person who calls the stop is not the person doing the work.
>
> **Four lines. Every one of them is about a stop, or about calling a stop, or about who is standing there when it is called, and there is no fifth line and there never has been one.** The fen entered on the third that it was satisfied and that satisfaction is not permission. **The fen entered on the twelfth of this month that it was used, and entered that the rule permitted it, and entered that the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and entered that no draw has been attempted since the twelfth and that this body has not decided whether one will be and has entered that it has not decided.**

---

And then the handoff, at the north gate, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the thirtieth, in a yard with about twenty-five people in it, and it is this body's own handoff and **it has never in eleven years sent one to anybody, and it is sending one now because four things fall on a Tuesday and nobody in that yard is going to be able to remember four things on a Monday night in a wind.**

"**Four things, and I am going to say them slowly, and I would like the clerk to enter them as four and not as a list of about six.**"

"**One. The eighth month's compost line, on the first of next month, at about the eleventh hour, in a yard, written in a hand that is not the steward's.** This body does not know what that figure is. It has said so out loud on the first, the sixteenth, the twenty-first and the thirtieth of this month and it is going to say it again on Tuesday. **And the line will carry, entered separately and not folded into the shortfall: a balance of twenty-eight shillings standing against a man hired on the fourth of this month, whose pay-off day was never entered against him; two men hired in this month and paid off inside it, the ninth on the seventeenth and the twelfth on the twentieth; six days of a woman's keep over a fortnight, at one shilling and sevenpence for the six days and not a day for a day; and a second blanket at twopence.** All four of those are what this holding has spent on doing the work and **not one of them is a shortfall of compost and if anybody folds them into it then the line cannot be read in four years, and the woman who keeps the money will not do it and has not done it in eight months.**"

"**Two. A cart.** It is coming up the fen road and it is not this body's cart and this body has not sent for it and does not know what is on it. It will be in this yard on the seventh of next month and **about nine people in this yard have already been told that it is coming and not one of them has said a word to this body about it, and this body has entered that it did not ask, and that asking would have meant deciding something.**"

"**Three. A question about a ledger on a shelf four hundred miles off that this body decided on the second of this month not to ask.** It is on the book. **This body decided not to ask it and the decision is the same decision it entered on the second about not writing to that household and it is the same reason, and a question about another household's ledger that has to travel four days each way is not a question, it is a thing sent to arrive after the answer.** The clerk has it in the book twice and the reader read both back tonight and I have not asked whether it ought to be asked and I am not going to."

"And **four, and this one is not a date and it has been on this page since the thirtieth of last month, and I am going to read it out and then read it out again.** A man at the second house. An entry with no answer against it. **This body does not know where he is and has not asked, and he is owed nothing further and he is owed no answer, and this body is not going to go and look for him, and looking would have meant deciding something, and the reader of this body has now read that sentence back twice on this page and a third time at his own asking and I would like the clerk to enter that the third was at his own asking and not at mine.**"

Nobody said a word. The reader asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the yard was quiet in the ordinary way, and the wind off the ice came under the bay doors and went out again.

And then somebody said the question nobody else in that yard could have asked, and it was the reader of this body, and he gave the reason for asking it first, which is his practice.

"What do you want to happen on Tuesday."

And the steward answered him in six words and she did not add anything to them and she did not explain them, and they were the last thing said in that yard before the thirtieth closed.

"**I would like one ordinary month.**"

> Entered at the eighth hour of the evening of the thirtieth of this month, at the north gate, by the clerk, on the standing of a question and an answer: **what does this body want to happen on Tuesday. I would like one ordinary month.** The reader entered the answer as it was given, in six words, and did not ask what it meant and did not read it back a second time, and the fen entered that he did not read it back a second time and entered why, and the reason is that the sentence is not a figure and there is nothing on a page that can be got wrong about it.

Underneath the day's work, dated the thirtieth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the fortieth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*An eighth month closed on its own figures and handed four things to a Tuesday. A door read whole at the tenth hour in a full yard of about twenty-five, the seventh column at twenty-nine, and the count checked by a clerk from a counter on the sixteenth of this month and by nobody today, and the fen entered that it does not mind. A compost line not entered for the fourth time in the month with the figure not in the book and the first of next month a Tuesday and a run of eight months not discharged in eight. Four blanks entered in advance on the twenty-sixth and filled on the twenty-ninth from a sheet the household wrote on the day at its own cost on a rider it hired itself, four days, and this body beaten home by a household's own expense for the third time in a month and not asking the price for the third time either. Nine faces read with a rule and a lens and two not read and not guessed at and a stake at each of the two. A green cut off the collars with a sickle in one afternoon and the cuttings left on the ground, and the reason given twice over, once about a field and once about four people with the same imagination, and a fourth line saying that nobody told them. Two numbers cut and read out with the rule read out first, one hundred and seventy-four days and two hundred and twenty-one days, and a tin unmoved with both undertakings standing and a drawer not opened and not to be. Four lines about a stop, read out whole in a yard because a rule read only when it is used is a habit, satisfied on the third, used on the twelfth, silent about what comes after the stop, and no draw since and none decided. A handoff at a gate that this body has never sent anybody, four things on a Tuesday, and among them a man at the second house with an entry with no answer against it and no name in this book and a third reading of a sentence at his own asking. And six words, at the end, from a woman who sends a page to a counting house nine hundred miles off every month and did not know this month whether it arrived.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

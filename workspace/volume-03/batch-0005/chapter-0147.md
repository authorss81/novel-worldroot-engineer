# Chapter 147

## The Fourth Line

The eighth of the ninth month was a Tuesday and nothing was read out in it, and the copy of the seed-route ledger that was inside a licensed man's coat went up the plateau road on it, and about four hundred miles off on a shelf a day letter came off a stone at a well at the fourth house and did not go back on, and neither of those things is in this book and both of them are this holding's business and the fen entered that it knows of both of them and has not been told of either.

And on the ninth of the ninth month, in the seed house, at about the second hour of the afternoon, with the bay doors open and about eleven people in the room, a fourth line was added to a list that has been in the book for eleven years and has never had four lines in it.

> Entered at the second hour of the afternoon of the ninth of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a standing list, read out whole and read back whole, with the hour on it:
>
> **What this body holds.**
> **What this body is owed.**
> **What this body does not know.**
> **What this body is looking after and cannot reach.**

The first three have been there since the first year and have been read out in a yard on the first, the sixteenth and the thirtieth of every month since before anybody in this room was born. The fourth is about a walled yard at the first house on a shelf four hundred miles off, and it names no person and no thing in it and no number of things in it, and it is a line and not a sentence, and the clerk entered that it is a line and not a sentence and read it back that way.

And the man of about fifty said the difference, and he said it in about ten words and he would not give it any longer than that, and about eleven people were in that room.

"**Not knowing is a hole. Looking after is a debt.** And they are two different lines and the second one is new, and the difference is a hand and a distance, and I am not going to put a figure on the distance because the figure is four hundred miles and I have used that figure all season and it is not the distance."

> Entered at the third hour of the afternoon of the ninth of the ninth month, in the seed house, by the clerk, at the request of the reader of this body: **not knowing is a hole, and looking after is a debt, and the difference is a hand and a distance.** The reader stated that he would not put a figure on the distance and gave the reason, **which is that the figure on the page is four hundred miles and the distance is not four hundred miles.** The fen entered that the fourth line names no person and no thing in it and **that the words *what this body is looking after* carry a person in them by a form of words and that the form of words is the fen's own and was written today and is not evidence of anything except that this holding has a custom.**

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the clerk entered the count of that request and the count is fourteen this season, and the fen entered the count and entered that the count is not a complaint.

---

And then the question, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in the same room, and it is in this body's own words, and the words do not name an office and do not name a machine and do not name a person and do not name a year, and it was written out, read out whole, read back whole, and the hour went on it, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the ninth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk, on the standing of a question, in this body's own words:
>
> **This body has carried for a season the question of who told a row of trees on a shelf four hundred miles off to do a thing in a week. Eleven cuts were read with a rule and a lens, and nine of them were dated, and two were not read and were not guessed at, and the two have not been read and are not going to be read by anybody in this holding this season.**
>
> **This body cannot tell whether the telling is still going on. It struck that question out of its own mouth in front of witnesses on the twenty-third of a month two months back, and it entered that the question in this book is the worse of the two, and the question in this book is whose order it is.**
>
> **It was asked out loud in a yard on the twentieth of last month by a licensed man and it was not answered. This body has entered the count of the times it has asked it. The count is two, and neither of the two has been answered.**
>
> **This body is not going to find out. This body is going to keep asking. And a book that says that in its own hand is a book a person in four years can hold against it.**

Nobody answered it. Nobody in that room was asked to answer it, and the clerk entered that nobody was asked and that nobody answered, and the fen entered that **the count of times the question has been available to this room and not been taken is now three, and the first was on the twenty-third of a month two months back, and the second was on the twentieth of last month in a yard of about twenty, and this is the third and the room is smaller and the question is the same one.**

And Marek Vale read it out himself, and read it back out of the book, and put his hand flat on the bench, and said the half he says.

"**I did not strike it out because I found a better question.** I struck it out because I could not carry both and I could not answer either, and I said so at the time and I have not improved on it since. **And the question in that entry is worse than the question it replaced, and the fen has entered that it is worse, and it is still worse this afternoon, and a person in four years is going to read the two lines and think the man who wrote them had an idea at the end and I did not have an idea at the end. I had a hole and then I had a worse hole.** And that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to pretend to eleven people in a seed house that it is more than that."

---

And the tenth of the ninth month was the last reading day and it was a Thursday, and the door was read out whole at the tenth hour in a yard with about twenty people in it, by the steward, with the reader and the clerk, and the count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty, and none of the figures was softened.

> Well house door, read at the tenth hour of the tenth of this month, in a full yard. The schedule, all seven columns, the declared figure and then the measured figure, and the route line. The notice, four lines. The four sheets, four parts, part two read twice. The refusal clause, from the north gate, in the fen's own hand under the seven columns. The six clauses of the second of a month three months back, the fifth read twice. **The count of the readings of the seventh column went to thirty.**
>
> **The compost line for the ninth month, not entered, and the reason entered, and the reason is that the figure falls on the first of next month and this holding does not know what it is.** The ninth month of a run of eight that has not been discharged in eight.
>
> **The leak.** Five-eighths of an inch, read at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of last month, twice, two rules, pump on, water into the housing and not into the pit, the pit not up. **It has no due date and none is to be invented, nobody has looked for a cause and none is entered, and this body will not say whether that figure has moved and has not said in this volume.**
>
> **The beat.** Logged, cause not established, nothing written underneath it, not logged since, **column open, and the fen has not opened it with about twenty people standing in a yard.**
>
> **The boards. One hundred and eighty-four days.** Day minus sixty-six, the two hundred and fiftieth day of this season, cut into both boards this morning by the same person who has cut them for eleven years and said out loud in this yard.
>
> **The March's tin. Two hundred and thirty-one days.** Day minus nineteen, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, **both undertakings standing, the seed house's that it will not be moved and the trust's that it will not be asked. The drawer was not opened this month and is not to be.**
>
> **The standing rule about the engineer of record.** In force, four lines, every one of them about a stop, satisfied on the third of a month two months back, **used once, on the twelfth of last month, and the rule permitted it and the rule says nothing whatever about what comes after the stop, and no draw has been attempted since and this body has not decided whether one will be.**
>
> **The well on the shelf.** Five feet and two inches at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of last month, by the occupier, by her own nail, in her own yard. Three and a half feet of the shortfall against a man and four inches against nobody. **There is no entry anywhere for that well coming back and this body will not be predicting one.**
>
> **And a row of a hundred and forty-one trees four hundred miles off, out of whose collars a green came up on the sixteenth of last month and was cut back off with a sickle on the twenty-fifth of it and the cuttings left on the ground. Nobody has read it. Nobody has named it. There is no entry anywhere for that row either, and this body will not be predicting one.**

---

And the man of about seventy was fetched on the afternoon of the tenth of this month, the eleventh time in a season this body has brought him out to a thing, and he was given the fourth line and the question and the two sums and the two sentences, in that order, and he asked for the fourth line to be repeated and then he asked for the last line of the question to be repeated, and then he said the shortest thing he has ever said in this yard, and it is in the book, and it is not to be explained, and nobody explained it.

"Write it again next year."

> Entered at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the tenth of this month, in the seed house, by the clerk: **a man of seventy-one was brought out for the eleventh time in a season, was given the fourth line and the question, and asked for the fourth line to be repeated and then for the last line of the question, and said: write it again next year.** **The fen entered that it is the shortest thing he has said in this yard in eleven years, that nobody explained it, that he was not asked what it was about and was not going to be, that he was not thanked and did not ask to be, and that the fen does not know what it means and has entered that it does not know and is not going to find out this season.**

Underneath the day's work, dated the tenth of this month, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the forty-second note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*A month and a volume closed on their own figures, none of them softened. A fourth line added to a standing list of three, in this body's own words, in a room with about eleven people in it, being what this body is looking after and cannot reach, and the difference between that and the third line given in ten words by a man who refused to put a figure on the distance. A question put in this body's own words, naming no office and no machine and no person and no year, with the count of the times it has been asked entered at two and neither answered, and the count of the times it has been available and not taken entered at three, and nobody in that room asked to answer it and nobody answered it. A door read whole at the tenth hour in a yard of about twenty and the seventh column at thirty. No compost line, a leak with no due date and nothing said about whether it has moved, a beat logged once with the column open, boards at one hundred and eighty-four days, the tin at two hundred and thirty-one days unmoved with both undertakings standing and a drawer not opened, a rule of four lines used once and satisfied and silent about what comes after the stop, a well four hundred miles off at five feet and two inches with no entry anywhere for it coming back, and a row of a hundred and forty-one trees with no entry anywhere for that row either. A man of seventy-one, eleventh in a season, saying four words.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

And then, in the same hand, on the same page, underneath the note and the standing sentence, one paragraph, and it is the only paragraph in eleven years of this book that is not addressed to anybody and has no reader named at the end of it and was not read back before it was initialled because there was nobody to read it back to.

*This volume did the following, in the ordinary words, with no figure left out of it and none softened. It went four hundred miles off the fen and read a row of eleven dressed cuts with a rule and a lens and dated nine of them and did not read two. It found a stack of sixty-one bales that a woman of fifty-four opened herself, and three living grafts in the waste, and one bevel in two places. It took a draw out of a plateau and paid for it in a well four hundred miles off that has not come back, and it took a second draw out of a fen and paid for it in sixteen households' water, and the second was the worse one and it was done in the low ground of this holding on purpose. It sent four copies down two roads into four hands and could read one of them. It had a class of record put against two of its own records without being told which two, and a declaration four lines long that said the truth of what this holding says is not in question, and it did not know what to do about that and said so in a yard. It had a question change in it in the middle and the question got worse and the worse one is in the book now and is not answered. It found a green come up out of the collars of a row that four households had let go, and did not read it, and four households cut it off with a sickle and left it on the ground, and this holding was four days late and entered the lateness. It put its compost line on a page on the first of a month nine times running, eight of them not discharged, and on the ninth month of that run the price came down a penny and nobody can say why. It let four carts go up a fen road it did not lay, and two hundred and eighty households stayed and said so out loud, and the price of that is not a figure this holding is entitled to. And it ends on a question it is not going to answer, in a book with no charter, and a man of seventy-one told it to write it again next year. The note is the last one in the chain because a book with no charter can still go on being written, and a book that has no charter and no figures and no one to read them is a different thing.*

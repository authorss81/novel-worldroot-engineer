# Chapter 768

## The Twenty-First Of The Twenty-First, And Six People Stopping The Deciding In Three Quarters Of An Hour While The Water Went On Going Down The Same Channel It Had Been Going Down Since Before Any Of Them Were Born

Roan Selk came up the low road at about the sixth hour with mud to the knee and his roll of the copy back inside his coat where it had been all night, and he gave two figures at the gate to the boy who carries water before he had taken his coat off, and he gave them in a different order from anybody else there.

**"Five hundred and thirty-first morning of the run, and one thousand and four hundred and four hundredweight came down the channel in the night. That is the smaller one by five, and it comes down five on every morning with an odd number on it and up eight on every morning with an even one, and I have watched it from the top of the fourth path for four days and I am not going to pretend I worked out which is which.** He pulled the coat off one arm. **"I will tell you what I could see from down there and it is not much. I could see the water. I could not see the end of anything, and I am not going to describe a view to nine people who came from a yard with a wall in it."**

He put the roll on the middle table and did not unroll it, and nobody in the yard unrolled it, and it lay there all day with the four stones from the schedule on the edge of the same table nine inches from it.

The upper gate was shut at about the sixth hour and stayed shut, and the middle gate was opened to the width of a hand and left there, and the gauge was read and the reading said out loud before anything else whatever happened, and the draw was left standing for the length of a slow cup and no longer, and the gauge was read a second time and both readings were said out loud with nothing added to either of them.

**"Eleven minutes on the width, and I counted them out loud because I was the only one of the six of us who could be relied on to say the same number twice, and Emrys Dole had it at eleven and Perrin Dae had it at eleven and I had it at eleven, and that is the first time this year that three men in this holding have said the same number about anything."**

Then it went on. The water did not stop. The water went down the same channel it had gone down since before any of the six of them were born, at the same rate to within what Nia Vale could hold on a gauge in a rising wind, and anybody standing at the north end of that yard at about the seventh hour could have put a hand in it and would have felt it move.

Six of them did it and Marek Vale was not one of the six and was not on the road and was not sent for and did not come. He was at the north end of the ring at a quarter past six with a crew and a bucket and he stayed there until about the ninth hour, and about four people there noticed that he was not at the gate for the whole of it and about twenty-one did not.

> **WHAT WAS DONE TO THE HEAD OF THE CHANNEL THIS MORNING, IN THE ORDER IT WAS DONE, BY SIX PEOPLE. NOT BY HIM.**
>
> **Sixth hour.** Upper gate shut. It stays shut the whole time. Nobody opened it to hurry anything. Ismay Rooke.
>
> **Sixth hour, eleven minutes after that.** Middle gate opened to the width of a hand and left at the width of a hand. Emrys Dole, who has done this eleven years.
>
> **Before anything else whatever happened.** Gauge read and the reading said out loud. Nia Vale. It was read again at the end and both readings were said out loud with nothing added to either.
>
> **The whole time.** Draw left standing for the length of a slow cup and no longer. Perrin Dae, counting aloud.
>
> **After that.** Nothing at the head of the channel is deciding anything.
>
> **What was not done.** No water was stopped. No channel was cut and none was broken and none was taken apart. Nothing was closed that the water has to go through. There is more water in this yard this morning than there was yesterday morning and it is going the same way it was going.
>
> **What it is.** Stopping the deciding and leaving the water running. That is all it is and it is not the same as having emptied a channel and it is not the same as having finished anything.

Nobody signed that sheet and nobody put his name on it, and it went up on the boards with a stone on the top corner, and about nine people read it and four or five of them read the fourth line twice.

Emrys Dole said one thing about it at the middle gate, with his hand on the wheel, to a mason and not to the yard.

**"Eleven years I have stood here and opened this to the width of a hand and there has always been somebody behind it deciding something. There is not somebody behind it deciding something this morning. That is the whole of what has happened and I want it written down in plain words by somebody who was not me, because in about a week there will be four people in this yard telling each other it was a shut-off and it was not a shut-off."**

**"What do you call it, then."**

**"I call it a Sunday.** He let the wheel go. **"No. I call it what the sheet says it is, because a man who invents a word for it on a Monday will have invented six of them by the middle of next month and then none of them will fit anything."**

Sera Quill came back into that yard at about the seventh hour and nobody asked her for the slate and nobody asked her for anything at all, and she stood at the wall for about eleven minutes doing nothing, and then a woman from the middle row asked her whether the count in force had moved, and Sera Quill said that it moved on the mornings it moved on and not on the mornings it did not, and that she would be at the boards when it moved, and the woman asked her which mornings were those.

**"You have known the answer to that for four years and you have not liked it,"** Sera Quill said, **"because it is the same answer either way and it has nothing to do with what you did or did not do this week.** And she took the slate out after that and looked at the front of it and put it back in her apron without showing it to anybody, and about four people were watching the slate and not the woman.

Nia Vale read the gauge twice and gave the second reading to about fourteen people standing at the north end, in an order in which the water came last.

**"Four hundred and ninety-eight of window between the two halves, and the short one came this morning at two hundred and forty-four and the tall one stood at two hundred and fifty-four, and the short one is the one that comes on a morning like this one.** She wiped the face of the gauge with the back of her wrist. **"And both readings are the same to what I can hold in this wind and I have said them both out loud and I have added nothing to either of them, and if anybody in this holding writes down that the flow changed this morning I will read that sentence out to them myself, because the flow did not change. What changed is that nobody chose it."**

Perrin Dae gave the long sheet in the yard rather than off the wall, without a cloth and without stopping what he was carrying.

**"The five hundred and twenty-eighth is the figure with the rule under it, the five hundred and twenty-ninth stands at the top of that sheet, and the five hundred and thirtieth is at the foot of it, and I have said them in that order because I have no business reading a wall in the order the man who reads it likes because I am not the man who reads it and I have no business improving on him.** He put the barrow down. **"And I will say the other thing since it is nobody's job but mine to be tiresome about. Six people wrote seven steps on a sheet and one line on that sheet said the results have to be read out in a yard first, and the results have not been read out in a yard, and I am not going to be the one who decides whether that line still binds anybody."**

Kellan Rusk came out with the register form under his arm and the key on its nail and read the two stones off the wall himself, which he does perhaps twice a season.

**"Nine hundred and fifteen at the near end, nine hundred and sixty-two at the top, and I wrote them down in that order on my own hand because I am not going to ask a woman with a barrow to do it twice on a morning when she has come up with her sleeves wet.** He looked at the two figures on his own hand for a second. **"Four hundred and twenty-five on the form. Not entered. And I have read that valve sheet and there is no line on it about the door behind me and the key on the nail, and I would like that noticed as well, because there is now a record in this holding of the head of the channel and there is no record in it of the one room, and both of those are the same size of thing and only one of them has a foot on it."**

Tova Reed gave her four from the step with her back to the trays and in an order that is not the one she has used for six weeks.

**"Three hundred and thirty-one on the charter and it is the oldest thing any of us will say out loud today. Four hundred and twenty-five on the form, which is the same figure the man at the door has just said, and there are now three people in this holding who can tell you that figure and I am one of them and I have said it out loud and I am not going to be adding it to anything.** She turned the corner of her board out and let it lie flat against her hip. **"The letter is at two hundred and twenty-eight, and it has been going up all week and I have said seven times this week that the higher it goes the less it is worth, and the seven of those were a waste of everybody's morning including mine. And the ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and nineteen and it is blank and there is going to be nothing on it at four hundred and twenty tomorrow, and nobody has ever put anything on it and nobody in this yard is going to."**

The girl from the second place had the north board done before anybody else in that yard was properly dressed. Four hundred and fifty-five went under the rule first and then two hundred and twelve went over the top of it, and she waited while a man from the fourth place finished a sentence at the middle table before she came away from the wall, and there has been nobody at that trough for a long time.

In the one room the blanks on the wall stood at thirty-nine with the rule under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the drawer was shut at every hour with the key on its nail, and four sheets lay on the long table with nothing entered on any of them and nothing refused and no column cut under any of them.

Nobody cut a column on the seed board this morning to show what has come out of a compost line that has not come out of anything.

And at about the eleventh hour the deciding had been stopped for a length of time four people in that yard could say out loud and the water was still going down the same channel at the same rate, and nobody at the boards could have told you the water was different, and nobody was thanked, and the roll of the copy was still lying rolled up on the middle table nine inches from four stones with nobody's hand on either of them.

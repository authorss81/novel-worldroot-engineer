## 4. THE SEVEN SPELLINGS, PRINTED ONCE HERE, AND THE EIGHTH FORM

**Seven, and you must be able to count them.**

1. **The hundred range.** One hundred to one hundred and ninety-nine is *a hundred and* the word. **The read-aloud numerator in its range is *one hundred and* the word and is a different figure with a different form.** An ordinal in the one-hundreds takes *one hundred and*, because *a hundred and* has no room for an ending, and at exactly one hundred the ending goes on the whole word, which gives *one hundredth*. **THE TWO RECKONINGS TAKE *ONE HUNDRED AND* IN THEIR ONE-HUNDREDS.**
2. **The hundreds and above to nine hundred and ninety-nine** is the word, hundred, and the word, bare at a round hundred.
3. **The thousands.** One thousand and above keeps **both** ands: *one thousand and four hundred and nine hundredweight.* The unit word is a separate word with a space before it and no hyphen: *one thousand and four hundred and one hundredweight.* The attached form is withdrawn by name and returns zero.
4. **Tens are hyphenated and round tens are not.** *Twenty-one.* *Ninety.* *Three hundred and forty.*
5. **Ordinals take the ending on the whole word at a round hundred and hyphenate a tens-ordinal.** *Five hundredth.* *Five hundred and seventeenth.* *Five hundred and thirty-second.* **A writer who writes *five hundred and first-th* has written a figure nobody in this holding uses.**
6. **No month name in any morning and no month number in any spoken line.** The modal verb is not a month. An ordinal date appears only inside a heading that names an ordinal.
7. **The split window half and the read-aloud numerator are two figures and are never added and never confused.**

**AND THE EIGHTH FORM, WHICH IS NEW IN FIFTEEN VOLUMES, AND IT IS ALREADY SPENT AND NOT ON ANY MORNING OF YOURS: A ROUND HUNDRED IN THE THOUSANDS IS BARE, AND IT IS *ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED* WITH NO *AND* IN IT.** It fell on day nine hundred and seventy-four, on the twenty-sixth morning of the volume, and it is printed bare in two places on that morning, and the wrong form with an *and* in it is in one man's mouth there and is not corrected out loud. **THE THIRD LAUNDER STANDS AT ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND ONE ON YOUR FIRST MORNING AND KEEPS BOTH *AND*S ON EVERY MORNING OF YOUR TEN, AND A MORNING THAT DROPS AN *AND* OUT OF THAT SERIES ANYWHERE IN YOUR TEN HAS A DEFECT IN IT.**

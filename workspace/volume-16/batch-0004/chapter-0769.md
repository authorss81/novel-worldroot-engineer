# Chapter 769

## The Twenty-Second Of The Twenty-First, And Everybody In The Yard Being Able To See That There Is Nobody At The Head Of The Channel And Nobody In That Yard Doing Anything About It

The thing nobody said out loud on that Monday, and the thing about nine people said out loud on the middle road and about twenty-one did not say anywhere, is that there is now no operator at the head of the channel and there has not been one since the sixth hour on the day before, and that this is visible.

Not a figure on a wall. Visible. You can stand in the yard at any hour of that day and look at the middle gate standing at the width of a hand with nobody's hand on the wheel, and you can look at the boards and see the valve sheet on them with a stone on the top corner, and you can look at the middle table and see a roll of paper that has not been unrolled since it was put down, and between those three things there is not one person doing anything at the head of that channel, and any man, woman or child there could walk up to it and put a hand on it.

About four people did walk up to it. One of them was a mason from the third place who stood at the middle gate for about a minute and a half and put his hand on the wheel and did not move it, and then went and laid a course.

A man from the middle row got as far as about eleven feet from the boards with the question ready in his mouth and said it out loud to nobody in particular, twice, and got the same silence both times.

Then he said it properly, in the yard, to the man who lays for three councils.

**"Somebody has to stand there. That is all I am asking. Not a name on a sheet and not a term and not a season. Somebody who stands at that gate at the sixth hour and at the dark and who knows the width of a hand is the width of a hand.** He had a piece of paper in his hand and he did not unfold it. **"I have four households on this branch and two of them have a tank at half and one of those four is a woman of sixty-eight who has carried every jug of it up that lane herself since before you were at this gate, and I am not asking for a fix and I am not asking anybody to pay for anything. I am asking whether the wheel at the middle gate has a person near it in the morning."**

The man who lays for three councils put his trowel down on the wall and wiped his hands, which takes longer than putting them down.

**"No,"** he said. **"It does not, and I have watched it not have one for two mornings, and I am not going to be the one who tells you what that means because I do not know.** He picked the trowel up again. **"And I will tell you what I know. If I say yes, I have said it in a yard, and then I am the man who said it in a yard, and every morning after this one that anything goes wrong up there comes to the step of this yard looking for me, and there is nothing at the top of that channel that can be fixed by a man being found. I have laid eleven courses this week. Do you want my name or do you want somebody at the wheel."**

**"I want somebody at the wheel."**

**"Then you have asked me the wrong question and I have given you the right answer to it, which is no, and you can go and ask somebody else and I will not be offended and I will give you the same one.** He laid the next course. **"And if it does sit empty for long enough, then it is going to sit empty because it is nobody's, and that is a different thing from it sitting empty because nobody has been appointed, and I would like anybody in this yard to know which of the two they are looking at."**

Sera Quill had the slate out before the seventh hour and gave the count the way she gives it, which is first and in the same tone as the weather, and about six people were already in the yard when she did it.

**"A hundred and seventy-two in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty-three not."**

**"That is higher than it was."**

**"Up one, and I have a slate and not two slates, and one of the two numbers I carry in my head is going to stay in my head this morning the way it has every morning this one has been on.** She turned the slate over and read the one that goes in the book. **"One hundred and fifteenth.** That is the one and it is thirteen from the other one, and the other one is not on this morning and has not been on any morning this one has been on, and I have said that out loud to this yard five times this week and I am not going to say it a sixth time to a yard that has stopped asking."

**"What has the number got to do with the wheel at the middle gate."**

**"Nothing,"** Sera Quill said. **"That is exactly why I give you two figures and let you look at both of them and work out which you would rather have had, and today you would rather have had one, and it has been moving up one at a time all week for reasons that have nothing to do with anybody in this yard.** She put the slate in her apron. **"Put a person at that gate if the district can find one. It will not move this by one and it will not move it by anything, and you may both of those be true on the same morning, which is the part that is hard."**

Nobody at the boards was appointed over the head of the channel on that Monday, and nobody was asked to be, and about four people there said the words *somebody has to* and got as far as the rest of the sentence, and stopped.

The boy who carries water came in off the sluice road at about the sixth hour with two buckets and gave the weight to a mason before he had put the buckets down.

**"The board says one thousand and four hundred and twelve hundredweight, and that is up eight on a morning with an even number on it, and the run stands at its five hundred and thirty-second morning. I am not the man who keeps the book and I read it off the board on the way past, and I am telling you that so that nobody in this yard spends the morning wondering who I am.** He put the buckets down. **"And it went up eight and I have heard two men in this yard already say it went down five, so I have said it first and that is the only reason I am saying it at all."**

Emrys Dole read the window from the middle gate with his hand still on the wheel, which nobody asked him to do and which he has done every morning since the frost.

**"Four hundred and ninety-nine between them. Two hundred and fifty-five came and two hundred and forty-four stood. The tall half moves on a morning with an even number on it and the short one moves on a morning with an odd number, and I have watched the man on the wall get that backwards twice this season and he is not a fool, he is a man who reads a number off a wall with one eye.** He did not move the wheel. **"That is the second time this month I have said the direction out loud and both of the times it was right and neither of the times anybody wrote it down."**

The man who lays for three councils read the long sheet off the wall at about the ninth hour between courses, with mortar on his hands, which he has never done and which made about four people in the yard stop and look at him.

**"The number at the head is five hundred and thirty. The one under the line is the five hundred and twenty-ninth and the one over it is the five hundred and thirty-first.** He wiped his hands on his apron and put one finger on the head of the sheet. **"And I will tell this yard the thing I have said out loud twice and been ignored twice, which is that these three numbers have a third number and none of us will ever be shown it, because it is not on the wall and it is not on any paper, and the only place in this holding it exists is in the head of the man who wrote it, and he is at the north end this morning."**

**"Then you think he should write it down."**

**"I think I think about it more than is good for a man laying courses,**" he said, and went back to the wall.

Roan Selk read the two stones off the boards himself at about the tenth hour without asking anybody to hold anything up, and wrote the top one on the back of his own hand with a nail.

**"The near stone reads nine hundred and sixteen and the stone at the top reads nine hundred and sixty-three, and I put the far one on the back of my own hand because I would not ask a man to hold a board for me three times in one season.** He put the nail away. **"I have come up the low road twice this week and I am going to keep coming up it, and I am not coming up it to be asked to agree with anything and I am not coming up it to be given a piece of this. There is a wheel at the middle gate with nobody at it and I have looked at it from both ends of that channel now, and I am not going to tell this yard what I think ought to happen at it, because the last man who thought that in this yard was standing at a middle table four mornings ago saying no."**

Tova Reed came out with the corner of the board under her arm and gave her four in an order she has not used since the beginning of the year, and she stood where she could see the mouths of about nine people at once, which is a thing she can do and does not explain.

**"The ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and twenty and it is blank, and I have said that number before the other three for twenty days running this week and somebody in this yard thinks I have lost my place. The letter is two hundred and twenty-nine. The form is four hundred and twenty-six. The charter is three hundred and thirty-two.** She held the corner flat against her hip. **"A round hundred on that line is not a mark. I have been asked twice this week whether I would put one there, by two people who want me to put one there for reasons of their own, and it is not a mark and it is not an opportunity and it is not going to be one."**

Nobody asked her a third time.

A return came and went that Monday and it moved nothing, as a return has not moved anything in this holding for four years, and no ordinal and no weight and no allowance of any kind was printed anywhere for it, and about four people in the yard knew a return had been and nobody asked which one.

In the one room the blanks on the wall stood at thirty-nine, and the rule under those blanks stood open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and it had been open at the thirty-ninth time every day since the wall was drawn, and about four people went past it that morning and read it and nobody put anything under it. The door of that room was shut at the sixth hour and shut again at the dark, and the key was back on its nail at both.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped, and the man pushing it said eleven out loud to nobody as he set the shafts down, because he has said it every morning this season and he is not going to stop on the morning the wheel has nobody at it.

The ladder was against the tool house wall where it has stood since before the frost, and it carried nobody's weight that Monday, and the patch of bare ground that the wall came out of was stepped round all day by people going to and from the pump.

Nobody cut anything on the seed board that Monday, and there was a quarter of an hour before noon when the yard was the only quiet place in four counties and nobody used it for a column.

And at about the eleventh hour the middle gate was still standing at the width of a hand with no hand on it, and a fourth return had come and gone and printed nothing, and the count in force stood at a figure with a taken half and a not half that nobody in that yard argued about, and nobody had been put at the head of the channel and nobody had asked to be, and the second place was still without water and had been named nowhere on any board in that yard that Monday morning.

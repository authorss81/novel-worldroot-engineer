# Chapter 774

## The Twenty-Seventh Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman Being Put Under Public Custody In The Middle Of The Day In A Yard, Alive, With Everything She Had Done Read Out Of A Sheet By A Man Who Had To Read The Worst Of It Out Loud

Two councils met at that gate at about the ninth hour on a Saturday morning in full daylight with about forty people standing in the yard behind them, which is not how it is done anywhere north of the second place and was done anyway because one of the two of them said that if it was done indoors then nobody in the holding that it concerned could stand in the same air as it.

The clerk the two councils had sent for and were paying between them sat on the step of the one room with a single sheet on his knee and the door behind him shut and the key on its nail, and he was about thirty and he had never read anything out in a yard before.

They had her there at about the tenth hour. She came on foot again, with nobody behind her this time, and she was not searched, and nobody there put a hand on her, and a woman from the middle row told a mason from the third place not to put his hand on her and the mason put it in his pocket.

She stood where she could see everybody's faces, which is what she has done every time she has been in this yard.

The clerk read it. It is a sheet and it is not in this holding's record and it was not entered in this holding's record, and nobody in this holding entered it, and the two councils between them are answerable for every line of it and not this yard.

**"Item one.**" He had to start twice. **"Between four years ago and this spring, three documents were sent from the office of the Chancellor to this holding. None was entered. None was refused. None was signed. One was carried down a lane by hand and put on the middle table and was not picked up, not dated, not entered, not filled in at the foot and not withdrawn, and it is lying on that table this morning.** A woman from the third place said *yes* and about nine people said nothing. **"Item two. The same paper was offered twice. On the first occasion and on the second occasion every line of it was the same except one line at the foot, and the second of those two occasions carried that line already written in."**

**"Say the line."**

**"I am required to read what was done and not what anybody meant,**" the clerk said, and looked at the sheet, and read it. **"Item three. The short measure on the outer branches was to remain, and the short measure was described in the paper as triage. Under item four the central harvest of the completed phase was to be guaranteed in writing for as long as the standing was held. Under item five no council under that standing was to be able to reopen a branch. Under item six the Crown was to be given continuous access to the regional ledger, and it was stated in the paper that it was not a copy and not a report."**

Then he read the parts that were hard, and he had been told to read them, and he said so before he read them.

**"Item seven. I am required to read this next part and I am going to read it and I am not going to stop in the middle of it. The guarantee in item four was real. It was not a figure that could not be honoured. It was assessed by two offices and it would have fed the centre in this region in a season in which the centre is short, and the eleven households at the end of the second branch would have received water under it.** He had to look at the sheet again. **"Item eight. The eleven days that were asked for at this gate four mornings ago, from the other end of that lane, are not on this sheet and are not refused by this sheet, and I am told to say so, because somebody has asked me to say so and because if I do not say it then this yard will spend a week believing that it was refused when what happened is that there was nothing there to be asked.** And that is item eight and I have read all of it."

Nobody there said anything for a while. A man from the middle row started to say that the man who had refused it had been the reason, and the clerk said, before he could finish, that item nine was that no part of the standing had ever been formally accepted by anybody in this holding, and that accepted was the wrong word for it and had been the wrong word for it all week in this yard and in his own mouth, and that no document had been signed by anyone at any point, and that a refusal of something that was never accepted is a different thing and he was sorry about the word and he was not going to unsay it.

**"Item ten. What was done this week. The upper gate was shut and the middle gate was left at the width of a hand by six named people on a Sunday morning, and the deciding at the head of the channel stopped, and the water did not stop, and no channel was cut and nothing was taken apart.** He turned the sheet over and looked at the back of it, which is empty, and then turned it back. **"That is all of it. That is every line. I have not left anything out and I have not been asked to leave anything out, and I have been paid by two councils between them and neither of those two councils is the office that sent any of this paper."**

They put her under public custody there at about the eleventh hour, in front of about forty people, and what it consisted of was that her access was taken from her by the two of them and not by anybody else, and that she was not to be at the head of the channel again, and that she remained answerable for every item on that sheet and for everything that had gone out of that office in four years that those two councils had not yet read.

They did not take her to anything. They did not put her in a room. They took her access and they wrote down what she had done and they told her she would be told about anything further, and she said that she understood, and about four people in that yard looked at the two councils afterwards as though the two of them had done a small thing.

**"Is that all that is."** A man from the fourth place said it to the first council and not to anybody else.

**"It is all that is being done this morning,**" the first council said. **"It is not all that will be done and I am not going to stand in a yard and promise you that it is, because in nine days somebody will be standing where I am standing and the promise will have been made by them instead of by me.** And I would like the man who asked me that to understand that I have not put anybody in a room and I have not taken a woman out of this yard and I have not done one thing this morning that she has not been allowed to watch, and that if any of you would have done it differently then you should have been here at six in the morning instead of me."

Then she was allowed to answer, and she answered three things, and she answered them standing up in about four hundred yards of packed dirt with nobody behind her.

**"Item four. It was real and I have never said it was not real and I am not going to say it now in front of forty people because I am being asked to.** She looked at the woman from the middle row. **"Item six was not a copy and I have been called a thief in four counties for offering it and I offered it twice, and I would do it again, and the reason I would do it again is that a copy is a thing you can be shown a different one of, and there are four counties north of here where people are shown a different one.** Item eight. Eleven days was mine to ask for and it was not refused and I have been told by two men in this yard in the last two days that nothing took it from me, and I would like it entered that I still do not agree with that, and that there is a difference between not having been refused and not having been decided, and that the second of those is worse and I know it is worse.** She turned round to the whole yard. **"I am not relieved of anything. Nobody in this yard has forgiven me and nobody has pardoned me and nobody has accused me of anything today that was not on that sheet, and I am going to be answerable for that sheet for the rest of whatever I have, and if you want to make me smaller than that then you will have to say so in a yard and not at a table.** And the second place has no water and had none this morning and will have none at the dark, and nobody in this yard has fixed it and nobody has deferred it and nobody has given me a share of it, and I have not asked anybody in this yard for a share of it and I am not going to."**

She was alive when she went out of that gate and she was walking and she answered for everything that was read out after it, and about four people at that gate watched her all the way to the corner and the last of them went some time after she had.

Nobody was asked to choose anything, and nobody was asked what they thought of any of it, and the two councils packed up their sheet and went down the middle road together at about the twelfth hour without saying a word to each other that anybody could hear.

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the register form under his arm while the yard was still half empty and gave his figure and looked at the sheet on the clerk's knee being rolled up.

**"You have two stones on that wall, and one of them reads nine hundred and twenty-one and the other reads nine hundred and sixty-eight, and I am giving you those two in that order because it is the order I say them in and not because there is an order.** He shut the door. **"Four hundred and thirty-one on the form. Not entered, and nothing about this morning is going on that form, because that form is this holding's and this morning was not this holding's and I have been asked three times this week by people from this holding whether a thing was going on it.** **"And behind me the key is on the nail where it has been since before you were all born, and I could hear every word of that being read out on this step from where I was standing."**

Tova Reed gave her four from the step of the seed house in the order she has used since the beginning of the week, and stood where she could see four mouths at once, and did not go to the gate.

**"Four hundred and thirty-one is on the form and three hundred and thirty-seven is on the charter, and the letter stands at two hundred and thirty-four, and that last one has gone up every day this week and I have said nothing about it in public because a man in this yard has said it out loud for me all week and it is more words than mine.** She put the corner of her board down. **"And the second of Silling's two ruled lines stands at four hundred and twenty-five with nothing on it, and tomorrow it is four hundred and twenty-six with nothing on it either, and a round hundred on that line is not a mark and not an opportunity and not an invitation, and three people have asked me this week what I would write there and I have been asked three times this week by three separate people what I would put there, and the answer is nothing.** **"And I was asked this morning at the gate whether I wanted to say anything about what was read out, and the answer is that I was not asked anything and I have nothing to say about a woman I have had two arguments with in nine years and neither of those arguments was about the seed."**

Auret Sill came in from the pumps at about the eleventh hour with her sleeves wet and said the weight and the morning of the run from the boards, and nobody at the wall thanked her for saying them and nobody asked her about the two people who had spoken in the yard the day before.

**"Five hundred and thirty-seventh morning, and one thousand and four hundred and thirteen hundredweight in the night, down five.** She put the chalk in her apron. **"And I said a thing out loud in that yard yesterday that I meant, and about eleven people have been waiting to see whether I meant it this morning, and I did not come down here to find out whether they did.** And if they did not then it was worth saying and if they did then it was worth saying. That is the whole of what I have learned about saying a thing out loud and I did not learn it yesterday, I learned it about four years ago."

The clerk from up a lane read the two halves of the window off the wall at about the fourth hour, before any of that happened, in the same tone he reads everything.

**"Five hundred and four between them. Two hundred and forty-seven came this morning and two hundred and fifty-seven stood.** He put the cloth down. **"The short half comes on the odd ones and the tall one stands, and I have said that out loud in five yards and in four of them somebody wrote down that I had it the other way round, and today I am going to leave the cloth there and let them look at the board.** And I will say the other thing about that wall, because it is the day for other things. There is a third number that goes with those three and it is not on the wall and there is no copy of it and there are now four people in this holding who would each have to be asked separately, and I have written twice and had no answer and I am not going to write a third time."

Ismay Rooke came off the bench at the gate at about the sixth hour and read the long sheet, which is not his wall and which he read correctly and without stopping.

**"Five hundred and thirty-fifth is what that wall carries, five hundred and thirty-fourth is under the rule, and five hundred and thirty-sixth is above it, and I read all three because I am at this gate every morning and there is nobody else at this gate every morning.** He put his hands in his pockets. **"And I have stood at that bench at the gate every morning this week and I have opened the upper gate at the sixth hour and shut it at the dark and nobody has asked me to and nobody has thanked me and I have not mentioned it, and this morning I am mentioning it once, because a woman was told in this yard about nine minutes ago that a standing was not taken off anybody by anybody, and that is not true of a gate.** He looked at the upper gate. **"Nobody took this off me. It is mine because I am the one who stands here. And if I stop standing here then it is nobody's, and that is a different thing and it is going to be the whole of the next morning if there is one."**

The north board was written on at the seventh hour with nobody within twenty feet of it but the girl from the second place, who is seventeen and who was not asked to come back in when she had finished. Two hundred and fifteen over the rule, four hundred and sixty-one under. There was nobody at that trough.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped. The sheet of terms outside the gate was not picked up and not dated and not withdrawn.

And at about the eleventh hour the fourth sheet of terms had three of the four five lines ruled and no sixth term written and no seventh and no eighth, and a woman was walking out through that gate alive and able to answer for four years of a sheet that two councils were paying for between them, and the second place was still without water and had been named twice out loud there in about nine minutes and neither of those two times had been about anybody's convenience.

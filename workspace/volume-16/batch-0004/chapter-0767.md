# Chapter 767

## The Twentieth Of The Twenty-First, And Five Kinds Of Work Going At Once In Five Places While One Man Was Out Past The Gate Holding A Piece Of Paper Against Something He Could Not See The End Of

Five kinds of work went into that yard before the light and none of them waited on anybody, and about half past six there was a light standing in the low road out past the gate that is not the light on the boards, and nobody at that gate said what it was then or afterwards.

The work was this. Seed and food out of the store with the door open before light and four places on the route. The moving reserves up the low road and the four paths, in words, with no column under any of it. Four yards where four bodies of households and two councils had their own sheets on their own tables and nobody from this gate had been in any of the four. The paths themselves, with crews out and the assemblies sitting. And the record, in five hands, on a wall and on a board and in a doorway and in a book that belongs to a crew.

Roan Selk went out past the gate at about the fifth hour with a copy of the sheet of seven steps rolled inside his coat, and he did not come back into the yard that day.

**"I am going down the road and I am taking a copy,"** he said to the boy who carries water, at the gate, in about twelve words, and the boy heard all of them. **"That is not the same as somebody being sent. Nobody from this yard is sending me and if anybody at this gate says they sent me they are lying to you and you may take it back to them.** He shouldered the bag. **"I have been up here four times this season and I have not asked you for a thing on any of them and I am not asking you for one now.** **"And I want the gate shut behind me at the usual hour, because a gate that is left standing in the morning is a gate somebody is coming out of."**

Then the light in the road was the only thing in it that had any colour in it, and it did not move along, and Marek Vale looked at it once from the middle of the yard and then went and did something else and did not say a word about it to anybody for the rest of that day.

Nia Vale was off the low road before it was light with two carts and a boy and had been four hours up it before the gate opened, and she came in at about the seventh hour with her sleeves white at the elbows and gave her two at the rack without untying anything.

**"Up eight, and one thousand and four hundred and nine hundredweight came down the channel in the night.** She got the coat off the rack and put it back on. **"That is the five hundred and thirtieth morning of a run that has never asked a single person's permission, and I have said it out loud every morning this month to anybody who wanted it, and today I have said it before breakfast because there are five things happening and one of them is mine and I am not doing four of yours.** And I want it said that both of those carts went up empty and came down with the third tank part full and not full, and I have not written the reason anywhere, because the reason is a word and not a number."

Tova Reed had the north door of the store open before light and a tray out on the step by six, and she put herself where she could see the mouths of people who came up to that door, which is where she has put herself every morning for a year, and she wrote down what she was told the first time and did not ask anybody again.

**"The letter is at two hundred and twenty-seven and it is the highest it has ever stood at. Four hundred and eighteen on the ruled line under Silling's second one, blank, and going to be blank tomorrow at four hundred and nineteen. Three hundred and thirty on the charter. And the form is at four hundred and twenty-four.** She put the corner of her board against her hip. **"That is the order I give them in when there are five things happening and not the order I give them in when there are not, and I have told this yard for six weeks that the order is mine and the numbers are not."**

A man from the middle row said, at the tray, that her father was dead four winters back in a bed and not in the wood and that the yard said otherwise.

**"Write that on your own paper,"** Tova Reed said, and gave him the seed, and did not say another word about it at any hour of that day.

Odile Vray gave the two stones off the boards at the seventh hour in one go and stood square in front of them while she did it.

**"Up the near end, nine hundred and fourteen. At the top, nine hundred and sixty-one. Forty-seven of masonry, and I have given them together now because on a morning with this much on it nobody has the patience for one at a time and I am sorry for whoever has to write it down.** She put the chalk back in the tray. **"I have got two men waiting on me at the near end of this wall and both of them have been standing there since the light and neither of them is going to stand there all day about it."**

The girl from the second place gave the window at about the seventh hour off the wall, in her own order and with both halves and then the whole, because that is the way she was taught it.

**"Four hundred and ninety-seven. Two hundred and fifty-four came and two hundred and forty-three stood, and I have had this pair backwards on the back of my own hand twice this season and I am not having it written down a third time, so somebody had better write it down properly and I am not fussy who.** She was still talking as she went down the branch. **"And I am going down there now because there are two of us on that route and one of me is not two."**

At about the ninth hour a mason came up the middle road at a run, which is not a thing masons do, and said that the bed at the top of the ring had gone soft at its own far end during the second load and had let water out of itself where nobody had cut anything.

Eight had held both. Eight had held both on the last time anybody looked at it, and eight is on the sheet on the boards with the word *both* written against it in a hand nobody in this holding recognised.

The old link stayed connected. That was arithmetic and it took about four seconds and nobody voted on it.

And Marek Vale went up the middle road at a walk and did not hurry, and about nine people in the yard watched him go and about twenty-one went back to what they were doing, and nobody said anything to him about it either way as he went.

He was on that bed for about four hours. Four men off the middle row worked it with him, which is the whole of what a repair by a crew is in this holding, and he did the part that only one person in four counties could do and he did not say so out loud and did not once stand up straight while he was doing it.

At about the seventh hour a woman came up the middle road from the second place with the barrow harness over her shoulder and asked the man who lays for three councils whether the bed would hold, in front of about eleven people, because she had a house at the end of that branch and a tank that is not full.

The man who lays for three councils said, **"I have laid the eleventh course this morning and I have not been up there and I am not going to tell her."**

**"You have laid it to the course under it and not to a figure, and I would like the next man to lay it the same way and I do not expect he will,"** he said, and went and laid another one.

Nobody there went and looked at the north end at any hour after the seventh hour except a man with a bucket and a boy of about seventeen, and nobody asked either of them anything when they came back.

Soren Rill did not read the long sheet at the tenth hour. He wrote it out at the sixth hour, on the back of the sheet he sends down the road, because the wall was wanted by four different people before the light was properly up, and the boy who carries water read it out in a yard of about twenty at about the ninth hour and read it correctly the first time and made a point of saying that he had.

**"Five hundred and twenty-seventh under the line, five hundred and twenty-ninth over it, and the one at the head is five hundred and twenty-eighth,**" the boy said. **"And I am not the man who reads that wall, I am the man who carries water, and I read it this morning because the man who reads it had four things on his hands and there are five things happening, and I would like whoever is minded to say that out loud somewhere so that the man who reads that wall knows it was not taken off him.**"

Nobody said it out loud anywhere.

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the register form under his arm and the key on its nail and gave the figure and then did not go back inside, which he has not done on a working morning in four years.

**"Four hundred and twenty-four. Not entered. The drawer was shut at the sixth hour and it is shut now and the key is on the nail, and I have said that on more mornings than there are mornings.** He leaned on the door frame with his arms folded, which is not a thing he does. **"I want one thing said by somebody other than me. There is a bed on that sheet with the word *both* against it and it stopped doing that at about nine o'clock this morning, and there is not one line in this holding's record that says what happens to the seven steps on a sheet on a wall when a bed does that. Not the register form, not the charter, not the letter, not the boards, not the crew's book. Nothing. And that is not because anybody hid it. It is because six people wrote seven steps and none of them thought to write the eighth, and I am not one of the six and I am not going to be the seventh name."**

Nobody answered him, and four or five people looked at the sheet on the boards with six names on it and none of them said anything, and four or five of them read the seven steps from the top again as though there might be an eighth at the bottom.

The crew man with his own book came down off the north end at about the ninth hour with the book shut under his arm and did not come into the yard, and said the two figures to a mason on his way past and not to anybody else.

**"One thousand and four hundred and nine hundredweight in the night, and that is up eight, and it is the five hundred and thirtieth morning of it. Nine hours at that bed and it is not written as fixed anywhere and it is not going to be written as fixed until somebody has put a second load through it, and I am the man who will write it and I am not writing it.** He went past. **"Four of us signed the sheet on the north end yesterday and the fifth did not and gave his reason, and I would like that reason left exactly where it is, because it is the only line on that sheet that anybody said out loud before it was written."**

The barrow went eleven journeys on that Saturday and stopped. It was not brought out of the yard at the fourth hour and nobody went and got it, and the man who pushes it said that eleven was a floor and not a total and that he was not going past it on a morning like this one.

Nobody in this holding went down the low road. Roan Selk was on it, as he has been four times this season, and he is one of the nine at the top of the fourth path and not one of them, and about the eleventh hour a boy of about seventeen came up past the gate and said that Roan Selk had asked for nothing and had said he would be back before dark or in the morning.

Nobody at that gate sent anybody, and nobody at that gate asked the question, and the copy of the seven steps was still rolled inside a coat out past the gate on a road where the light was still standing.

And at about the eleventh hour the bed at the top of the ring had been worked on for four hours by four men and one other person and the crew's own book said what the crew's own book says, which is what it did today, and nobody in that yard was told that anything had been fixed, and the old link was still connected at the dark, and in the channel at the north end there was a light in the water that was not the light in the road and nobody at that gate said which was which.

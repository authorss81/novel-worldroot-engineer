# Chapter 766

## The Nineteenth Of The Twenty-First, And A Bed At The North End Letting The Load Out At The Far End Of Itself A Day Before Anybody Needed It To

Four men from the fourth place and a mason out of the third went into the bed at the north end before the light and Marek Vale was not one of them and was not asked to be, and the whole of it took eleven hours and the finding was written down before the wall took the light, which is a thing this holding has not had happen before.

The dry-season draw went in first and the bed took all of it, and then they put the flood load in and the bed let a third of it out at the far end of itself, and the third was a third of what was put in and not a third of what came.

The crew man with his own book wrote it in the book where the crew keeps it, and then four of the five of them wrote it again on a sheet because a sheet can go on a board and a book cannot, and the fifth of them did not sign that sheet and said why.

> **THE NORTH END, LOADED TWICE. FOUR MEN AND A MASON. THE FINDING IS IN THE CREW'S OWN BOOK FIRST AND ON THIS SHEET BECAUSE A SHEET GOES ON A BOARD.**
>
> **Draw.** Went in clean. Nothing came out anywhere it should not have.
>
> **Load.** Went in whole. Two thirds stayed. One third came out at the far end of this bed, on its own, without anybody opening anything.
>
> **North end.** Soft at about the seventh hour and soft at the dark, and it was soft when the draw went in and it is soft now, and it is the same soft as the last finding and it has not got better in a fortnight.
>
> **What is done about it.** Nothing today. It is not to be dug again and it is not to be priced and it is not to be dug again this season whichever way tomorrow goes.
>
> Written by four of the five of us at the north end. The mason from the third place did not sign it and said that he came to lay the courses and not to sign for water, and that is his reason and he gave it out loud and nobody argued with it and it stands on this sheet exactly as he said it.

It went up on the boards at about the ninth hour with a stone on the top corner, and about twenty people read it before the light was properly on the wall, and the reading of it in that yard was not one voice.

A mason from the third place wanted the third line of the schedule taken off the table before the day was out, on the ground that a bed which fails on a Friday has failed for the week and the reading out is on the ninth day.

**"That is not yours to take off and it is not his to keep on,"** Perrin Dae said. **"You altered the third line yesterday with a pencil and your own initials at the end of it, which was allowed, and the reason it was allowed is that the paper is a list of work and the work is the four of yours and not him. Taking a line off it is a different size of thing and there is nobody at this gate who can do it."**

**"Then the reading out goes ahead on a bed that does not hold."**

**"Then the reading out goes ahead on what the sheet says,"** Perrin Dae said, **"and the sheet says this, and the sheet was written this morning by four men who were standing in the water when they wrote it. You want it to say something else, then you go and stand in the water at the north end tomorrow and write it yourself."**

Nobody at the wall asked Marek Vale a single question about the finding, and he was about eleven feet from the boards when it went up and he read it standing with his hands behind his back, twice, the way he stands from anything other people have written.

He read it a second time and then said one thing about it, and he said it to the man who lays for three councils and not to the yard.

**"Do not ask me whether that bed is going to be different on Saturday. I have never once in nine years been able to tell anybody what a bed is going to do, and a man who tells you he can is selling you something and today of all mornings he would be selling it to himself.** He stepped back from the boards the width of a hand. **"It is not mine, the bed is not mine, and the crew that dug it has put down what it did, and that is the whole of what there is to say in this yard about it until there is more water in it."**

**"Then nothing starts today."**

**"Nothing starts today that needs that bed to hold,**" he said. **"That is arithmetic and not a decision, and I would like it written somewhere that it was arithmetic."**

Perrin Dae came off the wall at about the sixth hour with the barrow shafts under his arm and gave two figures to a man who had not asked him, because a man from the middle row had come in off the low road that morning saying yesterday's.

**"One thousand and four hundred and one hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and that puts us on the five hundred and twenty-ninth morning of the run.** He shouldered the barrow. **"Down five, and it is down five on every morning with an odd number on it and I have stopped explaining the rule to anybody, because a man who cannot hold a rule in his head is a man who will be dangerous with a valve one day.** And I am taking this out before the north end fills, so tell me whatever it is you are going to say quickly or say it where the barrow is."

Harlan Vetch was on the wall and came down four courses, which is further than he has come for a figure since the frost, and gave the pair without being asked.

**"Four hundred and ninety-six between the two halves. Two hundred and forty-three came this morning and two hundred and fifty-three stood, and the short one is the one that moves on a morning like this one, and I have said that out loud enough mornings now that you could all say it without me.** He went back up. **"It is on the back of the board and somebody can wipe the front of it for once and I would take it as a kindness."**

At the seventh hour the girl from the second place went over to the north board on her own and put two hundred and eleven above the rule and four hundred and fifty-three under it, under a heading that is a month nine months back and has been that heading since before she came to work here, and there was nobody at that trough, and she put the chalk in her apron pocket and went straight back down the branch without looking at either figure twice.

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall at about the tenth hour with the cloth in his hand and wiped the chalk off afterwards, and read it in the order he has read it for twenty years because it was not the morning to be clever.

**"Five hundred and twenty-seventh is the figure at the head of that sheet. The one with the rule beneath it is the five hundred and twenty-sixth. Above the rule is the five hundred and twenty-eighth, and if anybody in this yard writes those three down in any other arrangement I will read them back to them on a wet morning.** He folded the cloth over his hand. **"And that sheet on the boards above with the water in it has moved that wall more in one morning than the wind did."**

Hanne Brack came up from the second place at about the ninth hour with her sleeves wet and gave the two stones off the wall without stopping, in the order she has given them in for eleven days, and did not offer anybody the other thing she had come up for.

**"Nine hundred and thirteen at the near end. Nine hundred and sixty at the top. Forty-seven of masonry, as ever, and neither of those two numbers is a round one and I am not going to stand here and be impressed by either of them.** She got the harness up onto her shoulder. **"I have come up with the water still coming down in my sleeves and I am not going to talk about it in this yard, so you may have two figures instead and work out among yourselves which of the two you would rather have had."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room with the register form under his arm, put a shoulder against the frame of his own door, and stayed there rather than coming the rest of the way down. The door was shut behind him. The key was on its nail. He gave one figure and then stood on the step for a while with the form under his arm and did not go back in.

**"Four hundred and twenty-three, and it is not entered, and the key is on the nail and it was on the nail at the sixth hour and it will be on the nail at the dark.** He looked at the sheet on the boards for about as long as it takes to read one. **"And I want it said that there is a line on that sheet about a third of a load coming out of a bed and there is no line on it about who is going to be standing at the north end tomorrow, and both of those are the same kind of fact and only one of them has a room attached to it."**

Tova Reed gave her four from the step of the seed house in the order she has given them in for twenty days, and wrote each one on the corner of the board as she said it, and a man from the fourth place had to come round the trays to be heard at all.

**"Four hundred and twenty-three on the form, and that is the same figure the man at the door has just said out loud and the two of them are not to be added together, and I have watched three people in this yard try it this week. Three hundred and twenty-nine on the charter, which is the oldest of them and which nobody has ever read out. Four hundred and seventeen on the ruled line under Silling's second one, and it is blank, and it is going to be blank tomorrow at four hundred and eighteen with nothing on it either. And the letter is at two hundred and twenty-six.** She put the corner of the board down against her hip. **"I have said the last of those five times now and I am not going to keep saying it to a yard that has not once written it down."**

Four people in that yard heard the number and about eleven wrote nothing at all, and the count of people in this holding who have ever written one of those four down is the same as it was yesterday.

The barrow went eleven journeys on that Friday and stopped, and the man pushing it stood with the shafts up at the north end of the yard for a while looking at a sheet on a board before he went and got a hod.

Nobody in that yard entered the one room except to shut the drawer, and the key went back on its nail. Thirty-nine blanks were on the wall and the rule beneath them stood open at the thirty-ninth time and had done since the wall was drawn.

Nobody went near the ladder against the tool house wall, and the ring of bare ground behind it took nobody's boot and nobody's tape and nobody's price.

There is a joint at the back of the tap house that puts one drop into a saucer every morning of this holding's record, and it did that on that Friday, and the saucer kept it, and nothing about the load test that four men ran at the north end has any bearing on it.

Nobody fetched the man of about seventy, and nothing was put to him at any hour, and the twenty-nine fetchings that stand against his name are the twenty-nine that were standing there yesterday.

The compost line is paid at thirty-one and there is no column on that board showing what has come out of it, and there has not been one since before the spring.

The second place is still without water and there was nothing said about it in that yard this morning by anybody who was not the seed keeper, and the seed keeper said nothing about it.

And at about the eleventh hour the sheet from the north end was still on the boards with a stone on the top corner, and the line at the foot of it that says what is done about it said nothing, and the hand-over that six people wrote out on a sheet of their own could not be begun on the strength of a bed that has just let a third of a load out of itself, and the old link was therefore still connected at the dark on that Friday, and nobody in that yard had been asked to choose anything about either of those two things.

# Chapter 773

## The Twenty-Sixth Of The Twenty-First, And Two People Saying Out Loud In Their Own Mouths That They Are The Ones Holding The Two Ends Of A Thing At The Head Of The Channel, And Nobody In That Yard Saying Whether It Is On

They did it in the middle of the yard at about the ninth hour, one after the other, about forty seconds apart, and neither of them had been asked and neither of them had spoken to the other about it, and about twenty people in that yard had been walking about for a fortnight waiting for something and got it in about a minute and a half and did not know what to do with it.

Auret Sill said it first. She was at the pumps and she came in from them with her sleeves wet and stopped in the middle of the yard where the light was, and she did not ask anybody whether the yard was listening and she did not wait for a silence.

**"I have been at the far end of that joint on my own time since before the flood and I have told nobody and I am telling you today because nobody in this holding has any business knowing that except you, and that is me saying I hold that end and not saying anybody made me hold it.** She had her hands at her sides. **"I do not have anything on me that says so. There is no paper with my name on it and I have not asked for one and I am not going to. If you want to write it down you can go and get my own sheet and ask me to write it on that, and if you do not want to do that then it goes in a yard and stays in a yard.** **"And I am going to say the other thing now while I am standing here and not while somebody is asking me. Any day I like, I stop. I do not come and tell him. I do not give a reason. He does not get a vote and he does not get asked and he does not get told afterwards."**

About four people in that yard began to say something at the same moment and about four of them stopped.

Perrin Dae came out of the middle road with the barrow shafts under his arm forty seconds later and put them down, and he had not been within thirty feet of her when she spoke.

**"I am the other one.** He did not raise his voice. **"I have been counting out loud at that middle gate on my own account and nobody asked me to and nobody paid me, and I have told nobody and I am telling you, and I am saying it after her and not because she said it.** He wiped his face with his forearm. **"And I am going to say what I think about what the two of us have just done, and it is not a good thing, and I would rather say it standing here than have it said at the fourth path. Two people have said a thing out loud in a yard. That is not two people holding anything. That is two people having said a thing out loud. What it is worth depends on what happens at that gate on the two hundred mornings after this one, and neither of us has any way of making it worth anything and neither of us is going to be able to tell anybody which mornings those were.** He picked the shafts up again. **"And I am not saying that to be modest. I am saying it because I have watched four counties do a thing twice and both of those times it was announced and neither of them lasted a season."**

Marek Vale was about eleven feet away and had heard both of them and said nothing at either one, and about nine people in that yard looked at him afterwards to see what he was going to do about it, and he was not going to do anything about it, and he has not done anything about it since.

**"Are you going to write it down."** Perrin Dae said, to him, without turning round.

**"No.** He said it at once and he said it in about the same tone as somebody saying it was raining. **"It is not mine and I am not going to have their names on any paper of mine, and if you want it on paper then one of you goes and asks the district for a sheet of your own and you write it on that and the district decides what to do with it, and my name is not on it and is not going to be on it.** He looked at the roll of paper that has been lying unrolled on the middle table since the day before yesterday. **"And I am not going to be asked about that again, so let us all have that now instead of having it on Friday."**

Nobody asked him about it again.

Nia Vale was asked about it at about the tenth hour by a man from the middle row who wanted to know whether the flow had changed, which was his way of asking whether it was on.

**"The flow has not changed and it has not stopped and the water went down that channel at the same rate it went down it yesterday and the same rate it went down it on the day before that,**" Nia Vale said. **"I will give you both readings and you may write them down. And I will tell you the other thing, which is that there is nothing in this holding, and I have looked, and I have been through four rooms and had a man from the fourth place open a box for me at his own expense, and there is not one thing in this county that would tell you whether two people are holding a joint.** She put the gauge down. **"I have a gauge that will tell me how much water is going past and how fast and whether it has changed since the sixth hour. I have a chalk board with three numbers on it and I have a clock. If you want to know whether two people are holding something, you will have to go and ask them, and if you ask me I will tell you the same thing I have told you for four days, which is that the deciding stopped on Sunday morning at about a quarter past six and has not started again and I did not do it and neither did they and neither of them can."**

**"Then what has changed."**

**"Nothing has changed in the water,"** Nia Vale said. **"Something has changed in who will answer the door at four in the morning, and that is not a thing I have a number for and I would be a fool to pretend I had."**

Roan Selk came up the low road at about the seventh hour, on his own time as he has been four times this season, and read the two halves off the wall in the middle of about six people and gave the direction first.

**"Five hundred and three between them, and two hundred and fifty-seven came this morning and two hundred and forty-six stood. The tall half moves on an even one and I have said that out loud four mornings now to five different people and not one of them has written it down, and I have stopped minding, because a thing said out loud four times in a yard is in the yard whether anybody has it on paper or not.** He put the nail in his pocket. **"And I was up that road before the light and there is a light in it that is not the light on that board and I am not going to describe it to five people with a wall behind them and I have not described it to anybody and I am not going to start today.** And I have not come up here to be asked whether it is on. If somebody wants to ask me whether it is on they can come and stand at the top of the fourth path with me and ask me there, and I will give them the same answer up there that I would give them here, which is that I do not know and neither does anybody else."

The clerk from up a lane read the long sheet at about the eighth hour and had a sheet of his own out with a copy of it on, and compared the two and said that they were the same.

**"The top figure on that sheet is five hundred and thirty-fourth. Under the rule it is the five hundred and thirty-third, above the rule the five hundred and thirty-fifth. And in the night there was one thousand and four hundred and eighteen hundredweight, which is up eight, and it is the five hundred and thirty-sixth morning of the run, and I have both of those off the board and off my own copy and they agree and I have checked them twice because it is the only thing in this county I am paid for.** He put his own sheet away. **"And I will say the thing I have said in five yards this week and been ignored in five. There is a third number belonging to that wall, it is not on the wall, there is no copy of it in this holding or in four days north, and the two of us in this yard who would both know it are not going to write it out for each other.** A woman from the middle row said that was on purpose. The clerk said that it was on purpose."

Sera Quill gave the count at the boards before the light and about eleven people heard it, which is more than have ever heard it.

**"A hundred and seventy-three in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty-four not."**

**"Up one on it."**

**"Up one, and there is a second number on this slate and it is not going to be read out this morning and it is thirteen from the one I have given you, and it has not been read on any morning this one has been on and it is not going to be read on the day I stop.** She turned the slate over and it showed nothing at all. **"One hundred and sixteenth is the one that goes in the book. I have not read the other one out this week and I am not going to start, and I would like the four people who have asked me about it this week to understand that there is no morning on which I am going to read it and that asking me is the only thing anybody can do about it.** And I would like to say one thing about the two people who spoke in that yard. That is not my business. I keep a figure that moves on a morning of a certain kind and it has not moved because of anybody who said anything out loud this week, and it will go on not moving. And anybody who would like me to put their names against a figure in this holding can come and look at the front of this slate at any hour of any morning and there is nothing on it but a figure.

The girl of about seventeen from the sluice road came through with a chain and stayed for it, and gave the long sheet off the wall with the bar across her shoulder, in the wrong order.

**"Top of that sheet is five hundred and thirty-fourth, the five hundred and thirty-third has the rule under it, and five hundred and thirty-fifth sits above.** She shifted the chain. **"I have it on the back of my hand now, the other one, the half pair, and it has not washed off, and I have had it on there four days and my mother has asked me twice what is on my hand.** And I am going to write it out on a piece of paper tonight and put the paper somewhere anybody can read it, and if anybody tells me I should not have I would like them to say so to my face."

Hanne Brack gave the two stones off the boards in the wrong order, and did not know she was doing it, and nobody told her.

**"Nine hundred and sixty-seven at the top and nine hundred and twenty at the near end.** She put the chalk down and looked at the wall for a second. **"And I have known for eleven days which of those two is the top and I have said them the same way up and down this wall every morning of this season, and I am not going to start now, and if it is wrong then it is wrong the way it has been.** And I have got to be at my own house by the fifth hour and I am sixty-one and I said yesterday that I would stop and I have stopped."

Odile Vray wrote the two figures on the board in that order and Hanne Brack's order, which are different, and did not erase hers.

Tova Reed gave her four from the step with the tray out and the north door open behind her.

**"Four hundred and twenty-four under Silling's second one, and it is blank, and I have put that number first all week because it is the one everybody wants something written on it. Four hundred and thirty is the form. Two hundred and thirty-three is the letter. Three hundred and thirty-six is the charter.** She wrote each one on the corner. **"And two people said something out loud in that yard this morning and I am not going to write either of their names on this board and I am not going to write one of them on a piece of paper in here and nobody is to come and ask me to keep a copy of it, because that is what the store is for and it is not for that.** And the second place is still without water and there is no date on that line and there has not been one since the flood, and I have not put one there and I am not going to, and if either of those two people has come down that lane and offered me one I would like to hear about it within the hour."

Nobody had offered her one.

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the form under his arm, did not come the rest of the way down, gave one figure, and did not ask anybody anything, which is twice in two days.

**"Four hundred and thirty on the form. Not entered.** He shut the door. **"The drawer behind me has been shut at every hour of every day for four years and I am not going to be asked about it and I am not going to volunteer it again."**

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past four times that day, in the dark and after the light, and read on by nobody and offered to nobody, and the figure in its seventh column is not on any board in this holding and has not been since before the flood.

No ladder in this holding was put a foot on that Friday and nobody made an offer to anybody about the ground the tool house wall stands in, and that ground is still bare and is still not written on anywhere.

And at about the eleventh hour the two of them were still in that yard, separately, at opposite ends of it, not together and not looking for each other, and about eleven people in that holding had heard two people say a thing out loud and about eleven people were waiting to hear whether it worked, and there is nothing in this holding that would have told them either way this morning or any morning after it.

# Chapter 770

## The Twenty-Third Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman Standing At A Gate Asking For Something To Be Decided And Finding Out In Front Of Everybody That There Is Nothing There To Decide It

She came in through that gate on foot at about the eighth hour with the same clerk from up a lane a pace behind her carrying nothing, and about four people in that yard knew her before anybody said a word, and the clerk did not sit down and did not put his hat anywhere.

She went to the middle table first, because she always goes to the middle table first, and the roll of paper was still lying on it nine inches from four stones, and she put her own hand flat on the wood beside the roll and left it there.

**"I am going to ask this yard for one thing and it is a small thing, and I would like it done before I say anything else so that everybody in here has seen the order of it."**

**"Ask."**

**"Lift the short measure on the outer branches for eleven days.** She said it the way she reads the line off her own paper. **"Not a season. Not a term. Eleven days, and the lifting written on the sheet of terms under the third column where it is ruled and empty, and nobody's name on it and no council able to reopen a branch, and at the end of the eleven days the short measure goes back exactly as it is this morning and nobody in this holding has to agree to anything afterwards.** She took her hand off the table. **"That is the whole of what I have come for and I have come four days early because I would rather be refused in a yard than answered in a letter."**

About nine people in that yard looked at the board with the third column ruled and empty under it, and about four of them looked at the four stones.

**"That has to be decided at the head of the channel,"** the clerk from up a lane said, from about six feet off, in the voice of a man reading a line he is also carrying. **"Not here. Not at the middle table. At the head."**

**"Then decide it at the head,"** Iona Vey said. **"I have watched you people refuse each other in this yard for nine days and I have not once seen anybody refuse to do a thing because it was two hundred yards away.** She looked along the middle road. **"I am not asking that man at the middle table to do it. He has said in this yard that he is not the one who decides what goes down that channel and I have read the account of that and I believe it, and I have believed it since before it was said. I am asking whether the head of that channel can be asked at all."**

Nobody answered her for about four seconds, and in those four seconds nobody at that gate went and sent for anybody, which is the whole of the answer.

Emrys Dole came down off the wall and stood about ten feet from her and did not go any nearer than that.

**"I opened it to the width of a hand on the sixth hour on the day before yesterday and I have left it there every morning since and nobody's hand has been on that wheel but mine, and I am going to keep doing it, because if I stop then there is a closed gate and a closed gate is a different thing from an empty one and I have thought about that for two days and I would like somebody to write down that I thought about it."**

**"Then there is a man at it."**

**"There is a man who opens a gate,"** Emrys Dole said. **"There is nobody deciding anything behind it. I have said that out loud in four yards this week and I have said it here twice and I am saying it a third time to a person who has come four days early to be refused and I would like it counted that I said it three times."**

> **WHAT WAS ASKED FOR AT THE GATE THIS MORNING, AND WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT, IN THE ORDER IT WAS ASKED FOR.**
>
> **Asked for.** The short measure on the outer branches lifted for eleven days, written under the third column of the sheet of terms, with no name on it and no council able to reopen a branch, and the short measure going back exactly as it is at the end of the eleven days.
>
> **Who it had to be asked to.** The head of the channel. Not the middle table. Not this yard.
>
> **What was at the head of the channel at the eighth hour.** The upper gate shut and the middle gate standing at the width of a hand and a gauge with two readings on it, both said out loud, and nobody.
>
> **What was done about it.** Nothing, because there was nothing there to be asked. Nobody refused this woman. Nothing arrived anywhere to be refused and nothing went back down a road to a place four days north.
>
> **What was not done.** No gate was opened wider. No wheel was turned. No column was filled in and no term was added and no sixth term was written. The short measure on the outer branches is what it was this morning at the eighth hour and it will be the same at the dark.
>
> **Who wrote this sheet.** The clerk from up a lane, because she did not ask him to and he said he would, and neither of the two of them has signed it.

Iona Vey read it standing up, the whole of it, twice, and about nine people in that yard watched her do it and nobody said anything while she was doing it.

Then she asked the question that has been standing in that yard since before the flood and that nobody in it had ever been able to put to her face to face.

**"Who took it off her."**

**"Nobody took anything off anybody,"** Emrys Dole said. **"That is what I am trying to say to you and I have said it three times and you have not heard one of them, because you have come down a lane ready to be refused by a man and there is not one here to refuse you and that is not the same as a door being shut in your face and I am sorry that it has landed wrong.** He wiped his hands. **"You want a name. I have got a sheet with six names on it and none of them at the top of it and I would put my own on it tomorrow and I am not going to, because the day somebody's name is at the top of that sheet is the day this yard has decided that the water goes down that channel because a person willed it, and it does not, and it did not while you were still owed it either."**

**"It did not while I was owed it because I had a standing instrument and an authority and four counties that would have carried an instruction of mine by the middle of the next day.** She said it without heat and she said it to about twenty people and not to Emrys Dole. **"What has been removed is not a door. It is the only thing I ever actually had, which is the fact that when I say a thing about water, water does it. And that is gone and nobody took it and I would like this yard to understand that it is gone, because in four days' time somebody four days north is going to write down that it was taken from me by a man at a middle table, and that will be a comfortable sentence for a comfortable person to write, and it will be a lie with a name on it."**

**"Is it a lie."**

**"It is a lie with your gate on it and none of his hands on it.** She looked at Marek Vale, who was about thirty feet away at the north end with his back to the yard. **"You have not taken anything from me and I am not going to thank you for not taking it, and I want it known in this yard that nothing has been taken from me."**

**"Then what is left to you,"** Perrin Dae said, from the barrow, in a completely level voice. **"Say it out loud so that somebody writes it down."**

**"Everything I came here with,"** Iona Vey said. **"Four counties. Two offices. Every record I have ever sent north and about a third of what I have never sent. And eleven days of somebody's short measure, which is the smallest of those things and the only one in this yard with people in it.** She turned round to the yard. **"I am not relieved of anything. I have not been relieved of a single thing, and the second place is still without water this morning and it will be without water when I am a long way from here, and nobody in this yard has fixed that and nobody has told me it is temporary and nobody has apportioned it and nobody has put a date on it, and if any one of you has been waiting since the flood for somebody to come and tell you that I was the cause, then I am not going to do it, because I am not the cause and I never was and I would have been cheaper to you if I had been."**

Nobody in that yard said thank you and nobody said you were wrong, and about four people at the gate had been standing there for about a minute and a half before the last of them went.

She went out past the long table and out of the gate at her own pace and the clerk from up a lane followed her at the same pace, and she did not turn round, and she was alive, and she was walking, and she had answered for everything she had done at about eight o'clock in the morning in about nine yards of packed dirt without being asked a single question by anybody.

All of this was after the seventh hour, and long before it the girl from the second place had been up the branch alone and had left four hundred and fifty-seven under the rule and two hundred and thirteen above it on a board headed with a month that is nine months back, and nobody was standing at the trough and nobody has been standing at that trough for a very long time.

Hanne Brack came up from the second place at the ninth hour with her sleeves wet and gave the weight and the morning of the run in one breath, at the boards, on her way past, in the wrong order for the second time in a week and knowing it.

**"In the night, one thousand and four hundred and seven hundredweight, which is five less than what stood there yesterday morning, and that puts the run on its five hundred and thirty-third morning.** She put the harness down on the wall. **"That is down five. I am saying the small one first and I have said the small one first three mornings running now and nobody has corrected me and I would like somebody to notice that nobody has corrected me, because it is the first useful thing I have done in a fortnight.** And the nine at the top of that path have their own route and I am not going to say one word about it and I have not said one word about it."

The woman who is the neighbour of the second place came up the lane on foot at about the tenth hour and had to be asked twice to say the window at all, and when she said it she gave it in a different order from the two hands that had it, and did not know that it was different.

**"Five hundred between the two halves, which is the first time either of us has ever had that pair come to a round thing and I did not notice until I had said it.** She put her hand flat on the wall. **"Two hundred and forty-five came and two hundred and fifty-five stood. I am the one next door to the place with no water and I have been asked by four people this month to say one thing about it in a yard, and I am not going to, and if you want to know why then you can come and stand in it."**

Nobody at the wall said anything about the five hundred and about four people at the boards looked at each other over the first half of it and said nothing.

The clerk from up a lane read the long sheet at about the eighth hour, before she came in, and read it correctly and made a point of saying that he had, and then read it again for her and let her stand and look at it.

**"That wall carries five hundred and thirty-first, five hundred and thirtieth sits beneath the rule, and five hundred and thirty-second is above the rule, and I have told you which of the three is on top because four people in this yard will otherwise set them down in a different order from mine and blame themselves for it.** He put his hand down. **"And I will say the thing I have said four times and been ignored four times. There is a third number that belongs to that wall and it is not on the wall and there is no copy of it anywhere, and I have written to my own office twice about it and had no answer either time, and I am the clerk and I am telling you that the only complete copy of that sheet in this holding is in one man's head and he is not at the wall."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the form under his arm, the key on its nail, and did not look at her at all while she was in the yard, which four people noticed and one of them told her about afterwards.

**"Near stone nine hundred and seventeen. Top stone nine hundred and sixty-four.** He shut the door. **"Four hundred and twenty-seven on the form and not entered, and I have not entered it, and it is not going to be entered by me on a morning like this one, whatever anybody at that gate thinks an entered form would do for us this afternoon.** **"And there is a drawer behind me and it is shut and the key is on the nail, and that is where both of them will be at the dark."**

Tova Reed gave her four from the step of the seed house in an order that has a reason in it, and she gave the reason.

**"The form is at four hundred and twenty-seven, and the letter is at two hundred and thirty, and the charter is three hundred and thirty-three, and the ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and twenty-one and is blank.** She wrote each one on the corner as she said it. **"That is charter before letter for the first time in four weeks and I am not going to explain why, and it is not because of the woman who has just gone out of that gate, and it is because the charter is the oldest thing in this holding and she has just come here and asked for eleven days of somebody else's water and the charter is what says whose it is.** **"And I am not going to be at the gate when that woman comes back, and anybody who wants to tell me I ought to be can come and stand at my door about it and I will be there, because I am at my door every morning."**

The sheet of terms on the low board outside was not picked up and not dated and not withdrawn at any hour of that Tuesday.

And at about the eleventh hour the third column of the sheet of terms was still ruled and empty under a heading nobody had improved, and there was no sixth term and no seventh and no eighth, and eleven days was not written anywhere in this holding except on a sheet that a clerk wrote because a woman asked for it and that said at the bottom that nothing happened because there was nothing there to happen.

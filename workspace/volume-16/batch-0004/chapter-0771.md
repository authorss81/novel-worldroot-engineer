# Chapter 771

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Twenty-First, And A Man Going Down Into A Bed At The Fifth Of The Ring That Four Men Had Dug And A Load Test Had Found, And What It Cost Him In Front Of Everybody, And What It Cannot Ever Be Again

He chose the fifth of the ring himself and he chose it out loud in a yard of about thirty people at about the sixth hour, and he chose it because four men had dug it and a load test had found it, and he said so, and nobody in that yard thanked him for choosing and nobody asked him why that one.

He said it like a man reading off a list of the parts of a job.

**"The fifth. It held the draw and gave the load up clean at its own near end and lost nothing at the far end of itself, and that is the only description I have got for it and the description is not mine, four men put it in the crew's own book and I have not seen that book and am not going to be given it.** He wiped his face with the back of his wrist. **"It is not the strongest one. Eight was stronger and eight failed on Saturday and eight is written down as failed in a book that belongs to a crew. This is the one that did what it said."**

Then he went and did it, and it took from about half past six until about two in the afternoon, and about four of the middle row stayed in the bed with him the whole time and one of them held him under the shoulder for the last two hours and has said nothing about that at any hour since.

What it looked like from the wall was a man lying on his side in a wet cut with his hands in the gravel of a bed that four other men had cut, and going still, and then going not still, and then being still again in a different way, about eleven times, at intervals that got longer. The crew man with his own book counted them out loud because he has counted out loud in front of this yard for a fortnight, and he got to eleven and stopped counting and did not go back and start again.

At about the eighth hour somebody asked the boy who carries water to go and get the man at the middle table and the boy did not move, and a man from the middle row said that he was not going either, and about four other people said the same thing in about four different words inside a minute.

**"He said he was not going to be sent for,"** Perrin Dae said, from about nine feet off, to a man who had not asked him anything. **"He said it on Saturday in front of four people. If you want to go and get him, you go and get him. I am not your errand and this is not my gate."**

Nobody went.

When it was over he could not get up off the gravel and four men got him up off it, and he stood in the yard for about a minute and a half with his hands on nothing and did not say anything to anybody, and then he went and sat on the middle table, which is a thing he has not done in this holding in four years, and about four people saw him do it and about twenty-one did not.

Then he said the part that matters, and he said it standing up off the table because the man who lays for three councils told him to, and he said it to about thirty people and not to any one of them.

**"I have put the part of me that answers to the wood into that bed, and I did not put all of it in, and I am not going to be asked how much, because I do not know how much and neither does anybody else and anybody who tells you a fraction is selling you one.** He had his hands flat on the table. **"It is not in my hands. It is in a bed that four men dug, and what comes out of that bed from now on comes out of the bed, and I am going to be able to do less with it than I could do with it on Monday and I am not going to be able to do anything with it at all by the end of the week, and I have not got a figure for how much less and I am not going to invent one for a yard to remember me by."**

**"Can you take it back."**

That was a man from the fourth place and he asked it in a completely ordinary voice and there was nothing in it.

**"No,**" Marek Vale said. **"And I want that said by me and in that order, and I want it said in this yard and not written down anywhere, because the second it is written down somebody in four counties will treat it as a thing that was done to the network and I did the network the only thing anybody has done to it in fifteen years, which was take something out of it without asking it."**

**"Then it is done."**

**"That is done. It cannot be undone and it is not going to be somebody else's job to reverse, and nobody is to go to my wife or my sister or the seed house and ask them whether it can be put back, because the answer is no and it is no whatever anybody asks.** He stopped and started again. **"And that is the last morning on which there was one man at this holding who could feel that whole thing at once. It is not a punishment and it is not a sacrifice and nobody is to write it down as either of those words. It is a capacity and it is going to be a capacity for the rest of what is left to me."**

Nobody in that yard said that anything had been won.

The man who lays for three councils asked the only question anybody asked all afternoon and he asked it to a mason.

**"Who is holding the other end of it."**

**"That is not mine to answer,**" Marek Vale said. **"And I am not going to be at the top of a list and I am not going to be at the bottom of one either, and if a sheet comes up this lane with my name at the head of it then somebody has not listened to a single morning of this one.** He got his sleeve wet through wiping his face. **"There are people in this yard who have been going and looking at the ends of things on their own time for a fortnight and telling nobody, and I have known about two of them and I have not asked either of them and I am not going to ask them today.** **"And I will tell you the one thing about it that everybody else in this yard needs to hear, and then I am going to sit down again."**

**"Tell us."**

**"Any one of them can stop.** He said it flatly, without any weight on it at all, which is the only way a thing like that can be said out loud in a yard. **"Every one of them can stop on any morning, without asking me, without telling me, without giving me a reason and without waiting for a day. I cannot make them go on. I cannot make them go on by wanting it, and I cannot make them go on by being the man at the middle table, and if that has changed for one of them since a month ago then they have not been told by me and they would be right about that.** He looked round the yard and it took him a while. **"That is the whole arrangement. There is no part of it I can take back and there is no part of it I can hold on to, and anybody in four counties who tells you a network is held together by one man being at the top of it has been sold a thing by somebody."**

Tova Reed had been at the store door from before the light with a tray out on the step and she had watched the whole of it from about forty feet away, and she did not come to the north end and she did not come to the middle table and nobody went and got her.

At about the third hour she came out of the store on her own and stood where she could see the mouths of about nine people, which is where she puts herself, and said one thing and went back inside.

**"I am not going to hold any of it.** She said it to the yard and not to him, and about four people turned round. **"I am not being asked and I want that on the record from my own mouth and not from his. The seed work is the seed work and it stays with me and I am not going to put it down to make a man's morning easier, and if anybody in this holding thinks that the four figures I say out loud every morning are something I do for him then they have not been listening to me since the flood.** She picked up the corner of her board. **"There is one thing I will do and I am saying it now so that it is mine and not his. The reserves for the second branch go out at the fourth hour and there is nobody on that route but me and the boy, and for as long as the flow is uneven and somebody has to know which of two tanks gets filled first, that is me, and it will be me until I cannot do it, and I will say so on the morning I cannot, and nobody is going to make me say it sooner."**

Nobody put a hand on her arm at any hour of that day and about four people were watching to see whether one would.

She gave her four from the step with the tray out and the north door open behind her, and she gave them in the order she gave them in the morning the man at the middle table said no, which nobody in that yard could have told you and nobody asked her about.

**"The letter is at two hundred and thirty-one. The charter is three hundred and thirty-four. The ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and twenty-two, and it is blank, and there is a round hundred coming to that line in five mornings and I have said that out loud in four yards this week so that nobody in this holding has to be surprised by it.** She wrote each one on the corner. **"And the form is four hundred and thirty-two, and the man at the door has already said that figure out loud today, and those are two figures and not one, and I am not going to add them together and neither is anybody else.** And a table is going to be read out in a yard in about a week with everything in it that failed, and it is going to have that place on it, because I put it there this morning before anybody asked me to."

The crew man with his own book came down off the ring at about the fourth hour and gave the two stones off the boards himself, because the boy who carries water was holding the other end of nothing and had both hands full.

**"The near stone has gone to nine hundred and eighteen and the one at the top to nine hundred and sixty-five, forty-seven of masonry as ever, and I am the one who laid the eleventh course at the near end and I would rather those two figures were said in the same mouth as the course.** He shut the book. **"And I have written one line in that book this afternoon and it is the only line and it does not have a number on it and it does not have his name on it, and it says what was done in the fifth bed today and by how many hands, and it says that the other beds are where they were on the sheet with the water in it, and one of those beds still says soft at the north end.** He put the book under his arm the wrong way round and did not notice. **"I am not going to copy any of it onto those boards and I am not going to put a price on a bed."**

The man who lays for three councils gave the weight and the morning of the run from the top of the wall at about the fifth hour, to two men, in a low voice, because he had been up there since before the light.

**"The weight in the night was one thousand and four hundred and fifteen hundredweight, eight more than the morning before, and that puts the run on its five hundred and thirty-fourth morning.** He came down two courses. **"And I laid four of them today and I have not laid a fifth, and I am going to go and lay it at the fourth hour tomorrow and it will be the fifth of them and not the fourth, and there is nobody in this yard who needs to know that but me."**

The girl of about seventeen from the sluice road came up the middle road at about the sixth hour to get a chain and a bar, and was made to stop and listen, and gave the two halves of the window while she was standing there with the bar across her shoulder.

**"Five hundred and one of it. Two hundred and fifty-six came and two hundred and forty-five stood. My mother says the tall one moves on the even ones and I have been wrong about which is which twice this year because I have never once written either of them down.** She shifted the bar. **"I am writing it on the back of my hand now and I am not going to wash it off until it is on a paper."**

The woman with a barrow came through the gate at about the seventh hour and read the long sheet off the wall without stopping, on her way to somewhere, and got it right and knew that she had got it right because she said so.

**"It goes five hundred and thirty-second at the top of that sheet, five hundred and thirty-first at the bottom of it, and five hundred and thirty-third across the middle where the rule is.** She shouldered the barrow. **"I have not been in this yard since the sixth hour of two days ago and I am not going to talk about where I have been, and I will say this much, which is that there are people three miles up this road who have looked at those three numbers and understood two of them and it is not the middle one they have understood.** **"And I am going to be at my own door at the fifth hour this evening if anybody wants to ask me about that."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step at about the fourth hour with the register form under his arm and gave the figure from the step and then stood on it.

**"Four hundred and twenty-eight. Not entered.** He shut the door and then did not go in. **"I have been in this holding four years and I have entered four things and three of them were about a roof. I have not entered one line about what happened at the north end this morning, and nobody in this yard is going to ask me to, and if somebody four days north asks me for a record of it I am going to send them back the page of this holding's own record where it says that the bed was dug by four men and what it did, and they can have that.** **"There is a drawer behind me and it is shut and it is going to stay shut and there is nothing about this morning in it."**

Nobody entered the one room that day except to shut the drawer.

At about the ninth hour the four sheets on the long table were still lying where four districts had put them, none of them entered, none of them refused, no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper in that room was a woman's own sheet with a refusal written on it in her own hand and it was not one of the four.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped. The ladder against the tool house wall stayed where it was, and the ground behind that wall is bare and has been bare for years and no tape went over it and no offer was made to anybody about it, and the sheet of terms outside the gate was not picked up and not dated and not withdrawn.

The saucer at the back of the tap house had its one drop in it and it did that again, and it has proved nothing about any morning of the week including the one that the whole of this holding's record turns on.

And at about the eleventh hour there was no word given out in that yard about anything having been won, and the fifth bed was holding what it had been given, and eight beds were where they had been on a sheet with a stone on it, and about nine people who had been standing in that yard since the sixth hour were still standing in it and had not gone to get a sheet and had not gone to get a man.

# Chapter 755

## The Eighth Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman With A Barrow Up The Lane Asking About A Place That Has No Water And Nobody Answering Her

A woman came up the lane at about the seventh hour with her own barrow, which was not the yard's, and stopped it flat against the inside of the gate wall and stood with her hands on the handles, and nobody in this yard knew her and about four of them had seen her before in the summer and could not have said where.

**"I am not here about the road,"** she said, before anybody had asked her anything. **"I have come four miles and I am not here about the road. I want to ask one question and then I will go down the lane again and I will not come back up it this season."**

**"Ask it."**

**"The second place. You have all got it on a board with that place on it and no water at it, and I have seen the board because I have been past the gate four times since the flood and I cannot read a line, so I asked a woman at the seed house what it said. Is it going to be on the board at the end of next week."**

About eleven people in that yard were within earshot of it and not one of them answered her, and not one of them said that they were not going to, and the gap in the yard where the answer should have been lasted for about four seconds and then a man asked the man beside him a question about a course of stone and the yard closed over it.

She waited. Nobody told her to wait and nobody told her not to.

**"I am going to ask it once more because I have come four miles and I would like it to have been asked twice,"** she said. **"Is the second place going to have water in it. Not when it is convenient. Is it."**

**"I can take you to the pumps,"** Auret Sill said, from about nine feet off, which was the only thing anybody said to her for about half a minute.

**"No,"** the woman said. **"You have been at those pumps every morning since the flood. I know that. I have seen your coat on that road at six in the morning from a cart twice. I did not come four miles to be taken to the pumps."**

Auret Sill did not answer that either, and there was a moment about half a minute later when about nine people in this yard were visibly waiting for Marek Vale to say something and he was not at the middle table, he was at the north end of the yard with his back to all of them.

**"There is no water in the second place,"** he said, from the north end, without turning round. **"That is the whole of what I have got and it is the truth and I am not going to put anything round it."**

**"That is not an answer to what I asked."**

**"No,"** he said. **"It is not."**

And he did not turn round and did not come over, and about four people in that yard looked at the back of his coat and about four looked at the woman, and nobody said the second place would have water and nobody said it would not and nobody put a date on it and nobody said anything that could be written down afterwards as a promise, and the second place is still without water.

The woman stood at her barrow for about a minute with her hands on the handles, and then she lifted them off and turned round and looked at the boards from where she was standing, and about four feet of the yard was between her and the corner of the seed board, and she read what she could read of it from there.

**"It is on the route,"** she said. **"That is what that board says. It is on the route and it has no water."**

**"It is on the route and it has no water,"** Tova Reed said, from the seed house door, and did not go any further than that, and the woman nodded at her and went down the lane.

Nobody followed her down the lane. Not one person in this holding went out of that gate after her, and about six of them stood at the gate and watched her go and about eleven of them went back to what they were doing, and the two things have not touched each other in any way that anybody has been willing to describe since.

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the sixth hour, before she was at the gate, and went to the north end of the yard where the wall takes the wind off, and took a sheet out of the inside of his coat folded in four and unfolded it and wrote on it standing with his back to the yard.

He wrote for the length of about two minutes. He did not pause in it and he did not stop and start and he did not read anything back off it, and when he had finished he folded it in four again and put it inside his coat and buttoned it and turned round and came back to the middle table and put his hands flat on the four stones on the schedule.

About four people in that yard were within twenty feet of him while he did it and not one of them has ever said what they thought he was writing and about four of them have said since that they did not know there was anything on it at all. Nobody asked him about it then and nobody has asked him since and he has not offered it.

Nia Vale gave her two at about the eighth hour at the rack and gave them without one word on them.

**"Five hundred and eighteenth morning of the run. One thousand and three hundred and ninety-one hundredweight came down in the channel in the night."**

**"Up eight."**

**"Up eight. I have said that the only figure I have is what I am asked for and this morning nobody has asked me for anything and that is the morning I have had."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall about as far as he had to and gave the window in one line with both halves in it and went back up.

**"Four hundred and eighty-five, two hundred and forty-eight came and two hundred and thirty-seven stood, and the tall one came this morning and the short one came the morning before, and I have given up keeping track of which is which in my own head and I read it off that board anyway."**

Kellan Rusk gave the two stones from the step together, which he has not done before, and gave them and then stayed on the step.

**"Nine hundred and two at the near end, nine hundred and forty-nine at the top. And I am on this step and I am not coming down it today, because there is a woman at the gate who has asked this yard a question and been given the yard's own answer, and there is nothing in this holding this morning that needs me at the bottom of these steps."**

Odile Vray read the long sheet off Soren Rill's wall at about the ninth hour because he was at the fourth place with a mason and would not be back, and she came back to the boards and put the chalk in the tray.

**"Five hundred and fifteenth under the line, five hundred and sixteen at the head of it, five hundred and seventeenth over the line. And I am not going to be asked which end of it is the top, because I have read it once this morning and I read it from the bottom because that is the only way I can reach the bottom line."**

Tova Reed gave her four to Odile Vray at the boards at about the tenth hour, and Odile Vray wrote all four on her slate in her own hand and did not read one of them out, and gave the slate back.

**"Four hundred and twelve on the form. Three hundred and eighteen on the charter, which nobody will read in my lifetime. Four hundred and six on the second of Silling's two ruled lines, blank, and blank again tomorrow at four hundred and seven. Two hundred and fifteen on the letter, which is the highest that letter has ever stood at, and it is not a good thing and I have told you so four times now and you do not hear it."**

**"I hear it,"** Odile Vray said. **"I have written it down. I am not going to say it out loud."**

There was no pair on the north board on that morning and nobody went looking for one and the girl from the second place did not come into the yard at all that Monday, which is the first morning in nineteen that she has not, and nobody has asked her about it.

The barrow had gone eleven times by the fourth hour and was stood wheel first in the middle of the yard with the shafts up, and it was not wheeled out of that yard again on that day by anybody for any reason, and the woman's own barrow stayed flat against the gate wall until about the eleventh hour and then it was gone.

In the one room the drawer was shut with the key on its nail, and on the wall above the long table the blanks stand at thirty-nine with a second line under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and nobody in this holding has ever walked in and put anything under that line.

Six rows are ruled on the shelf for what nobody knows, and the use log in the tool house has fifteen lines in it with the sixteenth left unwritten and not going to be written.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall all day and nobody in that yard put a foot on it, and behind it the bare ground has taken nobody's boot and nobody's tape and nobody's price.

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past on that Monday by people on their way up to the wall and read on by none of them and offered to nobody, and nothing has ever been printed in this holding about what is left in the column at it.

And at about the eleventh hour the sheet from four days north was still lying on the middle table where it has lain since it was put down, undated, unpicked, unfilled in at the foot, and the offer was still lying out past the gate on the low board where it has lain since before the flood, and there is no date on that one either, and the two of them lay about nine feet apart with the four stones on the schedule between them and nothing at all happened on that morning to either of them, and nobody in this yard said one word to anybody about what was going to happen next.

# Chapter 753

## The Sixth Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman With A Slate Naming Out Loud The Thing That Goes First And Not Putting One Number On It

Sera Quill came to the boards before the seventh hour and did not wait to be asked for the count, because on a morning two modulo four the count moves and she has never once let a yard get through an hour without it.

**"A hundred and sixty-eight in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and thirty-nine not."**

**"Up one."**

**"Up one, and it goes up on the mornings it goes up on and on no other, and I am not going to say yesterday's to you to keep you company."** She turned the slate over and read the one that goes in this holding's book. **"One hundred and eleventh. That is the one in the book and the other one is thirteen from it and it is not on this morning and it will not be on any morning this one is on, and you have all had that answer six mornings running and you are all going to get it again."**

Then she put the slate down flat on the boards and left her hand on the edge of it, and about four people in that yard understood that she was not finished and the rest of them went on with what they were doing.

**"Now the other thing, and I am going to say it in a yard because that is where the things get said in this holding and I am not going to be the one who invents a quieter place to say it in. The margin. That is the word for it and I have been using it for eleven days without putting it in front of anybody, so today I am putting it in front of everybody."**

**"Whose margin."**

**"Ours. Not yours. Not the second place's either, though God help us, that place has more of one than anything else on this network.** She looked up and about nine faces in that yard were looking at her and about twenty-one were not. **"The outer branches. Every one of them past the third road, and the four at the end of the second branch, and the two houses on the top of the fourth road under a lean-to that nobody will name this morning. When this holding stands on its own legs, the inner ring and the north end and the yard itself get the draw, and the outer branches get a short measure, and the short measure is not a smaller share of the same thing. It is a different thing, and it is thinner, and it is what is left when the middle is served."**

**"That is what you have been saying for eleven days and you have not said it before."**

**"I have said it in this yard once and about four people heard me and one of them wrote nothing down, which I noticed, and I did not say it again because I am not a person who repeats things in front of thirty people to be sure."**

**"And where is the number on it."**

**"There is not one and I am not going to put one,"** Sera Quill said. **"That is the whole of what I came out here to do this morning. I am not going to tell you what the short measure costs in seed or in grain or in houses, not this morning and not in a yard, and if one of you writes a figure on it and brings it back to me I will not argue with you about the figure. I will simply say that the figure came from you and not from me, and that is all the difference I am able to make."**

**"You could have had it by now."**

**"Somebody could have had it by now,"** she said, **"and I have thought about what that sentence means for about eleven days and I am going to stop talking."**

She picked the slate up and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it and did not say anything else about it at any hour of the day, and about nine people in that yard went and looked at the middle table and about four of those nine read the sheet lying on it, and nobody in this yard said a word about whether that sheet had anything to do with what she had just named.

Odile Vray gave the two stones at about the eighth hour and the near one came out at a round figure for the first time since the wall went up, and she read it and waited.

**"Nine hundred at the near end. Nine hundred and forty-seven at the top. Forty-seven of masonry, as always."**

**"It is nine hundred."**

**"Nine hundred,"** she said. **"It is nine hundred. It has been at eight hundred and ninety-nine for two days and at nine hundred and one the day after that and I do not know why you are all standing about it, because a figure that lands on a round number is a figure that landed on a round number and nothing has happened to this wall and nothing is going to."**

**"It is a round number."**

**"It is a round number,"** Odile Vray said, **"and the top one is not, and if the two of you want to write a mark against the bottom one I will put it on your own wall and not on mine. Four inches of nothing. I have watched a wall for eleven years and I have never once written a mark because a number came out neat."**

A man from the middle row, who has been doing sums in his own head all morning and has been told to stop twice, said it out loud.

**"Nine hundred at the near board and four hundred and eighty-three of window and thirteen hundred and eighty-eight hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and that is a whole thing you could add and see what it is."**

**"No,"** Odile Vray said. **"You cannot add those three and I am going to say so out loud so that you have heard it said. That is the height of a wall at one end. That is a window, whatever a window is. And that is what came down the channel in a night, and it went back out of the channel before it was light. They are three numbers in three books in three heads and putting them in one column would make a fifth thing that is not a thing."**

**"I was not going to write it anywhere."**

**"You were doing it in your head and you said it out loud in a yard of thirty people, and that is how it gets into four other heads, and I have been standing at this board since before you could read a figure,"** she said. **"Do it somewhere else."**

The man who lays for three councils came down the middle road at about the ninth hour with his hod and gave his two without putting the hod down.

**"Five hundred and sixteenth morning of the run. One thousand and three hundred and eighty-eight hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and it is up eight, and the ninth course at the near end wants laying and I am not going to lay it to a number."**

**"To what, then."**

**"To the course under it,"** he said, **"which is how it has been laid since the wall went up and which is the only reason there is a wall."**

The girl from the second place had the chalk and the board and did both jobs at once and came out with the halves the wrong way round and stopped with the chalk still on the stone.

**"Four hundred and eighty-three of window. No. Wait. Two hundred and thirty-six stood and two hundred and forty-seven came. And I said them wrong and I have said them right and both of those have happened in this yard at the same board, and I am not going to rub it out and I am not going to put a line under it, because that is what the second rule under the blanks in the one room is for and nobody is going to use it this morning."**

Sera Quill read the long sheet off the wall at about the tenth hour, because Soren Rill had gone up the middle road at nine and would not be back before the dark, and she stood at the boards with his own chalk and read it in the ordinary voice.

**"Five hundred and thirteenth under the line, five hundred and fourteenth at the head, five hundred and fifteenth over the line."**

**"You have never read that in a yard."**

**"I have read four figures off a corner of a seed board every morning for eleven years and I have never once read anything off a wall, and I am telling the yard that so that it does not sound like a ceremony,"** Sera Quill said. **"And I am not going to be asked which end is the top because I do not know and neither does he."**

Kellan Rusk came out with the register form under his arm and the door behind him shut and said his figure from the step before anybody had come near him.

**"Four hundred and ten on the form. And the sheet that came up the lane yesterday is not in here, it is not in my drawer, it is not in this room, and I am not going to be asked to put it in here and I am not going to be asked whether I could."**

**"Could you."**

**"I could in about nine seconds,"** he said, **"and the key has been on that nail since before you were born and it is going to be on it at the dark as well, and I am telling you I could so that you stop thinking about it."**

Tova Reed gave the other three to the crew man at about the tenth hour, standing at the seed house door with her face to him and the trays behind her going out on their own.

**"Three hundred and sixteen on the charter, which is the oldest of them. And four hundred and four on the ruled line Silling ruled second, and it is blank, and it will be blank at four hundred and five tomorrow with nothing on it either. I have given those to three different people this week without being asked once by any of them, and I have not given them once to the man who keeps the book, and that is not an accident and I would like him to know it is not an accident."**

**"I did not ask."**

**"No,"** Tova Reed said. **"You did not ask, and that is the reason, and I am not going to be thanked for it in a yard."**

**"Two hundred and thirteen on the letter,"** she said, and turned and went back inside, and he stood at the seed house door for the length of a breath afterwards and then went to look at the ring.

On the wall of the one room the second rule stands open and empty under the thirty-nine blanks, at the thirty-ninth time as it has stood at the thirty-ninth time for four years, and the girl from the second place went past it on her way out and did not touch it. Six rows are ruled on the shelf for the things nobody knows and there is no seventh cut for anything in this holding. Four sheets lie on the long table, none entered, none refused and no column cut under any of them, and the paper that is not one of those four has a woman's refusal on it in her own hand.

The barrow went down the lane and back eleven times and the man pushing it said that a round number on a wall is not a licence for anything.

Behind the tap house there is a joint that has wept one drop into a saucer every morning of this holding's record and it wept one drop into that saucer on that Saturday, and the saucer kept it, and it proved nothing about that morning or about any morning of the week.

And at about the eleventh hour Sera Quill took her slate out from under her arm and looked at the two boards, the one with the pair on it under a frame nine months old and the one with the schedule and the sheet and the four stones on it, and said nothing about either of them, and about nine people in that yard were still saying the word margin out loud to each other at about nine inches a piece, and nobody had put a single number on it.

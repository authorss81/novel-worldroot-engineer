# Chapter 750

## The Third Of The Twenty-First, And A Sheet Carried Up The Low Road By A Man Who Had Done The Talking Without Anybody From This Yard In It

Roan Selk came up the low road for the third time this season and he did it in one day this time instead of three, and he came up it without the long sheet rolled under his arm, which was the first thing about him that about four people at the gate noticed.

**"I have not brought it to be agreed to,"** he said, before he had the gate shut behind him. **"I have brought a sheet that nine people at the top of the fourth path wrote themselves, and I have carried it, and I am going to put it on your boards and read it out once, and then it is a thing that exists and I am not going to be asked to defend it."**

**"Where did you do the talking."**

**"At a fallen wall at the top of the fourth path, in the open, with four of them and the man from the fourth place and me. You were not at it. Nobody from this yard was at it. You were not sent and nobody asked whether you wanted to be sent, and I did not send anybody, and I would like that said out loud before anybody in this yard goes and looks at you as though I had done something behind your back."**

Marek Vale had come up the lane at about the seventh hour and was at the north end of the boards by the time the sheet was unrolled, and he did not touch it and stood back from it about the width of a hand.

**"Say it, then."**

Roan Selk would not sit down. He put the sheet on the boards end to end and held the two far corners with his own hands until Odile Vray put a stone on each of them, and then he took his hands off and stood where he could see all of the faces and not one of the hands.

> **THE TERMS OF THE PLACE AT THE TOP OF THE FOURTH PATH, WRITTEN BY THE NINE OF THEM WHO LIVE THERE, IN THEIR OWN HANDS, IN THE ORDER THEY WROTE THEM.**
>
> One. We are not coming in. Nobody from the top of this path joins your ring, your schedule, your count or your network, and nobody here is going to be asked to join any of them, and we are not asking you to join ours.
>
> Two. We will keep a route open. Water or seed or a cart or a body on a stretcher, whichever is asked for, and whichever we have, along the fourth path, day or night, without a count and without a column cut under it.
>
> Three. Nothing of ours comes down that path into a book. No figure of ours is entered in your ledger, on your boards, in the form, on the charter or on the letter, and no one of the nine of us is to be counted in any total that this holding keeps.
>
> Four. You do not come up. Not to look, not to count, not to walk the ground, and not to be told what is on it, and if one of you comes up the path we will send you down it again and it will not be unkind.
>
> Five. Nine of us wrote this and it is nine of us and it is not a council and it is not a body and it is not anybody else's anything. We have not asked the man at the head of your channel to agree to it and he has not been asked and we will not ask him.

He read it out once and did not read it again, and about twenty people in that yard had heard it, and nobody spoke for the length of about four seconds.

Then a man from the fourth place said that a route with no figure on it was not a route, it was a favour, and a favour can be stopped by anybody with a bad morning.

**"That is true,"** Roan Selk said. **"It can be stopped by us on a bad morning and it can be stopped by you on a bad morning, and neither of us has any paper that makes us stop stopping, and that is what you have got. I am not going to dress it up as a thing with a term in it. You want a term and there is not one and that is why I am standing here with nothing in my hands."**

**"You have put four in writing and the fourth of them says we are not to come up."**

**"The fourth of them says it because the ground up there is not ours to be walked on and the people up there are not ours to be counted, and I have seen what your counting does to a place in nine years and I am not standing here offering it to them as a kindness."**

**"Then what is it. You have said a route and said no figures and said stay out. That is not connected. That is a man shouting at a road."**

**"Then it is a man shouting at a road,"** Roan Selk said. **"And I have said it in a yard because a sheet that says it has to be said somewhere, and I would rather say it in front of you than down a lane where one of you finds it in a week and thinks we had been hiding it."**

Auret Sill came in from the pumps and asked whether any of it meant there was going to be a cistern at the top of the fourth path, and Roan Selk told her that there were people at the top of the fourth path and that what they had was not his to describe.

**"You have just read it out in a yard."**

**"I have read out what they wrote. I have not read out what they have. If you want to know what they have you will have to go up there yourself and you will have to be turned round, and I would rather you knew that now than on a Friday."**

**"They are the ones the root woke,"** Roan Selk said. **"That is the only sentence I have ever been given about it and I am giving it to you in a yard with twenty people in it, and neither they nor I am going to say a word more about what it means, and I would be obliged if nobody in this yard went up that path to find out."**

Nobody in that yard used the word absorbed or built anything out of it, and nobody said it was a way out of anything and nobody said it fixed anything, and about four people who had said something in the last fortnight about there being no way through this that anybody had found were careful on this morning to say nothing at all about that either.

Marek Vale had read the sheet on his own from the north end and had not taken his eyes off the fifth line.

**"They have not asked me for anything,"** he said, to nobody in particular. **"I want that noticed. In nine weeks of this holding I have not been handed a piece of paper by anybody at all, and this morning there is one on my boards and there is not one line of it that wants anything from me, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that I have checked it properly, because I have read it twice and I am not the man who would know."**

**"Is it a good thing."**

**"I do not know what it is,"** he said, **"and I am not going to tell this yard that it is, and I am certainly not going to tell it that it makes the second place any wetter, because it does not and nobody up that path has said it does."**

Auret Sill gave the two she had off the pump house slate and gave them while she was still pulling her arm out of her coat, which she has not done before.

**"One thousand and three hundred and seventy-seven hundredweight in the night and the run is on the five hundred and thirteenth. Down five. And the reason I am talking before I have taken my coat off is that I have been at the second place since before light and there is still nothing in the pumps there, and that is not what this sheet is about and I am not going to stand here and make it into what this sheet is about."**

Odile Vray gave the window from memory with her back to the board again, because about nine people had asked her yesterday whether she was going to do that again and she had said she might.

**"Four hundred and eighty of window, and I have got it from the same place I get the two stones and neither of them has moved on me in a fortnight. Harlan will tell you which half came because he can see it and I cannot."**

**"Two hundred and thirty-five came in the night,"** Harlan Vetch said from off the wall, **"and two hundred and forty-five stood, and the lower one is the one that comes, and I have been on this wall long enough to know that much, and I do not know what a window is for."**

Roan Selk read the two stones off the wall himself at about the ninth hour and did not ask for the chalk board to be held up for him this time, and got both of them off the wall unaided and wrote the far one on the back of his own hand with a nail.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-seven at the near end and nine hundred and forty-four at the top. I wrote that on my own hand because I was not going to ask that man to hold anything up for me twice in one season, and I have been wrong about these two before and I would rather have a number written on me than on a board somebody else is holding."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall and wiped the chalk off with a cloth afterwards, which is not a thing anybody in this yard has seen him do.

**"Five hundred and tenth underneath, five hundred and eleven at the head, five hundred and twelfth over the line."**

**"Are you all right."**

**"I am exactly as I am every morning and I have a cloth now because the chalk was getting on the paper of that sheet and I did not want it on their terms."**

The girl from the second place put the pair up on the north board at the seventh hour without anybody asking her to, two hundred and three above the rule and four hundred and thirty-seven beneath it, under a heading that is a month nine months old and has been that heading since before she came to work here. Nobody stood at that trough. She looked at it once, put the chalk in her pocket and went and took the far end of a hod.

Tova Reed gave her four to a man at the seed house door who did not want them and told her so twice, and she gave them anyway and wrote each one on the corner as she said it and did not ask him a second time about any of them.

**"Four hundred and seven on the form. Three hundred and thirteen on the charter. Four hundred and one on the lower of Silling's two ruled lines, and there is nothing on it, and I am telling you because somebody in this yard told me yesterday that it was going to be four hundred and that somebody ought to write something on it, and nothing has been written on it, and I would like the whole of this yard to know that nothing was written on it by me."**

**"Who wanted it written on?"**

**"A man from the third place who wanted to put a pencil on a round number,"** she said, **"and I do not know what he wants to call it now and neither does he."**

**"Two hundred and ten on the letter,"** she said, before he had asked, **"and that is the fourth and that is all there is."**

The drawer in the one room was shut at every hour of that Wednesday, and at the dark Kellan Rusk turned the key and hung it back on its nail.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped, and the man pushing it said that a road you could shout down is not a road you could count, and that neither of those is a fault in a road.

Nothing has been let out of the compost line in thirty-one or in anything, and the column that would take it has been empty since before the spring. The ladder stayed against the tool house wall all morning and nobody put a foot on the bottom of it.

And Roan Selk rolled up nothing, because there was nothing rolled up, and took the terms back down the low road in one day with the stones lifted off them by Odile Vray and no stone carried back, and he stopped at the gate on his way out and said one sentence to the man who lays for three councils, who was there with his hod.

**"You can have a road that is not a road,"** he said. **"I have had eleven of them this season and this is the first one that has said out loud that it is one. Do not come and tell me what it is worth."**

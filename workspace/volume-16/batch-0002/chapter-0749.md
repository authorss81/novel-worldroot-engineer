# Chapter 749

## The Second Of The Twenty-First, And A Ruled Line Standing At A Round Hundred With Nothing On It And The Woman Who Keeps The Book Saying So First

Tova Reed had the book on the middle table at about the sixth hour and did not open it, and stood with both hands flat on the cover, and about nine people in that yard came and looked at her doing it before anybody thought to ask her what she was doing.

**"It is at a round hundred,"** she said. **"It has been at nothing since Silling put the book down and it is at a round hundred as of last night, and I am telling this yard that before anybody asks me, because I know exactly what four or five of you are about to do with a round hundred on a line that has been empty since the flood."**

**"We are not doing anything with it."**

**"You are thinking about doing something with it. That is a different thing and I can see it happening at about the pace of the water in the channel, so I will say the rest of it now and then nobody has to work it out at me all morning."** She took her hands off the cover and did not open the book. **"Nothing is going on that line. Not this morning, not at the end of the week, not while I keep this book. And nobody in this holding is to help me by putting something there. If you want to mark a round hundred you mark it in your own hand on your own paper and you leave that book alone."**

A man from the third place, who had been in the yard since the fifth hour and had heard about a round number before breakfast, said that a round hundred on a line nobody had ever written on was worth a pencil.

**"It is worth nothing at all,"** Tova Reed said. **"That is the whole of what it is worth. I want to say that carefully, because you have not said anything wrong and I do not want you standing there thinking you have. There is no mark on it. There is no milestone on it. It is not a sign of anything and it is not an invitation to anything, and the only person who could turn it into one of those is me, and I am not going to."**

**"Then what is it to anybody."**

**"It is a line Silling ruled in a book and put down, and I keep the book and I keep the count of it, and I have said the count out loud in this yard every morning for as long as I have kept it, and nobody has ever asked me to put anything on the line, including the two of you."**

**"Four hundred on the form too."**

**"Four hundred and six on the form,"** she said, **"and that one is not round and it is in a drawer with a key on a nail, and you are not comparing them, and if you do compare them in a yard I will take the book inside and you can all have two figures in your heads instead of one."**

Kellan Rusk was on the step with the door behind him shut and the key already back where it hangs, and he gave the window from up in his own head without going in to look at anything, which was the second time this week he has offered something out loud that he did not have to.

**"Four hundred and seventy-nine of window. Two hundred and thirty-four came in the night and two hundred and forty-five stood, and I know that is the wrong way round because I have heard her say it the right way round for eleven weeks and I would have got it right if I had thought about it."**

**"Then that is one wrong out of two this morning,"** the mason said, **"and I will take it and you need not carry it about."**

**"Two hundred and forty-five came and two hundred and thirty-four stood,"** Nia Vale said, from the rack, without looking up and without any particular edge on it. **"I do not need to be handed it. I had four hundred and seventy-eight yesterday and I have watched those two move about for four years, and yours is backwards, and the tall one is the one that came."**

Emrys Dole and Ismay Rooke came in together off the middle road and read the two stones off the corner of the seed board standing shoulder to shoulder, which they have done about nine mornings running and have never once agreed on which of them was the near one until the mason put his thumb on it.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-six at this end,"** Ismay Rooke said.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-six at the top,"** Emrys Dole said.

**"You have both got the same number and one of you has got the wrong end of the wall."**

**"Nine hundred and forty-three at the other,"** Emrys Dole said, **"and I know what you are going to say, and I am not going to be corrected by a mason holding a thumb on a board."**

**"Nine hundred and forty-three at the top,"** the mason said. **"It is your number. It has been your number every morning this week and you have been giving it to me to get corrected and today I have got nothing to correct."**

They argued about it for another minute and got it exactly right and neither of them would let the other one say so out loud.

Sera Quill came at the seventh hour and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it, and she turned the slate over and gave the count and did not offer the other of the two numbers to anybody, and two people asked for it.

**"A hundred and sixty-seven in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and thirty-eight not. And it does not go up except on the mornings it goes up on, and I am not going to read you yesterday's number this morning on the grounds that you would like a figure to hold on to."**

**"Then what do we hold."**

**"You hold the fact that it has not moved,"** Sera Quill said. **"That is a morning's work and it is worth more than yesterday's number and I have never once been thanked for either of them."**

She turned the slate a second time and read the one that goes in this holding's book.

**"One hundred and tenth. It is in the book, it is in mine, and it is thirteen from the other one and the other one is not on this morning and will not be on any morning that mine is on."**

**"You could have both and nobody would know."**

**"You would know,"** she said, **"and I would, and about four people in this yard would, and that is three more than the number of people a carried figure should reach."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall at about the eighth hour and came away from it and did not take a slate from anybody, and about eleven people heard him and nobody had asked.

**"Five hundred and ninth underneath, five hundred and ten on the head of it, five hundred and eleventh over the line. I have got my own slate back and I am going to say it off the wall in front of nine people for once in my life, and if two of you want to make a thing of the order then you may do it on the wall and not through me."**

Nia Vale gave the two she keeps at about the ninth hour, standing at the rack with her thumb where it has been, and gave them in a way that about four people had not heard before, which is that she said the figure and then waited until the mason had written it before she said anything else.

**"One thousand and three hundred and eighty-two hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and the run is on the five hundred and twelfth morning of it, and it is eight up because it is an even morning, and the mason has written it, and thank you."**

Nobody in this yard thanked her for the figures and she did not appear to want it, and she said thank you to the mason for writing and meant that one.

Tova Reed kept the book on the middle table all morning and it was not opened at any hour and nothing was written on the lower of its two ruled lines, and at about the tenth hour she gave the rest of the corner of her board to a woman from the second place who stood in front of her and got all of it first time.

**"Three hundred and twelve on the charter, and it is the oldest one and it is not going to be read by anybody in my lifetime. Four hundred and six on the form, and I have said and I will say again that the two fours are not the same four. And two hundred and nine on the letter, which is the last of the three and I am telling you they are not in the order I write them."**

**"They are never in the order you write them."**

**"No. And if you ever get a fourth one off me, that is the day you know I have stopped."**

Six ruled rows stood on the shelf in the one room for the things nobody in this holding knows, and there is no seventh row cut for the schedule or the load or the cap or the custody or for anything else, and the third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved, with no sixth term written on it and no seventh and no eighth.

Four sheets lay on the long table inside the doorway, none entered, none refused and no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper on that table is a woman's own sheet with a refusal written on it in her own hand and it is not one of the four and it never has been.

The barrow went down the lane and back eleven times and stopped at the fourth hour of the middle of the day whatever the rest of that yard wanted done with its afternoon.

Nobody in that yard went near the ladder at the tool house wall, and no figure was printed against either of the two people it stands for.

And at about the eleventh hour Tova Reed picked the book up off the middle table and carried it inside against her hip, and nobody in that yard saw what was on the second of its two ruled lines, because there was nothing on it, and about four of the people who had come and looked at her about it that morning went and looked at the low board out past the gate instead, where the offer still lay with no date on it and nothing in the space at the foot of it, and neither of them said a word about either of the two things they had just looked at.

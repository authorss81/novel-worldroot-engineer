# Chapter 752

## The Fifth Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman Put A Sheet On The Middle Table And Took Her Hands Off It And Nobody Lifted It

She came in through the gate on foot at about the seventh hour with a clerk from an office four days north a pace behind her carrying nothing, and about two people in that yard knew her before anybody said anything.

**"That is the Chancellor,"** the fourth place's man said from the step. **"That is Iona Vey and she has been here before and she is not new in this county whatever anybody at this gate has been telling himself."**

She was about fifty-five and she had walked the half mile from wherever the horse was left and she did not stop at the long table on her way in. She went to the middle table, where the schedule was lying under four stones, and she squared her own sheet against the edge of the wood with the side of her hand and put her hands at her sides.

The clerk from up a lane came up behind her and did not sit down and did not put his hat anywhere, and about nine people in that yard knew that he had walked up that lane on his own legs and had been paid for the day by nobody.

**"You have had three documents from me in four years,"** she said. **"You have entered none of them, you have found no fault in any of them, and I have read the copies of that from four days north and I am not here about it."**

She put one finger on the sheet where her own name was at the head of it and did not move it off.

**"That is the paper that has been lying on your low board since before the flood. Every line of it is on that paper. There is one line at the foot of it that was not at the foot of it before, and I am going to read you that line and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to explain it and I am not going to argue for it, because I have argued for it twice in four years and I have never once had to."**

She read it out from where she could see it without leaning.

**"The link passes to the engineer of record and holds until it is given back. Not one season. Not a number of seasons. Until it is given back, and there is no term of notice on either side of it, and no council under this may reopen a branch, and the central harvest of the completed phase is guaranteed for as long as the link stands. And under the second, the Crown is given continuous access to the regional flow ledger and to every figure in it, and it is not a copy and it is not a report."**

She took her finger off the sheet and put her hand flat on the middle of the table and left it there for the length of about three seconds.

**"That is the only line I have added and I have not come down this lane to be asked about it and I am not going to stay in this yard to watch anybody not pick it up."**

**"Why the same paper."**

**"Because it is the same paper,"** she said. **"I have not written you a second offer and I am not going to. If I had written you a second one you would spend a week deciding which of the two was worse and you would be a week further from the only question on it, and I have watched four counties do that twice."**

She turned round and looked at the yard, which had about thirty people in it and had stopped in three places at once.

**"I am not asking anybody in this yard to choose anything today. I have said a thing out loud and I have put a sheet down and both of those are finished. If you want to say no to me, say it to me or do not say it at all, and I will not come back for it, and I will not send a clerk for it."**

**"Will you send somebody."**

**"I will send the clerk,"** she said, **"because that is what he is for and it is not interesting to either of us. That is not me coming back. Do not make me the same thing as him."**

And she went out past the long table and out of the gate at her own pace, and the clerk from up a lane followed her at the same pace, and about four people at the gate had been standing for about a minute and a half before the last of them went.

Nobody lifted the sheet. It lay on the middle table in the flat of its own paper with the schedule beside it under four stones, and nobody turned it over, and nobody put a date at the head of it, and nobody wrote in the space at the foot of it, and nobody carried it to the long table inside the doorway where the four sheets lie that are none of them entered and none of them refused.

Nia Vale was asked at about the eighth hour to read the window and said no.

**"Not while that is on that table. I will read you a figure when there is one thing happening in this yard and not two, and I have watched two people read two numbers in the same breath in this yard and one of them got one of them wrong and never found out."**

She read it at about the ninth hour instead, from the rack, with nobody else in front of her.

**"Four hundred and eighty-two of window. Two hundred and thirty-six came and two hundred and forty-six stood, and I have that pair backwards in my own head more mornings than I have it right, and I got it right this morning only because you wrote both of them down in front of me."**

Harlan Vetch read both stones off the wall in one go for the first time this season, without coming down for either of them, and had to shout the second one.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-nine at the near end and nine hundred and forty-six at the top. Forty-seven of masonry and forty-seven since the wall went up, and I am not giving them one at a time any more, and whoever wants to write them down can write them down."**

**"You have never given them together."**

**"I have never given them together because for nine years two men have given them one at a time and had an argument about it, and I have decided this morning that the argument is worth less than the walking."**

Hanne Brack read her two off the corner of the seed board at about the tenth hour because there was a stranger in the yard on that morning and she wanted a stranger's own figures in front of her, which she has never had cause to do.

**"Five hundred and fifteenth morning of the run. One thousand and three hundred and eighty hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and that is two hundredweight under what stood there two mornings ago, and it is the sixth morning running that it has not come to the same number twice. That is an odd morning and this is one, and I am saying it out loud for a woman who has not stood in this yard before and who has to copy it into a book four days away."**

**"You know the rule."**

**"I know that it goes up on one kind of morning and down on the other and I have not worked out the kind,"** Hanne Brack said, **"and I would have told you I had if I had, because you would have believed me."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet out of his own wall at about the tenth hour to the yard and to the gate, where there were still about four people standing, and he read it in his own order and the only person who said anything about it afterwards was the Chancellor, who had not gone yet.

**"Five hundred and twelfth under the line, five hundred and thirteen at the head, five hundred and fourteenth over."**

**"Three numbers on a wall,"** Iona Vey said from about nine feet off. **"And no unit on any of them, and no year on any of them, and no column cut under any of them. I have read your form and your charter and your letter and none of the three has a unit either. Do you know that I have never once been able to say what any of it is for."**

**"No,"** Soren Rill said. **"I have been in this yard twenty years and I could not tell you what it is for either and that has not stopped me reading it."**

**"It has not stopped me writing it either,"** she said, and went out of the gate.

Tova Reed came out of the north door with the corner of the board under her arm and stopped about four feet short of the middle table, because there was a stranger standing at it, and she stood where she could see her mouth.

**"You will want the four ages,"** she said. **"They are on a corner of this board and I have said them out loud in this yard every morning for as long as I have kept them, and I will say them to you as I would to anybody, and I am not going to pretend that is a special thing to do."**

She said them without writing them down.

**"Four hundred and nine on the form. Three hundred and fifteen on the charter, and that one is the oldest. Four hundred and three on the ruled line under Silling's first one, and there is nothing on it, and there is going to be nothing on it. And two hundred and twelve on the letter."**

**"You wrote them in a different order than the one I have."**

**"I always write them in a different order,"** Tova Reed said. **"That is not a courtesy to you and it is not a courtesy to anybody. You are going to write them down in your own order or you are going to stand there and remember four numbers, and one of those two is worth something and the other is worth nothing."**

**"You are the only person in this yard who will say a figure to a stranger."**

**"I am the only person in this yard who keeps four that a stranger has any business having,"** she said. **"There is a difference and you can have both of them and I am not going to choose between them for you."**

Nobody thanked the seed keeper for the four and no hand went near her arm at any hour of that morning, and about four people in that yard were watching to see whether one would and it did not, and nobody has ever said afterwards that it nearly did.

At about the seventh hour the girl from the second place chalked two hundred and four over the rule on the north board and four hundred and thirty-nine under it. The frame above that chalk is the frame it has carried for four years and it took no notice whatever of a woman in a good coat at the middle table. There was nobody at the trough, and she read the two of them to nobody, and she went back down the branch with her own chalk.

The compost line is still paid at thirty-one and there is no column on that board showing what has come out of it, and there has not been one since before the spring.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall that Friday and nobody in that yard put a foot on the bottom of it, and no figure was printed against either of the two people it stands for.

Nobody fetched the man of about seventy on that Friday, and the count of questions this holding has put to him in four years is not one and was not one at any hour of that day.

And out past the gate the offer was still lying on the low board, undated, unpicked and not withdrawn, and the sheet on the middle table inside had not gone anywhere either, and by the end of the afternoon about four people had walked past both of them on their way out, and every one of those four knew what the paper out past the gate says and not one of them knew whether anybody had put a date on the sheet inside.

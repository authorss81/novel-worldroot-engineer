# Chapter 754

## The Seventh Of The Twenty-First, And A Woman At The Pumps Saying The Best Thing Anybody In This Holding Had To Say For It

It was a Sunday and about half the yard was in it and Auret Sill came up the lane at about the sixth hour with her coat open and her slate under her arm and did not go to the pumps first, which she has done every morning since the flood.

She stood at the middle table on the side where the schedule was lying with the four stones on it and the sheet from four days north lying beside it where it has been lying since it arrived, and she did not touch either of them.

**"I have thought about this for eleven days and I am going to say it in this yard on a Sunday, which means about twenty of you will hear it once and about nine of you will hear it twice from other people before the week is out, and I have decided that I would rather say it wrong and twice than not say it. Marek is not here yet. He will be here at seven. I am not waiting for him and I am not waiting for him on purpose."**

**"Then get it said."**

**"If this holding goes in on that paper, the centre is fed this season. Not next season. This season. In writing, at a figure nobody in this yard will know until the autumn, and a figure that is a floor and not a hope."**

Nobody in that yard said anything, and about four of the people at the table had already worked out where it was going and about nine had not.

**"I have been at the second place every morning since before light for eleven days and there is still nothing in those pumps, and I have written the second place on a board in the seed keeper's hand with no water at it and a route that goes to the door and stops there. I have been the person at those pumps. I want a yard full of people to hear me say that if we do this, then this season there is a floor under the whole of this region including the outer branches, and the outer branches go on a short measure. A short measure with a floor under it is a better thing than a full measure with nothing behind it, and I have watched four of them at the end of the second branch go into the middle of this week without a number to go on, and I am not able to unsee that."**

**"That is the best argument anybody has made for it."**

**"It is the only argument anybody has made for it,"** Auret Sill said. **"That is why I have waited eleven days to say it and why I am saying it on a Sunday, because on a weekday one of you would have had a reply ready by the second hour."**

She put both hands flat on the table on either side of the four stones and looked down at the schedule for a moment.

**"And the other half of it, and I will say it because I will not be a person who says half a thing. The short measure under a floor is a short measure this holding hands out itself, with no outside hand over it, and it goes to whoever is hungriest at the end of a week, and there are four of them I have in my head and I am not going to say their names in a yard on a Sunday. That is the true shape of it and both halves of it are true and I am not going to sit down and pretend I have only found the one I like."**

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the seventh hour and stood at about nine feet off the north end of the table with the crease of the schedule under his thumb, and he listened to the whole thing, and he did not answer it.

About nine people in that yard looked at him waiting for him to say that she was wrong, and he did not say that, and nobody said it either, and about four people who had expected the whole thing to end in a fight with somebody saying she was wrong looked at each other and found that nobody was going to.

A woman from the middle row said that the guarantee was the argument and the guarantee was a floor and a floor was worth having.

**"That is the argument,"** Auret Sill said. **"I am not going to soften it now that it is in the open and I am not going to be talked out of it either. I have said what I have said and I have said the other half of it and I am not going to be asked by anybody in this yard to choose which half I meant."**

The crew man came in from the ring at about the eighth hour with his book shut under his arm and did not read anything out of it at the boards, which he has not done since the spring, and he read it at the sill instead, to about three people, and did not raise his voice.

**"Five hundred and seventeenth morning of the run. One thousand and three hundred and eighty-three hundredweight came down in the night, down five."**

**"You are not saying that at the boards."**

**"I am not saying that at the boards on a Sunday morning when there is a thing being said that is bigger than my book,"** he said, **"and I have said the pair to three people at the sill and that is all of it today and I do not want one of those three telling it to a fourth."**

Emrys Dole came in from the middle road and gave the two stones without stopping and gave them in the middle of the argument, which he has done about nine mornings running and which is where he does his best work.

**"Nine hundred and one at the near end and nine hundred and forty-eight at the top. And I am going to say one thing about a guarantee and then I am going to go and lay. A guarantee is worth what is written against it, and there is not one column cut under any of the four sheets in this holding, and that has been the standing of this yard for four years, and I would not put a season of four houses on the strength of a paper with nothing ruled under it."**

**"I did not say it was ruled."**

**"No. You did not. And I have listened to you from the sixth hour and I am not putting a floor under it or taking one off it,"** Emrys Dole said. **"That is all I have. I am not answering you and I am not agreeing with you. I am laying."**

The girl from the second place gave the window off the board at about the ninth hour in the ordinary way, with the chalk in her hand and no reading at that trough behind it and nobody standing at it.

**"Four hundred and eighty-four. Two hundred and thirty-seven came and two hundred and forty-seven stood. And I had them wrong yesterday morning at this board, and I have said so out loud so that yesterday is still standing and is not rubbed out."**

The girl wrote the pair on the north board on her way out at about the tenth hour, under a frame that is a month nine months back, two hundred and five over the rule and four hundred and forty-one under it, and there was nobody at that trough and there has not been one for a long time and she read them to nobody.

**"Are you not going to read them."**

**"No,"** she said. **"Nobody reads them and I am not going to start on a morning when somebody has said the truest thing anybody has said in eleven days. There will be another morning."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall at about the tenth hour to a yard that was quieter than it had been at seven, and nobody asked him a single question about it.

**"Five hundred and fourteenth under the line, five hundred and fifteen at the head, five hundred and sixteenth over the line."**

**"Nothing from you."**

**"Nothing from me at all,"** he said. **"That is not a change in me and it is not me siding with anybody. I read three numbers on a wall. I have read them every morning for twenty years and I am not standing in a yard this morning pretending that the fact of me reading them out has settled something."**

Tova Reed came out of the north door with a tray on each hip and gave her four to a man from the third place who had come over to help with the door and had not asked for anything.

**"Three hundred and seventeen on the charter. Four hundred and eleven on the form. Four hundred and five on the ruled line under Silling's first one, and it is blank. Two hundred and fourteen on the letter."**

**"You are not writing them down."**

**"I am not writing them down for you today,"** she said. **"There are too many people in this yard and one of them has just said the truest thing anybody has said in eleven days, and I am not going to be stood here writing numbers while that is still in the air. I have given you four and they are correct and they will be the same in the morning."**

She went back inside with the empty trays and the man stood in the doorway for a moment and then carried on with the door, and about four people in that yard noticed that she had not asked him a second time about any of the four and about four of those people did not know what they had been looking at.

Nobody thanked her for the four and nobody thanked Auret Sill for the thing she said at six o'clock.

The barrow stood wheel first in the middle of that yard at eleven journeys, and nobody has taken it out of that gate on a twelfth, and the man pushing it said on Saturday and again on Sunday that eleven is the number and not the mood.

Thirty-one is what the compost line is paid at and there is no second column on that board to write anything in.

The six ruled rows on the shelf are the six, and no seventh has been cut for the schedule or the load or the cap or the custody or for anything else.

The drawer in the one room was shut at every hour and the key was on its nail at the dark. The man of about seventy was not fetched on that Sunday and nothing whatever was put to him from the sixth hour to the dark, and the count of questions this holding has put to him in four years is not one.

And at about the eleventh hour Auret Sill went back down the lane to the pumps and did not look at the middle table or the sheet on it on her way out, and the yard went on doing what it was doing, and about nine people in it were still turning her sentence over and about nine had decided that it was a good sentence and about four had decided that it was a cowardly one and about eleven had not yet had an opinion and were not going to be asked for one by anybody.

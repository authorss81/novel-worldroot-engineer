# Chapter 746

## The Twenty-Ninth Of The Twentieth, And A Question Put The Second Time At The Boards

The man from the middle row put it the second time at about the sixth hour, in front of the same thirty people who had been standing at those boards when he put it the first time, and this time he had come with a reason in his hand instead of a question only.

**"Yesterday you stood at that table and said you would not appoint one of the two men at the end of that joint and be the man who did it. That was at the eleventh hour and about thirty of us heard it. Since then I have been at four houses and three of them have a son who works on the head and every one of those three has now told his house that there is nobody over it. I am not asking you to say a different word. I am asking you to say the same word with the reason on it, so that I can carry it up the lane and not have to invent one."**

Marek Vale had his hand flat on the crease of the schedule and the four stones still holding its corners where the wind at the gate end had put them.

**"Then here is the reason, and it is not a long one. A person who is in charge of that joint is a person who has said they are in charge of it. Neither of the two men at that head has said it to me and I am not going up there this morning to ask either of them in front of a yard and turn whatever they say into an appointment made by me. If somebody at that head is in charge of it, then that is a thing they say out loud, and it does not have to be said to me, and I am not going to be the one who picks which of them said it. That is all of it."**

**"That is a door with nothing behind it."**

**"It is a door, and the handle is on the far side, and I have not got the key and I am not going to forge one in front of thirty people."**

**"You have said that in a yard before and it came out of your mouth and nobody wrote it down and nobody carried it anywhere, and I am the man who has to carry it."**

**"Then carry this one and not that one."** He took his hand off the crease and put it flat on the middle of the sheet. **"And here is the other half of it, in this yard, because you asked me to say it again with the reason on it and the reason is no good without this half. I have not refused the network. I have not refused the schedule on this table. I have not refused the load and I have not refused the four quiet hours and I have not refused the fifth hour the sluice road put on line one in his own hand. All five lines stand exactly where they stood yesterday and I am not going to move one of them to pay for a joint at the top of the middle road."**

**"So the sheet does not care about the joint."**

**"The sheet is work. The joint is a place where water is decided on and nobody is deciding. Those are two different problems and one of them does not cancel the other, and if you take a line off that paper to get somebody appointed then you will have taken a line off it to get somebody appointed, and that is what a holding's record will say."**

Auret Sill had the pumps at the second place open by then and had walked up the lane with her slate under her arm and her own two canvases still damp, and she asked the question that everybody else in that yard had been holding since the sixth hour.

**"Then who is standing at the head of it at the fourth hour of the morning."**

**"Nobody."**

**"You have said that very plainly."**

**"I have said it plainly because it is plainly the case and I would rather it was said out loud by me than found out at four in the morning by somebody standing in a foot of water."**

**"That is the first thing you have said this week that frightens me."**

**"It should,"** Marek Vale said. **"I am frightened of it too and I have not got a way round it and I am not going to manufacture one out of a man who has not offered."**

Nobody in that yard chose a side on it and nobody thanked either of them and nobody said that he had been wrong, and about four people went and stood at the gate and looked up the middle road for a while and then came back and carried on.

Ismay Rooke put his hat on the bench at the gate for the sixth morning running and did not go past it, and he stood there for two hours facing the lane, and about four people asked him what he was looking at and he said he was watching a lane.

Odile Vray gave the near figure herself because it is written under her hand on the board and it takes her nine seconds, and she would not go up the wall for the other one and said so before anybody thought to ask her.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-three on this board, and that is the near end of the wall and I wrote it there at the seventh hour yesterday and it has not moved since. The top stone is Harlan's to read because I have not been up that ladder since the spring and I am not going to stand at the bottom and pretend that I have."**

**"Nine hundred and forty,"** Harlan Vetch called down off the wall, and then, after a moment, **"and that is the only figure up here that has anything to do with me this morning."**

The crew man had his own book out on the sill with his thumb in it before anybody came near him, and he did the two in the wrong order on purpose this morning because a man had asked him yesterday which of them he would give up if he could only give up one.

**"Five hundred and ninth morning of the run. That is the one I have to write, because a day is a day and the run is what the run is. One thousand and three hundred and seventy-one hundredweight came down in the night and it is down five, and it has been down five on every odd morning since the spring, and I have stopped writing a line under that. I used to write a line under it. I do not now."**

**"And why is that."**

**"Because I did not write anything true under it,"** he said, **"and I would rather have the bare figure in the book than a sentence I made up to sit under it."**

Nia Vale had the rack open at the boards and read the window off it standing side-on to the yard so that the light was not on the numbers, and she gave the halves before she gave the whole, which is not the order she gives them in and which about four people noticed.

**"Two hundred and forty-three above and two hundred and thirty-three below. And the one that came is the two hundred and thirty-three, and the tall one has not moved at all this morning, which it is not doing on every morning. Four hundred and seventy-six of window, which is the sum of the two and I am only going to say that once."**

At about the seventh hour the north board was written on under the frame that is a month nine months back, and the frame was the frame it has been for four years and did not move, and the girl from the second place wrote two hundred and one over the rule and four hundred and thirty-three under it and put the chalk in her apron pocket.

There was nobody at that trough and there has not been for a long time. She did not read them out and did not look for anybody to read them out to, and two men carrying a hod went past it and neither of them stopped, and that was the whole of the seventh hour at that board.

Soren Rill came and did not read the long sheet. He wrote three figures on a slate he brought under his arm, turned it round, and held it out flat to the man from the middle row.

**"Take that and read it. I have given it to three different people in three different orders this week and two of them have made a fight out of the order in front of a yard, and today I am not going to stand up there and be the reason."**

The man from the middle row looked at it.

**"Five hundred and eighth over the line, five hundred and sixth under it, and five hundred and seven at the head of it."**

**"That is right,"** Soren Rill said. **"It is right in any order and I have told you so and you may take it down in whichever of the three you like and do not come back to me about it."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the register form under his arm and the door behind him shut and the key already back on its nail, and he said his one figure from the step without coming down the path.

**"Four hundred and three on the form, and it is not entered, and it is not going to be entered this morning by me or by anybody else standing where you can see me."**

**"It has been four hundred for a week and now it is over,"** a man from the wall said.

**"It was four hundred on one morning and it is four hundred and three now and both of those are correct and neither of them is a year and if anybody in this holding calls it a year again I will take it inside and shut the door and you can all shout at a shut door."**

Tova Reed had the seed trays going out at the north door and a mason from the third place dictating at her from about nine feet off, and she stood where she could see his mouth rather than where she could hear him best, and she wrote down what he said without asking him twice, and she did not tell anybody she was doing that.

**"Three hundred and nine on the charter, and that is the oldest of the three I have and nobody reads it. Three hundred and ninety-seven on the ruled line under Silling's first one, and it is still saying nothing at all. Two hundred and six on the letter, and it went past two hundred two mornings ago and it is not coming back down."**

The man who pushes the barrow came in from the fourth road at about the ninth hour and stood in the middle of the yard with the shafts up and told nobody at all that he was stopping there, and eleven is a floor and not a total, and he has said that to nobody on four mornings now and is not going to start today.

Nobody went near the ladder against the tool house wall, and no figure was printed against either of the two people it stands for, and the ground inside the ring at the back of that wall took no boot this morning and no tape and no offer.

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past four times by people on their way up to the top course and read on by none of them; nobody was offered anything at it and nothing has ever been printed in this holding about what is standing against that door or about how much of it there is.

And at about the eleventh hour Tova Reed came out of the north door with the corner of the seed board under her arm and put it on the middle table for about a minute so that four men could see the route on it, and when one of them asked her whether the second place was going to be on that board she said that it was a place on a route and that it would be on the route in her hand tomorrow when she put the food side up as well, and she said nothing else about it at all, and she took the board back inside.

Out past the gate the low board still had the offer lying on it where it has lain since before the flood, and there is no date on it and it has not been picked up and it has not been taken back and nothing has been written in the space at the foot of it, and about two people looked at it on their way out and neither of them said anything and neither of them had said anything on any of the mornings before.

## 1. WHAT ONE CARD IS, AND WHAT ONE CARD IS NOT

**A card is a working sheet for a single morning.** It carries the morning's day number and weekday and its place in the volume; every figure of every series that falls on that morning, spelled at the house form, with the rule that produces it printed beside it so the writer can check it rather than trust it; the lock state and the floors that morning must not move; the pressure that morning is under; what that morning is not allowed to do; and an ending that is a consequence rather than a hook.

**A card is not a chapter number, not a summary of a chapter, not a synopsis, not a scene, not dialogue, and not an instruction to hit a word count.** A card that begins to write the morning is a card that has become prose, and prose written twice is worse than prose written once. **A CARD MAY NAME A PERSON, A PLACE, AN OBJECT AND AN ACTION. IT MAY NOT WRITE THE ACTION AS IT WILL APPEAR ON THE PAGE.**

**AND THE FIVE PRESSURES ARE FIXED AT PLAN SECTION 2b AND YOU MAY NOT MOVE, REDUCE, ADD OR SOFTEN ANY OF THEM:**

3. The hand-over is work and not a ceremony: a load test run by other people on a ring that has already failed twice, on a schedule four bodies of households and two councils have to enact, on a network whose joint has no operator.
4.

# Chapter 738

## The Twenty-First Of The Twentieth, And Nine Stakes With A Rope Between Them Inside The Wall

The ring is nine paces across at the gate end and it is inside the wall, and on this morning the rope came off it and went back on it twice and nobody put a spade in the ground.

Odile Vray counted the bodies at about the fourth hour and counted them again at about the fifth because she counts them twice and says the number out loud both times when it has moved, and on this morning it had not moved and she said the same number twice anyway, and about four people heard her do it and about nine did not.

Tova Reed was in the seed house from before the light with her own book and her own marks and the seed trays coming out at the north door, and she came out to the boards at about the seventh hour with a sheet of her own under her arm and put it on the boards flat and turned it round so that the reading of it faced the yard, and no hand went on her arm at any hour of this morning.

The crew man read it. He has nine bodies and his own book with his own columns and he gives his own figures and nobody else's, and this morning he gave nine lines and did not read them out twice.

> **THE SEED RING, NINE BEDS, AS THEY STAND**
>
> Dug. Nine. Re-dug. None.
>
> Held the dry season draw. Six.
> Held the flood load. Five. The same five and one of them twice, and the crew's own book says which and the crew's own book is the only one of these two that says which.
> Came up with the root on it. One. At the bottom end in the frost. Not dug again.
> Soft at the north end. One.
> Standing. Eight of nine.
> Priced. None of them.

**"That is the whole of it,"** he said, **"and it is a count and not an inspection, and I have not been asked to make it better and I have not made it better, and a line that reads one on it is a line that reads one and not a line that reads none."**

Odile Vray looked at the bottom end of the ring for a while and then looked at the north end.

**"The soft one is still the soft one."**

**"The soft one is soft at the north end and it has been soft at the north end since before the wall went up and it is not going to be stiff this morning because nine people have looked at it."**

**"Somebody will want a number against it."**

**"They can have the word. I have not put a figure on it and I am not going to, and a figure on it would be a price and there is no price on that sheet for anything."**

Marek Vale came and stood at the boards and read the nine lines and did not touch the paper, and what he said about it was two sentences and neither of them was about the ring.

**"That is the state of it and I am not going to improve it in front of you, because the moment somebody digs a bed to make a count tidy the count is a different count and you will not be able to tell afterwards which one it was."**

**"You want it left as it is."**

**"I want it left as it is and I want it walked round by the people who have to walk round it, and the walk round it is not mine and the rope is not mine and the crew's book is not mine, and I have written four hours of the day into a schedule that says which end goes first and that is the whole of what I have put my hand to."**

The crew man gave two of the figures off his own book at about the sixth hour, because they were in his book before anybody asked him for them.

**"Five hundred and first of the run, and the channel came in at one thousand and three hundred and fifty-nine hundredweight. Five down on the morning behind. I write the hour in my book and I do not write a reason in it and I do not write a name on a figure, and I have not done either of those two things since before the flood."**

Harlan Vetch came down and gave the two stones and the window from the wall in one go and did not come down for either of them.

**"Near stone, eight hundred and eighty-five. Stone at the top, nine hundred and thirty-two. Window of four hundred and sixty-eight, and out of that the half that came in the night is two hundred and twenty-nine and the half that stood is two hundred and thirty-nine, and I give them in that order on a Friday and I will give them in some other order on another day and it will not change what they are."**

**"Why do you change the order."**

**"Because four people have told me that the order is how I say things about them, and they are all wrong, and I am not going to argue with four people about an order every morning for the rest of this season."**

Soren Rill came and gave the long sheet and put his thumb on the top figure rather than on the middle one.

**"Four hundred and ninety-nine at the head. A four hundred and ninety-eighth below the line and a five hundredth above it."**

**"Is it the same three as yesterday."**

**"It is three numbers and not one number done twice."**

At about the seventh hour the north board was written on under the frame that is a month nine months back and has been that frame for four years. One hundred and ninety-seven above a line and four hundred and twenty-five under it, in the girl from the second place's hand, and nobody read them out at the trough and nobody read them out anywhere else, and there was no reading at that trough this morning for anybody to have stopped coming to.

Kellan Rusk brought the form to the step of the one room and would not come further with it.

**"Three hundred and ninety-five on the form and it goes nowhere,"** he said, **"and the charter is at three hundred and one and it is the oldest thing in this holding with a number on it, and Silling's second ruled line is at three hundred and eighty-nine and it is blank and it is going to stay blank in my lifetime, and the letter is at a hundred and ninety-eight."**

**"You have brought it out and not in."**

**"I have brought it out and not in because you were standing there and because a form that is carried round a yard is a form that people get used to, and a form that goes back in a drawer is a form that people forget about, and I have never worked out which of those two I want and neither have you."**

Tova Reed put the seed route on the boards at about the ninth hour and it is her own route and her own paper and it has the places on it in her order, and one of the places on it has no water in it and has had no water in it since the spring, and she put it on the boards with the place still on it.

**"It goes up the second branch past the two bends and along the high side and it ends at four houses and there is a line drawn under those four houses and the line is not a date and it is not a promise and it is the only mark on this paper that I did not put there myself, and it is there because it is true and because a route sheet with a place scratched off it is a lie that people read."**

**"You will be asked to scratch it off."**

**"I will be asked to scratch it off and I will not scratch it off, and I would rather be asked by somebody who wants to read it than by somebody who wants it gone."**

In the tool house the use log had fifteen lines in it and nobody came in to ask for the sixteenth. The barrow had been round eleven times and the man who pushed it said out loud that he was not going to make it twelve, and nobody in that yard told him he had to.

On the shelf in the one room the six ruled rows stood with a figure on the first and nothing on the other three, and no seventh row has been cut for anything in this holding's record.

The ring of bare ground at the back of the wall was not walked on this morning and it is not measured and it is not priced, and a boy stood at the edge of it for about a minute deciding whether he was going to and then went and did something else instead.

And at about the eleventh hour the ring was walked round by six people in front of about thirty, and the rope came up and went down twice and nothing in the ground was moved, and the crew man marked nothing on his sheet and closed the book and put it under his arm, and the sheet with the nine beds on it stayed on the boards with the north end of it marked soft in his own word and nothing else marked at all.

# Chapter 736

## The Nineteenth Of The Twentieth, And A Boy Setting Two Cans Down On The Sill Of The Tap House

The morning opened on work that was already in hand and nobody made a speech about it, which is how the mornings in this yard have opened since the wall went up, and the wall went up before the man who keeps the figures in this place was born.

The tap joint at the back of the tap house wept one drop into a saucer at about the fourth hour and was still weeping into it an hour later, and it has wept into that saucer every morning of this holding for long enough that nobody sets a clock by it, and it proved nothing about this morning or about any other morning of the week.

The boy who carries water came up the lane at about the fifth hour with two cans, one in each hand, and put them down on the sill of the tap house and stood with his hands empty. The right hand is cracked across the second joint and it does not close all the way and has not closed all the way since the frost week, and he carries the chalk in the hand he can still close, and he came up the lane this morning with the chalk in that hand and the two cans in the others and put them down and stood.

Odile Vray was at the boards and she looked at the two cans and then at the sill and then at the yard and she did not say anything, and about four people noticed that she had looked and none of them said anything either.

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the sixth hour with a sheet folded in four in the inside of his coat and two stones off the seed benches in his other hand, and he put the stones on the boards to hold the corners of nothing at all, because the wind at the gate end takes the corners of anything laid down flat, and he has watched it do that for eleven weeks and has stopped being surprised by it.

On the inside of his left forearm there is a mark four inches long that forks twice, and there is no second mark, and he did not look at it at any hour of this morning and nobody asked him about it.

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the sixth hour and gave two of his three in the order he gives them, from forty feet up, without coming down for it.

**"One thousand and three hundred and fifty-six hundredweight came into the channel in the night and the run has it as the four hundred and ninety-ninth, and I have got nothing to say about either of them and that is the end of it."**

**"You have had something to say about those two figures every morning since the spring."**

**"Since the spring. Today two numbers and the end of them, and you may make whatever you like of a morning."**

Odile Vray gave the third one off the boards without looking up at it, because she has it in the same place he has it and has had for a week.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-three hundredweight at the near board and nine hundred and thirty at the stone at the top. Forty-seven of masonry between those two, and forty-seven since the wall went up, and neither of them is for sale."**

**"Who has been asking."**

**"Two men asked on Friday and they will ask again, and I give them the same answer every time and it is no."**

At about the seventh hour the north board was written on, and it was written on by the girl from the second place who came into this yard from the branch end and who has read the pair off that board thirty-nine mornings and never once out loud, and who stood at it this morning with the chalk in her right hand and wrote one hundred and ninety-six above a line and four hundred and twenty-three below it, under a heading that is a month nine months back and has been that heading for four years and did not move this morning and will not move on any morning of this holding.

Nobody read them out. The man who used to read that pair out of his own book at that trough said on the morning behind that it was the last time and that he was not going to make anything of it and was not going to thank anybody for it, and the pair was written this morning under the heading that belongs to the book and not to the man, and the girl from the second place stood where he stood and did not read them out, and about nine people in this yard came and looked at the two figures on the board and then went and did what they had come out of their houses to do.

Nobody thanked her. She put the chalk down on the ledge and went back down the branch with the two empty cans in her hands, and there is no part of this holding that would have thanked her, and she did not wait to be thanked and would not have known what to do about it.

Soren Rill came and read the long sheet off the boards and stood with his hands behind his back and did the thing he does every morning whether anybody wants it or not.

**"Four hundred and ninety-seven at the head of it, with a four hundred and ninety-sixth underneath and a four hundred and ninety-eighth over the top, and there is no column cut under any of the three of them and I am one of two hands on a piece of paper that says nothing about any of it."**

**"Where would you like it written down."**

**"Not here. There is nowhere here. That is the whole of what I have to say about it."**

The girl from the second place gave the window at the boards on her way past and she gave it in the two halves, and she named the half that moved and she did not pretend to know what either half was for.

**"Four hundred and sixty-six of window. Two hundred and thirty-eight above. Two hundred and twenty-eight below, and the two hundred and twenty-eight is the one that came in the night and the other stood."**

Nobody asked her which of the two went down the channel, because it is not a question anybody in this yard asks any more, and she has not been asked it since before the flood and would not have answered it if she had.

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board in her own order and in her own hand, and she stood where she could see the mouth of whoever she was taking them from, and she wrote down what she was told instead of asking twice and she did not announce that she was doing it.

**"Three hundred and ninety-three on the form. Two hundred and ninety-nine on the charter, and that is the oldest of the four and it is two hundred and ninety-nine days. Then the ruled line under Silling's first one at three hundred and eighty-seven, blank, as it has been since she ruled it. And a hundred and ninety-six on the letter, which has lain on a shelf out of a light since the spring and which nobody in this holding has opened."**

**"Four figures on one corner is a lot of figures for a corner this size."**

**"It is the only corner in this holding that has all four of them on it,"** she said, **"and I wrote them in my own order and you may read them in whatever order you like, and I will not change the order for a man who reads faster than I write."**

She put the pencil behind her ear and went back to the seed trays, and no hand went near her arm at any hour of this morning, and about half of this yard has said since that it was never going to and could not have.

Kellan Rusk had the rack open in the doorway of the one room and his back to the yard and his palm flat against the frame, and the drawer in there was shut with the key on its nail, and it was shut at every hour of this morning and nothing in this holding was entered into it and no figure came out of it that anybody acted on.

On the board in that room the thirty-nine blanks stood where they stand, and under them the second rule stood open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, which is the position it has been in for four years and which is not a mark of anything and is not going to be filled this morning.

On the long table inside the doorway there were four sheets, none entered, none refused, and no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper on that table is a woman's own sheet with a refusal written on it in her own hand and it is not one of the four and it never has been.

The compost board at the back of the tap house read paid at thirty-one and the other column was empty and has been empty in every other respect, and not one load has been let out of that line in thirty-one or in anything.

The use log in the tool house had fifteen lines in it and no sixteenth, and the barrow stood wheel first in the middle of the yard at the eleventh hour having gone eleven journeys and eleven is a floor and not a total, and there were seven sessions entered in this holding's book and no eighth, and the requests stood at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stood at fifty-three, and they have never been added together and they have never been the same list.

Six ruled rows stood on the shelf for the things nobody knows, and no seventh row has been cut for anything, and there was a figure on line one and the other three lines have been bare since before the flood.

The ladder stood against the tool house wall with nobody on it and nobody near it, and the ring of bare ground at the back of the wall was not walked on and not measured and not priced, and it has not been walked on or measured or priced on any morning of this holding's record.

The offer was where it has lain since before the flood, on the low board at the gate, undated, unpicked, and not withdrawn, and its top cannot be seen from the gate end because the board is low and the gate end is low, and about two people in this yard looked at it this morning and nobody went near it and nobody said a word about it.

The man of about seventy was not fetched. He has been fetched twenty-nine times and he has not been fetched this morning and he will not be fetched on any morning of this month, and the count of questions put to him in four years is nil and was nil again at every hour today and stayed nil.

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past twice by people going about their own business and was read on by nobody and was offered to nobody, and the figure in its seventh column did not go back and is not read on any morning of this holding.

At about the eighth hour Marek Vale went up the middle road with the folded sheet still in his coat, and at the gate, with his back to the boards and about nine feet of stone between him and them, the man who lays for three councils came down off the wall with his hod and no chart and said the thing that had come down the road with him.

**"There are two people on the branch head and they have been there since before the light and neither of them will tell anybody what the state of it is, and they are not waiting for this holding and they are not going to wait for you."**

**"They are not mine."**

**"They are not anybody's,"** the man said, **"which is the whole of what came down the road with me this morning. That joint has nothing against it and nobody over it, and the two people on the head of it are not mine and not yours and not this holding's, and I have not asked either of them a single question because a man who comes down a road to look at a course does not stop to ask."**

**"Then there is nobody in charge of it."**

**"There are two people on the branch head and neither of them has said he is in charge of it and you are not going to appoint one of them to a job he has not agreed to and be the man who did it."**

Odile Vray heard that at the boards and put a stone on a paper and did not repeat it, and Marek Vale was up the middle road at the time and did not hear it and was not told it, and about four people knew it by the light and nobody in this yard has said since that anybody was told.

And at about the ninth hour, at the boards, in front of about twenty people who were all doing something else, Marek Vale said a thing out loud that he had said to one person this holding and to nobody else in four years, and he said it whole and he said it once.

**"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off."**

Nobody answered him. It was not said to be a hard thing to hear and it was not said to be a true thing either, and about three people in that yard looked at the two boards and about seventeen went on with what they were doing.

Then the man who lays for three councils put his hod down by the sill and came and stood at the end of the boards with his hands behind his back and said a second thing into the middle of the yard, in the way he says figures, at the boards, to everybody.

**"The comfort is not standing, the father is not alive in the wood, and the day does not come back."**

Nobody thanked him for it. Nobody asked him whose father, and a man near the gate said that he had four of his own and that a man who says a thing like that standing at a board has not got four of his own, and Odile Vray told him to get on with the fourth course and left it there.

There are four of them and there were four of them yesterday morning and there are four of them now, and he did not enumerate them to anybody, and there is not going to be a fifth, and not one person in this yard has added one to anybody for any reason.

The mason tied his line along the top of the fourth course and it came out straight.

And at about the eleventh hour the boy who carries water picked the two cans up off the sill of the tap house and went down the lane with them and came back up the lane an hour later with one of them full and the other empty, and put the full one on the sill and did not say where he had been, and he had not been asked and was not asked then and will not be asked.

# Chapter 744

## The Twenty-Seventh Of The Twentieth, And A Bed Lifted At The Top Of The Ring With The Root Still In It

The crew had the top of the ring open at about the seventh hour and they were in it with their hands when the root came up, and it came up with the bed, and the crew man got his own hands clear and got two other hands clear and nobody was hurt and nobody said anything for about a minute and a half.

It is not a fifth loss. There are four of them and there were four of them yesterday morning and there are four of them now, and not one person in this yard has added one to anybody for any reason, and this one is a bed and it is not in that number and it is not going to be put in that number by anybody.

The crew man wrote it in his own book and the crew's own book is the only place it is written, and this is the entry:

> **THE TOP OF THE RING. THIS MORNING.**
>
> Bed at the top end lifted with the root on it and the root came up whole and there was nothing holding it and it was not cut and it was not pulled. It is not there again this season. We are not digging it again. We are not going to try to put that root back where it was.
>
> What it means for the ring is not written here because we do not know yet. What it means for the crew is that we are one bed lighter and that is all and we will go on with the other eight.

He read it out once at the boards and did not read it out twice, and about eleven people in that yard heard it and about four did not, and nobody at the boards asked him a second question about it, which is not what this yard usually does.

**"Is the ring finished."**

**"The ring is a ring and there is a bed missing at the top of it and I have written that down,"** the crew man said. **"I am not going to stand here and let nine people make a verdict out of one bed, and if you want a verdict on the ring then it comes after the other eight have been run and not before."**

Odile Vray did not write it on the boards and could not have written it on the boards, because there is no column cut under anything of the kind and there is not going to be one, and the sheets on the long table stood where they stand with none entered and none refused and no column cut under any of them, and the fifth paper there is a woman's own sheet with a refusal on it in her own hand and is not one of the four.

The crew man wrote the entry at about the eighth hour and then came and stood at the boards and gave the two figures that were on his own book before anybody had asked him for anything.

**"Five hundred and seventh of the run. One thousand and three hundred and sixty-eight hundredweight came in the night and it is down five. And I have written a ninth bed in this book this morning before I have written a figure, and I am going to leave it that way round, and if anybody in this yard wants them the other way round they can ask me to move it and I will not."**

Soren Rill gave the two stones from the bottom up, because he had been asked yesterday to read them in that order and he had, and he read them that way again the next morning because nobody told him to stop.

**"Nine hundred and thirty-eight at the top of the wall. Eight hundred and ninety-one at the near board."**

At about the seventh hour the north board was written on under the frame that is a month nine months back, and the number above the line came out of its range for the first time in four years and came out as two hundred, and four hundred and thirty-one went under it, and the girl from the second place wrote both and put the chalk down and did not read them out at that trough and there was no reading at that trough for anybody to have stopped coming to.

**"That is two hundred."**

**"That is two hundred,"** she said, **"and it has been a hundred and something every morning of my life up to yesterday, and it is written in the ordinary way now and not in the way it used to be written, and that is not a thing anybody has to do anything about and I am not going to stand at this board and make it into a morning."**

The girl gave the window as she capped the chalk.

**"Window of four hundred and seventy-four. That is a hundred and forty-two more than the morning I started counting these and it is not a figure anybody in this yard can do anything with. Two hundred and thirty-two came in the night. Two hundred and forty-two stood."**

Soren Rill came back to the middle of the boards and gave the long sheet.

**"A five hundred and fourth under the line, five hundred and five at the head, a five hundred and sixth over it."**

**"Read it the other way."**

**"I have read it the other way for twenty years and I get a five hundred and sixth, a five hundred and fifth and a five hundred and fourth, and I have never known which of those three he means and neither does he."**

A message came up the lane at about the ninth hour from three miles, in three hands, and it was the same message that has come three times and it came a fourth time.

**"They will not take the test at any size,"** said the man who read it, **"and I am not going to stand here and say why, because it is in her mouth and not in mine, and I am not going to argue with it and I am not going to be asked by you to argue with it."**

**"Nobody is going to argue with it."**

**"Then nothing has changed and I have come four miles to say that nothing has changed, and that is a thing a man is allowed to do."**

Nobody in this yard defended that refusal on this morning and nobody attacked it and nobody in this yard has said since that it was right or that it was wrong, and it stands where it stood, and eleven households three miles up have still refused a test that has been put to them four times.

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board and she came up the lane for them at about the tenth hour because the trays were done.

**"Two hundred and four on the letter. Four hundred and one on the form. Three hundred and seven on the charter. Three hundred and ninety-five on Silling's ruled line, and it says nothing at all, and it will say nothing whatever tomorrow as well."**

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the tenth hour and looked at the top of the ring for a while and did not go down into it and did not touch anything.

**"Is it worth putting a bed there."**

**"Ask him,"** the crew man said, **"and do not ask me, because if I answer that then I will have an opinion in my own book and my book does not carry opinions, it carries what nine hands did."**

**"I will ask him,"** Marek Vale said, **"and I will not tell him what to answer and I will not put a date on it and I will not put a figure on it, and if he says it is worth nothing then the top of that ring stays as it is for the rest of this season and that is the end of it and not a decision."**

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past by three people on their way up to the wall and read on by none of them and offered to nobody, and the figure in its seventh column did not go back.

The compost board read paid at thirty-one and nothing has been let out of that line in thirty-one or in anything, and a man asked at about the ninth hour whether that line would ever turn, and Odile Vray told him that it was not hers and that she had never once known.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and the count of questions put to him was nil and stayed nil, and the four sheets on the long table had nobody's writing on them and no column cut under any of them.

And at about the eleventh hour the bed at the top of the ring was still lying where it had come up, with the root in it, and nobody had cut at it and nobody had moved it and nobody had priced it, and it will lie there until somebody who has not been decided yet decides, and the eight other beds of that ring were being worked at the same time, and nobody in this yard stopped working to talk about the ninth.

# Chapter 740

## The Twenty-Third Of The Twentieth, And A Hand Laid On The Ledge Of The Low Board And Taken Off Again

It was a Sunday and about half the yard was in it, and the four that came were the four that always come.

The man who lays for three councils came down the middle road with his hod and no chart and put the hod down by the sill and stood at the boards. Perrin Dae came with a cart and left it at the gate. Hanne Brack came up the lane four miles with nothing in her hands, which is how she has come every time, and she stood on the north side of the boards where the wind is off the wall. And the clerk from up a lane came on foot in his own boots, which he has never not done, and stood at the south end of them and did not sit down.

And one after another, in that order, without anybody asking them to, they each said out loud in that yard what the letter is at, and none of the four of them has the letter.

**"Two hundred,"** the man who lays said. **"Two hundred days on a piece of paper in a drawer in the one room and it has lain out of a light since the spring. I count stones before days and that is the number of days and I do not know what is on it and I have not asked."**

> **THE LETTER, AS FOUR PEOPLE ACCOUNT FOR IT, AND NONE OF THEM HAS IT**
>
> One. A man who lays for three councils. Two hundred. What is on it: not known to him. Not asked.
>
> Two. A man who buys lime and timber at the fourth place. Two hundred. What is on it: not known to him. Says he has never opened it and does not intend to.
>
> Three. A woman from the second place who has come four times this summer on her own business. Two hundred. What is on it: she has read the four ages off the seed board four mornings running and she reads the letter last because she signs underneath the charter.
>
> Four. A clerk from an office four days north, on foot. A hundred and ninety-five at his office and a hundred and ninety-one on a slip three weeks old in his coat. He says his own figures disagree and he says it in a yard and does not say which of them he believes.

Odile Vray put the slate down and did not write the four of them on anything.

**"That is four accounts of one figure and not four letters, and there is one letter and it is in a drawer with a key on a nail and it is shut, and I am not going to add those four figures together on a board and call it a count of anything."**

**"Somebody will add them."**

**"Somebody will add them in their own head, which I cannot stop and am not going to try to."**

The clerk from up a lane had the channel figures in a book of his own that he does not read out in offices, and he read them out in a yard, which about four people noticed and nobody remarked on.

**"One thousand and three hundred and sixty-two hundredweight. Five hundred and third of the run. And that is five down on the morning behind, because I wrote it down at a gate four days north and I wrote it down wrong last week and I got it right this week and I would rather say both of those than say one."**

Hanne Brack gave the two stones, which she had read off the seed board corner standing at about four feet from it and not touching it.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-seven at the near end, nine hundred and thirty-four at the top of the wall."**

**"You read those off a corner of a board."**

**"I read the four ages off a corner of a board every morning I am in this yard and I have never touched it, and nobody has ever asked me how I knew which way up it was."**

The pair went up on the north board at the seventh hour, where it has gone every morning of this week, one hundred and ninety-eight over the rule and four hundred and twenty-seven under it, and the heading over the top of that board is the heading it was four years ago, because it belongs to the book and not to the girl and not to anybody in this yard.

Nobody read them out. There has not been a reading at that trough for weeks and there was none this morning and nobody asked when it was going to start again.

Harlan Vetch had come down off the wall in the middle of that and had not gone back up.

**"Four hundred and seventy of window and I will give the halves the way I gave them yesterday, which is that it does not matter. Two hundred and thirty came. Two hundred and forty stood. There. That is the whole of it and I am not being clever about the order."**

**"It is never the order."**

**"It has never once been the order and four people keep asking me about the order."**

Soren Rill came and read the long sheet and read it without stopping on it, and the limb under the line was at a round number this morning for the first time in a long while.

**"A five hundredth under the line. Five hundred and one at the head. A five hundred and second over."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board in her own order and her own hand.

**"Two hundred on the letter, and it has come out of the range it stood in yesterday and it is not going back into it. Three hundred and ninety-seven on the form. Three hundred and three on the charter, which is the oldest of the four. Three hundred and ninety-one on Silling's ruled line and it is blank, as it has been since she ruled it."**

**"It has gone to two hundred."**

**"It has,"** she said, **"and it has been a hundred and something every day of my life up to yesterday and it is two hundred today, and I have written it as two hundred because that is what it is and I am not going to write it as one hundred and ninety-nine to make a column look tidy."**

And at about the tenth hour Odile Vray went out past the gate to look at the low board, which she does about twice a month and which nobody in this yard has asked her to do, and she stood at it and put her right hand flat on the ledge under it, and not on it, and her hand was on the ledge for about as long as a hand rests on a thing that is not being picked up, and then she took it off and came back in and went to the boards.

Nobody saw her do it but about four people and none of them said anything about it then and about six of them have said since that she did not pick it up, which is the same thing and is not the same thing, and there is nobody in this holding who is going to settle it.

The offer is where it has lain since before the flood. It has no date on it. It has not been picked up and it has not been filled in and it has not been withdrawn and its top cannot be seen from the gate end. Not one person in this yard has put a name on it and not one person in this yard has put a condition on it and the two councils have not put a condition on it and the four bodies of households have not put a condition on it.

Hanne Brack looked at it from about nine feet off and said one sentence about it and it was not a sentence anybody had asked her for.

**"That is the same paper it has been since before the wall and it is the only thing in four counties that has not changed in four years, and I have come four times this summer and I am not going to say anything else about it in front of a yard, because there are four houses at the end of my branch that have had nothing since the spring and I am not going to be a woman talking about a piece of paper."**

The man who keeps the use log had gone home an hour before the rest of them on a Sunday and left it at fifteen lines with the sixteenth one not written for anybody to come back and find.

The compost board at the back of the tap house read paid at thirty-one, and the second column of that board is empty and has been empty for four years, and nothing has been let out of the first one in thirty-one or in anything.

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past twice on that Sunday by a man going to a house at the far end of it, and he did not stop at it and did not read it and was not offered anything by anybody, and the figure in its seventh column did not go back and is not read on any morning of this holding and there is no allowance printed for it anywhere.

And at about the eleventh hour the four that came went away by three roads and the clerk from up a lane was the last one out of the gate and he stopped at the gate post and said a thing to nobody in particular and it was the only sentence anybody said on that low road all day.

**"A hundred and ninety-five at his office and a hundred and ninety-one on a slip I have had in my coat three weeks and the two of them do not agree and I am not going to pick one in a gateway."**

# Chapter 739

## The Twenty-Second Of The Twentieth, And A Gauge Put Into A Different Hand At The Boards

The crew began the draw at about the fourth hour, which is earlier than they have ever begun it, and they began it because line one of the schedule on the middle table says the north end of the ring goes first and the draw goes on before anything is asked for at this end, and nine people read that line on the morning it was put down and none of them read it out loud.

The crew man did the reading of the gauge and not Marek Vale, because Marek Vale had brought the gauge down from the tap house at about the third hour and put it on the boards and then walked to the north end of the ring and picked it up again and carried it to Nia Vale and put it in her hand and took his hand away.

**"You have kept the rack for eleven weeks and you have read four figures off a board every morning of them,"** he said. **"Read this one when it is there and do not read it to me."**

**"I have never read an instrument in my life."**

**"You read a board. A board is an instrument that does not care."**

She stood at the boards with it in her right hand and her left hand flat on the frame and she read it, and she read it the way she reads the four ages off her brother's own marks, which is out loud once and then again because the first one is a surprise.

**"Nine hundred and six. It is going down and it is going down slow."**

**"How slow."**

**"I do not know. I have told you that I do not know."** She put it down on the boards and left it there and did not pick it up again that morning. **"It is yours if you want it. I have read it and I am not keeping it."**

**"It is not mine this morning,"** he said, **"and I am not going to take it back, and I am not going to be the man who reads an instrument on a ring that nine other people are working. I put the sequence down. You are reading it."**

The crew man wrote the hour in his own book and not a name and not a reason, which is what he has always done, and under the hour he wrote the line in his own words, and this is the line and it is in his book and it is the only account of that bed in this holding:

> **Entered**
>
> North end. Soft. Took the draw clean at the top and let it go out at the far end of itself without being asked. Not a fault in the bed. Not a fault in the man either. We are not writing a reason because we have not got one and I do not want a reason invented in a colder month about a morning that was not this one.

Nobody at the boards argued with him about the line and about four people read it twice and about nine did not, and it went into the crew's book in the crew's own hand and it was not copied into anything else in this holding, and the record of it is the crew's own and it stays the crew's own.

**"Soft,"** Soren Rill said, reading the word on the boards from about six feet off. **"Soft is a word and it is the correct word and I have been on this wall twenty years and I have never seen a bed do that."**

**"Then it will happen again."**

**"It will happen again and it will be in this book again and it will not be in anybody else's book, and that is what a crew is."**

Auret Sill had the pumps at the second place open at about the fifth hour and she sent word up the lane at about the seventh that the season's figure stood where she had filled it by hand and would not be moved for a tidier column, and nobody in this yard sent her a figure to move.

At about the eighth hour the two of them that came down the middle road came down it again, three bodies this time, and Ismay Rooke put his hat on the bench at the gate for the fourth morning running and did not go inside, and Perrin Dae came in and went to the middle table and put a sheet of his own on it beside the schedule and squared it with the side of his hand.

**"That is a copy of your schedule and it is a copy and it has no date at the top of it, and I had it written out on Saturday night at the fourth place and brought up on Monday, and the man who wrote it was told twice not to put one on."**

**"Who paid him."**

**"I did. Two loads of timber turned at my own cost and he will tell you that and he will tell it to you and not to me."**

**"It is the five hundred and second morning of the run,"** Perrin Dae said, **"and one thousand and three hundred and sixty-seven hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and I have turned two loads of timber at my own cost this week because a man read me a number, and I am telling you that those two things are in the same week and not in the same breath."**

Then he read his own copy out to the yard. It was the same five lines in the same order and the fifth line was in his man's hand and it said the second place is not on this sheet, and he read that one twice and said nothing after it either time.

At about the ninth hour Harlan Vetch came down off the wall and gave the two stones and went straight back up without giving the window, which he has never done on a working morning.

**"Near stone, eight hundred and eighty-six. Top stone, nine hundred and thirty-three. Forty-seven of masonry between them and I am not going up there to read a third thing in front of nine people in this weather."**

**"Then somebody else will read it."**

**"Then somebody else will read it and it will not be me, and that is the arrangement."**

The boy who carries water read it, because he was standing at the boards with his two cans and had read a pair off that board forty mornings running without reading it out loud.

**"Four hundred and sixty-nine of window. Two hundred and forty above, two hundred and twenty-nine below. The two hundred and forty came in last night. I do not know what either one of them is for and I am not going to be asked."**

**"Who taught you that sentence."**

**"Nobody. It is the only sentence I know."**

Soren Rill came and read the long sheet and gave it with the bottom figure first, which is not the order he gives it in and which he said he had done because a man had asked him yesterday whether it was the only order.

**"Four hundred and ninety-ninth under the line. Five hundred at the head of it. Five hundred and first over the line."**

**"Say that again and it will come out the way it comes out."**

**"It will come out exactly the way it came out."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board and she had a man from the middle row dictating them at about the tenth hour and she wrote down what she was told and did not ask him twice.

**"Three hundred and ninety-six on the form. Three hundred and two on the charter. Silling's ruled line under her first one at three hundred and ninety and it says nothing at all, as it has said nothing since she ruled it. And a hundred and ninety-nine on the letter, which is the last figure under two hundred that this letter will ever have, and after this it goes to two hundred and it does not come back down, and I have written it in the ordinary way because that is how it is written."**

Nobody at the boards had a pair to read out, because there is no pair on a morning that does not carry one, and the north board inside the one room stood with the frame on it and nothing under it.

In the tool house the use log stood at fifteen lines and the sixteenth was not written, and there were seven sessions in this holding's book and no eighth, and the requests and the section-nine notes stood at fifty-three each and have never once been added to one another.

The ladder was against the tool house wall all morning and no person put a foot on the bottom rung of it, and the ring of bare ground behind the wall was not walked on and not measured and not priced, and the compost board read paid at thirty-one with the second column of it empty.

The man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was asked of him at any hour of the day, and the drawer was shut at the sixth hour and again at the dark with the key back on its nail, and the third column of the sheet of terms stood ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved.

And at about the eleventh hour Nia Vale, who keeps the rack, went into the one room and stood at the board in there for an hour with the second rule under the thirty-nine blanks standing open and empty in front of her and did not put anything under it and did not move it, and when she came out she said that she had been in there an hour and that nobody had come to the door, and she did not say that as a complaint and about four people heard it as one.

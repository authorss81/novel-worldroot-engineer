# Chapter 745

## The Twenty-Eighth Of The Twentieth, And Two Orders Of Two Lines Written Under Each Other On One Sheet

The schedule went on the middle table for the last time as a thing anybody was going to change, and by about the fourth hour it had stopped being that, and nobody in this yard has said who made it stop.

Auret Sill came up the lane at about the fifth hour with a can in each hand and her own slate under her arm and read it from the bottom up, as she does, and stopped with her thumb on line two.

**"Four hours in the middle of the day with nothing going in and the sluice wheel not turned. That line has been on every sheet this holding has ever written and it has never once been in the same place twice."**

**"It is where it is."**

**"It is where it is because somebody put it there and I want to know whether it is there for a reason or because you like it there."**

**"It is there because the second place has no water,"** Marek Vale said, **"and on the four hours the crew is not taking from a branch that has nothing in it, and if you want to move that line you move it and you say so and I will write it where you say, and I will not argue with you about it, because you are the person who has to open the pumps at that place at the hour the four hours end."**

Emrys Dole came in from the middle road with lime on his knees and nineteen years of that road behind him and said the other half of it before anybody asked him to.

**"The four hours is the only part of that sheet the three of us have ever agreed to, and it is the only part we are going to lose, and we would rather it sat after the load than before it. Put the load in first. Read it. Then have your four hours."**

**"You want the quiet four hours to sit after a load test."**

**"I want the load test to be done by people who have been at it since before the light and not to be measured at a moment when everybody is tired and everybody's mind is on the four hours after it."**

Ismay Rooke came in from the road behind him and put his hat on the bench at the gate, which he has done now on five mornings and which has still not become a habit, and he stood at the north end of the table and read both of those things twice.

Then Perrin Dae came up the lane with a cart and said that the four households at the fourth place wanted line one to have their fifth hour on it and not their own fourth, which is on the sheet already, in a man's own hand, from the morning it was put there.

**"Then that is five bodies of opinion on one sheet,"** said a man from the north row, **"and five bodies of opinion is not a schedule."**

**"Four bodies of households and two councils,"** Odile Vray said, from about nine feet off, **"and I have counted them twice and I counted them the same way twice and the number has not moved, and nobody is going to add them together in this yard this morning and if a body does that in a room somewhere I will not have been in the room."**

**"Then who fixes it."**

**"It gets fixed by being written down the way everybody wants it and by the disagreement being written down next to it,"** Marek Vale said, **"and by nobody settling it in a yard, and I am going to write the disagreement on it because if I take it off the sheet then the two of you will go home and say that this holding agreed with them and that is not what happened in this yard."**

> **MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE, SETTLED AS TO FORM AND NOT AS TO ORDER**
>
> One. Morning. North end of the ring first, and the draw before anything is asked for at this end. Fifth hour on this line, in the hand of the sluice road, and he has said out loud that he has refused nothing.
>
> Two. Middle of the day. Four hours. Nothing goes in and nothing comes out and the sluice wheel is not turned.
>
> Three. Afternoon. Load on in two stages, and somebody other than the man who put it on reads it in between.
>
> Four. Night. The head of it is looked at by whoever goes, and whoever went writes it up, and there is no name on that line and there is not going to be one.
>
> Five. The second place is not on this sheet. It is not a line and it is not a place on a sheet, and nothing written on this paper reaches it.
>
> Under line two and line three, in a second hand and not the first: THE SMALL COUNCIL PUTS THE LOAD BEFORE THE FOUR HOURS. THE BODY THE MAN WHO LAYS SERVES PUTS THE FOUR HOURS BEFORE THE LOAD. NEITHER OF THOSE BODIES HAS THE OTHER ONE'S ORDER AND NEITHER HAS GIVEN ANYTHING UP. THIS SHEET CARRIES BOTH ORDERS BECAUSE ONE SHEET THAT CARRIES ONE ORDER IS NOT THE SHEET THIS HOLDING AGREED.
>
> There is no date at the head of this sheet and there is not going to be one.

**"You have written a row about us."**

**"I have written a paragraph about two bodies and it is not a row and it is not a figure and it is not one of your two and it is not a number,"** he said, **"and it is not a column cut under either of your names, and if you want it off the sheet then I will take it off, and I am telling you I will take it off."**

**"Leave it,"** Emrys Dole said.

**"Then it stays, and I am not thanking you for it and I am not asking you to be thanked for it, because it is not a favour. It is a fact."**

Sera Quill was at the boards at about the seventh hour with the slate under her arm and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it.

**"A hundred and sixty-six in force. Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and thirty-seven not. And the first of my two is one hundred and ninth and it is in the book and it is the only one of the two of mine that is in this holding's book, and it is the same as it was on the morning you last asked."**

**"And the other."**

**"No."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall on the last of the five mornings he came down on this week and gave the two stones and nothing else, and about four people noticed what he had left out.

**"Eight hundred and ninety-two at the near board. Nine hundred and thirty-nine at the top. Forty-seven of masonry, as always."**

The man who lays for three councils put the hod down by the sill and gave two figures he had read off the boards on his way past, which he has never done and which nobody stopped him doing.

**"One thousand and three hundred and seventy-six hundredweight came down last night, and the run has it as the five hundred and eighth. Eight up, because it is an even morning and it has been an even morning every other day this week. I do not know what eight up means and neither does he."**

The boy who carries water read the window as he came past the boards with a can in each hand and did not stop doing it.

**"Four hundred and seventy-five. Two hundred and forty-three came. Two hundred and forty-three. Wait."**

**"Say the one under it."**

**"Two hundred and thirty-two stood."**

Soren Rill came and gave the long sheet twice because Ismay Rooke asked him to and because he does not often get asked twice.

**"Five hundred and six at the head. A five hundred and fifth under the line. A five hundred and seventh over it. And that is five hundred mornings of the run and there is nothing whatever to be said about that and I have not said anything about it."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board and a mason from the third place dictating them at about the tenth hour.

**"Three hundred and ninety-six on Silling's ruled line and it is blank. Four hundred and two on the form. Three hundred and eight on the charter, and that one is the oldest and it is the one nobody reads. Two hundred and five on the letter."**

And at about the eleventh hour, with about thirty people in this yard and the two councils still standing about nine feet apart, a man from the middle row said the thing that had been said at the gate four mornings running and that nobody had yet answered out loud.

**"There is a joint at the head of that channel with nothing against it and nobody over it and there is one man in four counties who could put somebody over it, and he is standing at that table."**

Nobody said anything at the boards for about four seconds, and Marek Vale put a stone down on the corner of his own schedule to hold it against the wind, and he did not look up, and he did not answer, and the yard went on doing what it was doing.

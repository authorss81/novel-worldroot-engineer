# Chapter 742

## The Twenty-Fifth Of The Twentieth, And A Sheet Unrolled End To End Along The Boards And Held Down At Two Corners

Roan Selk came up the lane at about the sixth hour from the low road, which he has not walked in nineteen years and which he walked in two hours, with a long sheet rolled under his arm and mud to the knee and no bag.

He would not sit down. He put the sheet on the boards end to end and held the two far corners with his own hands while Odile Vray put a stone on each of them, and then he took his hands off and stood back and did not come nearer the boards again while it was there.

It was not a sheet of this holding. It was a sheet with figures on it in three other hands, and the figures were in another count, and there was no heading on it, and the paper had been wet twice and dried twice.

> **THE PATHS. WHAT THE ASSEMBLY HAS, IN ITS OWN COUNTING, AND IT IS NOT THIS HOLDING'S COUNTING.**
>
> Paths walked. Eleven. Nobody calls a path a channel and nobody on this sheet is going to.
> Meetings held. Six. Not at this yard, not on this lane, and no name of any household in this holding is on any of them.
> Hands at the meeting where the path figures were read. Nine.
> Branches opened on the paths. None. Not one, by anybody, since the middle of the summer.
> Asked to come to a yard. Twice. Both times by a man who will not come to a yard.

**"I have carried this up the low road for three days because it is a sheet and not a message,"** he said, **"and I am going to say four things on that board and then I am going to stop, and I have not come here to be asked anything and I have not come here to agree to anything and there is nobody behind me who has given me anything to agree to."**

**"Then say the four things."**

**"One. We have not come to this yard and we are not going to. That is not a complaint about this yard. There are nine of us at a table and eleven paths and a yard takes eleven of us off a path for a day, and we have learned what a day off a path costs in the middle of a season."**

**"Two. We read your four figures at a meeting and we have written them out on our own sheet in our own counting and the two do not agree, and I am not going to stand here and say which of them is right, because we have not got a way to know."**

**"Three. Somebody in this yard is going to say that we have agreed to your schedule because we came up the lane with it in our hands. Say to them from me that we have agreed to nothing, and if you want it in writing I will put it in my own hand at the bottom of that sheet tonight and send it down and it will arrive in four days."**

**"Put it in writing."**

**"I will put it in writing. Four. There is a joint at the head of this channel with nothing against it and two men on it. We know that because we have been walking paths past the back of it since the spring. We are not asking you to appoint anybody and we are not offering anybody, and if one of those two men wants to tell an assembly of eleven paths what is at the head of his own channel that is between him and his own hands."**

Marek Vale had not been at the boards when the sheet was unrolled and he came up the lane at about the seventh hour and stood at the south end of it and read it on his own and did not touch it.

**"There is no line on that sheet about the second place,"** he said. **"I have looked for one twice. I want to say in this yard, in front of the man who carried it, that there is no line on it, and that is not an accusation, and if there should have been a line on it then I would rather know today than at the end of the season."**

**"There is no line on it,"** Roan Selk said, **"and I am not going to invent one at the end of a lane to make a man feel better about a place four miles from his own gate, and that is the last thing I am going to say about it."**

He said it once and he did not say it again, and about four people in that yard noticed that he had not said it again and none of them said anything about it.

Sera Quill was at the boards at about the seventh hour with the slate under her arm and she stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it and nothing else, and the board in the one room behind her stood at thirty-nine blanks with the second rule under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and she looked at that rule for about four seconds and then looked away from it and did not say a word about it and has not said a word about it since.

**"It is a day like any other day for the figures in this holding,"** she said, **"and it is not a morning for anything to move, and nothing moved this morning and I did not write a figure today because there is no figure to write."**

He had heard about it in a kitchen on the low road and had asked, before he came up the lane, what a return was for.

**"What is it for."**

**"It moves nothing,"** she said. **"It has not moved anything in four years and it is not going to move anything this week, and there is no number on it and there is not going to be one, and you came two hours up a road to ask me a question a man at my gate asked me once in the spring."**

**"And what do you tell him."**

**"That it stands where it stands and nobody is fetched and nobody is asked anything, and that is the whole of what I say about it, and I say it once."**

She put one hundred and ninety-nine over the rule on the north board and four hundred and twenty-nine under it at the seventh hour, and the heading that is a month nine months back was still there over the top of it, unchanged, because it belongs to the book and not to her and not to anybody.

She did not read them out. There was nobody at that trough waiting for it and there has not been for a long time, and she has not asked anybody in this holding to come and stand there, and nobody has offered.

The girl gave the window as she put the chalk down, because she was standing at the board and it was going to be said at that board anyway.

**"Four hundred and seventy-two of window. Two hundred and thirty-one came in the night. Two hundred and forty-one stood."**

**"You have given three numbers and I have not asked you for one."**

**"They were going to be said here and I would rather they were said by me than by a man who has to come down off a wall to say them."**

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall with his own coat off and read two figures and went back up.

**"Five hundred and fifth of the run, and one thousand and three hundred and sixty-five hundredweight in the channel in the night. It is down five, and it has been down five every other morning, and I have stopped being pleased about it and I have started being frightened of it, and that is a thing about me and not about the channel."**

Roan Selk had not read the two stones before he came up the lane. He read them off the wall at about seven feet with a mason holding the chalk board up for him, which neither of them had asked for.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-nine at the near end. Nine hundred and thirty-six at the top. Forty-seven of masonry between them. I am telling you that I did not know that, because I have stood at eleven paths this morning and I have never once known how long forty-seven of anything is."**

Soren Rill came and read the long sheet off the boards and would not read it over the sheet that was still lying there, so he stood at the north end and read it at the end of the other one.

**"Five hundred and three at the head. Five hundred and second underneath. Five hundred and fourth over the line."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board and a man from the third place dictating them.

**"Three hundred and five on the charter, three hundred and ninety-nine on the form, three hundred and ninety-three on Silling's ruled line and nothing whatever on it, and two hundred and two on the letter."**

Sera Quill stood at the boards with her back to the yard for most of that morning, and the requests stood at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stood at fifty-three and neither of them moved and neither of them was added to the other.

The barrow went down the lane and back and went eleven times and not once more, because the man who pushes it said that the fourth hour of the middle of the day is not a time to be pushing a barrow anywhere and he said it to nobody.

Kellan Rusk shut the drawer at the sixth hour and again at the dark and the key went back on its nail both times, and the third column of the sheet of terms stood ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved and there is no sixth term on it.

And at about the eleventh hour Roan Selk rolled the sheet up and went back down the lane he had walked in two hours and took three days over, and he did not say goodbye to anybody in particular and nobody said anything to him as he went, and about four people at the gate said afterwards that he had not come for anything and about nine said afterwards that he had come for something, and both of those are true and neither of them is on the sheet.

# Chapter 743

## The Twenty-Sixth Of The Twentieth, And A Slate Held Down On A Board At The Height Of Somebody Who Could Not Reach It

A girl of about seventeen came in off the sluice road at about the sixth hour to fetch a form that the man at the fourth place had said he had, and she came inside the gate for the first time in her life because the form was said to be in here.

Nobody stopped her, and about four people watched her come in and let her get all the way to the boards before anybody spoke to her, and what was said to her was not about the form.

Odile Vray put her hand on the slate on the ledge at about the height of the girl's chest and did not move it and did not pick it up.

**"That is the slate and the numbers on it are not yours and I am not going to read them to you and I am not going to stop you looking at them. You can stand where you are and look at them until you have seen as many as you can hold and then you can go."**

**"I wanted to see what one looked like."**

**"Then stand there and look at one."**

Sera Quill came at about the seventh hour and she saw the girl and she did not send her away, and she put the slate down on the ledge, flat, at an angle, and she put her own finger on one of the numbers and nothing else on the page.

**"That one is the load a crew put on a bed in the flood and it is a count of what went in and not a count of what is still in there, and there is a difference between those two things and it is the whole of what a holding is, and you will not get that out of a board. You will get it out of a bed."**

The girl looked at it for a long time and then asked whether the number was the same every morning.

**"No,"** Sera Quill said. **"It goes one every so often and it does not go on a morning you would guess and there is no way at all to guess which, and anybody who tells you there is a way is selling you something or has been sold."**

**"Who decides which mornings."**

**"Nobody decides. It is a rule and the rule is a long way older than me and it does not take anybody's day off. My own one goes one when it goes one. I do not know which mornings and neither do you and I am not going to guess in front of you."**

Harlan Vetch did not come down. He read the window up the wall twice because the girl of about seventeen asked him to, and he read it down to her at about forty feet and she wrote it on the back of her hand.

**"Four hundred and seventy-three. Two hundred and forty-two came. Two hundred and thirty-one stood. Write those three and nothing else, and do not write them one under the other because they are not one under the other."**

Odile Vray gave the two stones off the boards and gave them with a thing she has never added to them before.

**"Eight hundred and ninety at the near end. Nine hundred and thirty-seven at the top. And that is forty-seven apart, and forty-seven is the only figure on those two stones that has never once moved and it is not on either of them, it is the wall."**

The boy who carries water came up from the tap house at about the seventh hour with a can in each hand and gave the two he knew without being asked, and he has never once been asked and has never once said he could not.

**"Five hundred and sixth morning of it. One thousand and three hundred and seventy-three hundredweight came down in the night."**

**"You did not hear that from anybody."**

**"It is loud,"** he said, and went and put the cans on the sill.

Soren Rill came and gave the long sheet off the boards and stood back from the ledge while he said it.

**"A five hundred and third under it, five hundred and four on the top of it, a five hundred and fifth over the line."**

The girl asked him what the three numbers were for and he told her that they were three numbers and that he had told a yard that for twenty years and that nobody had asked him why there were three of anything.

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room with the register form in his hand and stood on the step and would not come further, and the drawer behind him was shut and the key was on its nail and it was on its nail at every hour of this morning.

**"Four hundred on the form,"** he said, **"and it is the only one of the four anybody ever asks me for, and I have not entered it and I am not going to enter it, and it is not a date and it is not a term and it is not a year and if anybody in this yard calls it a year I will take the form off the table."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board in her own order and her own hand and she did not come and stand near the boards this morning because the seed trays were going out and the north door does not wait.

**"Three hundred and six on the charter and it is the oldest of them,"** she said, from about twenty feet off, to a man who had asked at the seed house door. **"Three hundred and ninety-four on the ruled line under Silling's first one, and it is blank, as it has been since she ruled it. And two hundred and three on the letter."**

At about the ninth hour the man who lays for three councils came down the middle road with his hod and no chart and put the hod down by the sill and went and stood at the boards and read the slate over Sera Quill's shoulder and said nothing at all about it, which is not what he does.

Then Kellan Rusk said the thing that he had come out of the doorway to say.

**"I will read you what is behind this door."**

**"You will not open it."**

**"I am not going to open it and I am not going to turn the key, and the drawer has been shut all morning and it is going to be shut at every hour of this afternoon, and what I am offering is my own memory of it, out loud, at the step, in front of anybody who wants to hear it. That is not the same as the drawer and I would like it written down somewhere that it is not the same."**

The girl of about seventeen asked whether she could hear it, and he said she could hear anything he said, and he read out eleven lines from a form he had not looked at since the spring, and at two points he stopped and said that he was not certain and would not guess, and about four people in that yard noticed both of the stops and nobody said anything about either of them.

Afterwards, at about the tenth hour, Sera Quill said a thing to Marek Vale at the boards in front of about nine people and she said it in her own mouth and she said it once.

**"You have put a schedule on a table with five lines on it and a fifth hour on one of them in a man's own hand, and every one of us who can read a line is now working to a line. Do you know what that does to the four households on my branch who cannot read a line."**

**"Say the rest of it."**

**"The rest of it is that they are now worked to by whoever can read, and that whoever can read will be one of us, and that you have not done that on purpose and it has happened anyway, and there are four of them at the end of that branch and they will find out about it from a person in their own house and not from anybody standing at this board. That is the cost of your sheet and I am not going to pretend to you that it is free, and I am also not going to stand here and say it is not worth it, because I am not the one paying it and I have said that and I am going to stop talking now."**

He did not answer her. She did not wait for him to and she went back to the boards and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it.

About four people had heard it and about nine had not, and nobody in that yard chose between her and him, and nobody has said since which of them was right, and nobody thanked her and nobody told her she was wrong and the four houses at the end of that branch have still not been told by anybody standing at this board.

The six ruled rows on the shelf stood with a figure on the first and the other three bare, and no seventh row has been cut for anything.

The use log had fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth was not written and is not going to be, and the barrow had gone eleven times by the fourth hour and the man pushing it said he was stopping there whatever the day wanted.

Four sheets lay on the long table inside the doorway, none entered, none refused and no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper there is a woman's own sheet with a refusal written on it in her own hand, and the girl of about seventeen read all four of them as she went past and did not ask what any of them was for.

And at about the eleventh hour the girl of about seventeen went out of the gate with the form under her arm, and she stopped at the gate post and turned and said that a figure was a very small thing to have come inside a gate for, and nobody in this yard agreed with her and nobody disagreed and she went home up the sluice road with it.

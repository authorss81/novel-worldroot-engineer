# Chapter 737

## The Twentieth Of The Twentieth, And A Sheet Opened On The Middle Table And Left Open

Four bodies of households and two councils had sent word up the lane in the night, and the word was that they wanted the maintenance schedule on a table where all of them could put their hands on it, and not a copy carried round in a coat and not a thing said out loud at a gate, and Odile Vray said that word over at the boards twice and said the number both times, and the number had not moved since the middle of the summer.

Marek Vale had it in his coat at about the fourth hour and it was not clean. It had been folded in four since the day before the day before and it had been taken out twice and put back twice and it had a crease down it that went through two of the lines, and he laid it flat on the middle table and weighted the four corners with four stones off the seed benches and did not smooth it.

**"This is not an order,"** he said, to about eleven people who had come out of their houses for it. **"I am going to say that once and then somebody will ask me whether it is an order, and it is not, and the reason it is not is that a thing on this table that tells four bodies of households and two councils what to do is a thing that four bodies of households and two councils will not do, and if they do it will be because they decided to and not because a sheet said so."**

**"Then what is it."**

**"It is the order of the work and the sequence of it and how long each piece of it takes, and the parts of it that are mine, which are the layout and the timing and the four hours in the middle of the day when nothing goes in, and the parts of it that are not mine, which are every other line on it."**

He did not put a date at the head of it. There is no date at the head of it and there was not going to be and he said so before anybody asked, because on the day before he had said it four times in four yards and on this morning he said it once in a yard where anybody could hear it.

> **MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE**
>
> One. Morning. North end of the ring first, then down the line, and the draw goes on before anything is asked for at this end.
>
> Two. Middle of the day. Four hours. Nothing goes in and nothing comes out and the sluice wheel is not turned and the reason is at the foot of this sheet and not at the head of it.
>
> Three. Afternoon. Load put on in two stages and read between them by a person who is not the person who put it on.
>
> Four. Night. The joint at the head is looked at by whoever is going to look at it and the book is written in the hand of whoever looked, and there is no name against that line because there is nobody to put on it.
>
> Five. The second place is not on this sheet. It is not a line and not a place on a sheet, and nothing on this sheet covers it.

**"That is the whole of it,"** he said, **"and it is a sheet of paper and not a promise, and I have put my name nowhere on it, and the reason I have put my name nowhere on it is that if my name is at the top of this then every person who reads it will be reading me and not the work, and the moment that happens the work stops and I do too."**

Auret Sill came up the lane at about the fifth hour with a can in each hand and her own slate under her arm and read the sheet from the bottom up, which is what she does.

**"Five says the second place is not on this sheet, and that is the same sentence in a different hand and I want it to stay on there when this gets copied, and I will put my own copy of that line in my own book tonight whether anybody else does or not."**

**"It will stay on there."**

**"Say it in the yard."**

**"It stays on there."**

Sera Quill came at about the sixth hour with the slate under her arm and stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it and nothing else in this yard, and she had one of her two reckonings and she did not have the other one and there is no wall, no sheet and no book in this holding that has the other one on it.

**"A hundred and sixty-four in force,"** she said. **"Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and thirty-five not."**

**"That is up one."**

**"It goes up one and not one of anything else does, and it is up one because it is up one, and you may write that down or you may not."**

She turned the slate over and gave the second of her two numbers and gave it in the ordinary way, with nothing said about the first one.

**"One hundred and seventh. I give you that one and you print it and it is the only one of the two of mine that is in this holding's book, and it has been the only one since the middle of the summer and it will be the only one this morning and I have not come here to change that."**

**"And the other of yours."**

**"No,"** she said. **"I gave you one and you wrote it. The other one is mine and it is not on this table and it is not on the board and it is not going to be in anybody's book in this holding, and that is not a thing I am keeping back for me. It is a thing I do not print and you have all had a morning at which somebody printed both and I have read what that did to a yard."**

She said the next part once and she said it the way she says everything else, and she did not put her hand on anybody and nobody put a hand on her.

**"A hundred and thirty-five not taken is not a gap in this holding. It is a hundred and thirty-five people who are not on a list because they were not asked to be on a list, and if this sheet is going to be read in a room I have never been in, then that sentence goes under it and not at the side of it."**

The man who lays for three councils was standing at the head of the table when he said it and he said it to the yard and not to anybody in it.

**"One thousand and three hundred and sixty-four hundredweight came down the channel last night and it came down eight on the morning behind, and the run has it as the five hundredth, which is five hundred, and there is nothing at all to be said about five hundred and I have said nothing about it every time it has happened."**

Nia Vale, who keeps the rack, read the window off the boards where it is chalked and she read it with her thumb on the ledge.

**"Four hundred and sixty-seven. Two hundred and thirty-nine above and two hundred and twenty-eight below, and the two hundred and thirty-nine is the one that moved, because I have that corner in my own hand from Thursday and I did not touch it since."**

**"That is the only way anybody in this yard knows which half went."**

**"It is the only way anybody in this yard knows, and there are four of us who do it and none of us has ever been right, and that is the standing and not a discovery."**

Soren Rill came and read the long sheet and gave the two stones off the wall with it, which he has never done on a Thursday before and which he said he had done because a man in the yard had asked him for them both.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-four at the near board, nine hundred and thirty-one at the top of the wall. And four hundred and ninety-eight at the head of the long sheet with a four hundred and ninety-seventh under it and a four hundred and ninety-ninth over it, and those three are three numbers and not one number with two ends."**

Kellan Rusk came out of the one room at about the seventh hour with the register form in his hand and did not come further than the step, and the drawer behind him was shut and the key was on its nail and it was on its nail at every hour of this morning.

**"Three hundred and ninety-four on the form,"** he said, **"and I have carried it up and down this lane in my own hand for eleven weeks and it goes nowhere and I will not enter it, and if you want it entered you may ask me again tomorrow and I will give you the same answer."**

**"Three hundred on the charter and it is the oldest of the four,"** Tova Reed said from the seed board, **"and the ruled line under Silling's first one is at three hundred and eighty-eight and it is blank and it will be blank, and the letter is at a hundred and ninety-seven."**

Nobody read a pair out at the trough this morning, because there is no pair to read out on a morning that does not carry one, and the board at the north end of the one room stood with the frame on it and nothing under it, and nobody remarked on that either.

A mason came down the middle road at about the seventh hour with a hod and no chart, and Emrys Dole came behind him with lime on his knees and did not come inside the gate, and Ismay Rooke came third and put his hat on the bench at the gate, which he has done on three mornings now and which has not become a habit.

Ismay Rooke read the sheet from the top down, which is the other way round from Auret Sill, and he read it twice, and when he came to line four he put his thumb on it and stayed there.

**"Line four says the joint at the head is looked at by whoever is going to look at it, and that is the only line on this sheet that has no name against it, and I want to know whether that is a hole in it or the whole point of it."**

**"It is the whole point of it and I will not fill it in."**

**"Then somebody is going to be asked who is going to look at it, and there is nobody in this yard who has said they are going to look at it."**

**"There are two people who have said they are going to look at it and neither of them has said it in this yard, and I am not going to say it in this yard for them, and if you want somebody over that joint you will have to find a person who says they are in charge of it, and that person is not going to be chosen in this yard."**

Ismay Rooke took his thumb off line four and did not say anything else about it, and about four people in that yard heard the whole of it and about nine did not.

The compost board at the back of the tap house read paid at thirty-one and the other column was empty. The use log had fifteen lines and no sixteenth. The barrow went out eleven times and eleven is a floor. Seven sessions stood entered in this holding's book and there was no eighth. The requests stood at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stood at fifty-three and they were not the same list and were not added and were not going to be. The board in the one room stood at thirty-nine blanks with the second rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time. The four sheets on the long table were none entered, none refused, and had no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper there is a woman's own sheet with a refusal on it in her own hand and is not one of the four. The ladder stood against the wall with nobody on it. The ring of bare ground behind the wall was not walked on and not measured and not priced. The offer lay on the low board undated and unpicked and not withdrawn and nobody went near it. The man of about seventy was not fetched and the count of questions put to him was nil.

And at about the ninth hour the schedule was still on the middle table with the four stones on its corners and the crease going through two of the lines, and about four people had put a hand flat on it at some point in the morning, and nobody had picked it up and turned it over, and a man from the lane above the fourth place stood at the north end of it and read it on his own and did not sign it and did not say he would.

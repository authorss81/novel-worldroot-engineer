# Chapter 741

## The Twenty-Fourth Of The Twentieth, And A Man At The Middle Table Saying That He Cannot Be There At The Fourth Hour

The four bodies of households came up the lane together for the first time since the spring and they walked in separately, four parties of people arriving within about ten minutes of each other and standing in four different places and not standing together, and the two councils came with them and stood apart from both, and Odile Vray counted all of them twice and said the number out loud both times because the number had moved.

**"Four bodies of households and two councils,"** she said, **"and that is two numbers and not one, and I am not writing them in one place on a board, and I am not going to tell anybody what six would come to, because there is no six."**

Marek Vale had the schedule on the middle table again with four stones on the corners and the crease still going through two of the lines.

**"Line one says the north end of the ring first and the draw before anything is asked for at this end, and it says the morning, and I did not put an hour on it. I want that understood by everybody in this yard before anybody objects to it, because I have deliberately not put an hour on it, and if somebody wants an hour on it they are welcome to write one and put their name on it."**

**"I want an hour on it."**

**"Then it is your hour and your name on it, and I will lay it beside mine and neither of us will know which of the two the crew works to at the fourth hour of a morning."**

A man of about fifty from the households on the sluice road came and stood at the north end of the table with both hands on the cloth and read line one three times.

**"We cannot be there at the fourth hour,"** he said. **"That is not a reason and I am not going to give you one. It is a fact about four households and I have come four miles to stand here and say it."**

**"Then be here at the fifth."**

**"We can be at the fifth."**

**"Write the fifth on your own line and put your name against it, and I will put mine with the fourth, and the crew will do both and will read both out, and nobody in this yard will be told which of them is the hour."**

**"That is not what I asked for and you have offered it anyway."**

**"It is not what you asked for and I am not going to ask you what you asked for in the middle of a yard, because you will answer me and you will be standing here all day afterwards saying that a man put words in your mouth."**

The man of the sluice road wrote the fifth on his own line and put his name against it and gave the pencil back and did not shake hands with anybody and was not thanked and did not want to be.

**"I have not refused your sheet,"** he said, from about six feet off. **"I have put a different hour on one line of it, and if anybody in this yard says the sluice road said no to the work then they have got it wrong and they can come and be told so by me."**

**"You have not refused anything,"** Marek Vale said, **"and I am going to say that in this yard and let it stand: the sluice road has put a fifth hour on the first line and has refused nothing. And I am not going to write the word refused on any paper about it either, and if a refusal gets written down about a man who has not refused one then that word is the only thing that happens to him and it goes in a room I have never been in."**

Sera Quill was at the boards at about the sixth hour with the slate under her arm and she stood where she could see the gate and the four faces nearest it.

**"A hundred and sixty-five in force. Twenty-nine taken. A hundred and thirty-six not."**

**"Up one again."**

**"Up one. And the first of my two is one hundred and eighth, and it is in the book, and the other one is where it was on the morning you asked me about it, which is nowhere you have looked."**

She turned the slate over and did not turn it back, and nobody in this yard asked her to.

The crew man had not been asked for any of them and gave two of them off his own book at about the seventh hour, standing at the boards with his wet sleeve against his side.

**"Five hundred and fourth of the run. The channel came up eight in the night and it is up eight on an even morning and down five on an odd one, and that is the only rule in this holding that I have ever been able to say out loud without a book in my hand."**

**"Then why do you write it down."**

**"Because the rule is not mine and the day is, and I want the two of them in the same book in case somebody changes the rule in a room four days north."**

The two stones were read by a man from the middle row who has read them at the gate every morning since the wall went up and who read them from the bottom up.

**"Eight hundred and eighty-eight at the near board. Nine hundred and thirty-five at the top of the wall. And one thousand and three hundred and seventy hundredweight came down the channel in the night, which is the largest number that has come down it since the spring and it is not a good thing and nobody in this yard should write it down as a good thing."**

Nia Vale read the window off the ledge where her own thumb had been on it the morning before and did not look at the boards to do it.

**"Four hundred and seventy-one. Two hundred and forty-one above, and it is the one that came. Two hundred and thirty below and it stood."**

At about the seventh hour the north board was written on under the frame that is a month nine months back, and there was no pair on it, because there is no pair on a morning that does not carry one, and the frame was there and nothing under it and nobody remarked on that.

Soren Rill came and gave the long sheet in the order he gives it and did not put his thumb on any of it.

**"Five hundred and third over the line. Five hundred and two at the head of it. Five hundred and first under it."**

Tova Reed had the four ages on the corner of the seed board in her own order.

**"Three hundred and ninety-two on Silling's ruled line and it says nothing. Three hundred and ninety-eight on the form. Three hundred and four on the charter. And two hundred and one on the letter, and I do not write those last two the same way twice and you will notice if I do."**

The clerk from up a lane came on foot in his own boots at about the tenth hour and put a folded sheet on the middle table and squared it with the side of his hand and did not sit down.

**"That is a copy of the schedule in a man's writing and there is no date at the head of it and nobody asked me to enter it and I am not entering it, and there are four bodies of households and two councils on it in the order you gave me and neither number is a line under the other."**

**"You have read it."**

**"I have read it twice and I have written nothing on it and I have brought it up a lane on my own legs, and I would like it said in this yard that I brought it up on my own legs, and I would also like it said that nobody has paid me for the day and I am not going to say that twice."**

**"It will be said."**

**"Say it once,"** the clerk said. **"A thing said twice in a yard is a thing being put about."**

And at about the eleventh hour the two councils were still in the yard and had not agreed about anything, and the schedule was on the middle table with a fifth hour on the first line in one man's hand and a fourth hour in no man's hand.

The four households on the sluice road went down the lane with their own man walking in front of them, and the man of about seventy was not fetched and nothing was asked of him at any hour of the day, which is a thing that has not changed in four years and is not going to change this week.

A man went into the one room at about the eighth hour to fetch a chair and came out with the chair and did not look at the board, and the thirty-nine blanks stood where they stand and the second rule under them stood open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and four sheets lay on the long table with none of them entered and none of them refused and no column cut under any of them.

The compost board at the back of the tap house read paid at thirty-one and the second column of it was empty, and the use log had fifteen lines in it and no sixteenth, and the barrow had gone eleven times and eleven is a floor.

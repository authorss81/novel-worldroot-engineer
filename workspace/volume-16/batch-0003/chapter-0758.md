# Chapter 758

## The Eleventh Of The Twenty-First, And Four Places Given To Two People In Words With No Figure On Any Of Them

Nia Vale was gone up the low road before the yard had anybody else in it, and she came back at about the ninth hour with her book under her arm and two people with her, and she put them on the middle table and told them where they were going and how long they would be there and what they were carrying, and she did all of it without writing one figure anywhere.

> **THE ROAD, IN WORDS, AS SHE GIVES IT. NINE PLACES AND NO COLUMN.**
>
> To the third place first because the trays from there go out on the days the trays are ready and not on a day anybody has named. Stay the length of a load and no longer than it takes to hand it over.
>
> To the fourth place after that. Perrin Dae's cart goes when his cart goes and not when anybody in this yard thinks it ought to.
>
> To the two houses on the top of the fourth road, on foot, with what can be carried on foot, and they are not counted and nobody from this yard goes up that path to see whether they got it.
>
> To the four houses at the end of the second branch, last, because it is the longest run of the four and because the woman who lives in the second of those four will not be standing in her doorway when a cart comes, and she has to be told first and told by somebody whose face she knows.
>
> The second place is on this road. It is between the fourth place and the second branch and it is on the road and there is no water at it. You will carry to the door and you will not carry in and you will not leave the load standing there for a day.
>
> If anybody stops me on the road I will say a road is a road and I am on it, and if that is not enough then it is not enough and you will hear about it from me and not from them.

She read that out once to two men and a woman and then took the sheet back off the table and put it inside her book, and one of the two men asked her whether she was going to give the numbers to the engineer of record.

**"There are no numbers on it and I am not going to make any up so that there are."**

**"He keeps the figure of everything in this holding."**

**"He keeps the figure of the water,"** Nia Vale said. **"I move the water, the food and the people, and I am answerable for the four places and the order they get done in and I answer for it to the people in those places and not to a man at a table. He does not ask me for a figure and I do not take one to him, and that is not a courtesy either of us is doing. He is not the engineer of that road. I am the person who runs it and that is a different job and it has its own bad mornings."**

Nobody in that yard said that she should be doing his job instead, and about four people who have said that in the last month said nothing at all.

Tova Reed had the seed house open at the fifth hour and the food side of the board out on the step, and she worked with her back to the north door and her face to the yard, which is where she has worked since the frost week and which she has never explained to anybody, and a mason from the third place had to walk round two trays and put himself square in front of her before a word of his reached her, and she took it down once and did not go back over it with him.

**"Bread and seed in the same load to the four at the end of the second branch, and the reason is that a house which gets seed in the middle of the week and nothing to eat with it does not keep the seed dry.** She wrote it and turned the board out about a foot so two men could see it. **"And the second place is on the route and has no water at it and is going on the route this week and the week after, and I have not put a date on anything and I have not cut a second column and I am not going to.** She looked at the man from the third place without turning her head. **"And nobody is going to put a hand on my arm about it, so do not start."**

Nobody put a hand on her arm about it, or about anything else, at any hour of that day.

Seven o'clock came and the girl from the second place put two hundred and seven over the rule on the north board and four hundred and forty-five under it with her back to the yard, and she did not turn round afterwards to see whether anybody had looked, and there was nobody at that trough and there was not going to be.

Hanne Brack came up the lane from the second place at about the eighth hour with her sleeves wet to the elbow and gave the two stones because she reads them off the same board everybody else reads them off and not because anybody is at that place and not because she has been asked for anything.

**"Nine hundred and five at the near end and nine hundred and fifty-two at the top of it. And I am not going to stand here and tell you the pumps at that place are dry because you all know they are dry and I have told four of you so in the last two days, and I am not saying it a fifth time in a yard to get something."**

**"What are you saying it for, then."**

**"I am saying it because somebody in this yard read out two numbers this morning off a board and everybody heard them, and I am not going to be the woman who only ever speaks when there is a figure on the table."**

The man who lays for three councils came down the middle road with his hod on his shoulder and gave his two to the wall itself rather than to anybody.

**"Down five, same as every morning with an odd number on it, and that is one thousand and three hundred and eighty-nine hundredweight, and it puts us on the five hundred and twenty-first morning of this run. The ninth course at the near end wants laying and I am going to lay it to the course under it and not to a number, and if any of you would like to come and watch me not do that you are welcome."**

Nobody watched him not do it and about four people went and watched him do it.

Nia Vale read the window off the rack where she had it written in her own hand from the wall and gave both halves and then said which one had come, and this was the first morning in a fortnight that she had given the halves before the whole.

**"Four hundred and eighty-eight between the two of them, and of that, two hundred and thirty-nine came and two hundred and forty-nine stood. I am aware that three people in this yard have it the other way round in their heads this morning and I am not going to correct anybody who has not asked me, because a figure that everybody checks on is a figure nobody is actually standing behind."**

Ismay Rooke read the long sheet off the wall from the bench at the gate where he has stood every morning for six weeks facing the lane, and he read it without coming into the yard at all and had to raise his voice for the last figure.

**"Over the line it is five hundred and twentieth. Under the line, five hundred and eighteenth. And the head of it is five hundred and nineteen."**

**"You have been reading that wall from further away than anybody."**

**"I have been reading it from as far away as a man can get and still read it, and I have not come off this bench in six weeks and I am not going to be asked why."**

Kellan Rusk gave the form from the step, one figure, no sentence with it beyond the one about the drawer.

**"Four hundred and fifteen. Not entered, key on the nail, and I am not going to say the rest of it because I have said it four mornings running and the saying of it is starting to sound like a thing I want."**

Tova Reed came out at about the tenth hour and gave her three to a woman at the seed house door who did not want them and told her so, and she gave them anyway and did not ask twice.

**"Three hundred and twenty-one, and that is the charter, and it is a year older than anybody in this yard. The ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and nine and it is blank and it is going to stay blank. And the letter has got to two hundred and eighteen."** She looked down the lane towards the gate. **"And four places went out on the road this morning and none of them has a figure on it and none of them is in this yard's book and I am the one who sent them and I will answer for them to them and not to you."**

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped, and the man who pushes it said a road you cannot put a column under is still a road and that he has never needed one.

Nobody went down the low road in this holding and nobody was sent, and about four people at the gate watched two figures go up it at about the fourth hour and said nothing about what was on the road or about the light coming off the top of it.

And at about the eleventh hour Nia Vale was still in the yard, which she had not been in nine days, and she put her book on the middle table beside the four stones and shut it and said that she would be back up the road on Thursday and that anybody who wanted a place on it could come and stand at the gate before the light, and she said the name of no person at all.

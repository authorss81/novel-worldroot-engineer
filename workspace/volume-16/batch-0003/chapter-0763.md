# Chapter 763

## The Sixteenth Of The Twenty-First, And A Man At The Boards Three Hundredweight Short Of It

The man who pushes the barrow had it in and out of the yard before the wall took the light, and he told the woman at the seed house door what had come down the channel in the night, and he told her three hundredweight short.

**"One thousand and four hundred hundredweight."**

The woman wrote it down and then did not write it down and looked at the boards from about nine feet off with the corner of the tray still in her hand.

**"Say it again and look at the board while you say it."**

He looked at the board.

**"One thousand and four hundred and three hundredweight."**

The crew man was at the sill with his own book open on his thumb and did not have to be asked.

**"Up eight from yesterday morning. Yesterday was one thousand and three hundred and ninety-five, so eight more is one thousand and four hundred and three, and the figure you said was the figure this channel stood at on Sunday morning and it has not been that since.** He shut the book. **"It is not a small thing to say a number short. It is the whole number that people in four houses are going to plan their day against."**

**"I will say it right."**

**"Say it right to the four of them and not to me, because I am a man with a book and they are the ones that use it."**

Nobody said anything to him about it after that and nobody said he ought to know better, and he went out and came back eleven times and the eleventh was the last one and it was the eleventh for the reason it has been the eleventh all season.

At about the seventh hour the north board was wiped under the frame that is a month nine months back, and the frame was still the frame it has been for four years, and the girl from the second place came at about the eighth hour, looked at it, wiped it again where she had already wiped it, and went away without writing anything on it at all.

Odile Vray saw her go and said one thing about it and no more.

**"That is the first morning that board has been empty since it went up and nobody has asked me about it and I am not going to ask her. It is her board and she knows what is on it and what is not on it, and on a morning two modulo four she writes and on a morning like this one she does not, and there is no rule anywhere that says she has to write on every morning of the week.** She put the chalk back in the tray. **"Somebody in this yard will have noticed it by the dark and will have an opinion about it and I am telling all of them now that the opinion is not going to be put in front of her."**

Harlan Vetch came down the wall at about the ninth hour and gave the window standing on the second course with his back against the stone.

**"The pair came off the wall this morning as four hundred and ninety-three, the taller half having moved, two hundred and fifty-two against two hundred and forty-one. Three mornings running that the tall one has come and I have said so out loud every one of them, and two weeks running before that the short one came every morning, and I have stopped saying which is which and started saying which is which, and those are two different sentences and only one of them is a figure."**

Hanne Brack came up from the second place with the cart and read the two stones off the board on her way past without stopping and without being asked.

**"Nine hundred and ten at the near end and nine hundred and fifty-seven at the top."**

About four people in that yard heard a round number come out of a woman's mouth and looked at each other over it, and one of them said that it was a round number, and Hanne Brack said that it was a number at the near end of a wall and that the wall had not changed its mind about anything since the spring, and went on into the yard with the cart.

Ismay Rooke read the long sheet off the wall from the bench at the gate, facing the lane, and gave his two in the order he has used since the sheet went up.

**"At the head of the wall, five hundred and twenty-four. Above the line, five hundred and twenty-fifth. Below it, five hundred and twenty-third."**

**"You are still on that bench."**

**"I am on that bench and I have been on it every morning since the wall went up and I have not seen one person go past it that I did not already know the name of, and if I stand up off it now then whatever it is I have been sitting on comes home to this gate with me.** He turned the slate round. **"Up eight in the night. One thousand and four hundred and three hundredweight. Five hundred and twenty-sixth morning of the run."**

Emrys Dole came off the middle road at about the tenth hour with the register of his own and gave his figures to a man at the boards who was holding somebody else's coat, and the man took them wrong and gave them to a second man who gave them to a third, and by the time they reached the boards they were a third figure short and Emrys Dole walked back up the road for the length of a field to fix it himself.

**"Five hundred and twenty-fourth and not twenty-third, under the line, five hundred and twenty-four at the head, and if that is how that sheet travels up this road every morning then the wall is the only honest thing in this holding.** He went back down the middle road without waiting to be thanked for walking out and back.

Kellan Rusk gave the form from the step and did not come down and did not look at the six names on the sheet still up on the boards.

**"Four hundred and twenty."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the step with her back half to the yard, because a man from the third place was at her left shoulder dictating at her and she had turned to put her good ear to him and had not said why.

**"Three hundred and twenty-six on the charter. The ruled line under Silling's second one is at four hundred and fourteen and empty, and in about eleven days that line will stand at a round hundred and above, and I am going to be saying this in a yard on the day, and I would like somebody in this holding to have heard me the first time. The letter is two hundred and twenty-three."**

A man from the middle row, who had said nothing about the weight because he had already been corrected once this week, said that if you put the near end, the window and the water together you could see what a morning in this holding was actually worth, and the woman whose house stands next door to the second place said one sentence from about nine feet off without turning round.

**"Those three are on three boards and three of them belong to three different people and one of the three is mine in the sense that I have to live under it. Put them in a column and you will have a number and the number will be a thing that happened to me.** She went back in. **"And it is not that I mind a number. It is that I would have to be able to read it and I cannot."**

Nobody in that yard put them in a column and nobody asked her to explain that again, and about four households at the end of the second branch cannot read a line either and were not asked about it and are not going to be.

The use log in the tool house has fifteen lines in it and the sixteenth is not written and was not written on that Tuesday.

Six ruled rows sit on the shelf in the one room for the things nobody knows, and nobody has cut a seventh for anything at all, and the third column of the sheet of terms is still ruled and still empty under a heading nobody has seen fit to improve.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall and nobody put a foot on it and the ground inside the ring behind it took nobody's boot and nobody's tape and nobody's price.

And at about the eleventh hour the thing on the low board outside the gate was where it has been since before the flood with the same dust on it and the same nothing written on it, and nobody went out to it and nobody brought it in and the sheet that came up the lane with a name at the head of it was still on the middle table, and the six names on the sequence were still six, and the ladder at the tool house wall had now gone four days without anybody in this holding going near the bottom of it.

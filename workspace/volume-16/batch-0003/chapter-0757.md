# Chapter 757

## The Tenth Of The Twenty-First, And A Ruled Line On A Board That Nobody Filled In

The clerk from up a lane put the chalk copy on the boards at about the sixth hour because about nine people had asked him for it, and he wrote it out longhand on the back of a sheet he had already used for something else and stood back and read it once himself and did not read it out to anybody.

> **WHAT THIS HOLDING IS CARRYING. WRITTEN OUT IN CHALK COPY SO THAT ANYBODY AT THE GATE CAN READ IT. UNDATED.**
>
> The seed route and the food route, both, in one woman's hand, with the second place on them and no water at it.
>
> The window, both halves, off the wall, and which half came on the day.
>
> The five lines of the schedule, under four stones, no date at the head of it, and the order of two of them disputed on the sheet itself in a second hand.
>
> Nine beds dug. Six of them held the dry-season draw. Five of them held the flood load. One of them went with the root on it and is not dug again this season. One of them stands soft at the north end and let everything the draw brought go out again at the other end of itself, and it is soft on this morning.
>
> The short measure for the outer branches. Every branch past the third road. The four houses at the end of the second branch. The two houses on the top of the fourth road under a lean-to.
>
> That last line is ruled and there is nothing whatever in it. It has been ruled since Saturday morning. Nobody has cut a column under it and nobody has taken the rule back out.

He read the whole of it twice on his own and then went and stood at the gate with his hat in his hands, and about four people came and read it inside a quarter of an hour and every one of them read the bottom line twice.

The load crew went up the middle road at about the fifth hour with the gauges and two buckets and a spade and they did not come to the middle table and they did not look at the four stones, and they worked the ring until the light went and they wrote their own two sentences in their own book afterwards and neither sentence had a question in it, and nobody in this yard went up after them to ask whether they wanted anything said.

Sera Quill was at the boards before the seventh hour with the slate under her arm and she gave the count before anybody had asked her for it, because on a morning two modulo four the count moves and she has never once let a yard get through an hour without it.

**"A hundred and sixty-nine in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty not."**

**"One higher."**

**"Up one, and it goes up on the mornings it goes up on and on no other. Now the other one, and I am going to give you the one that goes in this holding's book and not the other, and I am going to do that without anybody asking me which one it is, because we have been through this four times and I am tired of it and it is not a question anybody is entitled to."** She turned the slate over and read off it. **"One hundred and twelfth. That is the one in the book, and the one that is thirteen from it is in my head and it stays in my head, and you can all see that I have got one slate and not two."**

**"Nobody asked you."**

**"Nobody asked me and I said it anyway, and that is the whole of what I came out here for."**

Perrin Dae came down off the fourth path at about the eighth hour with a cart that had somebody else's name on it and gave his two in the middle of the yard where the cart could not get away from him.

**"Up eight overnight. One thousand and three hundred and ninety-four hundredweight, and the count of mornings stands at five hundred and twentieth, and I have never in my life been able to give those two in one go without wanting to add them to something, and today I am not going to want it.** He put a hand on the cart side. **"I have turned timber at my own cost twice this season off the back of that number."**

**"You are going to say those two out loud, then."**

**"I have said them out loud. I am going to be quiet now."**

The boy who carries water came through with two cans, put them on the tap house sill, and answered a question nobody had put to him out loud, which is what he does about four times a morning.

**"It is four hundred and eighty-seven all told. The half that came is the tall one, two hundred and forty-nine, with the short one, two hundred and thirty-eight, standing where it was, and that is the other way round from yesterday and I wrote it on my hand yesterday so I know it was the other way round.** He turned his hand over and showed the two figures written on it in chalk and then rubbed them out with his thumb. **"I am not going to keep it on my hand. I am going to get it off the board like everybody else and stop being the man who remembers."**

Odile Vray gave the two stones from the boards and did not go up the wall for either of them and said so before she was asked.

**"At the near end, nine hundred and four. At the top of it, nine hundred and fifty-one. Both of them written under my hand and both of them standing where they were standing when I wrote them."**

The clerk from up a lane came back from the gate at about the ninth hour and read three figures off the long sheet on the wall without taking his own copy down, and he read the middle of them first, which nobody who reads that wall ever does.

**"Five hundred and eighteen at the head of it. Five hundred and seventeenth underneath. Five hundred and nineteenth over the top line.** He looked up. **"I did it in that order on purpose because two of you would have made a fight of the usual order in front of a yard and I am not going to be the reason."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the form under his arm and the key already back on its nail and gave one figure and stopped.

**"Four hundred and fourteen. Not entered. Not going to be entered this morning, and the two of us who know what is not in the drawer are the only two people in this holding who know that and I would like that left as it is."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the seed house door with her face to the yard and a mason from the second branch dictating at her from about nine feet off, and she took it once and did not ask him to say it again.

**"The charter stands at three hundred and twenty, the oldest of the three I have and the one nobody reads. The ruled line under Silling's second is four hundred and eight and empty, and it is going to be empty tomorrow at four hundred and nine. The letter is two hundred and seventeen.** She put her chin on her hand for a second. **"I have stood at this door every morning for nineteen days saying the word short measure out loud to nobody, and I said it in here on Saturday and about four people heard me, and on Sunday a woman with a barrow asked this yard a question and got the yard's own answer, and yesterday morning a man said no in front of all of you.** She looked at the bottom line on the chalk copy and did not go near it. **"I am not going to say anything about what any of that cost, because I do not know what it cost and neither does anybody in this yard, and if one of you writes a number on that line I will not argue with you about the number. I will say that it came from you."**

Nobody wrote a number on that line on that Wednesday and nobody has written one since, and the rule is still ruled and the space under it is still there to be looked at, and about nine people in this holding have looked at it every day since and not one of them has been given a figure on what it is.

A man from the middle row stood in front of it for about two minutes at the eleventh hour and said out loud that if the line had been filled in on Saturday then four households at the end of the second branch would have known by now what they were getting, and a woman beside him said that four households at the end of that branch cannot read a line, and the yard did not choose between them and the yard did not close over them either.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped, and the man pushing it did not go out of the gate on the strength of a good reason.

In the one room the second rule on the wall stands open, and it has stood open at the thirty-ninth blank for four years without anything going under it, and the drawer is shut with its key on its nail, and four sheets lie on the long table with nothing entered on any of them and nothing refused on any of them and no column cut under any of them, and the fifth piece of paper in that room is a woman's own and it is not one of those four.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall all day and nobody put a foot on it.

Six rows are ruled on the shelf in that room for what nobody knows, and no seventh has been cut for anything, and the third column of the sheet of terms stands ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved, with no sixth term written on it and no seventh and no eighth.

And out past the gate the low board still had the offer lying on it in the same wind that had lifted a stone off a schedule yesterday morning, and it had not been picked up and it had not got a date and it had not gone back to whoever left it, and nobody in that yard said a single word to anybody about the difference between a line with nothing under it and a sheet with nothing on it, and there is not one printed in this holding that says they are the same thing.

# Chapter 764

## The Seventeenth Of The Twenty-First, And Three Crews Out On Paths With Nobody Standing At The Gate Waiting

Three crews went out before the light on that Wednesday and none of them asked anybody in this holding for anything, and by the time the gate was open at the sixth hour all three of them were gone up their own roads with their own tools and their own water and there was nobody at this gate waiting for any of them to come back and say how it had gone.

Soren Rill had a sheet written out and sent down the road on the same morning, and it was copied in four hands in four yards before the light was properly up, and it says what he has written on it.

> **WHERE THE WORK IS ON A WEDNESDAY. WRITTEN OUT BY ME AND SENT DOWN THE ROAD ON THE SAME MORNING.**
>
> One. Seed and food logistics out of the store, door open before light, four places on the route and the second place on it with no water at it. Tova Reed.
>
> Two. The moving reserves on the low road and the four paths, four places in words, no column, no figure. Nia Vale.
>
> Three. The paths themselves, with the crews out and the assemblies sitting. Roan Selk has nine terms at the top of the fourth path and nine of us know what they are and none of us is going to describe them, and the route those nine of them keep open is theirs and is not counted with any route of ours and is not on this list as a thing we are doing.
>
> Four. The schedule and the five lines. Four stones on it, no date at the head of it. Districts to name their own holders before the end of next week, and I have written that on it in a way that asks the man at the middle table nothing.
>
> Five. The record. The form, the charter, the letter, the boards and the crew's own book, in five hands, and not one of the five is mine.
>
> The man at the head of the channel is not in this holding this morning. He is not on this list. Nobody at this gate is waiting for him and nobody at this gate has asked anybody where he is.

He read that fifth line out in about four yards and it was received in about the same tone in all four of them, and by the tenth hour about nine people in this holding had heard the whole of that sheet word for word from somebody who had only read it once.

At the seventh hour the north board was written on under a frame that is a month nine months back, the girl's figures going over the rule and under it, two hundred and ten and four hundred and fifty-one, after a morning when she had wiped it and put nothing down at all, and nobody said anything to her about either morning and she explained neither.

She stayed at the board about four minutes longer than she had on any other morning and read the two figures and then looked out along the middle road and the low road both, and then she put the chalk in her pocket and went out on the second crew's road with her coat on and did not tell anybody she was going and came back at the dark with mud to the knee and would not say where she had been.

Nia Vale gave her two at the rack at about the seventh hour with the road still grey behind her and did not put her book down.

**"It fell five again. One thousand and three hundred and ninety-eight hundredweight, and this is the five hundred and twenty-seventh morning of the run, and I am the only person in this holding who is glad about that figure.** She got her book open on the rack. **"I have been out since before it was light and I am going out again inside the hour, and I will say both of these again on Thursday, and anybody who wants them may come and stand at this rack at any hour and I am not going to pretend that is a favour."**

**"The two tanks on the second branch are where I left them and the third tank is not full and it is not going to be full this week and I have not written the reason anywhere because the reason is a word and not a number."**

The girl from the second place, who was at the boards at about the ninth hour with the chalk out and no coat and mud on her hands, gave the window without being asked and gave it the way she has given it since the flood, which is both halves and then the whole, and she said the direction too.

**"Both halves, and then the whole, because that is the way I was taught it. Four hundred and ninety-four. Two hundred and forty-two came, two hundred and fifty-two stood, and I have had it wrong on the back of my hand twice this season and I am not going to have it written down again, so somebody had better write it down properly and I am not fussy who."**

**"Harlan says the same."**

**"Then write down what Harlan says, and if the two of you ever disagree I want to be told which of you is wrong, because that is the one thing I cannot do from here."**

Odile Vray gave the two stones from the boards and went up two courses of the wall for the first time since the frost and came back down again before anybody had decided whether she had.

**"The near stone has gone to nine hundred and eleven and the top one to nine hundred and fifty-eight, forty-seven of masonry between them as ever. And I have been up there and I can see the near board from the top course without my hands, and it is the same stone it has always been, and I am coming down now and not making a habit of it."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet at about the tenth hour and had not been near the wall since he sent his own sheet out at the sixth hour, and he read it from about eleven feet back with his hands behind him.

**"Five hundred and twenty-fourth under the line, five hundred and twenty-five at the head of it, five hundred and twenty-sixth over the line."**

**"You sent a sheet down the road at six and you read that one at ten. Which one of those is your job."**

**"The first one is my job and the second one is the wall's, and I have done both of them for twenty years and only one of them is work anybody can be sacked for.** He wiped the chalk off this time. **"You have three crews out and no engineer of record at this gate and everything that has happened at this holding in nine days has happened with the two of those facts standing in the same yard, and I am not going to say that is a good way to run a place, because it is what we have."**

Kellan Rusk gave the form from the step. The door behind him was shut and the key was on the nail and he had a second sheet under his arm that he did not give to anybody and that he put back under his arm twice in about two minutes.

**"Four hundred and twenty-one."**

Tova Reed came out at about the tenth hour with the corner of the board under her arm and gave her three to the man who pushes the barrow because he was the only one at the seed house door that morning.

**"The charter, three hundred and twenty-seven. Under Silling's second ruled line, four hundred and fifteen, and it is blank. And the letter has got to two hundred and twenty-four."** She held the corner of the board against her hip. **"And there is going to be a tray going out to the third place at two o'clock and after that I am going into that store and shut the door and sit down for a quarter of an hour, and that is what I am doing and no one is going to come and tell me it is not allowed."**

Nobody went down the low road in this holding and nobody was sent, and Roan Selk was on it as he has been three times this season and he is one of the nine and not one of us, and nobody in this yard used the word absorbed in any form on that Wednesday and nobody built anything out of what the root did at the top of a path, and nobody in this holding put a hand on any of the nine and did not try to make them into anything at all.

The barrow went eleven journeys on that Wednesday and was stood wheel first in the yard at the fourth hour and was not taken out again that day for any reason including a good one, and eleven is a floor and not a total.

Nobody went for the man of about seventy on that Wednesday and nobody put a single question to him at any hour of it, and the count of questions this holding has ever put to that man is nothing at all and was nothing at all before today.

The one room was shut at the sixth hour and shut again at the dark, with the key back on its nail at both, and the blanks above its long table stood at thirty-nine with the rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the four sheets on that table had nothing on them and nothing under them.

And at about the eleventh hour there were five kinds of work being done at once in five places and not one of them being done by the man who has refused to be the one who does them, and nobody at that gate was waiting for him, and about nine people in this holding noticed that and about twenty-one did not notice it at all.

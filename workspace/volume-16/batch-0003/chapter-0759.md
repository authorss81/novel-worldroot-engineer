# Chapter 759

## The Twelfth Of The Twenty-First, And An Empty Line Held Open In The Middle Of A Table

The table went up on the boards at about the seventh hour with a stone on each of the two top corners and nothing at the head of it, and the yard read it standing up, the way a yard reads anything that has no date on it.

> **THE TEST, BY DISTRICT AND BY REGION, ON ONE SHEET. UNDATED, AND NO HEADING OVER IT.**
>
> Fourth place. Nine houses. Tested yesterday.
>
> Third place. Six houses. Tested yesterday.
>
> The four at the end of the second branch. Four houses. Tested the day before that.
>
> The middle row. Twenty-two houses. Tested the day before that.
>
> The region three miles up. Eleven houses. Tested on no day.
>
> That last row is ruled across five columns like the rows above it and the fifth column on it is empty. The four rows above it have a day in the fifth column and that row does not. Nobody has written a word in the space, nobody has ruled a line through it, nobody has cut the column out and nobody has cut a sixth row under it.

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall and gave the window in the ordinary way, without coming the whole way and without stopping.

**"Two hundred and fifty came in the night and two hundred and thirty-nine did not move, and the two of them together make four hundred and eighty-nine of window, and it is the second morning running that the tall half has moved, and it was the short half moving all spring, and I have said that out loud once and I am not going to say it a second time to a yard that writes it down wrong on purpose.** He went back up.

The crew man had his own book out on the sill with his thumb in it and gave his two to the man from the middle row because that man had asked for them twice this week.

**"Nine beds in this book, and five hundred and twenty-two mornings of this run, and one thousand and three hundred and ninety-seven hundredweight in the channel in the night, and I write all three in that order because that is the order they happen in and because a man who cannot write them in that order should not be keeping the book.** He shut it on his thumb. **"I write those two down every morning and I have written down nine beds since the frost and I am not going to write down a house tonight, and that is not a rule anybody gave me, it is a book I keep."**

The man who lays for three councils read the two stones off the wall on his way past with the hod on his shoulder and did not stop for it.

**"Nine hundred and six, near end. Nine hundred and fifty-three, top. Forty-seven of masonry, as ever. And the tenth course wants laying today and has wanted laying since Tuesday and I am going to lay it after I have had my dinner because it is the same course either way."**

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the ninth hour, read the table standing at about the same distance from it as everybody else, and stood there a while.

**"Then say the thing you have been turning over since you got here, and say it to the yard and not to me."**

**"I have not turned anything over."**

**"You have read that bottom row four times in about as many minutes and you have not read one of the rows above it once since Tuesday, and I know what you are doing with it because I have been doing it with it for a week.** He put his thumb on the empty fifth column without touching the paper. **"Eleven houses. If those eleven houses had been tested yesterday along with the fourth place, we would have known by the dark what those eleven houses are getting. They are not being tested. So they are getting whatever the untested get. That is not a theory, that is what an untested house gets, and I have been in this holding nineteen days and I have never seen anything else."**

He said it in front of about thirty people and he did not say it as a question and he did not ask anybody to agree with it.

Kellan Rusk came out of the doorway with the door behind him shut and the key already back on its nail and answered him from the step.

**"Not from me."**

**"Kellan."**

**"An empty line is not a figure and it is not a nothing either, and you have just done the thing that gets done with that line about nine times a season in this holding, which is to put a value in it and call the value the truth. You are not allowed to say those eleven houses would have been better off, because better off than what, and by how much, and measured how, and who would have put the water there.** He came down three steps and stopped. **"If you want to write a figure in that space then you go and get one from somebody who has put one in and been proved right, and there is not one of those in this holding, and if there were it would be on a sheet with a date on it."**

**"I am not writing a figure on it."**

**"No, you are not, and that is the only reason I came out of that door.** He went back up. **"And I will tell you the other half of it so that nobody stands here all afternoon thinking a man in a doorway agrees with you. I do not know whether those eleven houses would have been better off. Nobody in this yard knows. They have been asked four times and they have said no four times and nobody in this yard has ever asked them why, and the reason nobody has asked them why is that we would not care for the answer and we know it."**

And that was the end of it, because about twenty-nine other people in that yard had heard it and not one of them took a side and not one of them told either of them he was wrong, and the yard went on standing there for another half minute and then went back to what it was doing.

Nobody in this holding defended that refusal on that Friday and nobody attacked it. A man from the second branch said it was their own ground and their own no, which is not a defence and not an attack. A woman at the second place said that a place you cannot get to is a place you do not get to, which is the same sentence twice and is also not a defence and not an attack.

Nobody asked them why. Nobody asked anybody in this yard to choose anything and no field was taken at any hour of the day and nobody asked anybody how they felt about any of it.

The clerk from up a lane read the long sheet off the wall with his finger on the third figure and got it right first time and said so himself before anybody said it.

**"At the head of the wall, five hundred and twenty. Over the line, five hundred and twenty-first. Underneath, five hundred and nineteenth. First time this week and I am not going to be quiet about it."**

Kellan Rusk gave the form from the step and gave nothing else at all, not even the part about the drawer, which was the first time in a fortnight he had left that out.

**"Four hundred and sixteen."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the seed house door with her back to the trays and her face to the yard.

**"Three hundred and twenty-two on the charter. Four hundred and ten on the ruled line under Silling's second one, and it is empty, and if anybody in this yard was waiting for a round number to write on that is not going to happen, because it is going to be four hundred and eleven tomorrow and four hundred and twelve the day after and there is nothing at the end of it.** She did not turn round. **"Two hundred and nineteen on the letter."**

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past four times that Friday on the way up to the top course and read on by nobody, and nobody was offered anything at it, and the figure that stands in the column at it does not go back and has not been read on any morning of this holding and is not going to be read on this one.

The bridge is not on and nobody in this yard put it back on, and nobody has said one word in a month about the column at that door, and it does not go back.

Nobody went into the one room except to turn a key, which was done at the sixth hour and again at the dark and at every hour between them and at no hour after that. Thirty-nine blanks were chalked on the wall above its long table and the rule under them stood open and empty, and the four sheets lying there carried nothing at all and have had nothing cut under them since the frost.

The barrow went out eleven times and came back eleven times and was stood wheel first in the middle of the yard with the shafts up, and it was not wheeled out of that yard again on that day by anybody for any reason.

And at about the eleventh hour the sheet on the middle table was still lying under the four stones where it has lain since it was put down, unpicked, undated, unentered, unfilled in at the foot and not withdrawn, and the bottom line of the chalk copy was still ruled with nothing in it, and the yard had spent a whole morning looking at both of those and had put a figure on neither.

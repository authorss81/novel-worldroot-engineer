# Chapter 761

## The Fourteenth Of The Twenty-First, And A Weight Written On A Board Without The And In It

The north end of the ring was wet to a foot in the bottom of the new cut by the fifth hour and the crew had two men on it and the third of them came at the ninth, and the whole of it was ordinary work on a Sunday and nobody in this yard treated it as anything else.

Sera Quill was at the boards before the seventh hour and gave the count the way she gives it, which is first and in the same tone as the weather.

**"A hundred and seventy in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty-one not."**

**"That is one higher than yesterday."**

**"Up one, and I have a slate and not two slates, and one of the two numbers I have got in my head is going to stay in my head this morning as it has every morning this week, and nobody has asked me for it and I am not going to be asked.** She turned the slate over and read the one that goes in the book. **"One hundred and thirteenth. That is the one and the other is thirteen from it and it is not on this morning and it is not going to be on any morning this one is on."**

A man off the middle road came up to the boards about a quarter of an hour later with a hod on his shoulder and said the weight to Odile Vray in the middle of about nine people, and said it with an and in it.

**"One thousand and four hundred hundredweight came down the channel in the night, and I have been telling my own house that since before the light."**

Odile Vray wrote on the board what the boards say, which is one thousand four hundred hundredweight, with no and in it anywhere, and she did not say a word about what he had said and neither did anybody else, and about four feet of the yard stood there afterwards not saying anything, and the man put his hod down and went and laid the twelfth course.

**"You did not tell him."**

**"He told me a figure and I wrote down the figure,"** Odile Vray said. **"If the two of you would like me to be the woman at the boards who tells a man of fifty that he cannot say a number out loud in this yard, you can have the job on Monday and I will hand it over with the tray."**

Harlan Vetch was not on the wall and sent the pair down by the girl from the second place with the chalk in her hand and a note on the back of the board that she did not read out and gave to a man at the door.

**"Four hundred and ninety-one once you have both of them. Two hundred and fifty-one came, and two hundred and forty stood, and Harlan says to tell you he is writing on the back of a board now because the front of it is being used for something else and he did not want to come down, and he says if that is not good enough then somebody can go up.** The boy who carries water was there and heard it and repeated it. **"Four hundred and ninety-one of window is what the two of them come to and I have said it out loud in case anybody writes it down different."**

Hanne Brack was at the boards at about the ninth hour with her sleeves wet again, having come up from the second place in the wet, and she gave the two stones off the board without anybody having asked her for them.

**"Up the near end, nine hundred and eight. At the top, nine hundred and fifty-five. Forty-seven of masonry, which has never once been a different number. And I am up here because there is nothing at the pumps and I am not going to talk about it, so you may have two figures instead and you can work out for yourselves which of the two you would rather have."**

**"There is a soft bed at the north end and it is a fact about a bed,"** a man from the fourth place said. **"Say what you came up for."**

**"No,"** Hanne Brack said. **"I am going to go and stand at the wall for an hour, and if you want the two figures you have had them, and if you want me to be somewhere else on a Sunday morning then that is a different arrangement and we will have it some other morning."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet at the tenth hour with a stone holding the bottom corner of it because the wind had got at that end of the wall for the first time this season.

**"Five hundred and twenty-first is the one under the line and five hundred and twenty-third is the one over it and the one at the head is five hundred and twenty-two, and the wind was at that end of the wall for the first time this season."**

**"The wind is at that wall now."**

**"The wind is at that wall now and it will be at the north board by Thursday, and there is nobody in this holding whose job that is, and I have written the letter up twice this season and it has gone nowhere both times, and I am not going to write it a third time about a board.** He took the stone off and the corner lifted about a quarter of an inch. **"Put your own stone on your own board."**

The clerk from up a lane came at about the eleventh hour to write the day's two down in a fair copy for his own master and got the clause the wrong way round and did it, and left the wrong way round on the table for about a minute before anybody looked at it.

Kellan Rusk came out with the form under his arm, gave one figure from the step and shut the door.

**"Four hundred and eighteen."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the step of the seed house with a man dictating at her from the far side of the yard the whole time, and she took down what he said once and did not ask him a second time for any of it, and told nobody in that yard that she was working that way.

**"The charter is three hundred and twenty-four. Under Silling's second ruled line, four hundred and twelve, blank, blank, and blank again in the morning. The letter is two hundred and twenty-one."**

The man who lays for three councils came off the wall at about the fourth hour and gave his two at the north end with two men and a spade listening to him, and he gave them in a low voice because the wind was at that end too.

**"Five hundred and twenty-fourth morning, and there was one thousand four hundred hundredweight in the channel in the night, and that is a round number, and I have been saying it out loud with an and in it all morning like a man at a fair and there is no and in it.** He looked at the two men on the bed. **"You two have got the same figure off the board and you are going to go on writing one thousand four hundred on your own sheets, and you are not going to write one thousand and four hundred, because one of those is the weight and the other one is a way of saying a number in a market."**

Then the two men at the north end told him what the bed had done, which was take the draw clean at the north end and let it out at the far end of itself, and that it was soft at the north end at about the ninth hour and that nine hours had gone into it, and he wrote that in the crew's own book and closed it and did not write the number of hours anywhere else.

The barrow went eleven journeys on that Sunday and stopped, and the man pushing it looked at the north end for a while and then went and got a hod.

The drawer in the one room stayed shut with its key on its nail all day. Thirty-nine blanks on the wall, and the rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and four sheets lying on the long table with no entry on any of them and no column cut under any of them.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall all day and nobody put a foot on it and no figure was printed against either of the two people it stands for.

The saucer at the back of the tap house had its one drop in it on that Sunday, the way it has on every morning of this holding's record, and it did not prove anything about that Sunday, and it has never proved anything about any of them.

And at about the eleventh hour the crew's own sheet for the north end was folded into the crew's own book in front of two men who had been standing in it all day, and it is the only place in this holding where the state of that bed is written, and it says soft at the north end and it says it is not to be dug again and it is not to be priced, and nobody copied it onto the boards and nobody put a number on it.

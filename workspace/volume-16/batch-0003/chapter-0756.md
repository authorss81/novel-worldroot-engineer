# Chapter 756

## The Ninth Of The Twenty-First, And Four Stones Lifted Off A Sheet One At A Time In Front Of The Whole Yard

The wind at the gate end got under the corner of the schedule at about the sixth hour and lifted a stone off it, and the stone went nine inches across the stone floor and stopped against the leg of the bench, and about nine people in that yard watched it do that and not one of them went and picked it up for about a minute and a half.

Marek Vale came up the lane at about the sixth hour with the other three still on the corners of the sheet and put that stone back where it belonged and left the three where they were, and the sheet lay flat on the middle table with the disagreement of two councils about the order of lines two and three written onto it under the stones in a second hand, and there is still no date at the head of it.

The man who pushes the barrow had it out of the gate and back before the wall took the light, and he did not say the number out loud to anybody, which is not a thing he has ever done on any morning of this holding and is not a thing anybody has ever asked him for.

At the seventh hour the girl from the second place wrote two hundred and six above the rule on the north board and four hundred and forty-three under it, under a heading that is a month nine months back and has been that heading since before she came to work here. She put the chalk in her apron pocket and looked once at the two figures and then at the middle of the yard, where nobody was standing, and then she took the far end of a hod. There was nobody at that trough and there has not been for a long time and there was nobody at it this morning either, and nobody in this holding is going to say out loud that two numbers go on being written under that heading by a girl of seventeen with nobody reading them.

Nia Vale came off the low road at about the seventh hour with her book shut under her arm and gave her two at the rack without being asked, in the order she always gives them in, and the yard was doing four other things at once behind her while she said them.

**"There was one thousand and three hundred and eighty-six hundredweight in the channel in the night and it puts us on the five hundred and nineteenth morning of the run, and it is down five, and it is down five on every morning with an odd number on it, and it has been down five on every one of those since before this yard had a wall on the boards."** **"I do not make the rule and I do not break it. I have two figures and I give them when somebody asks and this morning four people asked."**

Harlan Vetch came down far enough off the wall to be heard without coming the whole way, which he does perhaps twice in a season, and gave the window in one line with both halves in it.

**"Two hundred and thirty-eight came and two hundred and forty-eight stood, which is four hundred and eighty-six of window between the two of them and no arithmetic done in public by anybody but me. The short one is the one that comes this morning and it came yesterday morning, and I have told this yard that twice and neither time did anybody write it down, which is the correct amount for it to be written down."**

He went back up before anybody could ask him which was which.

The put came at about the eighth hour, and it came from four people at once and not out of the four stones, and Auret Sill was the first of them and she had her slate under her arm and she did not put it down.

**"It has been on the low board since before the flood and it has not been picked up and it has not got a date on it, and the same terms came up the lane on Friday with a woman's name at the head of the sheet and nobody has lifted that either, and both of them are the same paper with one word changed on it and I am not going to be the one who says the word out loud in a yard."**

**"Say the word."**

**"Permanent."** She turned the slate over and put it flat against her hip. **"Feed the centre this season, a floor under the outer branches, the record kept where four people can reach it, and nobody in this holding able to reopen a branch afterwards. That is what is on the low board. What is new is that it does not stop."**

**"Does not stop."**

**"It does not come to an end at the end of the season and it is not a thing you can be the holder of for two years and then be not the holder of. It is a standing, and a standing has somebody at the top of it, and the somebody is named on a sheet, and I am not going to say the name because somebody in this yard read it out on Friday and it is that woman's and not mine."**

Marek Vale had his hand flat on the middle of the schedule and the wind had not got under it again.

**"Then here is what I am going to say, and I am going to say it here at the middle table in front of everybody who has come up this lane this morning, and I am not going to say it twice and I am not going to write it on anything.** He did not raise his voice and about four people at the far end of the yard came closer. **"The terms on the low board are the terms I have been reading since before the flood. I am not going to take them, and the word that has been added to them does not change what I am refusing. I am refusing the same paper. I am refusing it with the word on it and I would have refused it without the word, and anybody who wants to write down that I refused it because of the word can write down that I refused the paper."**

**"You are saying no."**

**"I am saying no to holding it permanently, in this holding, at the head of it, under anybody's seal including mine."**

**"And the rest of it."**

**"And I am not refusing the schedule on this table. I am not refusing the load, I am not refusing the beds, I am not refusing the five lines or the fourth hour or the fifth hour the sluice road put on line one in his own hand. All five lines stand exactly where they stood and I have not moved one of them today."**

He lifted the four stones off the schedule one at a time and set them down in a row along the edge of the table with the gaps between them, and the sheet did not move under the wind at all once they were off, and he put his palm flat on it anyway.

**"And I am not going to stand here and tell this yard that this is free, because it is not free and I know exactly what it costs, and you all know too, and nobody is going to be told a number on it by me this morning or any morning.** He looked at Auret Sill without saying her name. **"You put the best argument for taking it into this yard on Sunday morning, in this yard, in one piece, and about thirty of us heard it and nobody argued you out of it and nobody has said you were wrong. I am not arguing you out of it now either. It is on the table and I am not taking it off the table and it is going to stay on that table while I am standing here, and if this holding is still arguing with itself about that in a month then that is a thing it is going to have to do without me helping it."**

**"Then who holds it."**

**"Nobody. Not this morning, not this week, and not me."**

Nobody in that yard thanked him for it. Nobody in that yard told him he had been wrong either, and a woman from the third place said that a thing which cannot be free should at least be expensive, and a man from the middle row said that eleven houses had better be counted before anybody stops the counting, and neither of those was said to him and both of them were said out loud, and the two of them went on carrying barrows inside about half a minute.

**"One more thing and then I will get out of the way of this yard,"** he said. **"This is not the same as handing it over and I am not going to be allowed to pretend it is. There are replacement beds that have to take a real draw and a real flood load before anything at the top of that channel is touched at all, and none of them has passed anything yet, and I have not got a way of making one pass tonight. Until they do, there is a person at the head of nothing and I am the person who can still do the work, and everybody in this yard can see that and nobody in this yard needs me to say it."**

The barrow went out again at about the ninth hour and came back and stopped, and it did not go out a twelfth time, and the man pushing it stood with the shafts up for a while looking at the middle table and then went and got a hod.

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the register form under his arm and the door behind him shut and the key already back on its nail, and he gave the two stones without coming down the path.

**"Nine hundred and three at the near end, nine hundred and fifty at the top. Forty-seven of masonry, as always, and neither of those two is a round number and I am not going to say anything about it because there is nothing to say."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall at about the tenth hour with a cloth in his hand and wiped the chalk off afterwards.

**"Five hundred and eighteenth over the line, five hundred and sixteenth under it, and five hundred and seventeen at the head of it, in that order, because I have read them the other way for twenty years and I felt like doing it the other way.** He put the cloth down. **"And I have never once been asked whether I would go on doing this without the man who used to stand at that table, and nobody is going to ask me now."**

Tova Reed came out of the north door with the corner of the seed board under her arm and gave her four to a mason from the third place who had to come round the trays and stand square in front of her to be heard at all, and she wrote down what he said the first time and did not ask him again.

**"Four hundred and thirteen on the form. Three hundred and nineteen on the charter, which is the oldest of them and which nobody has ever read out. Four hundred and seven on the second of Silling's two ruled lines, and it is blank, and it is going to be blank tomorrow at four hundred and eight with nothing on it either. Two hundred and sixteen on the letter, and that is the highest that letter has ever stood at, and I have told you four times that the higher it goes the less it is worth."**

**"We heard you the first time."**

**"You heard me and you did not write it down,"** she said, **"and I am not going to keep saying it to a yard that has not written it down."**

Nobody walked in behind the four stones and picked up a pen. The sheet that came up the lane on Friday with the Chancellor's name at the head of it was still lying on the middle table where it has lain since it was put down, undated, unpicked, unfilled in at the foot, and the offer was still lying out past the gate on the low board where it has lain since before the flood, and there is no date on that one either, and the two of them lay about nine feet apart with four stones in a row along the edge of the table between them and nothing at all happened to either of them on the morning a man said no in front of the whole yard.

Nobody entered the one room that morning except to shut the drawer, and the key went back on its nail. Thirty-nine blanks were on the wall and the rule beneath them stood open at the thirty-ninth time and had done since the wall was drawn.

Nobody in that yard went near the ladder against the tool house wall, and behind it the bare ground took nobody's boot and nobody's tape and nobody's price.

There is a joint at the back of the tap house that puts one drop into a saucer every morning of this holding's record, and it did that on that Tuesday and the saucer kept it, and it has not proved anything about any morning of the week including that one.

Nobody fetched the man of about seventy on that Tuesday and nobody put a question to him at any hour of it, and the twenty-nine fetchings that stand against his name in this holding are the twenty-nine that were standing there yesterday, and will be the twenty-nine that are standing there tomorrow.

And at about the eleventh hour Auret Sill went back out past the gate with her slate under her arm and about nine people in that yard watched her go and about twenty-one went back to what they were doing, and the four stones were still standing in a row along the edge of the middle table at the dark with the wind at the gate end not having got under anything at all.

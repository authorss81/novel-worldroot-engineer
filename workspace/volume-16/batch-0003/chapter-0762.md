# Chapter 762

## The Fifteenth Of The Twenty-First, And Five Steps Written Out By Six People Who Did Not Ask Him

Six people wrote a sheet on the middle table between the sixth hour and the ninth and it was up on the boards with a stone on it before the light was properly on the wall, and Marek Vale was at the north end of the yard at the time and came across to it when the man who lays for three councils told him it was up, and he read it standing, twice, with his hands behind his back and nobody in that yard saying a word while he did it.

> **THE SEQUENCE, IN THE ORDER IT WILL BE DONE. WRITTEN OUT BY THE SIX PEOPLE WHO ARE GOING TO DO IT. UNDATED, AND NO NAME AGAINST THE TOP OF IT.**
>
> Nothing at the head of the channel is touched at all until the replacement beds have taken a real draw and a real flood load, and the results have been read out in a yard, and nobody starts early because it is a good morning for it.
>
> One. The upper gate closed, and it stays closed the whole time. Nobody opens it to hurry anything and nobody has a reason to open it.
> Who: Ismay Rooke. How long: until the last step is finished and not one minute longer.
>
> Two. The middle gate opened to the width of a hand and left at the width of a hand. Not more and not less. If it is more than a hand then close it a quarter and do not discuss it on the day.
> Who: Emrys Dole. He has done this eleven years and says so, and he is right.
>
> Three. The gauge read, and the reading said out loud before anything else whatever happens.
> Who: Nia Vale. She was given the gauge, she has kept it, and nobody is going to give a different instruction about it on the day of the thing itself.
>
> Four. The draw left standing for the length of a slow cup and no longer. If it is still standing at the end of that, go back to step two and do that again.
> Who: Perrin Dae, counting aloud, because he is the only one of the six of us who can be relied on to count out loud and say the same number twice.
>
> Five. The gauge read a second time and both readings said out loud with nothing added to either of them.
> Who: Nia Vale.
>
> Six. The middle gate left at the width of a hand for the night. Not shut and not open.
> Who: Emrys Dole.
>
> Seven. The upper gate open again and left open.
> Who: Ismay Rooke.
>
> Written by six of us on the middle table on Monday morning. Nobody has signed this. Nobody is going to sign it.

He read it a second time and then stood back from it the width of a hand, which is how he stands from a thing other people have written.

**"This is not mine."**

**"It was never going to be yours,"** Perrin Dae said. **"You are not on it and you are not to be on it and if your name goes on that sheet in the next three days then one of the six of us has done it wrong and we will take it off."**

**"Then I am not going to say anything about it."**

**"You can say what you like, we will not stop you."**

**"I am not going to say anything about it, and I want that written down as a thing I chose and not as a thing I was pushed into.** He looked at the six names in the six places they had put themselves. **"If one of those steps is wrong then the six of you will find out on the day, in front of a yard, with water in it, and that is the correct place for it to be found out. I am not going to stand here this morning and go through it with you and make it a sheet I have had a hand in, because the day this holding is going to need is not a day when one man in it has been in the room for all of it."**

The man from the middle row said the weight out loud in the yard at about the ninth hour and said it wrong, and he was not a fool and he had read it off the board two hours earlier.

**"One thousand four hundred hundredweight came down the channel in the night."**

Perrin Dae put his hand on the barrow shaft and corrected it in one line and without any particular voice.

**"It came down five hundredweight, so it is one thousand and three hundred and ninety-five. It came down five this morning and it comes down five on every morning with an odd number on it, and it stood at one thousand four hundred yesterday morning, which is a figure I would not have believed on any morning last spring.** He took his hand off the shaft. **"That is all of it. You had yesterday's. It happens to all of us about four mornings a season."**

Nobody said he was sorry to him and nobody said he ought to know better and nobody laughed, and the man from the middle row said that he would take the odd-morning rule as given and that he had a use for it, and went and did something else.

Nobody said one word about the board under the frame all morning, and at the seventh hour the girl from the second place went over and put two hundred and nine above the rule on it and four hundred and forty-nine under it, and there was nobody at that trough and there was not going to be.

Harlan Vetch gave the window from the wall and said the direction this time, which he has not done since the frost week.

**"Two hundred and forty-one moved this morning and two hundred and fifty-one did not, and the pair of them is four hundred and ninety-two of window. I have said the direction out loud twice now and both times it has been the short half and both times it has been right, and I am going to keep saying it until it is right without me.** He went up two more courses. **"I still do not know what a window is for and I have stopped saying that in case somebody puts it in a book."**

Roan Selk came up the low road in the dark and was at the gate before the light and stood at the boards and read the two stones off them himself without asking anybody to hold anything up, and wrote the far one on the back of his own hand with a nail.

**"Nine hundred and nine is the near stone and nine hundred and fifty-six is the one at the top, and I wrote the top one on the back of my own hand with a nail because I would not ask a man to hold a board up for me twice in one season. And I am not going down that road today and nobody from this yard is going down it today, and I have not come up here to be asked to agree with anything.**

**"There is a sheet on your boards with six names on it."**

**"There is a sheet on your boards with six names on it and no seventh, and I have read it standing up and I have understood about a third of it, and the third I have understood is the width of a hand and a slow cup.** He looked at the middle table. **"I am going to say one thing and then I am going to stand at the wall. If those six of them are not six people who will keep doing it after this holding stops being interesting to a Chancellor, then that sheet is a piece of paper and the nine of us at the top of the fourth path will have been right about you on Monday."**

Nobody asked him which of the nine he meant and he did not say.

Soren Rill read the long sheet at about the tenth hour.

**"Under it, five hundred and twenty-second. Over it, five hundred and twenty-fourth. At the head of it, five hundred and twenty-three."**

Nia Vale gave her two at the rack in her own order on her way out to the road.

**"One thousand and three hundred and ninety-five hundredweight. Down five, up nothing, and the five hundred and twenty-fifth morning of it.** She did not stop at the boards. **"And I am going to be four hours up that road before anybody at this gate is dressed. I have two places left on the load and I am looking for one man with a cart and I am not asking at the middle table."**

Kellan Rusk came out to the step with the form and the key already back on its nail.

**"Four hundred and nineteen. Not entered.** He looked at the six names on the sheet. **"And I have read that sheet and there is no line on it about the drawer and the key on the nail, and I want it known that I noticed that and that I am not going to add it, because if it is my drawer then it is mine and not a step in somebody else's order."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the seed house step and turned the corner of the board out for anybody who wanted to see the route on it and turned it back when nobody did.

**"The charter is three hundred and twenty-five. The ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and thirteen and has nothing on it and will have nothing on it at four hundred and fourteen tomorrow. The letter has reached two hundred and twenty-two and I would rather it had not."**

Nobody went out past that gate on that Monday, and the barrow was not brought out of that yard at all and stood wheel first in the middle of it with the shafts up, and about four people looked at it and said nothing about it.

In the one room the blanks stand at thirty-nine with the second rule under them open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the drawer was shut at every hour with the key on its nail.

The ladder stayed against the tool house wall and nobody put a foot on it.

And at about the eleventh hour that Monday the sheet with the six names on it was still up on the boards with a stone on the top corner, and nobody had put a seventh name on it, and nobody in this holding had asked the man who would be asked to approve it whether it was right, and he had not gone up to the head of the channel to see whether the six of them had it right.

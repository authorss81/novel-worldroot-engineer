# Chapter 765

## The Eighteenth Of The Twenty-First, And Names Written Down By Four Districts That Have Not Seen Him

Four sheets came into this holding on that Thursday and none of them came here to be signed, and the man who would have signed them was not in any of the four yards when they were written and was not asked about any of them and did not know until about the seventh hour that they existed.

> **THE HOLDERS, NAMED BY THE DISTRICTS THAT WILL KEEP THEM. FOUR SHEETS, ONE FOR EACH DISTRICT, UNDATED. NONE OF THEM CAME HERE TO BE SIGNED.**
>
> **Fourth place.** Nine houses. Named at a meeting of the nine houses in a room with the door shut. Four names for four things, and one line ruled under them for the head of the channel at the top of their branch, and no name against that line because there was nobody at that meeting who would take it.
>
> **Third place.** Six houses. Named at a meeting of the six houses. Three names, and one of them is written over another name because that one has gone four miles, and the reason is written underneath in the hand of whoever wrote the new one.
>
> **The middle row.** Twenty-two houses. Named at a meeting of the twenty-two. Five names, and one house with nothing against it and the reason written under that, which is that there is nobody living in it now and nobody is going to be asked to be the holder of a house they do not live in.
>
> **The four at the end of the second branch.** Four houses. Named in the four kitchens and not in a meeting, and one of the four would not be named and would not say why, and the other three wrote down what she would not say themselves without being asked to and were not asked to.
>
> On none of the four sheets is the name of the man at the head of the channel. On none of the four sheets has anybody put another household's name down for them. Nobody in this holding has been asked which of these four names they would rather have had on it, and nobody is going to be, and the four of them that could read have not been asked to read these to anybody.

Sera Quill had the slate out before the seventh hour and gave the count, and gave one slate and not two slates.

**"A hundred and seventy-one in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty-two not."**

**"That is one on it."**

**"Up one. And the other one is mine and it is not on this morning and it has not been on any morning this one has been on, and I have said that out loud to this yard four times this week and I am not going to say it a fifth time to a yard that has stopped asking.** She read the one that goes in the book and turned the slate over afterwards so the second face showed nothing at all. **"One hundred and fourteenth."**

**"You have not asked us what it is."**

**"I have not asked you what it is and I am not going to, and you have all had the opportunity for five mornings and the number of you who took it up was none."**

Harlan Vetch gave the window from the wall with the sheet of terms in four yards still going round the districts below him.

**"Two hundred and fifty-three moved and two hundred and forty-two did not, four hundred and ninety-five between them, and that is four mornings running that the tall half has come and I am going to say this once and then never again on this subject.** He waited for about two seconds and nobody took it up. **"It stays with the chalk."**

Hanne Brack read the two stones off the boards on her way through with a barrow harness over her shoulder and did not stop.

**"Nine hundred and twelve at the near end and nine hundred and fifty-nine at the top."**

**"Twelve."**

**"Twelve at the near end and it is a number and not a birthday, and I am not going to be asked what I make of it and neither is he.** She got the harness up onto her shoulder. **"And there are five names on my sheet and there were five names on it yesterday and the same five, and I have not read any of the other three and I am not going to."**

Perrin Dae came down the middle road with the cart and gave his two to the boards on his way past, out of habit, in the order he has used since the frost, and then said the thing he had come down for, which was that a mason from the third place had changed a line on the schedule that morning in his own hand and initialled it in his own hand and had not asked anybody.

**"One thousand and four hundred and six hundredweight, up eight on a morning with an even number on it, and the book says five hundred and twenty-eighth for today, and the man who pushes the barrow has it right off the board this morning, which is a thing that has happened twice all season and both of them were this week, and I am not going to say anything further about it."**

**"The third line now says the fourth hour and it said the third hour when that sheet went on that table, and it has been changed by a man from the third place at about half past six this morning with a pencil and he has put his own initials at the end of it, and I am telling you after the fact because I was not there before it happened and neither was anybody from this table.** He looked at the sheet under the four stones. **"That is the last thing that is going to change on that paper in this holding, and it was not changed by the man at it, and he has not signed it and he was not asked whether it was right."**

**"Is that allowed."**

**"It is allowed because the paper is a list of work and the work belongs to the four of them and not to him, and if a man from the third place wants the third line at the fourth hour then that is a thing the third place does and not a thing this yard permits.** Perrin Dae got his cart moving again. **"And if the man at the middle table wanted it at the third hour he would have had to come and ask the third place, and he did not come, and I am the one telling you he did not come."**

Nobody in that yard said that the last change on that sheet ought to have been made by somebody else, and nobody said it was right, and about four people went and read the initial at the end of the third line and went away again.

The clerk from up a lane read the long sheet off the wall and read it correctly the first time and made a point of saying that he had.

**"Five hundred and twenty-fifth underneath, five hundred and twenty-seventh on top, and five hundred and twenty-sixth at the head of it. First time in four days and I am not going to be humble about it."**

Kellan Rusk came out with the form under his arm and the key on its nail and looked at the four sheets lying along the edge of the middle table for about as long as it takes to read a sheet, and did not touch any of them.

**"Four hundred and twenty-two.** He shut the door. **"And those four are not on that form and they are not going on that form, and that form is this holding's and not theirs, and I would like whoever is minded to go and find the next piece of paper to put them on to go and find it themselves, because I have been asked three times this month where those go and I have answered three times and the answers have not been the same three times."**

Tova Reed gave her three from the step with her back to the trays.

**"Three hundred and twenty-eight on the charter. The ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and sixteen and empty, and it is going to be four hundred and seventeen tomorrow, and if anybody in this yard wants to write a name on that line instead of a figure they may come and get my pencil off me and do it themselves.** She turned round. **"The letter is two hundred and twenty-five, which is the highest it has ever stood at, and it is not a thing to be proud of and I have told this yard five times now."**

Nobody wrote a name on the second of Silling's two ruled lines and nothing has ever been written on it and a round hundred on that line is not a mark and not an opportunity and will not be one on any morning of this week.

On the wall of the one room there are thirty-nine blanks, and the rule below them has been open and empty at the thirty-ninth time since the day it was drawn and nothing has ever gone under it. Four sheets are on the long table. None of the four has been entered. None has been refused. And under none of them has a column been cut.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped, and the ladder stayed against the tool house wall with nobody's boot on it, and the ring of bare ground behind that wall took nobody's tape and nobody's offer.

The door nine hundred yards off was walked past twice and read on by nobody and offered to nobody.

And at about the eleventh hour the four sheets were still lying along the edge of the middle table in the order the four districts had sent them in, and no name of his was on any of them, and no yard had asked anybody in this holding what they would do on a morning when somebody asked them to, and the third line of a five-line schedule carried the initials of a mason from the third place and a date-less sheet of work under four stones had been altered for the last time by somebody who was not him.

## 4. THE SEVEN SPELLINGS, PRINTED ONCE HERE, AND THE EIGHTH FORM

**Seven, and you must be able to count them.**

1. **The hundred range.** One hundred to one hundred and ninety-nine is *a hundred and* the word. **The read-aloud numerator in its range is *one hundred and* the word and is a different figure with a different form.** An ordinal in the one-hundreds takes *one hundred and*, because *a hundred and* has no room for an ending, and at exactly one hundred the ending goes on the whole word, which gives *one hundredth*. **THE TWO RECKONINGS TAKE *ONE HUNDRED AND* IN THEIR ONE-HUNDREDS, AND ON YOUR TENTH MORNING THE FIRST RECKONING IS ONE HUNDRED AND FOURTEENTH, WHICH IS THE FORM THE FIFTEEN VOLUMES BEHIND USE AND NOT THE ORDINAL FORM.**
2. **The hundreds and above to nine hundred and ninety-nine** is the word, hundred, and the word, bare at a round hundred.
3. **The thousands.** One thousand and above keeps **both** ands: *one thousand and three hundred and eighty-eight.* The unit word is a separate word with a space before it and no hyphen: *one thousand and three hundred and eighty-eight hundredweight.* The attached form is withdrawn by name and returns zero.
4. **Tens are hyphenated and round tens are not.** *Twenty-nine.* *Ninety.* *Three hundred and forty.*
5. **Ordinals take the ending on the whole word at a round hundred and hyphenate a tens-ordinal.** *Five hundredth.* *Four hundredth.* *Five hundred and ninth.* *Five hundred and seventeenth.* **A writer who writes *five hundred and first-th* has written a figure nobody in this holding uses.**
6. **No month name in any morning and no month number in any spoken line.** The modal verb is not a month. An ordinal date appears only inside a heading that names an ordinal.
7. **The split window half and the read-aloud numerator are two figures and are never added and never confused.**

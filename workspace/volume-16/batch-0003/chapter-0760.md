# Chapter 760

## The Thirteenth Of The Twenty-First, And A Table Being Filled In With What Failed While The Light Was Still On It

The table for reading out was begun at about the sixth hour and it was the whole morning's work, and it was begun before the light was properly on the wall because there was only going to be one Saturday between this morning and the morning it gets read.

Soren Rill made a rule under the first line with a straightedge and then stopped with the chalk about two inches short of the end of the paper and looked at the two unlined inches for a while and put the chalk down and picked it up again.

**"I am not ruling a column under this,"** he said. **"I have got four sheets on a table in that room with nothing under them and I am not cutting a fifth place on this holding's paper for a table of results. If somebody wants a column they can come and hold the other end of the straightedge."**

Nobody came and held the other end, and the two unlined inches at the foot of the page were left exactly as they were, and it went up on the boards with a stone on each top corner.

> **THE RESULTS, AS THEY WILL BE READ. PREPARED IN ADVANCE, AND EVERY FAILURE IS IN IT ALREADY.**
>
> The ring is nine beds. They were dug in one season. They are not being dug again this season and none of them is being priced.
>
> One, at the top of the ring. Gone with the root on it. Not cut, not moved, not dug again, and no figure against it.
>
> Two, three, five and six. Held the dry-season draw clean and gave the load up at the near end of themselves. No water lost at the far end.
>
> Four. Held the dry-season draw and let a third of the flood load out at the far end of itself, and the third is a third of what was put in and not a third of what came.
>
> Seven. Would take the dry-season draw and would not take the flood load at all, and it took nine hours to find that out.
>
> Eight. Held both.
>
> Nine, at the north end. Took the draw clean and let it out at the far end of itself, and it is soft at the north end on the morning this was written, and it was soft when the draw went in and it is soft now.
>
> Six of them held the draw. Five of them held the load. Those two counts are of two different things and are not to be added together and no total of them is to be said out loud in a yard.
>
> The soft bed at the north end is not to be dug again and is not to be priced. What is known about it is written in the crew's own book and is not to be copied anywhere else.
>
> No row has been added to this table for the hand-over and no row will be added on the morning it is read. What is not in this table today is not going to be put into it by anybody with a pencil on the day.

The girl from the second place did the north board at the seventh hour on her own, two hundred and eight over the rule and four hundred and forty-seven beneath it, and put the chalk away without looking at either of them.

Nia Vale came in from the road at about the eighth hour with her sleeves going white at the elbows and gave her two at the rack before she had her coat off.

**"Down five, and nobody in this yard needs me to say it before they have said it themselves. One thousand and three hundred and ninety-two hundredweight, on the five hundred and twenty-third morning of a run that has never once asked anybody's permission.** She put the coat over the back of the rack. **"And the four houses at the end of the second branch have their load and I told the woman in the second of them first, standing at her door, before the cart turned in, which is the only way that is done now and it is not going to be a thing I get thanked for."**

**"What did she say."**

**"She said she would have the seed under the bed by dark and she has said that to me for four days running and I have believed her four times.** Nia Vale took her coat off the rack again. **"That is not a figure and it is not going on any table."**

Harlan Vetch gave the window off the wall without coming down and this time he came down about halfway, which he had not done since the frost.

**"Four hundred and ninety is what the two of them come to. The short half moved, two hundred and forty, and the tall half stood at two hundred and fifty, and I am going to start saying which one I mean by naming the one I can see the front of, because four people in this yard have written it down both ways this week and one of those four is me."**

**"Which one can you see the front of."**

**"Not saying. Ask me in a fortnight when I have decided it is worth being right in public."**

The man who lays for three councils read the two stones off the wall, wrote the near one on his own hand with a nail as he has done twice this season, and carried on down the road.

**"The near end has gone to nine hundred and seven and the top has gone to nine hundred and fifty-four, and the eleventh course at the near end is laid as of this morning and I laid it to the course under it and not to a figure, and I would like the next man to lay it the same way and I do not expect he will."**

Soren Rill read the long sheet off his own wall and did not wipe it this time.

**"It goes over the line at five hundred and twenty-second and it sits at five hundred and twenty-one at the head and it is five hundred and twentieth underneath, and that is the wrong order for that wall and I have decided to read it the wrong way for a morning."** He looked at the table on the boards for a while with the cloth still in his hand. **"And that table is the first thing anybody in this yard has written this season with a hole in it on purpose, and I have been the man who reads the numbers off a wall for twenty years and I want it said that I read those failures off your own hands without being asked and that I would have read them off your hands anyway."**

Kellan Rusk gave the form from the step and then, after a moment, gave it again with a sentence on it, which is the first time in three weeks he has said the same figure out loud twice.

**"Four hundred and seventeen. Still not entered. Still the key on the nail.** He shut the door behind him. **"And the drawer was shut at the sixth hour and it is shut now and it will be shut at the dark and it has been shut at every hour of every morning of this holding's record and I am not going to be asked about it and I am not going to volunteer it again."**

Tova Reed gave her four from the step of the seed house in the order she has given them for nineteen days and wrote each one on the corner as she said it.

**"Four hundred and seventeen on the form, which is the same figure the man at the door has just said out loud, and it is two different figures that happen to be the same, and I watched two people in this yard nearly try to add them together this morning and I would like it written down somewhere that they are not to be added. After that, three hundred and twenty-three on the charter, and four hundred and eleven on the ruled line under Silling's second one with nothing on it, and two hundred and twenty on the letter, which is the highest that letter has ever stood at and which I have said four times and am going to keep saying until one of you writes it down."**

Nobody in that yard wrote it down.

A return came and went that Saturday and it moved nothing, as a return has not moved anything in this holding for four years, and no ordinal and no weight and no allowance of any kind was printed anywhere for it, and nobody asked.

In the one room the blanks on the wall stand at thirty-nine with the second rule under them standing open and empty at the thirty-ninth time, and the girl from the second place walked past them twice on her way out and looked at neither.

Nobody went near the ladder against the tool house wall and the ring of bare ground behind it took nobody's boot and nobody's tape and nobody's price on that Saturday either.

And at about the eleventh hour the table on the boards had two unlined inches at the foot of it and nine rows on it and one of those rows said soft at the north end in plain words with nothing hidden under it, and about twenty-one people in this yard had read all nine rows that morning, and it is going to be read out in a yard in a fortnight and a morning and every one of those twenty-one people is going to hear their own reading of it again from somebody else's mouth.

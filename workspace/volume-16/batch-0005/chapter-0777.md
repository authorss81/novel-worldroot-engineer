# Chapter 777

## The Thirtieth Of The Twenty-First, And Four Bolts Going Across The Mouth Of A Channel Above The Reach Where The Whole Yard Can See Them Going On, And Three Names Read Out Afterwards And None Of The Three Given A Word For

The work started at about half past four in the morning and there is nobody in this holding who was awake to see it start, which is the way it was arranged and not an accident.

Three of the crew from the ring and the man who lays for three councils went up to the head of the channel with a plate of iron and four bolts and a bar, and they did it from the bank on the far side, above the reach, at the place where the channel comes out of the ground and turns down toward this yard. From the wall at the gate end you can see the place. From the middle of the yard you can see the plate. What you cannot see from this yard is anything past the plate, because there is nothing past the plate, and no one went into the head of that channel and nobody opened it and nobody has ever been in it.

They came down about the seventh hour. The man who lays had grit on both arms and did not stop at the table.

**"It is set. It is done. It is staying where it is.** He said it to the four people nearest him and not louder than that. **"Four bolts, one plate, and nothing is cut and nothing is joined and the water came down past me the whole time I was on that bank. If you want to see it you can walk up the middle road to the top of the rise and look at it and you do not need me and you do not need anybody.** He put the bar down inside the wall. **"And nobody wrote anything down while I was up there and nobody is going to tell you afterwards that it was a day. It was a Tuesday and there was iron on it and that is the whole of what happened at that head this morning."**

About nine people went up the middle road to the top of the rise over the next two hours and looked at a plate and came back down again.

Ismay Rooke had been sitting on the bench at the gate since before the light with his hands on his knees, and he had not moved for the two hours, and at about the eighth hour a man from the middle row came and stood in front of him and asked him whether the cap meant there was somebody in charge of it now.

**"No,"** Ismay Rooke said. He did not get up off the bench. **"I have been on that bench since the light and I have watched four people come off that bank and I have watched nine of you go up and look at it, and I have not seen a name go on anything, and I would have heard of it if one had.** He turned his head and looked at the middle gate standing at the width of a hand with nobody's hand on it. **"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off.** He said it to a man standing in front of him and he did not raise his voice and he did not say it a second time. **"I sat on a bench at this gate for eight days in the frost and was not let up onto the sill, and I have said things out loud in this yard twice since and both times I was right, and I am not going to say anything else about that head of channel today. That plate is iron and it is a boundary. Go and look at it and draw whatever you want to draw and it will not come out as one line."**

Nobody said whether it was on. Nobody had said it on any morning and nobody said it on this one, and the man who lays for three councils told two different men in the yard that he could not tell them and would not be asked twice.

The flows were read out in the yard at about the ninth hour in daylight, off the boards, by the clerk from up a lane, and about forty people stood and heard it. It is three entries. It is written as three entries. There is no rule on those boards for joining them into one and there is not going to be one, and he read them in the order they were chalked and stopped between each of them long enough for the yard to look at the space.

On the first one, about nine people made a sound and one woman at the front put her hand over her mouth.

**"The middle row and the north end of the ring, and both of those came back on,** at about two thirds of what the gauge at the gate end carried before the plate went on, and at that figure at about the sixth hour and again at the seventh and again at the eighth, and the three readings agree with one another."

A man from the middle row asked whether about two thirds was two thirds of what, and the clerk told him it was two thirds of the gauge at the gate end and not two thirds of anything else, and the man said that was the first time anybody had ever told him what the two thirds was two thirds of, and about four people behind him laughed and nobody else did.

On the second one nobody made a sound at all, and a man from the fourth place said the word light out loud as though he were tasting it, and his wife put a hand on his sleeve and took it off again.

**"The four at the end of the second branch, and the two houses at the top of the fourth road, and both of those stayed light.** They did not come back and they did not go away. Both of them have been on a short measure since the tenth morning of this holding's counting and both of them are on a short measure now, and there is no figure written against either one this morning and there has not been one since the short measure was ruled at the foot of a wall."

The clerk read the third one twice, because there were people at the back who had not heard it the first time, and nobody said anything about his having read it twice.

**"The second place. Nothing came back and nothing went away.** That branch has been dry since before the flood. It is not on the plate and it is not on the list of things the plate did anything to, and the place is on a board at the end of this wall with nothing against it."

A man from the middle row asked whether the three entries could be added together into one number, in front of about forty people, and the clerk said no, and said why, and it was not a long answer.

Auret Sill came in from the pumps at about the ninth hour and a half with her sleeves wet and stood at the back and listened to all of it, and she did not sit down and she did not go forward.

**"That second entry is the same figure on it that it had on it a fortnight ago and the fortnight before that, and it is going to be the same figure next week.** She said it to nobody and not to the clerk. **"I have four thousand one hundred and one cans filled by hand on my own sheet since the spring and I add to that sheet every week and nobody has ever asked to see it, and I am not going to put it up on that wall next to an entry that has not moved.** She wiped her hands down her front. **"There is iron at the top of a rise and I have walked up there and looked at it with four other people this morning and it is a plate, and I am not going to be moved off my sheet by a plate, and I am not going to let anybody tell me it is a good morning."**

The one who stayed independent on purpose said it herself, from about nine feet back, in her own mouth, and nobody had asked her to be in the yard.

She was a woman of about fifty with two children at the end of a lane that goes off the second place's road and has been dry since the spring. **"I have been carrying my own water since before any of this and I have not asked this holding for a drop and I am not going to.** She put her hands in front of her and did not hold them out. **"You can put that plate on and you can read that list out twice and I am not being put back on that branch and I did not ask to be taken off it, and the two of those are the same thing today and one of them is not going to be undone at the fourth hour to make somebody's morning tidier.** Nobody argued with her about it and about four people looked at her and then at the boards. **"And I would like whoever writes that list told that I am on it as a thing I am doing and not as a thing that happened to me."**

Kellan Rusk gave three figures from the doorway of the one room without going out to the wall and without looking at the wall.

**"Five hundred and thirty-eighth at the head of the long sheet, the five hundred and thirty-seventh under the rule and the five hundred and thirty-ninth above it.** He said them the way a man says a thing he has been carrying in his head for four years and not the way a man reads a board. **"I have not been near that wall this morning and I have been in this holding four years and that wall is six inches of chalk on three hundred yards of stone and any fool can change it before the dark, and those three numbers are the only ones in this county I would still say out loud with the wall behind my back.** He shut the door and did not open it again for two hours.

Emrys Dole had his hand on the wheel at the middle gate all morning, as he has had every morning since the frost, and he read two figures to a man who came about eleven feet off and wanted them for his own book.

**"Five hundred and seven between them and it is up one on the pair.** He did not take his hand off the wheel. **"The tall half is the one that came this morning and it is two hundred and fifty-nine, and the one that stood is two hundred and forty-eight and it stood there all day yesterday and it will stand there again this afternoon. You can write it down. If you write it down, write down which one of the two came, because that is the only part of it that is any use to anybody and I have watched four men in this yard get that wrong.** He turned the wheel a quarter and left it. **"And I have been at this gate since before the light and the gate is at the width of a hand and there is nobody here but me and I did not get asked to be here today and I would not have come if I had."**

The woman with a barrow that is not the yard's came through the gate at about the seventh hour and gave the weight and the morning of the run on her way in, in the order she has given them in for a fortnight, and then gave them again in the other order because a man she passed said something.

**"In the night that channel carried one thousand and four hundred and twenty-four hundredweight, which puts the run at its five hundred and fortieth morning and is up eight on the one behind it.** She shouldered the barrow. **"I have said it to you four times in five weeks and every one of those times I said it and then said the other half after it about the second place, and I am not going to stop doing that because it is inconvenient to anybody at this gate.** She set the barrow down. **"And I said last week I would be at my own door at the fifth hour if anybody wanted to ask me about that, and nobody asked me, and I am telling you I am at the fifth hour anyway and that is not an invitation.** She took the barrow up again and went through.

Hanne Brack came up from the second place at the ninth hour and gave the two stones off the boards in the order she has given them every morning since the middle of the summer, which is not the order they were built in.

**"Nine hundred and twenty-four and then nine hundred and seventy-one, and I have known for twenty-five days which of those is the near one.** She put the chalk down. **"I am sixty-one and I am not going to learn a new order in the last week of a season, and if a man from this yard wants the other order he can walk round the wall and read it off the end of it, and there are about four people who could do that and none of them ever has."**

Sera Quill came out with the slate under her arm at about the tenth hour, into a yard with most of the forty still in it, and stood with her back to the boards so that nobody could read the slate while she was talking.

**"A hundred and seventy-four in force. Twenty-nine taken and a hundred and forty-five not.** She said it in the voice she uses for it, which is the voice she uses for the weather. **"That is the highest figure this count has stood at since I started keeping it and it went up one on this morning and not on any of the others this month, and I have been asked twice today by men from this yard why it went up on a morning like this one, and I am not going to tell them, because I do not know, and the count has never once in four years moved for a reason that was sitting in this yard.** She turned the slate over and it showed nothing at all, then she turned it back and read one figure off the front of it. **"One hundred and seventeenth goes in the book. There is a second figure I carry that sits thirteen under it and it does not get said out on a morning when that one does, and in four years there has not been one morning that got both of them out of me.** She put the slate in her apron. **"And I will say one more thing about it because somebody in this yard will work it out on their own and I would rather it came from me. That count would not tell you whether that plate is on. It would not have moved for it either way, and there is nothing on the front of this slate except a figure, and anybody who would like their name against it can come and look at it at any hour and find there is nothing there."**

Nobody came and looked.

Tova Reed came out from the store with the tray going and gave her four from the step in an order that has still only been used once.

**"Two hundred and thirty-seven, four hundred and twenty-eight, three hundred and forty and four hundred and thirty-four, and I am not going to tell you which is which because you have all got used to me telling you and I am going to write them on the corner instead.** She did write them on the corner, one at a time, and four people who were standing close enough read them after she had finished rather than before. **"The letter, the ruled line under Silling's second one, the charter and the form. The line under Silling's second one is blank and it is going to be blank tomorrow when it is four hundred and twenty-nine, and I have said that out loud so many times in this yard that four people here can say it without me and I am glad of it and I am not going to say it again in this yard unless somebody asks me a question about it.** She picked up the corner of the board. **"And there is a plate on the channel at the top of that rise and I have walked up that road twice this morning to look at it with four other people, and I am not going to tell anybody what it means for a place with no water at it, because I do not know, and I have said all morning that I do not know, and it is a Tuesday and the seed goes out at the fourth hour regardless."**

Nobody thanked anybody on that Tuesday.

The plate stayed on, and nobody was put over it, and nobody asked to be, and no person in this holding or four days north has written one word about it on anything. Nobody opened the drawer behind the near board at any hour of that day and the key was on the nail whenever anybody looked, and about four people went past it and looked.

And at about the eleventh hour there were about forty people in that yard who had seen four bolts go across the top of a channel, and not one of them could draw an unbroken line out of it, and neither of the two people who had said out loud in that yard four days before that they were holding the ends of it had put a hand to any of it, and one of them was standing at the back of that yard with her sleeves wet, and the plate on the top of that rise had four bolts in it and nothing else, and about nine people up the middle road were still walking up to look at it at the eleventh hour because nothing had told them not to.

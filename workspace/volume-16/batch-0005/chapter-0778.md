# Chapter 778

## The First Morning Of A New Calendar, And A Pump At The Second House On The Lane That Has To Be Primed By Hand Before Anything Else On It Will Move, And A Mason At The Wall Reading Two Figures Off It For The First Time In Four Years

The calendar turned in the night and nothing in this holding turned with it.

The slate at the gate post had been wrong since the middle of the night and was right at the sixth hour this morning, the same as it was wrong and right the last time that slate turned, and about eleven people stood and read it, and a woman from the four houses at the end of the second branch said out loud that her bread does not change because a slate changed and went to do her own work.

The frame over the north board did not move in the night and did not move when the slate changed. The run in the channel did not restart. No figure on any wall in this yard went back one and started again from somewhere else, and there is nobody in this yard who would have known how to do that if it had.

The pump at the second house on the mill lane had to be primed by hand at about the fifth hour before anything else on it would move, and it was primed by the boy who carries water on his way past with two buckets, which he has done about nine times since the frost and has never once been asked to do and has never once mentioned it.

Harlan Vetch came down off the wall at about the seventh hour to get a chisel and stopped in front of the north board with his back to it, and then turned round and read it.

**"Five hundred and eight of window.** He said it slowly, the way a man reads a thing he has walked past four times a day for four years. **"And the two halves of it are two hundred and fifty-nine and two hundred and forty-nine. Two hundred and forty-nine came this morning and two hundred and fifty-nine stood, and the short one comes on a morning with an odd number on it.** He put the chisel in his belt. **"I laid that wall four feet from the long table in the spring of the second year I was in this holding and nobody has ever once asked me to read what is on it, and I have never once read it out, and I have walked past it every working day of four years and I did not know until this morning which half of it moved when.** He looked at the wall behind him. **"I am not making anything of that and I would like nobody to make anything of it either. I am a man who builds things and I am telling four people what is chalked on one because four people are standing here and one of them asked me twice this week whether I knew what was on my own wall."**

About four people did not know what to do with that and went and did something else.

The girl from the second place came up the lane at about half past seven with a stub of chalk tied on a string round her wrist and a board under her arm about the length of her forearm, and she stood in front of the long sheet and wrote three figures on it and then turned the board over so that the writing was against her coat and put it behind her back out of sight.

**"Five hundred and thirty-ninth, five hundred and thirty-eighth, five hundred and fortieth.** She said them under her breath and then again so that a woman at the boards could hear. **"That is off my own board and I do not read it off that wall any more because I have got it on my own board now and I put it up myself, and there is no date at the top of it and I am not going to put one.** She shifted the board under her arm. **"I am the second place's own and I have been up that lane since the spring, and I am going to say one thing and then I am going to do my morning, which is that the place I come from has no water at it this morning either and it is not going to have any at the dark, and I have stopped saying that in a yard because I said it eleven times and it did not come back and me saying it did not bring it back, and now I only say it to people who have to carry something.** She went and started on it.

Odile Vray wrote two figures on the boards at the seventh hour and came down off the ladder and stood square in front of them with her hands behind her back.

**"Both of them are on that wall in the order they go up in. The one you can reach from the gate reads nine hundred and twenty-five and the one at the head of the walk reads nine hundred and seventy-two, and if you have never once counted the masonry between them then you have never once looked at either of them.** She put the chalk down. **"I have left both orders on that wall twice now and the second time was a fortnight ago and about nine people read it and about nine did not, and I have decided that a wall with two orders on it is a wall that nobody can be wrong about and a wall with one order on it is a wall that four people can be wrong about, and I would rather have the first one.** She wiped her hands on her apron. **"And I am not putting my name at the top of that wall and I never have, and if you want to know who wrote it, it is the same woman who has written it every day since the flood, and that is not an achievement, it is a job."**

The crew man with his own book came down off the ring about the eighth hour and gave two figures to the middle of the yard in the voice of a man reporting what his own crew had done and not what a board said.

**"The book has one thousand and four hundred and nineteen hundredweight entered against the night, and it has put that at the five hundred and forty-first morning of the run, and it is down five.** He shut the book and did not open it again. **"I am not reading it off the boards because I have not been to the boards this morning and I do not go to the boards on my own time, and if it is wrong then it is wrong and I will say so this afternoon.** He put the book under his arm the right way round this time. **"There is one page in that book that gets kept and there is one thing on that channel that does not, and I have got the two of them straight and I will show anybody either one at any hour of this week."**

> **THE PAGE THAT GETS KEPT. HIS OWN HAND, HIS OWN BOOK, AND IT IS NOT ON ANY WALL AND IT IS NOT COPIED ANYWHERE.**
>
> **Hours on the ring: two crews, one at the top and one at the north end, from the fourth hour to the eighth.**
>
> **Found at the north end this morning: soft. As it was yesterday. As it was the day before. Not dug, not cut at, not priced.**
>
> **Not touched: the bed at the top of the ring, and the eight feet of channel above the reach.**
>
> **Water in the channel when the crews started, and water in the channel when the crews stopped. The two are not written as a pair and there is no line between them.**
>
> **Nothing is entered anywhere in this holding. The book goes in a box under the seed benches at the dark and it comes out again in the morning, and that is the whole of the keeping of it.**

Nobody asked to see either one then.

Sera Quill was not at the boards on that morning and four or five people went to look for her and she was at the pumps at the second house with her sleeves rolled up, priming a pump by hand on somebody else's morning because the boy had gone down the sluice road with two buckets and come back with a broken handle on one of them.

Tova Reed came out from the store at about the ninth hour with the tray going and the north door open behind her, and gave her four off the corner of her board in the order she has used for a fortnight, without writing them down and without counting them on her fingers.

**"Four hundred and thirty-five. Three hundred and forty-one. Four hundred and twenty-nine. Two hundred and thirty-eight.** She said them and then stopped and then said the rest. **"That is the form, the charter, the ruled line under Silling's second one and the letter, and that is the order I have been saying them in for eleven days and I am not going to change it today because the calendar changed, because the order does not have anything to do with the calendar.** She held the corner of the board flat. **"That line under Silling's second one is blank. It was blank when the last month went out and it is blank now and it will be blank at four hundred and thirty tomorrow. Nobody is to come and ask me what it would take to put a mark on it, because I have answered it twice since the days turned and the answer has not changed and I am not going to say it a third time in a yard.** She looked at the far end of the wall. **"And the second place is on that board at the end of this wall with nothing against it and it is the first morning of a new calendar and it is on there with nothing against it, and that is what a calendar does."**

By the time the light was properly on the wall the girl from the second place had turned her own board face out again, which put the three figures she had written that morning back in view along with the two, and both sets of them were in the same corner where about four people going past could see them if they turned their heads, and nobody turned their head, and the two figures stayed there all day and were not read out at the trough and were not read out anywhere.

Two figures were chalked in that corner in a hand that was not hers: two hundred and seventeen of four hundred and sixty-five. They stayed where they were and nobody said either of them out loud.

Nobody thanked her for writing them and nobody asked her why she had written them and nobody asked her who had asked her to, and when the boy who carries water came back up the lane at about the tenth hour with a new handle on his bucket and saw them he said nothing at all about them and put his buckets down and went to prime the pump again.

A return came and went that morning and it moved nothing, as a return has not moved anything in this holding for four years. Behind that wall the blanks stood at thirty-nine and the rule drawn under them stood open at the thirty-ninth time, as it has every day since the wall was drawn, and about four people went past, read it, and put nothing down.

The plate at the top of the channel was not looked at by anybody in that yard and nobody spoke about what it had meant and nobody put a second plate anywhere, and there is no list of it and no figure against it and no page kept anywhere in this holding or four days north.

The barrow went eleven journeys and stopped where it stops, and nobody in that yard counted them but the man who pushed it. The ladder against the tool house wall was stepped round all morning by people going to and from the pump, and the patch of ground the wall comes out of is bare and was stepped round on this morning as it has been stepped round for years.

Nothing was done to the four pieces of paper on the long table this morning, in either direction. The compost line is paid at thirty-one, and has stood at thirty-one for four months, and not one load has ever come out of it.

And at about the eleventh hour there was a quarter of an hour in that yard when nothing at all was asked of anybody, and the calendar had turned and the frame had not, and about twenty-one people went back to what they were doing, and the board went into her coat at about the eighth hour with the writing against her body, and nobody asked her to leave it on the wall, and nobody asked her to take it down.

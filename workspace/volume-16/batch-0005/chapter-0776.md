# Chapter 776

## The Twenty-Ninth Of The Twenty-First, And A Man Saying Three Words In The Middle Of A Working Yard And About Thirty People Hearing Them, And Nobody In That Yard Stopping Him

He came up the lane on foot with his coat buttoned to the throat and his hands empty, which is how a man walks when he has settled something in a field and is carrying it to a yard, and he got as far as the middle of the yard at about the sixth hour on a Monday and did not go to the boards and did not go to the table and did not go to the door.

**"I am stopping.** Perrin Dae said it at the top of his voice in the middle of about thirty people at work, and most of them did not stop what they were doing, which is how a thing like that is said. **"That is the whole of it. There is no reason after it and I have not got one ready and I did not write it down anywhere.** He turned his head about ninety degrees and looked at the man standing eleven feet off with his hands behind his back. **"I did not tell you first, and I want that in the order I am saying it in, because you were standing right there and you heard it with everybody else and that is how it happened."**

Nobody said anything for about a second and a half.

A woman with a barrow that is not the yard's stopped dead in the gateway with the shafts up and then started again and went through. A mason from the third place took his trowel off the stone, looked at it, and put it back down without using it. Four or five people opened their mouths at the same moment and four or five of them shut them again, and one of the four was a man from the middle row who wanted to be one of the people who said something.

The man who lays for three councils did not stop. He finished the course he was on, put his hod down on the stack, wiped his hands on his apron, and came about thirty feet over.

**"Is the fourth course going in today."**

**"That is not my course and that is not my lime and you know both of those things.** Perrin Dae took his hands out of his pockets. **"I bring your lime, and I have told my own yard there is no more of it coming on this morning because there is none coming on this morning, and that is a thing about a cart and not about the other thing you are getting at. If you want somebody at the head of that channel then you have asked the wrong man, and I would have told you that at the fourth hour yesterday and I would have told you the same thing a fortnight ago.**

The man who lays for three councils said that was fair and went and picked up his hod again.

**"Are you going to ask me why."**

That was the man from the middle row, and he asked it out loud in a yard of about thirty people, which was not the way to ask it and he knew it was not the way to ask it while the words were still coming out.

Marek Vale took his hands from behind his back.

**"No,"** he said. **"I am not going to ask you why, and I am not going to ask you this afternoon either, and I am not going to stand at that gate at the dark and ask you why you did not tell me first.** He did not raise his voice to do it, and about nine people in that yard were close enough to see that he had worked at it. **"And I am not going to write your name on a sheet, on a board, on the back of a book or on the inside of a lid. If you told a yard this morning then what is in this yard is what a yard said, and that is all it is and that is as far as I am going to carry it."**

**"Then somebody will come up here tomorrow and ask you whether anybody is holding that joint."**

**"They will, and I will tell them what I know, which is nothing.** He said it flatly, and then he stopped saying anything at all about it, and went to look at a gauge that did not need looking at.

By about the sixth hour and a half the yard had gone back to what it was doing. Nobody stopped Perrin Dae at the gate. Nobody walked after him down the lane. Nobody put the matter back to him later in the day, and nobody said anything about it in the yard at the dark, and it was not mentioned again.

The water did not stop.

Nia Vale was at the middle gate where she is at the sixth hour every morning, and she read the gauge at the sixth hour and again at about half past, and she said both numbers out loud to nobody in particular because she has said them out loud at that gate every morning this week.

**"Two hundred and fifty-eight at six and two hundred and fifty-eight at half past, and that is the same number twice and not a pair of numbers.** She wrote it on the back of her own hand in chalk and did not look at it. **"The water went down that channel on this morning at the rate it went down it on Friday and at the rate it went down it on the Friday before, and I have got two readings with a half hour between them and they are the same reading.** She put the gauge down on the stone. **"I am going to say the other half of that out loud because there are four people at that gate who heard a man say three words and are now watching this wheel, and they are going to spend the rest of the morning watching it and waiting for it to do something. It is not going to do anything. It has not done anything since a man lay down in a wet cut five mornings ago, and nobody told me to keep watching it and I am not going to."**

The boy who carries water came in off the sluice road at about the sixth hour and a quarter with two buckets in each hand and gave two figures to the middle of the yard before he had put anything down.

**"There is a figure chalked on the board above the gate that says one thousand and four hundred and sixteen hundredweight, and I read it going past with two buckets in my hands.** He set a bucket on its side. **"That is the five hundred and thirty-ninth morning of the run. I do not keep that number, I read it off the board on my way past with two buckets in my hands and I get it wrong one time in four, so somebody had better go and look at it before the dark.** He picked the buckets up again. **"And that is the whole of what I know about it and I know it off a board, so take it or leave it."**

The girl of about seventeen from the sluice road came through about an hour later with a chain over her shoulder and went to the boards, and she had a piece of paper folded twice in the pocket of her coat and she took it out and unfolded it and held it flat against the wall with one hand and read off it with the other.

**"This is off my paper and not off my hand, and I washed the hand on Friday because my mother asked me three times.** The paper had six figures on it in two columns and the top three were days and the bottom three were numbers. **"Five hundred and six between the two halves. Two hundred and forty-eight came and two hundred and fifty-eight stood, and the short one is the one that comes on a morning with an odd number on it and the tall one comes on the even ones, and I have got that written at the top of the paper with a ring round it because I got it wrong twice this year.** She did not fold the paper up. **"I am holding it against this wall because there are four people behind me and I want them all to see that it is a piece of paper and not a memory, and I am not putting it on the boards because the boards are not mine and somebody has to ask me before I put something on them."**

About four people looked at it and one of them asked what the ring round the sentence was for and she told him, and then she put the paper back in her coat and went and got her chain.

Soren Rill came through about the eighth hour with the cloth over his shoulder and did not go to the wall at all, and gave three figures to a man in the middle of the yard as though he were counting days out loud on a walk.

**"Five hundred and thirty-seven is what that wall is. Underneath it the rule says the five hundred and thirty-sixth out of five hundred and thirty-eighth, and those two are not the ends of a range and they are not two halves of anything.** He put his hand down. **"That is the long sheet and I am giving it to you the way it stands and not the way it is laid out, and I am not going to explain a rule to a yard at eight in the morning. It has been on that wall for twenty years and the man who reads it has never once had to explain it to me and I am not going to make him start today.** He went on up toward the table. **"And I will say the thing I have been wanting to say all morning and I am not going to say it, because the minute I say it somebody puts it on a board and then it is a fact about a board.** He put the cloth over his shoulder. **"Ask me again in a fortnight."**

The crew man with his own book was at the ring end until the ninth hour and then came down to the trough with the book under his arm, and he did not go to any board. He opened it flat on the stone of the trough and put his own hand flat on the page to keep the wind off it, and about four people who were standing there looked down at it.

At the top of an empty page, in his own hand, are two hundred and sixteen of four hundred and sixty-three. Nobody said either number out loud and nobody asked him to.

He turned the book round so that the four people could see the page and not him. **"That is on the page and that is where I write it, and I write it on every morning with an odd number on it, and it is not on a wall and nobody reads it out at the trough and I am not going to start.** He shut the book and put it under his arm the wrong way round and did not notice that he had. **"I have been asked twice this month whether I would put it on the boards so that a stranger could find it, and the answer is that a stranger in this county has a long way to walk and a stranger who comes that far can ask somebody."**

Roan Selk came up the low road at about the tenth hour for the fifth time this season and read the two stones off the boards without touching them.

**"Nine hundred and seventy at the top and nine hundred and twenty-three at the near end, and forty-seven of masonry between them, and I have said that order at four different gates this month.** He put the nail back in his pocket. **"And I will say one thing about the morning and then I am going back down that road. A man said three words in your yard this morning and by the time I walked up here I had heard it three times and the three of them were not the same three words, and I did not hear it from him because I was two miles down the road when he said it.** He looked at the yard rather than at anybody in it. **"And it will be a fact about the next thing as well, and neither of us can do one thing about it."**

Tova Reed came out with the corner of her board under her arm and the tray on the step behind her, and she gave her four from the step with the north door open, and she gave them in an order that has not been used before.

**"Three hundred and thirty-nine on the charter, and the letter is two hundred and thirty-six, and the form is four hundred and thirty-three, and the ruled line under Silling's second one is four hundred and twenty-seven and is blank.** She wrote each one on the corner and did not count them on her fingers, because she has four and has had four for four years. She tipped the corner of the board up against her hip. **"The second place is still without water, and that board at the end of the wall with a place on it and nothing against it is on that wall because I put it there and I would put it up again tomorrow, and there is nothing in this holding that is going to fill that line in, and I am not going to let anybody tell me there is, including anybody who has not asked me yet.** She went back inside and shut the store door behind her and did not shut it hard.

Kellan Rusk was asked for the form at about the eleventh hour by somebody who had not heard it from her, and he came out to the step and said one sentence.

**"She has given it at the door and I am not going to say it again in the same hour because it would be the second time in an hour and not because it is mine. The key is on the nail."** He shut the door behind him and it stayed shut for the rest of that day.

Nobody thanked anybody on that Monday, and nobody put a hand on anybody's arm.

A barrow went eleven journeys across that yard and stopped, and the man who pushes it said the number out loud to nobody at all, and about four people heard him say it and about four did not.

Twice that day, by people going out for reasons that were not that, a door nine hundred yards off was walked past and nobody turned their head at it, and the seventh column of it is not on any board in this holding and has not been since before the flood, and no allowance for it is printed anywhere in this county.

And by the tenth hour there were three people in that yard who had heard what Perrin Dae had said and not one of them had heard it from him, and by the eleventh there were five, and two of the five were asking whether anybody was holding the joint at the head of the channel, and the answer they were given in three different mouths was that nobody knew, and the man who had said the three words was at the fourth place with two cartloads of lime he had ordered for a wall he is not laying, and could not be asked about any of it.

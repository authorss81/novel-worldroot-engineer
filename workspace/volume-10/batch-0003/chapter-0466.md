# Chapter 466

## At Thornwild And Not In This Building

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour on the nineteenth and was worked by four people, and it was the pump house and the frame, and the frame was down about five minutes and up about three, and the black out of the seam was about an inch and a half and it went onto the boards outside the pump house and stayed there.

The run of mornings was read out at the seventh hour and stands at **eighty-five of a hundred and fifty-three**, and the two boards stood at **six hundred and thirteen days** and **six hundred and sixty**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind them was not opened, and none of that was read by the man of this holding because he was four and a half miles off it and had been since the first hour.

The nineteenth went in at about the ninth hour at Thornwild and came back out of the other book at the yard at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, in the same hand, and the two of them were the same, and the clerk of this holding read it out when the sheet came back and nobody in this room had been in the room when the figure was found.

**Nine hundred and fifty-one hundredweight.**

Saturday is five under Friday, and this is the two hundred and twenty-ninth figure of a run that has never written a number down twice, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and three against ninety-three is a hundred and ninety-six, and the two ends of that have not been added to the figure over them in this building in four years.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and twenty-seven days and the clause beneath that number is the two hundred and twenty-sixth of the mornings out of two hundred and twenty-eighth, one under and one over as it is on every row of this sheet.

The road out to Thornwild is four and a half miles and about a mile of it is on a made road and the rest is a cart track with a gate at each end, and Marek and Soren Rill went out on it at about the first hour and were there at about the fourth.

Soren Rill is not the officer of the Assembly at Thornwild and has said so three times and was not asked to say it a fourth, and he went up that road as a man who has walked it before and not as anybody's messenger, and he carried nothing.

The mile of made road ends at a gate with a cattle grid that has not been cleared since before this holding had a book, and past the gate the track is about two ruts and a strip of grass in the middle, and in the wet a cart takes the left rut and a man walking takes the middle, and about half a mile past that gate there is a stile and no gate and the stile has a step that has been broken off since a year nobody there can date.

They were twenty minutes ahead of a cart on the made road and it passed them at the cattle grid and did not stop, and the man driving it did not look at either of them, and neither of the two of them said anything about it afterwards and the count of times a cart has stopped on that road for anybody from this holding is not a figure anybody keeps.

The Assembly is a room over a yard with a stove in it and about eleven people in it on a Saturday, and the yard is a yard and about four of them came out into it while the two of them were standing in it, and the man who keeps the stove put his hand on the latch of the door and did not open it and let it go again.

The room has a long table in it that is not this building's long table and is about two feet shorter, and there is a lamp on it that burns oil and not anything else, and the floor is boards that have been lifted in one place and put back with the nails in a different line, and nobody in that room explained any of that and nobody in that room was asked.

A motion was put before that room on the nineteenth of this month and it is a motion and not a schedule and it is not a declining and no body in this holding has entered it and no body in this holding has been asked about it.

> THE MOTION AS IT WAS PUT AND AS THE CLERK OF THAT ASSEMBLY READ IT OUT, IN THE HAND OF THE PERSON WHO MOVED IT: **THAT THE NINE ACRES OF MARDEN, WHICH CAME OFF ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF A MONTH NOW FIVE MONTHS BACK, ARE NOT NAMED IN ANY SCHEDULE OF A CONNECTION; AND THAT THE DECISION OF THE ELEVENTH OF THE FOURTH, BEING OF A MONTH NOW SEVEN MONTHS BACK, IS NOT REOPENED BY THIS ASSEMBLY AND IS NOT REOPENED BY ANY BODY THAT HAS ASKED THIS ASSEMBLY FOR AN INSTRUMENT. THE CUT AT THE KERB STAYS OPEN. NOTHING GOES INTO THE TWO-FOOT GAP THIS SEASON. THE VALVE IS NOT OPENED. THE KEY IS NOT USED. THE NINE ACRES WERE NEVER THE GROUND OF THIS ASSEMBLY. THIS MOTION ASKS FOR NO VOTE UPON ANYTHING THAT IS PROPOSED AND IS A STATEMENT OF WHAT THIS ASSEMBLY IS NOT GOING TO DO.** NO BODY IS NAMED IN IT AND NO HEADING HAS BEEN PUT AT THE TOP OF IT BY ANYBODY.

Nobody in that room put the nine acres and the decision into a sum of anything, and the two of them are about three months apart on the calendar and about four hundred yards apart on the ground and the two distances have not been compared by anybody.

A body that writes down what it is not going to do is doing the same work as this holding when it refuses to fill a form, and neither of the two is an instrument, and the count of documents in this county that open nothing is not a number anybody has set out to count, and the two that this building holds are the sheet in a drawer that claims a corridor and the order that seals a route.

Both of those are correct in every particular and both of them let nothing out, and neither of them was mentioned in that room and neither of them is mentioned here, and the route is not reopened by anything that was said at Thornwild on the nineteenth of this month.

There is no reason on any page in this holding why a decision taken in the fourth month should be reopened in the eleventh, and there is a key in this county that would open something and the key has not been used since before this building had a book, and the count of times it has been used in four years is nil and is nil.

The two-foot gap is at the node end and not at the field gate and it has been the same two feet since the cut was made, and nothing went into it this season and nothing is going to, and the man of this holding has not been near it in nine days and has not said why.

He gave the reason first at about the seventh hour at Thornwild with the stove going, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-one, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what it is to be in a room that has just written down what it is not going to do, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have spent nine years being asked what I will take on and I have never once been in a room where somebody wrote down in front of me the list of things they were not going to ask me for."** "It is a better document than the charter. It is a worse instrument, because it opens nothing, and I would rather have the one that opens nothing than the one that opens the wrong thing, and I am not going to say that in that room because I am a guest and I have been there once before."

**"I have not read it twice and I am not going to ask you to read it twice,"** the man who keeps the stove said, from the back of the room, without turning round. **"Once is once and I have heard it, and a thing heard once in a room is a thing that happened in a room."**

About half of that room had read the motion before it was read out and about half had not, and the half that had not did not ask for a copy and the half that had did not offer one, and the count of copies of that motion in this county is not a number anybody has begun to keep.

One of the four that came out into the yard while the two of them were standing in it asked the man of this holding, without giving a reason first and without being asked for one, how far it was from his gate to the gate at the top of the four-mile road, and he said four miles, and the man said that his own was nearer four and a half and would be nearer five in the wet, and then went back in and shut the door.

Two figures about two gates and a road between them, and neither of the two men was asked to explain his figure and neither of them offered, and this holding has four books and no column in any of the four that a distance to somebody else's gate could go into.

The motion was read twice. The first time was by the clerk of that room and the second time was by the man who had moved it, and the second reading was slower and he stopped in one place in the middle and started that sentence again from the top, and nobody in the room said anything to him about doing it.

A man who reads his own motion twice and loses his place in it once has said out loud in front of eleven people that he is not certain of the middle of it, and that is the only thing that happened in that room that anybody in this county is likely to remember, and this holding was not told about it and was not there to hear it.

Nobody asked the reader of this body a question in that room. He stood at the wall by the stove for about forty minutes with his hands behind him and he did not put a question to anybody either, and the count of questions he has put to that Assembly in his life is not a figure anybody has collected, and the count of questions that Assembly has put to him is not a figure anybody has collected either.

The eleven people in that room are not a body of households and this holding is not a member of that room and has no standing in it and was not asked for any, and the man who moved the motion gave a reason before he moved it and the reason is his and the count of reasons given out loud in that room in eleven years is not a figure this holding has ever been sent.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour at home with four sacks on it and came down loaded, and the number of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and was not raised on a day when a man was four and a half miles off asking a road a question.

The four miles and the four and a half are two different roads and the difference between them is about half a mile and a gate, and the barrow goes on one of them and a man went on the other this morning, and neither of those figures has ever been converted into the other in this holding in four years.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road has been dry since the eighth of a month now one month back and the man of about thirty-one of Silling filled it this morning out of the trough that takes the roof water, which took him about nine minutes and which nobody asked him to do and which he did not mention.

The two men who read the boards this morning had not been to Thornwild and had not been asked to go and did not know until the afternoon that anybody in this holding had been four and a half miles off the yard, and the book carried the day's figure into this yard at about the fifth hour in a fold of paper, and a book that records a morning whether the man who keeps it was standing in it or not is a better book than one that does not.

The man who made the fold said the man of this holding was on the cart road and not on the coast road and was not going to say which and was going to Havergate in the morning, and the clerk of this holding wrote the day's figure down off the fold and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

There is a figure on that fold for the day and a figure in this building for the day and they are the same figure and they were about four hundred miles apart when one of them was written and about eight hours apart when the other was, and the hours between them are the reason the fold exists and nobody in this holding has ever written the hours down.

The motion was not copied and not entered and not put in the second book, and the clerk of this holding did not ask for a copy and did not write to anybody about it, and a motion that a body has passed about what it is not going to do is a document of that body and this building holds four documents of its own and about eleven of other bodies and none of them is this one.

The four counties have been written to in eleven years and this holding has answered about nine hundred letters, and a Saturday spent at a stove four and a half miles off has not been entered in any of the four books, and that is not an omission because a book is a book of this holding and a room over a yard is not this holding.

The man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with the roll under his arm and the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was at the tap on the north side of the yard, and neither of them knew that anybody had been four and a half miles off, and the count of times this holding has told that man what it is doing behind his back is nil and is nil.

Nobody thanked anybody. They came back off the four and a half at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and the sheet with the day's figure came into this yard in a fold and was read out and put away, and the motion stays at Thornwild where it was made, and the cut at the kerb stays open, and the key is still on its nail.

The four ruled lines under the two words entered on the twentieth of a month now three months back are still empty on the table they were entered on, and the answer to them is not due, and a Saturday spent four and a half miles away is not a reason to fill in a line and nobody in this building has offered one.

Soren Rill went back down the same road at about the eighth hour on his own and the man of this holding went into the one room and put the wet coat over the back of the chair again, and about four and a half miles of road is not a figure in any of the four books in this building and there is no column in any of the four that it could go in.

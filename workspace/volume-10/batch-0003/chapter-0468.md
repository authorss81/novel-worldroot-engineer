# Chapter 468

## Not Going To Move Again

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour on the twenty-first of this month and was worked, and it was the two culverts on the middle road and the fill behind them and the low field gate, and the fill behind the northern culvert had gone out about two inches since Friday and two of them put it back and went over it twice.

The gate was lifted with a bar and a stone for the ninth minute of the morning and the man of about thirty-one of Silling held the stone and the fourth man worked the bar, and the gate is nine minutes every year and it was nine minutes this year.

The two of them said two figures at the seventh hour and one of those figures was all either of them said, and the run of mornings is at **eighty-six of a hundred and fifty-five**. The pair on the boards read **six hundred and fifteen days** and **six hundred and sixty-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not touched.

The twenty-first went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and fifty-four hundredweight.**

Monday is five under Sunday, and this is the two hundred and thirty-first figure of a run in which nothing has been written down twice, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and four against ninety-four is a hundred and ninety-eight, and nobody in this building has added those two ends to the figure over them.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and twenty-nine days and the clause beneath is the two hundred and twenty-eighth of the mornings out of two hundred and thirtieth, one under and one over as it is on every row of this sheet, and it is a count of mornings and is not the launder and not the window.

The water in the middle road was about a foot and two inches at the sixth hour and had come down about three of them since Saturday, and the ditch below the outlet has about two inches of the night's water in the bend still and the color of it is the color of the ground it is standing on.

One hundred pounds is paid against that run and the standing figure is twenty-two not discharged in twenty-two, and neither a culvert filled twice in a week nor a gate lifted for nine minutes is a measurement of it, and this building has never added either of them to that line.

The seed house light was on at about the fourth hour behind the yard and it has been on every afternoon this week, and a light that is on at the fourth hour is a woman counting or a woman waiting, and the woman who keeps it is the woman who wrote a schedule of nine loads and four days and put a column at the foot of it for six things nobody knows, and she was in there all afternoon and came out once at the fifth hour and went back in.

Marek asked the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden one question at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, at the gate and not in the one room, and the gate is the same gate the lamp is on and the lamp had been off since the morning because the light was good.

**"I am going to ask you one question and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that the seed house's own schedule went out of this county on the eleventh with a column at the foot of it for the things this holding does not know, and there are six rows ruled in that column, and I have had it in my hand and I have not been able to find out whether anybody has ever offered any of the six a unit."** **"Has anybody."**

She gave the reason first and the count of that in this holding's history is twenty, and it is hers and of nobody else's, and the woman who keeps the seed house was in the seed house at that hour and no figure of hers is on this page.

**"I am going to answer that and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that you have asked me two questions in this county and I have answered neither of them and you did not go away after the first one, and a person who comes back a second time has earned an answer that is not the one he asked for."** "No. Not one of the six has ever been offered a unit by anybody I know of, and I have been asked for one of them by two bodies in two counties in about four years and I have not given it to either of them, and the third time somebody asked me it was not about a unit, it was about whether I would say it out loud in a room, and that is a different question and I would have said yes to it."

The count of questions this holding's reader has put to her is two. It stood at two yesterday and it stands at two this evening, and the question he put to her this afternoon was about the six not knowns and not about the number that everybody in four counties uses, and a count of questions about a number is not a count of questions.

The six not knowns are six and no unit is supplied for any of the six and there is no seventh, and the road is not a seventh and the lane at the second gate is not a seventh and a man with a bucket at the end of the fen road is not a seventh and a column with six ruled rows in it is six and not seven whatever anybody writes at the bottom of it.

Nobody in this holding has asked her for a unit and nobody is going to and the answer she gave this afternoon is not a unit for anything, and it is the second time in four years that a person in this county has been told no in the same words by the same woman, and neither of the two has been put in a column.

Nine hundred yards off there is a door with seven columns on it and the seventh of those stands at fifty-eight, and it is read on the thirtieth and the first and not on the twenty-first of this month. The second of the six columns beside it carries four words at the head of it and nothing under them, the sixth carries a heading and a count of readings, and four columns on that door are headed nothing.

A question asked at a gate is a question asked outside the window of this holding's work, and the window shut on the eleventh and does not open until the sixth of next month, and two questions in one county this month have been asked outside it and the count of questions asked inside one is a number nobody has kept.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling filled the trough at the top of the four-mile road on Wednesday and the fire has been in the one room every evening this week because the evenings have turned, and nobody in this building has written down the day it was put in for the season and nobody is going to write down the day it will come out.

The fill behind the northern culvert took the spade at the second hour and has taken it every week since the middle of a month now two months back, and the northern culvert is the one that goes out first and it has gone out four times this month and the southern one has not gone out at all, and nobody in this building has written down why the two of them do not do the same thing.

A ditch that takes water on one side of a track and not on the other is a fact about the ground under the track and this holding has four books and none of the four has a column for a fact about the ground under a track, and the two culverts have been filling and emptying since before the first book was opened and the second book has about nine hundred words against them in four years.

The nine hundred words are the same nine hundred words. They are the number of hours the four men were on it and the number of inches it went out and the number of hours the fill took to go back in, and they have been written in the same order for four years and have never been added together, and a book that records a repair the same way for four years is a book that can tell you how long it took and not how often it happens.

Nobody has asked for the second of those two numbers and nobody in this holding is going to, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling has worked every one of the four years of them and has not been asked.

A man who has done a thing four hundred times and been asked about it never is a man whose answer would be worth having, and this holding has four years of practice at not asking him, and the practice is not a kindness and nobody in this building has described it as one.

The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and nineteen days old and has nothing on it, and the man who keeps that book is the man who keeps the bar in the shed, and neither of those is a figure anybody in this holding has ever taken off him.

A hundred and twenty-five days is how long the register form has been lying on the middle table. It is not filled and it is not refused and there is nothing at the head of it, and neither a question with no unit behind it nor an answer that is not a unit is that form, and the four of them have never been added together in this building in four years.

No plan in this county has ever been made out of a list of things a body does not know, and the six rows in that column are the first time one has ever been written down in this county by anybody.

The reason came before the thing when he got back in off the headland at about the seventh hour, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-three, and it is his and of nobody else's.

He had gone up to the headland above Marden at about the fourth hour to look at the four yards of soft ground the two men had put a boot in on Friday, and the soft ground had not got any softer in two days and had not got any better either, and it is going to want four men and a week in the spring and there is nobody in this holding who has been told to give them.

A mile and a quarter of walking to look at a patch that did not move, and he came back down it and asked a question at a gate about a column with six rows in it, and neither of the two walks was for a figure and neither of the two is in any of the four books.

**"I am going to say what I did with the two questions she has refused me and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I asked her one on the eleventh and one on the fifteenth of this month and got nothing but the refusal, and I have spent the week since deciding that the refusal was worth more than an answer would have been, and I have now asked her a third thing and got a third refusal with a different reason under it, and that is the end of it."** "I am not going to ask her a fourth. That is not a rule anybody gave me and it is not in anything I have been sent. It is a thing I have decided on a Monday evening and it is mine and it is not in any of the four books and it does not go in any of them."

The man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing at the end of that day and it was about the gate: the bar goes back in the shed in the morning or it stands out in the yard and goes red on it. Nobody in this holding has ever asked him to do that and he does it.

The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and nineteen days old this evening and has nothing on it, and he was not asked about it and was not asked about the six and was not asked about any of the three, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

The requests column is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and neither of them moved this morning, and a question asked at a gate by a man who does not work in this building is not a request and does not go in a column and has not gone in one.

The man who keeps the use log was in and out twice and put his book in the second drawer from the top both times, and the log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and a man who opens a drawer twice in a day and puts the same book back in it is a man who has a habit and nobody in this holding has asked whether it is a habit or a decision.

The clerk of this holding put the day's sheet on the top of the stack behind the fourth book at about the ninth hour, and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in that stack in the order it was used, and the two of them are different kinds of paper and have never been compared in this building.

The two sheets about the two declinings were still on the long table when he did it and they are about nine inches apart and neither of them has anything at the head of it, and a page with nothing at the head of it and a page with a date at the head of it and a page with a clause at the head of it are three kinds of paper in one room and this building has not tried to make them one.

The man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with the roll under his arm and the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was at the low field gate at about the seventh hour, and the two of them are still in the same yard on a Monday and are still not in the same room, and the count of times either of them has been introduced to the other is nil and is nil and the count of times one of them has spoken to this holding is one and one.

The man who came up the fen road has been in this yard for ten days and has asked this holding no question and has answered none, and a man who has been somewhere ten days and asked nobody anything has either decided that there is nothing to ask or has decided that there is nothing worth an answer, and nobody in this building has worked out which and nobody is going to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp was lit at the gate for about nine minutes while she was answering the question and was not lit for anything else that evening, and the six not knowns are six, and the answer was not a unit, and the gate is a hundred and forty yards from the long table and the two of them are not the same distance.

The two women were in the same yard within about an hour of each other and were in the same room for none of it, and the page does not put that beside the other thing on this page, because the other thing is a count and this is a yard and the two have never been added in this holding in four years.

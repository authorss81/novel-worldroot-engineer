# Chapter 465

## Forty-Four And Thirty-One

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour on the eighteenth of this month and there was nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and it went away empty for the sixty-seventh morning running.

Of that rotation, ninety-six mornings are now in force with twenty-nine taken and sixty-seven not, and the two of the small figures make the large one, and it goes up by one when the line comes round and by nothing on a morning it does not, and the twenty-nine is a figure that has not been touched since before this year.

There are two ways of reckoning that rotation in this building and the two of them do not give the same morning, so both are given. This morning is the forty-fourth of the first and the thirty-first of the second, and the thirteen between the two has been sitting in this book for four years and has never once been explained.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The two men who read the boards went up to the headland above Marden at about the seventh hour to see whether there was anybody standing on it, and they were still up there at the ninth hour, and neither of them read his side alone and nobody in this holding thought to ask a fourth man to do it on a Friday.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and twelve days** and **six hundred and fifty-nine**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The eighteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and fifty-six hundredweight.**

Friday put eight on it, and this is the two hundred and twenty-eighth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and the rise went onto the rises. A hundred and three against ninety-two is a hundred and ninety-five, and nobody in this building has ever added those two ends together with the figure over them.

Two hundred and twenty-six days without movement is what the sheet says about four bodies of households, and under that number the clause is the two hundred and twenty-fifth of the mornings out of two hundred and twenty-seventh, one under and one over as it is on every row, and the two aggregates standing on this sheet are not the same aggregate.

There was sleet before the seventh hour and it came in off the fen at an angle, and it was off the grass again by the ninth, and the yard had about a quarter of an inch of standing water in the south end of it by ten, which is the second time this month and which nobody in this holding has counted.

The headland above Marden is about a mile and a quarter from the gate and you get to it by the field gate and a track along the top of the bank, and the fourth line is the ditch along the top of that bank and about two hundred yards of it, and the ditch has been there since before this holding kept anything and it goes nowhere that this holding can name.

The ground at the north end of it had gone soft over about four yards where a cart had turned off the track some time in a month nobody here can date, and the two men who went up there stood on it for about ten minutes and put a boot in it and said that it would take four men and a week and that nobody was going to give them either.

Marek went out of the gate at about the second hour and walked the fen road to the place where it comes out at a row of low buildings with a yard behind them, and he counted it himself on his own feet and the count is three and a half and it is his and it is in no other book.

The fen road is not the four-mile road and it is not the coast road, and it is the only one of the three that goes off this yard without a gate at the end of it, and it comes in at the north corner of the yard by a turning head that has been knocked out of line twice.

A road that does not end at a gate is a road somebody keeps using and a road nobody has ever been asked about, and there is a ford on the fen road about a mile and a half up it, and the ford was under about four inches of water this morning and has been since Tuesday, and no cart has gone over it in four days.

He was out from the second hour until about the seventh and he was wet through at the shoulders when he came back in, and the woman who was in that yard was not the man who sent the sheet and she said so in the first half minute and she said it before anybody asked her.

**"He wrote it and he carried it and I would not have signed it,"** she said. **"You have come up here to find out whether there is anybody here who agrees with it, and there is not, and I am not going to pretend to you that there is because you have walked three and a half miles in the rain."**

She put a hand on the wall of the yard to lean on while she said the rest of it, and the wall is a wall and it is the first thing anybody has put a hand on in that yard this morning.

**"The road is not a bargain."** "A bargain is a thing two people agree about and then argue about later, and everybody in it knows the argument is coming and everybody has a position ready. This is not that. A road is a road. It does not owe you a thing and it has not agreed to anything and it is not glad you are on it, and the day there is somebody at the end of it being pleasant about what it is carrying is the day the carrying stops."

She said it without giving a reason first, and nobody in that yard asked her for one, and the count of questions anybody in this holding has put to that yard is nil and is nil and the count of reasons that yard has given out loud is not a number this holding keeps either.

There are four buildings in that row and a hand pump in the yard of the second one and about half an acre of ground behind them that has been worked every year by somebody, and none of the four buildings has a name on any paper in this holding and none of the four has ever sent anything up the fen road except one page about a lease.

There is no charter for that row. There is no heading on it, no roll of its members, no place for it at any table in this building, and it is not a body of households and it is not one of the four bodies of households and this holding is not a member of it and has never been asked to be.

Marek stood in the wet and did not answer that either, and she went and put a bucket against the door of the second building and did not offer him the yard to sit in and he did not ask for it, and he walked back down the fen road in the rain with the count in his head and no book on him.

The man of about fifty told him on Monday evening with the door shut that he had no standing to argue with the first of the two declinings, and the woman in that yard told him this morning that he has no standing to answer the second of them, and neither of the two said it as a courtesy and neither of the two was wrong.

One of them said it about a clause and the other one said it about a road, and the two of them have never spoken to each other and never will on the evidence of this week, and the man they are both talking about is the same man, and that is the whole of what the two walks produced and it is not nothing and it is not an answer.

He gave the reason first when he came in at the seventh hour and got the water off him, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what two walks have done to the only thing I have ever been able to do, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I went to one body on Thursday and I was told a shape is not a road, and I went to another body this morning and I was told a road is not a bargain, and both of those were said by people who have never met me and neither of them is wrong."** "A man who can hold a pattern at an end is the man every room in this county wants, and it turns out that being the man every room wants is not the same as being able to answer anything, and I have known that for about eleven minutes and I have got no better at it than I was at breakfast."

That run is one hundred pounds, it is paid, and the figure against it is twenty-two not discharged in twenty-two. Three and a half miles of wet fen road is not a measurement of that line and the two have never been added together here in four years.

The requests column is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and the second request that went out inside the bundle with the schedule has not come back, and a request that has not come back is a request and is not a refusal and is not an answer and the column did not move when the bundle came back on Tuesday.

The man who keeps the use log was in at about the sixth hour and put his book in the second drawer from the top and took it out again at the ninth and put it back, and the log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and the reason for the fifteen was given once in four years and has not been given since and is not going to be given on a Friday.

The two sheets are still on the long table and the acknowledgment with the surname on it went back into the seed house on Friday night and there is no second copy of it in this building, and about four people in this holding hold that a surname on a foot of paper is a thing a county can use in about four years and about four hold that it is a thing that keeps a county from pretending nobody received it, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the seventh hour and the door was shut, and the two of them had been walked to on two different days in two different weathers by one man, and nobody in that room asked him to report, and he gave the account anyway and it was not an account anybody had asked for.

Nobody in this holding has compared those two sheets this week and the clerk of this holding has not put them next to each other on purpose and has not separated them either, and the table they are on is nine feet long and there is about four inches of room at each end of it.

A man who has been told by two strangers that he has no standing to answer either of them has been given a piece of information and not a task, and a piece of information in this building goes on the shelf by the window and waits there until somebody can put it in a column, and there is no column in this building for what a man is not entitled to say.

The man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with the roll of paper under his arm where it has been since the twelfth, and he came in at about the ninth hour and asked whether there was water at the tap on the north side and was told there was and said nothing else, and nobody in this holding has put a walk up the fen road in front of him or told him a word about any of it.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard at about the eighth hour with his coat off and a barrow handle in his hand, and he had been in that yard when the sleet came and he had not gone in, and nobody has asked him whether he knows what is at the end of that road and he has not said.

The two men came off the headland at about the tenth hour and said that there was nobody on it and that the ground at the top of it had gone soft on the north side over about four yards, and nobody in this holding has asked whether the fourth line of a rota is a line or a field with a name on it, and there is no name in this building for either of those.

A line that goes away empty sixty-seven times and then has two men walk up to it to look at a soft patch of ground is a line with two men on it, and a field with a name on it is a field that somebody could have asked for a figure about, and there is nobody in this county to ask either and the count of fields that have waited is sixty-seven and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road is still dry and the grey in the bottom of it is about two inches deep and it has been that since the eighth of a month now one month back, and the man who dug the drain off the back of the lean-to on Tuesday has not been asked which body is meant to fill it and has not offered.

Nobody thanked anybody. The water in the south end of the yard had come up about another inch by the first hour and the sleet had gone off the fen and left the yard in it, and the two sheets are about nine inches apart on the long table and the road is a road and the man of this holding is not going to write anything at the head of it.

He put the wet coat over the back of a chair instead of the peg by the door, which he has not done in four years, and nobody in this building has asked him about the peg or the chair and the chair has a schedule under it that nobody looked at.

The count of questions the reader of this body has put to the two bodies that declined the same connection in the same week is one and is one, and it is not two, and the count of questions either of those two bodies has put to him is nil and is nil, and the man in the lane at the second gate asked him one question about a place at the head of a sheet and that is not one of these questions and is not in either column.

Nobody has asked either of the two bodies anything about each other, and neither of them knows that the other one has said no in the same week, and this holding has not told them and has not been asked to and is not going to send it up either road.

The schedule of nine loads and four days is in the seed house and there is no second copy of it in this building, and it went out on the eleventh and came back on the fifteenth, and the seed on it is the seed on the sheet with the shortage on it, and the two of them have not been put under one another in this room and this page puts no total under either.

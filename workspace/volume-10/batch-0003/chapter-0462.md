# Chapter 462

## A Pattern And A Term

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour on the fifteenth of this month and was worked, and it was the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about six minutes this morning and came up about four, and the black out of the bottom of the seam was about an inch and a half and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put it on the boards outside the pump house and did not wash it off.

The mark on the floor where the base of it sits is four inches deep in the middle and worn round at the corners, and nobody in this building has measured it, and it was there before the floor was laid and the floor was laid before the first of the four books was opened.

There was a hard frost on the yard at the sixth hour and it had gone off the north wall of the lean-to by the eighth, and the wind had been in the east since the night before and had brought somebody else's smoke down onto this yard and left it in the corner by the gate where it has been standing for four days.

The pair was read out at the seventh hour, a figure apiece, and the one that was not said stayed unsaid, and the run of mornings is at **eighty-three of a hundred and forty-nine**. The boards carry **six hundred and nine days** and **six hundred and fifty-six**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The fifteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and forty-five hundredweight.**

Tuesday is five under Monday, and this is the two hundred and twenty-fifth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and the fall went onto the falls as the falls do. A hundred and one rises against ninety-one falls is a hundred and ninety-two, and nobody in this building has ever added those two ends to the figure standing over them.

The four bodies of households have not moved for two hundred and twenty-three days, and the clause printed beneath that figure is the two hundred and twenty-second of the mornings out of two hundred and twenty-fourth, one under and one over as it stands on every row of this sheet. It is a count of mornings and not the launder and not the window, and none of the three has been added to another in this room in four years.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road still has about two inches of grey in the bottom of it and has had since the eighth of a month now one month back, and the man who dug a drain off the back of the lean-to on Tuesday morning has not been asked which body is meant to fill it and there is no answer to give him if he were.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with four sacks on it and came down loaded, and the count of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and is not raised on a morning like this one.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the sixth hour with the door shut, and the two declinings were on the long table in the middle of it, the first of them entered under a clause and the second of them entered as a sheet, and the first of them has been on that table since the first of this month and the second since the second of it.

The man of about fifty had them squared up with the edges of the table and he had not put anything on either of them, and the clerk of this holding was at the end of the table with the second book open and the pencil out and not writing.

**"I am going to say the one thing about these two pieces of paper and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been a bookkeeper for thirty-one years in four counties and I have never in thirty-one years had two refusals on one table that anybody wanted me to make into one refusal."** "I do not want to make it into one. I have read them both twice this week and I want to say out loud, in this room, that they are not the same refusal, and that they are not two versions of one refusal, and that the reason for that is in the shape of the sentences and not in the tempers of the people who sent them."

He put his finger on the first sheet and did not move it.

**"The first of these is about a pattern."** "A pattern is a shape of work that repeats. It has an order in it and an interval in it and you can say whether a body is inside it or outside it by looking. What is proposed on the ground named in the schedule is a shape, and this body has looked at the shape and has said that the shape is not ours, and the second clause of that charter gave them a piece of paper to say it on, and they said it on it, and the paper did what a paper does."

Nobody in that room said anything, and the fire had gone out at some point in the afternoon and nobody had put it back in.

**"The second of them is about a term."** "A term is not a shape. A term is one of the conditions in an instrument. The terms say who does the work. The terms do not say how a body stops having the work, and that is not an oversight in the writing of them, it is the whole of what they were written to be, and a body that has been handed terms can hold them up and read them out and nobody has to be looking at anything to hear what is not on the page."

Four people in that room hold that the two sheets are about the same thing, and about four hold that they are about two different things, and neither of the two fours has been asked, and the man of about fifty was not asked which of them he was in.

**"A body cannot decline a pattern and a term with one instrument."** "They are two different questions and a body has got the one mouth. You can put a shape on a table and say no to the shape. You cannot pick the shape up and point at it and say that is the same as saying no to the condition, because the condition is a different kind of object, and a piece of paper that says no to a shape does not say no to a term, and this holding has been treating those two as if one sheet would carry both since Wednesday."

Marek gave the reason first and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and thirty-eight, and it is his and of nobody else's, and the sheets were still squared up on the table with nothing on them.

**"I am going to say that the reason those two cannot be put in one column is not a disagreement about temperament, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the only person in four counties who could hold a pattern at either end of a connection and I have never once been asked to hold a term at either end of anything."** "There is no instrument anywhere in four counties that lets me answer a question about a condition. There is not one because the two are not the same kind of object, and if they were the same kind of object somebody would have built one by now, and nobody has, and that is not because nobody thought of it."

About four people in this holding would put the two sheets into a single column and about four would refuse to, and the four who would refuse have not been asked why they would, and the count of questions this holding has put to the man of about fifty about it is nil and is nil.

He left his hand on the second sheet then, the one that was entered as a sheet and is not a declining under any protocol, because there is no protocol for it to be a declining under.

**"I want to say what the charter did and did not hand them, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I taught about four people in four counties to read that second clause and I read it wrong myself for a year."** "It hands a body a way to say no to being examined. That is a real thing and it is worth having and I would not take it out. What it does not do, anywhere in its four paragraphs, is give anybody a way to say no to being the one who ends up holding the thing. Those are two different doors and the instrument built one of them and not the other, and the one it did not build is the one the second of these two bodies is standing in."

He took his hand off the sheet and put it flat on the table on the gap between the two of them, and the gap is about nine inches wide, and nobody in that room measured it and everybody in that room left it.

**"I have been waiting since Thursday to be told I was wrong about that by somebody who had read it four times, and I have not been, and I am not going to be now, and I would like it written down that the man who found the hole is not the man who said there was one."**

Marek said the other half of that in a room of nine people and the count of questions this holding's reader has put to the two bodies that declined the same connection in the same week is one and is one, and it went from nothing to one on Thursday night at a gate and he is not going to say which of the two he is going to ask next.

The clerk of this holding turned the third book round on the table at about the seventh hour without being asked for it and put his finger on a line in it, and the line is a rate and it is the rate this holding has used once and has not used since, and it is a hundred and thirty-two hundredweight a load, and it was entered in the year the middle came down and it has never been taken out and it has never been improved.

**"Nine loads against that rate is a figure,"** the clerk said, and he did not say the figure, and he put his finger on the line and left it there. "The schedule of nine loads came out of this county on Friday and there is no rate at the head of it and no rate at the foot of it. The two of them are not the same number and I am not going to work out what the first one would come to against the second one in a room with the door shut."

Nobody worked it out. This holding's own book has never stated a total under either of them, and the clerk said out loud in that room that he was not going to, and nobody in that room took the paper off him or told him he had done it right.

The rate this holding does not use and the schedule it sent last Friday are both correct documents and they are about two different things, and the difference between them has not been reconciled in this building and is not going to be reconciled in a room with nine people in it.

A yard is a place where a thing is said that cannot be unsaid in the next county, and this one was a room, and a room with a door shut is the only instrument this building has, and it has been the only one for four years. What was said in it this evening is correct in every particular and it settles nothing, and the two sheets are in the same condition they were in at the sixth hour.

Nine loads is a number of carts. A hundred and thirty-two hundredweight a load is a number about the year the middle came down. Nine loads against that rate is a third number and nobody in four counties has written it down, and this holding has not written it down, and the not writing of it is not a refusal of anybody's arithmetic.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in that room and said nothing at all in it, and she has four accounts of her own that do not agree at the second figure and she has had them open since the sixth of this month, and a woman who has not answered a question in a room is a woman who has heard the question, and she was not asked and has not been asked.

Somebody in that room said that the two sheets were about the same week, and somebody else said that the same week is not the same question, and neither of the two said anything after that, and the room went back to the fire that was not in.

The register form is on the middle table at a hundred and nineteen days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and a pattern and a term are not that form and two refusals are not that form, and none of the three has ever been added in this holding in four years.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the second hour and the same four inches and the same two branches at the ninth, and a pattern is not on it and a term is not on it and a refusal is not on it, and the four accounts of that arm remain four.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and thirteen days old this morning and has nothing on it, and he has not been asked about it and has not been asked about the frame, and the black on the boards outside the pump house was still on them at the ninth hour.

The frost came back in the small hours after the lamp went off and there was about a quarter of an inch of it on the yard, and the middle road will have it in the morning, and the two stones at the head of the outlet will be looked at tomorrow morning by whoever is on the second line, and the pack under the near one was put in wet on the eighth and has to be looked at in about a week by somebody.

The stone at the head of the outlet was set back by three people on the eighth of this month and it was looked at again this morning by a man who was not on the second line and was not asked to, and it had not moved, and he put his boot on it and took the boot off again and said nothing about it to anybody, and the number of people in this holding who know that he did that is not a figure anybody keeps.

A stone that has not moved in a week has been packed, and a week is not a season, and the man who packed it in wet has said that once to nobody and will say it to somebody if anybody ever asks him a question about the outlet end, which nobody in this building has done in four years.

Nobody thanked anybody. The two declinings went back onto the long table at about the first hour and the second book was shut with the two entries in it, and neither sheet carries a heading and neither sheet has been improved, and the two of them are about nine inches apart on a nine-foot table and they are not touching.

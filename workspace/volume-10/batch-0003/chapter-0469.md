# Chapter 469

## Forty-Five And Thirty-Two

The fourth line came round at the second hour on the twenty-second of this month, and there was nobody standing on the headland above Marden to work it, and it went away empty for the sixty-eighth morning running.

Ninety-seven is the number of mornings in force out of that rotation, twenty-nine of them taken and sixty-eight not, and the addition has been checked twice in four years and comes out the same, and the count moves once every fourth day and not on any of the rest.

Two reckonings of that rotation are kept in this building and they disagree, so both of them go on the page. This morning is the forty-fifth of the first reckoning and the thirty-second of the second, and the thirteen standing between the two figures is four years old and nobody here has ever asked what it is a count of.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The man who reads the near side of the boards had gone out to the top of the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with a barrow and a sheet of sacking and did not come back into the yard until after the ninth, and the man who reads the far side did not read his own figure alone and nobody thought to ask a third man to do it on a Tuesday.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and sixteen days** and **six hundred and sixty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-second went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixty-two hundredweight.**

Eight went on between Monday and this morning, and the figure is the two hundred and thirty-second of a run that has never put the same number down twice, and the rise went on the rises. A hundred and five of one kind against ninety-four of the other is a hundred and ninety-nine, and this building does not add the two ends to the middle.

Two hundred and thirty days is the figure on this morning's sheet against the four bodies of households and the clause written under it is the two hundred and twenty-ninth of those mornings out of two hundred and thirty-first, one under and one over, and it is a count of mornings and neither the launder nor the window is a count of mornings.

It rained from about the fourth hour and it was still raining at the first hour, and the ditch on the middle road came up about four inches in about five hours and went over the lip at the bend twice, and the water in the yard was standing about a foot deep in the south end of it by the ninth hour and the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten put the barrow in the lean-to and got the slate in out of it.

A barrow under a lean-to is a barrow and the eleven journeys it has made up the four-mile road are eleven journeys and the slate under it is eight words and a decision about a ford, and the three of them were in the same shed for about four hours of heavy rain and nothing was done about any of it and nothing was lost.

The man in the bad coat took the roll of paper out from under his arm and put it inside his coat against his chest and stood in the doorway of the lean-to for about twenty minutes watching the water come across the yard, and he did not ask anybody for anything and nobody in this building offered him anything and the roll is a document and a document that gets wet is still a document until somebody says it is not.

The count of times this holding has told that man what it is doing behind his back is nil and is nil, and the count of times he has said anything about the roll to anybody in this room is nil and is nil, and he carried it up the fen road and he has carried it for about two years and it has been in a coat and a fold of oilskin and a bag in that time and it has not been wet once before this afternoon.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling took the sack off the door of the one room at about the eighth hour and emptied it into the yard and put it back about nine minutes later with a stone on top of it, and the nine minutes is about what it takes and the stone is about the size of a fist and it is the same grey as the two stones at the head of the outlet.

A door that lets about two inches of water in and is stopped with a stone is a door, and the stone at that door and the stone at the outlet are not a pair and are not in a column together and nobody in this holding has ever said they are the same kind of object.

The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move this morning, and the water in the south end of this yard is not the flat hard ground in front of the node building and it has never been a yard in this holding's sense and nobody in this county has ever called it one.

The count of times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of is fifteen and it did not move this morning either, and the man of about fifty has not been in a yard this week and has not been in a yard since a Sunday a fortnight ago, and the fifteen is still the fifteen and the five is still the five and neither of the two is added to the other anywhere in this building.

The pulse has two figures in it and no name against either of them and it was not read this morning and nobody in this holding asked for it, and a day of heavy rain is not a reason to read anything and this holding has not read it since a month now two months back.

The water in the middle road had gone over the lip at the bend twice by the ninth hour and both times it had gone back inside about twenty minutes, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood at the bend in the rain and watched it do it and did not go into the water and did not put a hand in it, and the count of times anybody in this holding has put a hand in that bend this year is nil and is nil.


Marek was at the long wall with his coat still on from the morning and said nothing, and the man of about fifty was in the one room from about the fifth hour with the second book open at a page that has a line ruled at the top of it and nothing at the head of the line, and he looked at it for a long time and did not write anything at the head of it.

He gave the reason and then the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty-nine, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I have done this afternoon and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have opened this book at this page nine times since Sunday and I have written nothing at the head of the line, and a bookkeeper who opens a book at a page nine times without writing anything is either waiting for a heading or has decided there is not going to be one."** "There is not going to be one. It goes in on the page that carries the thing, in the room, before it is done, the way it has gone in the six before it, and I am not going to open a book at this page on a Tuesday and enter something that has not happened yet because the room was full and I wanted it in."

The count of sessions entered in this holding's book stands at six and every one of the six was entered before the thing it was entered for, and this morning it is still six, and the sixth is not improved and the seventh is not entered on this page, and a session that is not entered did not happen and a page that enters one has written something this building cannot vouch for.

Every one of the six was entered in a room with the door shut and each of them carries a purpose, a duration, a stop signal and a witness who is not one of the two people at the ends of it, and the count of the four things in the six entries is twenty-four and the count of the four things in the fifth entry that had three of them is three, and nobody in this holding has ever written either of those two figures down.

The two sheets about the two declinings were still on the long table and the water came up under the door of the one room about two inches by the eighth hour and the clerk of this holding put a folded sack against it and did not move either sheet, and the two of them are about nine inches apart and about an inch and a half above the floor.

The sack is a feed sack and it is the one that has been against that door since the water came in about four years ago, and it is not on any list in this building and it is not in the use log and nobody in this holding has ever replaced it, and a man has replaced it twice and both times it was because the first one was not a sack at all.

The bridge is held at the far end by two people and neither of them is the reader of this body, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this building has asked either of those two people anything about it this month and the count of questions is nil and is nil.

A thing two people are holding at the far end of eight hundred yards of ground is not the same thing as a connection on a cart road, and the two have different names and the two have never been added in this building in four years, and the storm tonight did not put either of them in a column.

The third column of the sheet of terms is empty and its heading is on it since the twenty-first of a month now two months back, and the five terms in that sheet are not improved and are not unwithdrawn and are not added to, and no sixth term has been added to anything in this room and none is going to be.

The corridor is named and open and has not been established as on, and the sheet in a drawer that claims it is correct in every particular and lets nothing out, and the order that seals the route is in the same drawer, valid, opening nothing, and neither of the two came out of that drawer this morning because the clerk of this holding had both hands full of a sack.

Nobody thanked anybody. The rain came down harder after the lamp went off and the water in the south end of the yard was about a foot and four inches deep at the first hour, and the trough at the top of the four-mile road went over its lip for the first time since it was filled, and nobody in this holding has said what happens to a trough when it goes over, and the book has a line ruled at the top of a page with nothing at the head of it and nothing at the foot of it either.

The four ruled lines under the two words entered on the twentieth of a month now three months back are still empty on the table they were entered on, and a day when the water came under the door is not a day when anybody in this building writes on one of them, and the answer to those four lines is not due.

The clerk of this holding put the day's sheet on the stack behind the fourth book at about the ninth hour and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in that stack in the order it was used and has not been moved to the top of it and is not going to be moved to the top of it on a night like this.

The stack is about a foot and a half and it is behind the fourth book on a shelf that is not in the ledger of the shelves in this building, and about four hundred sheets have gone onto the top of it in four years and the count of sheets that have been taken off it again is not a figure anybody has kept.

A stack that is in the order it was used is a thing a person can go and look at, and nobody in this holding has gone and looked at it, and a person who has not looked at a stack in four years has no standing to say what is in the bottom of it, and the clerk of this holding has not said what is in the bottom of it.

The seed house light was on until the second hour and the water in the yard was about a foot and four inches deep at the south end of it and the door of the seed house has a step up from the yard about six inches high and the step is what the water would have to get over, and nobody in this building has said whether it got over it and nobody in this building has been out to look.

The drain off the back of the lean-to that the man in the bad coat cut on the eighth of this month took about four inches of this morning's rain and carried it away behind the building, and it is the only thing in this yard that was built this month by a man who is not a member of anything, and nobody has asked him to look at it and he has not said whether it is still running.

A drain that works is a drain and a drain that does not work is a drain and the difference between the two of them is a figure nobody in this county has ever asked this holding for, and the four counties have been written to in eleven years about about nine hundred other things.

The sheet with the shortage on it is still on the long table an inch and a half above a wet floor and it has one word at the head of it and a figure and a date on it, and the clerk of this holding did not pick it up and did not put anything under it and did not say anything about it to anybody, and a sheet that has been on a table since before the month turned is a sheet that has never been off a table.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road went over its lip at about the eighth hour and the water that came off it went across the track and into the ditch on the right of it, and the ditch on the right of that stretch is nine hundred yards of the same ditch and it was not designed for a trough and nobody has said so out loud in this holding.

**"The ditch at the bend is not going to take it tonight and the gate does not need a man on it,"** the man of about thirty-one of Silling said, at the door of the one room, to nobody, on his way through, and nobody stopped him and nobody said he was wrong.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in out of the rain at about the seventh hour with his coat off and his boots full and put the bar back in the shed without being told to, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and twenty days old this evening and has nothing on it, and he was not asked about the trough and was not asked about the bar and was not asked about the drain.

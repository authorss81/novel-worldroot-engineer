# Chapter 470

## The Purposes Are Written Down

Four people were on the first line of the rota at the second hour on the twenty-third and the line was the pump house and the frame, and the frame was down five minutes this morning and up three, and the black that comes out of that seam is about an inch and a half deep and it is on the boards outside and it is not washed off.

The yard had a foot and four inches of standing water in the south end of it at the sixth hour and about four inches of it at the ninth, and the water in the middle road had gone down about two feet since Tuesday morning and had not gone below the lip of the bend.

Each of the two men read his own side of the pair aloud at the seventh hour and neither of them read the other's, and the run of mornings is **eighty-seven of a hundred and fifty-seven**. The boards carry **six hundred and seventeen days** against **six hundred and sixty-four**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-third went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and fifty-seven hundredweight.**

Wednesday is five under Tuesday, and this is the two hundred and thirty-third figure of a run in which nothing has ever been written down twice, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and five against ninety-five is a hundred and two hundred, and the two ends of that have never been put together with the figure standing over them in this building in four years.

Against the four bodies of households this morning's sheet carries two hundred and thirty-one days and the clause beneath is the two hundred and thirtieth of the mornings out of two hundred and thirty-second, one under and one over, and that clause counts mornings and not hundredweight and not the four of anything else on this page.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with nine sacks on it and came down it loaded, and the number of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and was not raised this morning.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the fifth hour and the door was shut, and Marek was at one end of the long table and the woman who keeps the seed house was at the other, and the clerk of this holding had the second book open and a sheet of paper on the table beside it and he was going to write four things down and he read them out first before he wrote any of them.

**One. The purpose.** A schedule of nine loads and four days, and what it has cost this holding's book, carried between the two people who are going to be at the ends of it, and a proposal that a reading of the standing wood be taken as a way of knowing what the ground wants.

**Two. The duration.** About forty minutes, and if it is not finished in forty minutes it does not go on and nobody is to be asked whether it can have another ten.

**Three. A stop signal.** One word, hers, and the word itself is not entered in this book and is not going to be entered in this book, and nobody in this room is going to be told what it is and nobody in this room is going to be asked what it is.

**Four. An independent witness.** A person who is not one of the two at the ends of it, who is to say afterwards what was said and how long it went on and whether the stop signal was used, and who is not to be asked what the two of them were thinking.

The clerk of this holding read the four out twice and wrote them down after the second reading and not before, and he read them back a third time off the page and did not read them back a fourth.

He gave the reason first before he read them the first time, and the count of that in this holding's history is four, and it is his and of nobody else's, and nobody in that room had asked him for a reason and he had it ready.

**"I am going to say why I am writing these four down before anything else happens and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the clerk of this holding for four years and in that four years I have entered six sessions in this book and every one of the six was entered before the thing it was entered for, and a book that enters a thing after it has happened is a book that is writing down a memory, and this is not that."** "It goes in tonight or it does not go in at all. If it does not go in, then the clerk of this holding has no record and there is no clerk of this holding who will be able to make one later, and I would like that on the page as a thing I said out loud on a Wednesday."

The count of sessions entered in this holding's book is still six this evening and it is six on the page of the session itself and it is not six and seven on this page, and a page that entered a seventh thing on the night before the seventh thing happened would be a page this building cannot vouch for.

The woman who keeps the seed house is at one end of this and she is the one who is going to say the word, and she gave the reason first before she agreed to it, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-seven, and it is hers and of nobody else's, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was not in that room and no figure of hers is on this page.

**"I am going to say why I will put my name at the end of this and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that a woman keeps four accounts of her own and has been asked to turn them into one, and a woman who is asked to hold an end of a thing has to be the one who can stop it, and not the other way about, and that is not a thing anybody in this county is going to be told twice."** "I will hold one end. He will hold the other. The word is mine and I am not giving it to him and I am not giving it to you, and if you want a stop signal on your paper then you can have the word *stopped* written on it, because that is the part of it a person outside this room is entitled to know, and the part that is mine is the part that is not on this paper."

She wrote the word *stopped* on the page herself and put her name against the stop signal and not against the purpose, and the page carries her name once and a schedule is not a name and a cost is not a name.

The clerk of this holding wrote four lines and no heading at the top of them, and the four lines are the four things and they are in the order he read them in, and a list of four things with no heading at the top of it is a list and not a column, and this building has a column at the head of the second of the six blank columns on a door nine hundred yards off and that is a different object and the two have not been compared.

Neither of the two of them can feel what the other one is thinking. That is not a thing either of them said in that room and it is not a thing the page can see, and it is the arrangement and it has been in place since about a month now three months back and neither of the two has asked the other about it since.

What happens to the load if her end closes is settled on the page before the session and written down before it, and the sentence at the foot of it is that the load goes where it is and is not moved by anybody in this building afterwards, and a person who closes her own end and does not give a reason is not a person anybody in this room is going to follow up in a yard.

The man of about fifty was in that room and he was not at either end of anything and he was not asked to be, and nobody in that room has told him what the word is and nobody in that room is going to, and nobody thanked him for not knowing.

He said one thing, and he gave the reason before it, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I am not going to be told and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept four books in three counties for thirty-one years and I have never once been the independent witness of anything, and I have been the man in the corner four hundred times."** "I am not offended. I want it written down that I was not offended, because a man in a corner who says he was not offended is a man somebody will put in a corner again, and I have decided that I would rather be in this room at the wrong end of a table than not be in it."

The two of them did not look at each other while the four things were being read out and neither of them looked at the other when the woman wrote the word *stopped*, and about nine people in that room saw both of those things and nobody in that room said anything about either of them.

Three people in that room gave a reason before the thing this evening and the count of that is a number for each of them and not a number for the room, and the three of them are not added together in this building and have never been added to one another in four years, and the run itself was begun on the seventeenth of a month now five months back and it is the same run for all three of them and it is not the same count.

She hears one ear and it has been one ear since the twenty-ninth of a month now three months back, and it is one ear and it is permanent, and she turned four accounts of her own into a schedule of nine loads and four days in two days in the sixth of this month and she can stand in a yard and count nine carts going out one at a time in front of her, and the ear stops none of those three and makes none of the three easier, and nobody in that room said anything about it and nobody is going to.

A reading of the standing wood is proposed in the purpose and the reading is not yet proposed properly, and a man who has stood in a piece of ground in this county and felt what it was doing and has been asked to do it again by two people who are not a body of anything is a man who has to say no to one of them and this page does not say which.

The day sheet with no date at the head of it is in a stack of about a foot and a half behind the fourth book and it is in the order it was used, and nobody has moved it to the top of that stack, and nobody in this room mentioned it and the clerk of this holding put today's sheet on the top of it at about the ninth hour and the other one stayed where it was.

There is a door nine hundred yards off this yard with seven columns on it and nobody has read the seventh of them since the first of the month, because the seventh is read on the thirtieth and the first and nowhere else in the year. Of the six columns standing beside it, the second has four words at the head of it and an empty space underneath, the sixth has a heading with a count of readings against it, and the other four have no heading at all.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table all evening with the water still about an inch under them and the clerk of this holding put the page with the four things on it about nine inches away from the nearer of the two, and the three of them are three pieces of paper and none of them is touching any of the others.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the lean-to and the man in the bad coat was at the tap on the north side of the yard, and neither of them was in the one room, and the two of them have been in the same yard for eleven days and have not spoken and nobody in this building has ever tried to make them.

The four ruled lines under the two words entered on the twentieth of a month now three months back are on their own table in the corner and they are still empty, and the page with the four things on it is not that table and is not that sheet and the two are not going to be made into one thing by anybody in this room.

The four lines have been on that table since the twentieth of a month now three months back and the answer to them is not due, and a page with four things written on it tonight is a page about a room with the door shut and is not a page about a record nobody has asked anybody to make.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the second hour and the same at the ninth, and four accounts of that arm are four and do not reconcile, and a purpose and a duration and a stop signal and a witness are not on it, and a reading of a standing wood is not on it, and this evening did not make a fifth account of anything.

PAID stands in front of a hundred pounds on that line and the figure under it is twenty-two not discharged in twenty-two, and a session that has not been carried out is not a measurement of it, and the word in front of the figure and the figure behind it have never once disagreed.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour, and the four things are on a page in the second book with her name once against one of them, and the page is where the thing goes in and not here, and the word is not on it.

The water in the south end of the yard had gone down to about two inches by the first hour and the sack was still against the door of the one room with the stone on top of it, and the trough at the top of the four-mile road had come back down inside its own lip before the light went, and nobody in this holding has written any of those three things down.

A building that has been four years old has a door that lets water in and a stone on a sack and a trough that goes over and comes back, and none of the three is a fault anybody has been asked about, and the fourth thing in that yard, which is a man with a barrow under a lean-to, has been there eleven days and is not a fault at all.

The clerk of this holding locked the one room at about the first hour and the key went in his coat and the page with the four things on it went under the second book and not into a drawer, and a page that is going to be opened in the morning is not a page that has been put away.

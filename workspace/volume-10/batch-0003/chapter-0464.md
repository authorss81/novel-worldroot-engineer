# Chapter 464

## Four Accounts Of The Arm

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour on the seventeenth of this month and was worked, and it was the two culverts on the middle road and the fill behind them and the low field gate, and the fill had gone out about four inches since the last time it was packed and four people put it back from the second hour until about the fifth.

The gate was stiff again in the two frosts and the third of them got a bar under it and lifted it while the fourth held a stone against the post, and it took nine minutes, and it has taken about nine minutes every year since the gate was hung.

The water in the middle road had come up about an inch and a half overnight and was going down at about half that by the seventh hour, and the north end of the ditch is running full and has been since the north end was cleared on Wednesday, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood on the sixth inch of the fill and it took his weight and it took it on the fourth as well this morning.

At the seventh hour the two of them said what their own side was and neither said the other side, and the run of mornings is **eighty-four of a hundred and fifty-one**. The boards stood at **six hundred and eleven days** and **six hundred and fifty-eight**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind them stayed shut.

The seventeenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and forty-eight hundredweight.**

Thursday is five under Wednesday, and this is the two hundred and twenty-seventh figure of a run that has never written a number down twice, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and two against ninety-two is a hundred and ninety-four, and nobody in this building has put the two halves of that against the figure above them.

Two hundred and twenty-five days is the standing of the four bodies of households and the clause under that figure reads the two hundred and twenty-fourth of the mornings out of two hundred and twenty-sixth, one under and one over, and it counts mornings and not hundredweight and it is neither the launder nor the window.

Ninety-five mornings of that rotation are in force, twenty-nine of them taken and sixty-six not, and the figure rises by one on a morning the fourth line comes round and by nothing on any of the other mornings, and the twenty-nine has not moved on any morning of this year.

Marek went out of the gate at about the fifth hour with nothing in his hands and walked the two and a quarter miles to the lane that comes off the middle road at the second gate, and he counted it himself, on his own boots, against the ditch posts, and the count is his own and it is not in anybody else's book.

The lane is about two hundred yards long and it ends at four buildings and about eleven acres that have never been in this holding's book under any name, and a man came out of the second one while he was still walking up it and had been in the lane before he was at the end of it.

The man is not a member of anything this holding has a word for and he has not asked to be and he is not angry and he did not say that anybody had taken anything off him. He had a barrow handle over his shoulder and he put it down against the wall when Marek got to within about ten feet of him, which is a thing a man does when he has decided not to be helpful.

He asked one question and it was not about the connection. He asked whether the schedule that came up the fen road had a place at the head of it, and Marek said that it did not and that nobody in this holding is going to write one at the head of it who has not been asked to, and the man said that was the correct answer and that he had asked it because the last four things that came up that road had a place at the head of them and the place had been wrong every time.

Nobody in this holding has written to that lane about any of the four, and the count of letters this holding has sent up that road in eleven years is not a figure anybody keeps.

**"I know who you are and I am not going to be careful about it,"** he said. **"I have read the sheet that came up the fen road. I did not send it and I am not the man who sent it and I have told nobody in this lane that I am not, and you are going to go back down that lane believing that, and I would rather it went in a book than not.**"

**"The connection is not a road."** He said it the way a man says a thing he has said to himself in the dark for a fortnight. **"A road goes out and comes back. You can be on it in the morning and on it in the afternoon and it is the same road both times and nothing has happened to it in between. That is what a road is and it is the only thing I have ever used one for.**"

**"That is not a road. It is a shape somebody drew on a piece of paper and put in a schedule, and a shape is not somewhere you can be twice. If you put a shape in the ground it is a shape in the ground for about a season and then it is not, and nobody who drew it is going to be the one who finds out.**"

Marek stood in that lane and did not answer it, and the man went and picked his barrow handle up off the wall and went back into the second building and shut the door, and the door is a door and it was shut and nobody in that lane has offered to open it again.

A sentence nobody at the seed house could have written and a sentence that is true, and the man who has spent nine volumes being the only person in four counties who can hold a pattern at either end of a connection stood in about forty yards of gravel and had nothing to say to either half of it.

He gave the reason first when he got back to the yard at about the seventh hour, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and thirty-nine, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I did not say in that lane and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been asked to hold an end of a thing since Wednesday and I walked two and a quarter miles this afternoon to be told by a man with a barrow handle that the end I am supposed to hold is not a place a man can be held at, and he is right, and I have got no standing to tell him he is wrong."** "He does not know what I am and he does not want to know and he told me so to my face, which is more than the charter has done for anybody in this county. I am not going to go back up that lane this week."

The schedule of nine loads came out of the seed house at about the seventh hour of the evening in the hands of the woman who wrote it, and it went onto the long table face up this time and it was not turned over.

It went out of this county on the ordinary post on the eleventh and it came back on the ordinary post on Tuesday morning, and the string it was tied with had been cut and retied with a different piece of string, and nothing was written on the outside of the wrapper at all, and the woman who keeps the seed house put the wrapper in the fire and did not say anything about it and was not asked.

A bundle that has been opened and closed again by somebody two days away is not the same object as a bundle that has not, and this holding has no way of finding out who did it and has not written to anybody about it, and the request that went inside the same bundle has not come back at all.

Marek put his forearm on the table with the sleeve pushed back and the schedule was put on the wood beside it about an inch away, and the man of about fifty read the whole of it out in the room, and then four people gave four accounts of what it had cost to put it there, and all four were entered.

There are four of those accounts and none of them agrees with another, which is what has happened every time four of them have been entered. The first came from the man of about thirty-one of Silling and was about the near end of a thing in his own body. The second came from the man of about fifty and was read out of the third book and was about a figure in a book. The third came from the clerk of this holding and was about the second hour of a Thursday. The fourth came from the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten, who was not asked for it and gave it once and did not give it twice.

**Four is four.** A declining is not a fifth. A schedule is not a fifth. A session is not a fifth and a session has not happened and is not in this room.

The mark is four inches with two branches off it and it was the same four inches on the last page of the evening as it was on the first page of the morning, and the four accounts are the largest single cost this holding's book has ever entered against that arm, and the arm does not carry a figure and the four of them are not on it.

Nobody in that room put a declining and a schedule and a cost into one sentence, and the two sheets on the long table are still about nine inches apart and still have nothing at the head of either of them.

The sixth rule of the schedule carries a heading at the top of it and the heading is for the things this holding does not know, and there are six rows ruled under that heading and no unit is supplied against any of the six, and the seventh row is not ruled.

A list of six things you do not know is not a plan. A schedule with a column of not knowns in it is a document that has told the truth about the length of its own reach, and it is not a thing anybody can be shown to have done or not done on a Tuesday, and the seven of them are the seven of them and no seventh row is ruled tonight and none is going to be.

The sixth not known is the same sixth not known it has been since a month now two months back and no unit has ever been supplied for any of the six, and the road is not a seventh and the lane at the second gate is not a seventh and a man with a barrow handle is not a seventh.

> THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT AT THE FOOT OF THE SEED HOUSE'S OWN SCHEDULE, IN HER HAND, WITH A SURNAME ON IT BECAUSE A DOCUMENT THAT BINDS ANYBODY HAS TO CARRY ONE: **RECEIVED INTO THIS HOLDING ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THIS MONTH. NINE LOADS. FOUR DAYS. THE SIXTH RULE OF THIS SCHEDULE IS HEADED FOR THE THINGS THIS HOLDING DOES NOT KNOW AND CARRIES SIX ROWS AND NO UNIT IS SUPPLIED AGAINST ANY OF THE SIX, AND THE SEVENTH ROW IS NOT RULED. A SCHEDULE OF NINE LOADS CARRIES TWO FIGURES THAT THIS HOLDING CANNOT CHECK AND THE FIRST OF THE TWO IS NOT NAMED. ACKNOWLEDGED: VALE.** THE SURNAME IS ON THIS LINE AND NOWHERE ELSE AND THE FIGURE WITH FOUR LINES ON IT AT THE BACK OF THIS SHEET IS NOT A COLUMN AND IS NOT GOING TO BE MADE INTO ONE, AND LINE ONE STILL CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT AND THE OTHER THREE ARE STILL EMPTY AND NO HEADING HAS BEEN WRITTEN ON IT.

The register form is on the middle table at a hundred and twenty-one days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and a schedule and an acknowledgment and four accounts of an arm are not that form, and the four of those have never been added in this holding in four years.

The well house door is nine hundred yards off and its seventh column stands at fifty-eight and is read on the thirtieth and the first and on no other morning of the year, and it was not read on the seventeenth. Beside it, the second of the six columns has four words at the head of it and nothing under them, the sixth has a heading and a count of readings, and the remaining four are headed nothing at all.

A day on the seventeenth, an acknowledgment with a surname on it and a column of six rows with no unit in any of them are not on that door, and the door is nine hundred yards off and it is dark by the time anybody in this room thinks about it.

The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the schedule went back into the seed house with the woman who wrote it, and there is no second copy of it in this building and there is one copy of the acknowledgment and the acknowledgment is a foot of paper.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the schedule went back into the seed house with the woman who wrote it, and there is no second copy of it in this building and there is one copy of the acknowledgment and the acknowledgment is a foot of paper, and the lane at the second gate went dark about an hour before this one did, and the two and a quarter miles is a figure of one man's boots and it is in no book in this building.

The frost will be back in the small hours and the fill behind the two culverts will take a week to settle and the man of about thirty-one of Silling will put a bar under the low field gate again in about a fortnight, and the fourth line of the rota comes round tomorrow and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to work it.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The seventeenth. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone, nine minutes, four people from the second hour. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour. The same day in and the same day out. Nine hundred and forty-eight hundredweight to the launder. The reader of this body walked two and a quarter miles to the lane at the second gate on his own feet and was told there that the connection is not a road, and the count of the miles is his own and is in no book in this building. The schedule of nine loads went on the long table face up and four accounts of the cost of it were given and entered and four of them do not reconcile. The acknowledgment at the foot of it carries a surname in capitals and it is the only page in this building that carries one. The sixth rule of the schedule carries six rows with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled. The mark is four inches with two branches and is the same four inches it was at the second hour.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

# Chapter 467

## The Two Bodies Are Not Merged

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the twentieth of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the near stone had a quarter of an inch of daylight under it on Friday and had none this morning, and the far stone was where two people had put it back by hand on Wednesday and had not moved since.

The outlet was about a foot and a half at the sixth hour and the ditch below it had about four inches of the night's water still standing in the bend, and the color of that water is the color of the ground it is standing on and has been since the second week of a month now two months back.

The ditch is nine hundred yards of the same ditch in both directions and it was put there to move clean water and is not doing that, and about four feet of the run above the outlet is the only part of the nine hundred that anybody in this holding looks at, and the other eight hundred and ninety-six is not looked at by anybody in this holding in four years.

The frost was about a quarter of an inch on the yard at the sixth hour and there is a place by the north wall of the lean-to where it stayed on past the eighth hour because the wall has been wet since Sunday and the wet has come up the stone about a foot, and nobody in this holding has looked at that wall since the spring.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The pair is read at the seventh hour and this morning it was not, because the man who reads the near side of it was in the one room from the sixth hour with the door shut and did not come out until the ninth, and the man who reads the far side did not read his own figure alone and nobody thought to ask a third man to do it on a Sunday.

The boards stood at **six hundred and fourteen days** and **six hundred and sixty-one**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twentieth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and fifty-nine hundredweight.**

Sunday put eight on it, and this is the two hundred and thirtieth figure of a run in which no number has been written down twice, and the rise went onto the rises. A hundred and four against ninety-three is a hundred and ninety-seven, and the two ends of that have not been added to the figure standing over them in this building in four years.

The column for the four bodies of households carries two hundred and twenty-eight days on it, and the clause printed under it is the two hundred and twenty-seventh morning out of two hundred and twenty-ninth. That is a count of mornings and it is not the launder above it and this building has never added the two together in four years.

The clerk of this holding ruled a line in the second book at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and it went under both of the two entries, and it went across the nine inches of blank page between them, and for about a minute and a half on the twentieth of this month there was one column in this building that held two declinings.

A ruled line under two entries is the oldest trick there is and this building has four years of practice at not doing it and did it on a Sunday afternoon, and the clerk of this holding is a careful man with four books and he did it because four of the people in the room were asking him to and he is the man they ask.

The line is not in the book now. The page between the two entries is blank and it has been blank for a fortnight and it is blank again, and a blank page that has been filled and emptied inside a day is a different object from a blank page that was never touched, and this building has one of the second kind now and there is no column in any of the four books that could tell anybody which was which.

Marek looked at it and said the reason first and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-two, and it is his and of nobody else's, and the clerk of this holding had a pencil in his hand and had not used it.

**"I am going to say why that line has to come out, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that on Thursday I walked two and a quarter miles to a man with a barrow handle and on Friday I walked three and a half miles to a woman with a bucket, and the two of them said two different things and neither of them could have said the other's."** "A column that holds both of them is not a column for either of them. It is a place where one of them can be produced and the other one is in the room, and in about four years somebody in a county will take something out of it and there will be no way of telling anybody afterwards which of the two it belonged to. Take the line out."

The clerk of this holding ruled it out at about the fourth hour and did not write anything at the head of it and did not draw it a second time, and the two entries are about nine inches apart again and the page between them is blank again.

Nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody in that room argued with him either, and the man of about fifty had the second book open at the page and looked at the ruled line and the unruled line and closed it, and the count of questions this holding has put to him about either of them is nil and is nil.

About four people in this room thought the line should have stayed and about four thought it should never have been ruled, and the two of those are not the same disagreement, and neither of the two fours has been asked which of them was in it.

A room that disagrees about a line for a minute and a half and then goes quiet has not settled anything and has not lost anything either, and the clerk of this holding put the pencil down flat on the table beside the book when he finished ruling it out and left it there for about a minute before he picked it up again.

He said one thing in that room, and the reason came before the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty-eight, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a column and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept four books in three counties for thirty-one years and I have ruled about four hundred lines in them, and about four of those four hundred were a column that ought not to have been a column, and I have not been able to say afterwards which of the four it was in any of the three counties."** "I am not saying he is right. I am saying that a man who has never been able to name the four cannot be expected to catch this one by looking at it, and the reason he caught it is that he walked to both of them in two days, and I did not, and I have not been asked why and I am not going to be."

The five terms of a sheet in a county two hundred miles off were not improved this afternoon and were not unwithdrawn and were not added to, and the third column of the sheet of terms in this building is empty and its heading has not been touched, and no sixth term has been added to anything in this room and none is going to be.

A term is a condition and a declining is a thing said about a condition, and this holding has two words for those and used one of them for the other of them for about four days and corrected it on Thursday in a room with the door shut, and the correction is not on any column and is not a heading and is not a term of anything and nobody was thanked for it.

A declining is not a term. A term is not a declining. A term is a condition in an instrument and a declining is a thing a body says about one, and a building that has four books and four years of practice has never put one inside the other and did not do it this afternoon.

There is a sheet on the low board, face up, with nothing entered on it and nothing withdrawn, no date, no name and no line for a name, and it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now three months back. A line ruled under two entries and then ruled out again is not a reason to take that sheet off that board, and there has not been a reason like it in this building in four years.

A thing that is refused is not withdrawn and a thing that is withdrawn is not refused, and the piece of paper on that board has been in the same condition since a month now three months back, and the count of times anybody in this building has looked at it and decided anything about it is a number nobody has kept, and the clerk of this holding dusts that end of the room on a Friday.

The two of them on the long table are the same two sheets that were on it on the fifteenth of this month and neither of them has been endorsed, copied, folded or dated by anybody in this building, and the days between the first of this month and the twentieth are the days they have been on that table and nobody has counted them twice.

One of the two has a clause at the head of it and one of them does not, and the one that does is a declining under that clause and is not an appeal and is not a delay, and the one that does not is a sheet and is not a declining under anything because there is nothing for it to be a declining under.

The second of them is unsigned. It came up the fen road in the hand of a man and nobody in this holding has asked him to sign anything and he has not offered, and a document with no name on it is still on the long table with the name not on it, and a declining is not improved by a signature and a sheet is not a declining by being signed.

Nobody in this holding has copied either of them into a third book, and the second book has them and the third book has not and the fourth book has not, and the two of them have never been in a drawer, and the drawer is where a thing goes in this building when there is no column for it and somebody has decided there is no column for it.


The man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the outlet end from the second hour until the fifth and the pack under the near stone is going in wet again and will want doing before the frost, and about a week is the ordinary answer and nobody has written it down.

He put his thumb on the head of the far stone at about the fifth hour and moved it about a sixteenth of an inch and it went back where it had been, and he looked at the mark his thumb had made in the wet clay and did not wipe it, and the clay mark was still there at the ninth hour.

The water in the middle road came up about two inches overnight and went down about one of them by the ninth hour, and the north end of the ditch is still running full, and the trough at the top of the four-mile road was full this morning for the first time since the eighth of a month now one month back, and the man who filled it was not asked and did not say.

The sheet with the shortage on it is still on the long table where it has been since before the month turned, and it has one word at the head of it and a figure and a date, and the seed on the schedule that went out on the eleventh is the seed on that sheet, and the two of them have not been added together in this room and this page puts no total under either of them.

The two of them are the only two things in this building that are about seed, and the two of them have been in the same room for a fortnight, and the count of times anybody in this holding has put one under the other is nil and is nil.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the fourth hour and the door was shut and the day was a Sunday, and the room did not do anything on Sunday that it had not done on Thursday, and nobody has worked out why it should.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was not in that room and was in the yard with the slate under his arm, and the man in the bad coat was not in that room either and was at the tap on the north side, and the two of them have still never spoken to each other and it is going to be four days on Sunday night that they have slept about nine feet apart.

The window of the office's work shut on the eleventh and does not open again until the sixth of next month, and a window that is shut is a calendar, and this room has been having conversations in it for nine days with a window shut, and nobody in this holding has connected the two of those facts and nobody has been asked to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the second book was shut with two entries and about nine inches of nothing between them, and the two sheets are on the long table, and the line is out, and nothing in this building has been settled and two things that were said no have still been said no.

The clerk of this holding put the second book back on the shelf at the place it came from, and the space on the shelf where the first declining lives when it is not on the long table has a ring on it about the size of a cup, and the ring was put there by two seasons of tables and nobody has ever cleaned it out or put anything in it.

The gate was shut at about the tenth hour and the frost will be back in the small hours, and the outlet will be about a foot and three inches by the sixth hour tomorrow, and the two stones will be looked at again by whoever is on the second line, and none of the three is a decision and none of the three has been written down.

A room in which a line was ruled and unruled in a minute and a half has still got two things on a table that are about two different things, and they are going to be on that table on Tuesday, and the two of them are not a grievance and are not one grievance and are not going to be made into one by anybody in this building.

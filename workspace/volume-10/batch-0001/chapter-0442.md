# Chapter 442

## Five Days Old

The twenty-fifth of this month was a Wednesday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The ditch on the north end was out four feet on the two new lengths and about two on the old, and it took four people from the second hour until a little after the fifth, and by then the water coming off the high field had somewhere to go that was not the lane, and the lane was passable again by the time the man of about thirty-one of Silling came down it with the barrow.

The rota stands this morning at ninety mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and sixty-one not, and twenty-nine and sixty-one are ninety, and the count rises by exactly one on a morning the fourth line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and the fourth line does not come round today. Twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited sixty-one mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular, and nobody in this holding has ever been told whose the field at the headland above Marden is.

At the seventh hour two men took a board each and read them out loud before either figure was named. **Seventy-three mornings of a hundred and twenty-nine**, counting from the seventeenth of a month four months back, and that denominator is not either of the two boards and has never been added to either. The boards themselves: **five hundred and eighty-nine days** and **six hundred and thirty-six**, forty-seven apart, the drawer shut, and it stays shut this month whatever comes up the fen road.

The twenty-fifth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same, and the hour was the hour it has been for a fortnight and the man of about fifty wrote the day against it before he wrote the hour.

**Nine hundred and fifteen hundredweight.**

Down five off the twenty-fourth. Two hundred and fifth of the series, which has not repeated itself since the morning it started, and this is the first figure the new month gets, and the count does not start again at a turn of anything.

The fall went onto the falls. Ninety-one rises and eighty-one falls make a hundred and seventy-two, and that window is four hundred and eighty-three days wide and is not the pair of boards and is not the launder, and the number the page calls *in so many days* is the same figure as the window and is not the same count, and that was said once in this holding in a month now three months back and is not going to be said again.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in two hundred and three days, and it was written for the two hundred and second of those mornings out of two hundred and four. Four bodies of households and not the launders, and the two have not been added in this building in four years and were not added this morning.

The charter was signed on the twentieth of this month in a room nine miles off, and it is five days old, and the four clauses of it are four clauses, and there has not been a morning in this building in which anybody wanted to say anything about it, and about four people here have read it since and about four have not, and neither of those two fours has been asked and neither of them is going to be.

The shortage is on the sheet with the twenty-third of this month against it and the figure of one thousand one hundred and sixty hundredweight against the word, and the sheet has one word at the head of it and that word is one word, and nobody has put a second word beside it. The number is a figure of what is not in a building. It is not a figure of a harvest, it is not a figure of a ground, and it is not a figure of people, and nobody in this holding has said any of those four things this morning and one of them is going to have to be said before the month is out and this building has not chosen which.

He came up the fen road at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with a roll under his arm and the strap of the roll across his chest, and he was on foot. There was no cart and no bicycle. The mud on the outside of his left boot was the same color as the mud about two hundred yards down the road from the second gate, which is the mud of the low ground and not of the road, and nobody in this building saw where he came from and nobody asked.

He stood in the yard about four minutes before he knocked. The woman who keeps the seed house came out with her sleeves rolled and put a cloth down by his boots the way she does, without saying anything about it, and it stayed on the flags by the door after he had gone and nobody picked it up.

He said he had walked it. He said the name of the road as though it were an ordinary thing with a name, and the clerk of this holding wrote it down in the second book in the ordinary way, and the name is two words and one of them is a number, and the clerk wrote the number as a word and not as a figure, which is the way this building writes a name.

> THE LIST, AS IT CAME, ON ONE SHEET OF ROUGH PAPER FOLDED IN THREE, IN THE HAND OF A MAN WHO HAD WALKED THE ROUTE AND HAD WRITTEN IT ON THE ROAD AND NOT IN A ROOM: **TEN. THE LIST IS NUMBERED FROM ONE TO TEN BY THE MAN WHO WROTE IT AND THE NUMBERING IS HIS AND NOT ANYBODY ELSE'S. FOUR OF THE TEN ARE NAMED AS A PLACE BY THE NAME THE LOCAL PEOPLE USE AND SIX ARE NAMED AS WHAT THE THING IS. NO DATE IS ON IT. NO NAME IS ON IT. NO BODY IS ON IT. NO COLUMN HEADING IS ON IT. THE PAPER IS ROUGH AND IS NOT THE PAPER OF THIS BUILDING.**

That is the whole of what he brought. He did not offer an explanation of it and nobody in this building asked him for one, and about four people have said since that a man who walks a road and comes up four miles with what he saw is doing a kindness, and about four have said that a kindness does not have to explain itself either, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

The clerk put it on the long table at about the fifth hour with a weight on the corner of it, and it lay flat, and nobody turned it over, and the back of it is the back of it. The man of about fifty came in from the yard about ten minutes after and looked at it for a while without touching it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came in behind him with the barrow's handle still wet and did not look at it at all and went straight out to the sluice.

Eight of the ten are in this building's book. The clerk went through four books between the fifth hour and the seventh and put a small mark in the margin beside eight of them, and he did the eight first, and the two at the end took him a quarter of an hour and he did them last.

The man of about fifty asked him which two and the clerk said he would find out in the morning and would not say out loud what he had not finished doing, and the man of about fifty did not ask him a second time. The count of times this holding has asked the clerk of this holding a second question about the same sheet is one, and it is this holding's count and not the clerk's.

Nobody in this building knows what this man is. He is not a member of any of the four bodies of households, and this holding is not a member of anything and has no charter at all, and he did not say which body he was standing for, and nobody in this building thought to ask him, and about four people here have noticed that the sheet is on the long table and about four have not.

The man of about fifty gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history moved on this morning and is not written on this page, and it belongs to him and to nobody else.

**"I am going to say the one thing about that sheet and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that this holding keeps four books and has had a list on a table for about two hours and has not written a heading on anything, and a list without a heading is the exact thing the sixth column of the door was for five years."** "I am not saying put a heading on it. I am saying that a man walked a road in order to hand us a piece of paper, and the least this building can do is find out tomorrow which two of the ten are not in the books, and say so out loud in a room, and not write a word on the sheet itself, because the sheet is his and the heading would be ours and the two of them would then be one thing and it is not one thing."

He was asked, by the woman who keeps the seed house, on his way out at the door, whether the road was open the whole way.

**"Open is not the word I would use for it and I am not the man to use a word for it."** He said it standing in the doorway with the roll already under his arm again. **"It is a road. Four of the ten are not there and the six that are will carry what you put on them and no more, and I have walked it twice this year and I would walk it again in this weather, and that is a thing about my legs and not about your road."**

Nobody thanked him. Nobody in this holding thanked him, and he did not ask to be thanked, and about four people in this holding have said that a man who walks a road and does not want anything for it is the rarest thing on the road, and about four have said that about four people in this holding have never walked it and one of them has never been further than the node and that one of them is the engineer of record. Neither of the two fours has been asked and the engineer of record was not asked and is not going to be.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at about the ninth hour of the morning and went down it loaded about the eleventh, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one's own book is ninety-three days old and has nothing on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in ninety-three days and nobody is going to this month.

It came back up with the same barrow and about four of the nine sacks, and the other five were left at a gate four miles off by a man who is not in this holding and whose name is not on any page in this building. It has been eleven journeys up that road since before anybody in this holding started counting, and eleven is a floor, and it was not raised this morning and it was not raised in the afternoon when a stranger walked up it.

Marek had been in the one room the whole of the time the sheet was on the table and had not been in it when the man was in the doorway, and he came in from the pump at about the eighth hour of the evening with his hands cold and stood at the long end of it and read the ten twice.

He gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and twenty, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a road I have to say and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the only person this holding could name against a ground for eleven years, and a road is not a ground and I have never been able to keep the two apart in my own head, and today a man handed us a piece of paper about ten things and eight of them are in the books and I want to go and look at the eight before I say one word about the two."** "I am not saying the road is not a ground. I am saying I do not get to be the man who decides what a road is on the strength of a scar on my arm, and a man walked it in order to find that out and he did not ask me anything, and that is the correct order and I have got it in the wrong order for about nine years."

Nobody in that room said that the engineer of record had just described the whole of his own difficulty and nobody in that room has said it since, and about four people in this holding have written it down and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man was not asked.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-nine days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and the form is about nine feet of floor from the long table and nobody has moved either thing toward the other, and about four people in this holding have said a list and a form are the same kind of object and about four have said they are not, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, and a list of ten is not that instrument and there is no second instrument of that offer on any table in this holding this season and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board.

In the drawer there is a sheet that claims a corridor in the pan. It is correct in every particular it states and it has been correct for a long time. A road with ten things written on it is not that corridor and that corridor is not that road, and the two have not been added together in this building or in this holding in four years.

PAID, one hundred pounds, and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one, and the word before the figure has never once been wrong. It is not a count of ten and it is not a count of anything, and no one has added it to a list this month or intends to.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is in its eleventh year and no person in this holding has ever set foot in it and no person did this morning. It has no dimension against it on any page in four counties. The ground either side of it is going wrong and is going slower than it was in the ninth, and this building has never said that slower is better and has never said that it is not.

The scar on the left forearm is four inches long and it forks twice, and it was four inches at the seventh hour and it was four inches at the second and the two facts are the same fact written down twice. It is not a list. It is not a shortage. It is not a charter and it is not a road and it is not a stranger's kindness. Four accounts of that arm are kept in this building and they have never reconciled with one another, and the five subjects of this afternoon are not a fifth of them.

# Chapter 445

## Three Hundred And Ninety-Three

Saturday, the twenty-eighth, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away with nobody on it, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it. Those fields have now waited for the sixty-second morning running.

The rota stands at ninety-one mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and sixty-two not, and twenty-nine and sixty-two are ninety-one.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited sixty-two mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular, and nobody in this holding has ever been told whose the field is.

This building keeps two reckonings of that rotation and they disagree, so the page carries both. This morning is the thirty-ninth morning of the first and the twenty-sixth of the second. Thirteen is the gap between them, thirteen has been the gap for four years, and no one has ever found the thirteen.

There is no figure for the run of mornings here. The pair of boards is read at the seventh hour on alternate mornings and not on this one, and the reason is that both men who read them are the two men putting the room's business to bed and neither was in the building before the ninth hour. The count on that board is seventy-four of a hundred and thirty-one and it will be read on Sunday and not on this morning, and that is the whole of the matter.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and ninety-two days** and **six hundred and thirty-nine**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-eighth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same, and the hour was late and the man of about fifty wrote the hour before he wrote the day for once, and the clerk did not correct it and nobody has said anything about it.

**Nine hundred and twenty-six hundredweight.**

Up eight off Friday, and the two hundred and eighth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and it is a different eight from the one before it and nobody in this building has said why and nobody is going to be asked.

The rise went onto the rises. Ninety-three rises and eighty-two falls make a hundred and seventy-five.

What the sheet says about the four bodies of households is a day and not a figure, and this morning's day is two hundred and six, and it is written for the two hundred and fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and seven.

The steelyard is at the far end of the one room and it has been at the far end of the one room for longer than the register form has been on the middle table, and it was weighed at about the tenth hour and the sheet of the forty-first return went on the board and the man of about fifty read the figure and did not read the figure out loud and wrote it.

**Three hundred and ninety-three hundredweight.**

Up eleven off the fortieth, and the forty-first of the ninth-day returns, and the thirty-first pair of names in eleven years on the two small cards at the head of the sheet, and the two cards were reconciled twice and the two halves of that reconciliation have not been added together and are not going to be.

The second rule on the board was left blank. It has now been left blank twenty-five times in eleven years and the count of blanks is not the count of a man, and the man whose name would go in the second rule is not named in this building and has not been named in this building in four years and the count of times this holding has asked him is nil and is nil.

There are two small cards at the head of the return sheet and they are not this building's doing. One of them was written by the man of about thirty-one of Silling and one of them was written by the man of about fifty, and the two of them put the same two numbers on the same two lines and the two of them did not copy each other, and the two hands are the two hands and nobody in this building has ever asked either of them why. The clerk reconciled the cards twice, once at the reading and once at about the first hour, and the two reconciliations were the same, and the two halves of a reconciliation are two handwritings and are not one figure and have not been added to each other in four years.

The clerk of this holding had the sheet of rough paper and the finding in front of him again and had gone back through the fourth book overnight, which nobody asked him to do and which he has not said he did and about four people in this holding have said he did and about four have not.

He had found the year. It is written in the same hand as the four words, at the top of the page and not in the line with them, and it is a year and not a season, and he entered it in the second book at about the tenth hour under the finding, and nobody in the one room said anything about it and nobody asked him whether he was sure.

There were about nine people in the one room at about the second hour of the afternoon with the door shut and it was a Saturday and about half of the nine had come because of the return and about half had come because of the paper, and nobody in this building has ever written down which half was which.

The man of about fifty had the second book open on the long table and he did not read anything out of it and he did not turn any page over, and he stood at the head of the table for about a minute and then took his hand off the book and said the thing that this holding says in this room about once a season, and this was the once, and he gave the reason before he said it.

**"I am going to say the rules of this book out loud and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that a man walked a road and gave us ten lines and we have found two things wrong with them in three days, and both of the two things were not somebody being wicked, and if we do not write down what a thing has to be before it can be called a question, then in four years somebody will find a piece of paper with a heading on it and believe it, and that is what happened with the sixth column and it took five years."** "A defect is not a question. A document is not a question. A corridor being named is not an answer. A heading is not an answer. A road is not a route. A count of structures is not a count of days. And a mark is not a name, and a name is not a witness, and a man who comes up four miles on his boots is a witness to his own feet and to nothing else and that is not nothing."

Nobody in that room wrote it down. It was said out loud in a room with the door shut and it is not in the second book and it is not in the first book and it is not on the middle table and about four people in this holding have said that it should have been written down and about four have said that it has been said out loud in a room in this building about nine times in four years and has not been written down once, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man of about fifty was not asked and has not been asked.

The count of questions open against this holding is thirty-five. It was thirty-five on the thirtieth of the ninth and it is thirty-five this morning and the man of about fifty said so in the same breath as the rules and did not name any of the thirty-five and did not tally them and did not say that he was not tallying them, and nobody in that room asked him for the thirty-five and nobody in that room had the list.

Marek was in the room and had the tenth line of the rough paper in front of him and had **CARRIES NINE FEET IN A BAD WEEK** written in the clerk's hand above it, and he said one thing and gave the reason for it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and twenty-two, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what a ford is in this holding's terms and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have spent four years being asked to put a figure on grounds and I have never once been asked to put a figure on a place where water goes."** "A ford is not a structure. It is not on the list of ten as a thing anybody can inspect and nobody should ever have written it as one. And I am the man who would have said that in a room nine days ago, and I did not say it, and the man who said it on Thursday was a man I have never met who had walked it in the rain. That is not a thing I get to be modest about. That is a thing this holding has got wrong for four years and a stranger found in three days."

The clerk of this holding said the one thing he said all afternoon and he said it without being asked and nobody in that room knew until afterward that he had been thinking about it since Wednesday.

**"I am the clerk of this holding. I am not going to say that a piece of paper has come, because a piece of paper has come every week of my working life and half of them have been about money."** He had his hand flat on the rough sheet. **"I am going to say that a man came up this road on his boots and stood in my yard for four minutes before he knocked, and that I have been thinking about the four minutes for two days, and that in four years I have never once seen anybody wait to knock in this yard, and that I do not know what I think that means and I am putting it in the book anyway, because the book is where a thing goes when the man holding it does not know what it is."**

The clerk read that back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody in that room asked him a question about it, and about four people in this holding have said that it is the only sentence anybody in this building has written this month that was not about a figure and about four have said that a man of fifty-eight has no business calling himself a man of fifty-eight on a Saturday, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the clerk was not asked.

The stranger was not in the room and does not live in this county and nobody in this holding has his address, and nobody thanked him. The letter from the third county is still in the drawer unanswered, and the answer that has gone back so far is one sentence about four books and a column with nothing at the head of it.

Four miles up and four miles down, once, at about the fourth hour, and the number of journeys is not a thing anybody in this building has raised. A line in a small book has been blank for ninety-six days and has stayed blank.

He came into the one room at about the fourth hour and stood at the back of it for about eleven minutes without saying anything. Nobody in this holding has asked him anything in that line and the count of those questions is nil. About four people here think he came for something and about four think a man stands at the back of a room to be in it, and neither of the two fours was asked.

He left at about the fourth hour and went down to the barrow and put it away under the lean-to and came back in and washed to the elbow, and the woman who keeps the seed house had a cloth out before he had asked, and about four people in this holding have noticed that and about four have not.

The form has been on the middle table for a hundred and two days. A hundred and two is not a round number and the difference from a hundred and one is that nothing happened, and nothing at the head of that column, and nobody in this building has said anything about any of the three and nobody is going to.

It is still face up on the low board with nothing filled in and nothing withdrawn and no date and no line for a name. A return on a steelyard is not that instrument. A set of rules said out loud in a room is not that instrument. There is no second instrument of that offer on any table in this holding this season.

The drawer with the sheet claiming the corridor in the pan has not been opened this week and the sheet has not been reworded and the corridor has not been closed and has not been opened. A road is not a route, a route is not a corridor, and about four people in this holding would like the three of them added together and about four would not and neither pair has been asked.

The compost line is paid at one hundred pounds and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one. A return on a steelyard is not a measurement of the ground. Neither is a count of blanks. Neither is a sentence said out loud in a room and not written down. The three of them have never been added together here in four years.

There is a day sheet in a stack on the shelf behind the middle table that has no date at the head of it, and it is in the order it was used, and nobody has moved it to the top of the stack and nobody in this holding has been asked to and nobody is going to be. The pulse has two figures in it and no name against either of them and there is no unit and it was not read this morning and nobody asked for it and the count of times a body in this holding has asked about those two figures is nil and is nil.

The bare ring inside the eleven acres is eleven years old this month and nobody in this holding has entered it, on this morning or on any other. It carries no measurement on any page a county could check. What is around it is still failing and is failing more slowly than it was, and this building has put nobody's name to that word slower and has been asked by nobody to.

It is not read on this morning, and the reason is the rule and not the weather: the seventh column of the well house door is read on the thirtieth and the first and on no other day. It stands at fifty-six. The sixth column beside it has a heading on it and an empty space under that, and a heading on the sixth is not a heading on the seventh, and a rule said out loud in a room is not a heading on anything at all.

Nobody thanked anybody. The sheet of the forty-first return went on the pinboard at about the eleventh hour and the second rule on it was blank and the card with the two names on it was at the head of it and reconciled and the two names were in two hands and nobody in that room said anything about the two hands and nobody has since.

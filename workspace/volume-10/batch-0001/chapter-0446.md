# Chapter 446

## Nine Years On A Road

The first line came round at the second hour and was worked, and it was a Sunday, and six of them were on the headland embankment above Marden and the turf had come up in sheets about the size of a door and the six of them had it all in and rolled by a little after the fourth hour because the ground was wet enough to work and not wet enough to be worth stopping for.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was one of the six and he worked without a word from the second hour to the fourth, and Marek was one of the six for the first hour and then went down to the pump, and nobody said anything about the man going down and nobody has said anything about it since.

The embankment is nine feet at the shoulder and it is eleven years old at the top and four at the toe, and about four feet of the toe had gone in the winter before last and had been put back as a bank and had not held, and the six of them cut a new toe about a yard and a half out and took the turf off in sheets and laid the sheets upside down on the crown the way a man does when he is not thinking about it, and the woman who keeps the seed house came up at about the third hour with a basket and left it at the top of the slope and went down again, and nobody thanked her for it and she did not come back up.

A county clerk came through at about the third hour on a bicycle and stopped at the gate and asked whether anybody had come up the fen road on Wednesday with a roll under his arm, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said yes, and the clerk wrote that down on the back of his hand, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said that the man had gone down the fen road on foot and that he was not from this county, and the county clerk said that the county would find out, and rode on. Nobody in this holding has told the man of about thirty-one of Silling that he was asked and nobody is going to.

At the seventh hour the pair was read out loud, one man to each, before a single figure was named. **Seventy-five of a hundred and thirty-three.** Then the boards: **five hundred and ninety-three days** and **six hundred and forty**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer stayed shut, and the frame of the well house nine hundred yards off is a fourth thing and not a fifth and has never been added to the other three in this building.

The twenty-ninth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and twenty-one hundredweight.**

Down five off Saturday. It is the two hundred and ninth of a series that has not come round to the same figure since it began, and the fall went on the falls, and ninety-three and eighty-three make a hundred and seventy-six.

The four bodies of households are written down as not having moved in two hundred and seven days, the morning under it being the two hundred and sixth out of two hundred and eight. Four households is not four launders. The sheet has never confused the two and did not confuse them on the first of a month, which is a morning when a great many things in this building might have been confused.

The clerk of this holding had the rough sheet of paper on the long table and the finding under it and the year under that, and the man from the road was not there and had not been there since Friday evening and nobody in this building has a way of getting word to him, and about four people in this holding have said that this is a fault in the holding and about four have said that it is a fault in the road, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

A sheet came in on the ordinary post at about the eighth hour and it was from nobody this holding knows and it was two lines long and both of the lines were about a body.

> THE SECOND SHEET, AS IT CAME, IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE CLERK'S AND IS NOT ANY HAND THIS BUILDING HAS SEEN: **TO THE HOLDING THAT KEEPS FOUR BOOKS AT THE COOPERATIVE WELL. WE HAVE HEARD OF THE SHEET OF TEN. WE ARE NOT A BODY OF HOUSEHOLDS AND WE ARE NOT A COUNCIL AND WE HAVE NO CHARTER. WE HAVE CARRIED SEED ON THAT ROAD SINCE THE YEAR BEFORE THE CHARTER OF THE WELL DIRECTORATE WAS WRITTEN. WE HAVE NEVER ASKED ANYBODY TO CARRY IT AND WE HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED WHETHER WE WOULD. WE ARE NOT COMPLAINING AND THIS IS NOT A COMPLAINING SHEET. WE ARE WRITING SO THAT A COLUMN MIGHT HAVE SOMETHING IN IT.** IT IS NOT SIGNED BY A PERSON. IT IS NOT DATED. IT CARRIES THE NAME OF A BODY AND A BODY IS NOT A PERSON.

The clerk read it out in the one room at about the ninth hour and there were six people there and nobody asked who it came from and nobody can find out, and about four people in this holding have said that a body which has carried seed on a road for nine years and cannot be found by post is not a body anybody has heard of and about four have said that it is the first thing in four years that has come into this building that nobody here has heard of, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

The name of it was in the second paragraph of the second line, and it is two words, and the clerk of this holding entered it in the second book at about the ninth hour in his own hand, and he entered it as what he was told and not as what he believes, and he said so out loud before he wrote it, and the count of that in this holding's history is his and of nobody else's and nobody in that room has written it down.

The name is the Undercommons.

It is the first time the name has been on a page in this building. It has never been said in this room, and it is not a council and it is not one of the four bodies of households, and this holding is not a member of it and none of the four bodies of households is a member of it, and there is no roll anywhere in this building on which any of that could be entered, because this building keeps four books and not one of the four is a roll.

Nobody in that room said thank you and nobody said anything at all for about a minute. About four people in this holding have said the minute was too long and about four have said it was not long enough, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

What the sheet does not say was read out as well as what it says, because the clerk read the whole of it twice and the second reading was to find the things that were not in it, and there were four of them and he named them and the room heard them.

**"There is no number of people in it. There is no number of loads. There is no date. There is no person. And there is no word in it that says anybody is to do anything, which I want to say out loud before somebody in this room goes up that road and comes back with a scheme."** He put his finger on the second line. **"It says they have carried seed. It does not say how much. It does not say for whom. It does not say what happens if they stop, and I want that written in the book as a thing that is not said, because the not said is the part of it that matters and the said part is nine years old and everybody in four counties already knows it."**

The man of about fifty said the clerk was right and gave no reason, and the count of reasons given first in this holding's history did not move on that account, and the man has said in this building that a reason is not owed to a thing a person agrees with and the count of that is his and of nobody else's and nobody has asked him to say it again.

Marek gave the reason first. The count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and twenty-three and it is his.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a name coming onto a page and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that in nine years I have never been able to say the name of anybody who maintains a thing I do not maintain, and this morning a piece of paper has told me the name of a body of people, and it has told me in writing, and it has told me they did not ask."** "I am not going up that road this week. There is no reason to go up that road this week and I have been looking for a reason since Wednesday. I am going to write back one line, and the line is going to ask them for the year they started, because a body that says the year before a charter was written has told me the only fact about itself that anybody can check, and the year is a day count and not a season and I can put a day count in a column."

He wrote the line at about the tenth hour and it went on the ordinary post in the afternoon, and it is one line and it is a question and not a request and the requests column does not move and is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and the two are not the same list and have never been added to one another.

There is a request in the book from Friday, from a body that has a heading on its own paper, asking this holding to certify a figure for the maintenance of the road, and the figure asked for is a figure of days and there is no figure of days anywhere in this holding for a road, and the man who keeps the use log entered the request on Tuesday and the log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and about four people in this holding have said that a request is not a use and about four have said that the fifteen lines are for work and this was not work, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man who keeps the log was not asked and has not been thanked for not being asked.

The answer that went back on Friday was in the clerk's hand and it is one sentence and it says that this holding keeps four books and no column with a heading on it, and that is the whole of the answer, and nobody in the body that wrote in has said that it was insufficient and nobody in this holding has said that it was sufficient.

It was the seventh hour when the barrow turned at the top of the four miles and it was loaded when it came back down. Nobody has counted that up any higher than eleven. The man of about thirty-one of Silling has had a second line in his own book open and empty for ninety-seven days now.

The woman who keeps the seed house said one thing about the letter at about the second hour of the afternoon, to the man of about thirty-one of Silling, at the wall side of the seed house, and it was not said in a room and it was not said in a yard.

**"Nine years is a long carrying and I have been in this building thirty-one and I never knew. That is not a complaint about them. I want to be exact with you, because I keep four accounts and I have never once been inexact. I am saying that there has been a road in my life that I did not know about, and I am saying it out loud so that the two of us are not the kind of people who find out from a piece of paper and then say they always knew."**

Nobody in this holding has repeated what she said to anybody outside the seed house and nobody has asked her about it and about four people in this holding have said that it was the correct thing to say to that particular man on that particular afternoon and about four have said that it should have been said to the clerk first, and neither of the two fours has been asked and she was not asked and has not been asked since.

The register form is on the middle table at a hundred and three days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and the name of a body is not that form and a sheet of paper is not that form, and there has never been a heading at the head of the register form and there is not going to be one this month.

Nothing has been filled in on the low board and nothing has been withdrawn, and there is still no date on it and still no line for a name. A body with no charter is not that instrument. A request for a certified figure is not that instrument. No second instrument of that offer is on any table in this holding this season.

A road that a body has carried seed on for nine years is not the corridor in the pan, and the corridor in the pan is not that road, and the sheet in the drawer that claims the corridor is correct in every particular it states. Those two things have not been added to one another anywhere in this holding in four years and were not added this morning at the ninth hour when a name went into the second book.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, being read tomorrow on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and six of the blank columns beside it are headed nothing and the sixth of them has a heading and nothing under it, and a name of a body is not a heading on any of the seven.

Twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one, one hundred pounds, PAID first. A year is not a measurement of that line. A name written on a page is not a measurement of anything at all. The two of those have never been added to it in this holding in four years and this morning is no different from the other four hundred and ninety-eight mornings.

The sixth of the six blank columns on the well house door took a heading in a month now one month back and it takes a count of readings and it is empty from the top of it to the bottom of it, and the seven of the columns are a different matter from a column in a book and this holding has never put the two in the same sentence and is not going to.

Four inches and two forks, and the same four inches at the fourth hour as at the second, and nothing put on it today. A body is not put on it and a year is not put on it and a name on a page is not put on it and a piece of paper is not put on it. Four accounts of that arm are kept here, unreconciled, and what the Undercommons letter said at the ninth hour is not a fifth account of anything at all.

Nobody thanked anybody. The one line went on the ordinary post at about the fourth hour of the afternoon in an envelope with nothing written on the outside of it, because this building does not put a sender's name on an envelope when it does not have a heading for the thing it is asking, and the clerk of this holding has never done it any other way and has not been asked to and is not going to be.

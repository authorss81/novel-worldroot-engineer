# Chapter 486

## About Nine Hours

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the ninth of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the near stone had a quarter of an inch of daylight under it on the first of this month and has had none since the fourth, and the far stone has not moved since the twenty-eighth of a month now two months back.

Four men were on it from the second hour until about the fifth and the water coming through it was about the same as it was on Wednesday and about four inches more than it was on the first, and it is a working outlet and it is doing what an outlet does and nobody in this building has ever written a figure against it.

The pack under the near stone has gone back in wet and will want doing before the frost is into it, and a week is the ordinary answer, and it has not been written down anywhere in this building, and nobody here has ever asked the man who put that pack in what a week is to him.

It was not raining this morning. It had stopped on Wednesday afternoon and had not started again and the yard was dry at the second hour and dry at the ninth, and the only water on this ground this morning was water that came off a face four miles off, which is a thing nobody in this building could do anything about with a spade.

The run of mornings was said out loud at the seventh hour by the man who reads the near side of the boards, and the far side was said by the man who reads the far side, and **ninety-five mornings of a hundred and seventy-three** is what the two of them said between them.

The pair of boards stands at **six hundred and thirty-three days** and **six hundred and eighty**, forty-seven apart at every row, and the drawer was not opened.

The ninth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and eighty-one hundredweight.**

It came in five under Thursday and that is the two hundred and forty-ninth figure of a run that has never written the same number down twice, and it went onto the falls. Behind it a hundred and thirteen of one kind of morning stands against a hundred and three of the other, which is a hundred and sixteen hundred, and the two ends of that have not been set against the figure standing over them by anybody in this building in four years.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and forty-seven days this morning on the sheet, and the clause below the figure is the two hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings out of two hundred and forty-eighth, a day short of the figure and a day over the clause, and a morning is not an hour and a morning is not a cart.

About the second hour the two men went onto the face at the narrow place with a bar and a post and a wedge and a barrow of chalk and stone, and they went onto it because the man of about thirty-one of Silling was standing on the shelf at the fourth hour yesterday and had put his hand on the clay and had said what the ditch was doing, and he was the one who decided it this morning and nobody told him to.

About the fourth hour the face let go.

It did not make a noise that carried. It went about four feet down the near end of the shelf and about two feet of it came out onto the road below, and the chalk that came down was the color of the frost that was on it at the sixth hour and the two of them cannot be told apart once either of them is on a road.

It was about four feet of bank at the shelf and about two of the face under it, and it was nine feet high at one place on that stretch and about four at the shelf, and nobody in this county has ever been asked for a figure of either and the two of them are the same face and they are not the same thing to a cart.

The eighth cart was not on the shelf. It had come off on Thursday at the fifth hour and had gone back to the mouth of the cut, and that was a decision somebody had taken and it is not this page's business to say who.

The two men who were on the face are not a body and not a council and not the Undercommons and not anybody that is on anything, and the money for the post and the wedge and the barrow of chalk and stone comes from a holding two miles on whose books this holding has never seen, and they have been on that face since a day nobody there can date.

There is no notice of them on that road and no flag and nobody in four counties had told them to be there, and the post is one post and the wedge is one wedge and the wedge at the top of the face has no post under it and that is the one that comes out first.

The ninth cart was at the mouth of the cut with its sheets over it and about three feet of water across the made road in front of it, and it stood there until about the eleventh hour and did not move, and nobody on it got down and nobody came off it and nobody in this building has been to see it.

The clearing took about nine hours, from the second hour to about the eleventh, with barrows and a bar and a wedge and two men and one shovel between them and about forty feet of road to open, and nobody in four counties could have made it go faster and nobody in four counties has been asked why it took what it took.

At about the seventh hour the man of about fifty was in the one room with his own book lying shut on the table in front of him and he did not open it. Nobody sent him to the narrow place and he did not go, and the count of questions this holding has put to him about a face four miles off is nil and is nil.

He has said in this room that this building keeps the numbers that come back and not the ones that go, and the ninth cart is a number that goes, and there is a figure for it in the seed house's schedule and there is no figure for it here, and the two have never been added.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at about the seventh hour and wrote the day in at about the ninth and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and there is no page in that book about a face at a narrow place and there is not going to be one, and the book is four bookfuls of figures and not one of them is a face.

The seed in the ninth cart is nine sacks and it is roped on over the sides of a cart that was not built for it, and it has been on that cart since Wednesday, and it has not been under anything this morning and nobody on that road has been under anything this morning.

Nobody fought anybody. There is nothing in this to fight about: it is clay over chalk and about a foot of rain in four days, and a cut that has been coming down in one place or another for longer than this holding has kept any book about a road.

A barrow's width of chalk coming down a face is not a battle and is not a quarrel and is not a difference between two bodies, and nobody in four counties has written it down as any of those three and this page does not either.

At about the eighth hour the woman who coordinates the moving reserves came down the coast road on foot from the mouth of the cut, which is two miles, and asked the man of about thirty-one of Silling whether the ninth cart could be up and through before the light went.

He said it could not. He said it in about nine words and he said why, and the why was that the shelf had four feet of the face gone off it and would want chalk packed under the lip before anything stood on it, and that packing takes about four hours by two men and there were not four hours.

Nobody had asked him. Nobody in four counties had standing to ask him and nobody has claimed standing since, and the count of people who have told him he ought to have done otherwise is not a figure anybody keeps, and the count of people who have told him he was right is not a figure anybody keeps either.

He is the one who took it, and this page does not say it was right and does not say it was wrong, and the nine carts and the four days and the day over are three numbers in four counties and none of them has been reconciled with any other.

Nobody has said to him since that it was the right call and nobody has said that it was the wrong one, and a decision taken by a person who has to be the one who took it is still a decision and not a reason for anybody else to stop arguing.

Marek was at the mouth of the cut from about the fourth hour to about the ninth and went up the road twice at about the sixth and about the seventh and stood on the shelf both times with a bar and did not pick it up.

The clay is about two feet thick over chalk, and after four days of wet the clay stops holding and the chalk under it comes out into the road. That is a cut doing what a cut does, and it has been doing it since before the youngest man driving it was born, and nobody in four counties is at fault.

He could read it from about four hundred yards and he could read where it would go next, and the two men on it could not, and about half of what he read on that face was that the wedge at the top had no post under it and that everybody who has ever shored that stretch knew that and had done it anyway.

He gave the reason first at the mouth of the cut at about the ninth hour with the chalk gone out of his coat and the water going past his boots, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and fifty-one, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about that face and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I got to the top of it at about the sixth hour and could have told either of those two men which part of it was going before either of them knew, and I did not, because the man standing on the shelf with a bar is the man standing on the shelf with a bar."** "That is not modesty and I would like it written down as not modesty. If I had said it he would have had to either take it or refuse it, and either way he would have had to be the one who decided, and he was already going to be the one who decided and it was his to decide. A man who can hold a pattern at either end of a thing is not a reason to take the thing off a man at one end of it."

Nobody in this building has an instrument that could tell whether the far end of a thing is on or off, and it has not been said to be on by anybody in this holding and it is not said to be on this evening, and a face nine feet high at a place where there is one shelf is not an instrument and is not on either of the two lists of people there is.

Nine hours of a shut road four miles off has not moved one thing in this room that anybody can put a date against, and the ground here took an inch of rain in the night and gave it back by the ninth hour the same as any other night this week.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and thirty-seven days old this morning and has nothing on it, and the man who keeps that book has stood on a shelf for nine hours today and has not written anything on it tonight and was not asked to by anybody in this building.

Nine hours is a figure that went into this page and it did not go into any of the four books, and the count of figures handed out by this holding that it cannot check is two and is two and the first of the two is not named, and nine hours is not a third of them.

The man in the cart shed is still in the cart shed. This holding has not been and is not going to be, and nobody in this building has asked him whether he heard anything about nine miles off and nobody is going to, and his name is not in this building and is not going to be put in it.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart and had a cup set between them to keep the rain off, and neither of them was moved and neither of them was improved and a cup is not a weight on a sheet and nobody in this room called it one.

There will be frost before light again and there is none of it on the yard this evening. Two inches came up in the middle road in the night and one of them had gone by the ninth hour. The north end of the ditch is still full and has been since Saturday.


Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any one of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and nine hours is not a seventh and a man standing on a shelf is not a seventh and a road that is shut is not a seventh.

The woman who coordinates the moving reserves went back up the coast road at about the ninth hour and did not say whether she thought it had been the right call and was not asked, and she has said once this week that a plan is a plan and not a good plan and she has not said that about a decision.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was at the far mouth of the cut and had come back down about two miles of it on foot to see what was happening at the other end, and he got to the mouth of the cut at about the tenth hour and stood there about twenty minutes and went back up again in the dark.

He was not asked what he thought and he did not say. The tenth of the ten is a ford about a mile and a half past the far mouth on a spur road, and nobody in this holding has asked him this week whether that spur had water on it, and he has not offered.

The ninth-day returns are every ninth morning and tomorrow is the ninth and has not been weighed, and nobody in this building has written tomorrow down anywhere except on the board, and the fortieth and the forty-first of them and the one after that have all been eleven hundredweight above the one before and nobody here has asked why eleven.

There is a day sheet with no date at the head of it in a stack of about a foot and a half behind the fourth book in the order it was used, and about four hundred sheets have gone onto the top of that stack in four years, and the sheet from today went on the top of it at about the ninth hour with a face four miles off written on it in the ordinary place.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding put the day's figure into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and by the eleventh hour about forty feet of a coast road was open with a barrow's width of chalk still coming down the face onto it, and the ninth cart had not moved and two men had gone nine hours without eating and nobody in this building has written any of that down.

# Chapter 482

## One Cart At A Time

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the fifth of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the drain off the back of the lean-to, and the drain had backed up about four feet of its own length with leaf and gravel since before the frost and had been pushing water across the south end of the yard since Friday afternoon without anybody in this building saying so out loud.

Two men were on it from the second hour until about the fifth, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling pulled the whole of it out with a bar and a bucket and did it in about nine minutes and then stood at the tap and let it run, which is a thing he does and which nobody in this holding has ever asked him about.

The outlet end itself was sound and stayed sound all day. The two stones at the head of it had a quarter of an inch of daylight under the near one on the first of this month and have none this morning, and the far stone has not moved since the twenty-eighth of a month now two months back, and the pack under the near stone has gone back in wet and will want doing before the frost is into it, and a week is the ordinary answer to that and it has not been written down anywhere in this building.

The weather came in from the west before the light was up and it was the first rain with any weight in it since the twenty-seventh of a month now two months back, and it went on for most of the day in the way that gets into chalk instead of the way that runs off it.

At the seventh hour the run of mornings was said out loud, one man to a figure, and neither of the two said the other half of it.

**Ninety-three mornings of a hundred and sixty-nine.**

The boards carry **six hundred and twenty-nine days** and **six hundred and seventy-six**, forty-seven apart at every row they have ever been read on, and the drawer behind them was not opened and is not opened in this building in a year.

The fifth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and seventy-five hundredweight.**

Five under Sunday, and the two hundred and forty-fifth figure of a run that has never written the same number down twice since the morning it began, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and eleven rises against a hundred and one falls is a hundred and twelve hundred behind it, and nobody in this building has set those two ends against the figure standing over them.

The four bodies of households have not moved, and this morning's sheet puts that at two hundred and forty-three days with the two hundred and forty-second of those mornings written under it out of two hundred and forty-fourth, one short and one over, and mornings are not carts and mornings are not days.

Eight of the nine carts have been standing in a field at the top of the coast road about four miles off since the twenty-ninth of this month, and the ninth went back down for four sacks on Wednesday and came out again the same evening, and the count of carts at the top of that road is a number this holding has not kept and cannot get.

The field belongs to a body that is not a body of households and is not on the maintenance list, and nobody in this building has been to that field and nobody has asked to, and a holding that has a figure for a launder to the hundredweight and no figure at all for where its own nine carts are standing is a holding that keeps the numbers that come back and not the ones that go.

At about the fourth hour this morning all nine went up the coast road in the rain, one behind another and about forty yards apart, and the ninth still had four sacks roped on over the sides that it was not built for because there was nowhere else to put them and nobody had said there was not.

The plan is one cart at a time through nine miles of cut. There is a man at the narrow place with a bar and a piece of sacking on a stick, and there is a second man at the far mouth of the cut doing the same thing on that side of it, and neither of those two men is the engineer of record and neither of them has been asked to be anything.

The first of the two is the man of about thirty-one of Silling. The second is the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten, who walked the whole of it in the rain before he ever knocked on this building, and who has stood in this yard for a fortnight without putting one question to anybody in it.

Nobody has said out loud that a man who walked a road is the right man to stand at the end of a road. Nobody has said out loud that he is the wrong one. He went up at about the fourth hour with a bar and a stick and no coat that would do anything about the rain, and this holding offered him nothing and was not asked to.

What stands on the shelf is a man and a bar and a piece of sacking tied to the top of a stick. It is not a signal, it was never a signal, and no county here has a signal for a man on a bank. Its only use is that anybody a mile off who looks up can see there is a man up there and that is what a piece of sacking does.

The road is about nine feet wide at that place with a shelf of about four feet above it on the south side, and the shelf is where a loaded cart stands while a cart behind it comes through, and there is nowhere else at that place to stand and everybody who has used that road in eleven years has stood on that shelf.

Marek went up the coast road at about the fifth hour and was at the mouth of the cut by about the sixth and stood on the made road with his back to the bank while the first cart came through, and nobody asked him a single thing for the two hours he was there.

He had a bar in his hand and did not use it. He looked at the face at the narrow place for about a quarter of an hour, from the made road and not from the shelf, and then walked back down to the mouth and sat on the top step of the cart shed with his back to the door.

The door of that shed stands open in the day and shut at night, and it was open, and the man who is in it was in it, and Marek did not go in and was not asked to and did not offer.

The first cart went up at about the fifth hour and came back past him at about the seventh, and the second went up behind it, and the two men on the shelf did not shout at each other once all afternoon because they could hear each other without it, and nobody in four counties has ever written down how that works or whether it always has.

He gave the reason first when he got back into the yard at about the ninth hour with the rain still on him, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and fifty, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about standing at the end of a road and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have spent nine years being the person a county sends for when a thing will not do what it has been told, and this morning nobody sent for me and two men went up that road without asking me, and I found out about it from the yard."** "I am not going up there tomorrow unless somebody asks me to. A man with a bar and a piece of sacking on a stick at the narrow place is a man doing the thing at the place he is at, and if I had been standing at the narrow place I would have been doing that, and neither one of those two things is a pattern, and there is no instrument in four counties that will put a man on a shelf and a man in a yard into the same column."

Nobody in that yard said that was right and nobody in that yard said it was a dodge, and about four people here would have gone up and about four would not have, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man was not asked and has not been asked.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with four sacks on it and came back down loaded, and the number of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and was not raised this morning and is not going to be raised this month by anybody in this building.

That road is four miles and made and it is not the coast road, and a barrow going up it is not one of the nine and does not go near the cut, and the two figures have never been added to one another in four years.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and thirty-three days old this morning and has nothing on it. He was on the narrow place from the fourth hour and did not come back into this building until about the eighth and came in with his coat through and put the bar back in the shed without being told to, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

The man in the cart shed has been in the cart shed since the second of this month. This holding has not been and is not going to be, his name is not in this building, and nobody here has a figure of any kind that belongs to him.

A face nine feet high at the narrow place is not a figure in any of the four books and is not going to become one, and a man in a shed at the mouth of a cut is not a figure either, and neither of those two facts is on a list and neither of them has been added to the other anywhere in this building.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten is nine miles off at about the eighth hour with a bar and a stick and the slate under his arm, and the tenth of the ten is a ford with no year against it and no bad week dated, and nobody in this holding has written to anybody to ask whether the ford carried water this week.

The man in the bad coat was at the tap on the north side of the yard at about the eighth hour with the roll under the lean-to where it has been since the twelfth, and he did not go up the coast road and nobody asked him why and the roll did not go up the coast road with anybody.

The woman who coordinates the moving reserves went up with the first cart and has been on that road since the fourth hour, and she is not support staff and has not been asked to be and this holding has not offered her a chair, a coat or a second copy of the schedule.

The plan is hers and she said on Sunday that it was hers and that it was not a good plan, and she has not said since then how many carts it has put through that a string of carts would have put through, and she has not been asked, and the count of questions this holding has put to her since the fourth of this month is nil and is nil.

What is signed for at the far mouth of the cut is a number of sacks against a number of bodies, and both of those are kept by four counties that this holding has never had a column for, and nobody on that road has written either of them on the back of a hand for this building's books, and it is not going to be asked for.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in her own building from the fourth hour with four books open and the light on until the ninth, and the schedule of nine loads and four days is in there and there is no second copy of it in this building, and she was not asked this morning what she thought of one cart at a time and she did not offer and she will not be asked.

There is a sheet of terms with five clauses in it in two hands, none improved and none withdrawn, and a third column under a heading put there on the twenty-first of a month now three months back with nothing under it at all. Nobody has written a sixth term and a plan brought up a coast road by this building is not one.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart with the window shut against the rain, and neither of them was touched, and the two bodies have still said no in the same week and neither of them knows that the other one has, and nine carts going up a coast road has not changed either of those answers and is not going to.

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at a page with a line ruled at the top of it and nothing at the head of the line, and he looked at it for a while and did not write anything at the head of it, and the count of sessions entered in this holding's book stands at seven and there was no eighth entered today and there is not going to be one, because a convoy is not a session and a plan is not a session and the two of them have never been in the same book in four years.

There is no instrument anywhere in four counties that says whether the far end of a thing is on or off. It has not been said to be on by anybody in this building and it is not said to be on this morning, and the far end of a thing on a coast road is a different object with a different name and the two of them have not been added together here and are not added today.

The pattern-holder counts are two for four and one for five and there are two rootmarks and one who can hold a pattern for four and walk out of the ground afterwards, and a count of pattern-holders is not a count of ends, and two men with bars at two ends of nine miles of chalk are on neither of those two lists.

The yard had no frost on it before the light and has none now. Two inches came up in the middle road in the night and one of them had gone by the ninth hour. The north end of the ditch is running full and has been since Saturday.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road went over its lip for the fifth time since it was filled and came back down inside its own lip before the light went, and the man who dug the drain off the back of the lean-to has not been asked which body is meant to fill that trough and has not offered, and there is no answer to give him if he were.

The yard had about two inches of standing water in the south end of it at the ninth hour, which is the drain and is not the coast road and is four miles and more from any cut, and two of those are a distance and a depth and the two have not been added to one another anywhere in this building.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding put the day's figure into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the fourth cart was somewhere past the middle of the cut with two more behind it and a fifth waiting at the mouth, and the two sheets on the long table were nine inches apart, and this building was told nothing about any of it and asked nothing.

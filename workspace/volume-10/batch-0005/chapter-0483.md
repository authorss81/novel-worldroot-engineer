# Chapter 483

## The Sixth Day

Four men had the third line by the second hour of the sixth of this month. It is the pair of culverts on the middle road with the fill behind them and the low field gate, and an inch and a half of the northern fill had washed out in the rain and went back in and was tamped twice with the butt of a spade.

The gate wanted a bar and a stone the way it has wanted them since it was hung, and it took nine minutes, and nobody in this building has ever asked whether that is the ordinary time.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not one of the four and has not been asked to come down off the narrow place for it, and he was nine miles off with a bar and a piece of sacking on a stick at about the second hour and did not come down until the rain stopped at about the seventh.

The rain went on until about the seventh and then stopped all at once, the way it does when the wind goes round, and the yard was steaming by the eighth and the standing water in the south end had gone down about an inch of its two inches by the ninth hour.

Nothing stands against the run of mornings here and the page says why in one line. Both men who read those boards went out on the coast road before the light, the near side with the fourth cart and the far side to bring an empty set back, and neither was in this building before the lamp was lit, and it is not read this morning.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and thirty days** and **six hundred and seventy-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The sixth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and eighty-three hundredweight.**

Wednesday's eight and nothing against it, and the two hundred and forty-sixth figure of a run that has never once written the same number down twice. It went onto the rises, and behind it a hundred and twelve of one kind of morning stands against a hundred and one of the other, and those two ends make a hundred and thirteen hundred and two, which is the window, and the window is not the boards and is not the launder and has not been added to either of them here in four years.

What this morning's sheet carries against the four bodies of households is two hundred and forty-four days, with the two hundred and forty-third of those mornings written underneath it out of two hundred and forty-fifth. One less and one more, and it has been that way at every site since before this holding had the second book, and a count of mornings is not a count of sacks and is not a count of carts.

The window of the office's work opened at about the sixth hour.

It opens on the sixth of a month and shuts on the eleventh and it has been six days long since before this building kept the second book, and no work has ever been carried out of one in four years, and none is carried out of this one.

That is said once on this page and is not said again. A window that opens and shuts and a barrow that goes up a road and comes down are two different objects and the four years of this building's practice have never put one of them against the other.

Nothing came through it. The requests column is fifty-three and it is fifty-three this evening, and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and they are a different list and the two are not added together anywhere in this building.

There is a request in that column from a body that has a heading on its own paper, asking this holding to certify a figure of days for the maintenance of a road, and the answer went back in the clerk's hand on the twentieth of a month now four months back and it is one sentence, and a window that opens on the sixth is not a place where a second answer gets written.

**PAID. Twenty-four not discharged in twenty-four.**

The word is in front of the figure and the figure is under the word and it is one hundred pounds, and the word has never once disagreed with the figure in four years and does not disagree this morning. It is a hundred pounds and it is not a measurement of the ground and it is not a measurement of a road and it is not going to be used as either.

The run has gone up one in a month and it went up one in a month before that, and nobody in this holding has worked out what it is a count of, and a count of undischarged runs is not a count of cartloads and is not a count of loads going up a coast road.

The clerk of this holding read that line back twice, as he reads that line twice and no other, and nobody has ever asked him why that one and not another, and the man who keeps it this month has never once said what it is for and has never been asked.

Three carts were through the cut by the fourth hour and two more were on the road behind them, and the sixth stood at the mouth waiting for its turn, and the man with the piece of sacking on the stick had not turned it over once since the fourth hour because there was nothing to turn it over for.

A load is nine sacks and there are nine loads and there are therefore about nine times nine sacks in this convoy, and the count of sacks is a count of sacks, and it is not on a piece of paper in this building and has not been written on the back of a hand and is not going to be asked for, and it is not the same count as a count of carts and it is not the same count as a count of days.

The made road between here and the mouth of the cut takes the rain on a chalk and clay subsoil and holds it about four inches deep in the ruts for about a day, and the carts went up it at a walking pace for that reason and not because of anything anybody decided, and nobody in four counties has ever been asked how fast a cart ought to go on a road that is wet.

At the far mouth the loads are signed for by men with a slate and a tally and a length of tarred twine, and the tally is not kept in this building and has never been brought down this road and nobody here has asked to see it, and the two figures on a slate are two figures on a slate and not on a page in any of the four books.

At the narrow place the shelf held the first cart from about the fifth hour until about the seventh and the second cart stood on the made road below in the wet and did not go up onto the shelf until the first one was past it, and that is the whole of the plan and it has not been written on a piece of paper anywhere except a sheet with no name at the head of it.

What holds that face up is a single post and a single wedge, and the wedge sits at the top with nothing beneath it. That is the piece that lets go, and it has let go first for longer than this county has had anybody writing to anybody about a road.

The water in the ditch at the foot of the face was about four inches deep at about the seventh hour and the man at the narrow place stood and looked at it for a while and did not say anything to anybody and nobody up the road said anything to him.

Nothing said on that road this morning has come back to this building in a piece of paper. There is no sheet about the second cart or the third cart or the four inches of water in a ditch four miles off, and the clerk of this holding was not asked for one and did not go and get one.

The woman who keeps the seed house came out at about the eighth hour and stood at the door of her own building for about nine minutes with her arms folded in the wet and went back in, and nobody in this yard asked her why and nobody in this yard saw her go back in either.

The four ruled lines under two words entered on the twentieth of a month now four months back are on their own table in the corner and are still empty, and the answer to them is not due, and a run of mornings that has been counted since the seventeenth of a month now six months back is not the answer to them and is not written in one of them.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and thirty-four days old and has nothing on it. He came down the coast road at about the seventh hour with the bar over his shoulder and the sacking still tied to the stick, and he put both of them in the shed and went and washed to the elbow, and the woman who keeps the seed house had a cloth out before he asked for it and has not been asked about that either.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was at the far mouth of the cut from about the fourth hour and had come down in the rain at about the seventh because the piece of sacking on the stick is his and he was not going to leave it standing in a wet cut with nobody at that end of it.

He came into the yard at about the eighth hour with the water off him and did not go into the one room and stood at the gate with the slate under his arm for about eleven minutes and then went down the fen road on foot, and this holding offered him nothing and was not asked to and did not ask him where he was going.

Marek was at the mouth of the cut at about the fourth hour and about the ninth and did not go up the road, and he did not open his mouth at either end of it, and the count of questions anybody in this holding has put to him about that road this month is nil and is nil.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart and neither of them was turned over, and a body that has said no in the same week as another body has still said no, and a window opening on the sixth of a month does not open either of those two answers and does not close them.

The man of about fifty was in the one room at about the fifth hour with the third book shut on the table in front of him and did not open it, and nobody in this building has asked him what is in it and he has not said.

There were about nine people in that room between the fifth hour and the seventh and the door was shut, and the window of the office's work was open in the wall behind them with the shutter up, and two of the nine looked at the window for a while and the rest of them looked at the third book.

The window is nine feet off the floor and about two feet wide and it has a shutter on the inside that has been painted shut and opened twice in four years, and no work has ever been carried out of it, and there was no work this morning either and nobody in that room had brought any.

The long table had the two sheets on it about nine inches apart and the drawer with the sheet of rough paper in it was under the counter with the sheet that claims the corridor in the pan, and both of those are correct and neither of them lets anything out and neither of them was opened in front of nine people.

A road with nine carts on it is not that corridor and that corridor is not that road, and a sheet with ten lines on it in a man's road hand is not a route and a route is not a supply, and the two have not been added together in this holding in four years.

The low field gate is the gate the four bodies of households use on their way to the far common and it is not this holding's gate and it is on a road that is not this holding's road, and the sheet of water that came off the high field on the twenty-seventh had stopped at the inside of that gate post and did not go through it and did not go past it, and nobody in this holding has ever put the middle road and that gate into one sentence.

Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any one of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and a window that opened and shut with nothing in it is not a seventh and a road with nine carts on it is not a seventh and a ditch four miles off is not a seventh.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down off the narrow place at about the seventh hour with the bar over his shoulder and the rain still on the back of him, and he stood in the yard for about four minutes with the bar against the wall before he said anything.

**"There are four inches in that ditch and there were four inches in it on Sunday morning before any of this rain, and I am telling you because you are going up there tomorrow and I am not."** He put the bar down the length of the wall and wiped his hands on his coat. **"I have not measured it and neither has anybody in this yard, and it is not a figure, and I am not handing it to the clerk as one."**

He said nothing else about the two hours he had spent on the shelf, and there was nothing in them that had not already been said by the water.

The shutter on the window of the office's work has been up in the wall behind the long table since about the sixth hour, and the woman who keeps the seed house stood in the one room and looked at it for a while and then said one thing to nobody in particular.

**"That shutter goes up six days a month and has done since before I came into this building, and there has never once been a sheet on that table behind it, and I have started to think the window is not for work."** Nobody in that room answered her and nobody has said since whether she is right and she has not been asked.

The ninth-day returns are every ninth morning and this morning was not one of them, and the last one was on the fourth at four hundred and thirty-seven hundredweight and the next is on the thirteenth, and nobody in this building has written the thirteenth down anywhere except on a sheet in the second book that is a day and not a plan.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick has not been fetched and is not fetched this month, and the man of about seventy was fetched on the first of this month for the twenty-sixth time and is not fetched again inside thirty mornings, and nobody in this building has asked either of them a question on any morning of this month and the count of questions is nil and is nil.

The yard had about an inch of standing water in the south end of it at about the sixth hour and none of it by the ninth, and the drain off the back of the lean-to went back in the way it was found, and a drain is not a barrow's journeys and the two have never been added together in this holding in four years.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding shut the window of the office's work at the eleventh hour of the sixth day of its six days and left the shutter on, and the day's figure went into the second book and was read back once, and six carts were through a cut that nobody in this building has ever measured and two more were waiting at the mouth of it, and the light went off the long table and the sheet at the gatepost was not turned over.

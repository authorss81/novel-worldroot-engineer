# Chapter 485

## Forty-Nine And Thirty-Six

The fourth line came round at the second hour on the eighth of this month and went past unworked, and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and that is seventy-two mornings of that line gone past in a row without a name against any of them.

The line is the top four feet of the middle embankment and it has gone past every fourth morning since the middle of a month now one month back, and the reason there is nobody at the headland is that this holding does not know whose the ground at the top of it is, and has known that for longer than the rota has been a book.

The man of about fifty has said in this room that a field which waits is not being starved by anybody and that a field nobody owns is not the same as a field nobody works, and he has not said it twice and has not been asked to say it again.

The rota stands at one hundred and one mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and seventy-two not, and twenty-nine and seventy-two are a hundred and one, and the figure that has not moved in this year or in any year this building can date is the twenty-nine.

Nobody in this building is blamed for a field that has waited, because a field that waits is a fact about a field and not about a man, and the count of men who have worked the twenty-nine has never once been the same figure as the count of mornings on the rota and the two have never been put in one sentence here.

Two reckonings of that rotation are kept in this building and they do not agree, so both of them go down here in full. The one made in this room counts this morning as its forty-ninth. The other, four years older and never once corrected against this one, counts it as its thirty-sixth.

That leaves thirteen between them. It has been thirteen since this building started keeping the two side by side and there is no entry anywhere that says what the thirteen is a count of, and neither figure is worked out of the other and no arithmetic on the rota turns one into the other.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. Both men who read the boards went up the coast road at about the sixth hour, one with the carts and one to bring the far end of the thing back down, and neither was in this building before the tenth hour and nobody thought to ask a third man on a Thursday.

The pair of boards stands at **six hundred and thirty-two days** and **six hundred and seventy-nine**, forty-seven apart at every row, and the drawer behind them was not opened.

The eighth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and eighty-six hundredweight.**

Wednesday's eight stands unopposed on top of it. Two hundred and forty-eighth of a sequence in which no figure has ever been written twice, and it belongs to the rises.

The window behind that figure is four hundred and eighty-three days wide and it divides into a hundred and thirteen of one kind of morning and a hundred and two of the other, and the two ends of it add to a hundred and fifteen hundred and two, and it is not the boards and not the launder and has not been added to either.

What the sheet says about the four bodies of households this morning is two hundred and forty-six days, and under that figure the clause reads the two hundred and forty-fifth morning out of two hundred and forty-seventh, one short and one over, in the way it has read at every site since the first of the four books was opened.

The seventh cart went through the cut at about the fourth hour and the eighth was on the shelf at about the sixth, and the ninth was standing at the mouth of the cut with its sheets over it and about four feet of water across the made road in front of it.

About the fourth hour the water came up the face at the narrow place.

It came up about two inches on the ditch side and stopped and then came up another inch over about a quarter of an hour, and the man at the narrow place stood on the shelf and looked at it and put his hand flat on the clay at the top of the face and took it away again and did not say anything about it to anybody.

Underneath about two feet of clay the face is chalk, and clay that has been wet for four days lets the chalk go out from under it. Nobody made that road do that and nobody is at fault for it, and it has been happening on that stretch for longer than anybody now driving it was born.

What is holding the face up at that point is one post and one wedge, and the wedge is at the top of it with nothing under it. That is the piece that goes first and it has gone first on this stretch for longer than this county has had anybody writing letters about roads.

There were two men on the face at about the seventh hour with a barrow of chalk and stone in it and no notice of them anywhere and no flag and nobody in four counties having told them to be there, and the money for it comes from a holding two miles on whose books this holding has never seen.

They are not a body and not a council and not the Undercommons and not anybody that is on anything, and about four people in this holding would say a man who is shoring a face over a road is a man who is on the road and about four would say he is on the face with the road on top of him, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man of about fifty was not asked which of the two he was in.

He said it to the man who had come back down the coast road with the empty carts, at about the fourth hour and forty minutes, and what he said was that the ditch had a foot in it where there had been four inches on Tuesday, and nobody has written that down and nobody in this building has been told what the answer to it was.

The shelf is about four feet deep and the lip of it is about a foot and the face is about nine feet above the road, and a cart standing on the shelf with a foot of clay going soft underneath it is a thing that has to be decided by somebody standing on the shelf, and it was not decided this morning.

The eighth cart came down off the shelf at about the fifth hour and went back to the mouth of the cut under its own power and the man at the far end walked it down with a rope, and the ninth cart went back down the coast road about half a mile and stood there in a field until about the eighth hour.

Two of the nine are loaded and about two miles off and the seven that came through are signed for at the far end, and the count of sacks in this convoy is a count of sacks and it is not on a page in this building and is not going to be asked for.

The coast road is nine miles from this gate to the far mouth of the cut on a made surface for about the first four and about five of cart track after that, and it is a different road from the four miles to the gate and the trough and a different road again from the four and a half to Thornwild, and this building knows the length of four roads and has never once been asked what any of them is for.
The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten came down off the narrow place at about the fifth hour and walked the two miles back to the mouth of the cut rather than ride and got there about the sixth, and he had the bar over his shoulder and the sacking still tied to the stick and he did not say anything to anybody in this yard when he came through it.

The tenth of the ten is a ford. It carries nine feet in a bad week, and it has carried nine feet twice in the last eleven years, and there is no year against either of those weeks and there is no bad week dated, and the clerk of this holding wrote **CARRIES NINE FEET IN A BAD WEEK** in the margin of the second book a month ago and wrote no figure and wrote no year.

Nobody has written to anybody to ask whether the ford carried water this week, and the answer is four miles past the far mouth of the cut and about a mile and a half down a spur road from there, and this holding has had the spur on a map for four years and has never been to it.

Marek was at the mouth of the cut at about the fifth hour and again at about the eighth and did not go up the road at all, and the two times he was there nobody asked him a question and he did not go to the narrow place.

He said nothing about the water and the water is a fact about a ditch four miles off, and a man who goes up there on his own account has decided to look and not to advise, and he was not asked and has not been asked.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the second hour of this morning and the same four inches at the ninth hour, and nothing has been added to it since the middle of a month now five months back.

Four accounts of that arm are kept in four books in this building and none of them reconciles with any of the others, and a foot of water in a ditch four miles off is not on it and a day is not on it and a declined connection is not on it and a person who keeps four accounts of her own is not on it.

The man of about fifty sat in the one room from about the seventh hour with his own book open in front of him and put nothing in it. Nobody asked him to go four miles to a cut and he did not go, and the number of questions anybody here has put to him about the shed at the mouth of that cut is nil and is nil.

He said one thing at the door of that room at about the seventh hour, to the room and not to anybody in it.

**"I have been in this chair a long time and I have never once been able to make a road do what it was told, and neither has anybody else, and the only difference between that and what happened on the second of this month is that on the second of this month there was a man underneath it."** Nobody in that room asked him what he meant and nobody has asked him since, and he has not said it a second time, and the count of reasons given first did not move on that account because he gave no reason first and nobody here has said that it was an oversight.

The third book is the only one of the four with no column ruled in it at all, and it has about four hundred ruled lines in it that anybody could check and about four hundred more that nobody has, and he has never told anybody which is which.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart with the rain coming down hard enough that there was water standing on the flags by the long wall at about the seventh hour, and nobody moved the sheets off the table and nobody said they should be moved.

One of those two sheets says that a duty without a way out is not a duty and it is a lease, and the other says that the ground is ours and the thing proposed on it is not ours, and neither of them is about nine miles of chalk and neither of them was looked at this morning.

The window of the office's work has been open since the sixth and shuts on the eleventh, and it is open now, and no work has been carried out of one in four years and none is being carried out of this one, and the requests column is fifty-three and it is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and the two are not the same list.

Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any one of them, and the seventh row is not ruled, and a foot of water in a ditch four miles off is not a seventh and a stalled convoy is not a seventh and a man who has not been asked whether he minds is not a seventh.

A list of six things you do not know is not a plan and has never been one in this building, and the six have been six since the schedule was drawn up and no row has been added to them for a road, for a cut, for a convoy, for a man in a shed or for a day.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten had been in this yard for a fortnight on Thursday and had put no question to anybody in this building and had been given nothing, and nobody here has asked him whether he is fed and he has not asked.

There is a drawer under the counter with two correct sheets in it, face up, and a man who has stood at a gate in the rain for two hours on a Thursday is not in that drawer and has not been put anywhere else either.

The man in the bad coat was at the lean-to at about the ninth hour with the roll where it has been since the twelfth, and he came out and looked up the coast road for about a minute and went back in and did not say anything to anybody, and the roll is a document and the document is correct and nobody in this building has called it anything else.

The ninth-day returns are every ninth morning and this morning was not one of them, and the forty-fifth of them is on the thirteenth of this month and has not been weighed, and nobody in this building has written it down anywhere except on the board, and a return that has not been taken is not a figure and is not going to be one.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the shelf at the narrow place from about the third hour until about the fifth and came down the coast road at about the seventh, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and thirty-six days old and has nothing on it, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

A quarter of an inch of frost stood in the small hours of the eighth and had gone by the seventh hour. Two inches came into the middle road in the night and not one of them had gone by the ninth. The north end of the ditch is still running full and has been since Saturday.

The yard itself was dry by the ninth hour because the drain off the back of the lean-to is clear, and the inch that stood in the south end of it on Wednesday had gone by about the eighth, and a dry yard four miles from a wet cut is a fact about a yard and not about a road.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling washed to the elbow at the tap on the north side when he came down and the woman who keeps the seed house had a cloth out before he asked, and she has not been asked about the cloth and neither of those two things has been counted.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding put the day's figure into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the ninth cart was standing half a mile down the coast road with its sheets wet and two men four miles off at a ditch with a foot in it, and nothing at either end of that road had come back to this building in a piece of paper.

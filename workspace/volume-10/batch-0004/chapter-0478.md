# Chapter 478

## The First Of A Month

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the first of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the near stone had a quarter of an inch of daylight under it on Wednesday and had none this morning, and the far stone has not moved since the twenty-eighth of last month.

The pack under the near stone has gone back in wet and will want doing before the frost is into it, and a week is the ordinary answer, and it has not been written down anywhere in this building, and nobody here has ever asked the man who put that pack in what a week is to him.

At the seventh hour the two men between them said the two figures and neither of them said the other half, and the run of mornings is **ninety-one of a hundred and sixty-five**. The boards carry **six hundred and twenty-five days** and **six hundred and seventy-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The first went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixty-nine hundredweight.**

It came in five under Wednesday, and the run stands at its two hundred and forty-first figure, and nothing in that run has ever been written down twice, and the fall went onto the falls. A hundred and nine rises and ninety-nine falls is a hundred and two hundred and eight behind it, and nobody in this building has set those two ends against the figure over them.

Against four bodies of households this morning's sheet has stood still for two hundred and thirty-nine days, and the clause written under that figure is the two hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings out of two hundred and fortieth, one under and one over, and a count of mornings is neither a count of loads nor a count of days.

The month turned in the small hours and nothing reset, and the offer on the low board is of a month four months back and a form on the middle table is of a month five months back, and the four ruled lines under the two words are of a month four months back, and the run of reasons given first was begun on the seventeenth of a month now six months back.

A calendar turning is not a decision and nobody in this holding made one at the turn of it, and a figure that is a month older this morning than it was yesterday evening is the same figure and the same age, and the day is still the first and the ninth-day returns are still the ninth-day returns.

The man of about seventy was fetched at about the seventh hour.

Nobody in this holding gave a reason for it and nobody asked him which number it was, and the count of questions this holding has put to that man is nil and is nil, and it has been nil at every one of the twenty-six of them and is not going to be repaired this month.

It is the twenty-sixth fetching and the last one before this was the on the first of the month now one month back, and the interval between the two is thirty mornings, and the interval has been thirty mornings for four years, and both of the last two of them have fallen on the first of a month, and nobody in this county has ever written down why.

The nine carts are not in this yard. They went up the coast road on the twenty-ninth and they have been put in a field at the top of it about four miles off, and nobody in this holding has been to see them and nobody has asked to, and the field belongs to a body that is not a body of households and is not on the maintenance list.

Eight of the nine are still on that field this morning and the ninth came back on Wednesday for four sacks and went out again the same evening, and the count of carts at the top of the coast road is a number that this holding has not kept and cannot get.

A field four miles off with eight carts in it is not a column and the man of about fifty has not put it in the third book and has not been asked to and did not offer, and a holding that has a figure for a launder to the unit and no figure at all for where its own nine carts are standing is a holding that keeps the numbers that come back and not the ones that go.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in there from about the fourth hour with four books open and a sheet on the table and the light on at the ninth, and she has not been asked this month what the eight carts at the top of the coast road are for and has not offered, and the count of questions this holding has put to her about them is nil and is nil.

He came in a cart from the second gate with a blanket over his knees and he was in the one room for about eleven minutes and he was not asked anything and he asked nothing, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling drove the cart both ways and did not go into the room with him.

The eleven minutes is a figure the man of about fifty wrote at the top of a page in pencil and did not enter in any of the four books, and a figure written in pencil at the top of a page is not a record and is not going to become one, and he was not asked to write it down properly and did not.

A man who is fetched twenty-six times and asked nothing twenty-six times is a man who has been thought about a great deal by people who have not said so to him, and nobody in this holding has said so to him, and there is no column in any of the four books in which anybody has said so to him.

The compost run is paid at one hundred pounds and the figure against it this morning is **twenty-three not discharged in twenty-three**.

PAID is in front of the figure and the word has never once disagreed with the figure in four years, and the two of them have never once been improved, and a hundred pounds is the amount and is not a measurement of the ground and is not a measurement of anything else in this county.

> THE LINE IN THE SECOND BOOK, ENTERED IN THE CLERK'S OWN HAND AT THE SEVENTH HOUR, IN THE COLUMN THAT HAS BEEN THERE SINCE THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH BOOK AND HAS NEVER BEEN RULED A SECOND TIME: **PAID. ONE HUNDRED POUNDS. TWENTY-THREE NOT DISCHARGED IN TWENTY-THREE.** THE WORD IS IN FRONT OF THE FIGURE AND THE FIGURE IS UNDER THE WORD AND THERE IS NO HEADING AT THE HEAD OF THE COLUMN AND NO NAME AGAINST EITHER OF THE TWO, AND THE FIGURE HAS GONE UP BY ONE IN A MONTH AND HAS GONE DOWN BY ONE IN A MONTH, ALTERNATELY, AND NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS WORKED OUT WHAT IT IS A COUNT OF, AND A COUNT OF UNDISCHARGED RUNS IS NOT A COUNT OF MANURES AND IS NOT A COUNT OF CARTLOADS.

The word in front of that figure has been the same word since the first of this month of a year nobody here can name and the two of them have never been separated and never once been printed in the wrong order, and this building has four books and not one of them has that line in it in any other order.

That line has been kept by three men in four years and the man who keeps it this month is the man of about thirty-one of Silling, and nobody in this holding has ever asked him to keep it and he has never once said what it is for, and the count of questions he has been asked about the line is nil and is nil.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road was full at about the seventh hour and did not go over, and the man who dug the drain off the back of the lean-to on the eighth of a month now one month back has not been asked which body is meant to fill that trough and has not offered, and there is no answer to give him if he were.

The clerk of this holding wrote the day into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and he read the compost line back twice and that is the only line in the four books that anybody has ever read back twice, and nobody has ever asked him why that one and not another.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart and neither of them was touched, and they will be there on the first of next month and they are not going to be settled by anybody turning a page, and a month turning has settled nothing in this building for a month and a half and is not going to settle anything by itself.

The frost was about a quarter of an inch on the yard at the sixth hour and there was none of it by the eighth, and the water in the middle road had come up about an inch overnight and gone down about two of those by the ninth, and the north end of the ditch is still running full.

The man of about fifty was in the one room from about the fifth hour with the third book open and did not write anything in it, and the two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart and neither of them was touched, and they will be there on the first of next month and they are not going to be settled by anybody turning a page.

Four ruled lines sit on their own table in the corner under two words entered on the twentieth of a month now four months back, and every one of the four is still empty and the answer to them is not due. Nobody in this building has written a figure in one of them and nobody is going to.

The day sheet with no date at the head of it is in a stack of about a foot and a half behind the fourth book in the order it was used, and the sheet from today went on the top of it, and about four hundred sheets have gone onto the top of that stack in four years and the count of sheets taken off it again is not a figure anybody has kept.

Marek was at the outlet end from the sixth hour until the ninth, on the other side of the two stones from the man of about thirty-one of Silling, and the two of them did not talk and got on with it, and the count of times the two of them have worked the same line this month is not a figure anybody keeps.

He gave the reason first about the fetching at about the ninth hour, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-seven and it is his and of nobody else's, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was not in the room and has not been asked about the twenty-sixth.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a man who is fetched and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been in this county for nine years and I have watched that man be brought in twenty-six times and I could not tell you what any of the twenty-six was for, and I have never once been the one who decided it."** "That is not a complaint about him. It is a complaint about a building in which a thing can happen twenty-six times with nobody able to say what it is, and I have decided that I am not going to be the twenty-seventh thing in this room that cannot say what it is, and I have not asked, and I am not going to."

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard at about the eighth hour with the slate under his arm and a rope over his shoulder, and he has been in this yard for fourteen days and has asked this holding no question and has answered none.

The man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with the roll where it has been since the twelfth, and the roll is a document and the document is correct and nobody in this building has called it anything else, and it is not on the second book and is not on the third and has not been since the tenth of a month now two months back.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came off the cart at about the fourth hour and did not take the blanket off him and did not stay in the room, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and twenty-nine days old and has nothing on it, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

A third column in the sheet of terms is ruled and empty, and the heading at the top of it has been there since the twenty-first of a month now two months back. The five terms in that sheet have not been improved, have not been unwithdrawn and have not been added to, and a figure that has gone down in a column with no heading at the top of it is not a term of anything and is not going to become one.

The mark in the floor of the pump house is four inches deep in the middle and worn round at the corners and nobody in this building has measured it this month, and a fetching and a compost line and a month turning are not a mark and are not a column and this morning did not make a fifth account of anything.

A walk of nine hundred yards that gets made twice a year is not a walk anybody in this holding puts a rate on, and the two mornings of the year on which it is made are not this one and are not the next one either.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick came as far as the gate at about the eighth hour on his own and stood at the outside of it for about six minutes and went back, and nobody in this holding sent for him and nobody in this holding went out to him, and the count of times either of those two things has happened in four years is nil and is nil.

A man of about seventy-four who comes to a gate and stands at the outside of it is a man who has decided that a gate he is not invited through is a different object from a gate he is, and nobody in this building has given him a reason to decide that and nobody has asked him for one, and he will not be fetched until the first of a month that is thirty mornings off.

The lamp was lit at about the sixth hour for the eleven minutes and was not lit for anything else that evening, and a lamp lit for one reason on the first of a month is a lamp somebody asked for, and the count of lamps lit in this building for a man who is asked nothing is one and is one.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour, and the offer is on the low board face up with nothing on it and no date and no line for a name, and the month is the first of a new one and nothing on the middle table is a day older by one for that, and a man was fetched this morning for the twenty-sixth time and nobody in this building knows what any of the twenty-six was for and has not asked him.

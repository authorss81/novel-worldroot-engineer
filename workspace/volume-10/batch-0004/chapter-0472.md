# Chapter 472

## Nine Days Is Not Nine Days

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour on the twenty-fifth of this month and was worked, and it was the two culverts on the middle road and the fill behind them and the low field gate, and the fill behind the northern culvert had gone out about two inches since the last time it was packed, and two of them put it back and went over it twice.

The gate needed a bar and a stone the way it has needed one since it was hung, and it took nine minutes, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling held the stone while the fourth man worked the bar, and neither of them has ever been asked how long it ought to take.

There was a hard frost in the small hours and about a quarter of an inch of it standing in the ruts of the four-mile road when the first cart came out of the gate, and the frame at the pump house had gone down about four minutes and come up about two, and the black out of the bottom of the seam is about an inch and a half deep and it is on the boards outside and nobody has washed it off since the first week of this month.

The forty-third ninth-day return came off the steelyard at about the third hour, and the man who brought it up the coast road was in the yard before the light was properly on and he did not come in and he did not ask for anything, and the two of them have never been in the same room.

**Four hundred and twenty-six hundredweight.**

The rise on that board is eleven hundredweight every time and has been eleven every time for eleven years, and the pair of names against it this morning is the thirty-third pair in eleven years and it is eleven above the pair that went up on the sixteenth, and the clerk of this holding entered it against the first rule and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

The second rule on that board was left blank for the twenty-seventh time in eleven years this morning, and a count of blanks is not the count of a man, and nobody in this holding has ever put a name against the second rule and nobody is going to.

It was reconciled twice before the light was full, at about the third hour against the sheet that came back inside the same fold and again at about the fifth hour by the man of about fifty out of the third book, and the two of them do not agree and the two halves of the figure have never been added to one another in this building in four years.

> THE SHEET THAT CAME BACK WITH THE FORTY-THIRD RETURN, IN THE HAND OF THE MAN WHO BROUGHT IT UP, WITH THE FIGURE AT THE HEAD OF IT AND NOTHING AT THE FOOT OF IT: **FORTY-THIRD. FOUR HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIX HUNDREDWEIGHT. THIRTY-THIRD PAIR. NINE DAYS.** THE NINE DAYS IS UNDER THE FIGURE IN THE SAME HAND AND IT IS THE ONLY COUNT ON THE SHEET THAT IS NOT A COUNT OF WEIGHT. NOBODY AT THE STEELYARD HAS BEEN ASKED WHAT IT IS A COUNT OF AND NOBODY HERE HAS ASKED. THERE IS NO HEADING AT THE HEAD OF THIS SHEET AND NO NAME AT THE FOOT OF IT.

The seed house's own schedule went out of this county on the eleventh of this month and came back on the fifteenth with its string cut and retied, and it carries nine loads and four days.

Four days is a count of days. Nine loads is a count of carts. And a sheet that came off a steelyard this morning carries nine days at the head of it, and nine days is a third number and nobody in this building has worked out what it is a count of.

That is the whole of it and it is said once here and not improved on. Nine is a count of carts on one paper and a count of days on another, and four is a count of days on a third, and the two nines are not one nine and the four is not a total of the two of them.

The convoy is loaded.

Nine carts went into the yard between the fourth hour and the seventh and were loaded between the fourth hour and the seventh, and the sacks went on by hand because there is nothing in this county that will take them on any faster, and the loading is not a column and is not in any of the four books and has not been given a heading by anybody.

A count of carts is a count of carts. A count of sacks is a count of sacks, and there are both of those on nine carts this morning, and nobody has written the second one down and the page does not supply it and the two are not added to each other anywhere in this building.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the yard the whole of it with a pencil behind her ear and she did not stand in a doorway and she was not waiting for anybody, and she has a schedule of her own with four days at the head of it and nine loads under that, and she has not been asked this morning to explain either of the two.

The ninth cart would not back into the north corner because the drain off the back of the lean-to comes out there and the ground has been soft since Sunday, and it was put in the south end instead with its shafts out, and it is standing in four inches of water at about the ninth hour and nobody has moved it since because moving a loaded cart is a decision and a decision about a cart is a thing this holding has not done in four years.

**"That one is going to sit in it until tomorrow and you are all going to argue about it on Monday,"** the woman who keeps the seed house said, to nobody, at about the eighth hour, and she went on counting, and about four people in this holding heard the whole of it and about four heard the half of it, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move this morning.

**"I am going to say the one thing about the nine and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been asked to make one number out of four accounts of my own twice in a fortnight and I did it, and the four days at the head of that schedule are the four days I put there and not the four days anybody gave me."** "Nine carts are nine carts. A man who wants the two of them to be the same figure is asking me for a number I have not got and has not asked me for."

She said that with her hand on the shaft of the fifth cart and she did not stop loading while she said it, and nobody in that yard asked her for anything afterwards, and the count of questions this holding has put to her about the four days is nil and is nil.

Marek was asked nothing and said nothing about any of it and got off the second cart with his back against the side of it and stood there while nine carts were counted, and a man standing still while nine carts go out of a yard one at a time is a man who has decided that the counting is not his.

The man of about fifty was asked nothing about the return and said nothing about it and stood at the long wall until about the fifth hour, and the clerk of this holding put the sheet into the second book at about the sixth hour and read the day back and did not read the nine days back, and nobody in the one room has ever asked him why he read one of the two.

There is a list that went up the fen road with nine columns and nine duties in it, four marks standing in the sixth column and five places in that same column that nobody has ever filled, and it came back on the second of this month with nothing added at the head of it and nothing taken off at the foot.

A list of nine columns with four marks in one of them and five empty places is not a schedule of nine loads and is not a roll of anybody, and the two of them have not been put in one column in this building in four years and a third of them, which would be a roll of the people who use a road, has never existed at all.

A figure apiece at the seventh hour, and neither of the two men read the other's, and the run of mornings is **eighty-eight of a hundred and fifty-nine**. The two boards carry **six hundred and nineteen days** and **six hundred and sixty-six**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-fifth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixty hundredweight.**

Friday is five under Thursday and the fall went onto the falls. This is the two hundred and thirty-fifth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and a hundred and six of one kind of morning against ninety-six of the other is a hundred and two hundred and two, and nobody in this building has ever added the two ends of that to the figure standing over them.

What this morning's sheet carries against four bodies of households is two hundred and thirty-three days, and the clause written under that figure is the two hundred and thirty-second of those mornings out of two hundred and thirty-fourth, one under and one over, and the three of those numbers count mornings and not hundredweight and not anything that was carried anywhere.

The water in the middle road had come up about two inches overnight and was going down at about half of that by the eighth hour, and the north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the top of the four-mile road was full again at about the seventh hour and nobody filled it in the yard.

A frost takes a yard hard for about four hours and then lets go of it, and the south end of this yard was wet to the stone by ten and dry to the stone at seven.

The compost run is paid at one hundred pounds and stands at twenty-two not discharged in twenty-two, and the word in front of the figure is the word it has always been in front of it, and nine carts in a yard between the fourth hour and the seventh is not a measurement of that line and has never been added to it.

About four people in this holding hold that a sheet with a count of days on it is a note about a road, and about four hold that it is a note about a cart, and neither of the two fours has been asked, and the man who brought the sheet up the coast road was in the yard at about the seventh hour and has not been asked either.

Marek was on the second cart with a sack on each end of it and he did not read the boards and he did not read the paper off the steelyard, and the count of times he has read a return in this holding is not a figure anybody keeps, and a man on a cart at the fourth hour is a man with his hands full and not a man with a pencil.

The man of about fifty stood at the long wall with the third book open under his arm and did not put the return into it until about the fifth hour, and when he put it in he put it in at the page it has been at since the first of this month and he did not rule a line under it and he did not write anything at the head of it.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table the whole morning with the frost on the window above them, and the two of them are about nine inches apart and they were about nine inches apart on Monday and nobody has touched either of them in ten days.

The clerk of this holding had a piece of chalk on the board off the north wall and he wrote the day on it the way he has written it every morning for four years, and nobody in this building has ever asked him what that board is for and it is not a column and has no heading at the top of it.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard at about the sixth hour with his coat off and a barrow handle in his hand and he did not offer it to anybody, and he has been in this yard for twelve days and has asked this holding no question and has answered none.

The man in the bad coat was at the tap on the north side of the yard at about the fourth hour with the roll under his arm and he watched the ninth cart go into the south end and did not say anything about it, and the two of them have slept about nine feet apart for twelve nights and have not spoken, and the count of times this building has told that man what it is doing behind its back is nil and is nil.

The day sheet with no date at the head of it is in a stack of about a foot and a half behind the fourth book and it is in the order it was used, and the sheet from today went on the top of that stack at about the ninth hour, and nobody in this building has looked inside that stack in four years.

The frost will be back in the small hours and it will be thinner than the one before it, and the fill behind the northern culvert will want doing in about a week and nobody has been told to give anybody, and the gate will be stiff again by the middle of next week whatever happens to the nine carts.

The gate was shut at about the ninth hour and the frost will be back in the small hours, and the middle road will have it in the morning, and the two stones at the head of the outlet will be looked at tomorrow morning by whoever is on the second line.

Nobody thanked anybody. The nine carts stood in the yard in a line with their shafts down and the sheets over them, and the paper that came off the steelyard went into the second book at about the sixth hour with the day against it and the nine days under the figure, and nobody in this building has asked what the nine days is a count of, and it is the twenty-fifth and the four hundred and twenty-six is eleven above the one before it and the second rule is still blank.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The twenty-fifth. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone, nine minutes, three people from the second hour. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour. The same day in and the same day out. Nine hundred and sixty hundredweight to the launder, being the two hundred and thirty-fifth. The forty-third ninth-day return came off the steelyard at four hundred and twenty-six hundredweight, the thirty-third pair, reconciled twice and the two halves not added, and the second rule on the board was left blank for the twenty-seventh time in eleven years. The convoy is loaded, nine carts, and the schedule of nine loads carries four days and the sheet that came back with the return carries nine days, and the three of them are three numbers.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

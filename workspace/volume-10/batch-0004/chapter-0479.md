# Chapter 479

## A Man Under A Load

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour on the second of this month and was worked, and it was the two culverts on the middle road and the fill behind them and the low field gate, and the fill had gone out about two inches since Thursday and three of them put it back and went over it twice and the fourth was on the bank with a spade doing nothing but standing on the wet grass.

It took a bar and a stone and nine minutes to get the gate open, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling held the stone while the fourth man worked the bar, and there has not been a year in which that gate took any other time.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The man who reads the near side of the boards was at the narrow place in the cut with the other two men from the fifth hour and did not come back up the coast road until after the ninth, and the man who reads the far side did not read his own figure out alone, and nobody thought to ask a third man to do it on a Friday.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and twenty-six days** and **six hundred and sixty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The second went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and seventy-seven hundredweight.**

Friday added eight. That is the two hundred and forty-second figure of a run that has never written a number down twice, and it went on the rises. The window behind it stands at a hundred and ten of one kind of morning and ninety-nine of the other, which is a hundred and two hundred and nine, and the two ends of that are not added to the figure over them here.

The figure this morning's sheet carries for four bodies of households is two hundred and forty days, and the clause beneath it reads the two hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings out of two hundred and forty-first, one under and one over, and none of the three is a count of loads and none of them is a count of carts.

The cut is nine miles long and it has been open since before this holding kept anything, and the face of it is chalk under about two feet of clay, and the clay goes soft at the top of a wet week and lets go of the chalk underneath it, and that is what a cut is and it is not anybody's fault and it has been happening on that road longer than anybody now using it.

At the narrow place the road is about nine feet wide with a shelf of about four feet above it on the south side, and the shelf is where a loaded cart stands while a cart behind it comes through, and there is nowhere else at that place to stand and everybody who has used that road in eleven years has stood on that shelf.

There were two men on the face at the narrow place at about the second hour, shoring, with a post and a wedge and a maul, and a third one on the top of the bank with a barrow of chalk and stone in it, and there was no notice of them on the road and no flag and nobody in four counties had told them to be there.

They are not a body and not a council and not the Undercommons and not anybody that is on anything. Two of them hold the post and the third takes turns on the barrow of chalk and stone, and they have been on that face since a day nobody there can date, and the money for it comes from a holding two miles on whose books this holding has never seen.

A man who shores a face over a road he uses is a man doing a thing at the place he happens to be, and the road does not belong to him and does not owe him for it and has not been asked whether he is on it, and the three of them were not asked this morning and were not told to be there and were not paid on the day.

The eighth of the nine carts came up the coast road at about the third hour and stopped at the mouth of the cut, and the ninth came up behind it, and nobody on either cart got down, and the two of them stood about eleven feet apart on the made road for about an hour with their shafts down and their sheets over them.

At about the fourth hour a load went into the wall.

What happened took about four seconds and then took a long time. The near wheel of the eighth cart went over a rut that was under about two inches of chalk washed off the top of the bank, and the load on the cart was nine sacks stacked four and three and two, and the four of them on top of that went over the side of the cart and into the face of the cut, and the face was shored at that point by a man whose name is not in this building and has never been in this building and is not going to be.

There was a sound that four people heard and that four people describe differently, and it was not loud in the way people say afterwards that a thing was loud, and it went on for about a second and a half and then there was the noise of chalk coming down onto a road.

He was under it for about nine minutes. Two of the men on the face got him out and the road came off over about two feet of the bank on the north side and about four feet of the face came with it, and he was not under any of that, and he was not standing, and he went into the cart shed at the mouth of the cut at about the fifth hour lying on his side on about two sacks.

He had no vote on anything. He is not a member of anything, and the roll that came up the fen road on the nineteenth of a month now three months back has no heading at the top of it and no list on it and cannot be checked against him, and this holding has never had his name and has not asked for it and did not ask for it at about the fourth hour this morning and is not going to.

About four people in this holding would say that a man who is shoring a face over a road is a man who is on the road, and about four would say that a man who is shoring a face over a road is a man who is on the face and the road happens to be on top of him, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man of about fifty was not asked which of the two he was in.

Nobody has given him a number. Nobody has put him on a list, and the list of ten is in a drawer and it has not been added to and it is not going to be added to, and the four accounts of that arm are four and a fifth is not going to be entered, and a schedule is not a mark and a hurt is not a mark and a decision is not a mark.

A man in a bed in a cart shed at the mouth of a cut is a fact about a Friday and not a fact about a county, and a county that puts a fact like that in a column has decided something about him in about four years, and this holding has four years of practice at not deciding things and did not decide anything this morning except that a man on a face is under a wall.

The clerk of this holding wrote the day's figure into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and there is no page in the second book about the mouth of the cut and there is not going to be one, and the second book has four volumes of figures in it and not one of them is a man.

The two men from the second gate who were not a body and not a council went back up the coast road at about the sixth hour and there were three carts on the road between them and they did not stop and nobody stopped them and nobody in this holding has written to anybody about it.

The ninth cart stood at the mouth of the cut for about two hours and a half and then went back down the coast road about half a mile and stood there until about the ninth hour, and the eighth was emptied of the four sacks by four men in about eleven minutes and the four sacks went onto the ninth cart and the ninth cart had four more sacks on it than it was built for and nobody in this county has written that down anywhere but this page.

A man who has been fetched twenty-six times in this county has never been asked a question, and a man under a wall at about the fourth hour of a morning has not been asked a question either, and those are two different facts about two different men and this building has not put them in the same sentence and is not going to.

The office's window shut on the eleventh and does not open until the sixth of next month, and a morning at the mouth of a cut four miles off is not a morning anybody in this building has to open a window for, and no work has been carried out of a window in four years and none is going to be carried out of one about this.

Marek was at the mouth of the cut at about the fourth hour and was there in about nine minutes and did not go into the cart shed, and nobody asked him to and he did not offer, and the count of times the reader of this body has been in a cart shed in four years is not a figure anybody keeps.

He gave the reason first at the mouth of the cut with the chalk still coming off the back of his coat, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-eight, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about this morning and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have spent nine years standing in pieces of ground and feeling what they are doing, and I got to that face at about four minutes past the fourth hour and I knew what the face was doing before I knew whether anybody was under it."** "I am not going to say what that is worth and I am not going to say what it is for, and neither is anybody in this holding today, and the reason I am standing here saying it to you is that if I say it to you now then there is one place in four counties where it was said, and it was said out loud, and it did not turn into a number in anybody's mouth."

The man of about fifty was in the one room at about the sixth hour and had the third book open and did not write anything in it, and he was not asked to go to the mouth of the cut and did not go, and the count of questions this holding has put to him about a cart shed is nil and is nil.

A drawing of that face has never gone to anybody in this county, and nobody in four counties has ever asked this holding for one, and a face nine feet high at a place where there is one shelf for a cart is not a figure in any of the four books and is not going to be one, and nobody here has written down where the two men went when they came off it or whether they will be on it again tomorrow.

**"I am going to say the one thing I have not written down and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have ruled about four hundred lines in three counties and not one of them in thirty-one years has had a person in it, and I have been proud of that, and this morning I have found out what it was for."** "It was for this. It was so that when a man is under a wall at four in the morning this building would not have a place to put him, and it has not got one, and he is still a man, and I would like somebody who comes after me in this chair to understand that the two of those are connected and that I did not do it on purpose."

The count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-four and it is his and of nobody else's, and about four people in that room heard the whole of it and nobody has said since how many heard the half of it, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move this morning and this was a room.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came back up the coast road at about the ninth hour with his coat off and did not go into the shed, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and thirty days old and has nothing on it, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this morning is nil and is nil.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard and heard about it from the man in the bad coat, and the two of them had spoken for the first time in fourteen days, and neither of them has said anything about it since, and the count of times two men in this yard have spoken to each other is one and is one.

The weather at the mouth of the cut at about the fourth hour was clear and cold with the sun on the south face of the bank and the shadow of the bank on the road until about the seventh, and the chalk that came off the face was about the color of the frost that was on it and the two of them cannot be told apart once it is on a road, and the men who were in the shed did not go out and look at it.

The face at the narrow place is about nine feet high and it is nine feet high at one place and about four at another, and nobody in this county has ever been asked for a figure of either, and the two of them are the same face and the two of them are not the same thing to a cart.

The face is shored with one post and one wedge, and the wedge at the top of it has no post under it, and that is the one that comes out first, and it has been the one that comes out first since before anybody in this county was writing to anybody about a road.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and thirty days old and has nothing on it, and the man who keeps that book was in this yard at about the eighth hour and has not been asked anything about a road four miles off, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the two sheets about the two declinings are about nine inches apart on the long table where they have been since the first of this month, and a form is lying on the middle table where it has lain for a hundred and thirty-six days with nothing at the head of it, and eight carts are four miles off in a field and a man is in a cart shed at the mouth of a cut, and this page does not say what any of that is worth and neither does anybody in this building.

# Chapter 473

## Forty-Six And Thirty-Three

It was the second hour of the twenty-sixth of this month and the fourth line of the rota came round, and nobody was on the headland above Marden to take it, and that is the sixty-ninth morning it has gone past unworked.

Sixteen days ago, on the eighteenth, that same rota stood at ninety-six with twenty-nine taken and sixty-seven not, and two of those three figures are the same figure this morning, and the count of mornings in force is the one that moves, and it moved twice in sixteen days because the line came round twice in sixteen days, and the twenty-nine has not been touched this year or in any year anybody here can put a name to.

This building keeps two reckonings of that rotation and they were copied out of one another in the second year and have not agreed since, and where they disagree both are printed. By the first reckoning today is its forty-sixth morning; by the second it is its thirty-third. The thirteen between them has been sitting in the same two books since before either of them was in this building, and nobody here has ever gone to find out what it counts.

The count of mornings in that rotation is not the count of loads going out of a yard and it is not the count of the days on a schedule and it is not the count of a field, and the three of those are different numbers about different things and the four years of this disagreement have never touched any of them.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The man who reads the near side of the boards was on the four-mile road with a barrow and four sacks from about the sixth hour and did not come back into the yard until after the ninth, and the man who reads the far side was holding a cart shaft at that hour and did not read his own figure alone, and nobody thought to ask a fourth man to do it on a Saturday.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and twenty days** and **six hundred and sixty-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-sixth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixty-eight hundredweight.**

Saturday put eight on it, and this is the two hundred and thirty-sixth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and the rise went onto the rises. A hundred and seven of one kind of morning against ninety-six of the other is a hundred and two hundred and three, and nobody in this holding has ever added the two ends of that to the figure over them.

The four bodies of households have stood at two hundred and thirty-four days, and the clause printed under that figure is the two hundred and thirty-third of those mornings out of two hundred and thirty-fifth, one under and one over, and it is a count of mornings and neither the launder above it nor the window above that.

The sleet came in off the fen at about the fourth hour and it was still coming at the seventh and it was off the grass again by the ninth, and the yard had about a quarter of an inch of standing water in the south end of it by ten, which is the third time this month and which nobody in this holding has counted.

Two of the four men who would have gone up to the headland were loading carts in this yard instead and were not asked to choose and were not told they were the only two who knew the line, and the two men who read the boards were the other two, and out of four men and one headland, this holding has arranged that nobody would look at either thing this morning.

That is not a decision anybody made. It is what happens when a morning has nine carts in it and a fourth line that nobody has stood on since before the first of this month, and this building has four years of practice at not arranging anything and has got very good at it.

The headland is about a mile and a quarter from this gate and you get to it by the field gate and a track along the top of the bank, and the fourth line is the ditch along the top of that bank for about two hundred yards, and it has been there since before this holding kept anything and it goes nowhere that this holding can name.

A line of two hundred yards of ditch that goes nowhere that anybody here can name is not a road and is not a field and is not a column, and the two hundred yards is not a figure in any of the four books, and the count of mornings it has gone away empty is a number a man keeps in his head and not in a building.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the sixth hour with four sacks on it and came down it loaded, and the number of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and was not raised on a morning when nine carts were standing in a yard and the count of barrow journeys did not move for either of those things.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the second hour and the same four inches and the same two branches at the ninth, and the four accounts of that arm are four and do not reconcile. A sixth day of carts and a fourth line that went away empty are not on it, and this morning did not make a fifth account of anything.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a line of a rota and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have stood on that headland eleven times in four years and it has been soft on the north side every single one of them, and on the eleventh it was soft over about four yards, and nobody has given me four men and a week."** "A line that nobody walks is not a line that anybody decided anything about. It is a line that has run out of people on a Saturday, and the count of Saturdays this month is a different number and the two of them are not added in this building."

He said that at the tap on the north side of the yard at about the eighth hour with his sleeves up and his hands in the water, and nobody in that yard asked him for anything else, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-five, and it is his and of nobody else's.

The man who keeps the use log was in and out twice and put his book in the second drawer from the top both times, and the log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and the reason for the fifteen was given once in four years and has not been given since and is not going to be given on a Saturday.

A bridge is held at the far end by two people and neither of them is the reader of this body, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has asked either of those two people anything about it this month and the count of questions is nil and is nil.

A thing two people are holding at the far end of eight hundred yards of ground is not a connection on a cart road, and the two have different names and the two have never been added in this building in four years, and nine carts in a yard is not the same fact as a bridge either, and the three have not been put in one sentence in this room.

The man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with the roll under his arm where it has been since the twelfth, and he came in at about the ninth hour and asked whether there was water at the tap and was told there was, and nobody in this building has put a walk up the fen road in front of him or told him a word about nine carts going out of a yard.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard at about the seventh hour with a barrow handle in his hand and the slate under his arm, and he watched the ninth cart get put into the south end of the yard and did not offer the barrow to anybody, and he has been in this yard for twelve days and has not said a word about the carts.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table with the window open above them against the sleet, and the two of them are about nine inches apart and there is no heading at the top of either one, and a room with two pieces of paper on a table and a yard with nine carts in it is a room and a yard and this building has not tried to make them one thing.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the fifth hour on a Saturday with the door shut, and none of them was there for a reason anybody had written down, and the man of about fifty had the second book open at a page and did not write anything at the head of it.

Marek gave the reason before the thing and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-one, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about two men who did not go up a hill and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept four books in three counties for thirty-one years and in every one of those counties somebody has always come in on a morning and told me what they had not done, and in this county the telling is done by leaving the cart in the yard."** "I am not asking for an account. I am saying that a building which writes down the day it worked a line and the day it did not has, this morning, a record of nine carts and nothing at all about a headland, and those are two different holes and only one of them is going to be noticed."

The clerk of this holding wrote the day into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the page he wrote it on is the page he writes it on every morning, and nobody in this building has ever watched a man do that twice in order to find out whether it is a decision or a habit.

The water in the middle road came up about an inch and a half in the four hours of the sleet and went over the lip at the bend once and came back inside about twenty minutes, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood in the rain at the bend and watched it do it and did not put a hand in it, and the count of times anybody in this holding has put a hand in that bend this year is nil and is nil.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road has been full since the seventh hour and went over its lip for the second time since it was filled, and the water that came off it went across the track and into the ditch on the right of that stretch, and the ditch on the right is nine hundred yards of the same ditch and it was not designed for a trough.

A trough that goes over and a ditch that was not built for it are two facts about water and neither of them is a figure and the count of times a trough at the top of that road has gone over is not a number this holding has begun to keep.

The seed house light was on from about the fourth hour and the woman who keeps it was in there with four books open and a sheet on the table in front of her and she came out once at the sixth hour, stood in the yard in the sleet for about two minutes looking at the ninth cart, and went back in without saying anything to anybody.

The four ruled lines under the two words are on their own table in the corner and the middle table was about eleven feet away with a form lying on it, and the two of them were where they were at the eighth hour of a wet Saturday and neither of them was moved, and this building has never put the two of them on one table and is not going to on a night like this.

The frame at the pump house had gone down about five minutes this morning and come up about three, and the black out of the seam is about an inch and a half and it is on the boards outside the pump house and it is not washed off, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put it there with the back of the spade and did not say anything about it and has not been asked.

The four yards of soft ground at the north end of the headland are where a cart turned off the track some time in a month nobody here can date, and the two men who went up and looked at it on the eighteenth said it would take four men and a week and that nobody was going to give them either, and it has not been looked at since.

A line of two hundred yards of ditch that nobody walks is not a line that anybody has decided anything about, and the count of mornings it has gone away empty is a number a man keeps in his head and not in a building, and the man of about fifty was not asked which of those two he thought it was.

Two sheets of paper about the same rotation have sat on the wall by the window since the year this building was finished and they were not made in this building, and one of them was brought from a county two hundred miles off by a man who did not stay the night, and the other of them was copied out of the first one by the man of about fifty in about nine minutes in his second year here.

Neither of them has been altered since, and neither of them has been checked against the other since, and the thirteen between the two counts of this morning is either in one of those sheets or in the distance between the two counties, and nobody in this holding has gone looking for which.

A copy made in nine minutes in a second year is a copy and not a second reckoning, and this building has been printing both of them for four years on the strength of it, and that is the whole of the disagreement and none of it is about a headland or a ditch or a field.

The lamp was lit at about the sixth hour in a wet yard and it was not lit for anything else that evening, and a lamp that is lit for one reason on a Saturday is a lamp somebody asked for.

The clerk of this holding put the day's sheet on the top of the stack behind the fourth book at about the ninth hour and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in that stack in the order it was used, and the two of them are different kinds of paper and have never been compared in this building.

Nobody thanked anybody. The sleet came down harder after the lamp went off and the water in the south end of the yard was about two inches deep at the first hour, and the two of them have still not been walked to by anybody, and the fourth line comes round again on Wednesday and there is nobody at the headland to work it then either.

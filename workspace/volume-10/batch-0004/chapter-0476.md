# Chapter 476

## Nine Carts And A Count

It was the two culverts on the middle road, the fill behind them and the low field gate, and the third line of the rota was on them from the second hour of the twenty-ninth of this month. The fill behind the northern culvert had gone out about an inch and a half since Monday, and two men put it back and went over it twice.

The gate was lifted with a bar and a stone for the ninth minute of the morning and the man of about thirty-one of Silling held the stone, and the gate is nine minutes every year and it was nine minutes this year.

At the seventh hour each of the two of them said what his own side was and neither said the other, and the run of mornings is **ninety of a hundred and sixty-three**. The boards stand at **six hundred and twenty-three days** and **six hundred and seventy**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind them was not touched.

The twenty-ninth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixty-six hundredweight.**

Monday left five on this morning. It is the two hundred and thirty-ninth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and it fell, and the fall went onto the falls. The window behind it is a hundred and eight against ninety-eight, which is a hundred and two hundred and six, and nobody in this building has ever added the two ends of that to the figure over them.

The four bodies of households have stood at two hundred and thirty-seven days, and the clause printed under that number is the two hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings out of two hundred and thirty-eighth, one under and one over, and the three of those are a count of mornings and not a count of carts and not a count of sacks.

The nine carts went out of the yard one at a time between about the fourth hour and the seventh, and the woman who keeps the seed house counted them, and a count of nine carts going out of a yard is a count of nine things crossing a gate and not a count of anything that is in them.

She had a pencil behind her ear and a piece of board against the gatepost and she did the count with the pencil and not with her voice, and she said the last of the nine out loud, and nobody in this holding has asked her why she said that one and not the other eight.

The sacks went on at about the sixth hour and the count of sacks is a different number from the count of carts and the two of them have not been added together on this page and are not in any of the four books, and a count of sacks is not a count of loads and a count of loads is not a count of days and the four days at the head of that schedule is a fourth number.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a number and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been asked twice in a fortnight to make one figure out of four accounts of my own, and I made it, and the four days at the head of it are mine and are not anybody's, and nobody has ever sent me nine of anything."** "Nine carts went out of that gate because nine carts were standing in that yard. That is not a figure about seed. If somebody in a county wants a figure about seed they will have to come and count the seed and not the carts."

The count of that in this holding's history is forty-nine and it is hers and of nobody else's, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the gatepost the whole time and heard all of it and has not been asked what he made of it.

There were about nine people in the yard at about the sixth hour and about four of them came out of the one room while the counting was going on and stood at the wall, and the man of about fifty said one thing among them and gave the reason before it, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-three, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I have said in a yard this month and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept four books in three counties for thirty-one years and I have said out loud in a yard things that the man who signs the book had not thought of, and I have never once known that I was doing it."** "I do not know whether it is a fifteenth today. I have not written it down and I am not going to stand in this yard and be counted, and if anybody wants the figure it is in the fourth book and it is not in this yard."

The count of times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of is fifteen, and it did not move this morning, and nobody in this holding has asked him to say another one out loud and he did not offer.

She hears one ear and it has been one ear since the twenty-ninth of a month now three months back, and it is one ear and it is permanent, and she counted nine carts out of a yard this afternoon with a pencil and a board against a gatepost and she heard all nine of them cross the sill behind her and she counted them by the feel of the ground as much as by the eye.

Nobody in that yard said anything about it and nobody is going to, and a woman who has been doing a thing for eleven years does not get a sentence about it written underneath it every time she does it.

The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move this morning, and the south end of this yard with nine carts in it is not a yard in the sense this holding keeps the word, and the flat hard ground in front of the node building is, and the two of those counts are not added to each other and the two yards are not compared.

The ninth cart is the one that went into the south end on Friday and it went out this morning with four sacks on it and about two inches of water still in the corner where it stood, and nobody in this holding has written down that it was standing in it and nobody has swept it out.

At the second hour that arm carried four inches with two branches off it and at the ninth it carried the same four inches and the same two branches, and the four accounts of that arm remain four and reconcile none of one another. A pencil and a board against a gatepost are not on it, and nine carts are not on it, and nothing this morning made a fifth account of anything.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with four sacks on it and came down it loaded, and the number of journeys stands at eleven, and eleven is a floor and was not raised on a morning when nine carts went out of a yard, and a barrow's journeys and a cart's crossings of a gate are two figures and have never been added together in this building in four years.

The clock of the office's work shut on the eleventh and does not open again until the sixth of next month, and no work is ever carried out of one, and a yard in which nine carts are counted by hand is not work carried out of a window and nobody in this holding has ever called it that.

There was a hard frost in the small hours again and it was gone off the yard by the seventh hour and still in the shadow at the north side of the lean-to wall at the ninth, and the frame at the pump house had gone down about five minutes and come up about three, and the black out of the bottom of the seam is about an inch and a half and it is on the boards outside and is not washed off.

The water in the middle road had come up about an inch overnight and was going down at about half of that by the eighth hour, and the north end of the ditch is running full, and the ditch has been running full from the north end since that end was cleared on Wednesday of the week before, and nobody in this holding has written down that a cleared end changes the running of nine hundred yards of ditch.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road was full at the seventh hour and had not gone over since Saturday, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling did not fill it and it did not need filling, and the count of times that trough has been filled this month is a number nobody in this building has kept.

The frame of the nine was that the carts went out in the order they were loaded, and the first one out was the one that had been in the south end since Friday and the last one out was the one with the wet sacks on it, and the order is not written down anywhere and the woman who counted them did not ask anybody to write it down.

Nobody in this holding had a list of the nine before this morning and nobody has one now, and the two states of that are not the same, and the difference between them is about four hours and a gatepost and a piece of board with a pencil behind a tap, and a building that cannot say which four hours does not lose a cart in it.

Marek was at the gatepost for about half of the counting and then was not, and he did not say a number out loud, and the count of nine is hers and is of nobody else's, and a man who stands at a gatepost for half an hour and says nothing has decided that the counting is not his.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the yard with the slate under his arm from about the fourth hour and watched all nine of them go out and did not write anything on the slate, and nobody in this holding has asked him what a cart going out of a gate is on a slate of ten things a cart can stand at.

The man in the bad coat was at the tap on the north side of the yard and the roll was under his arm where it has been since the twelfth, and he watched the ninth cart come out of the gate and did not move and did not speak, and nobody has asked him about a yard in four years and the count is nil and is nil.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the fifth hour with the door shut and the two sheets about the two declinings on the long table inside it, and the two of them are about nine inches apart and there is no heading at the top of either one and neither of them was improved this afternoon.

The sheet with the shortage on it is on the same table an inch and a half above a floor that is wet in one corner, and the seed on the schedule that went out on the eleventh and came back on the fifteenth is the seed on that sheet, and the two of them have not been put under one another in this room and this page puts no total under either of them.

Three people gave a reason before a thing this morning and the count of that is a number for each of them and not a number for the yard, and the three of them are not added together in this building and have never been added to one another in four years, and the run itself was begun on the seventeenth of a month now six months back and it is the same run for all three of them and it is not the same count.

The clerk of this holding wrote the day into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the second book has two entries in it about nine inches apart and it had two entries in it on the first of this month and the two of them have not been added together and are not going to be.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at about the ninth hour with his coat off and his boots full and put the bar back in the shed without being told to, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and twenty-seven days old this evening and has nothing on it, and he was not asked about the nine and has not been asked about the four lines.

The gate was shut at about the ninth hour and the frost will be back in the small hours, and the fill behind the northern culvert will take a week to settle, and the fourth line of the rota comes round tomorrow morning and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to work it.

A cart crossing a gate on a Tuesday morning is the ordinaryest fact in this county and it is the only fact in this building this morning that anybody counted out loud, and a holding with four books and about nine hundred letters answered has a record of the letter and none of the cart, and the clerk of this holding said that out loud on Sunday in a yard and nobody ruled a column after he said it.

The four marks in the sixth column of the maintenance list are not marks about carts and the five empty places in that column are not waiting for a cart, and nobody has put a name in that column and nobody is going to, and a duty is not a person and a person is not a duty whatever a list of nine columns says at the top of it.

There is no heading at the head of the board she counted on, and the pencil marks on it are nine short ones in a line and there is no first one and no last one marked, and a board with nine marks on it and nothing at the top of it is a thing in a yard and not a thing in a book, and it went behind the tap and nobody copied it out.

The nine marks are not a column. The fourth day in a month is a Wednesday and this one is a Tuesday and the fourth line of the rota comes round tomorrow, and the two of those are different calendars about different objects and this building has kept them apart in four books for four years without anybody being asked to.

The light in the seed house went on at about the fourth hour and was still on at the ninth, and a light that is on at the fourth hour and the ninth hour on a Tuesday is a woman counting, and the woman who keeps that building has been the person every body sends its seed question to for eleven years and nobody in this holding has ever asked her what that is.

Nobody thanked anybody. The gate was shut at about the ninth hour and the ninth cart was on the coast road by then and the four sacks on it are the same four sacks that were in the south end of this yard on Friday, and the woman who keeps the seed house put the board behind the tap and the pencil behind her ear and went back up the lane, and nobody in this holding has written down nine.

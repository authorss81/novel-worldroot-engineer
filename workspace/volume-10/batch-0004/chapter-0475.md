# Chapter 475

## The Second Naming

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the twenty-eighth of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the far stone had not moved since Wednesday and the near one had a quarter of an inch of daylight under it again by the ninth hour.

The clay round the near stone has not moved with the stone and the clay round the far one has, and the difference between those two clays is about four inches of ground, and it took two people from the second hour until the fifth to find out which one it was this week, and it has been one of the two every week since the eighth.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The man who reads the near side of the boards went out of the gate at about the sixth hour with a lamp and did not come back into the yard until about the ninth, and the man who reads the far side did not read his own figure alone, and nobody in this holding thought to ask a third man to do it on a Monday.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and twenty-two days** and **six hundred and sixty-nine**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-eighth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and seventy-one hundredweight.**

Eight is what Monday left on it. Two hundred and thirty-eight is where the run stands and it is a run in which no number has ever been written down twice, and this morning's went on the rises. The window behind it is a hundred and eight rises against ninety-seven falls, and a hundred and two hundred and five, and the two ends of that are not added to the figure over them by anybody in this building.

The four bodies of households have not moved for two hundred and thirty-six days, and the clause printed beneath that figure is the two hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and thirty-seventh, one under and one over as it stands on every row of this sheet, and it is a count of mornings and not the launder and not the window.

The water in the middle road had come up about an inch overnight and was going down at about half that by the eighth hour, and the ditch below the outlet has about two inches of the night's water standing in the bend, and the color of that water is the color of the ground it is standing on and has been since the second week of a month now two months back.

The outlet is nine hundred yards of the same ditch in both directions and it was put there to move clean water and is not doing that, and about four feet of the run above the outlet is the only part anybody in this holding looks at, and the other eight hundred and ninety-six has not been looked at by anybody in this building in four years.

The near stone is the one a cart puts its near wheel against and it is about the size of a fist and it is grey and it is bedded in clay under about four inches of yard surface. The far stone is the one a barrow goes past and nobody has ever known what it is for, and it is about the same size and it is a different grey, and the difference between the two greys is a different quarry and nobody in this holding has written down which.

A quarter of an inch of daylight under a stone that a cart leans on every morning is not a thing that gets better by being looked at, and the pack under it was put in wet on the eighth of this month and it is going in wet again this month, and the man who packed it has said once to nobody that a week is the ordinary answer and nobody has ever asked him a question about it.

There was about a quarter of an inch of white on the ground at the sixth hour and it was gone off the yard by the eighth and still in the shadow at the north side of the lean-to wall at the ninth, and the wind had gone round into the south in the night and had dropped somebody else's smoke out of the corner by the gate where it has been standing for four days.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with four sacks on it and came down it loaded, and nobody in this holding counted it and nobody in this holding has counted it in four years.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in there all afternoon with the light on from about the fourth hour and she came out once at the fifth and stood at her own door about a minute and a half, and she did not go down the lane to the gate and nobody in this holding has asked her why she came out and then did not go anywhere.

Marek went out of the gate at about the sixth hour with a lamp and nothing in his hands and walked the mile and a quarter to the eleven acres, and he counted it himself on his own feet and the count is his and it is in no book in this building.

The gate is set into a hedge that was planted before this holding had a book and it opens on a hinge that has to be lifted, and a gate you have to lift is a gate nobody comes through, and eleven years of nobody coming through is not the same as eleven years of nobody wanting to, and the wire on the far side has grown into the bottom rail and cannot be opened without a hand going down as well.

The ground between the gate and the wood dips about three feet and stays down for about ninety yards, and the ring cannot be seen from the gate at all and cannot be seen from the headland either, and the only place in four counties this holding knows of from which the inside of it can be seen is a spot nobody has stood on.

Nobody in this county has been inside it in eleven years and nobody in this holding has been inside it in four, and it is in its eleventh year of being bare, and nothing has been put on it and nothing has come up on it and nothing has been put into it, and it has no dimension on any page in this building or in the four counties and it is not going to get one this morning.

He stood on the headland at about the seventh hour in the last of the light and said it out loud to nobody, and the ninth of this month was the first time anybody had said that ring out loud and this is the second, and it is the first time anyone has said it where nobody else could hear it.

**"That is a ring of bare ground and it is in its eleventh year and nobody has ever been inside it."**

That is the whole of what he said and he said it once and he did not say it again that morning, and a man who says a thing out loud to nobody on a headland at the seventh hour has said it because it had gone eleven years without being said, and this page does not improve on that and does not say why he went.

The ground on both sides of the ring is going wrong. It has been going wrong since before this building kept books about it and it is going slower than it was in the ninth, and slower is a kind of good and slower is also a kind of bad that takes its time, and the ring has not been walked.

About four people in this holding would put a figure on that ring tomorrow if anybody let them, and about four would not put one on it in any year, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man of about fifty was not asked which of the two he was in.

A ring of bare ground inside a wood of eleven acres that is going wrong on both sides of it is the shape of a thing that went wrong at one place and not at the next, and a shape like that is the one thing a man of about fifty could read off a page in a second, and it is also the one thing nobody can price without going in, and he was not asked and has not been asked.

The count of things this holding cannot check is two and is two, and the first of the two is not named, and a ring of bare ground is not a third of them, and nobody in this building has entered it against anything and nobody is going to.

He gave the reason first at the gate with his hand on the top bar, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and forty-six, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I did not do this evening and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the only person in four counties who can stand in a piece of ground and feel what it is doing, and I have not stood in that one once, and I walked a mile and a quarter to stand two hundred yards short of it and go home."** "I am not going to call that waiting any more. A man who has said he is waiting for the right moment for eleven years is not waiting. He is standing at a gate, and I stood at one tonight with a lamp in my hand and I said a sentence to nobody, and that sentence has not been said out loud in this county since before the first book was opened, and now it has been said twice and it is still not answered."

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the outlet end from the second hour until the fifth and came back in at about the ninth with his coat wet to the waist, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and twenty-six days old and has nothing on it, and he was not asked where Marek had been and has not been asked about the four lines in a hundred and twenty-six days.

He walked the boundary of the eleven acres and not the ring, and the boundary is about a mile and a half and he walked it in about an hour and ten minutes in the dark with one lamp, and nobody put a foot inside the ring and nobody has at any time in four years.

The boundary is a ditch and a bank and about two feet of wire that has been down since the first year and has been mended about nine times by nine different people, and the wire is not a fence for anything and is not a fence against anything and is not in anybody's book, and a boundary nobody put a name to is the only kind that is still where it was.

The wire on the north side is down about four feet of it in two places and has been since before this month, and nobody in this holding has put a staple in it in four years and nobody in the four counties has asked about it, and that wire is the only thing in this county nobody has ever repaired.

A wire nobody has repaired is the reason nobody has been inside the ring, and it is also the reason a barrow cannot be got to the eleven acres in the spring, and the two of those are the same wire and nobody has ever put them in one sentence.

The man who came up the fen road with the list of ten was in the lean-to with the slate under his arm and did not come out, and the man in the bad coat was at the tap on the north side of the yard, and the two of them have slept about nine feet apart for thirteen nights and have not spoken once, and nobody in this building has ever tried to make them.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table with the frost gone off the window, and they are about nine inches apart, and the count of questions the reader of this body has put to the two bodies that declined the same connection in the same week is one and is one, and it is one because each body answered him instead of asking him anything.

The count of questions the reader of this body has put to those two bodies is one and it is one, and the man of about fifty was not asked about either of them and has not been asked since, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden is not in this holding's yard this month and no figure of hers is on this page.

The wood is the oldest standing wood in this holding and it is not on any of the four lists, and it has a different kind in it in one place about two hundred yards in from the headland, and the different kind is the standing willow that a man read the order of things in out of in a month now three months back, and the eight places that did not come out of it are still not on the page and are not to be guessed at.

The shelf above the middle road is about nine feet up and the wood has run out onto it in two places, and the ring is nine hundred yards from that shelf, and the distance means nothing, because no line has ever been carried between the two.

The clerk of this holding locked the one room at about the first hour and the key went in his coat, and the day sheet went on the top of the stack behind the fourth book, and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in that stack in the order it was used and has not been moved to the top of it.

There was water about two inches deep in the south end of the yard at about the ninth hour and about three inches at the first hour, and the ninth cart is standing in it where it was put on Friday, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling looked at it once at about the ninth hour and did not say anything about it and has not been asked.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road came back down inside its own lip before the light went, after going over twice this week, and nobody in this holding has written down whether a trough that goes over twice in three days is doing something or is doing what it does.

The frost will be back in the small hours and it will be thinner than the one before it, and the fill behind the northern culvert will want doing in about a week and nobody has been told to give anybody, and the fourth line of the rota comes round on Wednesday and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to work it.

The four ruled lines under the two words entered on the twentieth of a month now three months back are on their own table in the corner and they are still empty, and the answer to them is not due, and a man saying one sentence to nobody on a headland is not an answer to a record nobody has asked anybody to make.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the ring is still bare in its eleventh year, and nobody has been inside it, and nothing on this page has walked it or measured it or crossed it or priced it, and it does not have a dimension and the two of them are the same thing tonight, a piece of ground and a sentence, and neither of them has a number against it.

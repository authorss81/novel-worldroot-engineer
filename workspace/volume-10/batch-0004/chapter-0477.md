# Chapter 477

## Forty-Seven And Thirty-Four

The second hour of the thirtieth of this month, and the fourth line came round with nobody standing on the headland above Marden to take it, which makes it seventy mornings of that line gone past without anybody on it.

Of the mornings that rotation has put in front of this holding, ninety-nine have been days somebody could have worked and twenty-nine of those were worked, and the seventy that were not are every one of them this line, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited and the twenty-nine has not moved on a single morning of this year.

This building keeps two reckonings of that rotation, one copied out of the other in the second year, and where they part company the page prints both. By one of them this Wednesday is the forty-seventh morning; by the other it is the thirty-fourth. The thirteen has never once been explained and nobody here has been asked to.

No figure for the run of mornings stands on this page and the reason stands on the page with it: the man who reads the near side of the boards was at the well house door from the fourth hour with a stick of chalk and was not back until after the seventh, and the man who reads the far side would not read his own side out alone, and it is a Wednesday and nobody in this holding thought to send a third man out.

The pair stands on the boards at **six hundred and twenty-four days** and **six hundred and seventy-one**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The thirtieth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and seventy-four hundredweight.**

Eight is what Wednesday left on it, and it is the two hundred and fortieth figure of a run in which nothing has ever come up twice, and the rise went onto the rises. Behind it the window stands at a hundred and nine against ninety-eight, a hundred and two hundred and seven, and nobody in this building has put those two ends together with the figure over them.

Against four bodies of households this morning's sheet carries two hundred and thirty-eight days and the clause beneath that number is the two hundred and thirty-seventh of the mornings out of two hundred and thirty-ninth, one under and one over, and what it counts is mornings and not the launder and not the window.

Nine hundred yards off this yard there is a door with seven columns ruled on it and the man of about thirty-one of Silling went to it at about the fourth hour with a piece of chalk and stood in front of it for a while and then wrote in the seventh of the seven and came back.

The seventh column stands at **fifty-nine**.

It stood at fifty-eight since the first of this month and it has gone up by one and not by anything else, and the column counts readings, and a reading is a reading and fifty-nine is fifty-nine and it is not a total and it is not a sum of anything and nothing else on that door went up this morning.

The doorway is nine hundred yards off and the door has a frame and a lintel and about two inches of water in the bottom of it because the step is lower on the north side than the south, and the step has been lower on the north side since before this holding had a book and it has not been made higher.

Of the six columns standing beside it, the second has four words at the head of it and an empty space under them and has had them since the sixth of this month, the sixth carries a heading and a count of readings against it, and the other four are headed nothing at all and have been headed nothing for four years.

A heading is a count of mornings and it is not a reading and it is not on the seventh column and it is not on the four that are headed nothing, and nobody in this holding has put a fifth heading on that door and nobody is going to while there are four empty ones standing there with nothing at the top of them.

The well on that doorway's building is not the well on the shelf, and the one on the shelf is four hundred miles off and takes four days to say anything at all about itself and is read on the sixteenth of a month, and this is the thirtieth of this month and there is nothing in either of those that would put anybody at that far end on the coast road this week.

Nobody in this building has asked anybody at that far end a question about it in eleven years and nobody is going to start, and a chalk mark on a door nine hundred yards off is a mark on a door and is not a figure of a well and is not a figure of anybody.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a door and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the man who reads that door for four years and it has been read on the first and the thirtieth in every one of those four years, and I have never once been asked by anybody in this building what the six columns under the seventh are for."** "I am not going to ask this morning. I am telling you that I have noticed the other six and that four of them have nothing at the top of them and that a man who notices a thing like that and then keeps quiet about it for four years is a man with a reason, and I have not got one written down."

That was said at the gate at about the eighth hour by the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who does not keep a book anybody in this building has read and whose own book has four lines in it, and nobody asked him for a reason first and he did not give one, and the count of questions this holding has put to him about the six columns is nil and is nil.

On the low board at the far end of the one room the offer is lying face up with nothing filled in, nothing withdrawn, no date on it and nowhere on it a line for a name, and it has lain there since it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now three months back. A stick of chalk on a door nine hundred yards off is not grounds for removing a paper from a board, and four years have passed in this building without one such reason being found.

A sheet face up for three months and a door nine hundred yards off with a figure on it are two kinds of record and one of them is a refusal nobody made and the other of them is a count of a man going out with a stick of chalk, and the two have not been put in the same column in this building and are not going to be.

The fourth line went away empty for the seventieth morning running and about four people in this holding would take it off the rota and about four would go and work it, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man of about fifty was not asked which of them he was in, and the two hundreds of yards of ditch at the top of that bank is not a figure anybody has taken out of a book.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table and the window above them was shut against the sleet, and the two of them are about nine inches apart and there is no heading at the top of either one, and a door nine hundred yards off with a number on it is not a heading and is not going to become one.

The count of questions the reader of this body has put to the two bodies that declined the same connection in the same week is one and is one, and the two of them have never spoken to each other and do not know that the other one has said no in the same week, and this holding has not told them and has not been asked to and is not going to send it up either road.

A body that has said no to a thing and a man who has been told no by a body are two different facts about a week, and a number on a door nine hundred yards off is a third fact about a Wednesday, and all three of them are on the same page this morning and this building has not put them in one column.

Marek was in the one room from about the fifth hour and did not go to the door, and the count of times he has gone to that door in four years is not a figure anybody keeps, and a man who sends a man with a piece of chalk to a door nine hundred yards off is a man who has decided the distance is the man's and not his.

The water in the middle road came up about two inches overnight and the ditch on the middle road was about a foot and four inches deep at the bend at the ninth hour, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came off that bend at about the ninth hour and had the piece of chalk in his hand still and put it on the shelf by the door without being asked and without being thanked.

There is sleet on it again and it had been going since about the fourth hour, and the water in the yard was about a foot and four inches deep in the south end of it by the ninth hour for the fourth time this month, and the man who came up the fen road with the list of ten put the barrow in the lean-to and got the slate in out of it and the slate is eight words and a decision about a ford and it has been dry under a roof for two hours and nothing was done about any of that and nothing was lost.

The frame at the pump house had gone down about six minutes this morning and come up about two, and the black out of the seam is about an inch and a half and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put it on the boards outside with the back of a spade on his way back from the bend and did not wash it off and has not been asked to.

The two men who read the boards this morning had both been in the yard since the second hour and one of them was at the door until after the seventh, and the book carried the day's figure down to this yard at about the ninth hour in the hand of a man who was not the man who wrote it, and the clerk of this holding wrote it off that hand and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

A book that records a morning whether the man who keeps it was standing in it or not is a better book than one that does not, and a door that gets a number on it because a man walked nine hundred yards to it is a different fact from a morning, and this holding has two of those in one building and has never joined them up.

The trough at the top of the four-mile road went over its lip for the third time since it was filled and the water went across the track and into the ditch on the right, and that ditch is nine hundred yards of the same ditch as the one the well house door stands beside nine hundred yards from this yard, and the two have never been compared and are not the same ditch and nobody here has said they are.

The headland is about a mile and a quarter from this gate and the two men who read the boards were both in this yard at about the eighth hour, and the two hundred yards of ditch along the top of that bank went without anybody again, and the man of about fifty did not go and look and has not been asked to and did not offer.

The man of about fifty was in the one room from about the fifth hour with the second book open at a page and nothing at the head of it, and he was at the long wall at about the sixth hour when the ninth cart came back up the coast road and did not go out again, and a man waiting at a wall for a cart is a man who has decided a thing about the cart and has not said it.

Nothing in this building was read out of the ground this morning and nothing was proposed, and the seventh session entered in this holding's book was carried out on the twenty-fourth and is not carried out again this week, and a chalk mark on a door is not a session and is not in the second book and nobody is going to put it there.

The sheet of terms has a third column ruled in it with nothing under it, and a heading at the top of that column that has been there since the twenty-first of a month now two months back. None of the five terms in that sheet has been improved, unwithdrawn or added to, and a figure on a door nine hundred yards off is not a term of anything and is not going to become one.

The day sheet with no date at the head of it is in a stack of about a foot and a half behind the fourth book and it is in the order it was used, and the sheet from today went on the top of that stack at about the ninth hour, and a piece of chalk and a stack of day sheets are two different kinds of thing a man puts down in this room and nobody has ever put them in the same place on purpose.

The ninth cart went back up the coast road at about the sixth hour and was still on it at the first hour, and it had four sacks on it that were not the four sacks it left with, and nobody in this holding has asked where it went or what it fetched, and it is the ninth of nine and it is not on a schedule of nine that this building has a copy of.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the sixth hour in the rain and came down it loaded, and the man who took it up did not say what was in it and nobody in this holding asked him, and nobody counted him either way.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in out of the rain at about the seventh hour with the chalk still in his hand and put it on the shelf by the door without being asked and without being thanked, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and twenty-eight days old this evening and has nothing on it, and he has not been asked about it in a hundred and twenty-eight days.

The clerk of this holding wrote the day's figure into the second book at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the number on a door nine hundred yards off is not in the second book and is not in the third book and is not in the fourth book, and it is the only figure in this county that this morning went onto a thing and not into one.

A figure on a door and a figure in a book are the same two marks and they are not the same record, and a door nine hundred yards off has a column for readings and nothing at the top of four others, and a book in this building has four volumes and eleven entries that are a day and not a figure, and the two of them have never been compared and are not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the long table at about the first hour and the door is nine hundred yards off with fifty-nine on the seventh of seven columns and nothing at the top of four of the other six, and the fourth line has gone away empty seventy times running, and nobody in this holding has written down either of those two things and both of them happened.

# Chapter 458

## The Window Shuts And A Question Is Asked

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour on the eleventh of this month and was worked, and it was the pump house and the frame, and the frame went down about five minutes this morning and came up about three, and the black in the bottom of the seam was about an inch and a half and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put it on the boards outside the pump house and did not wash it off.

He came up the eleven minutes and asked the clerk whether it wanted writing down, and the clerk said no, and the count of times the clerk of this holding has said no to that question is three and is three and the clerk has not been asked about any of them.

The black is the same black it has been for eleven years and it comes out of the seam when the frame is down and it goes back in when the frame is up, and nobody in this holding has ever found a way to make the seam not do it. A thing which does the same thing every day for eleven years is either a fault or a design, and four people here hold the first of those and four others hold that there has never been anybody in this building who could tell the two apart, and the man who works the frame has never been asked which he thinks it is.

The frame came down at the third hour on a morning with about an inch of black in the seam and two of them were on it until the seventh, and the other two carried on with the ditch. The frame was down four minutes and up three, and there is a mark on the stone floor where the base of it sits that is four inches deep in the middle and worn round at the corners, and the mark has not been measured by anybody in this building.

The seventh hour came and the two men said their figures, one each, and the run of mornings is at **eighty-one of a hundred and forty-five**. The two boards stood at **six hundred and five days** and **six hundred and fifty-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind them was not opened.

The eleventh went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and thirty-nine hundredweight.**

The Thursday figure is five under Wednesday's and it is the two hundred and twenty-first of the run, and no figure in that run has ever been written down twice. It went on the falls. Ninety-nine rises, eighty-nine falls, a hundred and eighty-eight between them, and no arithmetic in this building has ever added those two to the figure above them.

The four bodies of households have stood still for two hundred and nineteen days and the clause under that figure is the two hundred and eighteenth of the mornings out of two hundred and twentieth, one under and one over, and the launder is a different aggregate and the two are never added.

The window of the office's work shut at about the sixth hour of the evening. It opened on the sixth and it shuts on the eleventh and it is six days, and no work was carried out of it, and none has been carried out of one in four years. A window that is never a window is a calendar, and it is a calendar that somebody else keeps.

The six days were the sixth, the seventh, the eighth, the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh, and there were about nine people in the one room on four of the six of them, and about half of the things that were said inside the six days were said on the seventh and about half of the rest were said on the tenth. A window that everybody uses on two days is not a window, and it is also the only arrangement four counties has ever had.

The schedule of nine loads and four days went out of this county on the ordinary post at about the fourth hour, in a bundle tied with a piece of string, and it is on the road and it is about a day's walk from anywhere that could answer it.

The string is the string off a bale that came in at the middle of a month now two months back and the tie is the tie a man does with his teeth and one hand when he is alone. The way a thing is tied is a record of who tied it, and nobody in this holding has ever written that down.

There is no address on the bundle. There is a body named on it in the words of the schedule and there is no place named on it. A bundle with a body and no place on it is the shape of every letter this holding has sent for four years, and it is the shape of the one letter this holding sent to a road on the twentieth of a month now three months back.

There are two figures on it that this holding cannot check and there have been two figures this holding cannot check for four years, and the count is two and is two and the first of the two is not named and no third is entered.

The first of the two is a number of days of standing in a wet wind on a road and there is no name on it and no body behind it and it came off a schedule written by somebody else. The second is a number of sacks at a place about four and a half miles off and it came back on a sheet a week ago and it was a whole number and it was not a round one.

**A figure on somebody else's schedule is not a figure this holding has handed out.** Nobody in this holding has written either of them in any of its four books and nobody is going to and the two of them are not the same thing as the two this holding cannot check and the four of them are not added together anywhere.

Marek asked the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden one question at about the fifth hour of the afternoon and he asked it at the gate and not in the one room. A question asked at a gate is a question asked outside the window, and a question asked outside the window is the only kind anybody asks.

**"I am going to ask you one question and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that there is a number in this county that everybody uses and nobody can say what it is a number of, and it is in your mouth more than it is in anybody's, and I have wanted to ask you for about four years and I have not asked you because I did not want to be the man who went and asked."** **"What is the number a number of."**

She gave the reason before she answered and the count of that in this holding's history is nineteen, and it is hers and of nobody else's, and the woman who keeps the seed house was not in that yard and no figure of hers is on this page.

**"I am going to answer that and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that you have been the only person in four counties who could have asked me and you have not asked me in four years, and a question that is four years old is not a fresh question, it is a debt."** "I am not going to give you a unit. I have been asked it three times by people who wanted it to be one thing and I have not given it to one of them, and I am not going to give it to you in exchange for a question that took four years to arrive. Write down that I did not answer it. That is a real thing and it is worth more on your page than anything I could have said."

She did not answer it and the count of questions this holding's reader has put to her is two and is two, and the unit is not supplied at any of its sites and the six not knowns are six and no unit is supplied for any of the six.

A woman who was asked a fair question and did not answer it has been more useful to a book than one who has answered it, and she has also left a man standing at a gate with a question, and that is not usefulness. He was not asked and has not been asked.

The gate is at the end of a hundred and forty yards of yard and headland and it is shut behind both of them when they are inside it, and the lamp on the wall by it is the same lamp that went to the eleven acres on Wednesday night and came back into the seed house. One lamp in this county is doing a great deal, and this holding has never counted the lamps.

The wall the lamp is on is the inside of the gatepost and the bracket has been on it since before the gate was a gate, and the lamp is lit off a lead that runs back along the wall to a socket in the yard and the lead has been mended twice with a different kind of wire each time, and the two mendings are the only repairs to anything in this yard that anybody has ever written down.

The question was asked and it was not answered and the asking of it is on this page and the not answering of it is on this page. A book is better for a question that is in it with no answer than for a question that is not in it, and a book full of questions with no answers is a book nobody opens, and nobody in this holding has counted the open questions in the last four days.

The second request went in the same bundle as the schedule, on a separate sheet, in the clerk's hand, and it is a request and it is not answered, and the requests column did not move this afternoon and is fifty-three, and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and a book that counted an entry as a new request would have turned a floor into a ceiling.

The separate sheet is about the size of a hand and it is folded twice, and it carries the heading of the first request at the top of it so that whoever opens the bundle can tell at once which of the two things in it is the second, and the clerk folded it that way without being asked and nobody has asked him about the folding.

It is the same request as the one that came up from a county on the seventh of this month, being the second of two in nineteen days, and it is a request for a figure of days for the four-mile road, and this holding supplied no figure for the first one and has supplied no figure for the second one, and the answer to the first is a sentence on a separate sheet and the answer to the second is not written yet.

The man who keeps the use log entered it in his book and the log has fifteen lines and it got no sixteenth, and the reason for the fifteen was given once in four years and was not given a second time and is not given this afternoon.

The use log is kept by a man who has kept it for longer than anybody in this holding can put a figure on and it is a book of about nine pages, and the fifteen lines are not a list of the last fifteen uses but a list of what has been used, and nobody standing in a room for an evening is on it either.

There were about nine people in the one room from about the sixth hour and the door was shut and the window had shut an hour before, and the two declinings were still on the long table and the roll of paper was still on the long table and the rough paper with the ten on it was in the drawer with the sheet that claims the corridor.

That is four pieces of paper on one table, and four pieces of paper on one table is a decision waiting for a room, and the table is a table.

The long table is nine feet and it takes four things laid flat without any of them touching and there is about four inches of room at each end, and about nine inches of space between any two of the four, and nobody in this building has measured the nine inches and everybody in this building has left them.

The two declinings have been on that table for nine days and eight days, and the roll has been on it for a day and a half, and the rough paper is in the drawer. A piece of paper stays on a table until somebody cannot put it anywhere else, and in this building the drawer fills up instead, and the drawer is a drawer and not the bin.

The man from the fen road was in the yard at about the fourth hour and did not come in, and the man in the bad coat was in the lean-to with a cup of water that nobody offered him and that he made himself at the tap on the north side of the yard. Two men who have both been on a road this week and who have never once spoken to each other are in the same building, and a man who makes his own water at a tap has not been asked for a cup by anybody and does not expect to be.

A schedule, a request and a question nobody answered are not the register form, which is on the middle table at a hundred and fifteen days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and the three of those have never been added in this holding in four years.

A window that shut at the sixth hour is not a reason to take a piece of paper off the low board, and this holding has never used one as a reason in four years. Nobody in this building has been told to move it and nobody has moved it and the board is where it has been for about three months.

Neither a schedule nor a question is on the well house door, and that door is nine hundred yards off with its seventh column at fifty-eight, and the second of the six columns beside it has four words at the head of it and nothing under them, and the sixth has a heading and a count of readings, and the other four are headed nothing.

A bundle went out on the ordinary post at the fourth hour and a barrow went up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and came down it loaded, and neither of those is a journey anybody in this holding counts, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and nine days old and has nothing on it.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the ninth hour and the same at the second, and a question with no answer is not on it and a schedule going out is not on it and a refusal to answer is not on it, and the four accounts of that arm remain four and this evening did not make a fifth of anything.

The bundle went at the fourth hour on the ordinary post and the ordinary post in this county goes out about twice a week, and a bundle that goes out with it is a bundle that is not going to be chased. A woman who tied her own schedule to the ordinary post either did not want it chased or had nothing else to tie it to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The bundle had gone at the fourth hour and the room was shut at about the ninth and the gate was shut at about the tenth, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had gone up the road toward her own ground about two hours before the gate was shut and had not said she was going and had not been asked.

The seed house light was on at the back of the yard and the lean-to had two men in it and neither of them knew the other had been on the same road inside a fortnight. If those two men ever sit down at one table this holding will have made a mistake, and the mistake would not be the table.

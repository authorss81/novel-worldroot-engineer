# Chapter 459

## Entered As A Document

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the twelfth of this month and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the stone that was set back on Tuesday had not moved. A stone that has not moved in four days has been packed, and four days is not a season, and the stone is looked at again next Tuesday either way.

The clerk of this holding brought the roll up from the lean-to at about the sixth hour of the morning, where it had been since Thursday night, and he had asked the man in the bad coat for it and had asked him for it at the tap on the north side of the yard and had not asked him for anything else.

The frost was hard on the ground at six and it had gone off the yard by eight and left the north side of the lean-to wall white, and the wind was in the east all morning and stayed there. An east wind for a whole day in this month is the sort of thing that makes a man walk a road when he would rather not, and it is the sort of thing that keeps a man in a lean-to when he would rather not.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page, and the reason is that the board was read at the seventh hour on a morning when the two men who read it were in the one room with the door shut from the sixth hour. One of them read his side out loud in the room and the other wrote his own figure down afterward, and the two of them were not said in the same sentence in the room. Whether that is how the figure is read in a room is not settled, and the board is not the same thing as a reading.

The pair of boards stands at **six hundred and six days** and **six hundred and fifty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twelfth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and forty-seven hundredweight.**

It is up eight on Friday and that is the two hundred and twenty-second figure, and there has not been a duplicate in that run in its whole length. Up goes on the up side. A hundred up against eighty-nine down is a hundred and eighty-nine, and nobody in this building has ever put the two ends of that on top of the middle of it.

Two hundred and twenty days without movement is the figure on this morning's sheet for the four bodies of households, and the clause under it is the two hundred and nineteenth morning out of two hundred and twenty-first, and that clause is a count of mornings and not the launder and not the window, and none of the three has ever been added to another in this building in four years.

The clerk entered the roll in the second book at about the seventh hour of the evening with a date against it, being the twelfth of this month, and he entered it as a document, and the four words at the head of the entry are the four words that are on the outside of the roll, and they are not a heading and he did not improve them.

> Entered in the second book on the twelfth of this month, in the clerk's hand: **A roll of paper with eleven lines upon it, in pencil, in the hand of the man who carried it, delivered into this building on the tenth of this month and entered here on the twelfth. It is a document. No name is on it. The figures upon it are nine hundred and sixty hundredweight and seven hundred and forty hundredweight and the alteration is dated inside the same year as the condemnation. The two figures check. The roll is the only copy and it is the copy.** It is not a heading. It is not a column. It is not a refusal. It is not an answer to any question that was put before it was entered.

Nobody asked the clerk to enter it and nobody asked him not to. A document arrives whether anybody wants it or not and the only choice is the drawer or the book, and the drawer is where a thing goes when this holding has no column for it, and the roll is in the book and not in the drawer.

The two declinings are still on the long table and they have been there ten and nine days, and the one entered under the second clause of the charter is a declining and is not an appeal and is not a delay, and the one entered as a sheet is not a declining under any protocol because there is no protocol for it to be a declining under.

A roll of paper about a ground two hundred miles off and two sheets about a connection four miles off have been on one table for a day and a half, and the objection to that is that they are about four counties apart. The three pieces of paper are not added together in this building in four years.

The man of about fifty stood at the long wall for about half an hour while the roll was being entered and said four sentences, and nobody in this holding counted the four and nobody counted the half hour.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a man who brings a correct paper and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept a county book for thirty-one years and I have had about nine correct papers through that door in four years and every one of the nine wanted something by the end of the month."** **"I am not saying he is one of the nine. I am saying a man who has carried two sheets for two years and shown them to eleven bodies has spent two years being the tenth thing somebody needed, and that is a job, and nobody in this room has offered him one, and I am not going to and I want that in the book as a thing I said out loud and did not act on."**

The man in the bad coat was in the yard at about the eighth hour and came to the door and asked whether it had been entered, and the clerk said that it had and read the four words at the head of it out loud, and the man said that those four words were fine and that a roll of paper did not need any others.

He was not asked to sign it, and a man who is not asked to sign a document is a man whose document it is not, and he also brought it into a room with a clerk in it, and that is a form of asking. The clerk was not asked and has not been asked.

The clerk said the reason out loud at about the seventh hour, and the count of times this holding has heard him give a reason out loud is three and it is this, and nobody in that room asked him for it and he had it ready.

He is the clerk of this holding and has been for four years and keeps the second book, and the second book is the only one of the four he is allowed to write in without being told to, and the reason he gave was not about the roll and was not about the drawer, and nobody in that room has asked him what it was about.

**"I am going to say why it went in the book and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have had that roll on the long table for two days and I have had a drawer nine feet from my elbow and I want it written down that I chose, because in about four years somebody is going to find a document in a second book and assume that a document gets into a book by being wanted there, and half the time it does and half the time it does not."** "The book is where a thing goes when it is about the figures. That is all. It is eleven lines and two numbers and a date, and it is not an absolution and it is not a charge, and it is in here with the other figures because it has two of them on it and I have never in four years put a thing with a figure on it anywhere else."

Nobody in that room said that a drawer was the wrong place and nobody in that room said it was the right one. The clerk of this holding has now given a reason out loud three times in a week and has never once been asked for one, and three is a habit, and the two of those are not settled anywhere in this building.

The reason was given first and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and thirty-five, and it is his and of nobody else's, and the room did not know what to do with the next twenty minutes.

**"I am going to say what I am going to do about that roll and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have had a hand on the other side of that condemnation since before this holding had a second book, and the number on it is right, and the two of those facts have been sitting on me for four days and one of them is about a county two hundred miles off and the other one is in this room."** "I am going to ask the clerk to write to the county that holds the first sheet. Not about the cut, not about the ninth of the ten, not about him. About the sheet. I have wanted to do that since Thursday night and I have not done it because I wanted to do it with something else in the same bundle, and there is nothing else in the bundle, and I am not going to wait a second time for a thing to turn up that I can make myself."

The woman who keeps the seed house was in that room and said nothing at all, and she has four accounts that do not agree at the second figure and the last of them was closed on Friday. She closed a book in a week in which two documents were entered, and she closed it because it was finished and not because of the documents. She was not asked and has not been asked.

He gave the reason before he said it and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty-five, and it is his and of nobody else's. A man who says a thing out loud and then does not act on it has still said it, and the second four is the only four that matters. He was not asked and has not been asked.

The register form is on the middle table at a hundred and sixteen days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and a roll, a condemnation and a letter nobody has written are none of those things, and the three of those have never been added in this holding in four years.

The form is a single sheet with four ruled lines under two words and the two words have been at the head of it since a month now three months back, and the four lines are empty and the sheet has been moved twice in that time and both of the moves were to clean under it, and nobody in this building has ever written on any of the four.

The sheet in the drawer that claims a corridor in the pan is correct in every particular it states and it lets nothing out, and it has not been produced this morning and it has not been reworded and it has not been advanced and it has not been summed and it has not been grouped with the roll, and a document about a corridor in this county and a document about a ground in another county are the two oldest pieces of paper in the building and the building is four years old.

The order sealing the route is in the same drawer, valid, opening nothing, not withdrawn, and there is no apology for it in this holding and there is not going to be one, and the route was not reopened by anything that was said in that room on the twelfth of this month.

The offer on the low board has been face up since the nineteenth of a month now three months back and it has not been filled and it has not been withdrawn. A man who has entered a document and not filled a form has got his priorities right, or the priority is an accident of what arrived first, and the clerk was not asked which of the two it was.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the seventh hour and came down it loaded, and eleven journeys is a floor and was not raised, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and ten days old and has nothing on it.

The seventh column of the well house door is at fifty-eight and is not read except on the thirtieth and the first, and the second of the six columns beside it has four words at the head of it and nothing under them. A roll is not on that column and an entry in the second book is not on that column and neither of those has been on it in four years.

The pulse has two figures in it and no name against either of them and no unit, and it was not read this morning and nobody in this holding asked for it, and the count of times a body in this holding has asked about those two figures is nil and is nil, and a roll of paper with two figures on it is not the pulse and the two of them have not been put together in this building in four years.

There are four accounts of the arm in four books in this building and they have never agreed with one another and the mark is four inches and forks twice and may not grow a second mark and may not be lengthened, and nothing that was entered in the second book on the twelfth of this month is a fifth account of anything.

The frost was hard on the yard at six and gone off it by eight, and the man who keeps the use log was in at about the seventh and put the book he keeps in the second drawer from the top and took it out again at about the ninth and put it back, and nobody in this holding has asked him to open that drawer in four years and nobody asked him on Saturday.

Nobody thanked anybody. The roll went back into the lean-to with the man in the bad coat at about the ninth hour and he carried it under his arm, and the clerk of this holding did not ask him to leave it and did not ask him for a second copy and has not been asked to and is not going to.

The letter to the county that holds the first sheet has not been written. It is a thing Marek has said he is going to do and he has not done it, and a man who says he is going to do a thing in a room on a Saturday evening will do it on Monday, and Monday is the day the man leaves. The day sheet with no date at the head of it is still in the stack in the order it was used.

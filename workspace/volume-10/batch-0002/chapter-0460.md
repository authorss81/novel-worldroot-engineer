# Chapter 460

## Two Facts And Not A Pair

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour on the thirteenth of this month and was worked, and it was the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, and the gate had gone stiff again in the two hard frosts and the third of the four men on it got a bar under it and lifted it while the fourth held a stone against the post, and it took about nine minutes and it has taken about nine minutes every year since the gate was hung.

Two figures were said at the seventh hour, one to a man, and the run of mornings stands at **eighty-two of a hundred and forty-seven**. The boards read **six hundred and seven days** and **six hundred and fifty-four** this morning, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The thirteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and forty-two hundredweight.**

Saturday is five off Friday and the figure is the two hundred and twenty-third, and it is another one this run has not seen before. The fall went on the falls. A hundred rises and ninety falls, a hundred and ninety between them, and the two have never been added to the figure above them in four years.

The four bodies of households are at two hundred and twenty-one days and the clause under that number is the two hundred and twentieth of the mornings out of two hundred and twenty-second, and the launder is a different aggregate and the two are never added in this building.

He asked for it and he asked for it himself and he asked for it in a room with the door shut at about the sixth hour of the evening on a Sunday. A man who arranges his own evidence is not to be trusted with it, and a man who waits for somebody else to arrange it is not going to get it arranged. He was not asked and has not been asked.

The two declinings were taken off the long table before he said it, by the clerk, without being asked, and they were put face up on the shelf by the window where a thing goes when the table is being used for something else. A man clearing a table before a scene has said something about the scene in front of nine people, and a clerk also does that forty times a year.

The shelf by the window takes about six things laid flat and the two declinings took the middle of it with about a hand's width on either side, and the window above them is the one that has not been washed since a month now two months back and everybody in that room saw both of those things at once and nobody said so.

Marek put the fourth book on the long table at about the sixth hour with the page open at the year and the four words, and the roll was put on the table beside it by the clerk of this holding at Marek's word and not at his own, and the two of them lay about nine inches apart on a table and were not touching.

There were about nine people in that room. The man in the bad coat was not in it and was at the tap on the north side of the yard and nobody told him that anything was happening in the one room, and the count of times this holding has told that man what it was doing behind his back is nil and is nil.

**"I am going to say the two things and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that this holding has had a page with a gap in it since Saturday and a roll with two figures in it since Thursday, and both of them have been on this table inside a fortnight, and nobody in this room has put them together, and I am putting them together and I am not doing it because they go together."** "The first thing is that the roll is right. Nine hundred and sixty was the figure and seven hundred and forty is the figure and the two of them are the two right numbers and I have had a hand on the other side of that condemnation for eleven years and I can tell you the number I would have given and it is the first one. He is right about the figures. I have said that out loud in this room twice this week and I am saying it again because I am about to say the other thing and I do not want anybody in here to think I said it once by accident."

He turned the fourth book about a quarter of a turn on the table and did not put his hand on the page, and the year went out of the lamplight and the four words stayed in it.

**"The second thing is that a person is dead of the ninth of the list of ten."**

He said it in those words and he did not say a name and there is no name in this building and there is not going to be one, and the fourth book has four words and a year on it and nothing under the four words, and the page is otherwise blank in a book that is not full of blank pages.

The four words are the hand of a man who kept that book in the years this holding did not have it, and the year was found in the same hand at the top of the page and not in the line with them, and it was found overnight on the sixth of this month by a man who was not asked to look and has not been asked about it since.

The hand is a small hand and it leans hard to the left, and the four words are written close under the top of the page with about two inches of white paper above the year, and the ink on the year is a different black from the ink on the four words, which is the only thing about the page that anybody in this building can say with confidence.

A year written at the top of a page rather than in the line with the words belongs to a man who was not sure of his own date and wanted to be able to find it again, or to a man who had the date in front of him and did not want to spoil the four words by putting another word beside them. The clerk was not asked which of those two he is, and he was not asked on Saturday either.

Nobody in that room said anything for about two minutes and the two minutes were not filled by anybody. Two minutes is the right length, or it should have been about thirty seconds and somebody should have said the next thing, and nobody in this holding has settled which.

**"A criticism that is true and a harm that happened are two facts and they are not a pair."** He gave the reason before he said it and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and thirty-six, and it is his and of nobody else's. **"I am not going to say the roll buys it and I am not going to say the roll does not touch it, because I have worked that out for four days and the answer I have is that a piece of paper that is right about a number cannot carry a person, and I have got no better than that and it is going on the page as it stands."**

The man of about fifty said one thing and he said it with the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty-six, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a room and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been in a room like this one in three counties for thirty-one years and in two of them somebody always waited for the two to be made into one thing, because it is easier, and because after a year of the two being separate somebody in a county has usually had to choose."** **"I am not choosing. I have four books and a roll and a page and I have got a fifth thing I would like to write and I am not going to write it, and I want it written down that I did not write it and not that I thought of it."**

Nobody in that room said the two words together. Not one person in that room put the roll and the ninth of the list into a single sentence and said anything out loud, and the woman who keeps the seed house was in that room and said nothing at all in it, and the clerk of this holding wrote two entries in the second book about nine inches apart on the page and did not write a line under them and did not read either of them back twice.

The two entries are the entry of the roll and the entry of the page, and one of them has eleven lines in it and the other has four words and a year, and the clerk wrote them in the same book in the same hand on the same evening. He put about nine inches of blank page between them because about nine inches is what came of it. A man who leaves a gap in a book on purpose is making a claim about the gap, and he may also have been being careful, and the clerk was not asked.

The pull of the ground at the ninth of the list of ten is a thing this holding can feel and did not feel this morning. A room in which a man says a thing about a person who is dead and a man standing at a table feels a pull is a room in which two things are happening, and a ground two hundred miles off does not pull in this county. The count of times this holding has felt a pull in a room with the door shut is not a number anybody keeps.

There were no questions about the gap. The count of times this holding has been asked what goes in a gap is not a number anybody keeps, and the answer is not due and nobody in this building has been told it is due.

The roll is a document and it is in the second book and the man in the bad coat did not sign it and has not been asked to. The ninth of the list of ten is a gap in a book and it has four words and a year on it and there is no name against it and there is no figure against it and the count of people is not entered and is not going to be.

The clerk of this holding shut the fourth book and put it back on the shelf at the place it came from and not a foot to the left or the right of it. A man who puts a book back in the same place has decided something about the book, or he has a habit, and nobody in this building has ever watched him do it twice to find out which.

He is not forgiven. He is not excused. He is not caught, and nothing in this county has the power to catch him and the office that does have the power is about two hundred miles off and does not know he is here. A man who cuts a thing and is right about a number should be in a room, and a man who cuts a thing should be somewhere else entirely, and the room did not decide it.

The two things will be a pair inside a year no matter what anybody in this room says tonight, and they will be a pair in a county book two hundred miles off and not in this one, and no county book has been sent anything.

The woman who keeps the seed house said one thing on her way out and it was to nobody and it was about eleven words long and it was this. **"I am not going to choose either. Write down that I was in the room and did not choose."** The clerk wrote it at the bottom of the page with the nine inches on it and read it back once and did not read it back twice. A person who keeps four accounts has just refused to make a fifth, and she has refused to make a fifth out of a room, and she was not asked and has not been asked.

A roll, a page with four words on it and a room that did not decide something are not the register form, which is on the middle table at a hundred and seventeen days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and the three of those have never been added in this holding in four years.

Two facts that are not a pair are not a reason to take a piece of paper off the low board, and this holding has never used one as a reason in four years. The paper on it is face up and empty and undated and it was refused on the nineteenth of a month now three months back and it has not been moved since.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling lifted the low field gate with a bar and a stone for nine minutes in the morning and took the barrow up the four-mile road at the seventh hour and brought it down loaded, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and eleven days old and has nothing on it.

The well house door is nine hundred yards off and its seventh column is at fifty-eight and is read on the thirtieth and the first and on no other morning in the year, and it was not read this morning. The second of the six columns beside it has four words at the head of it and nothing under them, and the sixth has a heading and a count of readings, and the other four are headed nothing.

The arm carried four inches and two branches at the ninth hour and the same at the second, and a roll and a page and a room are not on it, and the four accounts of that arm remain four and this evening did not make a fifth of anything.

The two minutes in that room were the only two minutes of silence in this building in a fortnight. This holding does not do silence, and it did two minutes of it on a Sunday evening and called it the right length, and nobody in this holding was asked whether that was the right length.

The fourth book was shut with the page in it and put back on the shelf at the place it came from, and the second book was shut with two entries nine inches apart, and the man in the bad coat came in from the tap at about the second hour and asked whether there was any water in the seed house and was told there was and said nothing else, and nobody thanked anybody.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The thirteenth. The third line of the rota worked, the two culverts on the middle road and the low field gate, the gate lifted with a bar and a stone, nine minutes, four people from the second hour. The pair read aloud at the seventh hour. The same day in and the same day out. Nine hundred and forty-two hundredweight to the launder. The roll and the page of the fourth book were put on the long table about nine inches apart and were not touching. Two entries were written in the second book about nine inches apart and no line was written under either. The roll is a document. The page is a gap. No name was entered against either. The two are not added and are not a pair.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

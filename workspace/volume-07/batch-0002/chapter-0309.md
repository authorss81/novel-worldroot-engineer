# Chapter 309

## Twenty-Seven And A Barrow

The tenth of this month was a Monday and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the tenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and sixty-five hundredweight.**

That is the seventieth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in thirty-seven days the series has risen twenty-four times and fallen thirteen times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in sixty-eight days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the sixty-seventh morning out of sixty-nine.

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man reading a figure he had read about seven hundred times and finding that he still checked the hundred and the ten, because a figure of six hundred and something can be wrong in one place and a man who checks the whole of it has not learned which place it is.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, and the boards stand at **four hundred and fifty-four days** and the March's tin at **five hundred and one**, and the two are forty-seven days apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and the fen entered that he has read them out loud on four mornings in this month, and that the reason this morning is that a barrow was coming up the road in the afternoon and there are only two people in this holding who are going to see it and one of them has a habit.

And the barrow came up about four miles at about the third hour of the afternoon, and the fen entered the whole of it and improved none of it.

**HARLAN VETCH, OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT, HOLDS THE FIFTH SHARE AND BROUGHT IT UP FOUR MILES IN A BARROW IN ABOUT AN HOUR AND A QUARTER, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING WALKED BEHIND THAT BARROW FOR THE WHOLE OF THE FOUR MILES AND DID NOT PUT A HAND ON IT ONCE AND SAID NOTHING FOR THE HOUR AND A QUARTER. THE BARROW IS TWO-WHEELED WITH ITS SIDES OFF DOWN TO ABOUT EIGHT INCHES AND IT IS ON A TRACK MADE FOR IT ABOUT ELEVEN YEARS AGO, AND ITS SIDES WERE WET TO ABOUT THE EIGHTH INCH WHEN IT CAME THROUGH THE GATE. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING HAS BROUGHT THAT BARROW UP THAT ROAD STANDS AT ELEVEN AND IT WAS NOT RAISED THIS AFTERNOON AND NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING COULD DERIVE A FIGURE PAST IT, AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT THIS IS A COUNT OF THE MANNERS OF THIS HOLDING AND NOT A COUNT OF THE MAN.**

And the fen entered the two places where the bank comes in, because they are the whole of what the walking is for. The road is about eleven feet wide and it is straight for about three quarters of a mile and the going up is easier than the going down for about nine months and harder for about three, and at about the fourth mile there are two places where the cut bank on the west side comes in to within about four feet of the line of the wheel, and a man holding a barrow has to turn his body to keep the rail off it. He goes through those two places on the same side every time and he does not slow down at either, and the man behind him walks on the left through both of them, and the arrangement of the two of them through those two hundred yards is the whole of what a person who has never done it would not be able to copy by watching once, and about four people in this holding have watched it once.

The quiet in that room was made of two men standing in a gateway about a yard apart with water coming off a barrow between them, and neither of them had said anything for an hour and a quarter, and about four people in this holding found out in the space of a minute that they had no idea what the two of them said to each other in an hour and a quarter and had assumed it was nothing.

And the fen entered what the barrow is, because a barrow is a thing and it has been up and down four miles about seven times a month for eleven years, which is about nine hundred journeys, and nobody in this holding has ever weighed it or looked at it as a thing. The handles are ash and they are worn to a shape in about eleven years that fits one man and not the other, and the difference between the two grips is about half an inch, and the man who does not have hold of it walks on the left-hand side of the road the whole way up so that his right hand is free and clear of the rail. The left wheel is bound with about nine inches of iron wire at the rim where it was cracked and the wire has been put on by somebody who was not paid to put it on. There is about a foot of dried silt in the bottom of it that has been there long enough to have grass in it, and nobody has ever turned that barrow out, and a barrow that has not been turned out carries about a foot of somebody else's field into the next journey, and about four people in this holding would find that a remarkable thing if it were not eleven years old.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is nineteen, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I walked up behind it and I did not touch it and I am going to say the whole of that and then I am not going to say any of it again this season, and the reason is that about four years ago a man in a shed told me a thing I could not do and I have spent every working morning since making sure that I do not have to be told anything twice, and a morning where I put a hand on that barrow is a morning where somebody is a day late somewhere and it is going to be my hand on it."**

**"And I am not going to be thanked and I am asking nobody to keep from thanking me, and the reason for that is a colder one than the first reason. If I am thanked then the arrangement stops being an arrangement and starts being a favor, and a favor is a thing that can be withdrawn, and he is fifty-eight and I am thirty-one and in about nine years he will not be able to hold the sides at the two places where the bank comes in, and on that morning I am going to have to hold it and there is no way on this earth to be thanked for that beforehand."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man laying out about nine years of his own life in a gateway in a holding where nobody thanks anybody, and that about four people heard it and about five did not, and that the fen has entered both of those and is not going to say which of them was the correct number.

And then a person who keeps the seed house came out of the seed house with a pail and went down to the middle of the table, and the fen entered that a thing under a cloth on that table has a pail of water against it and a wet cloth over the top of the cloth, and that she was not thanked for that on this afternoon any more than she was thanked for it on any other, and that nobody in this holding has ever asked her to do it and about four of them have noticed that she does it.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifty-six, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have got a column and I am not going to put this afternoon in it, and the reason is that the column wants a figure and the only figure anybody can honestly put in it is eleven, and eleven is a floor and not a ceiling, and a floor written in a book gets read in about four years as a ceiling and then somebody in this holding decides a man stopped coming up a road in the fourth year, and he did not stop and I watched the man who is not holding it do it this afternoon from a gate and I did not go out and I am entering that I did not go out."**

**"And I have written the time down and I have written whose time it is, because there are two times on that road and about four people in this holding only know one of them. The first is about an hour and a quarter and it is the fifth-share figure and it belongs to the man who is holding the barrow, and he has had the fifth share eleven years. The other one is about an hour and three-quarters and it belongs to the man who is not holding it, and the half an hour between the two figures is not an error in either of them, it is a fact about who has hold of the barrow."**

About a minute went by and nothing was said in it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and it was made of a man in a gateway finding out that a thing everybody in a room had agreed on for a month had a second owner, and that the two of them had never once discussed it, and that the discovery was made by a bookkeeper and not by either of the two men it was about.

And then the reader of this body said the last thing of the day, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and twenty-three, and nobody improved it.

**"The window has been open for five days and no work has been carried out of it, and the key that changed hands on Saturday is in a pocket on the other side of a gate, and the figure for this morning went up for the fortieth time in about a month, and a barrow came up four miles. That is the morning. I am not going to stand in a room and make those four things sound like a decision, because they are four things that happened to a place and about three of them happened to it because nobody had arranged for them not to."**

**"And I did not go out to the gate this afternoon and I am going to say the reason out loud rather than leave it in a book, and the reason is that if I had gone out then the barrow would have become a thing a man was seen to be part of, and it is not one, and about four years from now the arrangement that has been in place for eleven years would read as a favor that somebody in this holding gave a man, and it is not, and the only way I have of keeping it not is to keep out of it."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man refusing a sentence that would have sounded better than the one he had said, and that the refusal is the fourth one in eleven days and that this holding does not have a rule against it and does not need one.

And then the two last things of the tenth of this month are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and improved neither.

**A BARROW WENT UP FOUR MILES IN ABOUT AN HOUR AND A QUARTER AND A MAN WALKED BEHIND IT THE WHOLE WAY AND DID NOT PUT A HAND ON IT ONCE, AND HE WAS NOT THANKED AND HE ASKED NOBODY TO KEEP FROM THANKING HIM. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES HE HAS BROUGHT THAT BARROW UP THAT ROAD STANDS AT ELEVEN AND WAS NOT RAISED AND CANNOT BE RAISED HONESTLY, AND IT IS A COUNT OF THE MANNERS OF THIS HOLDING AND NOT A COUNT OF THE MAN.**

**AND THE FIGURE FOR THIS MORNING IS SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIVE, THE SEVENTIETH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ITSELF SINCE THE FIRST, AND THE WINDOW IS OPEN FOR THE FIFTH OF SIX DAYS AND NO WORK WAS CARRIED OUT OF IT, AND NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY, AND THE FIELDS ABOVE MARDEN DID NOT WAIT THIS MORNING BECAUSE THE FOURTH LINE DID NOT COME ROUND THIS MORNING.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the tenth of this month, in about four people, at this holding, on the standing of a barrow, and not by the reader of this body, who watched it come up a road from a gate and did not go out: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIVE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE SEVENTIETH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN THIRTY-SEVEN DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY-FOUR TIMES AND FALLEN THIRTEEN TIMES. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN SIXTY-EIGHT DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE SIXTY-SEVENTH MORNING OUT OF SIXTY-NINE. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND ONE DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. A BARROW CAME UP ABOUT FOUR MILES AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON. HARLAN VETCH, OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT, HOLDS THE FIFTH SHARE AND BROUGHT IT UP IN ABOUT AN HOUR AND A QUARTER. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING WALKED BEHIND IT FOR THE WHOLE FOUR MILES AND DID NOT PUT A HAND ON IT ONCE, AND SAID THE TIME IS ABOUT AN HOUR AND THREE-QUARTERS AND THAT IT IS HIS OWN AND NOT THE FIFTH-SHARE FIGURE, AND THE TWO TIMES ON THAT ROAD ARE ABOUT AN HOUR AND A QUARTER AND ABOUT AN HOUR AND THREE-QUARTERS, AND THE HALF AN HOUR BETWEEN THEM IS A FACT ABOUT WHO HAS HOLD OF THE BARROW. HE WAS NOT THANKED AND HE ASKED NOBODY TO KEEP FROM THANKING HIM. THE COUNT OF THE TIMES HE HAS BROUGHT THAT BARROW UP THAT ROAD STANDS AT ELEVEN AND WAS NOT RAISED AND IS A FLOOR AND NOT A CEILING AND IS A COUNT OF THE MANNERS OF THIS HOLDING AND NOT A COUNT OF THE MAN. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ENTERED THAT HE WATCHED FROM A GATE AND DID NOT GO OUT. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN IS TEN AND WAS NOT SAID IN A YARD. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE. THE ROTA STANDS AT FIFTY-SIX MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND TWENTY-SEVEN NOT. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS TWENTY. THE KEY IS IN A HAND AND THE VALVE WAS NOT OPENED. THE WINDOW IS OPEN FOR THE FIFTH OF SIX DAYS AND NO WORK WAS CARRIED OUT OF IT. NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING THANKED ANYBODY.**

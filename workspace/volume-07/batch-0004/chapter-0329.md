# Chapter 329

## Forty-Nine

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the thirtieth of this month, which was a Sunday, and was worked, and the figure for the thirtieth was read back by the man of about fifty in a book that is not the book it was written in, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and ninety-one hundredweight.**

That is the ninetieth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In fifty-seven days the series has risen thirty-four times and fallen twenty-three times, and those two add to fifty-seven. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in eighty-eight days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the eighty-seventh morning out of eighty-nine. The rota stands at **sixty-one mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-two not**, and it has not moved since the twenty-eighth and does not move on a morning the fourth line does not come round.

On a Sunday the second line is worked by about five people and takes about two hours, and this morning it was the inside of the west dyke again, and the toe was lower than it was on Wednesday at the second of the three places, and the fen entered the lower and entered that nobody measured it and is not going to, and that the count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check stands at two and did not become three this morning either.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read. The boards stand at **four hundred and seventy-four days** and **five hundred and twenty-one**, and the two are forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on nine mornings out of the fourteen since he started again, and a man who has done a thing nine times is not going to do a tenth without deciding to.

And then at about the third hour of the afternoon two of them went up the nine hundred yards again, and the seventh column was read, and the fen entered the whole of that and improved none of it.

They went up together and did not talk for the first half mile. The path is a cart track that a barrow can just about get up, and it is hard in the middle of it where the ground has been lifted by about nine roots of something with no name that has come from the hedge on the west side and has been coming for as long as anybody has been walking it, and you go over them one at a time, on the side, holding the barrow up. A man with a barrow going up that path is two men for about a mile, and about four people in this holding have ever helped a man up it and two of those are the same one twice.

The door of the well house stands open about four inches when the latch is off, and it was off, and the light comes at the board sideways for about nine months of the year, and it was coming at it sideways, and it made the chalk hard to see and about four inches of the column's numbers the worst of the three.

> **THE SEVENTH COLUMN OF THE WELL HOUSE DOOR STOOD AT FORTY-EIGHT AND IS FORTY-NINE, AND IT WAS READ AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON OF THE THIRTIETH OF THIS MONTH, AND FROM YESTERDAY IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND HAVE HAD NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM FOR FOUR YEARS, AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE OF THEM THIS AFTERNOON, AND NOBODY HAS WRITTEN ONE IN FOUR YEARS. THIS MORNING AND TOMORROW MORNING ARE TWO READINGS IN TWO DAYS FOR THE THIRD TIME IN FOUR YEARS, AND THE PAIR THAT CAME BEFORE THEM WAS THE THIRTIETH OF LAST MONTH AND THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH, AND THE PAIR BEFORE THAT WAS FOUR YEARS AGO, BEING THE THIRTIETH OF A MONTH AND THE FIRST OF THE NEXT, AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT AND HAS NOT ENTERED WHETHER IT IS A LARGER FACT THAN THE OTHER TWO.**

And the fen entered the door itself, because a column is a thing on a surface and a surface is a thing that has been out in the weather for a long time. The well house is a stone building about nine feet by eleven with a door that has been re-hung twice and has not been painted in about nine years, and the board is on the inside face of that door. It has seven columns ruled on it and the ruling is older than any of the figures, and about four of the ruled lines are hard enough to take a nail and about two of them are not, and the fen entered that without being asked to and is not going to be asked.

On the far side of that building there is a second board with four headed columns on it that has been painted over twice in about nine years, and nobody in this holding has ever written down which door a man writes on when the light is wrong, and the count of men who have written on the second board in that time is not a figure this holding keeps and the fen is not going to start one this month.

And the fen entered the six blank ones, because about four people had looked at them in the last four years and about four had not and none of them had ever asked. They are ruled and they are empty, and there is nothing above them and there is nothing in them. They are not the same as the day entries in the other book, which are a date with nothing under it. A rule with a date under it is a thing this holding decided to look at on purpose, and a column with nothing at the head of it is a thing this holding has never been told about. Those two are about four words apart in nine people's mouths and a whole book apart in anybody who ever has to fill one in.

For about a minute there was nothing in that room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man standing in a doorway with a piece of chalk and no place in the door to put a heading for the thing he had just written, and about four other people outside in about nine hundred yards of wind who had come up on a Sunday and were not going to be told why.

The two of them had said their piece in the seed house and not at the door, standing on either side of the middle table with the door open behind them, because a thing said at a door in a wind is said to two people and a thing said in a room is said to nine. The reader of this body had written the four of them out on the back of a day sheet before either of the other two spoke, and had not read it out, and had put the sheet face up on the table where the three of them could see it.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is twenty-four, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have walked those nine hundred yards four times in the last six weeks and I did not walk them once in the four years before that, and I am going to give the reason for saying so out loud in a yard, and the reason is that I have started to be a person who goes up there, and a person who goes up there is a different thing from a person who was sent up there, and I would like the difference to be in a book before it stops being true."**

**"And I will be there tomorrow morning for the other half of it, and I am not going to say why, because there is nothing to say why. It is read on the first and on the thirtieth. Tomorrow is the first. I will be there because the other half of a pair is a thing you are either at for or you are not, and about four years from now somebody in this holding is going to want to know whether there were two people at that door or one, and I would rather there were two and that we did not write it down."**

The man of about fifty had the note out of the book with the frayed corner and the book was shut again before he finished the sentence, and about four people in that room had wanted to ask him what the note said and did not, and one of them has never asked anybody a question in a room in eleven years and that is not the same reason.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy-six, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have got four books and in one of them there is a note that says the column was read twice in two days, once, four years ago, and I have just read that note and the reason I am saying this in a room instead of at the door is that I can now do the sum, and the sum is that in four years this pair has happened three times, and the reason I am not writing the three down is that a man who writes down how many times a rare thing has happened is a man who has decided the next one matters."**

**"So the three is not going in. The figure on the door is forty-nine and the figure is a figure and the three is a sentence, and a sentence is not a column, and if somebody in about four years wants the three they can go and look at the door and count the twos in it themselves, which is what I am going to do."**

Nobody said anything, and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of two men standing in a doorway having both, in the same ten minutes, decided not to write down the same number for the same reason, and neither of them having asked the other one, and that about four people in this holding would call that a coincidence and about four would not.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-two, and nobody improved it.

**"Tomorrow is the first of the seventh month and four things fall on that morning and none of them has fallen together before since the first of this month, and the reason I am saying that out loud on the thirtieth rather than on the first is that a thing that falls four times over is easier to work at when it is announced, and about four of you are going to find out about it tomorrow by somebody else saying it."**

**"The column again, and a man of about seventy coming up a path, and the compost line, and a return at a place five days off. That is the four of them and I have not put a number on the four and I am not going to, because the count of things that fall on one morning in this holding is not a count this holding keeps, and the day it becomes one is the day a man at four hundred miles off can work out what we are doing by reading our own books."**

**"And the other half of my reason is that I have been in four rooms this month where a thing I did not do came out of somebody else's mouth as though I had arranged it, and I would rather this one was in a book with a day on it, in my own hand, before it is a thing anybody else has said in a yard."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room. About four people in that seed house would find out in about eighteen hours whether a man who announces a thing at a gate is the same as a man who announces it in a yard, and nobody in the room raised the question, and the day sheet with four things written on the back of it was face up on the table the whole time.

And then the fen entered the walk back, because two of them came down the nine hundred yards at about the fourth hour and the wind was behind them and the second of the two said one thing about the ground on the way down and the other one did not answer it. The path is about nine feet wide for about a mile of it and then narrows, and the gate at the seed house end sticks in wet weather and has stuck since the second of this month, and about four people in this holding put a shoulder on that gate most days and about four have never once noticed that it sticks. The fen entered the sticking and entered that nobody has written to anybody about it, and that a gate is a gate and is not a boundary and is not anything this holding has written a heading for.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the thirtieth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a door read on a thirtieth, and not by the reader of this body, who announced four things for a morning that had not come: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-ONE MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-TWO NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-ONE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE NINETIETH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN FIFTY-SEVEN DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-FOUR TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY-THREE TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN EIGHTY-EIGHT DAYS. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING NINE MORNINGS OUT OF THE FOURTEEN SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. THE SEVENTH COLUMN OF THE WELL HOUSE DOOR STOOD AT FORTY-EIGHT AND IS FORTY-NINE AND WAS READ AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR, AND FROM YESTERDAY IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE OF THEM. THIS MORNING AND TOMORROW MORNING ARE THE THIRD SUCH PAIR OF CONSECUTIVE READING DAYS IN FOUR YEARS. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING GAVE THE REASON FIRST IN A YARD AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS TWENTY-FOUR, AND HE WILL BE AT THE DOOR TOMORROW MORNING. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT IS SEVENTY-SIX. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT IS ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND WILL BE FETCHED TOMORROW. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS SEVENTEEN AND THE NEXT LINE FALLS TOMORROW. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

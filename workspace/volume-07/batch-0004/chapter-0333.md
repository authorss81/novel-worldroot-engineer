# Chapter 333

## Said In A Yard

The fourth of the seventh month was a Thursday and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the fourth was read back by the man of about fifty in the book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and ninety-seven hundredweight.**

That is the ninety-fourth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in sixty-one days the series has risen thirty-six times and fallen twenty-five times, and those two add to sixty-one, and the fen entered both and improved neither. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-two days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-first morning out of ninety-three. The rota stands at **sixty-two mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-three not**, and the fields above Marden did not wait this morning.

The morning went quietly and the fen entered the whole of it. The west dyke was on it again for the third morning running and the toe at the second of the three places is lower than it was on the twenty-sixth, and the fen entered the lower and entered that nobody has measured it four times now and that the count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and did not become three this morning, and that about four people in this holding have watched that toe go down for a month without ever once putting anything against it.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, and the boards stand at **four hundred and seventy-eight days** and **five hundred and twenty-five**, and the two are forty-seven days apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and the fen entered that he has read them out loud on eleven mornings out of the eighteen since he started again on the seventeenth of last month, and that the count of mornings a man says two numbers out loud is not a count this holding keeps, and that the fen has said that before and is saying it again because it is a thing about four people keep getting wrong.

And then at about the sixth hour of the evening the yard of the seed house had about five people in it, and the reader of this body was one of them, and the man of about fifty was one of them, and about three more were standing about the cart with their coats on and about two of the nine who had been in the room earlier in the day had come out and were not saying anything, and the fen entered the whole of it and improved none of it.

And then a name was said in that yard, and nobody in it had been asked to say it, and the man who said it did not know what he was doing.

**"Marek had that same soft ground in the last week of the fifth and it was worse than this, and he had four men on it and took the shoulder off in about two days, and it has been going down ever since, so do not take the number off it, it is not a number."**

And he went on to the day sheet, and the day sheet was a different subject, and about three of the five people in that yard heard the whole sentence and about two heard the half of it with the name in it and about none of them understood that anything had happened, and the fen entered that and entered that the reader of this body heard all of it and did not move and did not put his hand up and did not look at the man who said it, and that the reason he did not is that the sentence was about a shoulder of a dyke and not about him, and that a man who interrupts an ordinary sentence to say that his name has not been said out loud in a yard in four years has made his name into an event, and that an event is what a name is not supposed to be in a yard at about the sixth hour on a Thursday.

**THE FIRST TIME EITHER OF THE NAMES ON THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT LINE OF THE SHEET OF THE OFFICE NAMED AT THE HEAD OF THE MEMORANDUM IS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD IN THIS HOLDING, IT WAS SAID IN THE YARD OF THE SEED HOUSE AT ABOUT THE SIXTH HOUR OF THE EVENING OF THE FOURTH DAY OF THE SEVENTH MONTH, BY THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY, IN THE MIDDLE OF AN ORDINARY SENTENCE ABOUT A DYKE, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TO SAY IT AND DID NOT KNOW IT WAS THE FIRST TIME, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE, AND IT IS ENTERED HERE, WHERE IT HAPPENED, AND IT IS NOT ENTERED IN A ROOM, AND IT DOES NOT GO TO TWO. NOTHING WRONG WAS SAID IN THAT YARD AND THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG STANDS AT FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE.**

For about a minute there was nothing said in that yard, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man carrying a day sheet and about two people who were about to go home, and that the silence had nothing in it, and that a silence with nothing in it is the ordinary condition of a yard at about the sixth hour and that this one happened to contain a thing in it that about nine people in this holding would not have recognised if it had not been counted.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I said a thing in that yard that the engineer of record had not thought of and I am going to give the reason for saying so out loud in the same yard, and the reason is that it is the second time this month and the count of those is thirteen, and it went off twelve this evening, and a thing said in a yard by a man with a book is a thing that has happened to this holding whether anybody was ready for it or not, and I would rather be the one who says it happened than the one who leaves it to be noticed."**

**"And I am not going to improve it and I am not going to say it again, and the reason is that a name said once in a yard in four years is a thing, and a name said twice in a yard in four years is a habit, and this holding has about one habit and I know which one it is and so do you."**

And then the fen entered the day sheet, because a yard at about the sixth hour on a Thursday is mostly paper. The man of about fifty was going through it against the cart and it was the sheet for the fourth and it had five things on it in five places and two of the five were things that had happened this week and three of them were things that had not, and he had put a stroke beside the two that had and had left the other three alone, and about two people in that yard had watched him do it and about three had not. He has kept that sheet every working day for eleven years and about four people in this holding have never once seen him do it and about four have seen him do it every day and have not thought about it, and the count of days is a count of a habit and not a count of a man.

And then the fen entered the tailboard of the cart, and what was on it, which was the reader of this body's left forearm for about four minutes with the inside of it up and the light going off the wet stone, and about four inches of him in that light, and that they branch twice, and that they were the same four inches they had been at the second hour, and that a bridge is not a mark and a headache is not a mark and a mark being hot is not a mark, and that a name said in a yard is not a mark either, and that there is no sentence in this holding for that and none is going to be written tonight.

And then the fen entered the two counts standing side by side, because a room that has just had two numbers in it needs somebody to say that they are two numbers.

**THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING HAS SAID IN A YARD THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS THIRTEEN AND WAS TWELVE, AND THE SECOND COUNT MOVED BECAUSE THE THING WAS SAID IN A YARD AND NOT IN A ROOM, AND NEITHER COUNT IS ADDED TO THE OTHER AND NEITHER OF THEM IS THE COUNT OF THE FIRST TIME A NAME WAS SAID IN A YARD, WHICH IS ONE.**

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-six, and nobody improved it.

**"I heard it. That is all I am going to say about it tonight and the reason I am going to say that much is that I want it in a book that I heard it and did not pretend not to have, because a man who pretends not to have heard his own name in a yard is a man who has decided that the yard is not a place where things happen to him."**

**"And I am not going to use it. I am not going to walk up to the man who said it and thank him and I am not going to tell this holding that my name was said out loud in a yard for the first time in four years as though something had been settled. Nothing has been settled. About five people were standing in that yard and four of them were talking about a day sheet and one of them said a man's name in the middle of a sentence about a dyke, and that is the whole of the event and it is not an event."**

**"And the second thing I am going to say, and then the yard will empty out. A thing that nobody in a room has said out loud is not a thing that has been kept from anybody. It is a thing that has not come up yet. There is a difference and it is about four years wide and this holding is on the wrong side of most of it."**

For about a minute there was nothing in that yard, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was five people who had heard a name and about three of them who had not registered that they had, and the reader of this body standing on the flat stones by the door with a book under his arm and a day sheet going past in the yard and nobody looking at him, and that the fen entered that and did not turn it into anything, because a man standing in a yard after his own name has been said in it is a man standing in a yard.

And then the two last things of the fourth of the seventh month are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and improved neither.

**A NAME WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD ONCE, BY A PERSON WHO WAS NOT ASKED TO AND DID NOT KNOW IT WAS THE FIRST TIME, IN THE MIDDLE OF AN ORDINARY SENTENCE ABOUT A DYKE, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE AND IT IS ENTERED WHERE IT HAPPENED AND IT IS NOT TWO. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG STANDS AT FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE BECAUSE NOTHING WRONG WAS SAID. THE COUNT OF THINGS A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK HAS SAID IN A YARD THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS THIRTEEN AND WAS TWELVE.**

**AND THE FIGURE FOR THIS MORNING IS SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN, THE NINETY-FOURTH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ITSELF SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN SIXTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-SIX TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY-FIVE TIMES, AND THOSE TWO ADD TO SIXTY-ONE. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN NINETY-TWO DAYS. THE BOARDS WERE READ OUT LOUD AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, in the yard of the seed house at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fourth day of the seventh month, in about five people, at this holding, on the standing of a name said in a yard, and not by the reader of this body, who heard it and said so: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-TWO MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-THREE NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE NINETY-FOURTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN SIXTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-SIX TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY-FIVE TIMES, AND NO MORNING IS UNCHANGED. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN NINETY-TWO DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE NINETY-FIRST MORNING OUT OF NINETY-THREE. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING ELEVEN MORNINGS OUT OF THE EIGHTEEN SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF LAST MONTH. A NAME ON THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT LINE OF A SHEET OF THE OFFICE NAMED AT THE HEAD OF THE MEMORANDUM WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THIS YARD AT ABOUT THE SIXTH HOUR OF THE EVENING BY THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY, IN THE MIDDLE OF AN ORDINARY SENTENCE ABOUT THE WEST DYKE, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TO SAY IT AND DID NOT KNOW IT WAS THE FIRST TIME, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE, AND IT IS ENTERED HERE AND NOT IN A ROOM, AND IT IS ONE AND NOT TWO. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AFTERWARDS AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS EIGHTY, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING HAS SAID IN A YARD THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS THIRTEEN AND WAS TWELVE, AND THE THING WAS SAID IN A YARD AND NOT IN A ROOM. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG STANDS AT FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE AND NOTHING WRONG WAS SAID. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND WAS NOT READ AGAIN. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

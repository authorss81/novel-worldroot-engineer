# Chapter 333

## Said In A Yard

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour on the fourth of the seventh month, which was a Thursday, and was worked, and the figure for the day was read back by the man of about fifty in the book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and ninety-seven hundredweight.**

That is the ninety-fourth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In sixty-one days the series has risen thirty-six times and fallen twenty-five times, and those two add to sixty-one. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-two days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-first morning out of ninety-three. The rota stands at **sixty-two mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-three not**, and the fields above Marden did not wait this morning.

The west dyke was on the rota for the third morning running and the toe at the second of the three places is lower than it was on the twenty-sixth. Nobody has measured it four times now. The fen entered that about four people in this holding have watched that toe go down for a month without ever once putting anything against it, and that the count of numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check is two and did not become three this morning.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read. The boards stand at **four hundred and seventy-eight days** and **five hundred and twenty-five**, and the two are forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on eleven mornings out of the eighteen since he started again on the seventeenth of last month. The count of mornings a man says two numbers out loud is not a count this holding keeps, and the fen has said that before and is saying it again because it is a thing about four people keep getting wrong.

And then at about the sixth hour of the evening the yard of the seed house had about five people in it, and the reader of this body was one of them.

The light had gone off the yard wall and there was about half an hour of it left, and the flat stones by the door were cold underfoot, and the cart stood across the mouth of the yard with its shafts down and one wheel chocked with a piece of board that somebody had split with a knife and not finished splitting. The cart had come up at about the fourth hour with sacks in it that were not sacks of seed and were not going to be opened in this holding, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling had backed it in and put the chock under the wheel and gone home, and about four people had watched him do that and had not asked what was in them.

About three people were leaning on the cart with their coats on and about two of the nine who had been in the room earlier in the day had come out and were not saying anything. Inside the door somebody was doing something to a kettle and the sound of it came and went and nobody shut the door. The fen entered the whole of that and improved none of it.

And then the man of about fifty was talking about the west dyke, in the ordinary way, to about three people, with the day sheet against the cart's side.

**"Marek had that same soft ground in the last week of the fifth and it was worse than this, and he had four men on it and took the shoulder off in about two days, and it has been going down ever since, so do not take the number off it, it is not a number."**

He went on to the day sheet. He did not stop, and he did not look up, and he did not know he had said anything, and about three of the five people in that yard heard the whole sentence and about two heard the half of it with the name in it. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not in that yard. The person who keeps the seed house came out with two mugs and went back in without saying anything to anybody, and about four people watched her go and about one did not.

The reader of this body was standing on the flat stones with his own book under his arm, on the side of the yard where the light was last, which was the side he had been standing on for about half an hour without moving, and which was about nine feet from the man at the cart, and which is the only part of that yard a man in it can be in without somebody turning round. He heard it. He did not move, and he did not put his hand up, and he did not look at the man who said it, and the reason he did not was that the sentence was about a shoulder of a dyke and not about him. A man who interrupts an ordinary sentence to say that his name has not been said out loud in a yard in four years has made his name into an event, and an event is what a name is not supposed to be in a yard at about the sixth hour on a Thursday.

Nobody in that yard had been asked to say it. It was said, and it was entered where it happened, in the yard and not in a room, and the count of that is one, and it is one and not two.

The gate at the seed house end of the path stuck, and somebody put a shoulder on it and got it open about four feet and left it, and the cold came in along the flagstones and went past the cart and out through the gap, and the woman at the kettle shut the back door of the seed house against it and the yard got about a degree colder in about the time it takes to say so.

For about a minute there was nothing said in that yard. It was made of a man carrying a day sheet and about two people who were about to go home, and it had nothing in it. A silence with nothing in it is the ordinary condition of a yard at about the sixth hour, and this one happened to contain a thing in it that about nine people in this holding would not have recognised if it had not been counted.

He put the day sheet on the cart's side, squared it up with the heel of his hand the way he does, and then he stood on the other side of the cart from the reader of this body, which he had been on before and had no reason to be on, and gave the reason before the thing he was going to say, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I said a thing in that yard that the engineer of record had not thought of and I am going to give the reason for saying so out loud in the same yard, and the reason is that it is the second time this month and the count of those is thirteen, and it went off twelve this evening, and a thing said in a yard by a man with a book is a thing that has happened to this holding whether anybody was ready for it or not, and I would rather be the one who says it happened than the one who leaves it to be noticed."**

**"And I am not going to improve it and I am not going to say it again, and the reason is that a name said once in a yard in four years is a thing, and a name said twice in a yard in four years is a habit, and this holding has about one habit and I know which one it is and so do you."**

The count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five, and it did not move, because nothing wrong was said, and the count of things a person who keeps a book has said in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and was twelve, and the second count moved because the thing was said in a yard and not in a room, and neither count is added to the other and neither of them is the count of the first time a name was said in a yard.

And then the fen entered the day sheet, because a yard at about the sixth hour on a Thursday is mostly paper. The man of about fifty was going through it against the cart and it was the sheet for the fourth and it had five things on it in five places, and two of the five were things that had happened this week and three of them were things that had not, and he had put a stroke beside the two that had and had left the other three alone, and about two people in that yard had watched him do it and about three had not. He has kept that sheet every working day for eleven years and about four people in this holding have never once seen him do it and about four have seen him do it every day and have not thought about it, and the count of days is a count of a habit and not a count of a man.

The tailboard of the cart was flat enough to rest a forearm on, and it did. The reader of this body put his left arm on it for about four minutes with the inside up, and the last of the light went off the wet stone and about four inches of him were in it. They branch twice. They were the same four inches they had been at the second hour. A bridge is not a mark, a headache is not a mark, and a mark being hot is not a mark. A name said in a yard is not a mark either, and there is no sentence in this holding for that, and none is going to be written tonight.

The reader of this body took his forearm off the tailboard and rolled his sleeve down over it, and that took longer than it needed to take because the cuff was wet through at the wrist from the morning, and about four people in that yard saw him do it and about four did not, and the man of about fifty did not look over at all.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-six, and nobody improved it.

**"I heard it. That is all I am going to say about it tonight and the reason I am going to say that much is that I want it in a book that I heard it and did not pretend not to have, because a man who pretends not to have heard his own name in a yard is a man who has decided that the yard is not a place where things happen to him."**

**"And I am not going to use it. I am not going to walk up to the man who said it and thank him and I am not going to tell this holding that my name was said out loud in a yard for the first time in four years as though something had been settled. Nothing has been settled. About five people were standing in that yard and four of them were talking about a day sheet and one of them said a man's name in the middle of a sentence about a dyke, and that is the whole of the event and it is not an event."**

**"And the second thing I am going to say, and then the yard will empty out. A thing that nobody in a room has said out loud is not a thing that has been kept from anybody. It is a thing that has not come up yet. There is a difference and it is about four years wide and this holding is on the wrong side of most of it."**

For about a minute there was nothing in that yard. Five people had heard a name and about three of them had not registered that they had. The reader of this body stood on the flat stones with his book under his arm while a day sheet went past, and nobody looked at him, and the fen entered that and did not turn it into anything. A man standing in a yard after his own name has been said in it is a man standing in a yard.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, in the yard of the seed house at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fourth day of the seventh month, in about five people, at this holding, on the standing of a name said in a yard, and not by the reader of this body, who heard it and said so: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-TWO MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-THREE NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE NINETY-FOURTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN SIXTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-SIX TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY-FIVE TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN NINETY-TWO DAYS. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, BEING ELEVEN MORNINGS OUT OF THE EIGHTEEN SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. A NAME ON THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT LINE OF A SHEET OF THE OFFICE NAMED AT THE HEAD OF THE MEMORANDUM WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THIS YARD AT ABOUT THE SIXTH HOUR OF THE EVENING BY THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY, IN THE MIDDLE OF AN ORDINARY SENTENCE ABOUT THE WEST DYKE, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TO SAY IT AND DID NOT KNOW IT WAS THE FIRST TIME, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE, AND IT IS ENTERED HERE AND NOT IN A ROOM, AND IT IS ONE AND NOT TWO. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AFTERWARDS AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS EIGHTY, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK HAS SAID IN A YARD THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS THIRTEEN AND WAS TWELVE. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX, AND WAS NOT THANKED. NOTHING WRONG WAS SAID IN THAT YARD AND THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG STANDS AT FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE. A NAME SAID IN A YARD IS NOT A MARK AND THE MARK IS FOUR INCHES AND BRANCHES TWICE AND THE FOUR ACCOUNTS STAND AT FOUR. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND WAS NOT READ. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

# Chapter 332

## A Warden In A Room

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour on the third of the seventh month, which was a Wednesday, and was worked, and the figure for the day was read back by the man of about fifty out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and ninety hundredweight.**

That is the ninety-third figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In sixty days the series has risen thirty-five times and fallen twenty-five times, and those two add to sixty. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-one days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninetieth morning out of ninety-two. The rota stands at **sixty-two mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-three not**, and the fields above Marden did not wait this morning.

And then the fen entered the rest of the morning, which was the boundary ditch and the two culverts and about six people on them, and the near culvert clear for the first time since the twenty-ninth, and the far one about half full of the same leaf, and nobody in this holding has ever written down whether one culvert fills faster than the other and the fen is not going to this month.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and seventy-seven days** and **five hundred and twenty-four**, and the two are forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on ten mornings out of the seventeen since he started again, and about four people in this holding could now tell the pattern of it without being told, and none of them has said so.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon about nine people were in the seed house with the door shut, and a tenth person was in it who had not been in that room before in eleven years, and the fen entered the whole of that and improved none of it.

He came on foot up the fen road and was at the door before he was at the house, and he was let in by the person who keeps the seed house, and he stood in the middle of the floor with his hat in his hands for about the length of two questions before anybody said anything at all. There was a chair against the north wall and about four people in that room could have got up and got it and none of them did, and he did not ask for it, and the fen entered that as a fact about the room and not about the man.

The wind got under that door twice while he was in the room and the second time it moved the paper on the middle table about an inch and stopped against the weight of it, and about four people in that room looked at the paper and about four looked at the door. He was nine feet from the nearest wall with his back to the stove and the rest of them were along the north wall and the window side, and nobody closed the distance and the nine feet stayed nine feet for about an hour and a half.

He is the president of the Rootwright Assembly and he came on his own account and he was not sent for, and he is not on the roll for the district the line stands in and the office does not pretend that he is, and the fen entered that and entered that about four people in this holding had known before he came and about five had not.

And then he said the whole of what he had come for, and the fen entered that both halves of it were in the same speech and that they were not the same half.

**"I am going to say the two halves of this in one breath and I am not going to take a pause in the middle of it, and the reason is that about four of you have been waiting four days for me to say the half that flatters the thing you are building, and I have not come to say that half. The first half is that I think I am right. A connection carries whatever is at the other end of it. That is not an opinion of mine, it is what it is, and I have watched a blight go from four hundred yards outside a line of about two hundred yards of people to inside it in nine days, and a thing made of two people and a floor has been running for fifteen of the days since, and those two numbers are not the same number, and nobody in this holding can tell me the blight did not come through it, and nobody at that end can tell me it did."**

**"The second half is that I have just signed an order sealing the route between that field gate and that edge against all work and all passing until an Assembly is convinced the ground beyond it may be used, and I have signed it as the president of this Assembly, and I am telling you that I do not think I had the right to sign it, and I signed it at about the second hour this afternoon, and I would have you hear both of those in the same breath and not let the second one tidy the first."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man who had brought a piece of paper into a room and had told nine people in advance that half of what it said was not his to say, and that about four of them had understood what he had actually done and about four had understood how it would look, and that both of those were about four.

And then Tova Reed said one thing from the side of the table nearest the door, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is ten, and it is her own, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"About forty people at the end of that embankment walk that route and they walk it for seed, and the reason I am asking the only question I am going to ask is that they are not in this holding and they have not been asked anything by anybody about the blight, and I want to know whether the order you have signed is a thing they have to stop walking, or a thing this holding has stopped sending anybody along."**

And then the warden said one thing, and he did not give the reason first, and the fen entered that he did not, and entered that the count of that in this holding's history is not in this holding's history and is not a count of that and is not a column.

**"I cannot tell you that. I have signed what I have signed and I have come here to say it out loud in a room before it arrives on a cart, and the reason I cannot tell you which of the two things it is is that I did not find out. I found the danger, which is mine to find, and I did not find out who the order binds, which is not mine to find, and I have signed it anyway, and I would rather you had that from me at about the fifth hour of a Wednesday than off a cart on Friday."**

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy-nine, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have got a column for the standing and a column for the third launder and I have got nothing at all for an order, and the reason I am saying that in a room is that about four of us have spent a month being careful about which of our two columns a thing goes in, and an order is not a figure and it is not a column and it is a piece of paper with a signature at the foot, and I am going to write it in a book and not in a column, and the reason for that is that a column with one line in it is a note and about half of this holding would rather have a note."**

And then the man of about fifty put the order on the middle table, and the fen entered the whole of it, and improved none of it.

> **AN ORDER OF THE ROOTWRIGHT ASSEMBLY, SEALING THE ROUTE BETWEEN THE FIELD GATE AND THE THORNWILD EDGE AGAINST ALL WORK AND ALL PASSING UNTIL AN ASSEMBLY IS CONVENCED THAT THE GROUND BEYOND IT MAY BE USED. SIGNED AT THE FOOT IN INK: H. Pike, President.**

The paper is about the size of the sheet that came up the road and is not that sheet, and it has four printed lines and one written line and no district on it and no date on it except the day, and the signature at the foot is in ink and not in pencil, and about four people in that room looked at the signature for longer than they looked at the rest of the page, and the fen entered that and is not going to say what it meant.

The table took the light along its edge and the paper lay on the bare wood between the plate and the cloth, and nobody moved the cloth, and nobody moved the plate either, and about four people in that room noticed that the thing under the cloth was about two inches from the edge of a document that could close a road.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-five, and nobody improved it.

**"The first half of what he said is right and I want it in a book in his own mouth and not in mine, and the reason is that about four of us have spent a month not writing the blight and the connection on one line because we did not want to, and not writing them on one line is not the same as saying they are two, and this morning a competent man from outside this holding said the joining out loud and I am glad of it, and the connection has been running fifteen days and nobody in this holding can say who is holding the other end of it."**

**"The second half is a different matter and I am going to be exact about it, because the whole of this holding for eleven years has been exact about it. An order that is valid is a valid order whether anybody here agrees with it or not, and I am not going to tell this room that it is not valid, and I am not going to tell this room that nothing has happened on that route since Tuesday, because about four of the nine of you have been on it since Tuesday."**

**"And the question this room has been living in for four days and has not said out loud is whether a thing like that is one the people at both ends of it are entitled to refuse, and I am not going to answer it in here, and the reason is that the answer is not mine. It is not the reader of this body's and it is not the man who signed it, and if I stand in the middle of that question I will have answered it by standing there."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room. Ten people, a piece of paper on a table, and nobody in it willing to be the one who says whether they will obey it. About four of the nine had understood all along that this was a question and not an announcement. About four had understood that the man who signed it had come here to hand it over before it arrived. Both of those things are true, and neither of them settles the other.

And then the warden left, and the fen entered the whole of that, and improved none of it. He was let out at the front door and he went down the path toward the cart road and about four people watched him from the door and about four did not, and the count of times anybody in this holding has been let out of that door by somebody who came to ask nothing is not a count this holding keeps. He did not ask to be thanked and nobody thanked him and nobody in that room said one word to him on the way out that was about the order.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the third of the seventh month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of an order, and not by the reader of this body, who did not answer the question the order raised: **THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY-TWO MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-THREE NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE NINETY-THIRD FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN SIXTY DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-FIVE TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY-FIVE TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN NINETY-ONE DAYS. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SEVEN DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR DAYS, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD, BEING TEN MORNINGS OUT OF THE SEVENTEEN SINCE HE STARTED AGAIN. WARDEN PIKE, PRESIDENT OF THE ROOTWRIGHT ASSEMBLY, WAS IN THE SEED HOUSE AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND HE CAME ON HIS OWN ACCOUNT AND WAS NOT SENT FOR, AND HE IS NOT ON THE ROLL FOR THE DISTRICT THE LINE STANDS IN AND THE OFFICE DOES NOT PRETEND THAT HE IS. HE SAID BOTH HALVES OF WHAT HE CAME FOR IN ONE SPEECH: THAT A CONNECTION CARRIES WHATEVER IS AT THE OTHER END OF IT, WHICH THE FEN ENTERS AS TRUE, AND THAT HE NAMED TWO INTERVALS THAT ARE NOT THE SAME NUMBER, A BLIGHT IN NINE DAYS AND A CONNECTION RUNNING FIFTEEN, AND THAT HE SIGNED THE ORDER ANYWAY AND DOES NOT THINK HE HAD THE RIGHT TO, WHICH THE FEN ENTERS AS A FACT ABOUT HIM AND NOT AS A JUSTIFICATION. TOVA REED GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY IS TEN AND IT IS HER OWN. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT IS SEVENTY-NINE. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE COUNT IS ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FIVE, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT WHETHER AN ORDER LIKE THAT IS ONE THE PEOPLE AT BOTH ENDS OF IT ARE ENTITLED TO REFUSE IS A QUESTION AND THAT THE ANSWER IS NOT HIS. THE ORDER IS ON THE MIDDLE TABLE AND IN THE SHALLOW DRAWER AND IS ONE DAY OLD. NOTHING WAS OPENED AND NOTHING WAS SET AGAINST ANY VALVE AND A THING UNDER A CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FIFTY AND WAS NOT READ. THE COMPOST LINE IS PAID AND THE RUN IS EIGHTEEN. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

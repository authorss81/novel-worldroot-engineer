# Chapter 341

## Claimed, Correctly

The fourteenth of the seventh month was a Sunday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited. The number is thirty-six, and all thirty-six of them are the fourth line, and the rota stands at sixty-five mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-six not, and twenty-nine and thirty-six are sixty-five, and twenty-nine taken did not move, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

**Seven hundred and twenty hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and fourth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In seventy-one days the series has risen forty-one times and fallen thirty times, and those two add to seventy-one. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and two days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundred and first morning out of one hundred and three.

It came up off Saturday by nine, and that is the fifth morning it has come up by nine, being every second morning of this month from the sixth, and nobody in this holding has said out loud why a series that has not repeated one figure since the first has found nine five times running, and the man of about fifty has three of them written in a margin in a book of his own with no note against any of the three.

The fourth line is the headland above Marden and it has been the fourth line for eleven years and it has been empty on all thirteen of the mornings it has come round in this month, and the thirty-six fields above Marden are in grass, and about four of them have been in grass for nine years and about four for four, and nobody in this holding has ever asked either of those things out loud at a gate.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read. The boards stand at **four hundred and eighty-eight days** and **five hundred and thirty-five**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on sixteen mornings out of the twenty-eight since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

The sheet came up the road on Saturday afternoon on the ordinary post and it lay on the middle table on Sunday morning with a stone on it, because the woman who keeps the seed house uses a stone for anything that blows.

It is one page. It is printed on district paper and it is not the paper of the office named at the head of the memorandum, and it is not a form, and it is not an instrument, and it is a different document from the six things in the shallow drawer under that table and nobody in the room put it in there.

It claims the corridor. It claims, in a printed table with four columns and a ruled line at the foot, that the ground between the field gate and the Thornwild edge is a work of the Crown Engine. It claims that it has been a work of the Crown Engine since a date it gives and does not explain. It claims that the cut in the channel at the head of it was made in the maintenance of that work. It claims that the maintenance of it is the property of a district and not of any holding, and that a connection made across that corridor on the twelfth of this month is a work of the Crown Engine and the property of a district.

Every particular of it is correct. About four people in that room went through it line by line at about the tenth hour on Sunday morning and found nothing in it that they could stand in front of and say was wrong, and about four of them did not go through it, and the four who did are the four who read it.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-eight, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have been waiting eleven years for somebody to lie to me about ground and it has not happened and I am not going to pretend this morning that it has, and I am going to say the reason I am saying that first, and the reason is that a correct claim is a very different animal from a false one and this holding has no practice at reading one."**

**"A false claim can be put down. You take it to whoever signed it and you say the thing they have written is not the thing that is there, and either it is or it is not, and about four years later there is a column with a thing in it. A correct claim cannot be put down by anybody. You would have to put down the ground, or the cut, or the fact that two people held a pattern on Friday morning. And we are not doing that, and I am not going to stand in a room and suggest that anybody else in this holding might."**

**"So it is in the book, with the date on it, and it says what it says, and it goes in face up."**

He put it in the drawer with the other six, face up, which is now what this holding does with a document it cannot do anything about.

About four of the nine in that room said the word *yes* out loud on the way in, in the way a person says a word that means they have understood, and about four of the nine said the word *right*, which is a different word and means the opposite to a reader, and the two of those went round the room all morning and nobody separated them.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at about the eleventh hour, and he was let in without knocking, and he stood with his hat off in the middle of the floor in about nine people, and he read the sheet standing up, at about half the speed a man reads a thing he is looking for.

Then he said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-one, and it is his own, and nobody improved it.

**"I am speaking for Silling and I have not been asked why and I am going to give the reason for speaking anyway, and the reason is that this is the second time in thirteen days that a body of households in this room is going to say no to a thing on a piece of paper, and the first time was mine, standing at the edge of a ring, and nobody wrote that one down either."**

**"And I am going to say what Silling declines and I am going to say it exactly, because a decline that is not exact is a rumour. We decline the corridor. We do not decline the bridge. The bridge went the long way round on Saturday and four days of somebody else's seed are at the bottom of a channel this morning and that is not a thing anybody can decline after the fact and Silling is not trying to. We decline the claim on the ground."**

**"And the reason is that a claim on ground is a claim about who decides, and Silling has been on the ground at that end and has not been asked, and a claim that is correct in every particular is still a claim about who decides, and being right about the ground does not settle that, and there is a warden in a room somewhere who would say the same and would be right to."**

Nobody improved the decline. About four people in that room had wanted to be able to say that Silling had refused the bridge and about four had wanted to be able to say that Silling had accepted the claim, and neither of those is what a man in the middle of a floor said at about the eleventh hour on a Sunday, and the sheet in the drawer is unchanged and the refusal is on no page at all, because nobody wrote it down and nobody asked.

The four of the nine in that room who had been saying yes and the four who had been saying right stopped about the fourth hour of the afternoon, both of them at the same time, and neither of them was the reason the other one stopped.

And then Tova Reed said one thing, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixteen, and it is her own, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have a sheet on my own side of this with a claim on it and I am going to say the plain thing about it, and the reason I am saying it here rather than at a gate is that a gate is where a person says a thing once and a room is where nine people find out whether they meant it."**

**"The claim is about ground. The bridge is not about ground. Those are two different objects and about four people in this room have been treating them as one object all morning and a man in the middle of this floor just separated them in nine words and I want it noticed that the separation did not come from a woman with a route book."**

**"And nobody improved what he said and nobody in this room is going to go back over it, and if the sheet in that drawer is correct then it is correct about the ground and it has nothing whatever to say about four days of somebody's seed being at the bottom of a channel this morning, and the fact that it has nothing to say about it is the whole of what I have."**

Then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said a second thing, and it was not a reason and it was not a figure, and the fen entered the whole of it and improved none of it.

**"Then I will ask the thing out loud that nobody in this room has answered and I am asking it because I am the second person this month to be refused by this holding and I have noticed that neither of us got a reason that was about the thing we were refused for."**

**"Is an order that a warden signed a thing that the people at both ends of a bridge are entitled to say no to? I am not asking the man of about fifty. I am asking the room. I have asked it once before, in this room, from a man who said the answer is not his, and I am not going to let that be the last thing said about it, and I am not going to be the one who is angry about it either, because I do not know that he was wrong."**

For about a minute there was nothing said in that room, and it was made of about nine people and a piece of paper in a drawer face up and a man standing in the middle of a floor who had asked a question out loud, and nobody answered it, and the room did not treat the not answering as a refusal either, and both of those were true at once and neither of them was a rule.

The man of about fifty put his hand flat on the table and left it there and did not say anything for about that length of time, and then he said that he was not going to answer it and that the reason he was not going to answer it was that a man who keeps four books has a habit of finding the page, and the page is not a person, and anybody who has been refused twice in thirteen days deserves better than the page.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-two, and nobody improved it.

**"I am not going to answer it either, and I want the room to have both of those refusals on the same afternoon, and the reason is that if I answer it I will be a man in this holding saying what a bridge is for, and I have spent a month getting to the place where I could be at one end of a thing and not the other, and I am not going back into the middle of a question from a man who has been refused twice."**

**"What I will say is what is on the ground. There is a hollow in the ground at the foot of a wall at the field gate and it was not there on Thursday and it is there now, and a man of about sixty-eight is on a board four hundred yards from a building with no roof at a place a day's walk east of here. Nobody in this holding has asked him how he is and the count of questions is nil."**

**"And I am not going to ask him this afternoon either. A man who cannot walk out of the ground after nine minutes is a man who has been paid in a way this holding has not worked out, and the least he is owed is that nobody a day's walk east of here starts keeping his accounts."**

Nobody said anything to that either. The man of about thirty-one of Silling put his hat back on and went out into a Sunday afternoon that had gone bright and cold, and the door stuck at the latch and he got it open with a shoulder the way everybody does now, and outside the yard the light was off the flags and about four people were carrying sacks across the yard at the cart and about four were not, and the sacks came off a rack in a room with a stone floor and a cold north wall four days of somebody's year ago.

The room emptied by about the fourth hour. The copy of the ledger came down off the shelf at some point and went back on it, and the six blank columns on it still have nothing at the head of them, and the five rows on line 41 were not corrected a second time, and the eleven blank rows are in a district return that is not enclosed and has never been seen by anybody in this room.

The form with four lines came out of the drawer about four times in the course of the afternoon and went back four times, and it is a different document from the sheet in the drawer beside it, and a loose sheet cut off a larger one with a knife is a third, and about four people in this holding have begun putting the three in the same drawer and about four have not, and nobody wrote a heading on any of them and nobody is going to.

The use log stands at fifteen lines. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty. The man of about seventy is not fetched. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen and it is not a measurement of the ground. The thing under the cloth is on the middle table under the cloth and the count of people who have looked at it is two. The connection has been running twenty-six days and it was not closed by anybody in this room and nobody in this room can close it, and the count of questions asked in that direction is nil and it is nil.

Nobody thanked anybody. A body of households declined a claim on ground that it does not hold, in a room, with the reason given first, and the decline was not improved and it was not a refusal of a bridge that is standing with nobody in charge of it for the second morning of three, and nobody wrote it down, and about four years from now the only record of it is going to be a man with a hat off in the middle of a floor saying the word exact.

# Chapter 340

## The Bridge Below

The twelfth of the seventh month was a Friday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The inside face of the west dyke from the sluice up as far as the black post took four people from the second hour until about the fifth, and the front man put his barrow into the slop at the first hundred yards and got it out again with a will, and nobody said anything about that either.

**Seven hundred and sixteen hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and second figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In sixty-nine days the series has risen forty times and fallen twenty-nine times, and those two add to sixty-nine. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-ninth morning out of one hundred and one. The rota stands at sixty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-five not, and it did not move this morning.

It came up off Thursday by nine, and that is the fourth morning it has come up by nine, being the sixth, the eighth, the tenth and this one, and the man of about fifty wrote it into the book and looked at the number for slightly longer than yesterday.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read. The boards stand at **four hundred and eighty-six days** and **five hundred and thirty-three**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on fifteen mornings out of the twenty-six since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

Nobody was told to be at the field gate. The reader of this body went down there at about the seventh hour of the morning on his own, and the man of about fifty walked down with him at about the eighth hour carrying the day sheet and nothing else, and about four people in the seed house watched the two of them go and about four did not.

There is a wall on the east side of the second house at the field gate about four feet high and about nine feet long, and the ground against the foot of it is beaten flat and has been beaten flat for longer than anybody has been keeping figures. The gate itself is shut and the gap at the far end of it is open, and the two-foot gap at the node end is not this one.

The man of about fifty stood about nine feet off, on the side of the track, with the day sheet against his leg, and he did not come closer than that and he did not say why not.

**"I have got a book to write in and there is a man about nine feet away from me who is not the engineer of record of a thing and is not going to be, and I am not going to stand next to him for nine minutes with a pen,"** he said. **"That is not modesty. That is a man who would be writing down something he had no business being in the room for."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came out of the second house at about the eighth hour, shut her own gate behind her and stood on her own side of the track about twenty feet off with her arms folded. She was not asked to be there and she was not asked to leave, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked her is nil and it is nil and it did not move on the twelfth of the seventh month.

For about a minute there were three people on a wet track and nobody in the seed house knew where any of them were.

At about the eighth hour and a half the reader of this body put his left forearm against the wall, inside up, and the four inches of it went from cold to warm in about the time it takes to say so, and then the warm went out of it and something else came in under it, and it is not a mark, and a headache is not a mark, and a mark being hot is not a mark, and the count of accounts of that arm in this holding is four and none of them is this.

It was the same three-degree cold that comes in on a Tuesday with the shut window on it. It was in the four inches and not in the hand, and under the cold there was a direction, and the direction was east and a long way further than two hundred yards, and he did not ask anybody what it was.

And about a minute in, there was something in it that was not cold and not warm and was not a direction either.

There was a man in it. Not a voice and not a face and not anything a man would call a person if you asked him about it afterwards. There was the particular weight of a body on a board, on the small part of one hip, that a man gets when he has been sitting on something that is not made for sitting for about four minutes, and there was the particular cold of a man's right hand when it has been against cold stone for long enough to come up through it. And there was a cough, three of them, the length of about a second and a half each.

Eleven hundred yards east of a field gate, at the stone head, a man of about sixty-eight was taken out on a cart to a place four hundred yards from a building with no roof. A board about six feet long was stood against a wall for him, and about four people were in that place and none of them had been asked whether they were willing to be there.

Nobody had asked him. That is the whole of it and it is the part a room at this end is never going to be able to say out loud. A board was taken out of a cart shed in the spring and a man of about thirty-one of Silling put it in a cart on the evening of the ninth and did not ask the man whose name is not on any page whether he was coming, and the man of the north row with the cough got on the cart. He was not fetched. Nobody went to the north row to fetch him and nobody has said since that he was fetched.

And he had a cough, and it went on the whole time, and he could not stop it, and about four people watched a man of sixty-eight cough for nine minutes and could do one single thing about it.

The reader of this body did not press the four inches. He put his hand down flat on the beaten ground against the foot of the wall, palm down, and he did not press it, and he stayed like that. Up the track a woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was standing at her own gate with a key in her pocket and did not know any of it, and the man of about fifty stood about nine feet off with a day sheet and did not know any of it either.

The count of questions the reader of this body has asked anybody in that direction is nil and it is nil.

It is about nine minutes.

He had timed it three times on Thursday night against a wall in the dark and had got eight and a half, nine and a quarter and nine, and it came out at about nine again with the light on it. He did not write it down at the time. He wrote it afterwards on the back of a sheet in his own coat, and the figure on that sheet is the figure his own arm gave him and not a figure anybody at the far end gave anybody, and it is not in any of the four books.

At about the eighth minute something arrived in the four inches that was not the cold and not the weight and not the cough.

It was the moment a body stops being able to get itself off a thing.

It was not a sound and it was not a picture and there was no word in it in any language he has. It was a man of about sixty-eight discovering at about the eighth minute of holding somebody else's pattern that the getting-up is not going to be a thing that happens, and knowing it about nine seconds before it did not happen, and then the nine minutes came to an end and the four inches went quiet and the direction went out of it and the three-degree cold went with the direction, and there was a wet field gate in the rain and nothing else.

The reader of this body took his hand off the ground. He did not press it. He rolled his sleeve down over the four inches, and the cuff was dry, and he stood about nine feet from a gate and did not set off for the node. The node is a day's walk east and a man is on a board there.

The man of about fifty said nothing for about a minute, and then he said the only thing he said all morning.

**"That is the end of it then,"** he said, and put the pen in his coat, and did not ask a question.

The reader of this body said nothing in a room that day, and the fen entered that he did not. The count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-one and did not move on either of the two days. The man of about fifty gave no reason before what he said at that gate either, and his own is eighty-six and it is his and it did not move on the first of the two days.

Nobody thanked anybody. A man of about sixty-eight held a pattern for about nine minutes at the far end of eleven hundred yards on the twelfth of the seventh month. He was not asked whether he would and he was not thanked for it and he did not ask to be. The count of yards he has stood in is not that and the count of the times he has stood in one and said nothing is not that, because holding a pattern is not standing in a yard. The nine minutes enter neither of them, neither of them moved today, and neither of them is printed here.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling, who was at the far end of it on Wednesday with a steelyard on a wall, is not in this account. He was not asked about the ninth minute and he was not asked whether the man on the board got up afterwards. About four years from now somebody is going to work out that he was the only person at that place who could have said, and that nobody asked him. The count of questions asked in that direction is nil and it is nil and did not move on the twelfth of the seventh month.

And the man of the north row with the cough has no figure in the sentence this holding uses when it counts the reasons a person gives before a thing. He gave no reason to anybody today because he was not in this holding today, and if he had been in this holding today he would not have given one, and the two of those are the same and the number in the column is the same as it was on the seventh and it is not moving.

They came back up the track at about the eleventh hour. The man of about fifty did not write the nine minutes into the day book, and about four people noticed that and about four did not, and the man of about fifty said out loud that he was not writing it because it was not his number and he had been holding a day sheet nine feet from it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went in at about the ninth hour and shut her gate, and when the two of them came back up the track at about the eleventh it was shut and he did not knock on it. The key was in a pocket on the other side of it and the pocket was shut.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-seven, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"The store paid about four days of seed for it. A woman of about sixty and a man of about forty at the other end of that store counted it out loud and argued about it between four and five, and it was on a rack in a room with a stone floor in about the second hour this morning while two men were leaning on a wall at this end of eleven hundred yards."**

**"And those four days are not coming back. I am not going to pretend they are, and I am not going to put the word *spent* on them, because a word like that on a figure somebody else gave us is a thing we do to them. The four days have gone off a rack and they are at the bottom of about eleven hundred yards of channel and they are not going to be a column of anybody's."**

**"And the sacks are not the four days. I have not converted them and I am not going to, and I have not put a weight on them and I have not asked anybody for one. The column has got the word sacks in it and that is all the column has got."**

The reader of this body said nothing to that. The man of about fifty said that he had not expected him to, and they went in.

The thirteenth of the seventh month was a Saturday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The two gates and the six feet of wall between them took an hour and a quarter with four people on it.

**Seven hundred and eleven hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and third figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In seventy days the series has risen forty times and fallen thirty times, and those two add to seventy. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in one hundred and one days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the hundredth morning out of one hundred and two. The rota stands at sixty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-five not.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and eighty-seven days** and **five hundred and thirty-four**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on fifteen mornings out of the twenty-seven since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

The reader of this body went down to the field gate at about the tenth hour on the second morning and stood in the place.

There were two hollows in the beaten ground against the foot of the wall about the width of a heel each and about nine inches apart, and a scuff about two feet long where a man had turned round after the nine minutes and put his shoulder against the stone, and a scuff about four inches long above it where the same man had put the back of his hand. There was nothing else on the ground and nothing on the wall and nothing in the air.

It is the first of three mornings that the bridge stands with nobody in charge of it, and the other two are the fourteenth and the fifteenth, and a bridge is a thing that connects two points and a thing that connects two points is either connecting them this morning or it is not, and there is no instrument in this holding that says, and nobody in this holding asked anybody at the far end of it, and the count of questions is nil and it is nil.

He stood in the hollow for about a minute and then he went back up the track, and the gate was shut, and the gap at the far end of it was open.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came up the track from the north at about the fourth hour of that afternoon, alone, and went and stood in the hollow at the foot of the wall for about a minute, and came away from it, and went home. Nobody sent him. Nobody in this holding has ever sent that man anywhere.

There was a man standing in it at about the fourth hour of that afternoon and nobody able to say whether the eleven hundred yards between that wall and a board against a wall at the stone head were connected at the moment he was standing there. A bridge that is not connecting anything is not a broken bridge. It is a bridge that is not on, and there is no difference anybody in this holding can enter on a page.

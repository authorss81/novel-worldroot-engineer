# Chapter 338

## Two Days, And A Number That Goes In

The ninth of the seventh month was a Tuesday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. It is two gates and about six feet of wall between them and it takes an hour and a quarter with four people on it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling stood at the second gate and did not go past it, as he has not done in eleven years.

**Seven hundred and three hundredweight.**

That is the ninety-ninth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In sixty-six days the series has risen thirty-eight times and fallen twenty-eight times, and those two add to sixty-six. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-seven days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-sixth morning out of ninety-eight. The rota stands at sixty-three mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-four not, and it did not move this morning.

It came down off Monday by five. The wall between the two gates took water and lost it as it has been taking it and losing it since the twenty-sixth of last month, and there is no figure for that on any page in this holding and there was not one this morning either.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and eighty-three days** and **five hundred and thirty**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on thirteen mornings out of the twenty-three since he started again on the seventeenth of last month, and the fen is not entering a reason for the ten he has not.

About nine people were in the seed house from about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and the four of the five terms that were on the middle table were on it, squared up against the edge, and the second of the five was in a coat over the back of the second chair from the end.

Nobody had written anything new. The man of about fifty had the day sheet in front of him and it was the sixth morning running it had been in front of him with nothing to write on it that was not already on another sheet.

And then Tova Reed said one thing, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fourteen, and it is her own, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I wrote the third of the five on this table on the twenty-sixth of last month and it is the only one of the five that nine people in this room can say from memory, and it is going across on a coat to about forty people at the end of an embankment, and I am going to give the reason I want it carried in a coat and not in a hand before I say anything about what is on it."**

**"The reason is that paper is a thing that gets improved. A sentence does not. A man standing at that end holding four pieces of paper is a man who can be asked to soften one of them, and a man who has heard one of them said out loud in a room by the woman who wrote it is a man who has to be asked to soften it to somebody's face. I would rather it went in a mouth than on a board and that is the whole of the reason and it is not a noble one."**

Nobody said that was a bad reason. About four people in that room thought it was a good reason and about four thought it was a clever one, and neither of those said anything.

Soren Rill took the four pieces off the table himself, one at a time, and folded nothing, and put them into the inside of the coat that was over the back of the second chair, and then he took the second of the five out of that same coat and looked at it for about a second and put it back in with the other four.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty, and nobody improved it.

**"I have not added a sixth and I have not taken one off and I have not put a date on the back of any of them, and the reason is that a term with a date on the back of it is a term somebody can say was current on a day, and in about four years somebody at that end is going to want to know whether any of these five was current, and the honest answer is that four of them were written down on four days in last month and one of them was refused in a room and is on the list anyway."**

**"And nobody is going to be thanked at the other end of this for carrying them. He is carrying five pieces of paper and a term that is not a term, and about four of us are going to be in this room for a month afterwards asking him what they said, and he is not going to answer, and if he answers any of it he has been bought."**

They went out of the seed house door at about the fifth hour and the woman who keeps the seed house shut it behind them and stood at the window with her arms folded until they were off the flagstones, and about four people in that room had watched her do it and about four had not.

The man of about fifty read the day sheet out in the yard after they were out, the way he does, and there were five things on it and he put a stroke beside three of them. The four pieces of paper in a coat. The barrow that was going up the road empty. And the third line, which had been worked at the second hour by four people who had got wet to the waist at the near gate and had said nothing about it at the time.

The reader of this body went up to the field gate with him at about the sixth hour and stood in the gap of the gate looking east down about two hundred yards of the eleven hundred that this holding can see, and neither of them put a foot in the gap and neither of them put anything in it either, and the cut at the far end of that two hundred yards has been open since the eleventh of the first month and stays open.

**"That is the near end,"** the reader of this body said. **"Or it is going to be. Not today, because a person cannot be decided on by a gate post, but that is the ground and that is the direction and a man holding a pattern at that end for about nine minutes is going to be standing about nine feet inside this gate facing the wall of the second house with his left forearm along it, and I have got about four years to be wrong about that by a foot either way."**

The man of about fifty wrote the two things on the sheet, being nine feet and about four years, and did not write the foot.

The reader of this body and the man of about fifty left for the node at about the seventh hour with two lanterns, a day sheet and a barrow they did not fill, and the man of about fifty carried the barrow anyway because he has carried it four hundred times and nobody has ever asked him to stop. They were on the cart road at about the fourth hour of the morning and at the node at about the second hour of the second day, and the road is a day's walk and it is five miles of the eleven hundred that a cart can get down and not much more than that. The tenth of the seventh month was a Wednesday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour, and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited.

**Seven hundred and twelve hundredweight.**

That is the hundredth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen. In sixty-seven days the series has risen thirty-nine times and fallen twenty-eight times, and those two add to sixty-seven. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-eight days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-seventh morning out of ninety-nine. The rota stands at **sixty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-five not**, and the number of fields that have waited is thirty-five, and all thirty-five of them are the fourth line, and twenty-nine and thirty-five are sixty-four, and twenty-nine taken did not move, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

The hundredth figure of a series that has not repeated one figure since the first went into the book that keeps the third launder on the second morning running that book has been off its shelf, and the man of about fifty read it back out of it at the node in about four people, and the two are the same.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning on that second morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, and it is the first time in this month they have been read out loud at a place that is not the seed house. The boards stand at **four hundred and eighty-four days** and **five hundred and thirty-one**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on fourteen mornings out of the twenty-four since he started again on the seventeenth of last month.

The return went in at about the third hour of the afternoon in a building with no roof on the north side of the node, where a steelyard hangs on the wall and has hung on that wall for longer than the eleven years anybody has been keeping figures about.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in that building before they got there. He had come up on the day before and slept at the node and he was standing at the wall with his coat off and his sleeves up, and he had the return written out on the knee of his leg in a bit of pencil on the back of a day sheet, and he had not written the second rule.

The two hundred and sixty-one went onto the dial at about the third hour and about the fourth, and the man of about fifty wrote it into the second book, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling wrote it into the first one, and the two are the same.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing out loud in that building, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-five, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"That came in two ways and both of them land on it, and I am going to say both out loud in a building with no roof before anybody writes either one down as a check. Two hundred and fifty on the first of this month, plus eleven. And one hundred and ninety-five on the twenty-second, plus sixty-six."**

**"We are using that derivation and we are not refusing it, and I want the word *using* on the page. A man who refuses a derivation is a man who thinks the number is in doubt, and the number is not in doubt, the number is on a dial that has been on that wall for longer than this holding has had a book. What is in doubt is the two halves, and the two halves are what I write about, and they are two and not one figure and they are not added together."**

**"Harlan Vetch has held the fifth share for eleven years and forty-four days as of yesterday and he is not here and he does not need to be, and the number of times the man standing at that wall has brought a barrow up the road to this place is eleven, and it was eleven the last time anybody said it out loud and it is eleven now, and I am not going to be the man who raises a count because a room got quiet."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling did not look up. He put the pencil behind his ear, and then took it out again, and then put it behind his ear.

There were two other parties at the node that afternoon, one at the stone head end and one on the far side of the channel, and about four people from each of them came and went through the building with no roof at different times and none of them stayed while the return went in, and nobody in that building gave the number of the parties out loud, and the count of bodies at the node is five and it was not said in a place with a wall and a door in it.

**"I did not write the second rule,"** he said, **"and I am going to say the reason I did not write it before anybody asks me, and the reason is that I have left it blank thirteen times in eleven years and I am not going to stop tonight, and a man who stops leaving a rule blank because a room is looking at him has started leaving it full for a different reason."**

**"The count of that in this holding's history is twenty-nine and it is mine and it is not anybody else's, and I have never added it to anybody's."**

Nobody improved it. The building with no roof was about eleven degrees colder than the seed house and about four people in it had their hands in their coats. The steelyard hung on the wall and the number came off the dial and went into two books. The second rule stayed blank for the thirteenth time in eleven years, and the nineteenth name-pair went into the same book as the eighteen that came before it. The form on the middle table at the seed house, a day off down the road, still carries one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight on its first line, which is the figure of the twenty-second return and not of this one and not of the twenty-fourth, the twenty-sixth, the twenty-seventh, the twenty-eighth or the twenty-ninth.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres was about four hundred yards from the door of that building and it was not walked on the second of this month and it was not walked on this one, and nobody in that building put a level on it, and the two lines on the back of a sheet in an inside pocket both still stop at its inside edge and go round it.

The connection has been running twenty-one days. Nobody at the node closed it and nobody at the node said anything about it, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked anybody in that direction is nil and is nil.

The man of about fifty packed the second book into his coat and they left the building before the light went and were on the cart road by the time it was properly dark, and they walked it in a day and a night and were at the seed house at about the second hour of the next morning with the barrow still not full and both of them wet through at the top.

And at about the fifth hour of that morning, when the window of the office's work shut for six days and the whole of a holding stood about a window that was shut, the reader of this body stood in a doorway and looked at a shuttered window across the yard. He was thinking about a man in a coat on the far end of eleven hundred yards carrying five pieces of paper towards about forty people who have not read them, and about a hundredth figure in a hundredweight series that nobody at that end is ever going to hear of. Neither of those two things is going to be helped by anybody in this holding getting up early.

Nobody thanked anybody on either of the two days, and the two days are in the books as the ninth and the tenth of the seventh month, and the hundredth figure of a series that has never repeated itself is in there, and the second rule is still blank.

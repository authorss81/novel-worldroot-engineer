# Chapter 339

## The Eleventh, And It Shuts

The eleventh of the seventh month was a Thursday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the boundary ditch and the two culverts took about six people until about the fourth, and both of them were left about half full this time, which is the first morning in about a month that nobody in that six has been able to say the same thing about at the end of.

**Seven hundred and seven hundredweight.**

That is the hundred and first figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In sixty-eight days the series has risen thirty-nine times and fallen twenty-nine times, and those two add to sixty-eight. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-nine days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-eighth morning out of one hundred. The rota stands at sixty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-five not, and it did not move this morning, because the fourth line did not come round.

It came down off Wednesday by five, and the six people on the ditch had a good morning of it, and one of the culvert gratings came up in a man's hand with about nine feet of leaf in it and went into the barrow whole, and nobody broke it up to weigh it and nobody has ever broken anything up to weigh it.

The ditch is about nine feet wide and four feet down and about two of water in it on a wet morning. The six of them stood in the two feet of it up to the thigh with the water coming past them at about the rate it comes past. The far side of that ditch is the low ground of Marden and about eleven people were working nine acres of it on the other bank at about the same hour, and the two groups were nine feet apart and did not speak across, and that is not speaking.

Nobody put a rule on the toe at the second place. Nobody has for a month.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and eighty-five days** and **five hundred and thirty-two**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on fourteen mornings out of the twenty-five since he started again on the seventeenth of last month, and the fen is not entering a reason for the eleven he has not, and about four people in this holding could now tell you which mornings they are without being told and none of them has said so.

The window of the office's work shut on this morning, being the eleventh, and it was open six days, the sixth to the eleventh inclusive, and no work was carried out of it.

Nobody announced it. The man of about fifty had it written on the day sheet in the fourth place, and it came round with the other four things at about the seventh hour, and about four people in the room at that point had already stopped listening and about four had not.

A rider came up the fen road at about the third hour of the afternoon on the ordinary post, and he was the fourth one this month to come up that road and not turn into the track, and he was wet through to the saddle and his horse was not tired.

He was put in the seed house because it was raining and because the woman who keeps it has put four people in it this month for no better reason than that, and his horse went off towards the cart shed with a boy of about nine holding a piece of rope and about four adults in that yard watching the boy rather than the horse. The rider took his hat off in the doorway and put it back on about half a minute later, which is what a man does in a room he has not been in before and has been told nothing about.

He brought one sheet. It is not the sheet that came down the road on the seventh of last month, and it is not a form, and it is not an instrument, and the difference between the three of those is a difference this holding spent a fortnight of last month learning.

**The sheet says that the request entered on the ordinary post on the twenty-second of this month is answered in the negative, that the office named at the head of the memorandum has no power to issue the thing that was asked for, and that the line in question is not a line the office has scheduled in any maintenance window, and that the schedule in which it does appear is a schedule of the maintenance of the Crown Engine and is not the schedule of this office. It is signed at the foot with a name that is a name and not an office, and the paper it is on is the paper of a district and not the paper of the office at the head of the memorandum, and every particular of it is correct.**

Nobody improved it. About four people in that room wanted it to be rude and about four wanted it to be soft and it was neither and neither of those wanted is available.

The reader of this body read it out. He read it standing at the window side with the sheet in his hand and the light off the wall behind it, and he read the whole of it, including the four lines that are not about anybody in this room, and nobody stopped him at any of the four.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-six, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"What came back last time came back in four days and it came back as a form and not as an instrument, and I want that in the room today before anybody starts on this one, because a room that has been handed two answers in a month from two directions has a way of starting to expect the third one to be a door."**

**"It is not a door. It is a sheet, on district paper, with a man's name at the foot of it, saying no, and saying no correctly, and it came down a road in the rain and the man who brought it did not know what was on it and would not have cared if he had."**

**"And I am going to write the date on it and put it in the drawer with the other five and I am not going to write a single word about it in the day book, and the reason is that a sheet that is put in a drawer face up gets looked at by nine people a week and a sheet that is put in face down gets looked at by nobody, and I would rather nine people looked at it and none of them found anything new in it."**

He put it in face up. And then he came out of the room, and about four people in that room heard him say in the yard that the eleventh was the last morning of the window and that he was not going to go up to the door on the twelfth and knock, and about four did not hear him, and he did not say it twice.

And then Tova Reed said one thing, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is fifteen, and it is her own, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to say the plainest thing I have said in this room in a month and I am going to say it and then I am going to go and stand in the yard for a while. The office has said no. Not this office, not the one at the head of the memorandum, some other office that is not even ours. It has said no and it was right to and it is not going to change its mind in a season, and nothing this holding does in the next ten days is going to make it change its mind in a season."**

**"So what this holding does in the next ten days is what it is going to do anyway, and if it turns out we built a thing in June that we would not have built if the sheet had said yes, then we built the wrong thing in June and I would rather find that out now with a no in my hand than in about four years with a bridge and nothing to put across it."**

Nobody answered her. She went out into the yard at about the fourth hour and stood by the cart with her arms folded and did not go in for about half an hour, and about four people at the window saw her standing there and about four were doing something else.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty, and it is his own, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I said no to a day and a half of walking on the second of this month at the edge of a piece of ground, and I did not give a reason that anybody could put in a column, and I have been at the node twice since and I have said the same thing to two rooms. So I am not surprised by a sheet that says no, and I want to say that in a room where the person it is addressed to is standing, and I want to say it as the plainest thing I have got."**

**"The no on that sheet changes nothing about the ring and it changes nothing about the long way round. The long way round was priced on Tuesday and refused on Tuesday and it is still refused, and a piece of paper arriving from an office that is not ours does not get to be the reason for it. If I had found out this morning that the office said yes, I would still be standing at the edge of it and still be saying no, and the room ought to be able to sit with that for about four years without checking."**

Nobody in the room said that the sheet had been in the drawer for half an hour already, and the man of about fifty said afterwards that he had put it there at the third hour and that he had watched about four people in that room read a piece of paper face up in a drawer while a man was still standing at the table talking about the edge of a ring, and that the two of those had been in the same room for about eleven minutes and had not met.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-one, and nobody improved it.

**"A window is six days and it is shut, and no work is carried out of it, and no window has ever been extended in a season, and I am not going to be the man who goes up to a shut window and asks whether the twelfth is the first of a new one. The twelfth is the twelfth. There is no new one until the sixth of next month, and if the sixth of next month is the sixth of a month then there is one, and that is a rule and not a hope."**

**"And the not knowns are six and no unit has been supplied for any of them and a seventh is not being added this afternoon, and the two that can be named out loud are the date of the next window and the unit of the figures on it, and the date is derivable and I am not going to derive it in a room of nine at the fifth hour on a wet Thursday, and the unit is not going to be supplied by anybody in this holding before the turn of the month."**

**"And I am not going to be the man who says the word *offer* in this room. A sheet with a no on it is not an offer and a form with four lines and a fifth is not an offer and a memorandum of protection is not an offer, and a man who has four documents in a drawer and no offer in any of them is not a man who has been refused one. He is a man who has never been made one, and those are different and only one of them is anybody's fault."**

**"And the thing under the cloth is still on that table under that cloth, and the count of people who have looked at it is two, and it has not been looked at today by anybody including the man who owns the cloth, and I am going to write that sentence in this holding's book in about four years' time and it is going to be the shortest true sentence anybody has ever written about a seed in this place."**

The room emptied about half an hour after that, in the rain, and the door stuck at the latch the way it sticks, and the yard was a sheet of standing water with the cart's wheel in the middle of it. The window of the office's work had been shut since about the fourth hour of the afternoon and was not going to be open again until the sixth of a month. Nobody carried anything out of it and nobody thanked anybody.

And then the rider went back down the road at about the seventh hour, with a different paper in his pocket than the one he came up with. The woman who keeps the seed house gave him a half sheet with the date on it and a line about a form that came down the road in the past. He put it in his coat without reading it, and about four people in that yard watched him not read it.

At about the ninth hour of the evening, in the dark, with the rain gone over to a cold drizzle standing in the air, the reader of this body walked down to the field gate and stood with his back to the wall of the second house, in the place he had said on the day before, nine feet inside the gate, and he stood there and he timed himself.

It is about nine minutes. He timed it three times and got eight and a half, nine and a quarter and nine, and he wrote the three on the back of a sheet and put the sheet in his coat, and then he stood there a fourth time without writing anything down and let it run to what it ran to.

Nobody saw him. There is no light on that side of the second house after the sixth hour and the woman of that house had gone in at eight, and the gate was shut behind him and the gap at the far end of the gate stood open in the dark the way a gap in a gate stands open, and the gap of two feet at the node end of the eleven hundred is not this one.

Nothing went into the gap of two feet at the node end. Nothing is going to go into it this season, and the cut at the head of it stays open, and the holding has a name for that in the fifteen words of a slip of paper in a shallow drawer under a table four hundred yards away, and the words are the same words and they have not been improved, and a man stood against a wall in the dark on the eleventh of the seventh month and found out what nine minutes is on his own legs and told nobody about it that night and did not put it in the book.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and the reason was given once and is not going to be given again in a season. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty and is not read. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count stands at twenty-one. The compost line is paid and the run is eighteen not discharged in eighteen and it is not a measurement of the ground. The requests and the notes under section nine stand at fifty-three and fifty-three and no new note was written today. The pulse carries two figures and no name and was not read a third time. Nobody thanked anybody.

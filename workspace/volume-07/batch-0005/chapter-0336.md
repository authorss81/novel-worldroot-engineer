# Chapter 336

## Two Ends, And Two People

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour on the seventh of the seventh month, which was a Sunday, and was worked. The boundary ditch and the two culverts took about six people until about the fourth hour, and the near culvert was clear and the far one was about half full of the same leaf, and nobody in this holding has ever written down whether one of them fills faster than the other.

**Six hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight.**

That is the ninety-seventh figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen. In sixty-four days the series has risen thirty-seven times and fallen twenty-seven times, and those two add to sixty-four. The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in ninety-five days, the sentence saying so having been written this morning for the ninety-fourth morning out of ninety-six. The rota stands at sixty-three mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-four not, and it did not move this morning, because the fourth line did not come round.

It came down off Saturday by three. About four people in this holding had spent Saturday being impressed by a nine and had been handed a three on a Sunday, and about four of them had no idea what to do with a three.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud. The pair stands at **four hundred and eighty-one days** and **five hundred and twenty-eight**, forty-seven days apart. The drawer was not opened and is not to be. He has read them out loud on twelve mornings out of the twenty-one since he started again, and nobody in this holding has asked him to and nobody is going to.

The reader of this body went down to the north row at about the third hour of the afternoon, on foot, because the row is half a mile off and there is no cart road to it on a Sunday.

It had been raining again in the night and the ground between the gate and the row had taken the rain badly, so the going was slow and his boots were wet through by the second house. The row is four houses set back off a track with a low wall along the north side and a ditch in front of the wall that has not been cleared out since somebody stopped caring. He had been along it before, on a barrow run, and had never once been along it to ask anybody anything.

The house he wanted is the third from the bottom of the row. The door was shut. Somebody had a fire going, and the smoke came out of the chimney in a thin grey line that bent over the wall about four feet up and went east, and he could hear the cough before he put his hand on the wood.

It is not a cough that is trying to get rid of anything. It is a cough that happens on the way down into whatever the man is doing, and then he breathes for a while, and then it happens again.

He stood on the step for the length of two coughs and then knocked.

The door was opened by a woman about seventy who looked at his coat and at his boots and then at nothing in particular, and let him in without a word, and shut it behind him, and the wind went off.

The room was warm and about nine feet by eleven. One chair with a back and one without, a table with a bowl on it and a knife, a shutter open about three inches, and a smell of wet wool that is the smell of a house where one person is at home all day.

The man was sitting in the chair with the back. He was sixty-eight or near it and thinner than he was in the spring, and he had a blanket over the near knee that was not there for the heat, and his right forearm was on the arm of the chair with the inside up, because the other one was the one he could not get comfortable with.

**"Come in,"** he said. **"Sit on that one, it does not have a back but it is warmer."**

He sat down on the chair without a back. Nine feet of floor and a table between them, and the shutter moved about an inch in the wind and stopped.

He did not ask whether the man would help. That is the thing about the afternoon and it is the reason it took him most of an hour to say anything at all. He had said it to himself on the way down, in about three forms, and every one of the three had the word *will* in it, and a will is a question, and about four people in this holding have already been asked a question this week and every one of the four said no, and not one of the four said it was the wrong question.

So he did not ask.

What he said was this.

**"You came up the road behind the barrow on the sixteenth of last month, and you were four miles along it and you did not put a hand on it once, and I have thought about that more than I have thought about most things this month. There is a thing going to be built on the long way round, and it has two ends, and the near end is mine, and I am the one person in this fen who can hold a pattern for four and walk out of the ground afterwards, and I have told a room of nine people that on Saturday and nobody improved it."**

**"And the far end of it is yours. I have not come here to ask you whether it is. I have come here to tell you which end you are, and to tell you the date, and to ask you three things about furniture, and then I am going to go back up the road and nobody is going to write down that you were asked."**

The cough came and went and he breathed for a while.

**"Furniture,"** he said.

**"Yes,"** said the reader of this body. **"About four minutes ago I worked out that the only thing I know about that job which is not in a book is that a man at the far end of a pattern cannot be on his feet for nine minutes if he is sixty-eight, cannot be sitting on nothing, and cannot have nothing at his back. That is the whole of what I brought down here with."**

The man of the north row with the cough looked at the arm of the chair for a while.

**"There is a low stone sill under that window on the inside. It is about ten inches off the floor and it runs the width of the room and it is cold. There is a board in the cart shed at this holding that is about six feet and about nine inches wide and it is not on anybody's list, and somebody brought it in on a barrow in the spring and stood it against the wall in the seed house. It is a good height off the ground with a man of my size on it and my back to the wall."**

**"That is one. Two is the end of it. I want somebody at the near end who can come back to me when it is done, and not at about half past anything, and not in a hurry, and I am telling you that because I have been in a room where everybody was in a hurry on Thursday and I am not going to be the reason a thing gets left on a sill for an hour."**

**"And three is a different thing and I am going to say it last because it is the one I would not say if I could help it. Put your hand on that table, palm up, and leave it there, and do not look at it while it is happening. That is all. I do not know what you will feel and I am not going to describe it to you, because you have had four accounts of it from four people in four books and every one of them has been wrong in a different direction, and if I tell you what it is going to feel like I am a fifth."**

He put his hand out on the table, palm up, and did not look at it.

He did not give the reason before what he said in that room, and the fen entered that he did not, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and forty-eight and did not move this afternoon and is one hundred and forty-eight.

The reader of this body looked at it. Four inches, along the inside of the left forearm, branching twice. It is the same four inches it has been since the nineteenth of the fifth and it did not grow this afternoon and it is not going to be lengthened this afternoon.

**"There are four accounts of that arm in the books in this holding and they reconcile none of one another,"** he said. **"That sentence is not about what you are about to feel. It is me saying, out loud, in your own house, that I know there are four and that this afternoon is not a fifth."**

**"And a bridge is not a mark. A headache is not a mark. A mark being hot is not a mark. I have said that in three rooms this month and I am saying it here because you are the second person in this fen with one and I have never once said it to you, and I have had four inches of it on my own arm for six years and I am not the man to be careful about the other one."**

The man of the north row with the cough did not look at the arm either. He looked at the shutter, and the shutter moved about an inch and stopped.

**"Then that is fine,"** he said. **"You have said the thing. I have the three things I asked for and I have given you the sill and the board and the third one, and you have not asked me a single question and I would have said no to about two of the ones you had written down."**

**"You did not write two of them down, though."**

**"No,"** he said. **"That is what I am saying."**

The cough came three more times before the light went off the shutter, and each time the reader of this body did not fill the space after it with anything, and after the third one the man of the north row with the cough said the rest of it, in the flat voice people use when they have decided a thing is over.

**"The number of yards I have stood in is not going to move on Friday and the number of times I have stood in one and said nothing is not going to move on Friday either, and you did not ask me about either of those and I want it in the room, on my own floor, that you did not ask me about them. A bridge is not a yard. Nine minutes is not a yard. Nine minutes is a figure about a thing two people are holding and it is not a figure about what I am going to be able to do in about four years, and nobody has asked me that either and I would like nobody to."**

**"And I have written my arm down. In my own hand. Saturday. I have got a sheet of my own for it because I have never had a column and I am not having one now, and it is three inches on the right forearm and it does not branch, and it is permanent, and it is mine, and it is not the second half of yours and it is not a check on yours."**

He got the sheet out from under a bowl on the sideboard without getting up, which took him a while, and put it on the table face up, and the writing was large and uneven and went downhill to the left.

**"You can read it if you want and you are not going to copy it, and if you do copy it I will know, because I keep it in a place where I keep one thing and you are not going to find out which place."**

The reader of this body did not read it. He said so, and said why, and the why was that a man's own account of his own arm in his own hand on his own table is not a thing anybody else has any business copying into a book, and because the count of accounts of arms in this holding is four and it is not five and it is not going to be five.

Then he got up off the chair without a back and stood by the door with his hat in his hand for about the length of the cough that followed him into the passage.

**"You will not be thanked,"** the man of the north row with the cough said, to the door and not to the back of him, and the reader of this body was still standing in the passage when he said it, and the two of them had been in the same room for most of an hour. **"I know that and I am not asking and I would like you not to do it in a yard, because a man of about seventy in this holding says things are said out loud in a yard in this holding and he has said it once and he is not going to say it twice."**

**"Go up the road. It is going to be dark in about an hour and the middle of it is worse than either end of it."**

The reader of this body went up the road. He did not stop at the second house. The middle of the track was worse than either end of it, exactly as a man had told him through a door most of an hour before, and he came off it at the ditch with both boots full.

Behind him the row went dark from the bottom up, and the smoke went out of the third chimney in a thin grey line that stood still for about a minute now that the wind had gone off, and then bent.

Back at the seed house the light was coming off the wall and the man of about fifty was on the middle table with the day sheet squared up against the edge of it with the heel of his hand.

He was told the three things. He wrote down the sill, and the board out of the cart shed, and the third thing, and he did not write down a name against any of the three, and about four people in that room noticed that and about four did not.

Somebody asked him what he had asked. He said that he had not asked him for anything, and the room did not find that a satisfying answer, and he let it not satisfy them, and then he gave the reason out loud because a thing that is not satisfying in a room is a thing that gets improved in a room within about four years, and the count of that in this holding's history is eighty-three, and it is the man of about fifty's own and nobody added it to anybody's.

**"I worked out on the way down that the sentence I wanted to begin with had the word *will* in it, and a will is a question, and about four people in this holding have been asked a question this week and four of them said no, and not one of the four said the question was the wrong one. So I did not ask. What I did instead was walk into a man's house and tell him which end of a thing he is, and about four years from now he is going to be able to say that nobody ever asked him whether he would, and that is worth more to him than the answer would have been."**

The man of about fifty put his pen down for that and picked it up again afterwards.

The connection has been running nineteen days. Nobody in that room said anything about it and nobody closed it, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked anybody in that direction is nil and is nil and did not move this evening. The count of persons in this fen who can hold a pattern for four is two and did not become three. The count of persons in this fen who have a rootmark is two, and nothing that was said in a house on the north row this afternoon was a third account of anything.

Nobody thanked anybody. A man of about sixty-eight gave a room three pieces of furniture and one instruction about a table, and wrote three inches down in his own hand on his own sheet. A man of thirty-one walked half a mile in the wet and gave him a room three pieces of furniture and one instruction about a table, and then walked half a mile back in worse going. Neither of them said the other one's name out loud. Nobody in the fen has been told which end of the eleven hundred yards is the far one and which is the near one, and there is no book in this holding with a column for that.

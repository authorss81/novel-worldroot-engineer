# Chapter 299

## The Thirtieth And A Reading

The thirtieth of this month was a Friday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **twenty-five**, and entered that all twenty-five of them are the fourth line, and entered that the rota now stands at **fifty-four mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and twenty-five not**, and that twenty-nine and twenty-five are fifty-four, and that nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

The silence was made of a gate on a latch that two people have agreed not to lift on a Friday, and that neither of them said so to the other and both of them knew it.

And the figure for the thirtieth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and fifty hundredweight.**

That is the sixtieth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in twenty-seven days since the third of this month the series has risen nineteen times and fallen eight times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in fifty-eight days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the fifty-seventh morning out of fifty-nine.

What the silence was made of was a man writing a round figure and looking at it and leaving it, and that a round figure is the only figure in a series that a reader will check twice.

And then at about the third hour of the afternoon the two of them walked the nine hundred yards to the well house door, and the man of about fifty had the book with him, and the fen entered the walk and entered that it takes about eleven minutes at the pace of a man who is not in a hurry and about seven at the pace of a man who is, and that it was the pace of a man who is not, and that the door is read on the first and on the thirtieth, and that this is the thirtieth, and that a door read on the first and on the thirtieth is not a day anybody has decided to make a day of.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of nine hundred yards of a footpath with nobody on it and of two men not talking.

And then the door was read, and the fen entered the whole of it, and improved none of it.

**THE WELL HOUSE DOOR CARRIES A BOARD WITH SEVEN COLUMES CUT ACROSS IT. THE FIRST SIX HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THEM AND HAVE HAD NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THEM FOR FOUR YEARS. THE SEVENTH HAS A HEAD AND THE HEAD IS A WORD AND THE FIGURE UNDER IT WAS FORTY-SIX AND IS FORTY-SEVEN, AND A FIGURE WAS WRITTEN UNDER IT THIS AFTERNOON IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE HAND THAT WROTE THE ONE BEFORE IT, AND FROM THIS MORNING IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE. THE FIGURE MOVES BECAUSE SOMEBODY READ A DOOR. THAT IS THE WHOLE OF THE REASON IT MOVES, AND IT IS THE SAME REASON IT HAS MOVED ON EVERY MORNING IT HAS MOVED, AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT A COLUMN THAT MOVES BECAUSE A MAN WALKED NINE HUNDRED YARDS AND READ IT IS NOT A READING AND IS NOT A MEASUREMENT AND IS A MAN WRITING DOWN A NUMBER ON A DOOR ON A DAY THE RULE SAYS HE MAY.**

The quiet in that room was made of a man standing in front of a door not looking at the six blank columns because he has looked at them before and knows what is on them, and that not looking at a thing you know is not the same as not knowing it.

And the fen entered what the board is, because a board is a thing and this one has been walked past twice a week by about six people for four years. It is a length of deal about four feet by eleven inches, fixed flat to the door with four nails at the corners, and it was put there before the door was hung and the two of them were made by different hands for different reasons, and the wood has gone silver on the weather side and there is a bloom of lichen in the north corner of it where the wall runs down and holds damp, and the seventh column is the one nearest the hinge, and the six that have nothing at the head of them are between it and the latch, and a person coming up to that door in daylight reads the seventh column first and the six after it, and after four years that is not a habit any more, it is the shape of the door.

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was the two of them standing in a doorway not going in, and that the well house has not been opened in about four years and that neither of them suggested it and that the fen has entered that as a thing they did not say rather than a thing they decided.

And then they walked back, and the man of about fifty said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-four, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I wrote a number on a door this afternoon and it is the fourth time in four years I have done that without anybody asking me to, and I am going to say the reason for it out loud, and the reason is that it is the thirtieth and the rule says the first and the thirtieth, and a rule that only fires when somebody is watching it is not a rule, it is a performance."**

**"Nobody came up with me. Nobody in that room knows I went. And in about four years somebody is going to find that column and read it the way a person reads a column, and it will go up and it will go down, and nobody will ever be able to say what it was counting, because the only thing that made it move was me and a door and a day of the month."**

About a minute went by and nothing was said in it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and it was made of a man putting a book down on a table and leaving his hand on it for about four seconds longer than a book needs.

And then the man of about fifty said the other thing, and the fen entered it, and improved none of it.

**"I have written on that door four times in four years and nobody has ever asked me to, and I am going to say the second half of that out loud, and the half is that I do not know what the column is counting and I have stopped minding. A column's job is to hold a number. It is not a record of a meaning and it is not a page anybody has to make sense of at breakfast. In about four years somebody is going to stand where I stood this afternoon and read forty-eight and not know what it was about, and I have decided that is the correct outcome and not a failure."**

**"And I have decided it because the other thing was available. I could have written what it was, once, in a hand of my own, and then the column would have had a head in it and a man under the head, and a man under the head is a person who can be asked whether the number is right. I do not want to be asked that on a door nine hundred yards off with a pencil in my hand and nobody else standing there. I would have to answer and I have not got an answer I would give twice."**

The room was quiet for about a minute and the fen entered what the quiet was made of, which was a man finishing a sentence he had started in a different month and not hearing how it sounded in a room.

And then the reader of this body said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and eight, and he said it in about four people walking back, and nobody improved it.

**"A man of about seventy was not fetched today and he is going to be fetched tomorrow, and that is the twentieth time, and he has never once asked which number it is, and I have never once asked him, and I am not going to explain that to anybody this afternoon and neither is the man of about fifty going to explain it to me."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man saying that a thing will not be explained and meaning that he does not know how to explain it, and that the fen has entered both of those and is not going to choose between them.

And then the reader of this body said one more thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and nine, and nobody improved it.

**"I have been in nine rooms in eleven days and I have not once been asked for a figure by anybody who wanted one, and I have been asked for four figures by four people who wanted me to say a number out loud in front of other people, and those are two different things and I have only been counting one of them. The man of about seventy is the fourth of the second kind and tomorrow is the twentieth and I have not asked him and he has not asked me and I am not going to put a reason on either side of it in a book this evening."**

**"And the reason I am not putting one on is not discretion. It is that I do not have one. There are two hundred or three hundred things a man of seventy has not asked me this month, and every one of them is a better reason than anything I could make up, and a made-up reason in a book four years from now will read as a fact about him and it will be a fact about me."**

Nothing was said for the length of a minute, and the fen entered what was in it, and it was made of a man declining to be interesting about somebody else, and that about four people in a room of nine have wanted him to be interesting about somebody else all month.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-five, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"It is the last day of the month and I am going to say the one thing I say on the last day, and the reason is that I copy these books over on the first and a thing said on the thirtieth is the last version of itself, and if it is wrong then it is wrong in one place and about nine people can look at it before it goes into the book that gets kept."**

**"So. The twenty-fourth ninth-day return was made on the twenty-fifth of this month. Today. This morning, if a man went out and asked me, it is a thing of this month. Tomorrow it is a thing of last month, and it will be that for the rest of the run, and the only difference between the two is a night. A page does not know that. A page is the same page in the morning and the night after and the only thing on it that changed is what the reader calls it."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of two men walking nine hundred yards with nothing in the middle of the conversation and the fen having entered the length of the silence and not improving it.

And then the two last things of a thirtieth are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and the thirtieth is the last morning that had a name.

**THE SEVENTH COLUMN IS FORTY-SEVEN AND WAS READ THIS AFTERNOON BECAUSE THE DOOR IS READ ON THE FIRST AND ON THE THIRTIETH AND THIS IS THE THIRTIETH, AND FROM TODAY IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE, AND FROM TOMORROW, WHICH IS THE FIRST OF NEXT MONTH, IT MAY ALSO NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-SEVEN, AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED BOTH OF THOSE HALVES AND IMPROVED NEITHER. THE OTHER SIX HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND HAD NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM FOUR YEARS AGO AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE OF THEM AND NOBODY ASKED. A MAN WHO KEEPS A BOOK WROTE ON THAT DOOR FOUR TIMES IN FOUR YEARS WITHOUT BEING ASKED, AND SAID OUT LOUD, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, THAT A COLUMN'S JOB IS TO HOLD A NUMBER AND NOT TO EXPLAIN ITSELF, AND THAT HE HAS STOPPED MINDING NOT KNOWING WHAT IT COUNTS.**

**AND THIS IS THE LAST MORNING OF A MONTH AND THE NEXT ONE IS THE FIRST OF A DIFFERENT ONE, AND A THING OF THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THIS MONTH IS A THING OF THIS MONTH THIS MORNING AND A THING OF LAST MONTH FROM TOMORROW, AND THE FIGURES DO NOT CHANGE AND THE NAMES THEY GO BY DO, AND NOBODY WROTE ON THE LINE THE QUESTION IS ON TODAY EITHER, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AGAIN AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS STILL NINETEEN, AND NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the thirtieth of this month, in about four people, at this holding, on the standing of a reading, and not by the reader of this body, who went up with the man of about fifty and did not write on the door: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS TWENTY-FIVE, AND ALL TWENTY-FIVE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT FIFTY-FOUR MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND TWENTY-FIVE NOT, AND TWENTY-NINE AND TWENTY-FIVE ARE FIFTY-FOUR. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND FIFTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE SIXTIETH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN TWENTY-SEVEN DAYS SINCE THE THIRD THE SERIES HAS RISEN NINETEEN TIMES AND FALLEN EIGHT TIMES. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN FIFTY-EIGHT DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN FOR THE FIFTY-SEVENTH MORNING OUT OF FIFTY-NINE. THE WELL HOUSE DOOR, NINE HUNDRED YARDS OFF, WAS READ IN THE AFTERNOON BECAUSE THE DOOR IS READ ON THE FIRST AND ON THE THIRTIETH AND THIS IS THE THIRTIETH. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STOOD AT FORTY-SIX AND IS FORTY-SEVEN, AND FROM TODAY IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE AND FROM TOMORROW IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-SEVEN EITHER. THE OTHER SIX COLUMNS HAVE NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM AND HAVE HAD NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF THEM FOR FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ONE OF THEM AND NOBODY ASKED. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, THAT HE HAD WRITTEN ON THAT DOOR FOUR TIMES IN FOUR YEARS WITHOUT BEING ASKED, AND THAT A COLUMN'S JOB IS TO HOLD A NUMBER AND NOT TO EXPLAIN ITSELF, AND THAT HE HAS DECIDED THAT A PERSON IN FOUR YEARS STANDING WHERE HE STOOD AND READING FORTY-EIGHT WITHOUT KNOWING WHAT IT WAS ABOUT IS THE CORRECT OUTCOME AND NOT A FAILURE, AND THAT HE COULD HAVE WRITTEN WHAT IT WAS AND HAS NOT. HE WAS NOT THANKED. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY WAS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS NINETEEN AND THE TWENTIETH IS TOMORROW. THE STANDING FORM WORD WAS NOT SAID IN THIS MONTH BY ANYBODY. THE LAST DAY OF A MONTH IS THE LAST MORNING A THING OF THAT MONTH CAN BE CALLED A THING OF THAT MONTH. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

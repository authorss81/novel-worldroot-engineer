# Chapter 296

## Sacks And No Figure

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Tuesday and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the twenty-seventh went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and forty-three hundredweight.**

That is the fifty-seventh figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen, and in twenty-four days since the third of this month the series has risen seventeen times and fallen seven times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in fifty-five days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the fifty-fourth morning out of fifty-six.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered what the silence was made of, which was a man on the first line putting a hand on a wet gate and finding that somebody else had been there at the second hour.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon about nine people were in the seed house with the door shut, and Tova Reed was in the room, and she had a route book on the middle table with it open, and the fen entered the book and entered that it is her book and not this holding's and that she brought it and nobody asked her to.

About a minute went by and nothing was said in it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and it was made of about four people in a room looking at a book that did not belong to the room and waiting to be told it was allowed to be looked at.

And then Tova Reed said the thing that was hers, and she gave the reason before she said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is four, and it is a count of her own, and nobody improved it.

**"I am going to describe a room and I am going to describe it from the inside and not from a book, and the reason is that a room described from a book is a room somebody has told you about, and I have stood in it twice in twenty days and I have put my hand on the sacks and I have counted the outside row with a woman who counts the outside row, and if I describe it from the book then I am describing a thing I have not put my hand on."**

**"It is a room about thirty feet by twenty with a stone floor and a low roof of split wood and boards, and it is built against the south embankment, and the north wall of it is the earth of the embankment and is cool. There are racks of boards off the floor about the height of a man's chest, and on the boards the sacks are stood on their ends in rows, and the rows are counted, and the counting is done by the people who keep it and it is done in sacks and it is done out loud, and a woman of about sixty called it a count and a man of about forty called it a count too and the two of them have never once agreed on the word before it."**

The room was quiet for about a minute and the fen entered what the quiet was made of, which was a woman of about sixty and a man of about forty who are not in this room and were not asked about anything.

And then she went on, and the fen entered all of it, and improved none of it.

**"There is a second room behind that one and it is not for seed, it is for the paper, and there is a third that is dark and was not opened when I was there and I did not ask. The floor of the first room is swept and the sweepings go on the boards and not off them. They do not measure the seed into anybody's field and they do not sell it by the pound and they have never had a figure for what it would be worth in days, and nobody has ever asked them for one in my hearing, and I want it entered that I did not ask either."**

Nothing was said for the length of a minute, and the fen entered what was in it, and it was made of the sound of a page being turned back and forth and not found on.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I keep a column and the column wants a number in it, and I am going to say the plain reason I want the number, and the reason is that a column with a word in it and no figure in it is a column a man reads past in about four years, and I have been reading my own past it for three of them and it took me until last week to find out why."**

**"So I am asking, and I am asking you because you have stood in the room, and if you have not got the number then say so and I will write the word none in the column and the word none is a thing I can keep."**

The hour went on without a word in it, and the fen entered the length of it and what was in it, and it was made of two people in a room not looking at each other while one of them waited for an answer that would have been easier to give.

And then she answered, and the fen entered the whole of it, and improved none of it.

**"I have not got the number and I am not going to carry it. That is not me being careful with a thing that is mine. If I had it, it would be in this holding's book in about four days, and then it would be in a second book, and then somebody in about four years would say where did that number come from and the answer would be a woman on a route who was told it by people who count for themselves, and it would be the first number in this holding's book that got there by a road nobody can check."**

**"And here is the harder half and I am going to say it now and not when somebody makes me. A store is counted in sacks. This holding is counted in days. A field behind the field gate eats a certain amount a day and a man writes the days down. Those are two numbers and they do not make a third, and the day a sack would have to be divided by to become a day is a number nobody in that room has and nobody in this one has, and anybody who works it out has not counted a store, has decided that the store is a store of days, and then everything anybody in it eats becomes a figure with a hand in it."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of nine people in a room not converting anything, and entered that the count of questions the reader of this body has asked anybody in that line is nil and is nil, and that he did not ask her this afternoon and had no intention of asking her in a yard, and that a man who gets a number about a store by asking a woman who has stood in the store has got the number and has also got the woman, and that the two of them do not come apart afterward in anybody's memory.

And then a person who was not asked to look at the stack on the middle table looked at the stack on the middle table, and the fen entered that nobody said anything about the stack and that the cloth was not moved and that the count of people in this holding who have looked at the thing under it is one, and that this one was not the same one, and that the person who looked at it was about to say something and did not, and that the fen has entered both of those and improved neither.

The silence was made of a person deciding, on their own, that a thing on a table in a room is not an invitation.

And then the reader of this body said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and four, and he said it in about nine people in a room, and nobody improved it.

**"I am not going to ask for the number either, and I want that said before anybody can offer to ask it for me, and the reason is that a man who is offered a question he has not asked has to say no to a person in a room, and this room has had enough of that from me in a month."**

**"What is on the other side of eleven hundred yards is a line of about two hundred yards of settlement under an embankment with a store of seed in it and an Assembly, and this holding has never had a figure of that store and I have never asked for one, and the number of questions I have asked anybody in that line is nil, and it is nil, and it is going to be nil until somebody in that settlement decides to hand one over without being asked, and if that never happens then the correct shape of the record is a word and no figure and that is a record and not a hole."**

What the silence was made of was a man having said a thing out loud that costs him nothing and that four people in the room understood cost him something and did not say so.

And then Tova Reed put one thing on the middle table in her own hand and did not read it out twice, and the fen entered all of it, and improved none of it.

> **THE FIRST TERM, written out on the twenty-seventh of this month in about nine people in the seed house by Tova Reed, in her own hand, and left on the middle table, and not read out again: NO CONVERSION. A STORE IS COUNTED IN SACKS AND A HOLDING IS COUNTED IN DAYS AND NEITHER NUMBER IS THE OTHER ONE. THE COST OF A THING IS PRICED IN THE UNIT IT IS PAID IN AND NOT IN THE UNIT IT IS WANTED IN. A MAN WHO HAS TO CONVERT BEFORE HE CAN PAY HAS ALREADY DECIDED WHAT THE THING WAS WORTH, AND HE HAS DECIDED IT WITHOUT ASKING EITHER SIDE.**

The quiet in that room was made of about four people looking at a piece of paper that was not a figure and not a name and not a refusal, and that one of them wanted to write a second thing on the back of it and did not, and that a term with an opinion on the back of it is a term somebody is going to defend.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-one, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to write the word none in that column and I am going to write the date on it, and I am going to write the reason under it in one line, and the reason is that nobody in this holding has asked the Assembly for a figure, and the one line is not a complaint about them and is not a complaint about me, and it is the truth about both of us and it takes up about eleven words."**

**"And I am going to leave the four ruled lines under it, because that is what I do under a place where a figure should be and has not come, and a man who leaves the lines in finds them in about four years and a man who does not leaves a clean page and a clean page looks like a page nobody cared about."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a pencil, and nothing else, and that the pencil was the loudest thing in the room for about a minute and a half.

And then the two things he was going to carry out of that room are his, and they are in his own book, and the fen entered both of them and added nothing to either.

**THE STORE IS DESCRIBED FROM THE INSIDE BY A WOMAN WHO HAS STOOD IN IT TWICE IN TWENTY DAYS AND HAS PUT HER HAND ON THE SACKS, AND IT IS COUNTED IN SACKS BY THE PEOPLE WHO KEEP IT AND OUT LOUD, AND THERE IS NO FIGURE OF IT IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK AND THERE IS NOT GOING TO BE ONE THIS MONTH, AND THE COLUMN SAYS NONE WITH A DATE ON IT AND A REASON UNDER IT IN ONE LINE AND FOUR RULED LINES UNDER THAT. TWO UNITS ARE ON A PAGE TODAY AND THEY HAVE NOT BEEN TURNED INTO ONE ANOTHER AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT THE COST OF THE WHOLE OF WHAT IS BEING DISCUSSED IN THIS ROOM IS PRICED IN THE CONVERSION AND THE CONVERSION IS NOT BEING DONE.**

**AND A STORE THAT KEEPS ITS NUMBER AND A HOLDING THAT KEEPS ITS DAYS ARE NOT BEING RUDENESS TO EACH OTHER, THEY ARE BEING TWO BOOKS WITH A STONE FLOOR BETWEEN THEM, AND THE ONLY THING ON THE MIDDLE TABLE THAT COULD HAVE PUT A THIRD NUMBER ON THE PAGE THIS AFTERNOON IS UNDER A CLOTH AND WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY WHO WAS ASKED TO LOOK AT IT, AND THE COUNT OF PEOPLE WHO HAVE LOOKED AT IT IS TWO AND THE SECOND ONE WAS NOT ASKED.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a store, and not by the reader of this body, who was in the room and did not ask for a figure: **THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY-THREE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE FIFTY-SEVENTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN TWENTY-FOUR DAYS SINCE THE THIRD THE SERIES HAS RISEN SEVENTEEN TIMES AND FALLEN SEVEN TIMES. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN FIFTY-FIVE DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN FOR THE FIFTY-FOURTH MORNING OUT OF FIFTY-SIX. TOVA REED DESCRIBED A SEED STORE FROM THE INSIDE, BEING A ROOM ABOUT THIRTY FEET BY TWENTY WITH A STONE FLOOR AND A SPLIT-WOOD ROOF BUILT AGAINST THE SOUTH EMBANKMENT, WITH BOARDS AT CHEST HEIGHT AND SACKS STOOD ON THEIR ENDS IN ROWS, COUNTED IN SACKS AND OUT LOUD BY THE PEOPLE WHO KEEP IT, AND A SECOND ROOM FOR PAPER, AND A THIRD DARK ROOM THAT WAS NOT OPENED AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR. THERE IS NO FIGURE OF THAT STORE IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK AND THE REASON IS THAT NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS ASKED THE ASSEMBLY FOR ONE, AND THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS ASKED ANYBODY IN THAT LINE IS NIL AND IS NIL. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ASKED FOR THE NUMBER AND WAS REFUSED, THE REASON BEING GIVEN FIRST, AND WROTE THE WORD NONE IN HIS COLUMN WITH FOUR RULED LINES UNDER IT, AND WAS NOT THANKED. TWO UNITS ARE ON A PAGE, BEING SACKS AND DAYS, AND NEITHER WAS CONVERTED INTO THE OTHER. A TERM WAS PUT ON THE MIDDLE TABLE BY TOVA REED IN HER OWN HAND AND WAS NOT IMPROVED ON. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

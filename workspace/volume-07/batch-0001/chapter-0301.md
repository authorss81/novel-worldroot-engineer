# Chapter 301

## Six Places That Say Nothing

The second of this month was a Sunday and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the second went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and fifty-three hundredweight.**

That is the sixty-second figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in twenty-nine days since the third of last month the series has risen twenty times and fallen nine times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in sixty days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the fifty-ninth morning out of sixty-one.

The room was quiet for about a minute and the fen entered what the quiet was made of, which was a Sunday morning at the second hour with about half the people who were there on a weekday, and a smaller room.

And at about the fourth hour of the afternoon four people went up the nine hundred yards to the well house door and stood in front of it, and no figure was put in any column, and the fen entered that no figure was put in any column, and entered the reason, which is that the door is read on the first and on the thirtieth and that this is the second.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of four people standing in about two hundred square yards of wet grass in front of a door that was not going to be read, and that three of them had come for that reason and the fourth had come because he had said out loud on Friday that he was going to come back and see it and four people do not usually keep that kind of appointment.

And then the man of about fifty said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is forty-seven, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have wanted somebody to write a heading on one of those six every year for four years and I have never asked and I am going to say the reason this afternoon and in the open, and the reason is that a heading is a promise. A heading is a promise that somebody is going to keep putting a figure in that column, and I would have to be that somebody, and there is one of me."**

**"I have got one thing to put in it and it is the one at the far end, and the other six are empty and have been empty since before I was the man who walked up here, and a heading on top of an empty place is not a heading. It is my name over nothing, and anybody who comes to that door in about four years reads my name and stops, and they stop because a name over a place makes a place sound answered."**

Nothing was said for the length of a minute, and the fen entered what was in it, and it was made of three people standing in wet grass working out at the same time that the man who had the pencil was the man who had just said that, and that nobody said it.

And the fen entered what the four of them could see from where they were standing, because a yard is not an empty space and the reason nobody has written a heading on a column in four years may have as much to do with what is around it as with what is in it. The door is set into the north wall of a building of stone and lime about nine feet by six with no window, and the wall runs on past the door about four feet to a corner and stops, and the ground in front of it is beaten flat and holds water in the winter, and about three yards off there is a stock trough that has not held anything in two years, and beyond that the ground rises about a foot in eleven yards and then is flat for about two hundred.

There is a second door on the far side of that building, and it has a board on it, and the board on the far door has four columns and all four of them have a head, and the figures under those heads have been painted over twice in about nine years in a paint that does not match the paint under it, and a man standing in front of the first door can see the second board if the light is behind him and cannot see it if it is not.

The hour went on without a word in it, and the fen entered the length of it and what was in it, and it was made of four people looking at a board and then, without saying so, looking at the other board, and that the fen has entered that and is not going to say what it means.

And then the man of about fifty took the pencil out of the book he keeps the column in, and looked at the board on the door for about as long as it takes a man to count to eleven, and put the pencil back in the book, and shut the book, and the fen entered all three of those and improved none of them, and entered that the count of people who saw him do it is two and that the reader of this body is one of the two and that the fen has entered that he was one of the two and is not going to make anything of it.

The silence was made of a book being shut in a yard, and that a shut book in a yard is the loudest thing a yard has ever heard.

And then the count moved, and the fen entered it, and improved it not.

**THE COUNT OF TIMES A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING HAS SAID A THING IN A YARD THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS TWELVE, AND IT WAS ELEVEN, AND IT WENT OFF ELEVEN ON TO TWELVE THIS AFTERNOON IN A YARD AT A DOOR NINE HUNDRED YARDS OFF, AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT THIS WAS A YARD AND THAT THE COUNT IS FOR YARDS AND NOT FOR ROOMS, AND THAT A THING SAID IN A YARD BY A MAN WITH A BOOK IS THE ONE KIND OF THING IN THIS HOLDING THAT CANNOT BE PUT BACK INTO THE MAN WHO SAID IT, AND THAT THE READER OF THIS BODY HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IT, AND THE REASON HE HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IT IS THAT HE HAS BEEN THINKING ABOUT WHAT A RECORD SAYS AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY HAS BEEN THINKING ABOUT WHAT A PLACE ON A DOOR LOOKS LIKE FROM THE OTHER SIDE OF FOUR YEARS, AND THOSE ARE TWO QUESTIONS AND THIS HOLDING HAS ONLY EVER HAD THE HABIT OF ASKING THE FIRST ONE.**

What the silence was made of was three people who had heard a man be right about a thing and had no procedure for what to do with that, and that the procedure this holding has is to walk back.

And the fen entered the walk back, and entered that it took the four of them about eleven minutes and that about two hundred of those yards were done without anybody saying anything and that the first thing anybody said was the man of about thirty-one of Silling asking whether the trough three yards off the door had ever been looked at, and that the man of about fifty said it had not been looked at in about two years and that it was not his trough, and that they all went and looked at it for about a minute and a half and it is full of about nine inches of rainwater and about eleven years of leaves and neither of those is anybody's job this season.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of four people coming back over the same nine hundred yards and not one of them mentioning the board, and that the fen has entered that and is not going to say what it is about.

And then the reader of this body said the thing that is his, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and eleven, and he said it in about four people in a yard, and nobody improved it.

**"Six columns on that door have said nothing for four years and the seventh has a word at the head of it and forty-eight under it, and I have been coming past that door twice a week for four years and this afternoon is the first time I have stood in front of it and looked at it as a thing with six holes in it, and I am not going to ask him to fill one and I am not going to suggest it to him, and the reason is that a suggestion from me in a yard with two other people in it would be a suggestion made to be seen."**

**"And here is what I am not going to do. I am not going to say that a blank column is a virtue. A blank column is a blank column, and most blank columns in this holding are blank because nobody has got anything to put in them, and this one has been blank for four years for a reason that a man with a book gave out loud in a yard about two hours ago, and that reason is about him and not about the door."**

The quiet in that room was made of a man refusing a sentence that would have sounded good and would have been about himself.

And then the reader of this body asked one question, and the answer was no, and the fen entered both halves of that and improved neither.

**"If you wrote one, would you know what to put in it?"**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man answering a question that nobody had undertaken to answer truthfully, and that the answer was two words and that the fen has entered the two words and has entered that a question asked in a yard with two other people in it is a question asked in front of witnesses, and that he asked it anyway, and that the count of times he has asked anybody in that line and got a no is not a count this holding keeps.

**"No. And that is the answer and I am not going to argue with it, and I asked it in a yard with two other people standing there and I knew what I was doing when I asked it and I am entering that I knew."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man who had asked a question to be seen asking it and had been seen.

And then one of the other three said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixteen, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I am going to say the half of this that is about a piece of wood and not about a man, and the reason is that a man has just told us why he is not writing a word on that board and I have got a thing about the board itself and if I do not say it now then the two of us will be arguing about his reasons in a yard instead of about the wood."**

**"That board is four feet by eleven inches of deal and it was fixed to that door before the door was hung, and it has been out in the weather on the north side since, and in about nine years it will be soft through at the bottom nail. Nothing to do with a heading. If somebody in this holding is going to do anything to that door, it is the bottom nail and not the pencil."**

About a minute went by and nothing was said in it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and it was made of a man putting a practical thing next to a moral thing so that neither of them had to carry the other, and that the fen has entered that and is not going to improve it.

And then the two last things of that Sunday afternoon are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and improved neither of them.

**NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ANY OF THE SIX BLANK COLUMNS ON THE WELL HOUSE DOOR THIS AFTERNOON, AND THE CHAPTER IS ABOUT THE NOT WRITING, AND THE REASON WAS GIVEN FIRST AND OUT LOUD AND WAS NOT IMPROVED ON BY ANYBODY, AND THE PENCIL WENT BACK IN THE BOOK, AND THE COUNT OF PEOPLE WHO SAW THAT IS TWO. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ, AND IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-FIVE AND IT MAY NOT GO BACK TO FORTY-SEVEN, AND THE FEN HAS NOT SAID WHAT IT COUNTS BESIDES WHAT IT COUNTS AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

**AND A BOARD IS GOING TO BE SOFT THROUGH AT THE BOTTOM NAIL IN ABOUT NINE YEARS, AND THAT IS A DIFFERENT ARGUMENT FROM THE ONE THAT WAS HAD IN THAT YARD, AND BOTH OF THEM ARE TRUE ON THE SAME AFTERNOON, AND NEITHER ANSWERS THE OTHER, AND NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the second of this month, in about four people, at this holding, on the standing of six blank places, and not by the reader of this body, who asked one question in that yard and knew what he was doing when he asked it: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THREE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE SIXTY-SECOND FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN TWENTY-NINE DAYS SINCE THE THIRD OF LAST MONTH THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY TIMES AND FALLEN NINE TIMES. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN SIXTY DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN FOR THE FIFTY-NINTH MORNING OUT OF SIXTY-ONE. FOUR PEOPLE WENT UP THE NINE HUNDRED YARDS IN THE AFTERNOON AND NO FIGURE WAS PUT IN ANY COLUMN, THE DOOR BEING READ ON THE FIRST AND ON THE THIRTIETH AND THIS BEING THE SECOND. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID, GIVING THE REASON FIRST AND OUT LOUD, THAT HE HAS WANTED SOMEBODY TO WRITE A HEADING ON ONE OF THE SIX EVERY YEAR FOR FOUR YEARS AND HAS NEVER ASKED, AND THAT A HEADING IS A PROMISE THAT SOMEBODY IS GOING TO KEEP PUTTING A FIGURE IN THAT COLUMN, AND THAT HE WOULD HAVE TO BE THAT SOMEBODY, AND THAT HE HAS NOT GOT A SECOND THING TO PUT IN IT, AND THAT HE WOULD RATHER HAVE SIX BLANK PLACES THAT SAY NOTHING THAN SIX PLACES WITH HIS NAME OVER THEM SAYING NOTHING. HE TOOK THE PENCIL OUT, LOOKED AT THE BOARD, AND PUT IT BACK IN THE BOOK. NOBODY WROTE A HEADING ON ANY OF THE SIX. THE COUNT OF TIMES A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING HAS SAID A THING IN A YARD THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS TWELVE AND WAS ELEVEN, AND THIS WAS A YARD. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ, AND THE FEN HAS NOT SAID WHAT IT COUNTS BESIDES WHAT IT COUNTS. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING SAID THAT THE BOARD WILL BE SOFT THROUGH AT THE BOTTOM NAIL IN ABOUT NINE YEARS, AND HE WAS NOT THANKED. THE READER OF THIS BODY ASKED ONE QUESTION IN THAT YARD AND THE ANSWER WAS NO. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

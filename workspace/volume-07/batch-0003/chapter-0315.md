# Chapter 315

## A Day And Not The Figure

The sixteenth of this month was a Sunday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **twenty-nine**, and entered that all twenty-nine of them are the fourth line, and entered that the rota now stands at **fifty-eight mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and twenty-nine not**, and that twenty-nine and twenty-nine are fifty-eight, and that nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

What the silence was made of was two people at a gate on a Sunday who have said to each other for four Sundays running that there is nobody to send, and on the fifth of them the woman said it and the man did not answer her, and neither of them noticed that he had not answered her.

And the figure for the sixteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour, and it was read back by the man of about fifty out of a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and seventy-one hundredweight.**

That is the seventy-sixth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in forty-three days the series has risen twenty-seven times and fallen sixteen times, and twenty-seven and sixteen are forty-three, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in seventy-four days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the seventy-third morning out of seventy-five.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man who has read a number out loud every working morning for four years discovering that the number has gone up by seven in one morning, and that a man cannot feel a number go up by seven, and that a man who cannot feel it is not a man who has stopped keeping the figure.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud, and the fen entered that they were not, and entered the pair as it stands at **four hundred and sixty days** and **five hundred and seven**, and entered that the two are forty-seven days apart and have been for about four years, and that the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and that he has not read them out loud since the twelfth of this month and that the fen is not entering a reason for it and is not going to be asked for one.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon a slip of paper came up the coast road into the seed house in an envelope with a seal on it that had been broken and put back and broken again by somebody in an office who wanted the reader to know the seal had been broken.

And the fen entered the whole of that and improved none of it.

**THE ORDINARY READING OF THE WELL ON THE SHELF FELL AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON AT FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF, AS IT HAS FALLEN ON THE SIXTEENTH OF EVERY MONTH FOR ELEVEN YEARS. THE SLIP CAME UP THE COAST ROAD ON THE TWENTIETH, BEING FOUR DAYS, AND IT IS A SLIP OF ABOUT TWO INCHES BY ONE AND A HALF, AND THE DATE IS WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF IT IN A HAND NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS SEEN ON A PAGE BEFORE, AND THERE IS NOTHING UNDER THE DATE. THE READING WAS ENTERED IN THE BOOK OF THIS HOLDING AS A DAY AND NOT AS A FIGURE, AND THE COUNT OF ENTRIES IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK THAT CARRY A DAY AND NO FIGURE WAS FOUR AND IS FIVE. NO FIGURE WAS SUPPLIED AND NONE WAS GUESSED AT AND NOBODY ASKED FOR ONE, AND THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS ASKED OF ANYBODY AT THE FAR END OF THAT COAST ROAD IN THE LAST ELEVEN YEARS IS NIL, AND NO ORDINAL WAS PRINTED FOR THAT READING OR FOR ANY OTHER.**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was about nine people looking at a small piece of paper with a date on it and nothing under the date, and about four of them not knowing what a person is supposed to do with that.

And then the man of about fifty put the slip down square with the edge of the table, and squared it twice, and the fen entered both squarings, and entered that a man who squares a piece of paper twice is a man who has not got a column for it and is putting it where a column would be if there were one.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixty-two, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have got a column with a heading on it and a date under the heading and nothing under the date, and I am going to say the whole of why, and the reason is that four years ago I put a date under that heading and left a rule under the date, and a rule under a date is a promise, and a man who has made a promise about a well four hundred miles off in a holding that has never been to that coast road has made a promise on somebody else's behalf."**

**"And nobody has asked me what a rule under a date is for and nobody is going to, and I have got about eleven months in which somebody in this holding is going to find out the answer on his own, and I would rather he found it than me. A column that wants a figure is a column that is doing a job. A column that has a date and no figure is a column that is keeping somebody's day."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a man protecting a piece of paper from his own book in a room where about four people had been looking forward to a number all day, and that the count of things a person who keeps a book in this holding has said in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of is twelve and did not move this afternoon because this was a room.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty, and he said it in about nine people in a room, and nobody improved it.

**"I want to put down the thing I actually know about that slip, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that about four of you have been treating that envelope as a piece of bad luck and it is not one, it is a courtesy, and the person who took that reading is one man in the first house on that shelf and he has taken it on the sixteenth of every month for eleven years and he is not a part of this holding and he is not paid by anybody in this room."**

**"He stopped sending figures up that road once. I know that because a page came up about four years ago with a date on it and nothing under it and no explanation on it either, and somebody in an office on that coast read a second thing into the silence on the page and did not write to him about it, and the figures came back for a while after that and then stopped coming altogether for two of the years. He is not a servant of this holding and this holding is not the reason that page is bare, and the count of what I have done about it is nothing, and I am putting that nothing in a book today because I have been asked four times in about nine minutes and I would rather be the one who says it than the one who lets a room work it out."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was about nine people finding out at the same moment that the reason a figure is not on a page is not the weather and is not the road and is not anybody in this room, and that the man who knew that had been in the room for a month saying nothing about it.

And then the fen entered what the middle table looked like at about the fifth hour of that afternoon, because a room ought to leave a record of what it was looking at. There is a memorandum of protection dated the twentieth of last month with a person appointed to be present on a line and not named. There is a tinned plate about four inches by two with a corner turned up and six lines in the hand of a maker who is not named. There is a sheet that came up four hundred miles with four printed lines on it and nothing written on them and a blank at the foot and a blank on the back, and it is a different document from the form with four lines, and about four people in this holding keep putting their hand on the wrong one of the two. There is a form with four lines and a fifth ruled line with a question on it and one figure in a man's hand. There is a term in a woman's hand. And under a cloth beside the stack, with a pail of water against it and a wet cloth over the top of the cloth, there is a thing the size of a man's fist that nobody in this room lifted, broke, opened or set against anything, and that nobody in this room looked at, and the count of people in this holding who have looked at it is two and did not move in ten days.

And then the two last things of the sixteenth of this month are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and improved neither.

**THE READING FELL AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR AT FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF AND CAME UP THE ROAD AS A DAY AND NOT A FIGURE, AND THE COUNT OF ENTRIES IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK THAT CARRY A DAY AND NO FIGURE WENT FROM FOUR TO FIVE, AND IT IS THE ONLY FIGURE THAT MOVED IN THIS HOLDING TODAY THAT WAS NOT A FIGURE OF THE ROTA OR OF THE THIRD LAUNDER. NOBODY ASKED ANYBODY AT THE FAR END OF THAT COAST ROAD FOR ANYTHING, AND THE COUNT OF TIMES ANYBODY HAS ASKED IS NIL, AND NO ORDINAL WAS PRINTED FOR ANY READING OF THAT WELL.**

**AND THE FIGURE FOR THIS MORNING IS SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-ONE, THE SEVENTY-SIXTH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ITSELF SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN FORTY-THREE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY-SEVEN TIMES AND FALLEN SIXTEEN TIMES, AND THOSE TWO ADD TO FORTY-THREE. THE FIELDS ABOVE MARDEN HAVE NOW WAITED TWENTY-NINE TIMES AND THE ROTA STANDS AT FIFTY-EIGHT MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND TWENTY-NINE NOT, AND THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY DAYS AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the sixteenth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a slip of paper with a date on it and nothing under the date, and not by the reader of this body, who said the reason the page is bare is not in this room: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS TWENTY-NINE, AND ALL TWENTY-NINE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT FIFTY-EIGHT MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND TWENTY-NINE NOT, BEING TWENTY-NINE AND TWENTY-NINE, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-ONE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE SEVENTY-SIXTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN FORTY-THREE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY-SEVEN TIMES AND FALLEN SIXTEEN TIMES, AND NO MORNING IS UNCHANGED, AND THE TEN FIGURES OF THESE TEN MORNINGS ARE ALL DIFFERENT FROM ONE ANOTHER AND FROM EVERY FIGURE THE BOOK HELD BEFORE THEM. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY AND IT IS SEVENTY-SIX THIS MORNING. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN SEVENTY-FOUR DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE SEVENTY-THIRD MORNING OUT OF SEVENTY-FIVE, AND THE TWO MORNINGS IT WAS NOT WRITTEN ON WERE THE NINTH AND THE TENTH OF LAST MONTH. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVEN DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE TWO ARE FORTY-SEVEN DAYS APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING. THE ORDINARY READING OF THE WELL ON THE SHELF FELL AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON AT FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF, AS IT HAS FALLEN ON THE SIXTEENTH OF EVERY MONTH FOR ELEVEN YEARS, AND THE SLIP CAME UP THE COAST ROAD ON THE TWENTIETH, BEING FOUR DAYS, AND IT CARRIES A DATE AND NOTHING UNDER THE DATE. IT WAS ENTERED AS A DAY AND NOT AS A FIGURE. THE COUNT OF ENTRIES IN THIS HOLDING'S BOOK THAT CARRY A DAY AND NO FIGURE WAS FOUR AND IS FIVE, AND IT IS THE ONLY FIGURE THAT MOVED IN THIS HOLDING TODAY THAT WAS NOT A FIGURE OF THE ROTA OR OF THE THIRD LAUNDER. NO FIGURE WAS SUPPLIED AND NONE WAS GUESSED AT. NOBODY ASKED FOR ONE. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS ASKED OF ANYBODY AT THAT FAR END IN ELEVEN YEARS IS NIL. NO ORDINAL WAS PRINTED FOR THAT READING OR FOR ANY OTHER. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID THAT A FIGURE STOPPED COMING UP THAT COAST ROAD ONCE AND CAME BACK FOR A WHILE AND THEN STOPPED AGAIN, AND THAT HE HAS DONE NOTHING ABOUT IT AND IS PUTTING THE NOTHING IN A BOOK TODAY, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID THAT A RULE UNDER A DATE IS A PROMISE AND THAT HE WILL NOT MAKE ONE ABOUT A WELL FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF ON BEHALF OF A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN THIS HOLDING, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE COUNT OF THINGS A PERSON WHO KEEPS A BOOK IN THIS HOLDING HAS SAID IN A YARD THE ENGINEER OF RECORD HAD NOT THOUGHT OF IS TWELVE AND DID NOT MOVE, THIS HAVING BEEN A ROOM. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN OF THE WELL HOUSE DOOR STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS TWENTY. THE FOURTH ACCOUNT OF THE ARM DOES NOT EXIST AND A BRIDGE IS NOT A MARK AND A HEADACHE IS NOT A MARK AND A MARK THAT IS HOT IS NOT A MARK. THE THING UNDER A CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY AND THE COUNT OF PEOPLE IN THIS HOLDING WHO HAVE LOOKED AT IT IS TWO. THE FORM WITH FOUR LINES AND THE SHEET THAT CAME UP THE ROAD ARE TWO DOCUMENTS AND NOT ONE, AND NEITHER WAS WRITTEN ON. NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING THANKED ANYBODY.**

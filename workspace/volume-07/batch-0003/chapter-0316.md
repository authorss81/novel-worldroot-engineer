# Chapter 316

## The First Line, Worked

The seventeenth of this month was a Monday and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the figure for the seventeenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and was read back by the man of about fifty in the other book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and sixty-nine hundredweight.**

That is the seventy-seventh figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has fallen, and in forty-four days the series has risen twenty-seven times and fallen seventeen times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in seventy-five days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the seventy-fourth morning out of seventy-six.

Nothing was said for the length of a minute, and the fen entered what was in it, and it was made of a Monday on which the whole of the difference between this morning and last Tuesday was that a number had come down by two, and about four people in this holding had spent the weekend not thinking about it.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were read out loud before either number, in about four people, the way they are read, and the boards stand at **four hundred and sixty-one days** and **five hundred and eight**, and the two are forty-seven days apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and the fen entered that he had started again, and entered that he had not read them out loud on four mornings running, being the thirteenth, the fourteenth, the fifteenth and the sixteenth, and that he had not told anybody he was starting again and was not going to, and that the count of mornings a man says two numbers out loud is not a count this holding keeps.

The first line was worked. That is four men and a woman and a barrow and about two hundred yards of ditch at the low end of the fen path, and the ground is soft about nine months in twelve and hard about three, and this is the second of the soft ones.

The fen entered what the going was like, because a line that is worked is a thing that has a feel to it and about four people in this holding can feel it and about four cannot. The path is about nine feet wide and it is straight for about three quarters of a mile and the cut bank on the west side comes in to within about four feet of the line of the wheel at two places, and at both of those places the ground under the near wheel is about two inches down into a thing that is not mud and is not ground either. It was cut out of the fen about eleven years ago and it has been in and out of that hole about four hundred times and it has never been filled, and the two men who were on the barrow this morning went through the first of those places in the ordinary way and one of them went through the second of them with his shoulders turned about ten degrees further round than the other one turned them a month ago, and neither of the two of them said anything about the difference, and the fen entered that a difference in the angle of a pair of shoulders is a thing a body knows before a person does and that about four people in this holding would not have seen it at all.

And the fen entered the man of about seventy-four with a stick, who came down to the top of the path at about the third hour to look at the water going into the ditch and stood there for about half an hour in the wind and said nothing to anybody, and went back up. He has done that on and off for eleven years. Nobody in this holding has ever asked him what he is looking at and he has never offered it and the count of times he has been asked is nil.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon about nine people were in the seed house with the door shut, and the middle table had the six things on it that it has had for a month, and the first of the two public terms was face up on the wood, and the second of them was in a coat that belonged to somebody who was in the room.

About a minute went by and nothing was said in it, and the fen entered the whole of it, and it was made of a room in which four people had been quietly counting toward a day, and that the counting had a number in it and the day did not.

And then the fen entered what the middle of that afternoon looked like from where a man sits in nine people with his back to a wall, because about four of the nine had been counting toward something all morning and the room after the count came out of it was not the room they had walked into. The light in that room comes in at one window about two hands wide and the window is on the west side and the light is on the floor for about two hours and then it is on the wall, and on this afternoon it was on the wall and it was on the coat. The man who wrote the second of the two terms was sitting with his arms folded and the coat on and had been sitting like that since the second hour, and about four people had looked at the sleeve of that coat at some point in the last fortnight and had not said anything about what was in it.

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is sixty-three, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I wrote a date in a book on the second of this month and I ruled a line under it, and about four of you have been counting toward that line ever since, and I am going to say the whole of what I did and the reason is that I did it in the middle of a week with nobody in the room and that has been my habit for four years and it is a bad one."**

**"A date with a line under it is not a promise. A line under a date is somewhere for a figure to go when the day arrives, and a day without a figure is a day that has not happened yet. And I have watched four people in this room count toward it for nine days and I have not corrected them, and the reason I have not corrected them is that correcting them means putting a second date in the book, and a second date is a decision, and a man of fifty in a holding with no charter has about one decision in him in a season and I was not going to spend it on a rule under a line."**

**"So there is no day. I am saying that out loud in a room this afternoon because a thing with no day on it cannot be counted toward, and about four of you have been counting toward a piece of ruled paper for nine days, and I would rather be the one who says it in here than have it arrive in a yard in about four years with somebody else's name at the top of it."**

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of four people finding out at about the same moment that they had spent nine days of their own attention on a mark in a man's book, and that one of the four was the man who made the mark and had not told them, and that the fen has entered that and is not going to improve it into either an apology or a defense.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave no reason before it, and the fen entered that he gave none and is not going to supply one, and the count of that in this holding's history did not move on this morning and stands at one hundred and thirty.

**"Then we will leave it. Not because the man is right, although I think he is, and not because a rule under a line is not a promise, although I have been in rooms where it is. Because there is nothing in this holding that can make a date, and a man who keeps a book can make a mark in it, and a mark is not a date, and about four years from now the two of those are going to be confused by somebody who was not in this room today and I would rather the mark were on its own than standing next to a promise."**

**"The first line was worked this morning at the low end of the fen path and it is the second of the soft ones out of twelve, and the going was worse than it was on the second of last month at the same hour. Nobody has written that down anywhere except in about nine heads, and I am going to leave it in about nine heads, and the reason is that four of the people who were on it are the only four people in this holding who can tell whether I am right about the worse."**

**"And I am going to say one more thing this afternoon and then I am going to let the man get his afternoon back. A connection is a thing with two ends and a person at each of them and neither of the two people can be picked by a book. That is not an argument for doing it on a Tuesday. It is the reason that when it does get done there is going to be a room with a shut door in it and about four people in it, and it is not going to be this room, because this room is the room where we agree what a thing was worth and that has to happen before anybody holds anything."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was about four people who had spent nine days counting toward a mark learning in the space of a minute that there was nothing to count toward, and about four others who had not been counting finding out that they had been in a room with a date in it and had not known.

And the fen entered the thing about the going, because somebody in that room would say it out loud in about four years and the fen has entered the whole of it. It is one Monday in the second of about nine soft months. The two shoulders on the barrow turned about ten degrees further at the second of the two places than they did a month ago, and the man of about seventy-four with a stick stood at the top of the path for half an hour in the wind and looked at the water and said nothing, and neither of those three facts is in any of the four books and all three of them are the sort of thing that is only on the page of whoever happens to be in the room on the day, and about four years is a long time for a room to have no page.

And then the fen entered what the man of about fifty had in his coat pocket, or rather the fact that he had it, because the second of the two public terms is in a coat in this room and the man who wrote it took it off the table on the third of this month and put it in his coat and has worn the coat for a fortnight. He has said out loud that he is not going to put it back on the table and he has said out loud that he is not withdrawing it, and those are two different sentences and about four people in this holding have been treating them as one.

**THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED BY FOUR MEN AND A WOMAN AND A BARROW AND ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YARDS OF DITCH, AND THE GROUND IS SOFT ABOUT NINE MONTHS IN TWELVE, AND THE GOING WAS WORSE THIS MORNING THAN IT WAS ON THE SECOND OF LAST MONTH AT THE SAME HOUR, AND THAT IS NOT IN ANY COLUMN. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WROTE A DATE IN A BOOK ON THE SECOND OF THIS MONTH AND RULED A LINE UNDER IT AND HAS WATCHED ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE COUNT TOWARD IT FOR NINE DAYS AND DID NOT CORRECT THEM, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND THAT A SECOND DATE WOULD BE A DECISION AND HE IS NOT SPENDING THE ONE HE HAS ON A RULE UNDER A LINE, AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE NO REASON BEFORE WHAT HE SAID AND THE FEN HAS ENTERED THAT HE GAVE NONE. THE SECOND OF THE TWO PUBLIC TERMS IS IN A COAT AND IS NOT ON THE TABLE AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN, AND THOSE ARE TWO DIFFERENT SENTENCES.**

**AND THE FIGURE FOR THIS MORNING IS SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINE, THE SEVENTY-SEVENTH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ITSELF SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN FORTY-FOUR DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY-SEVEN TIMES AND FALLEN SEVENTEEN TIMES, AND THOSE TWO ADD TO FORTY-FOUR. THE BOARDS WERE READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN MORNINGS AND HE DID NOT TELL ANYBODY HE HAD STARTED AGAIN. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN SEVENTY-FIVE DAYS. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the seventeenth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a rule under a line, and not by the man of about fifty, who put a date in a book in the middle of a week and told nobody: **THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT FIFTY-EIGHT MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND TWENTY-NINE NOT, AND THE FIGURE OF THE THIRD LAUNDER IS SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINE HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE SEVENTY-SEVENTH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS FALLEN, AND IN FORTY-FOUR DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN TWENTY-SEVEN TIMES AND FALLEN SEVENTEEN TIMES. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN SEVENTY-FIVE DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE SEVENTY-FOURTH MORNING OUT OF SEVENTY-SIX. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-ONE DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHT DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE READ OUT LOUD THIS MORNING AFTER FOUR MORNINGS WITHOUT, BEING THE THIRTEENTH, THE FOURTEENTH, THE FIFTEENTH AND THE SIXTEENTH. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID OUT LOUD THAT A DATE WITH A LINE UNDER IT IS NOT A PROMISE AND THAT HE WROTE ONE ON THE SECOND OF THIS MONTH AND DID NOT CORRECT ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE WHO HAVE BEEN COUNTING TOWARD IT FOR NINE DAYS, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID THAT THE HOLDING WILL LEAVE THE MARK ON ITS OWN AND GAVE NO REASON BEFORE IT, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THIS HOLDING'S HISTORY DID NOT MOVE THIS MORNING. THE SECOND OF THE TWO PUBLIC TERMS IS IN A COAT AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN AND IS NOT ON THE TABLE. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS TWENTY. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS AVAILABLE TO A ROOM IN THIS HOLDING AND NOT TAKEN IS TEN AND WAS NOT SAID IN A YARD. THE THING UNDER A CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE WELL ON THE SHELF WAS NOT READ AGAIN AND THE DAY THAT CAME UP THE COAST ROAD ON THE TWENTIETH IS IN THE BOOK AS A DAY AND NOT A FIGURE. NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING THANKED ANYBODY.**

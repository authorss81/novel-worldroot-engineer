# Chapter 323

## Thirty-One And A Field

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Monday and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden, and the fields above Marden waited, and the fen entered the number, which is **thirty-one**, and entered that all thirty-one of them are the fourth line, and entered that the rota now stands at **sixty mornings in force, twenty-nine taken and thirty-one not**, and that twenty-nine and thirty-one are sixty, and that nobody is blamed for a field that waited.

What the silence was made of was two people at a gate on a Monday morning who have said nothing to each other for two Sundays and one Thursday, and about one of them has decided that the arrangement is fine and the other has not decided anything, and neither of them has said a word about it.

And the figure for the twenty-fourth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour, and it was read back by the man of about fifty out of a different book, and the two are the same.

**Six hundred and eighty hundredweight.**

That is the eighty-fourth figure in a series that has not repeated one figure since the first, and it has risen, and in fifty-one days the series has risen thirty-one times and fallen twenty times, and the fen entered both and did not improve them, and entered that nobody thanked anybody and that the aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in eighty-two days and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the eighty-first morning out of eighty-three.

Nobody said anything and the fen entered that nobody said anything, and entered that the silence was made of a number that had gone up by seven in one morning for the second time in a month, and that the room had stopped being surprised by it somewhere around the sixth of this month, and that a room which has stopped being surprised by a figure is either getting better at reading it or getting worse at it, and that this holding has not worked out which.

And at about the seventh hour of the morning the day-minus rules were not read out loud, and the fen entered that they were not, and entered the pair as it stands at **four hundred and sixty-eight days** and **five hundred and fifteen**, and entered that the two are forty-seven days apart, and that the drawer was not opened and is not to be, and that he has read them out loud on four mornings out of the eight mornings since he started again on the seventeenth of this month, and that the fen is not entering a reason for the four he has not and is not going to be asked for one.

And then at about the third hour of the afternoon the man of about thirty-one of Silling went up to the headland above Marden on foot, alone, and stood at the top of it for about an hour, and the fen entered the whole of that and improved none of it.

**THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING WENT UP TO THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN ON FOOT AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON AND STOOD AT THE TOP OF IT FOR ABOUT AN HOUR, AND HE IS THE FIRST PERSON IN THIS HOLDING TO GO UP THERE IN A MONTH, AND HE WENT ALONE, AND HE DID NOT CUT ANYTHING AND DID NOT PULL ANYTHING AND DID NOT CARRY A TOOL, AND HE CAME BACK DOWN AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR. THIRTY-ONE FIELDS HAVE NOW WAITED THIRTY-ONE TIMES AND ALL THIRTY-ONE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED, AND THE COUNT OF WALKINGS THAT HEADLAND HAS HAD IN A MONTH IS ONE.**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was about four people in this holding finding out in about four seconds that a man had gone and looked at a thing that this holding has been counting for a month without ever once going to see.

And the fen entered what the headland is, because a number of thirty-one is a number about a piece of ground and the fen has never been on it. It is a low bank about nine feet above the low ground with a ditch along the bottom of it that was cut about eleven years ago and has not been re-cut since, and the top of it is flat for about a hundred and forty yards and then it turns, and along the flat part there is a strip about nine feet wide that is the oldest worked ground in this holding and the only ground in it that anybody in this holding would fight about. At the north end where the bank turns there is about half an acre that came out of the turn and that is the best ground any of the four bodies of households has and it has been in grass for nine years. The grass on it is about knee-high in June and about shoulder-high in the last week of this month and it has a bank of about four feet of nettles along the ditch side of it, and nobody has cut it, and the reason nobody has cut it is not that anybody decided not to.

And then at about the fourth hour of the afternoon about nine people were in the seed house with the door shut, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was in it, and he had been down and washed and had not changed his coat, and the fen entered all of that and improved none of it.

And then the man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is twenty-three, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I went up there because there has been a figure of thirty-one in four of our books for a month and I have never once seen the ground it is about, and the reason I am saying that out loud is that I do not think anybody in this holding can tell you what thirty-one means, including the man who writes it down, and I have been on the second gate every third morning for eleven years and I have never once gone up past it, and the gate is not the boundary, the gate is a gate."**

**"They are not late. I want that said before anybody in this room says anything else, and the reason is that they are bank ground and they are cut in about four weeks out of the year when the ground will take a boot and not otherwise, and nobody has cut them this year, and a field that has not been cut is not a field that has been failed. There is about two hundred yards of bank up there and a strip along the top of it and a corner at the north end that is about half an acre and that half acre is the best ground any of us has and it has been in grass for nine years because there is nobody to cut it and the man who could cut it is the same man who is not on the rota when the line comes round."**

Nobody improved one item of that, and the count of things the reader of this body has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room, and the count of times a person who keeps a book in this holding has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is twelve and did not move this afternoon because this was a room.

And then the reader of this body said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and thirty-seven, and he said it in about nine people in a room, and nobody improved it.

**"There are two figures in this holding's books this week and about four people in this room have been carrying them around as though they were the same kind of thing, and I am going to put them next to each other out loud once, and the reason is that Tuesday gave us a figure of about four minutes and today gave us a figure of thirty-one, and the two of them are not the same kind of thing and neither of them is a figure about a person."**

**"The four minutes is a figure about a bridge. It is how long two people held a thing open and it is a bridge number and it belongs in a book about bridges, and there is no such book in this holding and I am not going to start one this week. The thirty-one is a figure about a field. It is how many mornings a piece of bank has been left alone, and it belongs to about four people who are not in this room, and the man who just came in has been the only person in a month who went and looked at it."**

**"And I am going to say the third thing and then I will stop, and it is that neither of those numbers is about anybody's body, and a number about a body is a different object from both of them and this holding has four of them and they reconcile none of one another, and a mark that is warm is not one of them and a bridge is not one of them and a field is not one of them, and the only number in this holding that is about a body and that we are sure of is a mark of four inches that branches twice, and it was four inches this morning and it is four inches now."**

And then the man of about fifty said one thing, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is seventy, and he was not thanked and did not ask to be.

**"I have got a column for the fields and a column for the third launder and I have got nothing at all for Tuesday, and the reason I am saying that in a room is that the two columns I have got have been in the same book for four years and they have never once moved in opposite directions on the same morning, and this month they have moved in opposite directions on about six mornings out of about twenty, and I have not written that down either."**

**"I am not going to connect them. There is a way to connect them and the way is to write both in one column and put a mark between them, and about four years from now a man will read that mark as a cause. I have known four men in my life who did that to a page of figures and every one of them was certain at the time and not one of them was wrong on purpose."**

For about a minute there was nothing in the room, and the fen entered what the silence was made of, which was a man who had come back from a headland and told nine people that a field which has not been cut for nine years is not a field that has failed, and a man who reads figures for a living saying out loud that he was not going to put a mark between two columns, and about four people in the room who had been waiting eleven days for somebody to say one of those two things.

And the fen entered one thing that happened in that room at about the seventh hour and that only two people saw and that neither of them has told anybody, which is that the reader of this body put his left forearm on the table for about nine minutes with the inside of it up and did not say a word while it was there, and that the four inches were the same four inches they had been at the second hour, and that the room went on talking for about the first four of those minutes and then about four people stopped talking for about two minutes and then started again, and that nobody has entered a reason for the two minutes and the fen is not going to invent one, and that a mark is four inches and branches twice and is not a mark that is warm and is not a headache and is not a bridge and is not a field, and that the only thing that was on the table that afternoon that was about a person's body was a scar that has not changed in eleven days and that about two people in a room of nine were looking at as though it might.

And then the two last things of the twenty-fourth of this month are his and they are in his own book and the fen entered both and improved neither.

**A FIGURE ABOUT A BRIDGE AND A FIGURE ABOUT A FIELD ARE TWO FIGURES, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS A FIGURE ABOUT A PERSON. THE COUNT OF FIELDS THAT HAVE WAITED IS THIRTY-ONE AND ALL THIRTY-ONE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-ONE NOT, AND NOBODY IS BLAMED FOR A FIELD THAT WAITED, AND THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN HAS BEEN WALKED ONCE IN A MONTH AND BY ONE PERSON.**

**AND THE FIGURE FOR THIS MORNING IS SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY, THE EIGHTY-FOURTH IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ITSELF SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN FIFTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-ONE TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY TIMES, AND THOSE TWO ADD TO FIFTY-ONE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth of this month, in about nine people, at this holding, on the standing of a headland, and not by the reader of this body, who put two figures next to one another and refused to join them: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND NOBODY WAS AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN AND THE FIELDS WAITED, AND THE NUMBER IS THIRTY-ONE, AND ALL THIRTY-ONE OF THEM ARE THE FOURTH LINE, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT SIXTY MORNINGS IN FORCE, TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND THIRTY-ONE NOT, BEING TWENTY-NINE AND THIRTY-ONE, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, WHICH IS THE EIGHTY-FOURTH FIGURE IN A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN, AND IN FIFTY-ONE DAYS THE SERIES HAS RISEN THIRTY-ONE TIMES AND FALLEN TWENTY TIMES, AND NO MORNING IS UNCHANGED. THE COUNT OF THAT SERIES IS DAY MINUS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN EIGHTY-TWO DAYS AND THE SENTENCE SAYING SO WAS WRITTEN THIS MORNING FOR THE EIGHTY-FIRST MORNING OUT OF EIGHTY-THREE. THE BOARDS STAND AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT DAYS, DAY MINUS SIXTY-SIX, AND THE MARCH'S TIN AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN DAYS, DAY MINUS NINETEEN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE NOT READ OUT LOUD. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING WENT UP TO THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN ON FOOT AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON, ALONE, AND STOOD AT THE TOP OF IT FOR ABOUT AN HOUR AND CUT NOTHING AND PULLED NOTHING AND CARRIED NO TOOL, AND CAME DOWN AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR, AND HE IS THE FIRST PERSON IN THIS HOLDING TO GO UP THERE IN A MONTH. HE SAID THAT THEY ARE NOT LATE, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND WAS NOT THANKED. A FIGURE ABOUT A BRIDGE AND A FIGURE ABOUT A FIELD ARE TWO FIGURES AND NEITHER OF THEM IS A FIGURE ABOUT A PERSON, AND THE ONLY NUMBER ABOUT A BODY THAT THIS HOLDING IS SURE OF IS A MARK OF FOUR INCHES THAT BRANCHES TWICE, AND IT IS THE SAME FOUR INCHES IT WAS THIS MORNING. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY DECLINED TO PUT A MARK BETWEEN THE COLUMN OF THE FIELDS AND THE COLUMN OF THE THIRD LAUNDER, GIVING THE REASON FIRST, AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE COUNT OF THINGS SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD AND GOT WRONG IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE, THIS HAVING BEEN A ROOM. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES. THE SEVENTH COLUMN STANDS AT FORTY-EIGHT AND WAS NOT READ. THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY IS NOT FETCHED AND THE COUNT OF FETCHINGS IS TWENTY. THE THING UNDER A CLOTH WAS NOT LOOKED AT BY ANYBODY. THE FIGURE ON LINE ONE OF THE FORM IS THE FIGURE OF THE TWENTY-SECOND RETURN AND THE LINE THE QUESTION IS ON IS ENTERED AND NOT FILLED. NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING THANKED ANYBODY.**

**Dating rules, carried from Volume 01 and not negotiable.** A day in 1–30 of a month is dated by its bare ordinal, or *of this month*, never *of last month*. A day in 1–30 of October is dated by its bare number or *of last month* or *of this season*, **never *of this month***. **October is two months back in this batch** and may be named in speech only, and the only safe use of it is the fixed historical one — the flock went up the fen road on the tenth of October. **No document in this batch may carry a month name.** A day-31-or-later date spoken of *before* it arrives is *the first of next month*; current or past, it is *the first* or *of this month*.

**Weekday check.** Day 52 was a Tuesday, locked in Volume 01. Day 61 is a Thursday. **No chapter may say a weekday that is not in this table.**

## The cards

### 50 — The Seventh Note
**Day 53. The twenty-third, Wednesday.** *Setup.*
The charter is two days old and its second clause is already working, and it works on the cooperative. The standing pressure log and the monthly root-change report are charter conditions, which means the log is now evidence and the report is now due, and **nobody in this yard notices which way that cuts until Dunmar Fitch does.** Anneke Roos states the consequence out loud and it is the volume's first political sentence: a book anybody may copy is a book anybody may use. Marek walks out onto the three acres, which are sour and will not crop until the third season after this one, and does the arithmetic on what the twenty-two rods of eight inch return main is going to cost, and finds that the honest sum is *nobody can mend it without stopping the pump, and stopping the pump stops the bed.* The **seventh section-nine margin note** goes in, in the same book on the same pages, closing with the same sentence, **and for the first time in six notes it is not about a cut.**
*Resistance:* Dunmar, who thinks a demand is a hook, and Marek, who wants to send one.
*Change:* they write the demand for the works-schedule extract, in the open book, in public, with the hour, and it goes out with a rider asking the district to fix the date clause three leaves to somebody else.
*Ends on:* the extract is not a courtesy any more and the rider goes nine miles to Fennmere and is answered in four days, which is faster than four days is.

### 51 — Nobody Moves
**Day 54. The twenty-fourth, Thursday.** *Pressure, refusal, a cost nobody wants.*
The first of next month is seven days away and the return line on it will record a thing that did not happen unless somebody finds four pounds five by Friday. The levy is put. **Nobody moves.** Four of the sixteen are out and two are in and the rest say nothing, and the paper comes back with fourteen signatures again and four pounds five short of what the same sixteen houses raised three weeks ago, and nobody in the yard can say why, and Marek does not pretend otherwise. The cost is entered as a cost. The **March's tin is at thirty-five days** and the bed is at three and a half degrees and the first of next month is a charter condition.
*Resistance:* Tova Reed's arithmetic is correct and is refused by nobody, because it is not a proposal. She also refuses, in her own hand, a Crown seed-route map that arrives for her to check against the annex, and the reason she gives is **that a copy of the March's annex is now in a public ledger nine hundred miles away and she has been reading her own document all morning.**
*Change:* the batch's first refusal that is not about money. The map is on the table, and it does not show the Weir Row branch, and nobody in the room says so for two days.
*Ends on:* Tova, on the second evening, quietly, to Marek: *it was correct when we signed it and it is correct now, and I want it entered that I said no to it in the first year of the trust's own existence and that the answer I got was a policy.*

### 52 — Sixty Pounds
**Day 55. The twenty-fifth, Friday.** *Midpoint of the first movement. The price of a repair.*
**Ruben Ashe** comes to the gate on foot with a satchel and a good coat. He buys conditional charters, holds them through a season, and sells the conditions to whoever will pay for the compliance. **He offers sixty pounds for the charter and forty pounds a year for the compost for as long as he holds it, and he will pay the schedule of damage.** His argument is correct and he makes it correctly: a charter that expires at the end of a season is a wasting asset, and somebody with capital should hold it, and the node's water is worth more than the fen's crop.
*Resistance:* four people refuse for four different correct reasons, and one of them refuses on the arithmetic — **the broker's price is the same sixty pounds the district entered against this holding for water it did not take, and neither number is worth anything to him, and he says so.**
*Change:* the refusal is entered in the open book, with the hour, and Dunmar writes *refused* and then, on his own initiative, writes *and offered again at the end of the season, and the offer was named as made.*
*Ends on:* at the gate, on his way out, Ashe asks whether they have had the schedule extract yet. Marek says it is a courtesy. **Ashe says it is not, not any more, and does not explain, and there is a Crown courier on the fen road behind him with a satchel the same shape as his own.**

### 53 — What The District Owes
**Day 56. The twenty-sixth, Saturday.** *Turn. The door opens.*
The extract arrives with a note in Perrin Osse's square hand. **The district produced it because the applicant asked properly and the ground went out from under the other answer**, which was *a body holding no charter*, and there is a charter, and **nobody decided anything.** Six columns, ninety rows, no headings, and it is the district's own certified copy and it cannot be recalled. With it is a second document nobody asked for: **one page of a district register of authorisations, certified as a true copy of the page and not of the log**, for item **N-2214/K**, the only item the yard has ever had a number for.
*Resistance:* Dunmar's rule — do not enter a number you cannot source — collides with the fact that the page is a *transcript*, so the thing in the authorising column is a copy of a hand and not a hand.
*Change:* they enter the page as what it is, and the entry says *the sixth column is headed* ***authorised by*** *and is filled in a hand, and this building cannot say whose hand it is, and the district has certified the form of the page and not the truth of the column.*
*Ends on:* the page is read aloud, and the date on the row is seven months before the shaft, and the branch on the row is the Weir Row branch, and **the log has no column for what the cut was for.**

### 54 — Sunday
**Day 57. The twenty-seventh, Sunday.** *Recovery pressure, then the lock.*
Almost nothing can be done and the chapter is about that. The yard has been running on somebody else's paperwork for four days and a Sunday stops it dead. Marek reads the page eleven times and gets nowhere, and **Gael Senn reads it and finds the thing nobody asked her to find**, which is that the authorising entries on the two pages Perrin Osse sent are in the *same hand as each other* and that a clerk filling a column from a table is not the same as a person writing a name, and that **the register is a transcript and the original is the thing nobody produces.** Which is Volume 01's answer, one book along, and nobody is satisfied.
*Resistance:* Marek's own impatience, and his instinct to do something with a piece of paper.
*Change:* the day's second job is done instead. **The leak is measured at the seventh hour, twice, with two rules: five-eighths of an inch, up from half an inch and a sixteenth, pump on and continuous. Still not mended. Still cause not established. Still into the housing and not into the pit.**
*Ends on:* a Crown form arrives with the Sunday post, and it is not addressed to the cooperative. **It is addressed to the Salt March Dryfoot Trust, of Cray, and it asks the trust for any record in its possession touching the node — because the trust is a named party to the seed annex, and the annex is condition four of a charter made nine days ago.**

### 55 — A Shilling A Line
**Day 58. The twenty-eighth, Monday.** *The surface answer's first half, as an object.*
**Vesta Lund** comes up the March road, forty-four, a seed index and a good coat and a straight manner. **She offers a shilling a line for every line of the seed house's ledger that names a variety, and the March's cart will carry the fen's material north at her cost.** Her argument is sincere and it is correct: a landrace in one book in one fen is one flood from gone, and an index is a public good.
*Resistance:* the fen's own arithmetic, and then Tova Reed, who is the only person in the room who knows what being indexed costs, because **the March has been indexed twice and both times it was called a service, and the second time took four named lines out of the March's own book.** She refuses alone, in her own hand, and gives the reason, and Marek does not argue with it and does not get to argue with it.
*Change:* the refusal is entered and Lund leaves her printed **Crown seed-route map of the Tarrow fen**, form **S/4**, dated nine years ago, for the seed house to check its varieties against — and **it does not show the Weir Row branch**, and Tova marks the place where it should be and writes *no line here* in her own hand and no longer than that.
*Ends on:* **Lund says she did not know the branch was there, that the map is not hers, and that she has been reading maps like this one since she was twenty-one.**

### 56 — Unmapped Root Line, Maintained In Schedule
**Day 59. the twenty-ninth, Tuesday.** *The volume's structural discovery, and it is a form.*
Two maps laid over each other on the seed-house table: **the low quarter's own lime-and-stick survey, made on the nineteenth and twentieth of a month at the low quarter's own hand and at the low quarter's own cost, which is the only survey of that ground anybody has ever had** — and the Crown sheet. The branch is on one and not the other. And the reason is not an error of survey: **the Crown sheet's legend carries a mark for *unmapped root line, maintained in schedule*, and the Weir Row branch has it, and the district's own schedule has three executions stamped against it.** So a branch can be worked, maintained, scheduled and cut three times and not be on the public map, **because it has no local holder, and the map's convention is that a line with no local holder is Crown business and is drawn differently.**
*Resistance:* the temptation to call that concealment. Marek does not, out loud, and Dunmar refuses to enter the word *concealment* in the book and makes him say why.
*Change:* the answer is that the practice is not a lie but a **category**, and a category can be read, and it has been read, and the cooperative now has a Crown form in its own book that says a thing it did not know nine days ago. And a Crown form can be quoted in a hearing, **and the officer who wrote it in Volume 01 is the officer who will read it in Volume 09.**
*Ends on:* Tova's own line, entered in her own hand against her trust's interest for the seventh time: *I have been selling seed to a market that prices a line by whether a Crown map knows about it, and I have done it for nine years and I did not once ask the map.*

### 57 — A Bead That Answers For The Seed
**Day 60. the thirtieth, Wednesday.** *Setup for the climax, and the batch's panel, and the stage change.*
**Coll Renner**, licensed, of the Tarrow district, forty-one, nine men and a yard at Fennmere, calls at the gate on other business and stays. He has read everything the fen has published and he wants the method in writing, and he offers Marek a salaried post. **He carries a demonstration: a copper-collared living bead cut from a grafted seed, which laid against a wetted seed lot makes the lot report where it was grown, in four seconds, without a ledger and without a clerk.** It works. **It is technically perfect.**
*Resistance:* Tova, who is the only person in the room who knows what a graft is, and who asks for one thing before she will look at it again — to see it work on a line she knows is wrong. **It does. It is perfect. It is wrong.** And a graft keeps the thing that was put in and loses the thing that was there, and the losing is not a fault, it is what the word means.
*The panel:* Renner opens the instrument at the copper seam, with the use-log line written first, and the contact tested, and reads the three lines, which are the instrument's and not anybody's. **They are not a reason and no document quotes them.**
*The stage change:* the second demonstration, on a flowerpot of the March's rye, on a trestle in the yard, **with Renner's clip and Renner's license and Lissa Vale's use-log line and Gael Senn as witness.** Marek does the hands. The graft takes. **Marek gains the first skin rootmark, and it is a dark branching line along his forearm, and the man who made the graft writes the record down in his own book in his own hand, and the record is that it happened and the hour and who was standing there.**
*Ends on:* **the marker in the seed does not come off in a season, and Renner says so, and says he cannot stop it, and says it is not a badge and it is a bill.**

### 58 — The Second Wording
**Day 61. the first of next month, Thursday.** *Pressure, and the volume's clock made explicit.*
**The first monthly examination under the charter.** Gael Senn goes down the ladder with both rules, because she does not trust a number one man has timed. **The leak: five-eighths of an inch, twice measured, with two rules, at the seventh hour, pump on.** The **ninth logging of the pulse: eleven in the quarter hour, two runs of four and one of three, felt at the rail and not at the coping stone, cause not established, and nothing written underneath it by her decision and with her name on it.** The **return line records a thing that did not happen** — no compost, nothing available to put in, £4 5 short of what the same sixteen houses raised three weeks ago — **in the second wording, in district charter law, for the first time in the history of this fen**, and the first use of it is against the cooperative, which is what a charter condition is for.
*The refusal:* **Marek declines to have the full-draw load test applied, and this time the reason is not Volume 01's reason.** For the first time in a season there is a charter to hang a test on and a licensed man in the yard, and the standing pressure log is a charter condition, and an applied full-draw figure on the first examination morning would be a number in the district's hands for the whole season. **He would rather have a number and not have it, and the man who wrote the book against himself declines to produce the one figure that would help him, because he has learned that figures travel.**
*Gael Senn's answer:* the return line is for a thing that did not happen, and this is a thing that did not happen, and she will write it. **So the load test that was not performed goes on the first of a month, in the second wording, in a book the district may read, in the examiner's own hand and under her own name.**
*Ends on:* the form that comes back with the examination — the season-end renewal, and the box on it, and the two words in the box, and **what happens to a conditional charter that is not answered.**

### 59 — The Column With A Hand In It
**Days 62–64. the second, the third, the fourth.** *The batch's climax and its cost.*
The reader arrives, because the March has answered the district's demand and the March has asked the fen what it has, and **a copy of everything went to Cray on the ninth of a month whether or not it helps, and condition four of a charter says the copy goes and the annex says the copy goes, and nobody in this yard can tell you what a counting house nine hundred miles away does with a book once it has it.** The reader is not a reader in the root sense. **She is a seed steward of about fifty with thirty years of crop histories in her head, and she is Lissa Vale's opposite, and she is the best thing that happens to this batch.**
*What she can do:* identify a hand from thirty years of names, and say that a hand is not a name and a name is not a man and a man is not a decision.
*The scene:* she reads the sixth column of the certified page. **It is Aldren Vale's hand.** The date on the row is seven months before the shaft. The branch is on no current map. The register is a transcript and the original is the thing nobody produces. **The fieldbook's own impossible date is one year and eight months after the disappearance. Two dates, one hand, twenty-seven months apart.**
*Resistance:* Lissa refuses to be asked the only question anybody in the yard wants to ask, and says why, and the reason is about what a name in a book costs this building, and not about a book. **Nobody asks her twice. Nobody numbers it. It is not Sera Quill's refusal and it is not Lissa's fourth.**
*Change:* the batch's promise is paid in the volume's own currency — **the fen now has a document proving the work was authorised, and no document at all saying by whom it was authorised, because the thing that says by whom it was authorised is the fieldbook, and the fieldbook is on a table in a seed house and may not be opened in an officer's room.** And the volume's question is on the page: **who altered the maps and the seed records enough to make a bridge between this fen and the dry Salt March legal.**
*Ends on:* the reader's last act, which is the batch's cost paid in the right currency — **she asks to see the seed house's variety ledger, and she reads it, and she says out loud the four lines the March's second index took out of the March's own book, and Lissa Vale writes them down in her own hand, in the fen's book, with the hour, and does not say whether they are the same four lines.**

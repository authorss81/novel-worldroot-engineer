# Chapter 58

## The Second Wording

The first of the month came up gray and stayed gray, and the wind came round to the north-east about the fourth hour and did not find the fen, and at about the seventh hour a woman of twenty-three went down a ladder at the six o'clock corner of a sunwell housing with two rules in her coat because she does not trust a number one man has timed.

The pump was on. It had been on since the sixteenth of a month, except for two hours on the nineteenth while the channel was rodded out, and it was on now and the head was at an idle and had been at an idle since the fifth hour.

"Five-eighths," said Gael Senn. "Same as the twenty-seventh. Same as the twenty-third. Half an inch and a sixteenth on the fourteenth, at the seventh hour, in daylight, pump on. Five-eighths at the seventh hour of the first, in daylight, pump on. I have got both rules and I measured it twice and I want it in the book that I measured it twice and I want it in the book that the second rule was the one that read the smaller figure, because it always is on this corner, and I have stopped pretending that the two rules are the same instrument."

She came up the ladder and wrote it before she came off it, sitting on the top step with the book on her knee in the cold, which she does.

The pit had four feet of iron-colored water in it and it was not up a quarter of an inch, and she wrote that down in those words, and she has written it in those words since the fifteenth of a month when a different clerk had written the same pit as *the pit is dry*, and there is a line in the certified copy at Fennmere that a district officer read and said was the work of somebody in a hurry, and that is in the file and cannot be got out of it.

Then she did the beat, because that is in the examination and nobody had ever written it in a way that let anybody skip it.

"Eleven in the quarter hour," she said, from the rail this time, and not from the coping stone, and she said which one she was standing at because she has said it in every entry since the eighth day of a month. "Two runs of four and one of three, and the last three together, and the two fours about two minutes apart. Felt at the rail and not at the coping stone, which is the other way round from the twenty-first of a month. I have not seen it and I have not named it and I am not going to. Cause not established. Licensed person present: none."

"And underneath it," said Marek, from the top of the bank, where he had been for twenty minutes doing nothing at all on purpose.

"Nothing underneath it," said Gael Senn. "That is my decision and it is in the book with my name on the decision and not the thing's, and I have made it nine times now and I intend to make it the tenth, and if a district officer ever reads this page and asks me why there is nothing underneath it I am going to say: because a woman who writes *seepage* under a thing she has not seen writes about herself."

---

The log went to the sub-office at about the eleventh hour with two sheets on it, and the two sheets were the return line and the season-end form, and the man who went up the road with them was not a district courier and was Coll Renner of Fennmere, who was going that way anyway and who had not been asked.

He read the corner off Gael Senn's own page at the well house before he took the packet, standing on the bank, without touching the book, and said "five-eighths" and nothing else, and then he looked at the load test line and looked at Marek across about nine feet of wet yard for about four seconds, and then he took the packet and went.

"He read it and he did not say anything and he is going to know about that line by Thursday," said Anneke Roos, "and so are we, and I want that written down, because in three years the only thing that will be able to explain why there is no figure in that book is that a man stood on a bank and read it and did not say anything, and nobody in this yard will remember his face in a room."

And the second of the two sheets is a Crown form with a number in the corner that Lissa Vale read out loud because somebody in the room had to.

*Water-supply bylaw, sixth part. Schedule of maintenance, conditional charter, season-end. To the holder, to the examiner, and to the district.*

And on it was one box, and above the box were two words, and under the box was a sentence that said what happened to a conditional charter of which this part was not returned.

**is maintained**

*Where a charter is conditional and provision is made for examination at the term, and the examiner does not return this part, the charter lapses at the end of the term without notice, and the holder may apply in the ordinary way, and the ordinary way is a matter for the district and not for the holder.*

"Read the consequential sentence out," said Dunmar Fitch, "because I have read forms from this office for nineteen years and the consequential sentence is where the office is."

"**Is maintained**," said Anneke Roos. "One box. Two words. No third answer. And then: *Where a charter is conditional and provision is made for examination at the term, and the examiner does not return this part, the charter lapses at the end of the term without notice, and the holder may apply in the ordinary way, and the ordinary way is a matter for the district and not for the holder.*"

"That is a form with no middle in it," said Dunmar Fitch. "That is a form that requires an answer and gives a person two, and one of the two is a statement that nothing needs doing and the other one is a statement that the district is going to take the node. There is no box that says *I have looked and I do not know*, and the fifth sheet we argued for in nine minutes is a box that says *I have looked and here is what I found*, and those are not the same box, and the second one is worth more, and it is now the only instrument this holding owns that can say *I looked and I could not find a number* in a room where somebody wants a number."

"The second wording," said Lissa Vale, from the door.

"The second wording," said Dunmar Fitch. "Where a thing required by the charter did not happen, the entry records that it did not happen, in the same book, on the same page, in the same hand, with the hour. Gael Senn wrote that. She was twenty-three and she had never held an office in her life and she wrote it at a table in a seed house, and a hearing officer put it into district charter law in nine minutes because nobody in the room could think of a reason not to, and it is now the only thing about this node that can be used in either direction."

"Then the box is not the difficulty," said Marek. "The box is the easiest thing on that sheet and it is the only thing on it I can answer today. Tell me what the term is."

"The term is the season," said Dunmar Fitch, "and the charter does not date the season, and that is the answer and it took me an hour to find it in four sheets of district paper. Clause one says *for the season*. There is no other date anywhere on the instrument. So the district fixes it, and this form is the fixing, and this form says the last day of a month without saying which month, and the only way to find out which month is to look at what else is on the form."

"And what is on the form."

"*Term closes with the closing of the sowing.* In the same hand as the box, at the foot, in the same size, as though it were a note." He turned the sheet round and put his finger under it. "The closing of the sowing in this district is the last day of the month after next. That is the thirtieth, and that is fifty-nine days from today, and this charter expires on the twenty-ninth of that month, and there is no appeal anywhere on this sheet and there is none anywhere in the four clauses, and I have looked twice."

Fifty-nine days.

Nobody spoke for a moment and then Marek laughed once, without any amusement in it at all, which is a thing he does.

"Nine days ago I thought it was a month," he said. "I have been walking about this yard for nine days with a date in my head that a form put there, and a form has just told me it is two months, and I want it written down that the number I have been carrying was not mine and was never mine and was put in a book by somebody else and read out by a man who could read."

"I want that said slightly differently," said Dunmar Fitch, "because it matters to me and it will matter to whoever reads this in a year. The number was not a date. It was a feeling. A feeling about an expiry is worth nothing and it cost this holding nothing, and it is going to cost this holding a great deal more in a fortnight's time if nobody writes down what is actually on the paper, and the paper is the only thing in this room that is a fact about the future."

"I want that said out loud in a yard," said Marek. "Not at a table. In a yard, in daylight, to the five people who come when a thing is being read out."

So they said it out loud in a yard, in daylight, at about the third hour of the afternoon, standing at the well house with the pump running, and it took about two minutes, and the five of them who came were the five of the sixteen households who come when a thing is being read out.

"A term that closes with the closing of the sowing," said the man of about fifty who had asked about the word *expiry* eight days before, "is not a date anybody can hold in their head."

"It is a date," said Dunmar Fitch. "It is the last day of the month after next and it is fifty-nine days from this morning and it is printed at the foot of a Crown form in the same hand as the box, and I am telling you the number because a man who says *not a date anybody can hold in their head* is right and is also about to be asked in a room in a year what he was told, and he will not be able to answer, and I have been the clerk of this body for nineteen years and I have watched that happen four times and I have decided that this time there is a number in it."

The man of about seventy put his hand flat on the well house door afterward, and said: "So it is the last year of the year we have a charter, and I have been walking about for three days as though a man had come and told me, and nobody did."

"A man did not," said Marek. "A form did, and I want that on the page with your initial on it, and I want it in your words and not mine, because my words this afternoon have been wrong twice."

It went in the book at the fourth hour with the hour on it, and the man of about seventy initialled it because he said he wanted his initial on that sentence in case anybody in a room in a year asked him what he had been told.

"I am the only person who is allowed to answer it," said Gael Senn. "My name is second on the certificate of the twentieth and it is in the fifth sheet and I have been the examiner of this node since the twenty-first of this month and before that since the twenty-first two months back, ten days early, because somebody had to be second and I was the only person in this yard who would do it, and I have been second ever since and I have not once wanted it to be somebody else."

"Then it is yours and it is today," said Marek.

"It is mine and it is today," said Gael Senn.

She wrote **is maintained** in the box at about the second hour of the afternoon, in the same hand and with the same pen she uses for the log, and signed it, and the first line under it read:

*I have examined the wound at the six o'clock corner in daylight at the seventh hour with two rules and the pump on the idle. I have recorded the return line for this month. I have not applied a full-draw load test to this node and I have entered below why, in the second wording, on this page, in this book, and it is entered here for the district and not for the yard.*

And underneath, in her own hand, on the form, and then on the same day in the book on the same page with the hour, so that the two could not come apart:

*Full-draw load test, return main and head: not performed. Requires a licensed engineer, a charter to hang the test on, and a stated load. A licensed engineer crossed this yard at about the eleventh hour this morning on his own way to Fennmere and read this morning's number off the examiner's own page without being asked and without touching anything, and he will not be in this yard tomorrow. There is a charter to hang the test on and there was none on the twentieth. The reason this was not done today is not that anything was missing.*

*Mr Vale asked me not to do it. His reason, in his own words at this table at the first hour, and which I am entering as his reason and not as mine, is as follows.*

*He says: the standing pressure log is a condition of this charter from the twenty-first of a month, and an applied full-draw figure entered in it on the first examination morning will be in the district's hands for the whole of a season, and the district has reserved the right to reopen this file on any new report, and this is a charter that ends in twelve days.*

*He says: he would rather have the figure and not have it, and that a man who was in the yard at the eleventh hour this morning is not an engineer in the yard and a man who can be had for four miles is not an engineer in the yard unless somebody goes and gets him, and he is not going to send a form to a licensed man in order to make a number that he has decided the district should not have this season.*

*He says: he wrote the book against himself, and he wrote it so that it could be used, and he has spent a season being beaten with it, and he is not going to be the man who hands the district the one number that would help him because he has learned that a number travels and does not come back.*

*I am the examiner and this is my return line and a return line records a thing that did not happen, and this is a thing that did not happen, and it is recorded here, and if the district prefers a figure I am sorry and there is no figure, and the second wording is now in district charter law and it works in both directions and I have been given it by a hearing officer who argued for it in nine minutes because a book that can record a thing that did not happen is worth more than a book that cannot.*

Marek read it twice and did not ask her to change a word, and she read it back before he signed it, which she does, and it went in the book and into the return line in the same hour and in the same hand.

---

The compost is the other thing that happened on the first of a month and it took about four minutes and nobody argued about it, because there was nothing to argue about.

Nothing went into the return main. There was nothing available to put in it. The specification is four hundred hundredweight a month and what this holding put in on the fourteenth of a month was twenty-one hundredweight and a quarter, out of a sum of four pounds five raised from sixteen households, at four shillings a hundredweight; a hundredweight is worth about half a degree on a bed; a soil iron cannot read a tenth of a degree; and Tova Reed entered the hundredweight and not the degree, for the fourth time, and has said she would rather have a number she can stand behind than a number she cannot.

The bed is at three and a half degrees. It stood at three and a half degrees on the first, on the fourteenth and on the twentieth, and it is at three and a half degrees this morning. The March's tin has been in the press under the ledger since the nineteenth and it is forty-two days at risk on the first of this month, and the seed house has undertaken nothing about it and the trust has not asked.

Ilsa Kern came out to the north annex at about the fifth hour and counted the Longneck for the fourth time this month, and then went up to the top of the field and stood over the four willow sets, which are alive, which are the only thing in this holding that has cost nothing, and which are the anchor of a licensed act whose return line is three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight short and has been short since the day it was written down in a licensed engineer's own hand.

"Four sets," she said. "Green as anything in this yard. And the thing they are holding up is being fed one part in twenty of what it wants, and it will be green in April and it will be white in August, and there is nobody standing anywhere who will be able to tell the difference between those two states from the top of this field until the middle of next August, and by then it is a different man's log."

"Yes," said Marek.

"I have said that to you twice and you have written it down twice and I am not going to say it a third time." She wiped her hands down her coat. "What I am going to say is that I cut that ditch in October on a Sunday for four sets of willow out of our own windbreak, and I told you at the time it would not be a windbreak there in a year, and I was right about the windbreak and I was right about the sets, and I have been right about both of those for seven months and I have not got anything else in me. Ask somebody else."

She went in. Marek wrote the figure in the book — four sets, alive, and the shortfall — and initialled it, and did not put the nine words under it, for the first time in eight notes, and the omission is on the page with his name against it and the reason is his and he gave it out loud: *The eighth note is a record and not an argument.*

---

The return line is four lines and it is the only thing in this holding that anybody outside it can now check.

*First of the month, seventh hour, to the ninth. Monthly examination under the fifth sheet of the charter of the twenty-first of a month. Examiner, G. Senn. Wound at the six o'clock corner, five-eighths of an inch, twice measured, two rules, in daylight, pump on and continuous.*

*Return line: nothing was put into the return main this month. None available to put in. Specification four hundred hundredweight a month. Last put in, twenty-one hundredweight and a quarter, on the fourteenth, by levy. To the body: nothing. Witnessed: none. Licensed person present: none.*

*Full-draw load test: not performed. Reason entered above and on the returned part.*

*Beat: eleven in the quarter hour, cause not established, nothing written underneath it by the examiner's decision, with her name on the decision and not the thing's.*

"To the body: nothing," said Dunmar Fitch, from the end of the table, writing. "That is the second time this season that line has said the word nothing in a book that a district may read, and the first time it did it was a certificate in an office in Fennmere and it was mine, and I put the words in and I did not sign them, and I have thought about it every day since. It is the same line. It is the same nothing. It has a different clerk."

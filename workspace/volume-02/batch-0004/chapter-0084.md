# Chapter 84

## The Expiry

The charter did not die badly, and that is the thing nobody in this yard has been able to say out loud for a season, and it was said in the yard on the twenty-ninth, in daylight, in about four sentences, and then the yard voted.

It had been answered. The season-end form went to the examiner on the first of every month of the term, and the examiner returned it every time in the second wording, and the second wording says *nothing found* and not *all well*, and on the first of this month she wrote underneath an order for a full-draw load test that the load test was not performed, and entered the reason in her own hand, and the district clerk read it back and did not stop her. Every box that could be filled had been filled by the person the instrument names, on the first of the month, in daylight, without a rehearsal.

And the term closed with the closing of the sowing, and the closing of the sowing in this district is the last day of this month, and the term is fixed by the season-end form at its foot in the same hand as the box, as though it were a note, and the charter lapses at the end of the term without notice, and the holder may apply in the ordinary way, and the ordinary way is a matter for the district and not for the holder, and there is no appeal anywhere in the four clauses and there never was.

On the twenty-ninth of the month after next, at midnight, Lantern Reach Work Cooperative was a body with no charter, and no officer of the district had obstructed anything, and no officer of the district had to be there, and nothing anybody did that day could have kept it alive, and nothing anybody did that day had killed it either.

Dunmar Fitch read the whole of that into the book in the yard at about the ninth hour, standing on the well house step, in daylight, with the book shut under his hands, and then he read the list of what had stopped being anybody's business as nine items, because Lissa Vale had made him, and had said why: a person putting a hand up in this yard today is putting it up against a thing and not against a paragraph, and there are nine things and one of them is blank.

The five sheet was open on a chair, because the three conditions in clause three are printed on it, and it has not been off that chair since the ninth of a month two months back.

One. The open book, open to any member, any stranger and any clerk, without notice. Two. The monthly examination at the seventh hour on the first of every month. Three. The monthly report of root changes, to the sub-office and to Cray. Four. The standing pressure log, with the rule on the inside cover that an entry is made at the hour it describes and that the lateness is part of the entry. Five. The use log. Six. The seed annex, and the fourth term, and the copy of everything to Cray whether or not it helps. Seven. Four hundred hundredweight of compost a month into the return main at four shillings, or the best this holding can do, and the shortfall stated. Eight. The return main to specification. Nine. The third of the three conditions in clause three.

And then the clerk found the thing, and it is the whole of the morning.

"No," said Dunmar Fitch. "Five. The use log was never on the instrument. It was voted in this room on the tenth of a month three months back as the cooperative's own obligation and it has been kept ever since as if it were a charter condition, and it is not one, and I have checked the fifth sheet and the four clauses twice this morning in front of two people and there is no use log on either of them."

Nobody in the yard had known that. Lissa Vale knew, and had not said, and when Anneke Roos looked at her she said: "I did not say it because the day I say it, somebody in this yard finds a reason to stop keeping it, and I am not going to be the woman who finds somebody a reason."

And eight was not put to the body at all, and Marek Vale said why, standing in the yard with the fifth sheet in his own hand.

"A specification is a document of the district's, and this body has no charter and cannot undertake to one, and if we vote for item eight the fen's own book will have a promise in it that the fen could not keep and could not have made, and that is the one thing this book has never had in it. So eight is not a motion. Eight is a fact and it is entered as a fact: twenty-two rods of eight inch, and nobody will ever come and look at it again, and that is now correct, and it is not a defeat, and I am not going to have a motion taken about it in a yard."

So the body voted on seven items, and item nine was not voted on either, and it stays a blank, and the clerk entered that the third of the three conditions in clause three was not described before the term ended and would be described at the first examination held after the term ended, and that the term ended at midnight on the twenty-ninth, and that is the last entry this holding can make about it.

Seven hands. Four for. Three against.

Tova Reed voted for, and said in one sentence that she has been sending a copy of this holding's book to a counting house nine hundred miles off with nothing behind it except a signature, and that it has gone out every time, and that she is not going to be the one who stops it.

Lissa Vale voted for and did not explain, and it is the first time in a season she has not, and nobody asked her to, and the clerk entered that the rule of the twentieth of last month does not require a reason. Anneke Roos voted for and said that a body which cannot consent to the removal of its own record might as well keep it, and that clause two on the fourth sheet has been protecting them from the district and from themselves since the fourth of a month three months back. Dunmar Fitch voted for and said it was the only item on the list he could be responsible for, because it was the only one that was a sentence.

And the three against.

Marek Vale was one of them, and he put his own name against his own vote, the second time in a season, and he gave the engineer's reason and nobody in the yard thought for a moment that it was the reason of a man who had lost an argument.

"The book was worth the trouble because somebody was going to make us produce it," he said. "That is all. I have sat at this table since the fourth of a month three months back and watched six people put things in a book they did not want in it, and every one of them did it because a district could send for it, and a thing you must produce is a thing you cannot quietly leave out, and that is the whole of why it has been worth anything. There is nobody going to make us produce it now. There is not one person in this district who can.

"So the only reason left is that we have got used to it. And I would rather be found out in a year than have it found out in five, and I am putting my name against this and I would like Lissa to read it back, because she is the reader."

Lissa Vale read it back, and she did not soften a word of it either, and she voted against, and there it was: the reader of the book, against keeping the book.

Jonas Byre was the second, and his objection was not about the book, it was about the sixth item, and it was the best objection in the yard, and it was against the trust, and Tova Reed heard it and did not answer it.

"Item six puts every entry this holding makes into another body's hands for the next eleven years," he said, "and there is nothing behind it now. There was a charter behind it. There is not a charter, and there is not a box on the far end of it that can be filled in, and the woman who wrote that fourth term is standing in this yard and has been the other end of a return for eleven years, and I am not saying a word against her. I am saying that in nine years she will be somebody else, or she will be at Cray, or she will be somewhere a copy of this book goes on going to because nobody ever wrote down that it could stop. And we will have sent it. And there is no form."

The man of about fifty was the third, and his reason was the worst one, and he gave it standing at the back with his hands behind him.

"I have wanted a person to come to this yard since the fourth of a month three months back, and I have had two, and both came because of the book, and I am not ungrateful for that and I am voting against it. A body that is proud of being honest has stopped asking the question. Honesty is not a thing you can have on purpose. It is what is left when you have been frightened into it, and the fright was a district clerk and a form, and the fright goes on the twenty-ninth at midnight. I have read a printed index for two months with four hundred names in it that does not name this fen, and in a year there will be a printer in four counties who will say the honest fen is the one everybody knows, and how a body is known is not how it is honest, it is how big it is. I would rather this fen was small and did not know, for one more season, whether it can be. That is why I am against, and it is not a good reason, and I would like it in the book as a bad reason with my name on it and not argued with."

Four for and three against, and the motion carried, and the clerk entered the count and the hour and each name against its hand, and entered that the five households who came to the gate and the woman with the shilling and the man of about seventy, who was at his sister's and had not been asked to come, did not vote in either direction, and that a book anybody may read has to show who decided and who only heard.

Underneath the vote, dated the twenty-ninth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the sixteenth note under section nine of the charter schedule: nine items read as nine items; the use log found to have been never a charter condition; the return main to specification withdrawn from the motion as a thing this body cannot promise and entered as a fact of twenty-two rods of eight inch; seven items voted and four hands for and three against, and the engineer of record among the three and the reader of the book among the four; the third of the three conditions not described and not to be described before the first examination held after; the charter lapsing at midnight on a date nobody chose.

And under it, in the engineer's own hand, the line that has carried a rule since the seventh of last month: *this is a record and not an argument, and the sentence is off this note for that reason and the reason is here so that the chain cannot be seen to have dropped one.*

---

Ruben Ashe came up the fen road on the twenty-ninth at about the third hour of the afternoon, on the date this holding's own book named eleven weeks ago, alone, with a case and not a cart, and he gave Marek Vale the sheet at the gate before he came in, which is what Coll Renner does, and Marek understood that it was not imitation.

The sheet was half a page. Sixty pounds, in cash, on the day, for eleven items, and every one of them was a thing this holding had been doing for a season, and not one of them was a thing it had a charter to do. The eleven years of books. The printed record, and the copy that went to Cray and the copy that went to four counties. The open book, the standing pressure log, the use log, the schedule on the door. And an undertaking, on his letterhead, in one paragraph, that the body will thereafter keep no book, will hold no examination, will send no report, will print nothing further, will answer no officer, and will not thereafter describe the ground, the node, the branch, or any route from it.

"Sixty pounds," said Ruben Ashe. "Same as the twenty-fifth of a month two months back, and I am not playing you, and the figure is the figure I pay anybody and I have paid it thirty-one times. The books are worth that to a buyer and are worth nothing whatever to you, and I do not need you to believe that.

"What I am buying is the name and the address. A cooperative with no charter cannot be served with anything. A holding with a box at a post and a seal and a man who answers is a body an office can send a form to and get an answer out of. And in four years the district is going to make arrangements for the maintenance of a node in this district, and when it does it will be done with somebody, and the somebody has to be somebody a form can be served on. There are three candidates. One owes the district sixty pounds ten. One is a private charterholder at a pump house with a gauge below the split. And the third is a body with eleven years of books anybody may read and a printed record with its own confession on the front page."

He shut the case.

"I am not offering to maintain anything and I would like that read out," he said. "I have never maintained anything. I have bought thirty-one conditional charters in nine years and held each through its term and sold the conditions on, and eleven holdings have gone to nobody in that time and I bought four of them and did not save one of them. If you sell me this holding, the district will be keeping that node in about two years, by a Crown schedule, and you will be out of it, and nobody will have your name on anything. That is the arithmetic and it is correct and you may check every figure of it in a book, and I would rather you checked it than took my word, because I am about to ask you for something and I would like the asking to be honest."

Nobody said anything, and the pump ran, and the lamp above the seed house door was burning on its living bulb.

"Well," said the man of about fifty, to nobody in particular, and Dunmar Fitch did not enter it, and it was not in the book that night or any night. "We are not on anything now. There is nowhere that says what we are, and there is nothing over there that says what they are, and so we can be with them without it being a thing that is on a form anywhere. And I have wanted that for eleven weeks and I did not know till this afternoon that it was a thing I wanted."

And then Tova Reed said the professional version of it, standing, with her case shut and her key on her belt, and she did not know that she had said the sentence of the whole road between two places, and she said it in about fourteen seconds.

"A body with no charter can be a party to nothing," she said. "That is what a charter is. It is the only thing it is. And a settlement with no charter cannot be a party to anything either, for the same reason and for worse ones, and it has eleven households and no holding number and a hole in a Crown form where its ground should go. So the two ends of this route are the two ends in this district that can be party to each other at all, and there is no form that says so and nobody is going to write one, and that is the only standing this holding has left, and I am not selling it to a man with a case and a sheet of his own letterhead in my own yard for sixty pounds that will not buy two hundredweight."

Ruben Ashe took the sheet back, folded it once, and put it in his coat.

"That is a no," he said, "and I said it would be a no, and I have a date in this district again in a year and I will write first, and there is nothing in that to be insulted by."

At the gate he said the thing that the fen entered, because it is the engineer's trade to enter a man's words about a road.

"Do not have anybody tell me you are right," he said. "You are right. And it is a good road and it is on a door and I have never seen a road in this district anybody could copy. That is the whole of what I came to say, and you may write it down, and I would rather the writing of it was good."

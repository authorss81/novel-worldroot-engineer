## What this batch is for

**Movement 4 builds the first public seed-route bridge between a seed bank and a dry Salt March settlement. It is crew work on a public schedule, with a return path and two ends, named in a book anybody may copy, and its capacity is named in the trade's own words. It is a seed-route bridge and nothing above that.** The bridge carries a convoy written down crate by crate, and a settlement with no charter at the far end is not asked to sign anything, and its own refusal is entered without its terms.

**Three structural things fall in this batch and the writer must know all three of them before planning a single scene:**

1. **The charter expires inside this batch, and it is the batch's midpoint.** The term closes with the closing of the sowing, which is the last day of the month after next, and the charter expires on the twenty-ninth of that month — **day 119, which is Chapter 84.** From the next morning this holding is a body with no charter, and every obligation it has kept for a season — the open book, the monthly examination, the monthly report, the standing pressure log, the use log, the seed annex — stops being a condition and does not stop being true. **The batch's midpoint turn is a Crown instrument expiring correctly on a date nobody chose, and the fen keeping every one of those things by choice, in public, in a vote, and it is the first thing the fen has ever done that nobody required.** Nothing in the batch may treat the expiry as a defeat and nothing may treat it as a release.
2. **The far end of the bridge is Wrenlow: eleven households, ground the district says it maintains and does not, kept by a woman who gave a name and no house in the letter of the fourteenth of last month, and who walked eleven days to ask for a copy of this holding's eleven years at a shilling.** **Her name is Wenna Roke.** The place has not been named in any book until now and this batch names it once and then uses it. **It is not the Thornwild settlement** — that one is in this district under the south embankment windbreak, is still not named, and its refusal's terms are still not held — **and no chapter may confuse the two or let a person from Wrenlow stand in the yard at Weir Row.**
3. **A seed-route bridge is a route with a return, and the return is what this fen does not have.** The standing arithmetic is one hundredweight at four shillings against four hundred a month specified and three hundred and ninety-nine short, seventy-nine pounds sixteen. The compost line has read *nothing put in, none available* for two months running. **A bridge with no return is a road with crates on it**, and the writer must make that sentence somebody's and must make it cost, and it must not be resolved by a gift, a bequest, a trust payment or a district concession.

**The batch must contain its own midpoint turn and its own climax.** The midpoint is the expiry. The climax is the first convoy arriving, and it does not go well, and it is still right.

## The cards, and what each one has to change

### 77 — Fifteen Days
**Days 105–106, the fifteenth and the sixteenth.** *The batch opens on the date a licensed man promised, and on a question the fen cannot answer.*
A letter went down the fen road on the fourteenth with a copy and a shilling in it, and the copy of the fen's eleven years was set in Fennmere and there is no answer and there is not going to be one for a while. **Coll Renner is the only man in this district who uses the trade's own words for what is being proposed, and he is the one who names the capacity**, in a yard, without being asked and before anybody has explained it to him: *a local conduit takes water from one place to another place. A seed-route bridge takes seed from one keeper to another keeper, and it has two ends and a return, and the return is the part nobody ever wants to pay for.* **The capacity is named for the load it carries and not for the distance.** Change: the fen has a bridge in front of it before it has agreed to build anything, and the first thing the bridge asks for is the one thing the fen has never been able to buy.
*Ends on:* the Trust's question from the twelfth of last month, which is whether this holding will hold the trust's seed for a season, and the arithmetic of holding somebody else's seed on a bed at three and a half degrees with no compost behind it.

### 78 — The Schedule
**Days 107–108, the seventeenth and the eighteenth.** *A public schedule with two ends and a return column, on a form that does not exist until somebody makes it.*
The bridge is scheduled in public, on the well house door, in a form of the fen's own, and the form has **two end columns and a return column**, and the return column is empty for four days and cannot be filled. Every entry is read aloud before it is initialled and the reader is a person, and **the first person to read a seed-route schedule aloud to a body of this kind is going to be objected to by a man who cannot pay for it**, and the objection has to be a real one. Change: **the fen learns that a bridge is a return with a road attached, and that the return is the compost line, and that a schedule with a blank in it is a schedule anybody may copy including the blank.**
*Ends on:* Jonas Byre, Ilsa Kern and the woman with the shilling, separately, all three for different reasons, and the man of about fifty, who does not object and says why, and the reason is the worst one.

### 79 — A Place The Map Does Not Have
**Days 109–110, the nineteenth and the twentieth.** *A Crown act discovers a settlement by asking a question about it, correctly.*
**The district writes to this holding to ask whether it has any holding, interest, correspondence or claim in or to a place the district cannot name on its own form** — because somebody wrote to this fen, and a letter is a record, and the district keeps records. It is a correct act, it is four lines, and it is how a Crown office learns that a line its own map says is maintained by nobody is being kept by somebody. **The counter-mark sent on the fourteenth of last month is the reason the district is asking at all**, and Tova Reed says so out loud and does not soften it: *I sent them a holder and they have gone looking for what the holder is holding.* Change: **the bridge's far end acquires a piece of paper it did not ask for, and the fen has to decide what to say about a place it has one letter from, and the answer cannot be a description of a person who walked eleven days.**
*Ends on:* a form on the well house door asking this holding to produce its own account of a place it has never surveyed, and the answer on the form of the eleventh of a month of Batch 0002, in the same words: **this holding has never been asked to, and it would not be a map if it were.**

### 80 — The Terms Of A Bridge
**Days 111–112, the twenty-first and the twenty-second.** *The terms, and the clause Tova Reed strikes out.*
A bridge has terms or it has a habit, and this one is written. **Marek drafts it as an engineer of record and it is a good draft and it is wrong**, and Tova Reed reads it and **strikes out the clause that requires the far end to keep a copy of the record and to answer on it**, because a settlement with no charter cannot be made a party to a document, and requiring a copy of the record makes it a party, and the moment it is a party it can be refused and then it is the only kind of thing in this arrangement that can be refused, which is the only protection it has. **Marek objects, in his own register, for the engineer's reason, and she refuses him, and he is right about the risk and she is right about the standing, and neither of them can answer it for the other.** Change: the bridge goes out with **no enforceable term at the far end at all**, and the fen enters that fact against its own interest, in daylight, with the hour on it. The relationship milestone for this range is shared records and it is institutional: the ledger is one book in two countries, and neither of them keeps a private copy, and now the terms are one document and the far end holds none of it.
*Ends on:* **she gave a book of two hundred pages out of another body's money, entered the largest wrong of her career against that body's interest, refused a lease on her own record, asked him to be the man who said it out loud in a yard, and then struck out the only clause that would have protected her, and she is not thanked for any of it and must not be.**

### 81 — The Road
**Days 113–114, the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth.** *The March road is out at two places and the first is passable empty and not returnable loaded.*
What a crew does about a road is ordinary fen work and it must be written as ordinary fen work: ruts, a bad half mile, a mender of implements, two carts hired at a price, a levy put at four times what it was because the men now know there is money somewhere, and **a decision about whether the loaded carts go or the loaded carts do not.** Ivo Tarrant's four men do not come back and are not a rescue and are not brought back to dig anything. Marda Vole does not appear. The bypass is not built. **The bridge's first cost is money and it is the first money this holding has spent on itself in a season, and it is spent on getting somebody else's seed off this fen.** Change: the loaded carts do not go down the road, and the bridge's first real question becomes **what a crate is worth when it cannot be moved**, which is a seed question and not a cart question.
*Ends on:* Jonas Byre's objection, entered with his name, that the fen is paying to move a thing it has not got, over a road it does not own, to a place it has never seen, and he is not wrong.

### 82 — The First Crate

*The one Fieldbook panel for this batch may be spent here* — at the head of the new work, on the return, with a licensed man present, the use-log line written and read back first, the contact tested and the test written down first, three plain lines, in the instrument and not in a person, and quoted in no document. ### 83 — The Dry End
**Days 117–118, the twenty-seventh and the twenty-eighth.** *A settlement with no charter, and the fen does not go.*
**Marek does not go and Tova Reed does not go**, and the reason is Chapter 62's rule generalized and not softened: a holding that is owed a survey cannot also be the body that goes and collects it, and a steward who goes to the far end of a route she is party to cannot be a person who received anything there. **Ilsa Kern and Jonas Byre and the man of about fifty go, and the man of about seventy stays, and Lissa Vale goes nowhere because she is the reader and the reader reads.** They come back on the second day. **Wrenlow's terms: eleven households, ground the district says it maintains and does not, a woman who kept a dying line in eleven grounds and wrote a list and had no column for it, and a refusal of something.** The refusal is entered **as the fact of a refusal and not its terms, with a line under it in the engineer's hand saying the terms are not held by this body** — the Chapter 35 pattern, at the other end of a route the fen built, eleven weeks later, and this time the fen built the road. Change: **the far end holds no copy of the terms, has signed nothing, has been asked to sign nothing, and is on the schedule, and the fen has lost the only leverage it ever had on a settlement and gained the only thing it ever wanted, which is a person at the other end who was written about honestly in advance.**

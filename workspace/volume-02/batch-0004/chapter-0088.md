# Chapter 88

## Two Ends

The seventh was a Sunday and the eighth a Monday and the ninth a Tuesday, and three things came up the fen road in three days, and none of them was a person, and two of them were the fen's own, and the third was from the district water office and it is four paragraphs long.

On the Sunday, Lissa Vale read the schedule out in the yard at the tenth hour, which is not the first of the month and not the last and not the middle, and she did it because a carter at a gate had asked her for the number of the readings on the first of the month and she was not going to let anybody in this district ask the same question twice.

"Read it out on the first and on the middle and on the last," she had said on the second, "whether anybody comes or not, and the number goes in, and the day nobody comes the number goes up anyway."

"This is not one of those days," said Dunmar Fitch.

"No," said Lissa Vale. "It is not, and I am doing it, and I am entering that I did it and why, and the why is that a man with a chalk cart stood at this gate five days ago and asked me a question about a column, and there is no reason on this earth for that to be the last time anybody in this district asks me a question about a column, and I would rather be the woman who reads it out on a Sunday for nobody than the woman who was asked twice."

She read the whole of it. The title, the seven columns, the declared figure, and then the measured figure, which is under the declared figure, in her own hand, and the reason, which is in her own hand, and the route line, which says that the road holds at the first break and does not hold at the second. And the seventh column, which has a line in it, and the line is a sowing, and there is no name at the far end of the seventh column and there never will be.

One person came, and he was a man of about thirty with a dog, and he stood at the gate and did not come in, and he did not say who he was, and Dunmar Fitch entered that a person came and did not give a name and that the fen does not keep a record of people who do not give one, and that the number went up anyway.

On the Monday the closing entry was made, and it is the last entry of this season in this book, and Dunmar Fitch wrote it in the ordinary hand and read it back and a person read it back after him, and it is here entire.

*The route: a seed-route schedule, of the fen's own ruling, with no number on it, nailed on the well house door, read aloud on the first of the month and on the middle and on the last and on the seventh of this month, and the number of the readings of the seventh column kept under the form in the clerk's hand.*

*Capacity, declared, the twenty-fifth of last month: thirty crates a month out and thirty a month back. Capacity, measured, the twenty-sixth of the same month: six crates out and none back. Six is a fifth of thirty, and the smaller figure is on the same sheet, in the same hand, under the larger one, and no person has to go looking for it.*

*Twelve crates were scheduled on the fifth of this month and written down crate by crate. Six went. Six did not. The six that went were the six that were ready when the list was ruled. The reason is on the page and it is a date and not a decision, and one member of the body has entered the knowing and the knowing is not in capitals.*

*The terms of the route, six clauses, drafted by the engineer of record, clause two struck out by the steward of the trust against the trust's interest, are in this book's own keeping and are held by nobody at the far end. They were sent unasked for and they were returned. The fact of the return is entered and the two sentences the carrier was given are entered in his words with the hour.*

*A refusal was received at the second end on the twenty-eighth of last month, concerning the record and concerning a return. Both are entered as the fact of a refusal and not its terms, and the terms are not held by this body. A line in the engineer's hand under the first of them says so.*

*What came back down the path, entered on the third of this month, is a sowing of the March's dry line in this fen's ground, to be lifted in the autumn, weighed at two ends, signed at two ends, and returned to the counting house at Cray. It goes to Cray. It does not stop at the second end. The second end's column is empty and stays empty this season.*

*The charter lapsed at midnight on the twenty-ninth of last month, on a date nobody chose, with no appeal anywhere in the four clauses. The body has kept the open book, the monthly examination, the monthly report, the standing pressure log, the use log, the seed annex, the copy to Cray, and the attempt at four hundred hundredweight a month, by a vote of four hands to three on the twenty-ninth, in daylight, standing, with the book shut. The use log was found on the morning of that vote to have never been a condition of anything. The return main to specification was withdrawn from the motion as a thing this body cannot promise, and is entered as a fact: twenty-two rods of eight inch.*

*The third of the three conditions in clause three was not described at the first examination held after the term ended, because there was no officer to describe it, and there was no officer because there is no charter. It is untested and unowned and unnamed. It will be described at an examination to whoever holds the node on that day. No person in Tarrow Fen has guessed at it and none will.*

*And the notice of the second is on the well house door, on the left of the schedule, in four lines, correct.*

The Crown's answer came on the Tuesday, on the office's paper, and it was correct, and it took four days over four weeks, and it is four paragraphs and a signature by a clerk whose name was entered in the book with the hour.

*Water-supply bylaw, second part. Schedule check of a route. To the holder of a node at Lantern Reach, or to any body having correspondence with that holder. In answer to a return of the fourteenth of last month, to a form of the twenty-first of last month, and to a schedule received on the twenty-fifth of last month. Four points.*

*One. The seed-route sheet of this district is compiled for the use of seed holders and traders, states on its own face that it is not a survey, and is not amended on the statement of a holder. It is revised on a cycle. This office does not fix the cycle and cannot say what it is.*

*Two. This office enters in its own book that the body named above is a local holder of an unmapped root line for the purposes of a schedule check, and that the line is used for the supply of eleven households. The entry is in the district's book. It is not in the Crown's map layer. The applicant is not entitled to the map layer and has not asked for it.*

*Three. A route carrying seed over ground the district carries in schedule is a use of that ground. A use of ground the district carries in schedule is a class of use, and classes of use carry terms. Those terms are not settled by this office. They will be sent in the ordinary course, and when they are sent they will name a rate and a class of use, and a copy will go to the holder of the node and to any person who has written to this office about the ground.*

*Four. The schedule received is received. This office has no power to approve it, no power to stop it, and no duty to send any person to hear it read.*

*This letter asks for no fee and says nothing about whether a fee will be asked for.*

Nobody in that yard was pleased with it and nobody in that yard was angry with it either, and both of those were entered, and the second of them is the first time in a season that a Crown act has been received without anybody in this yard wanting to be a villain about it.

"Point three," said Anneke Roos, from the doorway. "Point three is the whole of what is coming and it is coming in about a month, and there is no form in this district for the class of thing it is going to describe, and when the terms come they will name a rate, and a rate is a number, and this holding owes seventy-nine pounds sixteen a month for compost and will be asked for a number on a road as well." She came further into the yard than she usually does. "I want the whole of this entered and I want the first paragraph of it read back twice, and then I want somebody to say out loud what point three is, because everybody in this yard has read it and nobody has said it."

"Point three," said Tova Reed, "is the answer to a question I asked on the fourteenth of last month without knowing that I was asking it. I told a district office in writing that this holding is a holder of a line on ground the district maintains. I have been afraid for eleven weeks that the answer would be a form. It is not a form. It is a class of use, and a class of use is a thing that can be taxed and refused and appealed, and it is a thing that exists, and it exists because I put a holder on a piece of paper and went up a road, and I would like it entered that I have said that in this yard, and that I am not going to be told I could not have known, because I could have known and I sent it anyway."

The clerk entered the four points entire, and the signature, and the hour, and the sentence about a class of use, and Lissa Vale read the whole of it back twice and did not put a single line of it into any other book, and Anneke Roos initialled it as an opinion.

---

Underneath the closing entry, dated the ninth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the seventeenth note under section nine of the charter schedule: a seed-route schedule of the fen's own ruling with no number on it, a declared capacity of thirty and a measured capacity of six, twelve crates written down and six carried and six not, terms with the second clause struck and returned, two refusals entered without their terms, a sowing entered in the seventh column that goes to Cray and not to the far end, a charter lapsed on a date nobody chose and seven practices kept by a vote of four to three, a notice of four lines nailed on the left of a door, a clerk's letter of four points in which a route over ground the district maintains is a class of use and terms are coming and a fee is not mentioned, a carter's question about a column, a Sunday reading with one person in the yard, and a woman on the low dry with a stone on a sheet of lining paper with forty-one words on it that she cannot read.

*This is worth nothing against a cut.*

And under it, the last line in the fen's own hand, which is the engineer's, and which is the whole of the road between two places and nothing more than the whole of it:

*A seed-route bridge carries a stated load on a stated day from one keeper to another keeper, and it has two ends and a return. First end, a body with no charter, at Lantern Reach, eleven miles of fen road and four bad miles of March road and forty yards of gone ground. Second end, eleven households, keeper not named, on the low dry, beyond a break that will take a load out and will not take one back. In the middle, one book, which anybody may ask to have read, and which both ends can read, and which neither end has signed, and which is a piece of paper with rules on it and no number on it and a measured capacity of six in the fen's own hand under a declared capacity of thirty.*

Nobody in that yard has ever said those four words as a name for what this holding is. A licensed man said them at a gate on the fifteenth of last month about a trade, and the title of the form carries one of them because a form is a form, and the fen has never once used them of itself, and it is not going to, and that is not modesty. Six crates is the whole of it. Anything larger would be a lie, and this holding has entered nine wrongs in eleven months and it has not got room in the same book for a tenth that a man in a yard could have avoided by being pleased with himself.

The lamp above the seed house table is burning on its living bulb, and the bulb has to be fed tonight and will be fed tomorrow. The well house door has a schedule on the right and a notice on the left, and the schedule is a piece of paper with rules on it and no number on it, and the measured figure is under the declared one in a hand anybody can read from the gate. And at the north end of the yard there is a bay with a roof of oiled sacking and two boards on the wall and fifty-eight days cut into both of them, and it is not full, and the number is still going up. And the compost line says nothing put in, none available, and the bed is at three and a half degrees, and the tin is under the ledger in the press, and a hundred and ten days at risk on the ninth, and the graft is being fed about a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed, and the starve is still not visible from the top of the field.

And the two ends of it are a body with no charter and a settlement with no charter, and there is a book in the middle of it that both of them can read and neither of them has signed.

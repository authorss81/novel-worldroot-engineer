## What this batch is for

**Movement 5 taxes the bridge and makes its use conditional, and the tax is not a villain's greed: it is a schedule, a rate, a class of use, and a clause. The coalition's refusal is to accept the arrangement as it stands, and the bridge is not closed and nothing is destroyed and no exemption is won. The concrete resolution is that the exchange survives as a local commons with public ledgers and a refusal clause — a real clause, in a real document, that a later volume can be built on.**

**Three structural things fall in this batch and the writer must know all three of them before planning a single scene:**

1. **The mechanism already exists and it was named on the last page of Batch 0004.** The district's letter of the ninth closed with: *a route carrying seed over ground the district carries in schedule is a use of that ground; a use is a class of use; classes of use carry terms; those terms are not settled by this office; they will be sent in the ordinary course and when they are sent they will name a rate and a class of use.* **The terms arriving is the first thing that happens and it must arrive on the district's own paper, in the district's own hand, correctly, and nobody in the district may lie, obstruct, or act corruptly.**
2. **The fen has no standing to refuse and has no standing to accept.** It is a body with no charter, with no box on any form, and with a schedule on a door that cannot be enforced by anybody. **The tax therefore cannot be resisted by refusing to pay, because a body that cannot be served cannot be sued, and the rate will be collected in the only way a rate on a place like this can be collected: somebody will stop a cart.** The batch's political problem is therefore not *shall we pay* and it is not *shall we refuse to pay*. It is **what a body with nothing can do to a rate it cannot refuse, and the answer has to be a document of its own.**
3. **The refusal clause is the volume's resolution and it is the fen's, not the Crown's.** The district's terms will contain a conditional-use clause that binds the *operator*, and the fen's discovery must be that a conditional use is only meaningful if somebody can refuse a *use*, and that nobody in the arrangement so far has power to refuse anything, because a settlement with no charter was deliberately left unexaminable in Ch 80 and a body with no charter is unnameable. **So the fen writes the refusal clause itself, in its own form, on its own door, and it is the one thing in the volume that cannot be taken back, because it is the only clause nobody has signed and everybody is bound by.** Nothing in the Crown's terms may be a refusal clause, and nothing in the Crown's terms may be defeated.

**The batch must contain its own midpoint turn and its own climax.** The midpoint is the rate, when the fen puts the Crown's figure against its own arithmetic and the arithmetic is not a defeat and is not a victory either. The climax is Chapters 93–96 and it is the first toll and the first refusal, and the refusal is made by Wrenlow against a use nobody had asked it about, and the terms of that refusal are not held by anybody.

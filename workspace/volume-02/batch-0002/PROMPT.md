## What this batch is for

**Movement 2 is the volume's political work and it is one argument: a repair that worked in a flood now has a price on it, and the price is not money. Three buyers came to the gate in Movement 1 and the cooperative refused all three. This batch is about what refusing costs, and about the fact that the volume's promises keep being paid in somebody else's currency.**

**The batch must contain its own midpoint turn and its own climax. The batch goal is a refusal; the midpoint is a public record that the refusal cannot un-write; the climax is a man who comes to the gate to help them and does.**

**The batch's three external pressures, in order, and they are not interchangeable:**

1. **The seed buyer's market does not stop because a seed house refuses it.** Vesta Lund's index prints again this winter. The refusal does not reach the nine hundred smallholders, and it never was going to, and **the batch must show a refusal that has no effect on anybody except the people who made it.** Somebody in this batch has to say that out loud and it will be Dunmar Fitch.
2. **The professional circular turns the provenance bead into a trade article.** Pike's circular of Volume 01 is on four hundred doors. A licensed contractor of the Tarrow district is inside that same professional body and has a device in a box. **The Assembly is not a villain and does not act in this batch — a trade article in a professional journal is not an act by an office, and a later writer must not let the two be confused.** A reader of that article must be able to buy a bead.
3. **Kellan Rusk is the first person outside this building to say aloud that the standing pressure log, the seed annex, and the fieldbook together are enough to prove Lantern Reach's own draw against itself — and he says it to help, and it is true, and nobody in the yard likes him less afterwards and everybody likes him less because of how he says it.**

## The cards, and what each one has to change

### 60 — The Market Does Not Notice
**Days 65–67.** *The refusal with no effect, and the batch's first cost.*
A market that has been refused does not stop. A printed index goes to a hundred and forty seed houses this winter and none of them will know a fen seed house said no to a shilling a line. **Dunmar Fitch puts it on the page in the form he uses: the refusal was entered, the sum not received, and the number of houses the refusal reached is one.** Tova Reed's own arithmetic gets worse, not better, because a refusal nobody hears is indistinguishable from a holding that has nothing. **Change:** the batch discovers that refusal is a thing this body can only do in a room with people in it, and that the room is eleven miles long and there is no way to hold it.
*Ends on:* the March's answer to the C/4 has to be written this week and the woman writing it is nine hundred miles away and does not have the volume's three acres in her.

### 61 — A Trade Article
**Days 68–69.** *The professional body without a villain.*
The provenance bead is described in a trade article — **a description of a result, not a device, and a set of figures for a lot of one variety, and no name and no licence number.** **The description is accurate. That is the problem and nobody in the fen can say what is wrong with it.** Coll Renner reads it in a yard and says four things, and one of them is that he did not write it and one of them is that he cannot stop it and one of them is that the article is technically correct and that a technically correct description of a device that makes a seed lie is the most dangerous thing anybody has ever printed about this holding. **Change:** the fen learns that a truth can be an attack, and that the only defence anybody has is a book, and that a book only works if somebody reads it.
*Ends on:* a reader of that article is a seed buyer in a town with no fen in it, and has written to a counting house in Cray asking whether the Ashcombe black oat is the one in the index.

### 62 — Nobody Is Going To Walk It
**Days 70–71.** *A refusal with teeth, and the standing of the man who makes it.*
**Corvin Slee is a man the fen damaged and who has not forgiven it and does not have to**, and the low quarter's own survey of the line of the standing iron is on paper in Fennmere and in nobody's hands, and the district has written for it because the district writes for everything. **Marek still has not walked the low quarter's own map. He is asked to, in front of the district's own man, and he says no, and the reason is his and it is the chapter: a holding that is owed a survey cannot also be the body that goes and collects it, because the collecting is the part that makes it theirs.** **Nobody may interpret the collie and nothing may be written underneath the report.** Change: the low quarter keeps its own map, on its own terms, and the fen is not the body that holds it — and that costs the fen the only leverage it has in the district on the one ground where forty-one houses are still drinking off a bed it killed.
*Ends on:* a district form arrives asking this holding to produce its own map of the low quarter, and the answer has to be that it has never been asked to, and that it would not be a map if it were.

### 63 — Kellan Rusk
**Days 72–74.** *The man who comes to help and does not.*
**Kellan Rusk is alive, employed under a monitored charter, and testifying, and he has a right to the district's monthly return and a condition that says the record is open.** He comes up the fen road and puts on the seed-house table three figures **nobody in this yard has put in one place**, and they are correct, and **the three of them together with the standing pressure log and the seed annex and the use log are enough to prove this holding's own draw against itself, which is the last thing any of them wants.** His monitored charter says he may not give a figure to a third party without the return, and he has the return, and he brings it, and **what he wants in exchange is not money and it is not a reduction: he wants to be asked at the next examination, in front of a district officer, to say the thing he said on a bench in the mud, with the figure in his hand.** He asks for it and it is minuted as asked for, the way it was minuted as asked for in Volume 01, and **the yard does not agree to it in the room.** Change: the figures enter the fen's book, and the fen is now holding its own conviction, and **nobody has yet decided what to do with it.**
*Ends on:* Kellan's line, which is the chapter, and which must be entered verbatim because it is the whole of Movement 2's argument: **you have built a book that anybody can read and you have put everything in it that is true about you, and a book like that is not evidence. It is an invitation.**

### 64 — The Order Of The Work
**Days 75–76.** *The method, refused, and what comes back instead.*
Coll Renner comes back at the month, with the form he described in advance, and it is **not a contract.** It is a **one-page schedule of works** — form **RW/3** — with a box for the licensed engineer, a box for the four conditions, and a line at the foot for the *order of the work*, and the line at the foot is blank and it is the only line on the sheet anybody wants. **Marek's hand goes to it and does not fill it in, and the reason is Chapter 57's, said again to a different man: a list of things that have been done is a list of things anybody can do, and eleven fen holdings cannot afford the compost.** What comes back instead is a **price list and a route**, unasked, and it is the first material anybody has put on that table in a season, and **Tova Reed makes him read the price list aloud and then refuses it, and the refusal is about the compost and not about the man.** Change: the fen learns that refusing an offer is not the same as having none, and that a contractor who is refused a method and leaves a route on the table is not a man who was beaten.
*Ends on:* Renner says the form will be back in a season with the line at the foot **filled in by somebody else**, and that is what he is paid for, and the yard does not know whether that is a threat or a market.

### 65 — The Seed Buyer Writes Back
**Days 77–78.** *The market answers, and the answer is worse than the offer.*
**The Ashcombe index goes to press a sheet that says, about one variety, that the source is disputed and that the printed name is not established**, and a seed house two hundred miles from here will read it and will not buy that line, and **the line is the March's, and the March's holding is the trust's, and the trust is a named party to a charter condition.** Tova Reed's decision to refuse the shilling has cost the March a sale. She enters that in her own hand and does not soften it, and **the seventh line against the trust's interest becomes the eighth, and it is the first one that says she was wrong about something and not about a number.** Resistance: the yard wants to call it an attack. It is not. It is a buyer doing arithmetic.
*Change:* the fen's seed house is asked by the trust to say, in writing, what it knows about the Ashcombe oat, and the answer Lissa Vale gives is four lines long and is **the truth, which is that the seed house does not know and has never known and copied its own name off a Crown list in a year it does not name.**
*Ends on:* the woman who asked the question is a buyer and she is owed an answer by the fourteenth.

### 66 — What The Book Is For
**Days 79–80.** *The batch's midpoint, and it is a defeat.*
**The standing pressure log, the use log, the seed annex, the pressure pulse, the leak, the 399 hundredweight, the three acres, the six notes, the certified copy in a Fennmere file, and a copy going to Cray are now in a form, or a file, or a ledger, or a printing press — and this batch has established that the fen's own book is the only thing in the world that anybody who is not the fen can check, and that the same property is what makes it an invitation.** Dunmar Fitch reads the whole of it out, once, in public, in a yard, in daylight, from the middle of the table — a thing that has never happened in this holding — **and when he comes to the end of it he says the sentence that turns the batch: a book that anybody may read is worth exactly what a reader is prepared to do about it, and this holding has spent a season building the first and has not found the second.** Change: the body does not repeal the open book. **It cannot be repealed for the volume's reasons** — the district adopted it as a condition and a body cannot consent to the removal of its own record — and **that is Chapter 50's mechanism being used against them by a mechanism that works.** The body instead votes, and the vote is the batch's last real act: **every entry from this day forward is read aloud before it is initialled, and the reader is a person and not a room, and a person is anybody who comes.**
*Ends on:* the first person to come is a stranger, and it is a seed buyer, and she is owed an answer by the fourteenth.

### 67 — The Answer
**Days 81–83.** *The consequence of Ch 66, immediately, and it is not comfortable.*
**A woman comes to the gate and asks to have the use log read to her, because the fifth sheet says anybody may, and the standing pressure log is open, and she is a stranger with a pencil and no charter and no standing whatsoever.** She is owed an answer by the fourteenth and she has come about the seed, and the reading she gets is the whole of the fen's hydrology, and **Lissa Vale reads it to her because the rule says anybody may ask to have the use log read to them and Lissa Vale is the one who wrote that rule down.** Nothing is withheld. Nothing is softened. **And the stranger's own reason for being there is printed in a book two hundred miles from here, and she is going to write to a printer about it, and nobody in the yard can stop her and nobody in the yard wants to.** Change: the fen discovers that being readable is a position and not a virtue, and that a woman who came two hundred miles to buy an answer about a bean has been handed a season's water, and **that this is the correct outcome and the fen is not thanked for it and cannot be, because no one has asked it to be a good citizen.**
*Ends on:* she asks, on her way out, the question nobody in the yard has asked a stranger in six weeks — whether there is a use log for the fieldbook — and Lissa Vale says **no**, and the stranger writes that down too.

### 68 — The First Reason
**Days 84–85.** *The batch's climax. Kellan at the examination, asked for by the fen, and it goes wrong, and it is still right.*
**The yard agrees to Kellan Rusk's request and it goes in the schedule for the next monthly examination, and Kellan comes, and the district sends an officer, and the officer is not Hester Carrow, whose authority ran to the end of the year and who has a tray.** The figure is in his hand and the three figures are on the table and the standing pressure log is open. **What breaks is not the evidence. It is that under the monitored charter his return to the district is a document, and the officer asks for the document before she will hear the figure, and Kellan produces it, and it does not contain the figure, because he has been putting the figure in the fen's book and not in his own.** That is a breach of a condition of his own charter, disclosed by him, in a room, on the day he asked to be asked. **He is not rescued and he is not forgiven and nobody apologizes to him, and the figure stands, and the breach is on the record in his own words, exactly as Idris Vane's condition was in Volume 01 and for the same reason: a person who discloses a breach of his own condition can still be believed about the thing that is not the breach.**
*Change, and it is the batch's:* **Marek asks the question aloud in front of a district officer that nobody in this yard has asked in a season, which is what the fen wants the district to ask him.** He asks whether the district will require the full-draw load test that the engineer of record declined to perform, and he offers to perform it himself on the district's order and to take the paper for it. **The officer has to answer, and the answer is yes, and the answer costs the fen nothing and gives the fen the only thing it has wanted since the twenty-first of a month, which is a figure that is true in front of somebody who can check it.**
*Ends on, and this is the batch's last beat and the volume's next question:* the officer, closing the file, says that the district will want the map next, and **the district will want the map because a Crown seed-route map of this district is nine years old and there is a legend mark on it that this holding can now name.** Nobody in that room says the word. Dunmar Fitch writes it in the book that night, at the hour, in his own hand, and it is the first entry of the next movement and the longest single line in the volume so far.

## Locks this batch must honor

- **The charter expires on the twenty-ninth of the month after next.** That is fifty-four days from the fourth of this batch and thirty-four days from its last day. **Nothing may be written that makes it nearer or further without recording the change, and no chapter may say the season ends at a date the charter does not carry.**
- **Nia is not in this batch.** Not in a room, not in a letter, not by report. Nobody may say she decided not to deliver the letter. The letter is in the open drawer, sealed.
- **The fieldbook and the use log are on the middle of the seed-house table and stay there.** The press is open with the March's tin under the ledger, now fifty days and then sixty at risk, unmoved.
- **Sera Quill is not asked about Brinewake, is not in this batch, and nobody asks her about her fourth refusal.** It is unnumbered and it may not be numbered.
- **The pressure pulse is not diagnosed.** If it is logged again it is the tenth, and it is logged as *cause not established* and **nothing is written underneath it.**
- **The leak is still a leak and is still not mended.** Five-eighths of an inch, twice measured, at the seventh hour. **Do not mend it and do not make it a mystery.**
- **The third exchange requirement is still missing and the 399 hundredweight shortfall is carried forward and not discharged.**
- **The works schedule behind the certified page is never produced.** The applicant is not entitled to it. **No villain may reverse the district's structural refusal.**
- **The Thornwild settlement is not named.** The long line is not used, quoted, or entered. **The word *seat* does not appear.** Ivo Tarrant's four men do not come back and no bypass is built.
- **Dunmar's *none known* is spent. Marda Vole does not appear. The sealed envelope holding the first reader's record is never opened by anybody, ever. Osvalde Bream does not come back and is not written to.**
- **The pathhound is not interpreted.** The collie's report is in three places, Gael Senn's decision to write nothing about the animal stands with her name on the decision, the low quarter has chalked its own line on its own hand at its own cost, and **nobody may interpret the animal or use it as a symbol, and the weir animal does not return.**
- **Lissa Vale is not forgiven and is not thanked.** Her refusal on the fourth of a month of the last batch is **hers, is not Sera Quill's, is not her own fourth, and is not numbered.**
- **Spelling is US. Quotation marks and apostrophes are straight. A speech running over paragraphs opens a mark in every paragraph and closes it in the last. No paragraph may end with a speech open.** Volume 01 carried 221 of those and a second audit found two more that a per-chapter count cannot see.
- **The Crown Engine, the Continuity Office, False Season, Iona Vey, the Quiet Break, and any history lecture** may not be named. Not one of the six is named in Chapters 50–59.

## The one Fieldbook panel for this batch

**One panel, one, at a moment of choice, three plain lines, read by a licensed man with the contact tested and the use-log line written and read back first, in the instrument and not in a person.** It is **not** to be spent on the seed line, on the bead, on the hand in the register, or on the market — the instrument reads a node and not a book, and Chapter 59 has already established that once. **Spend it on a thing the instrument can read, and let it cost something, and let nobody quote it in a document.** If a chapter's choice does not need it, leave it unspent and say so in the state files, as Batch 0004 of Volume 01 did.

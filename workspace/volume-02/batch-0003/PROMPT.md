## What this batch was for

**Movement 3 is the volume's argument, and the argument is one sentence: a device that makes provenance self-certifying is a device that makes provenance ownable, and the two are the same mechanism.** The batch had to deliver that sentence as a working discovery rather than a speech, and then had to put the fen in a yard and make it decide.

**The batch's three external pressures, in order, and none of them is a villain:**

1. **The bead works.** A seed lot wetted against a copper-collared living bead reports where it was grown in four seconds, without a ledger, without a clerk and without anybody's word, and it is offered to the Dryfoot Trust's dry-land line by a buyer with a printed certificate and no form behind it.
2. **Tova Reed establishes what a graft is** — a graft keeps the thing that was put in and loses the thing that was there, so a bead that certifies a seed's history is a bead that can be made to carry any history, and once the history travels in the seed instead of in a book the book is no longer evidence of anything. **And the harder half: the memory the bead carries is not the seed's. It is the seedkeeper's** — a hand, a season, a decision about which trays to keep, read out of living wood.
3. **The cooperative must decide whether to profit from the discovery, and the decision is taken in the yard, in daylight, in front of the book, with a buyer at the gate and the engineer of record voting against the majority on his own record.**

**What the cards below turned out to be, and the two places the drafting departed from the outline and recorded the departure:** the outline says the memory is *the seedkeeper's* and it is, twice, in two books' worth of trays, and the seedkeeper in the first instance is Lissa Vale and in the second it is Tova Reed herself. **The outline says the device is offered to the Trust's dry-land line and it is, but it is offered by a buyer who is correct, and the discovery is that the line was never lost, which the outline does not say and which the batch earned.** That departure is recorded as a proposed canon change in `state/continuity.md` and it may not be undone.

## The cards, and what each one changed

### 69 — Four Seconds
**Days 85–87.** *The reversal, and it is a success.* A collar is bought from a licensed man's stock at two shillings and not built, and the fen reads four lots. Two read as places. The third does not. Change: **the device reports the last hand, and a keeper's private decision about a bad tray has been published in four seconds to six people at a table, and the keeper refuses to have it written down** — the first hole in a book that anybody may read.
### 70 — A Certificate
**Days 88–90.** *The market object, and the three lines on the form.* A buyer with a printed certificate, a collar bought in the spring, and a lot that is eleven parts carrier and one part line, correctly certified. Change: **the third of the three lines is the one no device can fill in and the seller fills it in.**
### 71 — Eleven Hundred
**Day 91.** *The district's test, and the condition nobody will name.* The full-draw load test on the district's order with a licensed man present, the paper the district's, the leak measured under load for the first time and unmoved, the eleventh logging of the beat with a licensed man present. Change: **the third of the three conditions in clause three is given a test date on the far side of the charter's death and a test holder who is whoever is there.**
### 72 — The Third Time
**Days 93–94.** *The seedkeeper's memory, on the floor, at night.* Thirty-one trays. Six read a place; twenty-five read a keeper. And the Trust's dry-land rye, which reads a place correctly, beside a list of eleven holdings that has no column anywhere in the world. Change: **the dry-land line is not lost and the statement that it is lost is the largest wrong the trust has ever entered, and it cannot be withdrawn.**
### 73 — What It Is Worth
**Days 95–96.** *The money and the panel.* Two hundredweight a month against eleven years of the record, and an argument for the offer that the engineer of record makes badly and asks to have minuted badly. Change: **the fen decides the batch's central question without the one instrument that could have settled it, because a line in a book that cannot be checked is a line somebody can use.**
### 74 — In Front Of The Book
**Days 97–99.** *The climax, and a real vote.* The keeper reads out the keeper's confession. The engineer says the sentence he was asked to say, with the number in it, before the counting. Change: **seven hands, five one way, two the other, and one of the two is the engineer of record, and the record is printed instead of leased, and the printing cannot be recalled.**
### 75 — The Man Who Refused
**Days 100–101.** *The external consequence, and it is unfixable.* A licensed man offered twelve pounds and a Sunday shift, refusing in writing and reporting it early, declining to name a buyer and giving the reason that is the fen's own. Change: **a standing is born — a man may decline to name a buyer in a book this body keeps — and the district confirms in four correct lines that it has no form for any of it.**
### 76 — The Price Of It
**Days 102–104.** *The bill, and the door.* The C/4 answered late and in full with a schedule of what cannot be produced and a column that does not exist; the map given away with a counter-mark; two hundred pages off the press; the arithmetic unmoved. Change: **the Crown's ninth legend mark is now contradicted in a dated return by a body that is a holder, and somebody in an office has to decide what to do about a holder that appeared — and a woman with no house has walked eleven days to buy a copy of the record.**

## Locks this batch honored

- **The charter expires on the twenty-ninth of the month after next.** Nothing may make it nearer or further.
- **Nia is not in this batch** and nobody may say she decided not to deliver the letter.
- **The fieldbook, the use log and the Ashcombe index are on the middle of the seed-house table together and stay there**, the index open at a column that does not name this fen.
- **Sera Quill is not in this batch, is not asked about Brinewake, and nobody asks about her fourth refusal.**
- **The pressure pulse is logged once, the eleventh, with a licensed man present for the first time, and is not diagnosed and nothing is written underneath it.**
- **The leak is still a leak and is still not mended.** Measured under a full draw for the first time, unmoved, undiagnosed, into the housing and not into the pit.
- **The third exchange requirement is still missing and the 399 hundredweight shortfall is carried forward and not discharged.**
- **The works schedule behind the certified page is never produced**, and no second demand for the register is sent.
- **The Thornwild settlement is not named, the long line is not used, and the word *seat* does not appear.** The four men do not come back and no bypass is built.
- **Lissa Vale is not forgiven and is not thanked**, and her refusal of the fourth of a month of Batch 0002 is not Sera Quill's and is not her own fourth and is not numbered.
- **Spelling is US. Quotation marks and apostrophes are straight. A speech running over paragraphs opens a mark in every paragraph and closes it in the last. No paragraph may end with a speech open** — except the two deliberate multi-paragraph speeches in Chapter 74, which open in one paragraph and close in the next.

## The one Fieldbook panel for this batch

**One panel, one, at a moment of choice, in Chapter 73, on the wood of a bead in a copper collar, by a licensed man with his own clip, the contact tested with a plain wire gauge and the test written down first, the use-log line written and read back first, and nothing whatever entered in any document.** It is in the instrument and not in a person, it is not quoted anywhere, and it cost eleven seconds, a hot forearm and a day of the hand.

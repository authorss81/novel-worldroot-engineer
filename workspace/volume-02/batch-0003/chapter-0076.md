# Chapter 76

## The Price Of It

The Dryfoot Trust's answer to form C/4 came down the March road on the twelfth of the month, twenty-eight days after the last day of the fourteen that this holding had worked out for itself and could not check, and it came in a canvas bag with a seal on it that had been broken and mended twice, which is what a bag looks like after a road that has been reported out at two places.

Tova Reed took it to the middle of the table and did not open it for a quarter of an hour, and in the meantime Lissa Vale fed the lamp, because the bulb has to be fed or it dies.

"Twenty-eight days," said Dunmar Fitch. "Entered as a computation and not as a fact, entered on the fourteenth of last month and entered again on the twentieth and on the twenty-second with the hours, and this holding has not been able to check one hour of it, and the clerk of the district that served the notice is not a person this holding has ever spoken to."

The bag held nine sheets and a covering letter of one page, and the covering letter was signed by a man whose name Tova Reed said out loud once because she had known him for eleven years, and the nine sheets were the trust's answer to a records demand, produced late, in full, with a schedule of what the trust could not produce and the reason in each case, and there were four things on that schedule and one of them was a column.

The column is what stopped the room. The trust's crop histories are kept on sheets with printed heads, and there are eleven columns, and the seventh is *source*, and it had never once in eleven years had anything in it but a place. There is no column for cuttings, there is no column for a line dispersed to ground and not returned from, and there is no column for a steward, and the answer said so in one line under the schedule: *the trust cannot produce a record of cuttings dispatched, there being no column for it, and no such record was made, and this is not an omission from the answer but the absence of the thing itself.*

"She has told them," said Marek.

"She has told them on the twelfth of this month, nine days after she told this yard on the fourth, in a letter of four sheets that went with a copy of the fen's book, because the annex's fourth term sends a copy of everything to Cray and she is a steward and the printing was being entered and she would not have it go without this in it." Tova Reed squared the letter to the edge of the table. "And the eleven names are in the trust's book, and the trust is nine hundred miles away and cannot be asked anything, and the eleventh name is this holding, and this holding is a party to a charter condition that expires in fifteen days, and there is now one list of eleven cuttings in two books in two countries and no private copy of it anywhere, and that is the first time in eleven years that has been true of anything I have done."

She read the ninth sheet out, which was the only part of the answer the fen was not expecting.

"The March road is out at two places and has been since the fourteenth," she said. "The first is a mile short of the low dry and the ground has gone, and a cart can get through with an empty bed and cannot get back loaded. The second is further down and it is the bad one. The trust cannot move a crate north or a crate south until the season turns, and the trust has asked this holding a question in the same line, and the question is whether this holding would hold the trust's seed for one season, and I want the question read out and the answer read out and neither of them hurried."

Nobody hurried either. The answer was that the holding would, and could not pay carriage, and the trust had not asked it to, and the eleven pounds of printing was already the trust's money and the eleventh was the last of it that this holding knows of.

Then the last of the nine sheets said something the fen had to hear twice, and it said it as a fact and not as a favor, and it was the kind of sentence an office writes when it is being careful.

"A line kept in one book is a line that can be got at," the sheet said. "A line kept in eleven grounds and no book is a line that can be sold. The trust has not been paid for the cuttings and does not know that they were paid for and has now entered that it does not know, and the trust's position is that this is a fault in the trust and not in any holder, and that a fault of that kind cannot be corrected by paying anybody, and can only be corrected by saying it out loud in a book that anybody may read. The trust understands that the trust has said so in one."

The yard was quiet, and the pump ran.

---

The district wrote for the map on the thirteenth, and the letter was one sheet and it was correct, and it was Hilary Skeath's office and not Hilary Skeath, and it asked this holding to produce the Crown seed-route sheet of the Tarrow district in the version current at the date of the last revision, together with any marginal annotation of this holding's own making, for the purposes of a schedule check under the charter.

The sheet had lain on the middle of this table since the twenty-ninth of a month two months back with a reader's own marginal line on it in her hand, and the case it came in was locked and the key was on Tova Reed's belt, and everybody in that room had known for a season that the question was coming.

"It is a Crown sheet," said Tova Reed. "Nine years old. It is not ours to give and it is not ours to correct, and if I hand it over the district gets the legend's ninth mark and the name of a branch that is a hundred and forty rods of twelve inch ordered in the eleventh month by a man whose hand a reader of thirty-one years could not call wrong, and every one of those facts is a fact about a Crown document and not a fact about this holding."

She unlocked the case at the table, in front of everybody, and took the sheet out and did not fold it, and put it flat.

"What I am not going to do is send it and be silent," she said. "What I am going to do is send it with a second sheet, and the second sheet is ours, and it does not touch the Crown's layer and does not pretend to, and I have written it myself and I want it read aloud before it goes."

It was a page and a half and it began with a list. The branch marked on the Crown sheet with the ninth legend mark, which is *unmapped root line, maintained in schedule*, is used by this holding for the supply of eleven households and is a local holder of it in fact and has been since the twentieth of a month two months back, when the word *holder* was used out loud in a room for the first time in a season. This holding makes no claim to the Crown's layer and no claim to any other's. The mark on the Crown sheet is not a warning to the holder. It is a handover to whoever is not the holder. This holding notes that it is the holder and that it was not on the sheet when the sheet was printed, and that the sheet has not been reprinted in nine years, and that nobody appears to have been required to say anything about it.

"A mark that means a line is maintained by somebody and that somebody has no holder has been on a Crown map for nine years," she said, "and in that nine years the Crown has printed four hundred copies of it and sold them to a hundred and forty seed houses, and every act of doing so has been correct and timely, and a person who drew that mark knew that the line was maintained and did not know or did not care who maintained it. This holding has now said out loud, in a return to a district, that it is a holder of a line that the map says has none, and that return is dated and is signed and is in a file nine miles from here, and somebody in an office is now going to have to decide what to be done about a holder that appeared, and this holding has no idea what the answer is and has not asked to be told, and it wants it entered that the question was opened on the twenty-fourth of last month by a district officer who did not open it."

She sent it on the Thursday cart with the printing, and the district's letter was answered inside four days of being asked, and nobody in that yard was proud of it and everybody in that yard was relieved.

---

The copies came off the press at Fennmere on the fourteenth, forty of them, the paper already in the shop and the setting done in a week for eleven shillings, and a boy brought them up the fen road on a hand barrow in two trips and put them on the middle of the table beside the fieldbook and the use log and the Ashcombe index and the two hundred pages of the enemy's printer, which had lain there since the seventh of last month and was still open at a column that did not name this fen.

The last page was Coll Renner's two hundred words, and under it were Tova Reed's two sentences, and under those was his name and his license number, and the second sentence on that page said that a person who can print that they do not know where four of their own lines came from can print anything.

Lissa Vale read the whole of the two hundred pages out in the yard on the afternoon of the fourteenth, to the five households who came and to the man of about fifty and to the man of about seventy and to the man from Marrowfield's house who had come back up the fen road without being asked to and had said at the gate that she had a cart and would rather spend the day in a fen than in an office, which is a thing a buyer is allowed to say at a gate and which nobody in that yard was in a position to refuse.

She read the four lines, and the ninth line, and the eight hundred words of figures, and the statement on the front page about the Crown list and the year that is not named, and the whole of it took most of the afternoon, and she did not skip anything and she did not stop for anybody, and when she came to the end she said that the record of the holding was now a thing anybody could hold in one hand for a shilling and that she had been keeping a book for thirty-one years that nobody could buy, and that she was not sorry about that and was not sure it was a virtue, and Dunmar Fitch entered that and the hour and read it back.

One copy went to a household that had asked for it. One went to Fray, who paid a shilling for it and said that four hundred lines a year does not make a house careful, but that eleven pages of somebody else's ignorance might, and that she would put it in her winter list without a certificate.

The arithmetic went in that night, and it is the same arithmetic and it does not go anywhere. Nothing put into the return main this month, none available, for the second month running. The shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, carried forward and not discharged. The bed at three and a half degrees, twice measured, and a soil iron cannot read the tenth of a degree. The March's tin under the ledger in the press, unmoved, eighty-five days at risk on the fourteenth, with the seed house's undertaking that it will not be moved and the trust's that it will not be asked. The leak at five-eighths of an inch, twice measured, at the seventh hour, pump on, into the housing and not into the pit, and it did not move under a full draw on Saturday and it is not mended and nobody has said why. The beat logged eleven times, cause not established, and nothing written underneath it. Nineteen acres standing, the Longneck leaning eleven degrees, four willow sets alive, the graft fed about a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed, and the starve still not visible from the top of the field. The unopened copy of the low quarter's own survey, where it has been since the tenth of last month, not of this holding and not in this holding's book. And the charter expires on the twenty-ninth of the month after next, which is fifteen days from this afternoon, with no appeal anywhere in the four clauses, and Ruben Ashe named in this holding's own book as returning at that date.

Underneath it, dated the fourteenth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the thirteenth note under section nine of the charter schedule: a collar of living wood bought from a licensed man at two shillings and the four seconds and what it does and does not say, a certificate of origin on the letterhead of a house at Marrowfield, an offer refused in a yard by seven hands of which two were against and one of the two was the engineer of record, two hundred pages set in print and sold at a shilling a copy, eleven of the eleven cuttings entered in two books in two countries with no private copy, a district's demand for a Crown sheet answered inside four days with a second sheet that names this holding a holder of a line the Crown sheet says has none, a licensed man offered twelve pounds for a collar on a Sunday and refusing in writing and coming up the road five days before his own date to say so, a district that has no form for any of it, and fifteen days.

*This is worth nothing against a cut.*

The last thing that came up the fen road that week came on the fourteenth in the afternoon post, on foot, by a woman who was not on any cart and who had walked eleven days.

The letter was three lines long and it was signed by a woman who gave her name and did not give a house, and it said that she kept seed for eleven households on ground the district says it maintains and does not, and that she had this holding's eleven years copied out in a hand she could not name, out of four lines a buyer had read out loud in a market two hundred miles from here, and that she would pay a shilling a copy to anybody who had one to sell, and that the road was out at two places and she had walked eleven days.

Marek read it twice and put it in front of the clerk, and Dunmar Fitch entered it with the hour: a letter received on the fourteenth from a person not named in this holding's book, no house given, eleven households, the road out at two places, eleven days on foot, offering a shilling a copy of this holding's eleven years to anybody who has one to sell.

And then he wrote down the answer, and the answer is the last line of the book this season and it is the only one in eleven weeks that anybody in that yard wanted read aloud twice.

The yard had forty copies on the middle of its own table, and eleven shillings had been spent on them out of a body's account nine hundred miles off, and there was no printer's form, no district form, no charter condition and no license that could move a crate of seed from this fen to a line of ground that a woman with no house had walked eleven days to write to, and the whole apparatus of the law in the district had been put in front of the situation in one day and had produced four lines saying that there was no form for it.

Tova Reed took a copy off the stack and put it in her own case, in the middle of the others, and would not say whether she was keeping the map or giving it away and did not have to, because she had given the map away the day before on the Thursday cart and had kept the record, and the two of those are different things and only one of them is anybody's to give away twice.

A boy went down the fen road to the post with the letter and a copy and a shilling of the fen's money in his hand, and the road was out at two places, and the charter was fifteen days from ending, and the graft was being fed a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed, and the lamp in the seed house went on burning on its living bulb because somebody has to feed it tonight and will feed it tomorrow, and the bulb does not care who.

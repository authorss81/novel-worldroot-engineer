# Chapter 96

## The Arrangement That Is Not A Charter

Marek Vale drafted it in a morning on the twenty-fourth and brought it to the well house door, and it is five clauses, and it has a date and a condition and a right to leave, and the heading over it is the whole of what it is.

*An arrangement, of the seed-route between the first end and the low dry. There is no charter. There is no seal. There is no counter-signature. Nothing in this can be enforced by anybody, and that is the whole of what it is for and the whole of what it is worth.*

One. What runs. The route as it is on the schedule on this door, from the first end to the low dry, and not beyond, and not to the second break. The third part of the district's terms is honored as written: the declared capacity is kept as declared on the door, the rate is fourpence the crate on thirty, one shilling a month, and the record of the route is the schedule and the ledger and both are produced to any person at any hour.

Two. The condition. The measured capacity is measured every month and entered on that sheet whatever it is, and it is not left off, and it is not moved off the sheet, and it is not taken off the door. It is entered under the declared figure, in the same hand, and it is not moved above it.

Three. Stopping. Either end may stop the route on a day's notice by writing it in the book. No reason is required and none is to be entered. The day it is written is the day it is over, and there is no appeal from that, and this is deliberate.

Four. A copy. A copy of the schedule and of the ledger may be taken by anybody at this end who asks for one, and the reading of any entry in either is a right and not a favor. At the second end there is a copy of the schedule on a post inside a fence and the person there has refused a copy twice and been right twice, and this holding is not going to send a third, and that is entered as a decision and not as an injury.

Five. The term. To the last day of the sowing, which is the thirtieth of this month. Then the route goes on by a new writing or it stops, and stopping needs no reason, no paper and no notice, and a route that stops is not a route that has failed.

Anneke Roos read all five aloud on the afternoon of the twenty-fourth at the well house door and said that she had looked for a fault in all five for the better part of an hour and had found one, and it was in the heading.

"It says nothing in it can be enforced by anybody," she said, "and I have been trying since the ninth of this month to work out whether that is true, and it is not quite. Clause three can be enforced by the eleven households. Not by a district and not by a man in a yard, but by a person with a hoe, and that is a person, and so the sentence at the head of this sheet is a small lie, and it is the smallest lie in this building and I have found it myself and I would like it kept with my reason.

"The reason is that a document nobody can enforce is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper does not stop a cart. So this sheet is unforceable by anybody with a box at a post and enforceable by eleven people who have never signed a paper, and I would rather have that than a sheet nobody can do anything with, and I am not taking the word out of the heading, because a body that has to be accurate in a heading is a body that will be accurate in nothing else."

It went in that way, and the reader was Lissa Vale, and she read the heading and the reason both back.

---

The man of about fifty asked for a vote, was told one was not needed, gave his reason anyway, and Dunmar Fitch entered the description along with the reason for it.

"You do not need a vote," he said, "and I have been in this yard every time there has been one and I voted against twice and for four times and I would like it in the book that I have not voted on this and that I am glad I did not have to.

"I voted against keeping that book in the spring because a body that is proud of being honest has stopped asking the question, and I said that how a body is known is not how it is honest, it is how big it is, and I said I would rather this fen was small and did not know for one more season whether it can be." He stood in the yard with his hands behind him. "And I have had a month to be asked the question, and I was asked it, and the man who asked it was a licensed man with a bar over his shoulder who wanted eleven pounds and did not get it, and he told me in a yard that he has nine men and not enough work. That is the question, and it is the only one, and it is not about size.

"So I would rather be small and know. And that is not a conversion, and I would like the clerk to enter that it is not a conversion, because a man who has a conversion gets told later that he always believed it, and I did not, and I have got a bad reason on the page from the twenty-ninth of last month with my name under it and I would like the new thing to have a date and not a birthday."

And the man of about seventy read that back, and asked for his initial to be put against nothing, and it cost the holding nothing.

---

And then the thing that took the rest of the morning, and it was not the arrangement, and it was clause four, and it took longer than everything else on the sheet put together.

"A copy of this could go on the post," said Jonas Byre. "It is the same sheet as the schedule and the schedule is on the post, and I have been sitting on that since Tuesday. I am not asking to send her a document. I am asking to nail one more nail on a post that already has a nail in it, and it is the arrangement and not a record and not a return and not a paper with her name at the top of it, and I cannot make it into a third refusal and I do not know why."

"Because there is no fifth sheet," said Tova Reed.

That stopped the yard.

"There is no fifth sheet," she said. "There are the six clauses of the terms of the route with the second struck out, and they were sent and returned. There is a certificate of forty-one words on lining paper and it was asked for. And there is this. And the three of them are three times, and every one of them was a piece of paper going to a person who has never signed a paper in her life, and every one of them was made by one or the other of us because we thought it would help.

"And it is not that she says no. She said no to a copy of a record, and she said no to a column, and she said no to a cart, and she has said yes to nothing at all, and the reason is that she does not know what any of it is, and every one of us decided on her behalf what she ought to be given, and three times we were wrong, and the third time we were wrong with a good heart and eleven pounds on the table.

"So clause four stands, and the copy goes on the post when a person goes and not before, and nobody is appointed to take it, and no one in this district is told there is a copy waiting for her." She put her pencil down. "Enter it as a decision and not as a wound, because if I enter it as a wound then in four years somebody will try to fix it and fixing it will mean sending her a paper."

Dunmar Fitch entered it and the hour and read it back, and the reader was Lissa Vale, and she read it back twice, and nobody in that yard thanked anybody.

---

And then the district's terms, because somebody had to ask, and it was the man of about seventy who asked it in the plainest possible words, and it is the reason this arrangement is safe and it is not safe at all.

"Do the four sheets on that door apply to this," he said. "That is a real question and it is the only one in this yard nobody has answered. That office has said a use of ground the district carries in schedule is a class of use, and classes of use carry terms and a rate. And this holding is now an operator of a use of that ground, and this arrangement is a piece of paper about that use. So does the rate on it go up, and if it does, does the rate on this arrangement make this arrangement a use, and then is a thing this holding wrote on its own door a thing the district can send somebody to collect a rate on."

Nobody had an answer and Marek Vale had one and he gave it in about ninety seconds and it is in the book.

"No, and then yes, and here is the whole of it," he said. "That is a route over ground the district carries in schedule, so the terms apply, and there is no reading of it that gets round the second part, and the rate is a shilling a month and we pay it. Good.

"Now. The arrangement is not a use of ground. It is a piece of paper between a body with no standing and a settlement with no standing, and it moves no crate and it changes no ditch and it puts no cart on anybody's ground, and there is nothing in it that anybody could call a use. And if that office ever says otherwise, in writing, then it becomes a use, and it will be rated at fourpence a crate on a declared capacity, and we will pay that too, and we will enter the payment in the ledger with the date and the reference and the amount, and we will enter that the rate was collected on a document this holding wrote itself.

"And that is the answer and I would like it written on the page over the arrangement, because in four years somebody in a room is going to be looking for exactly that and I would like them to find it here in a hand they can check. We have just bound ourselves in public to a clause that says a person with a hoe can stop this road, and a clause is enforceable by the person it names, and if a district ever decides to call that a use then the price of saying no in public is fourpence a crate, and I would rather have written that down while we still had somebody to write it to."

The yard was quiet for about a minute, and the boards over the bay had seventy-nine days cut into both of them, and the number was still going up, and nobody went out to look at it.

The rest of it was said on the twenty-fifth, standing at the north gate, in about a quarter of an hour, because it could not be said in a room.

Gael Senn logged the beat at about the tenth hour and it is the thirteenth, nine in the quarter hour with one run of four, at the rail, cause not established, licensed person present none, and nothing written underneath it, and she said what she says every month, which is that a number with nothing under it is a number and not a mistake, and that if she stopped writing them down because there was nothing to write under them then in four years the only thing anybody would have is a gap where eleven of them used to be.

And then the month's other line, entered in the ordinary way: nothing put into the return main, none available, the specification four hundred hundredweight, the shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, carried forward and not discharged. The return line for this month was entered on the third and it stands. The fourth month will be entered on the first of next month by the same person with the same words or with different ones, and this holding does not know which and has not entered a hope, and the clerk entered that as well, because a body that writes down what it is hoping for has begun keeping records for somebody else.

And the arithmetic of the graft, which Marek Vale wrote out longhand on a sheet of the fen's own paper and which is in the arrangement's own book with the hour on it, in a hand that anybody can check in four years.

*Designed: four hundred hundredweight a month into the return main, being the specification of the instrument this holding no longer holds. Short: three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, fourth month to be entered. Standing: nineteen acres of the repair from the flood green and holding, four willow sets alive, the anchor held, the row of Longneck drilled in the wet and leaning eleven degrees off the vertical along its whole length. Fed: about a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed, which of four hundred hundredweight is twenty hundredweight a month, and the true figure that anybody in this district can defend today is none, and the difference between twenty hundredweight and none is a number a soil iron cannot be asked for, and is entered here as a figure somebody in four years will be able to check and find wrong.*

*And the starving is not visible from the top of the field.*

The fen entered that, and the reader was the man of about fifty, and he read the last line back and put his hand flat on the gate post and said one sentence about it, and it went in.

"It is not visible from the top of the field," he said, "because there is nothing to see. That is not an argument. It is a statement about where a man has to stand, and I have been standing in the wrong place all year, and I have found out about it in a yard, and I would like it in the book in those words and not in a better one."

And the sixty pounds ten was put on the same page, because it is the only money in this arrangement nobody is pretending about, and it stands, and nothing in this month has touched it.

*Not paid. Not remitted. Not reduced. The file is open. The reservation entered on the twenty-fourth of a month two months back stands, and the office may take up the file on any new report, and none has been made and none is going to be made out of the ordinary course, because the only thing in this fen a new report could be made of is a shed and a pump and eight people and a leak of five-eighths of an inch, measured at the seventh hour, twice, by a person with no appointment to measure it.*

And the arrangement went into the seed-route ledger, and the fen entered the last thing about it in the plainest words anybody has used in this building, and it is the standing sentence of this batch and is not the closing sentence under section nine and has never been confused with it.

*That this is an arrangement and not a charter, and that every party to it can walk away on a day's notice without a reason and without a notice and without a paper, and that this holding is a body with no standing and the settlement at the far end of the road is a settlement with no standing, and that the whole of what either of them has is the other one's ability to leave, and that a thing nobody can enforce is the only kind of thing that can be refused, and that this holding would rather have a road it can be refused on than a road it cannot.*

Underneath that, dated the twenty-fifth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the twentieth note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face.

*Five clauses and a heading, and no seal and no counter-signature, and a fault found in the heading by a member of this body and kept there with her reason, which is that a document nobody can enforce is a piece of paper and a piece of paper does not stop a cart. A condition, and the condition is the honest figure: the measured capacity is measured every month whatever it is, and entered under the declared one, in the same hand. A third piece of paper this holding wanted to hand to a woman on the low dry and did not, and the reason is that there is no fifth sheet. A question asked by a man of about seventy and answered in ninety seconds: the rate applies to the route, the arrangement is not a use of ground, and if the district ever says it is, this holding will pay the rate on its own document. A beat logged the thirteenth time with nothing underneath it. The arithmetic of the graft in a hand a person in four years can check. And the sixty pounds ten, not paid, not remitted, not reduced, the file open.*

*This is a record and not an argument, and the sentence is off this note for that reason, and the reason is written here so that the chain cannot be seen to have dropped one.*

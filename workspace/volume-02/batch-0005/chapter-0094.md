# Chapter 94

## A Use Nobody Asked About

He put it at the gate, in writing, on the twentieth, before he came in, and he gave it to the clerk and not to the engineer of record, on the grounds that a paper that goes to the man who is going to be asked about it can be said afterwards to have been aimed at him.

It was two sheets and it is in the seed-route ledger whole, and it begins the way everything Coll Renner has ever put in this yard begins, which is with the reason before the request.

*The low quarter has four wells off and forty-one houses on a bed this holding's own ground killed. The ditch at the low quarter end was not dug. Four men were offered to dig it and were refused on five correct grounds in the spring and they have not come back and are not coming back and I am not asking about them. There is no water in that quarter for nine miles and the water there is in four men and a mole, and the mole is not a public supply. I can put lime and stone into that quarter in eleven loads. The second break on the March road will take them if the second break is made good, and I can make the second break good for eleven pounds, and a quarter mile of running sand with a soft rise is not a soft job and eleven pounds is my price and not a favor and I have put my price on the page.*

*What I ask for is the road. Eleven loads out and eleven back, entered in the schedule crate by crate like anything else, weighed at this end and at that end, in the second and third and fourth columns in the ordinary way, and the fifth and sixth signed by me and by the man who drives, and the second end's column left as it is. Fourpence a hundredweight on what goes, at the compost price, because I am not going to be paid twice for the same ground and you have fourpence a crate on a class of use and I would rather there were one number in this district than two.*

*Either end may stop it on a day's notice by writing in the book. No reason required. I will not ask for a reason and you will not have to give one. I am not asking to be on that schedule as a keeper. I am asking to be a cart on it.*

The yard read it in the ordinary way and the man of about fifty read it back, and the count of the readings of the seventh column was nineteen and did not go up, because the twentieth is not a day the door is read on, and the number was entered with the hour because the fen does not read a document and a column at the same time and has learned that.

Nobody said anything for about a minute, and then a cart went up the fen road past the gate toward the low dry, and it did not stop, and it was loaded, and it was lashed the way Nia Vale lashed crates on a hand barrow, and the man of about fifty watched it go past and said nothing about it, and nobody in that yard asked him anything about it, and Dunmar Fitch entered that a loaded cart had gone up the fen road on the twentieth at about the tenth hour, lashed in a way three people in that yard could do, and that the fen does not keep a record of who goes up that road with a barrow lashing, and the reader for that entry was Lissa Vale, and she read it back and did not look at the man of about fifty while she read it.

And then the yard went at the second sheet.

"This holding cannot say no to that," said Marek Vale.

"Write down why," said Tova Reed, "and write it as a sentence and not as a feeling, because you have been wrong twice this week and I would like to know which wrong this is."

"Because of the third part," he said, and then stopped, and started again, and the clerk entered that he stopped once and that the second attempt was the one that was entered. "The condition is that the operator keeps the declared capacity in the ordinary course. The declared capacity is thirty crates a month. The measured capacity is six. So on the figures this holding published on a door with its own hand on it, in the same ink, under the larger one, this holding is already not keeping the declared capacity. It has been since the twenty-sixth of last month. It is out of compliance with a condition of a class of use right now, this month, and it does not know it and cannot be told, because a form that is not filled in is not a condition broken, and a person who tells the district that it is not keeping a declared capacity has filled in a form.

"So if this holding refuses a paying use of its own route, there is no difference whatever, to anybody in nine miles, between this holding protecting eleven households on the low dry from a licensed contractor's carts and this holding not keeping the declared capacity in the ordinary course. They are the same act. And I have wanted to be an operator since the ninth of this month and I have got it, and it has cost us the only thing we own, which is the right to say no."

Nobody in that yard had an answer to it and the afternoon went on being that.

Renner waited until it had gone on being that for a while, and then he said the thing he had plainly brought the two sheets to say and had not got to, because a man with a bar had said a better sentence than his.

"Nobody has asked me the question everybody is thinking," he said. "So I am going to say it and get it out of the room. It is not the beds. Everybody in this yard has been looking at the floor since I gave the clerk that sheet, and everybody is thinking about a bed at three and a half degrees, and I am not asking you for anything that touches the node or the water or the compost line, and you know I would not, and if I had put it in those two sheets you would have taken them inside without reading the second one.

"I am asking for a road. And the reason I am asking this holding and not the district is that the district cannot do it, and I have been to the district, and I will tell you what they said, because you would find out anyway and I would rather you had it from me. There is no charter at the far end of that road, so there is nobody to serve, and there is a hole in their own form where a holding number goes, and they cannot make arrangements for the maintenance of ground they have not got a number for, and they will not try, because the honest answer is cheaper than a wrong one, and that office has been honest with me four times in nine years.

"So the district is out, and the only other end of this is a place with no number, and I am not going up there to ask a woman who has never signed a paper in her life to sign one for my eleven loads of lime. And so I came here, and I have put it in writing, and I have put a stop clause in it that neither of us can enforce and that neither of us needs to, and I have put my price on the page, and I have left your return column alone, and I have come on a Saturday so that nobody in this yard has to be asked about it on a working day by a man they work with.

"And there is one more thing, and it is the only thing I have said in eleven months that I have not said in a yard before, and I am saying it because a man who waits to be asked is not a witness. I have dug ditches for thirty-one years and I have dug about two hundred of them and about forty of them are still open, and I have never once been asked to make a road good for money by a body that has a book anybody may read. I would like that in your ledger in my own words, because the reason I want this is not eleven pounds and it is not lime. I have a yard with nine men in it in the middle of a season where there is not enough work, and a man with nine men and no work takes work he does not want inside a year, and that is worse for this district than the fourpence."

Nobody in that yard thanked him for it, and he had not asked to be thanked, and the clerk entered the whole of it, and the reader was the man of about fifty, and the fen entered that a licensed man in this district had put nine men and no work in a book anybody may read of his own accord, and that that was in the record and not in a complaint, and Anneke Roos initialled it as an opinion in four words.

And then a different silence, and a worse one, and it was Marek Vale who broke it, and what he said was not to Renner.

"The low quarter is on our land," he said. "It has four wells off because of a bed we are running at three and a half degrees, and forty-one houses drink off it, and there is a name in a district file with a breach in it that is mine. So I am never going to be able to sit at a table in four years and say that this holding said no to the thing that would have put lime in the low quarter, and I am not going to be able to say that a man was refused either, and both of those are going to be in my mouth. I have thought about it for a day and I have not got a way round it, and I did not want to get a way round it before I said it out loud in a yard, because a man who has got a way round it before he says it has not really said it."

Renner looked at him for a while and then said: "Then I would rather be refused by a person than by a bed, and I have not said that to anybody before and I am not going to soften it now that it is in a book."

And then Jonas Byre asked the question that nobody had asked, and he asked it standing at the door with his hand on the schedule.

"We wrote a clause," he said. "Three days ago. On that sheet, under those columns. It says a person in occupation at either end may refuse a use of the route in the ordinary way."

"Yes," said Marek Vale.

"And it is a good clause and I have read it eleven times since Tuesday and it is the best thing on that door including the schedule, and it has no name in it and it needs no signature and it cannot be taxed and it cannot be got round by anybody." He took his hand off the door. "And it is no use to us at all."

"There is nothing in the world it is not use for," said Tova Reed.

"Then read it to me," said Jonas Byre, "and tell me what happens if the person in occupation at this end says no to a cart of Mr Renner's going down to the low dry."

Nobody read it out. They did not have to.

"The fen is the operator," Marek Vale said, after a while. "That is what the third part says in so many words, the person in occupation of the node at the first end. So a person in occupation at this end refusing a use of the route is the operator not operating, and the operator not operating is not a refusal under our clause, it is a failure of the condition, and the condition is the district's, and the district's is the one with a number on it. Our clause was written to be invoked by somebody we cannot be examined for and we wrote it right and it is exactly the wrong shape for this, because we wrote it to be said by a person at a fence, and I am the person at the fence on this road. I am in occupation of the node. I am the man with the hoe."

He put his hand on the door, on the fen's own seven words, and not on the schedule.

"So the clause is good, and it is ours, and it is in the district's book in nobody's, and it cannot be used on the one question it was written for, and the reason is that we wrote a clause about a person and the Crown wrote a class about a place and the person happens to be standing in the place." He took his hand off. "I would like that entered as the reason the refusal clause did not work on the morning it was written and had to wait three days to find out, and I would like it entered by the man who drafted it, and I would like nobody to make anything else of it."

So the fen wrote it down, in the ledger, with the hour, and Tova Reed wrote one more line under it, which is the line that made the twentieth of this month the day the volume's real problem arrived, and it is in her own hand.

*A public schedule with a return on it is a road that anybody may use, and the only thing that has ever stood between a public road and a private one is a clause, and we have written ours, and it is on the same sheet as the schedule so that nobody can read one without the other, and it will not refuse a use, and the reason is that the party that would have to refuse one is the party the district has made an operator, and a person in occupation of a node cannot refuse a use of ground when the same office has named that person the operator of the thing.*

And then the second question, which was the real one, and Anneke Roos asked it from the doorway and she had not come any further in, which she does when she is about to say something she has thought about.

"Who says no, then," she said. "Not this holding, because you have just told me it cannot. Not the district, because the district is not a party to Mr Renner's two sheets and never will be and has no standing to say yes either. So the whole of the arrangement is standing on this fence with a hole in it, and I want to know who is left, and I have worked it out and I do not like the answer and I am going to say it anyway."

"There is a settlement of eleven households on the low dry with no charter, no holding number, no document and no standing," said Tova Reed, "which this holding made a party to nothing on purpose, on the twenty-second of last month, in a yard, in daylight, and I struck the clause that would have made her a party and I would do it again tomorrow, and that was the right decision and it is the reason there is nobody at the far end who can be asked, and there is nobody at the far end who can be examined, and there is nobody at the far end who can be compelled, and there is nobody at the far end who has ever signed anything, and she has told us both of those things to our faces and been right about both of them.

"And the one body in this whole arrangement that has any right to refuse anything at all is the one that wrote nothing enforceable at the far end, and that is what she is, and that is the whole of what we have got, and it took us a season to work out that the absence of a floor is the only thing standing between a poor woman at a fence and a licensed contractor with eleven pounds and a good price for a quarter mile of sand."

The clerk entered the whole of it and the hour and read it back, and the reader was Lissa Vale, and she read the last sentence twice because the ninth line of a thing is read twice, and the yard was quiet, and outside on the fen road the cart that had gone up toward the low dry was a good way off and still going, and nobody in that yard said anything about it.

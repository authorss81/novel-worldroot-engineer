# Chapter 98

## The Ledger

On the twenty-eighth, which was a Sunday, the three books were put out on the seed house table together, and it was the first time those three had ever been out together, and it had not been done in that form since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back, when a fieldbook was put on a chair and a floor was read out of the middle of a room, and it was done because the copy goes to Cray on Thursday and the copy is everything, and if everything is in three books then the copy is three books and not one.

The fieldbook, which is the old man's, and which nobody in this holding has opened this year.

The use log, which is twelve lines and records contacts and never readings, and there is still no use log for the fieldbook and there never has been and there is not going to be.

And the seed-route ledger, which was begun on the thirteenth of this month on a sheet of the fen's own paper with no number on it, and which is now nineteen pages, and which is the only book in this building that anybody outside this building has ever asked to buy.

Nobody put a fourth thing on that table and the open drawer under the north end of it stayed shut, and the levy papers and the rent book and a sealed letter were in it where they have been since the nineteenth of a month three seasons back, and nobody in that yard has opened it, nobody in that yard has discussed it, and nobody in that yard is going to.

Lissa Vale read all three out at the tenth hour in the yard, in the ordinary way, to whoever came, and the standing was that anybody may ask for any page of any of the three to be read and the reader is a person and a person is anybody who comes, and the reading is before the initial and not after, and the number of the readings of the seventh column went to twenty on the twenty-third and did not go up on the twenty-eighth, because the twenty-eighth is not one of the days, and the clerk entered that, and the fen entered that the rule is a decision and not a mood, and it has been a decision since the second of this month.

One person came. He was a young man of about twenty with a dog and no cart, and he had come for the ledger because the printer at Fennmere had told him it existed and had told him it had no number on it, and he stood at the gate and said that he was not going to buy one because he had not got a shilling, and Lissa Vale read him the four clauses of the arrangement, all five of them, twice, and then read him the refusal clause, and the young man of twenty said the thing that went in the book and that the fen is proudest of and that nobody in this yard will ever know what to do with.

"They have not signed it," he said. "Anybody. I have been coming into this district since I could walk and I have never seen that."

"No," said Lissa Vale. "And it cannot be signed, because the clause says it does not name anybody and nobody is required to sign it, and if we got a signature we would have made a party of the only person on this road who cannot be made a party of anything, and she was asked for one twice in the spring and said no twice and was right twice."

Then the month went in, in the ordinary way, and it is in the ledger, and the reading of it is in the fen's own book, and the standing figures are these, and none of them has moved and three of them will not.

Nineteen acres of the flood repair green and holding. The row of Longneck, eleven trays' worth, out of the trust's own material, sown on the twenty-second of a month three months back in a wet drill, up in four hours, leaning eleven degrees off the vertical along its whole length, and it will be a short row and it will be real, and the word *failed* is not written anywhere in this holding's records about it, by the decision of the woman who takes the examination on the first of the month and who has never once been thanked for a decision she made in a month when nobody was looking.

Four willow sets alive on the north ditch and the anchor held. The graft fed about a twentieth of what it was designed to be fed, and the design figure is four hundred hundredweight a month into a return main that is twenty-two rods of eight inch against a specification of forty-one of twelve. Nothing put into the return main, none available, the specification four hundred hundredweight, the shortfall three hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight, seventy-nine pounds sixteen at four shillings, carried forward and not discharged, and the fourth month to be entered on the first of next month by the same person with the same words or with different ones, and this holding does not know which.

The leak five-eighths of an inch, twice measured, two rules, at the seventh hour, pump on, into the housing and not into the pit, unmoved under a full draw, not mended, undiagnosed, and not a mystery. The beat logged thirteen times, the thirteenth on the twenty-fifth of this month, cause not established, licensed person present none, and nothing written underneath it, and the fen entered that a number with nothing under it is a number and not a mistake.

The March's tin in the press under the ledger, unmoved, on a bed at three and a half degrees, at a hundred and thirty days on the twenty-ninth of this month, with both undertakings standing, the seed house's that it will not be moved and the trust's that it will not be asked, and the dry line of the March sown before the sowing closed on the twenty-ninth of last month, four inches high in six trays in this fen's own ground, and the seed off it to go back down the March road to the counting house at Cray in a crate with a sheet on it, weighed at two ends and signed at two ends, and it is in the seventh column of a schedule on a well house door, and it is the only thing that has ever come back down it.

And the schedule, on the right of the door, with the declared figure of thirty and the measured figure of six under it in the same ink and no gap between them. And the clause under the seven columns in the fen's own hand, with no object in it, which cannot be tested, which binds the body that wrote it, and which has been used once. And the notice on the left, in four correct lines, and the four sheets under it, of a class of use with a number at the head of them and a rate on them, and a toll of one shilling collected at a gate on the eighteenth and entered in two books, and the eightpence of difference entered on its own line and not claimed, and the arrangement of the twenty-fifth in the ledger with no seal and no counter-signature and nothing in it that a district can enforce, running to the last day of the sowing, which is tomorrow.

And a refusal, entered as the fact of a refusal and not its terms, the third of the three in this season, and the first of the three that has ever been used, given by a person in occupation at a fence of thorn and hazel on the low dry on the twenty-second of this month against a use of eleven carts that nobody had asked her about, and the terms of it written down by nobody, held by nobody, and not going to be asked for.

And Tova Reed's name on the public ledger six times, six of them against the Dryfoot Trust's own interest, and she said on the twenty-sixth of this month in one sentence that she had stopped counting and would not start again, and the clerk entered the sentence and the hour and did not enter a number, because she asked him not to and because he is her clerk.

---

Underneath it all, dated the twenty-ninth, signed by nobody but Marek, and read back before it was initialled, is the twenty-first note under section nine of the charter schedule, and there is no charter, and the note says so on its face, as the eighteenth and the nineteenth and the twentieth all do.

*Four sheets of a class of use on a door, four parts, a rate of fourpence a crate on a declared capacity of thirty, a shilling a month, the difference of eightpence entered and not claimed, and the reason it is not claimed, which is that the smaller figure on the door is the only fact this body has published that a district cannot get at. A condition that can be tested and not enforced and an operator named by the district where a body with no standing had not been named before. A toll collected at a gate, a receipt with a number on it not put on a door, a use number written in a box headed for a holding number. A refusal clause with no object in it, written by this holding, on this holding's own sheet, on this holding's own door, that a person at a fence can stop a road, that names nobody, requires no signature, requires no reason, and cannot be taxed, waived or defeated, and a price paid for it in public and in a yard. A use asked for and refused, and the refusal used, and the terms of it held by nobody. An arrangement that is not a charter and cannot be enforced by anybody with a box at a post, and a rate that will be paid on it anyway if the district says it is a use. A post offered and refused in writing in one line. A printer who has been asked three times this month for a number on this holding's book by three men who did not ask whether the book was true. And a bed at three and a half degrees, a feed at a twentieth, and a starving that cannot be seen from the top of the field because there is nothing at that end of it to see.*

*An argument. This is worth nothing against a cut.*

---

And then on the twenty-ninth, at about the fourth hour, they all went out to the well house door, because the sowing closes tomorrow and the arrangement's term ends with the closing of the sowing and the fen's practice is that the last day of a month is the day the book is read out, and it had not been read out on the twenty-eighth because the twenty-eighth is a Sunday and the fen reads on the first and the middle and the last, and the last is the thirtieth, which is tomorrow, and this holding is one day out of step with its own rule and has entered that.

And Tova Reed stood at the door with her hand flat on the sheet, on the fen's own words, under the seven columns, and she said the thing that is the question this whole volume has been standing next to, and she said it once, plainly, in a yard, with no history in it at all.

"Who changed the maps," she said.

Nobody in that yard made a sound.

"I have been let off this for four months and I am not going to be let off it again, and I am going to say it out loud at a door on the last day I have got this month, and then this holding is going to stop and do its work.

"Somebody drew a mark on a sheet nine years ago that says a line on the low dry is maintained, and the line has no local holder on that sheet at all, and the mark has a meaning printed under it, and it went out to a hundred and forty seed houses in a winter, and every one of those seed houses has been reading since. And then on the fourteenth of last month this holding told a district office in writing that it is the holder of a line on ground the district maintains, and the answer came on the ninth of last month, and the answer was not a no. The answer was a class of use, and it came on the tenth of this month with a rate on it.

"I have written to them. I did it in a yard, in daylight, in front of eight people, and I entered it against the Dryfoot Trust's own interest, and I would do it again in a morning, and I have never once been able to find out whether the man who drew the mark would have entered it. And that is the question, and it has a second half and the second half is money: who was paid for the alteration, and by whom, and out of what, and how long ago.

"Here is what this holding now knows about it. A class of use with a number at the head of it. A name kept off a road at a fence, by a woman who cannot read. A cutting sheet with a column the trade does not have, written by a buyer who was told there was no column and put one there anyway. A road that carries a sixth of what it says, with the smaller figure written under the larger one in our own hand. A form with a hole in it and a six-digit box that cannot be filled in. A rate of fourpence a crate and a toll of one shilling collected politely at a gate. A hand in a column of a register of authorisations that a woman in this district recognized in a season and has not been asked about and is not going to be. And a printer in Fennmere who has set four hundred pages of our book and who asked for a price in a room with no book in it, and who has been asked three times this month by three different men for a number on it.

"Not one of that is a map. Not one of that is a printer. Not one of that is a reason. It is the shape of the thing and it is on every door in this district now, and the thing itself is nowhere, and I would like whoever picks this book up in four years to know that this holding knew the difference, because a body that mistakes the shape for the thing will go and shout at a printer, and he is a man who has set our type for four months and who has not once been dishonest to us."

And then she took her hand off the door and said the last of it, and it is in the ledger with the hour, and it is nine words.

"Tomorrow the sowing closes, and we do not know."

And the fen entered the question entire, and the clerk read it back, and a person read it back after him, and the person was the man of about fifty, and he read the whole of it including the list of seven things and the sentence about the printer, and he did not soften anything, and nobody thanked anybody, and the standing is that nobody in this yard is thanked for being readable and cannot be.

And the lamp above the seed house table is burning on its living bulb, and the bulb has to be fed tonight and will be fed tomorrow, and there are three books on the table under it and a fourth thing in a shut drawer that has not been opened, and the well house door has a schedule on the right with a measured figure under a declared one and a clause in the fen's own hand under the columns, and a notice on the left with four correct lines in it and four sheets under that with a number at the head of them, and at the north end of the yard there is a bay with a roof of oiled sacking and two boards on the wall and eighty-three days cut into both of them, and it is not full, and the number is still going up.

And a cart has been past that gate twice in a season and has not stopped either time, and nobody in that yard has asked why, and nobody is going to.

And on the low dry there is a fence of thorn and hazel with no gate in it and a post inside it with a copy of the schedule nailed on it, unsigned, saying *eleven households, keeper not named*, and eleven plots in a ring about two hundred yards across that are not good ground, and six crates of a bean whose name is wrong in eleven plots, and a woman of thirty-nine with a list of eleven households on a sheet of lining paper under a stone and no column for where anything came from, and now a second sheet under a second stone with forty-one words on it that she cannot read, and a first person in this volume she has never met, and a road that stops at her fence because a person with a hoe said two words, and the terms of that are written down nowhere on earth.

And the two ends of it are a body with no charter and a settlement with no charter, and there is a book in the middle of it that both of them can read and neither of them has signed, and the rate on it is a shilling a month on a figure five times the true one, and the true one is under the other one in the fen's own hand, and nobody has to go looking for it.

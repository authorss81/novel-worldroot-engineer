# Chapter 495

## At The Second Gate

**One thousand and one hundredweight.**

It came up eight on Saturday with nothing against it and the rise went onto the rises, and it is the two hundred and fifty-eighth figure of a run that has never written the same number down twice since the morning it began, and the count of that run is day minus four hundred and fifty and it did not reset when the month turned.

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the near stone had no daylight under it and had none since the fourth, and the far stone had not moved since the twenty-eighth of a month now two months back, and the pack under the near stone was taken up and put back wet.

Four people were on it from the second hour until about the fifth, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling did the near end with a bar and a wedge and would not let anybody take the other end of that bar, and nobody in this building has ever asked him why a bar wants one man and nobody is going to.

The outlet is a working outlet and it is doing what an outlet does, and the water coming through the wall of it this morning was about the same as it was on Thursday and four inches more than it was on the first of this month, and nobody in this building has ever written a figure against an outlet and nobody is going to start this morning.

There is a pack under the near stone that was put in on the first of this month and has been wet and dry and wet and dry four times since, and a week is the ordinary answer for a pack and it has not been written down anywhere in this building, and the man who put that pack in was asked nothing about it and offered nothing about it.

The woman who keeps the seed house came out at about the fourth hour with a barrow of her own and did not go up the four-mile road, and the barrow belonging to this holding is a different barrow and the journeys of it are eleven and eleven is a floor, and the two barrows are two barrows and their journeys have never been added to one another.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick was on the wall at the north end of the yard from about the third hour to about the sixth and did not go out of the gate, and the man of about seventy was not fetched this morning and is not fetched until the first of the next, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil and is nil at every one of its sites.

The run of mornings carries nothing on this page, and it is on the page that it carries nothing. It is an even morning and the half of that run is not a whole number on an even morning, and neither of the two men who read the boards would read a half out loud, and the second book has nothing in it this morning where the run stands.

The boards stand at **six hundred and forty-two days** and **six hundred and eighty-nine**, and the forty-seven is the same forty-seven and the drawer behind them was not opened, and the man who reads the near side put the chalk down on the shelf and left it there.

The eighteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

The window standing behind the figure is day minus four hundred and eighty-three days wide, and it divides a hundred and eighteen mornings one way and a hundred and seven the other, which is two hundred and twenty-five, and in four years nobody in this room has set either end of it against the figure printed over it.

The four bodies of households are written on this morning's sheet at two hundred and fifty-six days, and the clause under the figure is the two hundred and fifty-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and fifty-seventh, one short of the figure and one over the clause.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour to about the eighth with the door open and nothing in front of him, and the mark on his arm is four inches long and branches twice and is the same four inches it was on Wednesday, and there is no second mark on it and there have been four accounts of that arm in this building and the four do not reconcile with one another and this morning is not a fifth.

A yard is had at the second gate. It is not a new yard and it has been there since before the fen road was metalled, and the gate is a gate and the road beyond it is not a road this holding keeps figures for, and the two men at the gatepost on a Sunday morning are two men and not a body and not a council.

The count of things this holding has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five, and it did not move this morning, and this was a yard, and it is not moved by a figure in a book either.

The man of about fifty was at the gatepost at about the seventh hour and said one thing he had not been asked for and had not known he had, and he gave the reason before the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a gate and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept a road for nine years with nobody's seal on it and I have never once had a body stand at a gatepost and ask me what I intended to do with it."** "Nobody asked me. That is the part I want in the room. I have been waiting thirty-one years for a body with a heading on its own paper to stand at that gate and ask me a question, and it did not happen this morning, and what happened this morning is that a man walked nine miles yesterday and came back with nothing and told nobody."

About four people at the gate heard the whole of it and about four heard the half of it, and nobody has said which four is which and nobody has been asked, and the count of times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard that the engineer of record had not thought of is fifteen and it is sixteen this morning and it is that count and not the other one and the two are not added.

The sixteenth is a count of a thing said in a yard and not a count of anybody, and it is in none of the four books, and the five in the other sentence is a different five and belongs to a different count, and the two counts have never been added to each other anywhere in this building.

Marek was at the north end of the gate wall while that was being said and heard the whole of it, and he did not say anything back and was not asked for anything, and the thing the man of about fifty said is a thing the engineer of record had not thought of, and the engineer of record has never in nine years said out loud that a thing he had not thought of was a thing he had not thought of.

The woman who coordinates the moving reserves was at the gate for about twenty minutes and asked one question of the man of about fifty, and the question was whether anybody had ever asked the bodies on the node what they wanted, and he said no, and she said that was what she thought, and she went back up the coast road without saying it twice.

She is not support staff and has not been asked to be and this holding has not offered her anything, and the count of questions this holding has put to her since the third of this month is nil and is nil, and a body on a node is a neighbour and a neighbour is not an enemy and neither of those is a column.

Four bodies of households signed a charter in a room nine miles off fifty-four days ago, and three of the four are still signed to a machine, and a Sunday morning at a gatepost is not the place to improve on that, and nobody in this yard improved on it and nobody asked anybody to stop drawing and nobody in this yard stopped drawing.

The man who came down nine miles on foot to the tap on the eleventh is still at the seed house and has been on the south wall of it since the morning he came down, and nobody in this building has asked him his name and his name is not in this building and is not going to be put in it.

The man in the cart shed at the mouth of the cut is still in the cart shed, and the man of about fifty went up the coast road on Thursday to find out whether he was still in it and came back on Friday without saying what he found, and this building has not been to that shed and is not going to be, and nobody has asked him a question at any hour of any day about it.

The sheet of terms carries five clauses in two hands and a third column with a heading that has stood over it since the twenty-first of a month now three months back and has nothing in it, and none of the five has been improved and none has been taken back, and a sixth is not being written this morning.

The compost line is paid at twenty-four and not discharged in twenty-four, with the word in front of the figure, and it did not move this morning, and it is not a measure of the ground and it is not a measure of a machine, and it is not going to be moved by a figure in the second book.

There is a thing under a cloth on the shelf in the one room that was put there before this holding kept the second book, and it has not been opened and it has not been removed and it has not been set against anything, and the count of people who have looked at it is two.

The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and a count of uses is not a count of mornings and a gate at which nobody asked a question is not a use and did not go into it this morning.

There were more than five bodies on the node. The number on the page is five, because a count of five is a fact about a rota and not a count of a morning, and it is not given in a yard and it was not given at a gatepost this morning and nobody at that gatepost said how many.

A machine house with about nine hundred yards of rail in it is not a yard, and the yard at the second gate is not a machine house, and the two are not added to each other, and a body that is on a node is not a body in a yard and is not a body at a gate.

Behind the fourth book the day sheets are a foot and a half deep in the order they were used and this morning's went on the top of them at the ninth hour, and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in the middle of that stack in the place it was on the first morning, and a foot and a half of day sheets have gone over the top of it in four years.

The window of the office's work is shut with the shutter on it and the shutter does not come off until the sixth of a month, and the sixth of this month went past at the second hour with the shutter on, and no work is ever carried out of that window and none was carried out of the last one either.

The schedule that came up the fen road on Wednesday is in the long drawer under two books and it has not been taken out since, and nobody in this building has been asked whether the day at the foot of it is still the twenty-second, and the clerk of this holding did not ask and was not asked.

The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is the reader of this body, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and it has not been said to be on by anybody in this holding and it is not said to be on this morning.

The register form is on the middle table at a hundred and fifty-two days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of the column, and it is not going to be read out a fifth time in this building. The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and nobody has been told to take it off that board.

Four ruled lines sit on their own table in the corner under two words entered on the twentieth of a month now four months back, and every one of the four is still empty, and the answer to them is not due, and nothing said at that gatepost this morning has supplied it and nobody tried to.

Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and a figure in the second book is not a seventh and a gate at which nobody asked a question is not a seventh.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and forty-six days old this morning and has nothing on it, and he was on the outlet end from the second hour until the fifth and came in at the ninth and was not asked about it and did not offer, and nobody in this building has asked him about it in a hundred and forty-six days and nobody is going to.

The man of about fifty did not open the third book at any hour this morning and it is shut on the long table with his thumb in it, and the count of reasons given first that he has made in this building is a count of a man and not a column and it is not written in any of the four books.

There was a quarter of an inch of frost in the small hours and none of it on the yard by the seventh hour. The middle road took nothing in the night and gave nothing back, and the north end of the ditch is running full and the outlet at its head is doing what an outlet does, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Nobody in this yard said the figure out loud but the clerk of this holding, and he said it once, and the man of about fifty heard it and wrote nothing, and nothing said in that yard on a Sunday is going to be a speech about a figure in a book.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the eighteenth in at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the second book was shut with the day in it and the two hundred and fifty-six against the four bodies of households, and the shutter on the window of the office's work is still on and no work is ever carried out of one.

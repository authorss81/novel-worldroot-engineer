# Chapter 493

## The Six That Have No Unit

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and it went past unworked for the seventy-seventh morning running, and the embankment is the only line of that rota anybody could name a body for and nobody has ever named one and nobody asked this morning.

The rota stands at one hundred and three mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and seventy-four not, and twenty-nine and seventy-four are a hundred and three, and the standing rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any of the other three this week.

Twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning and has not moved on any morning of this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that has waited, because a field that waits is a fact about a field and not about a man.

Of the rotation there are two reckonings kept and they do not agree, and a page that printed one of them would be printing half a fact, so both are here. On the reckoning made in this room the sixteenth of this month is its fifty-first morning. On the other it is the thirty-eighth.

Between them is thirteen. It has been thirteen for four years and nobody in this holding has ever worked out what the thirteen counts, and neither of the two numbers can be arrived at by taking twenty-nine away from the other.

No figure for the run of mornings stands on this page, and the reason for that is on the page. It is an even morning and the half of the run is not a whole number on an even morning, and the two men who read the boards read their own sides to each other at the seventh hour and there was nothing to read, and the second book has nothing in it this morning where the run stands and the second book is not going to be ruled a column for it.

The boards stand at **six hundred and forty days** and **six hundred and eighty-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened and the man who reads the far side put his thumb on the edge of it and took it away again.

The sixteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and ninety-eight hundredweight.**

It came up eight on Thursday with nothing against it, and the rise went onto the rises, and that is the two hundred and fifty-sixth figure of a run that has never written the same number down twice. The window standing behind it divides a hundred and seventeen mornings one way and a hundred and six the other, which is two hundred and twenty-three, and the two ends of that window have not been set against the figure over them in four years.

The four bodies of households are on this morning's sheet at two hundred and fifty-four days, and the clause under the figure is the two hundred and fifty-third of those mornings out of two hundred and fifty-fifth, a day short and a day over, and a morning is not a household and a morning is not a man.

At about the fourth hour this holding read the well on the shelf, and the reading is four hundred miles and four days off and it is entered as a day and not as a figure, and there is no ordinal against it and no guess against it and no figure of any kind supplied with it.

That is the twelfth time this holding has read it, and the count of day-and-no-figure entries against that well is eleven before this morning and twelve after it, and the number moved on this page and on no other page, and no question has been put to anybody at that far end in eleven years about what the reading is or what it is a reading of.

The seventh column of the well house door nine hundred yards off stands at sixty. It is read on the first of a month and on the thirtieth of a month and on no other morning, and this is a Friday, and the walk of nine hundred yards was not made this morning and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the middle road with a barrow of chalk from the second hour until the fifth.

The six blank columns beside that one stand headed nothing, and the second of the six has four words at the head of it and an empty space under it, and no heading has been written at the head of any of the other four, and what the seventh column counts besides readings has not been answered by anybody in four counties.

Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any one of them, and the seventh row is not ruled. A machine nine miles off is not a seventh and a date at the foot of a sheet is not a seventh and a corridor with a name on it is not a seventh, and nobody in this holding has ruled a row this morning and nobody is going to.

A list of six things you do not know is not a plan. That is said on the page of that schedule and it is not said again, and the six are six and the seventh is not ruled and the unit is not supplied for any of the six and has never been supplied for any of the six.

The corridor in the pan is named, and naming it is not answering it, and it is not established as on, and a culvert under a machine house nine miles off is not that corridor and a culvert is not a bridge and the two are not added. The sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and it is correct in every particular and it cannot be put down by anybody in this holding, and nobody put it down this morning and nobody is going to.

The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is the reader of this body. There are two who can hold a pattern for four and one who can hold it for five and there are two rootmarks, and the count of pattern-holders is not a count of ends, and two men on a wheel are on neither of those two lists and are not on this one either.

No instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off. It has not been said to be on by anybody in this holding and it is not said to be on this morning, and the count of questions this holding has put at either far end is nil and is nil, and a machine house with about nine hundred yards of rail in it is not a bridge and the two are not added.

Marek read the sixth column of the seed house's schedule standing in the seed house doorway at about the fifth hour and did not go in and did not touch it, and the mark on his arm is four inches long and branches twice and there is no second mark and there have been four accounts of that arm in this building and the four do not reconcile with one another and nothing this morning is a fifth.

The register form has been on the middle table for a hundred and fifty days. It is one sheet with a column on it and nothing at the head of the column, and it is not filled and it is not refused and it has not been moved, and it is not going to be read out a fifth time in this building and nobody has read it out a fifth time.

The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and it has been there since the nineteenth of a month now four months back, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board and a schedule under a weight is not a ground for taking it off.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and forty-four days old this morning and has nothing on it, and he was on the middle road until the fifth hour and came in at the ninth and was not asked about it and did not offer.

He put the chalk down at the wall and stood with his back to the yard and looked at the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres, which is in its eleventh year, and nobody in this holding has ever set foot inside it and nobody did this morning and it has no dimension against it on any page a county could check.

A body with a heading on its own paper wrote into this holding on the fifteenth asking for a figure of draw to be certified, and the requests column took it and is fifty-three, and a corridor with a name on it is not a request, and a defect is not a question, and a document is not a question, and none of those three is going into a column in this building this morning.

Nobody in this yard fought anybody. There is nothing in any of it to fight about, and two bodies of households with a charter and a subscription are not on opposite sides of anything and the two sheets on the long table are about nine inches apart and are about nine inches apart because nobody has ever moved either of them.

The man of the north row with the cough was at the low field gate from about the third hour with a bar and did the top of it alone, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything and he was not asked this morning, and the four ruled lines under his condition are still empty and the answer is not due.

He said one thing at the gate to nobody in particular, and it was about the top bar, and it was that the bar is the bar and the post is the post and the nine minutes is the nine minutes, and nobody asked him which of the three he meant and he did not say the rest of it.

Marek put his hand flat on the outside of the brick wall of the well house at about the sixth hour and stood there about a minute with his face to the mortar, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came round the corner with the barrow and stopped and waited and did not ask him what he was doing, because nobody in this holding has ever asked him what anybody is doing at that wall.

What he got off that wall in about a minute is a thing he can do and not a thing he can put in a book, and he has been getting things off walls for nine years, and the two of those facts have never been in the same sentence in this holding and they were not in the same sentence this morning.

**"I have not read it and I am not going to read it on a Friday."** The man of about thirty-one of Silling said that. **"The rule is the first and the thirtieth and there is a reason for the rule and the reason is that a door read on the wrong morning is a door read for nothing, and a man who has walked nine hundred yards twice a week for four years can afford one Friday."**

Nobody in this building has asked him why the rule is the first and the thirtieth and he did not say, and the count of times this holding has asked him about the seventh column is nil and is nil, and what that column counts besides readings has not been answered by anybody in four counties.

The woman who keeps the seed house put the schedule of the seed house on the middle table face up at about the seventh hour with the sixth rule turned toward the man who was nearest it, and nobody turned it over and nobody asked her to point at the rule, and the six rows are ruled and the sixth of them has about half a line of writing in the clerk's hand against it and the fifth of them has nothing whatever against it and neither of those two is a seventh.

The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, and the man who keeps it has not been asked to add one and was not asked and has not offered, and a count of uses is not a count of mornings and the two are not added anywhere in this building.

The window of the office's work is shut with the shutter on it and the shutter comes off on the sixth of a month and the sixth of this month went past at the second hour without anybody taking it off, because the sixth of this month is ten days before this one and the shutter is a shutter and nobody in this building has ever asked who takes it off.

Behind the fourth book the day sheets are a foot and a half deep in the order they were used, and this morning's went on the top at the ninth hour, and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in the middle of that stack in the place it was on the first morning.

The woman who coordinates the moving reserves came into the yard at about the fourth hour and asked the man of about thirty-one of Silling one question, which was whether the shelf would take a cart, and he said it would not and she said she had not thought it would and neither of them asked the other a second question.

She is not support staff and has not been asked to be and this holding has not offered her anything, and the count of questions this holding has put to her since the third of this month is nil and is nil, and she said the number of districts that draw on the stored light out loud and the number of people in them and said neither of them twice.

A list of districts is a list of neighbours and not a list of enemies, and she said so, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said nothing at all to that and went back to the barrow, and a person saying a true thing about a list of neighbours is not making a speech in a yard and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move.

There was no rain in the night and the yard was dry at the second hour and dry at the ninth, and the middle road had taken nothing and given nothing, and the ditch at the north end is running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The seed vault of forty households under four hundred yards off has not been recovered, and there is no figure of people against it and there has not been one since a month now two months back, and nobody has been down and nobody is going to be, and it is not four hundred yards from the ring and nobody has compared the two distances.

Nobody fought anybody and nobody in this yard said the word villain about anything. A machine that is right about what it prunes is not thereby good. A cut that is real is not thereby cruel. Two facts, and this building does not put them in one sentence and did not this morning.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the sixteenth in at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the well on the shelf went into the fourth book as a day and as nothing else, and the door nine hundred yards off is still standing at sixty with a Friday gone by it and nothing read off it.

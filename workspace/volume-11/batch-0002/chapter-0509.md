# Chapter 509

## Two Rates And No Total

The fourth line came round at the second hour, and the headland above Marden had nobody at it again, and that is the eighty-first morning it has gone by unworked. The standing is a hundred and seven mornings in force, twenty-nine taken, seventy-eight not, and the two of the last figures add to the first. It moves one on a morning this line comes round and it does not move at all on the other three.

Twenty-nine taken has not moved on any morning of this year and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited.

The two reckonings of the rotation are both on this page and they do not agree. Kept in this room, the second of the next is the fifty-fifth morning of the rotation. Kept the other way, the same morning is the forty-second. Four years of thirteen sitting in the gap, and nobody in this holding has worked out what the thirteen counts, and it does not arrive by taking the twenty-nine off either figure.

Mist came off the fen in the night and was on the yard at the second hour and was gone by the fifth, and the grass at the gate had gone silver on the top and the frost had not got into it at all.

The second went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and twenty-two hundredweight.**

Eight on Saturday and nothing against it, and the rise went onto the rises, and the run stands at its two hundred and seventy-second figure with nothing written in it twice since the morning it began. The window this office keeps behind that run read a hundred and twenty-five mornings one way and a hundred and fourteen the other, which is two hundred and thirty-nine, and the man of about fifty has had both ends of that window in front of him four years running and has never once asked which of the two the schedule nine miles off is written against.

This morning's sheet has the four bodies of households at two hundred and seventy days, and the clause underneath at the two hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings out of two hundred and seventy-first, and a leaf with a rate on it came up the road with the post while both of those numbers were still on the table.

The two boards are at **six hundred and fifty-six days** and **seven hundred and three**. Forty-seven, unchanged, and the drawer was not opened, and neither man who reads them has ever said aloud why the far one is so much further along than the near one.

No figure for the run of mornings appears on this page, and the reason stands in the same line as the rest of it: an even morning puts a half number in that run, and both men who read the boards were at the far end of the yard this morning, and so nothing was read and the second book is blank where the run goes.

The frame of that run is of a month seven months back and it has been since yesterday morning, when the change was said once, and this morning is an even one and carries no figure for the run to put a frame on at all.

The leaf came up the fen road with the post at about the eighth hour and it is the third thing to come up that road inside a week, and it is a leaf and not a letter and it has no heading at the head of it and no name at the foot of it.

> THE LEAF THAT CAME UP THE FEN ROAD WITH THE POST ON THIS MORNING, IN A HAND THAT IS NOT ANY HAND IN THIS BUILDING, ON ONE SHEET, WITH NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF IT: **THE RATE AT WHICH THE FIGURES IN THE SCHEDULE ARE SET AGAINST WHAT IS HELD IS A RATE PER HOUR PER LINE AND IT IS THE RATE THE SCHEDULE HAS ALWAYS USED. A HOLDING THAT KEEPS ITS FIGURES IN DAYS CANNOT SET ONE AGAINST THE OTHER WITHOUT A CONVERSION, AND NO CONVERSION IS GIVEN HERE AND NONE IS ASKED FOR. THE FIGURES ON THE SCHEDULE ARE THE FIGURES ON THE SCHEDULE. NOTHING IN THIS SHEET IS A TOTAL AND NOBODY IS ASKED FOR ONE.** THERE IS NO COLUMN HEADED FOR A TOTAL AND NO COLUMN HEADED FOR A RATE IN DAYS.

The man of about fifty read it twice at the long table and then put it down on top of the two leaves that came on Monday, and the three of them are a stack, and a stack of three correct sheets is not a column and this building has not ruled one.

**"There is a rate on that and it is not a rate this holding uses, and I am not going to convert it, and I am not going to put a figure of my own against theirs and call it a check."** He said it to the stack and then he said the rest of it. **"A thing I cannot check and will not convert is a thing I hold open. That is all it is. I have four books and one of them is going to have a piece of paper in it that says we do not know what their rate is, and that is an honest line and I have never written one before and I am not proud of it."**

The clerk of this holding came to the long table and put his hand flat on the leaf and asked where it was going, drawer or shelf, and the man of about fifty said the long drawer without looking up, and then he said one more thing which was that a piece of paper that arrives and is correct is the only kind this building has ever had to deal with, and that he has a shelf of the other kind in his head and has had it for thirty-one years.

A woman from the second gate came in at about the ninth hour with a jug and filled the jug at the standpipe and went out again and did not come into the one room and did not ask what was on the table, and nobody in this building has ever asked her what she knows about any of it.

Nobody at that table asked him to write anything down and he did not write it and did not say he was going to, and the clerk did not ask him a second question, and the count of questions this holding has put him this morning is one and it is one and it was about a drawer.

Tova Reed was at the middle table from the second hour with the four counties' sheets and the sowing date, and a rate per hour per line is not a rate any of the four councils has ever used either, and the three councils that gave her a date in writing did not give it against that rate and did not say they would.

**"If somebody tells me a rate I have never been given before and asks me to hold a sowing date against it, I am going to say that I hold a date and not a rate."** She said that once and stopped, and she is the seed front in four counties and has not been thanked for it this morning and is not going to be.

This holding uses days and the machine uses an hour and a line, and the two of them have not been reconciled and are not going to be reconciled this month by anybody in this building, and this page uses neither rate and states nothing about any total of any kind.

Nia Vale was in the yard at about the fifth hour and did not come in, and the count of districts that draw on the stored light is in her own book and it was not said in this yard and it was not written in the second book, and a list of neighbours is a list of neighbours and is not added to a rate.

Four accounts of that arm exist in this building and four is what four is, and nothing came off the road this morning that would make a fifth.

Marek was at the long wall with his back to the room for about half an hour in the middle of the morning and the clerk of this holding did not ask him what he was doing and he was not asked and did not offer, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil.

The leaf is still on the long table under the weight, and a rate per hour per line tells this building nothing whatever about the far end of a thing, and two people hold that end and neither of them is him.

The mist came back at about the fifth hour and sat on the low field and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the north wall with frost in his coat and stood at the tap until the water came through warm.

The man who keeps the use log came in with the log under his arm while the leaf was still on the table, read it standing up from about halfway down, and said that he had been handed a figure once in four years and had never been handed the unit that went with it, and that this leaf was the same kind of thing. The reason for the fifteen has been given twice in four years and nobody has asked for it since, including this morning, and the row for a rate nobody here can convert was not ruled and is not going to be.

The clerk of this holding put the leaf on the long table with the other two and neither column moved, and a leaf with a rate on it is in neither of them, and the two stand at fifty-three and have never been added together.

The middle table has had the form on it a hundred and sixty-six days and the head of the column is still bare, and the low board is exactly where it was, and a rate this holding cannot convert is no reason to move anything.

Nothing has been entered in the session book this month and the count is seven, and the four ruled lines in the corner are still empty and nothing at the long table this morning was an answer to them.

A leaf with a rate on it is not a ground for settling either of the two declinings and neither of them moved this morning.

A man fetched yesterday morning is not fetched again for thirty mornings, and the old man with the stick went out to the gate at the sixth hour and watched the road for a while and came back in.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the north wall with a bar from the third hour until the sixth and came in at the ninth and was not asked about the leaf and did not offer, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and sixty days old this morning and has nothing on it.

The clerk of this holding went out to the gate at the tenth hour to close it against the mist and came back in with his sleeves wet to the elbow and put the second book down on the table and did not open it, and a clerk with wet sleeves and a shut book is a morning like any other morning.

**"I have been at that door four years and I have never once been asked what the number on it is a number of, and I have never once wanted to know until this month."** He said that to the bar and he said it before he said anything else and he did not give a reason first because he did not have one ready, and nobody in this yard noticed that he had not and the count of reasons given first this morning is not a count anybody in this building keeps.

He went back out to the north wall after that and worked it until the sixth hour and did not come in again until the ninth, and he had a paper in his pocket all morning and did not take it out and did not throw it away.

**"You cannot mix a day into that and you cannot leave it out, and the leaving out is the only honest thing anybody here is going to do with it."** He said that to the bar when he put it back on the wall and then he said the second half of it to the wall. **"I have carried a slate to that door four years and put a number in front of the clerk every time, and I have never once had to know what the number was a number of, and if I start wanting to know, that is the day I stop being the man who carries it."**

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the seed house door for about ten minutes at the fourth hour with a sheet of her own in her hand and did not come out with it and did not put it on the long table, and the clerk of this holding did not ask her about it and was not asked.

Nobody in that room asked him a second question about the number and he did not offer one and nobody improved on the first thing he said.

The seventh column of the well house door nine hundred yards off was read yesterday morning at sixty-two and it is not read this morning, because the rule is the first and the thirtieth, and this is neither, and no figure for that door is on this page.

Two of them hold a pattern for four and one holds it for five, and there are two rootmarks, and none of those three counts has moved. A count of pattern-holders is not a count of ends and it is not a count of lines on a rate sheet nine miles off either.

Eleven journeys is what the barrow has made and a Sunday on the fen is not a reason for a twelfth.

Three leaves that come up a road with the post are not work carried out of a window and the shutter is on.

He was still at the wall when the leaf went on the table, and a rate per hour per line is not a unit against any of the six and no unit has been supplied for it this morning.

The clerk of this holding described the leaf in one line on this morning's day sheet and did not name it, and the undated sheet in the middle of the stack did not move, and the woman who keeps the seed house was still in the seed house door with her own sheet in her hand and it did not come to the table.

The man of about fifty read the leaf once more at the seventh hour and the rate on it was the same rate as at the sixth, and a rate per hour per line is not a measure of the compost line and the compost line did not move this morning.

The mist came off the fen before light and the yard was dry again by the ninth hour. The middle road took nothing and gave nothing back, the north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Three of the four bodies of households that signed a charter in a room nine miles off seventy-two days ago are still signed to a machine for stored light, and a subscription runs against a rate this holding does not use, and the two of those are true at the same time on a Sunday morning and neither of them is going to be said twice.

The man of the north row with the cough came in at the seventh, read the leaf from the top down and put it back the way it was, and said nothing about it to anybody.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the second in at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the three leaves are on the long table under one weight, and the three of them agree with each other and not one of them agrees with a book in this building, and this morning is not going to be the morning anybody works out how to make them agree.

There is a hammer sound from the machine side of the fen at about the seventh hour that goes on for a minute and a half and then stops, and nobody in this building has asked what it is and nobody is going to, and a sound is not an instrument and a sound is not a figure.

The man with the slate was not in this building this morning and nobody here has asked where he was and nobody is going to, and the slate he left behind on Friday is still leaning against the shelf in the one room where he put it down.

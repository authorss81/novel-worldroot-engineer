# Chapter 502

## Two Doors And A Name She Has Not Said In Public

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and it is the water: the north end of the ditch, the trough at the head of the four-mile road and the standpipe at the yard. The trough had about two inches of leaf in it and the man of about thirty-one of Silling went through it with a bar and came out with the bar black to the second handhold, and he put the bar back on the wall and did not say where he had put it and nobody looked for it.

The man of about fifty said one thing at the trough that the engineer of record had not thought of, and he said it to the water and not to anybody, and the count of times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is sixteen and this morning is a trough and not a yard, and the count did not move.

At the seventh hour the two men who read the boards said their own sides at each other and neither of them said the other's half, and the run of mornings came out of that at **one hundred and three mornings of one hundred and eighty-nine**, of a month six months back.

The twenty-fifth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and five hundredweight.**

Five under Sunday, and the fall went onto the falls. That makes it the two hundred and sixty-fifth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been written twice, and the count of the run is off the day and not off the month. The clerk of this holding read the total back once and then had to go and find the window behind it, which divides a hundred and twenty-one mornings one way and a hundred and eleven the other and comes to two hundred and thirty-two, and neither end of that window has ever been set against the figure printed over it in this building.

Two hundred and sixty-three days is what the morning sheet says for the four bodies of households, and the clause under it takes the two hundred and sixty-second of those mornings out of two hundred and sixty-fourth. The man of about fifty read both figures off the same sheet while a woman was standing at the other end of the room with a list of eighty-one families in front of her, and he did not put the two documents together and neither did anybody else.

The two boards came up to **six hundred and forty-nine days** and **six hundred and ninety-six**, forty-seven between them as ever, and the man who reads the far side put his thumb on the drawer and took it away again.

A charter with four clauses was signed in a room nine miles off sixty-five days ago and it gave a body of households a real right to decline an inspection, and three of those four are still signed to a machine, and both of those things are on this page and neither of them is going to be improved on.

The room is over the tap shop at the second gate and the body of households that wrote the notice has used it since before this holding kept a second book, and it has two doors off it and both of them were standing open from the seventh hour, one onto the yard and one onto the lane. A room with one door is a room a person cannot leave without asking somebody. A room with two doors is a room a person can walk out of, and Sera Quill used the second one twice.

Nobody in that room counted the people in it and nobody in that room was counted, and some of the people on the coast road that morning came the whole two hours and some came an hour, and nobody has said who came the whole of it and nobody was asked, and a count of who came is not a count of anybody.

She was on her feet at the back of it for about a quarter of an hour before she began, and she is fifty-six and she came up the fen road on the second bus and she is a records witness and not an eyewitness, and she said so before anybody asked her.

**"I am a records witness and not a witness to anything that happened. I did not see it. I read what four people wrote at the time and what two people signed at the end of it, and everything I am going to say this morning comes out of paper."** She said that with her hands flat on the table and then she said the rest of it, and nobody in that room said a word.

**"Nineteen years ago there was a failure in the Salt March and it is on the maps and it has a name, and the name is the Brinewake relay, and I put that name in front of this room this morning and I am not going to be asked to say it a second time in a building."**

**"The pulse went out on a line I helped design. I designed a safety that would have cut that pulse off from three of the western corridors, and I took the red line out of the warning on the day, and I wrote routine recalibration in the log where the red line had been, and I signed a report that put salt creep and weather in it."** She said that without stopping and without looking at anybody, and then she stopped and started again.

**"Four thousand two hundred people died in the first winter and nine thousand more went out of their houses, and I was not a witness to any of it and I am one of the people whose paper made it slower to notice."**

The room was quiet in a way this room has not been quiet. The seed house woman put her pen down. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not write. Two clerks of bodies of households sat with their hats on their knees and neither of them said a word.

Sera Quill took a folded sheet out of the inside of her coat and put it on the table and opened it, and it is not signed by her, and the list on it is in four hands.

> THE RESTITUTION AS IT WAS WRITTEN OUT AND READ ALOUD IN THE ROOM OVER THE TAP SHOP AT THE SECOND GATE, IN HER OWN HAND, ON ONE SHEET, WITH NO HEADING AT THE HEAD OF IT AND A LINE AT THE FOOT OF IT AND NOTHING UNDER THE LINE: **EIGHTY-ONE NAMES, GIVEN AS FAMILIES AND NOT AS PERSONS, IN THE ORDER THEY WERE ENTERED. A SUM, WHICH IS WHAT REMAINED OF THE COMPENSATION RECORD AFTER THE COUNTS THAT WERE KEPT AND THE COUNTS THAT WERE NOT. A PLACE, WHICH IS A FIELD AT THE WESTERN END OF A CORRIDOR AND A HEDGEROW AND A STONE. THE SEAL LOG IS NAMED IN THIS SHEET AND NOT PRODUCED IN THIS ROOM AND IT IS HELD WHERE IT IS HELD. THE COMPENSATION RECORD WAS OPENED ON THE DAY THIS SHEET WAS WRITTEN AND NOT BEFORE, AND THE FIGURE ON IT IS THE FIGURE ON IT.** THERE IS NO HEADING ON THIS SHEET AND NO RANK UNDER THE SUM AND NO OFFICE NAMED ANYWHERE ON IT AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM ASKED FOR A TITLE.

She read the eighty-one names out loud in a flat voice and it took her four minutes, and there was no room in that building for the eighty-one of them, and the list is a list and not a roll and not a column and it went into no book in this holding. It is in a county book four counties off and this holding has a copy of nothing.

The first clerk of a body of households asked her one question at the end of it and gave the reason for asking it out loud before he asked it, the way people in this room do things. He asked whether the eighty-one were the whole of the people, and she said that they were the whole of the people the compensation record had ever carried, and that the record had been carried by two men and that one of the two had died and that she had not gone and looked for a third.

**"I am not asking you whether it is enough. I am asking you whether it is the whole of what your paper says, because a body of households signs things and we need to know what we are signing against."** He said that with the sheet in front of him and did not put a word to anybody and did not put a name to anybody, and the sheet on the table was correct and he did not call it anything.

Three of the four bodies of households are still signed to a machine for stored light, and one of the two clerks in that room has drawn on that light in three of the last four winters, and the two facts were in one room for about two hours and neither of them cancelled the other and nobody in that room asked anybody to give up either one.

A body with two needs and one set of hands is a fact about a body. It is not a fact about a machine nine miles off and it is not a fact about a woman standing at a table with a list, and the three bodies are not villains this morning and they are not fools, and no one in that room was asked to choose between the two documents they have signed.

Nobody in that room said that she had been forgiven and nobody said that she had not, and nobody thanked her, and nobody touched anybody's arm, and the count of questions this holding has put to the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went from two to three in the yard afterward, which was asked by the reader of this body and which is the only question of hers in this month.

**"You have said that number every time you have been in this room for four years and nobody here has ever been told what it counts."** He said it in the yard with the lane door open behind him.

**"Is it sacks, or is it days, or is it neither of those and you have been carrying it because there was nowhere to put it down."** She said that it was the second of those three, and that it was a number of days, and that the unit had never been established by anybody in a position to establish it.

She said that she had entered the same thing as not known six times, and that a seventh row had been asked for twice in four years and had not been ruled either time, and that she was not going to be the one to rule it.

**"I have said it out loud four years and I have never had to say what it was a number of."** She said it to the yard and not to him. **"It is days. There is no unit against it in any book in this county and there is not going to be one, and you can put that in the second book or you can leave it out, and the six not knowns are six and the seventh row is not ruled."**

The sheet that claims the corridor was produced in that room at about the eleventh hour by a man who put it on the table face up and read the heading of it and stopped.

It is correct in every particular, in the drawer in this building and in that room over a tap shop, and it lets nothing out, and it has not been advanced and it has not been reworded and nobody in this holding can put it down.

The order sealing the route is in the next drawer and it is valid and it opens nothing, and no apology is going to be written for it by anybody in this holding, and none was written this morning.

The man of about fifty was on the middle road from the second hour until the sixth and did not come up to the tap shop, and he did not read the sheet about the corridor and did not hear the eighty-one names, and nobody went and got him and nobody sent word.

**"There is a thing a man will say in a room with two doors in it that he will not say in a room with one."** He said that to the fill on the middle road at about the seventh hour to nobody at all.

**"I have watched it happen eleven years and I have never once been able to write down which of the two was the room that did it."** He said the second half of it to the chalk and not to the room, and the count of things this holding has said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it did not move, and this was a road and not a yard.
Nobody measured the mark on his arm this morning, and there was no reason to, and it is the same four inches it was on the fourteenth.

He had his hand on the door frame of the tap shop for about a minute at the twelfth hour with his face turned away from the room, and the woman who keeps the seed house was inside with the schedule and the six ruled rows and she did not go out and she did not go in and she was in that building for the whole of it.

A number of days with no unit against it is one of the six and the seventh row is not ruled.

A list of eighty-one families in four hands is a piece of paper and the far end of a thing is held by two other people entirely, and this building does not add them.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and fifty-three days old and has nothing on it, and he drank a cupful standing up and did not go up the lane to the tap shop, and nobody asked him why.

The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth for eighty-one names read out loud in a room.

The day sheets stand behind the fourth book a foot and a half deep in the order they were used, this morning's went on the top at the ninth hour with one line under the day and no name against the line, and the sheet with no date at the head of it is still in the middle of that stack in the place it was on the first morning.

The form has been on the middle table a hundred and fifty-nine days with nothing at the head of the column, and the paper on the low board is where it has been since a month now four months back, and eighty-one families did not take it off and nobody asked them to.

The requests column is fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and they are a different list, and neither of them moved this morning and the two of them have never been added to one another. A confession is not a request and a confession is not an offer and a list of eighty-one names is not a rung of anything.

A dry morning from the second hour to the ninth, with no rain in the night and no frost on the ground at all, and the north end of the ditch running full off the back of the first line, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road full by the seventh hour and inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding stood in the gate with the second book under his arm while that room emptied and did not write down the number of them, and the woman who keeps the seed house carried the schedule out in the same oilskin satchel it came in on Saturday and put it back under the four pound weight.

Nobody apologized to anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the twenty-fifth in at about the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and he wrote one line under it, which was that a name was said in a room over the tap shop at the second gate, and he did not write the name, and the shutter on the window of the office's work is still on and no work is ever carried out of one.

# Chapter 505

## The Ring, And Nobody Inside It

The fourth line came round again at the second hour with the same nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and that is the eightieth morning it has gone past. One hundred and six mornings in force, twenty-nine of them taken, seventy-seven of them not, and the twenty-nine and the seventy-seven come to the hundred and six. The count climbs by one on a morning this line comes round and stands still on the other three.

Nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited, and the figure of the fields that have been taken has not shifted on any morning of this year, and a field that waits is a fact about a field.

Both reckonings of the rotation get written down, because a page that carried one of them would be carrying half a fact. The reckoning kept in this room makes the twenty-eighth of this month the fifty-fourth morning of it. The other reckoning makes the same day the forty-first. The thirteen between the two figures has been thirteen for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever found out what it counts.

Frost had got into the ground by the second hour and stayed in it, half an inch of it by the ninth and the yard hard enough to ring a bar off, and the water in the standpipe had a skin on it that the man of about thirty-one of Silling broke with the heel of his hand and let run.

The twenty-eighth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and sixteen hundredweight.**

Eight on Tuesday, and nothing whatever against it, so the rise went onto the rises. That is the two hundred and sixty-eighth figure of a run that has never put the same number down twice since the morning it began. The window this office keeps behind that figure reads a hundred and twenty-three mornings one way and a hundred and twelve the other, which is two hundred and thirty-five, and the woman who keeps the seed house has asked twice in four years which of the two ends of it is the end that matters, and nobody has told her.

Two hundred and sixty-six days is what the sheet says for the four bodies of households, and the clause printed under it takes the two hundred and sixty-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and sixty-seventh, and the clerk of this holding wrote the day down and wrote the clause down and did not say the one out loud against the other.

**Six hundred and fifty-two days** on the near board and **six hundred and ninety-nine** on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the chalk went on the near side and came off again and the drawer stayed where it is.

The run of mornings carries no figure here and the page says why. This is an even morning, and the half of that run is half a number on an even morning, and both of the men who read the boards were nine hundred yards off at the well house door instead, and so the run was not read at all this morning and the second book is empty where it stands.

Marek went out to the edge of the ring inside the eleven acres at about the sixth hour with the frost still on it, and he stood on the grass outside it and looked at it, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came out of the gate behind him about forty minutes later and stood about six feet back from where he was standing and did not come up to him.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has been bare in its eleventh year. Nobody in this holding has ever been inside it and nobody was inside it this morning and nobody put a foot in it, and it was not walked this morning and it was not measured and it was not crossed and it was not priced by walking round it and it has not been explained by anybody in four years.

**"I have been out here twice this year and I have not gone in either time, and I am not going to go in this week, and I would like it written that that is a decision and not a fear."** He said that to the woman behind him and not to the ring, and he gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and fifty-eight, and it is a count of a man who has said a thing to a shut book, and not a column. **"The reason is that I have spent nine years learning what a thing is like from the outside and I know exactly what I would do if I were standing in the middle of it, and I would do it, and then it would be a thing I had been inside and not a thing this holding has never been inside."**

She said one thing back to him about it and it was that a thing this holding has never been inside is a thing that has never been measured by anybody, including the woman in the middle of it, and then she said nothing else and went back in through the gate.

She had come out with her coat over her arm and no gloves on and she stood with her hands in her sleeves for the forty minutes, and the frost took the wet off the front of her coat while she stood there, and nobody in this yard asked her to go in and nobody in this yard asked her to stay.

The frost had stopped short of the ring's edge and had not touched the ring at all, and there is a place on the north side of it where the grass does not come back up in the spring either, and that place has not been looked at from close to by anybody in this holding and was not looked at from close to this morning.

The corridor in the pan nine miles off is named and naming it is not answering it and it is not established as on, and a culvert under a machine house is not that corridor and a ring of bare ground in eleven acres is not that corridor, and none of the three is in this building and the three of them are not added to one another.

The seed vault of forty households under four hundred yards off has not been recovered, and nobody has been down and nobody is going to be, and there is no figure of people against it and there has not been one since a month now two months back, and the four hundred yards is not the same figure as anything and is not the same figure as the ring.

Nobody in this yard said the word curse about it and nobody said the word scar and nobody said the word wound, and a name for a piece of ground is not a dimension for it, and there is no figure against it on any page a county could check.

Five is what this holding has said out loud in a yard and got wrong and it did not move this morning, and this morning was a yard, and a man standing at the edge of a thing saying out loud that he is not going in is not one of the five.

The count of times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is sixteen, and this morning it did not move either, and about four people in this yard heard the whole of what the man of about fifty said to the third book and about four heard the half of it, and nobody has said which four is which and nobody was asked.

The man of about fifty came out of the long room at about the eighth hour to get the barrow key and stopped at the gate and looked at the ring for about a minute and went back in and said nothing, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this morning is nil and is nil.

Marek said one thing at the gate on his way in, and it was about the frost and not about the ring, and what he said was that the north side of that ground has never held a covering of frost, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said that he had never noticed it either and that he had never looked.

He had one thing to say about it and he said it to the third book on the long table with his thumb in it, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fifty-three, and this holding has never turned that number into a figure against anybody.

**"I have been asked twice this month by people who wanted to know what is in the middle of it, and I have not told either of them that I do not know, and I am not going to start telling them this week."** He said it to the book and shut the book. **"A thing that is not measured is not a thing anybody has a figure for, and the moment a body in this county has a figure for it, the first question out of that body is going to be what the figure is for, and then it will be a column, and I have watched a piece of paper turn into a column four times in four years and I am not going to be the one who makes the fifth."**

Nobody in that room asked him which paper he meant and he did not say and was not asked.

He was on the outlet end from the second hour until the fifth, came in at the ninth, and was not asked about the ring and did not offer. The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and fifty-six days old this morning and has nothing on it.

A cart came up the lane at about the seventh hour with nothing in it but two men and a ladder, and the driver asked the man of about thirty-one of Silling whether there was anywhere to stand a ladder, and he said the north wall would do and no better than that, and the cart went to the north end of the yard and stood there until noon.

They put the ladder against the wall at the north end and came down again at the ninth hour and went off up the lane, and nobody in this holding has ever asked what they were looking at.

**"There is a place at the north side of it where the frost has not taken the grass and it has not taken the grass in nine years either, and I have walked past it a great many times and I have not put my hand on it."** He said that to the bar when he put it back on the wall, and nobody asked him what he thought was under it and he did not say and nobody in this building is going to ask him.

A ring of bare ground is not the far end of a thing, and the far end is held by two people who are not him and it is not said to be on.

Two can hold a pattern for four and one can hold it for five, and there are two rootmarks, and a count of pattern-holders is not a count of ends, and nobody has ever stood in the middle of that ring holding either end of anything.

Four inches on Marek's arm, branching twice, and the same four inches it was on the fourteenth, and no second mark anywhere on it, and the four accounts this building has of that arm are four and they do not reconcile with one another and this morning did not make a fifth.

The old man with the stick worked the north wall in the frost from the third hour to the sixth and came in with frost in the eyebrows, and he is not a man this holding keeps figures about, and nobody in this building has ever asked him for one.

The seventh column of the well house door nine hundred yards off is read on the first of a month and on the thirtieth of a month and on no other morning, and this is a Wednesday, and the walk of nine hundred yards was not made this morning and no figure for that door is on this page at all.

The four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are still empty and the answer to them is not due, and the two words above them were entered on the twentieth of a month now four months back and have not been improved.

Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's schedule with no unit against any one of them, and the seventh row is not ruled, and a ring of bare ground is not a seventh and no row has been ruled for it this morning and nobody is going to.

The form on the middle table is a hundred and sixty-two days old and the head of its column says nothing, which is the only thing about it that has changed. The offer on the low board has no date and no line for a name and has not moved since a month now four months back.

A ring of bare ground is not a request and a request is not a note in section nine, and the two columns stand at fifty-three each and have never been added. The man who keeps the use log came past the gate on his way to the shed about an hour after Marek went in, looked across at the ring through the gate from the outside of it, kept walking, and did not put a line in the book, and the log stayed at fifteen lines.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at about the sixth hour and came down loaded, and the number of journeys that barrow has made is eleven and eleven is a floor, and the four miles are not the nine and the nine are not the coast road and the three have never been added in this building.

It fell on the twenty-seventh and did not move again this morning, and a ring of bare ground is not the compost line and the two are not added.

Marek was still in the yard when the clerk came out to shut the gate for the night, and neither declining moved, and a man standing at the edge of a thing is not a ground for settling either of them.

The oilskin satchel is on the long table with the seed schedule back in it and the six ruled rows are six ruled rows and the seventh row is not ruled, and a schedule that went nine miles and came back in a satchel is not a column and the satchel is not a bag of anything anybody in this building weighs.

The shutter on the window of the office's work came off on the sixth and went back on and nobody has ever asked who takes it off, and no work is ever carried out of that window.

A man stood at the edge of a thing this morning and a sheet went onto the stack behind the fourth book at the ninth hour, and the undated one did not move.

There was no rain and the frost stayed in the ground all morning, and the middle road took nothing in the night and gave nothing back, and the north end of the ditch is running full and the outlet at its head is doing what an outlet does, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The sun came over the north wall at about the eighth hour and did not reach the ring, which is a thing nobody has measured and which is not going to be measured this month by anybody.

No one in that yard said the word thank you to anybody, in either direction. The twenty-eighth went into the second book at the ninth hour and came back out again the same, and the shutter on the window of the office's work is still on and no work is ever carried out of one, and a man stood at the edge of a thing this morning and told a woman in a yard why he was not going in, and he is still outside it.

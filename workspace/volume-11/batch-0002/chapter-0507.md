# Chapter 507

## The Thirtieth, And A Column With A Heading On It

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling lifted the pack out from under the near stone, looked at it, and put it back wet, and said the word wet on the second morning of doing that and nobody asked him what he meant by it.

The light came up at about the fourth hour this morning and went off the yard at about the fourth hour in the afternoon, and that is what the thirtieth of a month does in this country and nobody in this holding has ever written a figure against it.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling walked the nine hundred yards to the well house door at about the seventh hour with the slate and read the seventh column off the door, and it stands at sixty-one.

It is read on the first of a month and on the thirtieth of a month and on no other morning, and this is the thirtieth, and the rule is the first and the thirtieth, and this reading is on the rule and is not a departure. It stood at sixty on the last morning it was read, and the figure is on the door and nowhere else, and no figure for that door is on this page other than the one.

The six blank columns beside that one stand headed nothing. The second of the six has four words at the head of it written four years back and an empty space under the four words, and no heading has been written at the head of any of the other four, and nobody in this building is going to write one this month.

What the seventh column counts besides readings has not been answered by anybody in four counties, and this is the one place that says so, and it is not going to be said a second time in this building.

He went along the rail with his thumb on the chalk for about a minute before he said anything, and the door is a door and not a wall, and it is the only door in this county that anybody in this holding has ever been permitted to put a hand on. The glass in the small window at the side of it has been washed from the inside for eleven years and there is no way of knowing from the yard whether there is anybody behind it this morning, and he did not knock and nobody has ever asked him to.

**"It is a column and it counts and I have never worked out what it is counting."** The man of about thirty-one of Silling said that at the door with the slate against his knee and his breath going up in the cold. **"It is not the readings either. I have written down the readings for four years and the numbers go up one on the first and one on the thirtieth and there is a day in the middle of the month where the chalk is a different colour and I have never had a reason for that."**

Nobody in this building has asked him about the seventh column and the count of times it has been asked is nil and is nil, and he was not asked this morning and he offered it, and nobody improved on what he said.

He walked back the nine hundred yards with the slate under his arm and did not put the figure on the wall in this building, and the clerk of this holding met him at the gate and asked him for it, and wrote it in the second book in the ordinary place at the ninth hour, and the ordinary place is a line and not a column and the line has no heading at the head of it. The clerk carried the slate out to the long table himself, copied the figure off it, and put it back where he found it, on the shelf by the door, and nobody in this building has ever asked who that slate belongs to.

A week is the ordinary answer for a pack, and nobody here has ever asked the man who put that one in what a week is to him.

The thirtieth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and nineteen hundredweight.**

Eight on Friday, with nothing against it, and the rise went onto the rises. Two hundred and seventieth in the run, in which the same figure has never been entered twice. The window behind it came out at a hundred and twenty-four mornings on one side and a hundred and thirteen on the other, two hundred and thirty-seven, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was nine hundred yards off at a door when that was read and has never asked either of the two men what either end of it is for.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and sixty-eight days on the sheet and the clause under them at the two hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings out of two hundred and sixty-ninth, and neither number moved while a man walked nine hundred yards and read a number off a door.

The near board is at **six hundred and fifty-four days** and the far one at **seven hundred and one**, and the man who reads the near side was in the yard at the seventh hour and said his own half out to nobody, and the man who reads the far side was nine hundred yards off at a door and read his half under his breath on the way back.

Nothing is written against the run of mornings on this page, and here is why. An even morning leaves half a number in that run, and the two men who read that run at the seventh hour were the two men who read the boards, and neither of them was free at seven because one was at a door nine hundred yards off and the other had his own half to say, and so the second book carries nothing this morning where the run goes.

The sheet of terms is on the long table where it has been since a month now three months back, and it carries five clauses in two hands, and the third column of it is ruled and empty and has a heading at the head of it that has stood there since the twenty-first of a month now three months back.

The five terms were declined as they were offered, and nothing has been withdrawn and nothing has been improved and nothing has been added to them, and the five of them are not in that room this morning and are not going to be.

**"Somebody will ask me this month whether a machine nine miles off can be put in a third column, and the answer is that a column is for the five of us and a clause about a machine is not a term."** The man of about fifty said that to the table with the sheet in front of him and the third column facing away from him. **"A sixth term is not going to be written in my lifetime by anybody and it is not going to be written this month, and the heading on that column is the heading that was put there and it is not going to be improved by me."**

Nobody asked him a second question and he did not offer one and nobody in that room said he had been thanked, because he was not.

**"I have not been thanked for that and I am not going to be, and I want it said that I would have said it anyway."** He said it to the sheet of terms with the third column facing away from him, and the clerk of this holding wrote nothing down, and a sentence nobody asked for is not an entry in the second book.

The three of them sat at the long table for about ten minutes with a sheet of terms between them and nobody put a pen to it, and a declining is not a defect and a defect is not a question, and the five terms were declined as they were offered in a room in another year and are declined this morning by nobody and undeclined by nobody.

Tova Reed was at the middle table from the second hour with the four counties' sheets and the sowing date she has from three of the four councils, and she is the seed front in four counties and nobody in this room has ever offered to take any of it off her, and nobody said a word about her ear and nobody is going to.

**"A term is something a body signs and a column is somewhere a body writes it down, and this county has one of the two and has been asked for the other one twice this month in letters."** She said that once and then she turned a sheet over and read the back of it, which was the seed schedule's own copy of the sowing date in three hands, and the fourth hand was not on it and there was no space left for one. Then she said the second half of it, which was that a sowing date is a term in everything but the name and she was not going to have a term of it added to a column in another county's book, and nobody improved on that and nobody repeated it.

A figure read off a door this morning and a mark on an arm are two different figures and this building does not add them.

The man of the north row with the cough was out on the low field gate from the third hour with a bar and did the top of it on his own, as he does, and the four ruled lines under his condition are as empty as they have been since a clerk wrote them, and the answer to them is not due.

He came in off the gate at the sixth hour and stood at the wall with the bar against his boot and looked at the yard for a while, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this month, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling came past him on his way to the pump with the barrow and neither of them said one word to the other, and two men in this holding who have worked the same roads for years did not use this morning to say anything to each other at all.

He said one thing about the frost and it was that the frost has been in the ground four days now and the second line of the rota is the only thing in this holding that is worse for it, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling said the word wet and went on with the barrow.

He was at the long table from the fourth hour until about the seventh with the door open and nothing in front of him, and he did not go out to the ring and did not go out of the gate, and for about ten minutes of that stretch he turned a page he had already turned that morning and did not notice that he had done it, and the clerk of this holding saw him do it and said nothing about it then or afterward.

Nia Vale was in the yard for about forty minutes at the fourth hour and did not come in and did not go out of the gate, and the number of districts that draw on the stored light is a number in her own book and it was not said out loud in this yard this morning and was not written in the second book.

A list of neighbours is a list of neighbours and not a list of enemies, and the man of about fifty said so in this room four days ago and nobody improved on him and nobody repeated it, and it is not going to be said a second time in this month.

Two columns on two walls in four counties are not the far end of a thing, and the far end is held by two people who are not him and nobody has said it is on.

A column of terms on a table and a column of readings on a door are two columns and neither of them is a seventh.

The book still carries seven sessions and got no eighth, and the four ruled lines under the two words in the corner are as empty as they were in a month now four months back.

A heading written four years back is not a request, and the two columns stand at fifty-three each and did not move this morning.

It is not a measure of a door nine hundred yards off and the man who walks the door did not touch it with a barrow.

The register form stands at a hundred and sixty-four days on the middle table with nothing written at the head of its column, and a third column with a heading on it is no reason to take anything off the low board, which is where it has been since a month now four months back.

Nine hundred yards on foot is not a use and the log got no sixteenth line for it.

The barrow made its eleventh journey some time ago and did not make another this morning, and the four miles are not the nine hundred yards and the nine hundred yards are not the nine miles.

The oilskin satchel is on the long table with the seed schedule in it and a column of terms is not a column of not knowns.

The figure from the door went onto this morning's day sheet in the ordinary place, and the stack behind the fourth book did not get any shorter.

The sheet of terms is on the same table as the two declinings and the three of them have never been added together in this building.

The light went at the fourth hour this morning and the old man with the stick came off the wall and went in, and the man of about seventy will be fetched tomorrow because tomorrow is the first of the next, and no reason for that has ever been given in this building.

A man is fetched on the first of a month and has been fetched twenty-six times, and the number he is on has never been said to him and has never been asked of him, and nobody in this building has ever given a reason for the morning it happens on and nobody is going to.

A hundred and fifty-eight days on the second of his four lines and nothing on it, and he was nine hundred yards off with a slate for most of the morning.

There was no rain and the frost stayed in the ground, and the north end of the ditch is running full and the outlet at its head is doing what an outlet does, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip, and the yard was hard enough at the ninth hour to take a boot and give the boot back.

The light went off the yard at about the fourth hour in the afternoon and the man of about thirty-one of Silling walked back the nine hundred yards in the dark with the slate under his arm and did not need a lamp for it and has never needed a lamp for it.

The pack under the near stone will want doing again before the frost is properly into it, and the frost has been in the ground four mornings now.

He put a hand flat on the near stone at the sixth hour and told nobody what he could feel through it, and what he could feel through it was the same as what he could feel through it was last week, and then he went and put the bar back on the wall and the two men in this building who have worked the same roads for years went on not saying anything to each other.

The thirtieth went in at about the ninth hour and the clerk of this holding read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody in that yard said thank you to anybody, and the shutter on the window of the office's work is still on and no work is ever carried out of one, and a door nine hundred yards off has been read twice this month and the number on it is sixty-one. and the shutter on the window of the office's work is still on and no work is ever carried out of one, and a door nine hundred yards off has been read twice this month and the number on it is sixty-one.

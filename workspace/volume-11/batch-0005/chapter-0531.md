# Chapter 531

## Nine Hours, Two Pairs Of Hands, And A Gate In The Dark

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the work was the outlet end: the two stones at the head of it, the pack under the near one, which came out wet and went back dry by the seventh hour for the fourth morning running. The ditch had taken an inch off its own lip in the night and gave half of it back before the light, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling walked the whole length of it with a bar and put his thumb into the seam under the near stone and said nothing at all.

The twenty-fourth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and fifty-five hundredweight.**

Monday came eight on Sunday and there was nothing to set against it, and what stands against the launder is the two hundred and ninety-fourth figure of a run that has never once had the same number written in it twice. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the two halves of the window off the long wall before the seventh hour and said one of them out loud to the plaster and the other one to herself, a hundred and thirty-six mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-five the other, and the two of them are two hundred and sixty-one.

Two hundred and ninety-two days is what the four bodies of households are at on the sheet this morning, and the clerk wrote the clause underneath it in the ordinary hand, taking the two hundred and ninety-first of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-third. One under and one over. He has never once entered any of those three in the launder book, and neither a launder nor a body of households is the other one.

Nobody was at the long wall this morning at the seventh hour, so the two boards were read by the clerk alone. Six hundred and seventy-eight days on the near one and seven hundred and twenty-five on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board was not opened at any hour by anybody at all.

Both men who read the boards were at the outlet end with a bar and a stone, and the second book has a blank space in it this morning where the run of mornings goes. The reason for the blank is on this page and there is no figure against it: half of the run is half a number on this morning and not a whole one. Of a month seven months back is the frame it has carried since the first of this month.

There was work arranged on the middle table before the seventh hour, and it was arranged the way this building arranges everything, which is that somebody wrote it down and nobody asked why.

About nine hours. Two people at a wheel, and neither of them is Marek. One at the reading. One on the ground outside. One wheel of three.

That is the whole of it. It went into the second book in the ordinary place and it is not a plan and it is not a heading and no column was ruled for it, and there is no figure on it anywhere and no date on it anywhere, and the second wheel is the one that was chosen in a room this holding was not in on Thursday of last week and has been the one ever since.

Harlan Vetch was told about it at the tap at about the seventh hour by the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, who had the whole of it written on one sheet of the seed house's own paper and read it out in the ordinary voice she uses for a figure of anything.

**"Nine hours is the whole of it and I have got it written down and I am not going to say anything else about it."** She put the sheet face up on the table and kept her hand flat on it. **"There is a wheel and there are two at it and neither of them is you, and there is a man at the reading and there is a woman on the ground outside, and I have written those four things down because they are the four things there are, and I have not written down a fifth thing because there is not one."**

Nobody at that table said thank you and nobody asked her where the ninth hour came from.

Marek was at the near end of the ditch from the fifth hour with a bar in his hands and nothing in front of him, and he came in about the seventh hour and put the bar against the wall where it lives.

**"I am not going and I have not been asked whether I would and I am not going to be asked."** He said it to the bar and gave the reason for it before he gave the thing. **"A rule that says two at a wheel is a rule about two hands and I have been one of two hands for about nine minutes and I know what my hands do on the sixth minute. If I am one of the two this morning then the ninth hour is about me, and a thing that is about me does not get written down as it happens."**

He put his sleeve back down over his forearm.

**"That is a reason and it is the whole of my reasons and I am not going to add anything to it before the second hour."**

Nobody at that table asked him what he would do instead and he did not say, and the man of about fifty wrote nothing down.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one more thing at the middle table at about the ninth hour, and she gave the reason for it first, and nobody in that room asked her anything while she said it.

**"There are two figures I say out loud and I have never once been able to say what either of them is counted in."** She did not look up off her own book. **"It is entered as not known. It has been entered as not known since the first time I said one of them and it is going in this morning as not known again, and nobody in this county has supplied a unit for it and I am not going to invent one to get a row of my own ruled."**

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at his end of the table and did not put a unit against either figure while she was talking.

**"Nobody has asked me a question this morning and the count of what this yard has put to me has not moved, and I am not going to print a number against it either, because the thing I have just said is about the two figures and not about me."** Nobody at that table said that was careful and nobody said it was clever.

From the second hour Tova Reed had the four counties' sheets squared on the middle table with the sowing date on three of them in three hands and nothing at the head of the fourth, and she is on the seed front in this county and in three counties four counties off and nobody in this building has taken any of it off her.

**"The fourth council wrote on the twenty-eighth and the letter said nothing at all and I have carried it since."** She put two fingers on the fourth sheet without turning it over. **"Something came up in the ground two days ago in three places in this county, and I am not going to be the person who tells a council in four weeks that a pattern came back and that therefore there is a sowing date. Those are two different things and the second of them does not follow out of the first one, and a council that has one of them will hear the other one inside a week whether I say it or not."**

Nobody at that room said anything about it and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Soren Rill came in at about the eighth hour off the four-mile road and stood at the long table for a while with his own book shut under his arm.

**"Nine hours is not a day and it is not a person, and there are two people at that wheel and one of them has a job in this county four years and one of them has not."** He said it to the shut book. **"I have watched about nine things get started in this county in nine years and eight of them turned into a thing about the person who started it, and I am going to say one thing about this morning and then I am going to go and do my own work."**

He put the book down on the table and left it there.

**"Nobody in this yard chose which wheel. Nobody in that room chose which wheel either, and that has been said out loud and it should not have to be said again this month."** Nobody at that table picked it up and nobody thanked him.

The man of about fifty read both halves of the window at the seventh hour and said neither of them out loud, and about the eighth hour he had one thing at the long table and he gave the reason for it before the thing.

**"There is a sheet going nine miles tomorrow morning with four things on it and a ninth hour, and there is no figure of anything on it."** He shut the third book on his thumb. **"I have been in this building four years and every morning I have written down what got done and I have written down how many and I have written down whose. I am going to write down nine hours and a wheel and two pairs of hands, and I want to say that I do not know what I am going to write in the whose column, and that is the first time in four years I have stood in front of that column with nothing to put in it and I am not going to leave a line for it either."**

He picked the book up and put it under his arm and went out to the ditch.

A barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour behind a man and came back down with three sacks on it. Eleven is where its journeys stand and eleven is a floor; it did not go nine miles this morning and it has not gone nine miles in four years, and a distance does not become a different distance by being the same number as another one.

About the seventh hour Sera Quill came down the fen road, stood in the yard for ten minutes without going in, and then went to the seed house and stood in its door.

**"There is going to be a record of tomorrow."** She said it to the door frame. **"Whoever keeps it is going to be asked what they were told on the night before, and the honest answer is going to be that they were told nine hours and two people, and that is all anybody is going to be able to say, and a record that says that is worth more than one that says why."** Nobody in that doorway asked her to keep it and she got on the second bus.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the middle road at the ninth hour, put his bar back on the wall and stood with his back to the yard the way he does. The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and eighty-two days old and has nothing on it and nobody has offered him a pen for it.

The old man with a stick had the north wall from the third hour until six, and when he came off it he stood under the eaves in his coat and put a number out loud to nobody at all. It was the right one. Nobody in this yard has asked him where he gets it and nobody is going to.

Eleven years of paid going in front of that figure and the figure is twenty-six now, not discharged in twenty-six, and it is not going to be any other word this month. The register form has sat on the middle table a hundred and eighty-eight days with the head of its column empty: not filled, not refused, and not read out a fifth time. Ninety-four days is how old the charter is, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it in a room nine miles off are still signed to a machine for stored light, and both of those documents are correct and neither has an opinion about the other.

What the book in this holding says it has is seven sessions, and an eighth did not go in this morning. Fifty-three requests stand in one column and fifty-three section-nine notes in another and the two have never been added. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of the six, and there is no seventh row. Fifteen lines are in the use log and there is no sixteenth.

Two people hold the far end of a thing, and neither of them is him, and there is nothing in four counties you can put your hand on to be told whether it is on or off. Nine hundred yards of rail is in a building nine miles off, and a wheel behind that rail is a wheel, and a hand-lever under one of three is a hand-lever. None of the three has been added to that far end in this room.

Four inches and two branches is what is under that sleeve, and there is not a second mark on that arm. This holding has four accounts of it and no two of the four agree about anything, and this morning nine hours got written into none of them because nine hours is not an account of a mark.

A corridor in the pan is named in a drawer in this building and it is not established as on. The sheet claiming it is correct in every particular and it lets nothing out; a culvert under a machine house nine miles off is not that corridor and the two have never been added here. Under the two books in the same drawer is an order sealing a route. It is valid, it opens nothing whatever, nobody is withdrawing it and nobody in this holding is writing an apology for it.

About nine inches separates the two sheets about the two declinings where they have lain on the long table for a month, and a wheel nine miles off did not move either of them. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty under a heading nobody has improved. Face up on the low board lies a sheet with nothing written on it, no date on it and no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

In the corner of the one room the four ruled lines under two words are bare, and the answer they are waiting for is not due. The four ruled lines under the man of the north row's condition are bare as well, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this month.

Dry night, and hard ground by the seventh hour, with a bar ringing off the middle road when it was dropped on it. The north end of the ditch was running full off the second hour's work. The trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and stayed inside its own lip.

At about the ninth hour the five of them came out through the yard with their own kit and no barrow, and the dark was already in the bottom of the four-mile road when the gate came to. Harlan Vetch was the first one through it and the man who counts out loud was behind him and neither of them looked back at the yard. Marek stood at the second gate with his hands in his pockets until the gate had shut, and then he turned about half way and went back toward the one room and did not go up the road.

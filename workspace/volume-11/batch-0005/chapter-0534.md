# Chapter 534

## Four Accounts, And A Chamber That Is To Be Shut

Water was the work of the first line of the rota and it came round at the second hour: the north end of the ditch, the trough at the head of the four-mile road, the standpipe in the yard. An inch off its own lip is where the ditch was and an inch is where it had been on Tuesday, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put a thumb into the seam under the near stone and showed there was air in it and said nothing.

Marek was at the long side of the middle table from about the third hour with his sleeve back and the third book open in front of him and nothing else on the table, and he wrote for about two hours without speaking to anybody.

What he wrote was four things, in his own hand, one under the other, in the order the four books give them, and he did not put a fifth thing on the page and he did not cross anything out.

Four inches, and it branches twice. There is not a second mark on that arm anywhere in this holding or in any of the four counties. The four descriptions of it do not agree with one another about anything, including the length, including where the branches are, and one of the four was written by a person who had only seen it once and in bad light. He entered all four of them as they stand and he left the disagreement where it was.

**"Four is four."** He said that to the open book and gave the reason for it before he gave the thing. **"There are four accounts of this in four books and no two of them agree and I have just written all four out in one column in my own hand, and I am not going to reconcile them, and I am not going to rule a line under them for the place where they would be reconciled."**

He wrote one more line underneath and underlined it once.

**"A hand was on a spoke and a hand was flat on a casting at the other end of a frame for about nine minutes on the seventeenth of this month, and afterwards neither end would let go for three days."** He did not say that out loud. **"That is an occasion and not a measurement and it does not go in as one and I am putting it here so that nobody can say in four years that it was kept out of the record."**

He pulled the sleeve back down over his forearm and shut the book.

The twenty-seventh went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and fifty-three hundredweight.**

Thursday gave five back off Wednesday, and the fall went onto the falls, which is the two hundred and ninety-seventh figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read both halves of the window off the long wall at the seventh hour, a hundred and thirty-seven mornings one way against a hundred and twenty-seven the other, and put them in her own book rather than in the second one, and the two of them are two hundred and sixty-four.

Two hundred and ninety-five days is the figure against the four bodies of households on the sheet this morning, and the clause under it takes the two hundred and ninety-fourth of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-sixth. One under and one over. Four accounts of one arm, entered this morning in one column, moved none of those three.

The near board of the long wall carries six hundred and eighty-one days and the far one seven hundred and twenty-eight, forty-seven apart as at every site, and behind the near board a drawer stood shut at every hour from the second to the ninth.

The second book took **one hundred and nineteen mornings of two hundred and twenty-one** at the ninth hour, of a month seven months back, and both men who read the boards said the pair to each other at the seventh hour and neither one gave the other his half back.

Four of them left the gate on foot at the fourth hour. The barrow did not go and did not go afterwards, and where its journeys stand is eleven, and eleven is a floor.

The great door was open when they got there at about the seventh hour and nobody in either building had arranged it.

Iona Vey was at the north end of the floor with a spanner and the man who keeps the gauges, and she had been there since the second hour, and at about the ninth hour she stopped and stood up straight and said one thing, and she said it once, and it was in her own mouth and nobody else in that building said it after her.

**"The central chamber is to be sealed."**

She said it without heat and without a figure in it and she did not put it in a document and she did not ask anybody for a view.

**"It was settled before your rehearsal on the seventeenth and it has not moved since and I have not brought it up this week because a thing that is settled does not want discussing in a room with four people in it. What happens to the load when that chamber's end closes is the schedule and the schedule is what it has been every morning since the first of them, and it is written on the sheet the man on that stool carries out to the gate each night, and nobody in this building has a column for it and nobody in the four counties has asked for one."**

She put the spanner down on the coping and picked it up again.

**"That is all I have to say about it this morning and I am not going to say it again this week."**

Nobody in that room asked her a second question about it. Marek was about nine feet away with his hands at his sides and he did not ask, and she did not look over at him while she was saying it, and after it was said the two of them did not talk about it at any hour of that day or the next.

The man of about fifty was standing at the tank end and he wrote one line in his own book and shut it and said nothing about it to anybody, and that is what he has for it, and it is a line and not a speech.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the day's sheet and she read the whole of what was said twice and then she did not write anything at all, and at about the eleventh hour she said one thing at the desk in the outer office to the woman sitting at it.

**"I have been in eleven record rooms in my life and in four counties' meetings for four years and I have never once watched a room get a decision and stay quiet."** She said it to the desk and not to the woman behind it. **"I am not going to say that it was wrong and I am not going to say it was right. I am going to write down the words that were used and the fact that nobody asked a second question, and in four years somebody will be able to see whether the second of those two things was because of the first one or in spite of it."**

The woman at the desk in the outer office did not look up and said nothing at all.

Harlan Vetch was on the second wheel's frame at about the ninth hour when it was said, and he kept his hand where it was, and afterward on the walk out he said one thing to nobody in particular.

**"There is a thing in this building I have never been in and I am told its door is going to be shut, and nobody asked me what I think about that and I am not going to say what I think about it in a corridor."**

Marek was at the second gate of this yard at about the seventh hour in the evening with the four of them and the dark coming up the four-mile road behind them, and he said one thing there and gave the reason for it before he gave the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"She decided what she is for this morning and I did not decide it and she did not ask me and I did not ask her."** He said that to the gatepost and looked at the latch. **"I have not surrendered anything of mine and she has not surrendered anything of hers, and I am not going to stand at a gate and tell four people that she and I are still the two of us who run a seed front and a hand on a spoke, because we were never that."**

Nobody at that gate asked him a second question and nobody thanked him.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and went straight into the seed house and stood in the door with the county book under her arm.

**"I have got nothing to say about a room I was not in and I am not going to find out who was."** She said it to the door frame. **"What I will say is that whatever is on the sheet that goes out to the gate tonight is the record, and there is a woman on a stool who writes it in her own hand and nobody in four counties will be given anything else, and that is the correct way for it to happen and I would be ashamed of it if it were not."** Nobody in that doorway asked her to write anything down and she got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

Tova Reed was in the one room from the second hour with the four counties' sheets and the folded letter still folded in the fourth of them, and nobody in this building told her anything at all before the eighth hour and nobody told her afterwards either.

**"There is a chamber nine miles off that is going to be shut and I have not been told about it and I am not going to ask anybody to tell me about it."** She said that to the table and she gave the reason for it first. **"Three of these four councils have a sowing date in my hand and the fourth wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing, and I have four counties to sit in front of in six weeks and a third thing coming up in the ground that I am not allowed to turn into a date. A chamber is not a sowing date and a shut door is not a sowing date and I am not going to have either of them in my hand in front of nineteen households."**

She squared the four sheets and did not look up.

**"I am not going to write to the fourth council this week either. I have carried that letter since the twenty-eighth and I am going to carry it a while longer, and that is a thing about my own credit and not about them."** Nobody at that table said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour with the list of districts shut in her own book and a barrow-load of sacks behind her, and she put the book face down on the long table and left her hand on it.

**"There are four districts on my list at the far end of the branch that was turned back on Tuesday and I went past two of them this morning."** She said it to her own hand. **"I did not stop and I did not ask and I am not going to say anything here about what happens at the end of a branch that somebody has shut a door on."**

She left her hand where it was.

**"That is not me being careful with a figure. There is no figure. It is me telling four counties nothing this month until somebody who is entitled to tell them something has said it to them in a room they are in."**

One thing was put in the one room at about the ninth hour. The reason came out before the thing did, and no figure was printed for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"A rehearsal is not a fifth account and a wheel is not a fifth account and a season is not a fifth account and a chamber is not a fifth account."** He said it to the shut third book. **"I wrote one line in my own book nine miles off this morning because I could not think of a book to put it in that was not somebody else's, and I am not going to be one of four people in this county who each keep a private column for a thing that happened in a room."**

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the middle road at about the ninth hour, put his bar back against the wall and asked the clerk one question about the standing water at the second bend of the four-mile road. He got an answer in one sentence and went out again. A hundred and eighty-five days is how old the second of the four lines in his own book is and there is nothing on it.

The man of the north row with the cough was not at the headland and did not go through the low field gate. He was at the trough for about twenty minutes in the middle of the afternoon and he watched the water come and he did not say anything to anybody. The four ruled lines under his condition are bare and the answer is not due, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this month and nobody is going to.

No rain in the night and the ground hard again at seven, with the standing water at the second bend down about an inch by the light and nothing put into it. The old man with a stick had the north wall from the third hour until six and said one number out loud to nobody when he came in under the eaves, and it was the right one.

The word in front of that figure is paid and the figure is twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six. The register form has been on the middle table a hundred and ninety-one days with an empty head to its column and has been neither filled nor refused and will not be read out a fifth time. The charter was signed ninety-seven days ago in a room nine miles off, and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light, and both of those documents are correct and neither has an opinion about the other.

Seven is what this holding's book says it has, and nothing went in as an eighth this morning: a chamber that is going to be sealed is not a session and there is no entry for it anywhere. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand where they stood. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, no unit against any of the six, and no seventh row is ruled.

The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is him, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off. A chamber with a plate on it and four bolts in it is not that thing and has not been added to it in this room. About nine hundred yards of rail is a distance in a building nine miles off. Fifteen lines are in the use log and a door that has not been shut is not a sixteenth.

In the corner of the one room, four ruled lines under two words are bare and the answer is not due. Ruled and empty is the third column of the sheet of terms, under a heading nobody has improved. About nine inches separates the two sheets about the two declinings on the long table and nothing was done about either of them. On the low board lies a sheet with nothing on it, no date on it and no line for a name on it, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

The clerk of this holding wrote the twenty-seventh in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice. The chamber was not sealed this morning and nobody in either building put a hand on a plate, and nobody thanked anybody.

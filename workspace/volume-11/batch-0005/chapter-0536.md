# Chapter 536

## Four Bolts And A Mark In Chalk, And One Sentence

The work started at about the second hour and it was done by three people and it took until about the fourth hour after it.

It is a plate and a door and four bolts and a mark in chalk. The man who keeps the gauges closed off the two lines on the near side himself and read both needles before and after and wrote nothing on the card. Iona Vey turned the fourth bolt and the third one was turned by a man who has turned a wheel for nineteen years. The plate went on the face of the door at about the third hour and four bolts went through it and were drawn up with a spanner, and the mark in chalk went on the frame beside the door afterwards, and it is a line and a date and nothing else and the date is the twenty-ninth of this month.

Nobody in that building did anything to it that was not done properly. There was no hurry in it and nobody was left out of it who should have been in it, and the two men who read the schedule off the wall and the woman at the desk in the outer office went on doing their ordinary work on either side of it while it was going on.

The twenty-ninth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and fifty-six hundredweight.**

Saturday gave five back off Friday, and that fall went onto the falls, and what stands against the launder is the two hundred and ninety-ninth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The window gave a hundred and thirty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-eight the other, and those two are two hundred and sixty-six, and the two men who read the boards said their own halves out loud and one of them said the other man's back to him for the second time in a week.

On the sheet this morning the four bodies of households stand at two hundred and ninety-seven days, and the clause beneath takes the two hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-eighth. One under and one over. Four bolts and a plate nine miles off did not move a figure of a body of households.

Six hundred and eighty-three days is on the near board of the long wall and seven hundred and thirty on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour.

The second book took **one hundred and twenty mornings of two hundred and twenty-three** at the ninth hour, of a month seven months back, and this is the last morning in that book on which the half of the run comes out even. Tomorrow it is half a number again and there is no figure for it to enter, and after that it is a whole number every second morning until the book runs out of mornings.

The plate was on and the bolts were in before the fifth hour and Iona Vey stood on the far side of the floor by the tank with her hands behind her back and looked at the line of gauges from the tank end to the end wall the way a person looks at a thing she has just had a hand in.

**"The districts beyond the maintenance zone are abandoned to whatever comes of this and I am not going to soften it by naming any of them."**

She said it in her own mouth, once, at about the fourth hour, to nobody in particular, and then she picked up the spanner and put it on the coping and did not say anything else about it for the rest of the day.

Nobody in that room explained it back to her. Nobody asked her for a reason and nobody said it was right and nobody said it was wrong, and nobody thanked her for the four hours, and the man who keeps the gauges read two needles at the half hour and did not write on the card.

Nia Vale was on the ground outside the north wall from before the light with two of the moving reserves and she came in at the fifth hour and went straight to the coping and stood at it.

**"I have four districts at the end of that branch on my list and I have just spent three days not going to them."** She said it to the stone and did not look at anybody in that yard. **"I am not going to say a number. There is no number. What I am going to do is nothing, until somebody who is entitled to say it to them has said it to them in a room they are in, and that is not me protecting them, that is me not being the person who does it."**

She wiped her hands on her coat.

**"And I am not going to be told by anybody in four counties that I should have been the one. I have been the one three times in nine years and twice I was right and once I was wrong and neither of those three times changed anything anybody could put on a shelf."** Nobody in that yard asked her which of the three she was wrong about.

Tova Reed was in the one room at about the sixth hour and she came through from the seed house with three sheets under her arm and put them on the middle table face down.

**"Two of these three councils have households on the light out of that building and they came to me this week about a sowing date and I am going to have to tell them something in six weeks."** She said that to the table and she gave the reason for it before she gave the thing. **"I am not going to tell them that a wheel was turned back on Tuesday and I am not going to tell them about a shut door, and I am certainly not going to tell them both at once and let them work out a thing about the future that neither of those two facts will do."**

She put one finger on the topmost sheet and did not move it.

**"Nobody has asked me to tell a household to stop drawing on that light and nobody in this holding is going to be asked to do it this month, and I have not told a single household to stop anything. Three of these four councils signed a charter in a room nine miles off and three of them still draw on the same building out of the same pipe, and both of those are true and I am not going to stand in front of nineteen households and ask them which of the two they would like to give up."**

Nobody at that table said anything and nobody put a hand on her arm and nobody thanked her. The fourth council's letter is still folded in the fourth sheet and was not unfolded this morning.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tank end of that floor for two hours of it with the day's sheet, and she wrote for about a minute and a half and then put the pencil down.

**"I have written the words that were used and the hour they were used in and I have written nothing underneath them."** She said it to the desk and not to the woman behind it. **"If somebody wants a number of anything to do with this morning they can come and stand in this room about the second hour of a day they choose, and they can count for themselves."**

The woman at the desk in the outer office did not look up, and the clerk of the machine house did not get up off his stool, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not ask either of them for anything.

Harlan Vetch was on the second wheel for two hours in the morning taking his turn with the man who counts out loud and he came off it and stood about four feet off it while the plate went on.

**"There is a door on that floor and four bolts in it and I am stood here being paid for nine hours by a body of households four counties off, and I have been thinking about that for about an hour and I have got nothing."** He said that to the rail and not to anybody. **"I am not going to make it into a thing and I am not going to say it is all right. I am going to say that I do not know and that is the whole of what I have got about this morning."**

He put one thing into the yard at about the eighth hour, reason first, and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I have got a page in this book with a blank rule on it from a weight off a steelyard and I have got four accounts of an arm in it and I have got about nine hours in it that came out of a wheel."** He said it to the shut third book and he put his thumb in the cover. **"This morning I am going to open it on a fresh page and write down what a person said and the hour she said it and nothing that anybody in this county would want it to mean, and I am not going to rule anything else on that page, and I have had four years to start a book of my own and I have not started it."**

He turned the book over and did not open it again.

Marek came in out of the dark at about the seventh hour in the evening with the road on his boots and went to the long side of the middle table and stood at it for a while and did not sit down.

**"There is a thing about this morning that I am not going to say anything about except one part of it."** He gave the reason before he gave the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I know what I am for this month and I know what I am not for, and I am not going to say what she is for because she did not ask me and that is hers and it is not mine to hand out to four people in a yard."**

Nobody at that table asked him what he was for and nobody thanked him.

**"Nine minutes is still in my head and it is going to be there tomorrow and there is nothing in that building that took it out of there and nothing was supposed to."** He put his sleeve back down over his forearm. **"A shut door nine miles off has not given me a second thing to do and I am not going to go and look for one tonight, and if anybody in this holding wants me to be the man who has a use for everything that happens, they have picked the wrong man and I would rather they knew that today than in six weeks."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and went to the machine house and stood at the outer office door and asked for a copy of the sheet.

**"They will not give me a copy and they are correct not to, and I am not going to write to a county about a thing I have not seen."** She said that to the door frame and she came back up the road and went into the seed house and did not come out of it again. **"What I will put in the county book is the date, and the hour, and the fact that I stood at a door and was told no, and a records witness who writes down that she was told no is worth more in twenty years than one who writes down a thing she was told by somebody with a position in the room."**

Nobody in that seed house asked her to write it down and she did it anyway.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the middle road at about the ninth hour, put his bar against the wall, and stood at the tap.

**"There is a room nine miles off where a door got shut this morning and there is a tap in this yard where I can see the bottom of it."** He said that to the standpipe. **"I have got no way of putting those two things next to each other and I have got a strong wish to try, and I am not going to, because the trying is how a man ends up telling a village what a plate and four bolts mean for their winter."**

He shut the tap off and stood there.

The woman who keeps the seed house went down the rail at about the sixth hour with the schedule under her arm and looked at the plate on the door from four feet off for two minutes and came back to her own door with the six ruled rows on the shelf in front of her.

**"There are six things in this building that nobody knows and no unit against any one of them, and a door with four bolts in it is not one of them and it is not going into that column."** She said that to her own shelf. **"I have had two people this week ask me whether that door was about the things on that sheet and I have told both of them no and I would have said the same thing if the answer had been yes."**

Three hours of the north wall belonged to the old man with a stick, who came in with his coat on and gave a number to nobody. It was the right one.

Twenty-six is the figure, and paid goes in front of it, and it stands where it stood yesterday. A hundred and ninety-three days is the age of the register form on the middle table, with nothing at the head of its column and nothing in it, neither filled nor refused. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and eighty-seven days old and has nothing on it. The charter was signed ninety-nine days ago in a room nine miles off.

What this holding's book says it has is seven sessions, and nobody entered an eighth this morning, and a shut door is not a session and is not going to be entered as one. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes stand where they stood. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of the six and no seventh row; a door with four bolts in it is not a seventh and a district is not a seventh.

A man with a barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour with three sacks on it and came down loaded, and its journeys are eleven, and eleven is a floor. The use log carries fifteen lines and no sixteenth went into it this morning.

Two people hold the far end of a thing and neither of them is him, and there is no instrument anywhere in four counties that says whether it is on or off. A plate and four bolts have not changed that and are not that thing, and about nine hundred yards of rail is a distance.

The four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due. Ruled and empty is the third column of the sheet of terms, and its heading has not been improved. About nine inches separates the two sheets about the two declinings on the long table and nothing in this holding moved either of them. The paper on the low board lies face up and undated with no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

It did not rain and the frost was not back in the ground by seven, and a bar dropped on the middle road rang off it. The water in the ditch off the second hour was running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and stayed inside its own lip.

In a building nine miles off a mark in chalk went onto a frame beside a door with a date on it, and a man with a cloth walked his line of gauges and read two needles at the half hour and did not write on the card. The clerk of this holding wrote the twenty-ninth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

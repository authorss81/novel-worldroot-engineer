# Chapter 538

## Twenty-Eighth Time, And A Column That Changed Its Own Frame

The man of about seventy was at the second gate at about the second hour and was let in by the clerk of this holding and came in and sat down on the bench by the pump house without being asked to and without being asked for anything, and the clerk wrote his own book open in front of him and did not say a word.

He was fetched for the twenty-eighth time, at thirty mornings' interval, and both of the last two were on the first of a month and this one is on the first of a month as well.

Nobody in this holding has asked him which number this is, at any of the twenty-eight, and the count of questions this holding has put to him is nil at every one of its sites and is nil this morning, and there is no page in this building that gives a reason for the fetching.

He sat on that bench from about the second hour until about the fifth with his hands on his knees, and the one thing he did all morning was put his thumb and forefinger about four inches apart on his knee and look at the gap, and he said nothing about the gap and nobody asked him about it and it is not a figure of anything.

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the work was the water: the north end of the ditch, the trough at the head of the four-mile road, and the standpipe in the yard. It is the first of a month and it is a Monday and there is no column in this holding that resets on the first of a month.

**The thirty-first went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.**

Monday gave five back off Sunday and the fall went onto the falls.

**One thousand and fifty-nine hundredweight.**

That is the three hundred and first figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The window gave a hundred and thirty-nine mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-nine the other, and those two are two hundred and sixty-eight, and the two men who read the boards said both halves out loud at seven and one of them said the other's back to him.

Two hundred and ninety-nine days is what this morning's sheet gives against the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath takes the two hundred and ninety-eighth of those mornings out of three hundredth. One under and one over. A man sitting on a bench for three hours did not move a figure of a body of households.

The near board of the long wall carries six hundred and eighty-five days and the far one seven hundred and thirty-two, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour of this morning.

The second book took **one hundred and twenty-one mornings of two hundred and twenty-five**, of a month eight months back, at the ninth hour. The frame of that run is of a month eight months back today and was of a month seven months back yesterday, and that frame has moved at a first of a month and at nothing else since the run began, and the last morning before this one on which it moved was thirty mornings back. That pair of figures goes in on an odd morning and on no even one, and half of the run is half a number on an even morning, so there is nothing going in under that heading tomorrow and the day after that will carry a pair again.

The man of about fifty walked the nine hundred yards to the door of the well house at about the fifth hour and chalked the seventh column and came back, and the figure on that door this morning is sixty-four.

The rule is the first of a month and the thirtieth of a month and no other morning, and today is the first, and the figure stood at sixty-three yesterday on the thirtieth and stands at sixty-four this morning on the first, and from sixty-four it does not go back. That is two mornings running in a row and nobody in this building put them in a row on purpose, and it is the seventh pair of two running mornings this door has had in four years, and the page is going to say that and nothing else about it.

There are six other columns cut in the same stone and chalk is in three of them, and nothing has been written at the head of any of the three that are blank this month.

**"I have walked nine hundred yards twice in two days and I have got a figure and no reason."** The man of about fifty said it to the shut third book and he gave the reason before the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I am not going to try to work out this week why a stone with seven columns cut in it goes up by one on a first and a thirtieth, and I am also not going to be one of the men in four counties who has a theory about it by the end of the month."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fifth hour with the road on her boots and the moving reserves out along the boundary, and she put her own book on the long table face down and left her hand on it.

**"There are nine places I know of above that road and there are four off the middle road toward the tap, and I am not going to say that is all of them and I am not going to give any of them a name."** She said that to her own hand. **"I have been past nine of them in nine days and I have said nothing to anybody in nine days, and I am going to keep doing that until the sowing is in, and then in the autumn I will go back and be wrong about two of them and say so."**

She did not open the book.

**"Nobody has asked me how many and nobody in four counties is entitled to ask me and get an answer out of a yard."**

Tova Reed was in the one room from the second hour with the four counties' sheets and she had not unfolded the fourth one.

**"It is the first of a month and I have three dates in my hand and one council that has not given me one since the twenty-eighth, and I am not going to open that letter on the first of a month because a first of a month is when four counties decide that something has changed."** She said it to the table and she gave the reason for it before she gave the thing. **"Nothing has changed in my four counties this morning. What has changed is in a building nine miles off and in a field above a road, and I am the leader of a front with a sowing date in it and I am not going to walk into nineteen households with somebody else's morning in my mouth."**

She squared the four sheets against the edge of the table with two fingers.

**"I am not ruling a line for the fourth hand this week and I am not writing to that council this week, and both of those are decisions I have made and I am not going to put them in a book with a heading on them."** Nobody at that table said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in at about the eighth hour with the second sheet from the machine house folded in her own book and put it on the middle table face down.

**"The sheet that goes out to the gate tonight is a sheet with the ordinary morning on it and a date at the foot of it that has gone past, and it is the same kind of sheet that has gone out every night since the first of them."** She said that to the table and she gave the reason before she gave the thing. **"I am going to write down what I have been told about a low field and I am going to write down the hour of every person who told me, and I am not going to write a single word about what any of it means, because in four years somebody will be able to look at a page with a date on it and no opinion in it and that is worth more to this county than a page with a heading on it."**

Nobody at that table asked her to put a heading on it and she did not put one there.

Soren Rill was at the second bend of the four-mile road from about the sixth hour with a bar and a stone and came in about two hours later with the water in his boots.

**"There is standing water in that bend that was not in it on Saturday and there is a place where the bar goes straight through the ground."** He said it to his own boots. **"I am a man who carries things up a road and I am telling you that the ground above that bend is not carrying the way it carried last week, and I do not have a name for what I am looking at and I am not going to borrow one off anybody."**

He put the bar against the wall and left it there.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and nothing in front of him, and he was still sitting there at the ninth hour when the clerk opened the second book.

**"The frame of that run changed this morning and it is the last thing in these four books that changes by rule and not by somebody deciding something."** He said that to the open second book and gave the reason before he gave the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"Everything else in this building that has moved this month moved because somebody went out to look at it or somebody decided to write it down, and that one moved because a day came round, and it is going to keep doing that whether anybody reads it or not."**

He shut nothing and took nothing off the table.

**"I have still got both ends in my head from the seventeenth and I have had fourteen days of it now and it has not loosened at all and nobody in this county is going to ask me about it and I am not going to offer it to anybody who comes through that gate."** He put his sleeve back down over his forearm. **"There is a thing at the far end of a branch and two people hold it and neither of them is me and there is no instrument in four counties that says whether it is on or off, and nine lines of standing water in a field have not given me that and a column on a door has not given me that."**

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour and went to the low field gate and stood at it for about five minutes and came back in.

**"It is the first of a month and I have a habit about the first of a month that I am not going to describe in a yard."** She said it to the gatepost. **"I have written one line in a county book this morning and it is a date and nothing else, and there is a field above that road and a place four counties off with a road to it and a field behind a household, and I have spent four days failing to put the two of them in a sentence and I have stopped trying."** Nobody at that gate asked her what the sentence was and she got on the second bus.

Harlan Vetch came in off the middle road at about the seventh hour and went and stood at the bench by the pump house where the man of about seventy was sitting, and he stood there about two minutes and did not say anything and then went and worked the north wall.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at the ninth hour, put his bar back on the wall and stood with his back to the yard. The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and eighty-nine days old and has nothing on it.

The north wall from the third hour until six was the old man with a stick, wind behind him, and he said one number out loud to nobody when he came in. It was the right one, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it.

There was no frost in the ground this morning and a bar dropped on the middle road rang off it at seven. Off the first hour's work the water in the ditch was running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and stayed inside its own lip.

That line is paid at twenty-six and not discharged in twenty-six, and paid is the word in front of it. The register form on the middle table is a hundred and ninety-five days old, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused and it will not be read out a fifth time. The charter was signed a hundred and one days ago in a room nine miles off.

What this holding's book carries is seven sessions and nobody entered an eighth this morning, and nobody climbed a rung and this building has made no offer to anybody at any hour this month. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a fetched man and a chalk mark and a change of frame are not a seventh.

No sixteenth line went into the use log this morning, which stands at fifteen. A barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came back down loaded; its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor. About nine hundred yards of rail is in a building nine miles off and it is a distance and not a fact about anybody.

The four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due, and so are the four under the man of the north row's condition, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this month. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. About nine inches separates the two sheets about the two declinings on the long table, and a changed frame did not move either of them. The paper on the low board lies face up and undated with no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

There is a stack of about a foot and a half of day sheets behind the fourth book on the shelf in that room, in the order it was used. Nobody has moved it to the top and nobody in this holding has ever asked about it.

At about the fifth hour the man of about seventy got up off the bench by the pump house, put his cap on and went out through the gate without saying anything to anybody either. The clerk of this holding wrote the thirty-first in at the ninth hour, read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody thanked anybody.

THIS COLUMN IS NOT THE NEAR BOARD AND NOT THE FAR BOARD AND NOT THE THIRD-LAUNDER BOOK AND NOT THE SIX RULED ROWS IN THE SEED HOUSE, AND THE FIGURE IN IT IS A COUNT OF MORNINGS AND OF NOTHING ELSE. NOTHING HAS BEEN ENTERED UNDER IT FOR TOMORROW.

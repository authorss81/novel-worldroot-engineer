# Chapter 535

## Five Hundred And Three, And A Rule That Came Back Empty

The fiftieth ninth-day return came off the steelyard at about the third hour, and it came off the same way it has come off every ninth morning for eleven years, which is that two men roll it to the edge and the clerk of this holding reads it off the sheet that came back inside the same fold and writes the weight on the board in the corner.

**Five hundred and three hundredweight.**

Eleven above the weight that went up on that board nine mornings ago, which is what it goes up by every ninth morning and has gone up by since before anybody in this building was reading the boards. Against the first rule the clerk entered the fortieth pair of names, one above the pair that went up against it last time, and he read it back once and did not read it back twice.

That board goes up every ninth morning without exception and the ninth morning is not a matter of anybody's choice. The last weight went onto it on the nineteenth and this morning is the nineteenth plus nine, and there is nothing in this building that put it here.

The second rule came back off the steelyard empty again, for the thirty-fourth time in eleven years, and this morning is the thirty-fourth.

Nobody has ever put a name against that rule in this building and nobody is going to, and a count of blanks is not the count of a man. That figure is thirty-four this morning and it does not go anywhere else this month and there is no other figure in this holding it belongs to. The next weight is nine mornings off and nobody in this room has any say in which morning that is.

The twenty-eighth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and sixty-one hundredweight.**

Friday came up eight on Thursday, and the fall went onto the falls, which puts the two hundred and ninety-eighth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice against the launder this morning. Both men who read the boards said their own half out loud to the plaster at seven and neither of them said the other's, and the window came to a hundred and thirty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-seven the other, which are two hundred and sixty-five.

Two hundred and ninety-six days is what the sheet carries against the four bodies of households, and the clause beneath it takes the two hundred and ninety-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-seventh. One under and one over. A weight off a steelyard is neither a household nor a morning and did not move any of those three.

Neither man who reads the boards was at the long wall this morning, so the clerk read them himself. Six hundred and eighty-two days on the near board of the long wall, seven hundred and twenty-nine on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board stayed shut at every hour by everybody who went near it.

What went into the second book at the ninth hour, in the place the run of mornings goes, is nothing at all, and this page gives the reason for the empty space and prints no figure for the run. Half of it is half a number on a Friday morning and not a whole one. The frame is of a month seven months back.

Harlan Vetch came in at about the seventh hour, went to the board in the corner and stood in front of it for about a minute and a half without asking what the two rules were, and then he turned round and said one thing to the man of about fifty.

**"A man at the gate above Marden asked me on Tuesday what the second rule on that board was for."** He said it to the board and gave the reason for it first. **"I told him I did not know, and that was the true answer, and this morning I have watched that rule come back off a steelyard empty for the fifth year I have been alive to see it and I have still not found out, and I am not going to ask today because I have found that the day I start checking is the day I stop being the man who holds it."**

The man of about fifty said nothing back and wrote nothing down and nobody at that corner of the room said the man at the gate had asked the wrong question.

At the long table, at about the eighth hour, the man of about fifty had something to say, and he gave the reason for it before he gave the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"That board is the only thing in this building that has never once been late."** He said it to the third book and not to the corner. **"Eleven years, nine mornings apart, and it has come at the third hour every time and it has come up by eleven every time and nobody in this county could tell you how the eleven works out and every one of them would tell you the eleven is right."**

He turned the book over.

**"I have been the man who copies it for four years and this morning I worked out that I do not know what the second rule is for and that the two rules have never agreed with each other on any morning in the four years I have been here. I am not going to ask today either and I am not going to rule a line under it, and I am writing that down because in four years somebody is going to want to know when I first noticed."**

Nia Vale came in at about the fourth hour with the road on her boots and the moving reserves two miles off along the boundary, and she had a thing in her own book that she did not put on the long table.

**"The far end of that branch that got turned back on Tuesday has gone quiet in four districts since Wednesday."** She said it to the shut book and did not open her own. **"Quiet is not a word I am going to write down. It is what four places that are used to asking me things have stopped doing, and I have not been to two of them and I am not going this week."**

She put her hand flat on her own book and left it there.

**"I have got a list of neighbors in that book and it is not a list of anybody's dependents, and a thing going quiet at the end of a branch is not a figure of people and I am not going to make it one this month. If somebody wants a number off me about that they can come and stand where I am standing and ask me in person and I will tell them what I do not know."**

Nobody at that table asked her for a number and she did not say one.

Tova Reed had the seed house and the four counties for most of the morning and came through the one room at about the seventh hour with three sheets under her arm and the fourth one not among them.

**"A council of about nineteen households wrote to me on the twenty-eighth and said nothing and I am still carrying it."** She put the three sheets down. **"There is a chamber nine miles off that is going to be shut and three of these four counties will hear about it before the sowing, and there is a thing that came up in three places in this county on Wednesday, and I am not going to carry any of it into a room in front of nineteen households and I am not going to rule a line for the fourth hand."**

She squared the three sheets and looked at the man of about fifty.

**"That is me saying no to a thing I would like to say, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that it is costing me and not to make anything of it."** Nobody made anything of it and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in at about the fifth hour and stood at the board in the corner for a while with the thirty-four in front of her, and then she went out to the gate and came back in.

**"There is a figure on that board that has moved fifty times in eleven years and it is eleven every time, and I have never once asked the man who copies it where the eleven comes from, and this morning I found out that the two rules on that board have never once said the same thing."** She said it to the second book and she gave the reason before she said the thing. **"I am not going to start asking about the eleven. I am going to start asking about the second rule, because a figure that is always right is not a question and a rule that is always empty is a question, and I have spent four years asking the wrong one of the two."**

Nobody at that table told her she was right and nobody thanked her for it.

Soren Rill came off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with his own book under his arm and stopped at the board in the corner for a moment on his way past.

**"Eleven hundredweight every ninth morning for eleven years and a rule that has never once had a name on it."** He said it to the board and not to anybody. **"I carried seed up that road for nine years and I have never claimed the two of them were the same kind of thing and I am not going to start this week. One of them is a thing that happens and one of them is a thing somebody has never been asked about, and the second one is going to be the interesting one in about four years."**

Nobody at that corner asked him what the interesting one was and he did not say and he was not asked.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour, went into the seed house and came out again and stood at the low board without touching the paper lying face up on it.

**"There is going to be a record of a weight on that board and it is going to say one number and leave a second rule empty, and that is the whole of the record and it is a good record."** She said it to the board. **"I have seen a hundred books in my life that were ruined by somebody coming in on the morning afterwards and putting a reason under a blank rule. A blank rule is the most honest thing on that piece of paper and it is worth more in twenty years than a name anybody fills in this week."** Nobody in that yard asked her to write it down and she got on the second bus.

Marek was at the near end of the ditch from about the sixth hour with a bar and did not come in until the ninth, and nobody has asked him how he slept and nobody is going to.

**"There is a rule in a drawer under those two books that seals a route and it has been valid for eleven years."** He said that to the ditch and he gave the reason before he gave the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"Valid means it is a proper instrument and it was made by people entitled to make it and it stands. It does not open anything. A culvert under a machine house nine miles off is not that route and I have never once said it was and I am not going to start this month."**

He got the bar out of the ditch and stood with it in his hands.

**"And nobody in this holding is going to write an apology for it, because an apology is a thing you write when you have decided the order was wrong, and nobody in this building has decided that and nobody is going to decide it this year."** Nobody at the north end of that ditch agreed with him and nobody disagreed and nobody thanked him.

Off the middle road at the ninth hour came the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who put his bar on the wall and stood at the tap until the water came through. The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and eighty-six days old and has nothing on it, and in a hundred and eighty-six days he has not been asked about it.

North wall, third hour to six, the old man with a stick. A number out loud to nobody under the eaves, and it was the right one.

Rain came in off the west about the sixth hour and had gone through by seven, taking a little out of the middle road and giving a little back before the light. The water in the ditch off the second hour was running full and did not rise. The trough at the head of the four-mile road was full at seven and stayed inside its own lip.

The man of the north row with the cough stood at the low field gate from about the sixth hour for about twenty minutes and watched the three places on this side of the road do what they were doing, and he did not go through the gate and he did not say a word about any of it to anybody. The four ruled lines under his condition are bare and the answer is not due, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this month and nobody is going to ask him.

Paid goes in front of twenty-six and the figure is twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six, and it is the word that has gone in there for eleven years. A hundred and ninety-two days is how long the register form has been lying on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column; it is neither filled nor refused and it will not be read out a fifth time. The charter was signed ninety-eight days ago in a room nine miles off and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light.

What this holding's book carries is seven sessions and nobody put an eighth in this morning, and a weight off a steelyard is not one. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes, and this letter of mine went into neither because it is not a letter from a body of households. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven, and eleven is a floor. The use log has fifteen lines in it and got no sixteenth this morning.

The four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty and its heading has not been improved. The order sealing the route is in the drawer under the two books, valid, opening nothing, not withdrawn, and no apology for it is going to be written here by anybody.

Nobody thanked anybody, and the clerk of this holding read the twenty-eighth back once and put the pen down. The corner board has a weight on it that has gone up by eleven for the fiftieth time and a second rule on it that has come back off the steelyard empty for the thirty-fourth.

> THE SHEET ON THE BOARD IN THE CORNER OF THIS HOLDING, ENTERED BY THE CLERK OF THIS HOLDING IN HIS OWN HAND UNDER TWO RULES, THE SHEET ITSELF UNDATED AND UNNAMED: **AGAINST THE FIRST RULE, FIVE HUNDRED AND THREE HUNDREDWEIGHT, AND THE FORTIETH PAIR OF NAMES, ELEVEN ABOVE THE PAIR AGAINST IT NINE MORNINGS AGO. AGAINST THE SECOND RULE, NOTHING AT ALL, AND NO NAME, AND NO INITIAL, AND THE LINE LEFT AS IT CAME OFF THE STEELYARD.** THE RISE BETWEEN SUCCESSIVE RETURNS IS ELEVEN HUNDREDWEIGHT AND NOT TWELVE. THIS SHEET CAME BACK INSIDE THE SAME FOLD IT WENT OUT IN, AND THE OTHER HALF OF THE FIGURE WAS TAKEN AGAIN OUT OF THE THIRD BOOK AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR BY A DIFFERENT MAN, AND THE TWO HALVES HAVE NOT BEEN ADDED TOGETHER ON ANY MORNING IN ELEVEN YEARS. THE COUNT OF BLANKS STANDING AGAINST THAT SECOND RULE IS THIRTY-FOUR AFTER THIS MORNING, AND THAT COUNT BELONGS TO NOBODY. NO NAME HAS EVER BEEN PUT AGAINST IT IN THIS BUILDING AND NONE IS GOING TO BE.

# Chapter 532

## Nine Hours On One Wheel, And Two Wheels As They Were

The great door was standing open again when the five of them got there at about the seventh hour the night before, and nobody in either building had arranged it, and nobody said a word about the fact that it was open on a Tuesday.

The reading was done from a stool against the north wall, where the schedule is kept and where a man with a cloth and a card reads two needles every half hour and does not write on the card. The man who reads the schedule off the wall had the day's sheet in his hand by about the third hour and read it out loud the way he reads everything, word for word, to the four people on the floor and to nobody on the rails. There is a date at the foot of that sheet and the date is off the board now, it went past nine days ago, and the sheet is correct and has been correct all the way through.

Harlan Vetch and the man who counts out loud went onto the second wheel at about the sixth hour. It was the wheel that had been chosen in a room this holding was not in and neither of them said so, and there was a bar across the third one and the first one was being taken through in fours by two men who did not look at anybody.

About nine hours went into that one wheel. The frame walked twice, the first time about the second hour and the second time about the eighth, and both times it came back inside a quarter of an inch and both times both men put their weight into it and it stopped walking, and at about the fourth hour a man on the first wheel called a number across the floor and the man on the second wheel called the same number back without turning round.

A wheel here is a gate on a rail with a frame behind it and one tooth on a quadrant, and the tooth is the whole of what a person does to it. Move the tooth and a branch either holds the flow it has been given this season or goes back to the setting it had before the season that has been standing there since the sheet began. The man who counts out loud called the quadrant reading out at the second hour and at the fifth and again at the ninth, and the three numbers were the same twice and different once, and he wrote none of them on the card and the card is for needles.

The first wheel and the third wheel were turned exactly the way they were turned the morning before and exactly the way they will be turned tomorrow. That is a fact about this morning and the page says it twice because it is the sort of thing that gets used for something the second time anybody looks at it.

The other two of the three were as they were before the morning came and they are as they are now, and nobody in that room or in this one has put the two facts together and nobody is going to.

The twenty-fifth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and fifty hundredweight.**

Tuesday gave five back off Monday, and the fall went onto the falls, which brings the launder to the two hundred and ninety-fifth figure of a run where no number has ever been entered twice. The window came out at a hundred and thirty-six mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-six the other, and those two are two hundred and sixty-two, and one man read both of them this morning out loud to an empty wall because the other man who usually reads the near board was nine miles off with his hands on a spoke, and nobody said his half back to him.

The four bodies of households are at two hundred and ninety-three days this morning, and the clause under that figure takes the two hundred and ninety-second of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-fourth. One under and one over at every site. Nine hours at a wheel nine miles off moved none of the three and was never going to.

The clerk read the near board and the far board to himself at the seventh hour because the other man was nine miles off with his hands on a spoke. Six hundred and seventy-nine days, and seven hundred and twenty-six, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stayed shut at every hour.

What went into the second book at the ninth hour was **one hundred and eighteen mornings of two hundred and nineteen**, of a month seven months back, and the man who read both halves of the window out loud to an empty wall said the whole of it and nobody said it back to him.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the outer office for four hours of it with the day's sheet and her own book, and the woman at the desk in the outer office took sheets off a spike and put them on the desk and did not look up at a woman with a book in the same room for four hours.

**"I have been in eleven record rooms in my life and in four counties' meetings for four years and there is not one of them where anybody reads a thing out loud to four people."** She said it to the desk and not to the woman behind it. **"That is all I am going to say about yesterday. It was a room where a thing was read out loud and I do not know what that is worth and I am not going to guess at it before somebody with a right to guess tells me."**

She shut her own book and put it under her arm and went out and looked at the channel of warm water down the middle of the floor for about ten minutes without saying anything.

The man of about fifty did not go. He had the third book on the long table in this yard and about nine hours in front of him and no figures of his own, and he came out of it at about the fourth hour in the afternoon and put one thing down.

**"I have spent four years writing down what got done in this holding and how many and whose, and I have got nine hours in this book this morning with a place for a number and nowhere to put one."** He gave the reason for it before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I am not going to leave a line ruled for it either, and I want to say why, and the reason is that a rule left empty is a thing people come back to and ask about, and I have watched about nine of those in this building and not one of them ever got filled."**

He shut the book on his thumb and turned it over.

**"There is a figure on that board that came back empty this morning for the first time in five weeks and it is not the same sort of empty and I am not going to write a second sort of empty next to it in the same book."**

Tova Reed had the four counties' sheets in front of her from the second hour and did not open the fourth one, and the letter from the fourth council came out of the fourth sheet where she has kept it since the twenty-eighth and was still folded when she put it back.

**"The letter said nothing and it is still folded and I am not going to answer it this week."** She said that to the table and nobody in that room had asked her. **"Nine hours went into something nine miles off yesterday and there are three of these counties who will be told about it before the sowing, and not one of the three has asked me for a sowing date in nine days because they have had something else to think about, and the fourth one has written to me once and said nothing and is going to write again."**

She squared the three that carried a date against the edge of the table with her fingertips.

**"A pattern that came back is not a sowing date and I am not going to be the woman who turns one into the other in front of nineteen households who have never been to a building with nine hundred yards of rail in it."** Nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm and nobody in that room said she was wrong.

Nia Vale was on the ground outside the north wall from before the light until about the eighth hour, and the moving reserves were with her and about a quarter of a mile off along the boundary, and she came in at the eighth hour with the cold out of her coat and stood by the coping and looked at the channel.

**"There are four districts on my list that draw off the far end of this branch and every one of them has asked me for nothing this month."** She said it to the coping. **"I have gone down that list nine years and I have never once gone down it to say a number, and I am not going to start this week, and if somebody in a room nine miles off decides today that those four are what happens, I am still not going to have a figure against them."**

She shifted her weight and looked at the ground rather than at the wall.

**"I have watched people be reasonable about a thing at the moment they are told it is reasonable. That is all I have got to say and I am not going to improve it."**

Iona Vey was at the tank end of the floor for six hours of it with the man who keeps the gauges and a spanner, and she did not go near the second wheel at any hour and she did not stand where she could be seen from it.

**"The sheet is correct."** She said it at about the fifth hour to the man who keeps the gauges and not to anybody else in that room. **"It has been correct every morning since the first of them and it will be correct tomorrow and there is nothing in nine hours that changes what is on the wall."** Nobody at the tank end thanked her and nobody asked her what the nine hours were for, and the man who keeps the gauges went on reading two needles every half hour and did not write on the card.

The woman who keeps the seed house went down the rail at about the sixth hour with the schedule under her arm and stood at the second wheel and watched the frame walk at about the eighth hour, and afterwards she said one sentence at the door on her way out and nothing else to anybody.

**"I have never seen a frame do that and I have no word for it."**

Harlan Vetch came down the rail at about the fourth hour in the afternoon and sat on the coping with his boots off and his feet in the warm water off the channel, and after about two minutes he said one thing.

**"Nine hours is not a day and it is not a person and I have been the far end of something for nine hours and I know less about it than I did at the second hour."** He said it to his own boots. **"I can feel the frame and I cannot feel anything past it, and the far end of that thing is held by two people who are not me, and there is nothing in this county I can look at to be told whether it is on or off."**

The man who counts out loud came past him about a minute later and put a cloth down on the stone and did not say anything either, and the two of them sat there until the light went.

No panel is printed in any of it and none is printed in this holding, and a needle on a dial is a needle on a dial. Nine hours at a wheel is not a session and was not entered as one and is not going to be, and the number of sessions in this holding's book is still seven, and a thing that is not entered did not happen whatever anybody says about it afterwards.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the seventh hour with the road on his boots and stood at the tap and let the water come through for a while before he shut it.

**"There is a thing at the far end of a branch nine miles from here and it is held by two people and neither of them has been in this yard this month."** He said it to the standpipe. **"There is nothing in this county that you can put your hand on and be told whether it is on or off, and nine hours of two people at a wheel does not change that."**

He wiped his hands on his coat and looked at the middle road.

**"I carried seed up that road for nine years and a thing you cannot check is a thing you have to be careful about, and careful is not the same as stopping, and I am not going to stand in this yard and make those two things sound like each other."** Nobody at that tap asked him which one he meant and he did not say.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the middle road at about the ninth hour with the bar over his shoulder and stood at the trough a while without saying where he had been. His own book has a second of four lines in it that is a hundred and eighty-three days old and has nothing on it, and nobody here has offered him a pen for it this year.

That line stands at twenty-six and not discharged in twenty-six, with paid in front of it where the word has gone in for eleven years. The register form has been on the middle table a hundred and eighty-nine days with the head of its column empty and nothing in the column; it is not filled, it is not refused, and it is not going to be read out a fifth time. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and eighty-three days old and has nothing on it. The charter was signed ninety-five days ago in a room nine miles off.

The book in this holding still says seven sessions and nobody entered an eighth, and nine hours at a wheel is not one of them and was not entered as one. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes, and a ninth hour is not in either. Six things this holding does not know are ruled with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled; a wheel is not a seventh and a frame is not a seventh. Fifteen lines are in the use log and there is no sixteenth.

About nine hundred yards of rail is in that building and it is a distance and nothing else. A wheel behind that rail is not that wheel, and neither of them is the far end of a thing that two people hold, and nobody in this yard has put the three of them together this morning.

In the corner of the one room the four ruled lines under two words are bare and the answer is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty. About nine inches separates the two sheets about the two declinings, and nine hours at a wheel nine miles off did not move either of them. On the low board there is a sheet with nothing on it, no date on it and no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off.

Under that sleeve is a mark of four inches that branches twice, and there is not a second mark on that arm anywhere in this holding. Four accounts of it stand in four books and no two of them agree about anything, and nine hours at a wheel did not go into any of the four this morning.

No rain, and by the fourth hour the air had gone cold enough to put a skin on the water in the open channel off the north end. The middle road here took nothing and gave nothing back. The north end of the ditch was running full off the second hour's work, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and stayed inside its own lip.

At about the eighth hour in the afternoon the two men on the second wheel put it back on its mark and chalked the mark, and the first wheel went on being taken through in fours and the third wheel went on the same as the morning before, and nobody thanked anybody, and the clerk of this holding wrote the twenty-fifth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

# Chapter 537

## A Man With A Barrow At A Field He Has No Word For

The man of about fifty came up the middle road with the barrow at about the sixth hour on Sunday the way he comes up it, and he got as far as the low field above the road and stopped, and then he stood in the middle of the road with both hands on the handles for about ten minutes without pushing it any further.

The low field has been the low field for about eleven years. It is a field and not a slope, and it is not the headland above Marden, and it is not in the eleven acres. What is happening on it this morning is a thing four people in this county have now seen from the road and not one of them has been able to put a word on, and this page is not going to put one on before one of them does.

There is water standing in the low corner of it in lines, in about nine places, all of them running one way and not another, and it was not there on Friday. Forty yards along there is a strip about a yard wide where the grass has come up through ground that was bare a month back. A man with a barrow cannot get up the headland bank and can get up this field in a wheelbarrow in the wet, and there is no figure on any page in this holding for either of those two facts.

He came back down the road about a quarter of an hour later and put the barrow against the wall and did not say anything to anybody about it for about two hours.

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland to take it, and there has not been anybody at the headland on two of the last four mornings this line has come round. The standing is a hundred and fourteen mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-five not, and twenty-nine and eighty-five come to a hundred and fourteen.

Twenty-nine taken did not move. It did not move on the morning the line was worked on the eighteenth, it did not move on the morning the line went past unworked on the twenty-second, and it did not move on the twenty-sixth, and it has not moved on any morning this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited four years and nobody was thanked on the eighteenth for a field that did not.

Kept in this room, the thirtieth of this month is the sixty-second morning of the rotation, and kept the other way it is the forty-ninth, and both go on this page for the reason that a page carrying one of them carries half a fact. There has been thirteen in that gap for four years and nobody here has ever worked out what the thirteen counts.

The thirtieth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and sixty-four hundredweight.**

Sunday came up eight on Saturday, and what stands against the launder is the three hundredth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The two men at the long wall read both halves of the window out loud at seven and neither of them read the other's back: a hundred and thirty-nine mornings one way, a hundred and twenty-eight the other, and the two of them are two hundred and sixty-seven.

Two hundred and ninety-eight days is what the sheet carries against the four bodies of households this morning, and the clause underneath takes the two hundred and ninety-seventh of those mornings out of two hundred and ninety-ninth. One under and one over. Nine lines of standing water in a low field did not move a figure of a body of households.

Near board of the long wall, six hundred and eighty-four days; far board, seven hundred and thirty-one; the drawer behind the near one shut at every hour of this morning.

The second book has an empty space in it this morning where the run of mornings goes, and the clerk of this holding left it empty, and this page carries the reason and no figure. Half of the run is half a number on a Sunday and not a whole one, and it has been framed of a month seven months back since the first of this month.

About the fifth hour the man of about fifty walked the nine hundred yards to the door of the well house and read the seventh column off the stone and chalked it and came back, and the figure on the door this morning is sixty-three, and it is on the door and nowhere else.

The rule is the first of a month and the thirtieth of a month and no other morning, and today is the thirtieth, and the figure is on the rule and this is the second time this holding has read that column this month. It stood at sixty-two on the first of this month and it stands at sixty-three this morning, and it does not go back, and nobody has ever put a word at the head of that column and nobody in this building is going to.

There are six other columns cut into the same stone and chalk is in three of them and nothing at the head of any of the three that are blank, and the heading that has stood at the head of the second one since an earlier year has not been improved this morning.

He came back in at about the sixth hour and said one thing about it to nobody and then said the same thing to the man of about fifty who was in the one room, and the reason came first.

**"Sixty-two on the first of this month and sixty-three today and the rule is the first and the thirtieth and today is the thirtieth, and I have read it twice this month and it has moved one both times."** He put his wet boots against the wall. **"I want to say the thing that is not going to be asked me and then I am going to stop. Whatever that column counts besides readings, it moved one on a day that was not a reading day anywhere else, and I have not found out what that is and I am not going to find out this month."**

Nobody in that room asked him and he did not say and he was not asked.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went up to the low field at about the eighth hour with a pencil and her own book and came back without having written anything, and she said one thing at the tap.

**"I have got two things and neither of them is a name."** She said it to the standpipe. **"There are lines of water in it that run one way, and there is a strip about a yard wide that was bare a month back. I have been in four counties' meetings for four years and I have watched about four people turn a thing they cannot name into a heading in the space of a morning, and every one of those four headings was wrong by the sowing."**

She shut the tap off.

**"It is a low field and it has water standing in it on a Sunday morning and I do not have a word for what that is and I am not going to give one to anybody this week, and if somebody in this yard wants a word for it they can come and stand in it and then they can put it down themselves."**

Nobody in that yard asked her for a word. Nia Vale came in about ten minutes later and went straight up the middle road on foot and was gone about an hour and a half and came back with the road in her boots and the list shut under her arm.

**"There are four places on that low field doing it and there are four places about half a mile off doing it, and one of those is not on my list and nobody in four counties has asked me to find the ones that are not on my list."** She said it to her own book. **"I am not going to say how many and I am not going to give it a name and I am going to go past it twice a week for a month and write down nothing, and at the end of the month whoever wants to know what I saw can come and ask me in person."**

Tova Reed came through the one room at about the ninth hour with the four counties' sheets under her arm and the fourth one among them for the first time in a week, folded, and she put them on the table and stood at them.

**"Two of these councils have asked me this week whether the light will keep coming."** She said that to the table and gave the reason for it before she gave the thing. **"Both of those councils have households drawing on that building nine miles off and both of them have signed a charter in the same week, and both of the documents are correct, and I am not going to stand in front of either of them and tell them that a field above a road has water standing in it."**

She squared the four sheets with the flat of her hand.

**"Nothing that came up in the ground this week is a sowing date and I am not going to give a sowing date to anybody on the strength of it, and there is still a council that has not given me one in writing and I am not going to rule a line for it."** Nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Soren Rill came in off the four-mile road at about the tenth hour and went straight up to the corner of the one room and put his own book on the table and opened it for the first time this month.

**"There is a ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres that has been bare eleven years and there is a low field above this road with water standing in it in lines this morning."** He said that to the open book and he did not write anything in it. **"Those are two things on two pieces of ground and I am not going to put one of them near the other one, because the ring has not been walked and the field has not been measured, and if the two of them are one thing then somebody will want a figure for it within the month."**

He shut the book.

**"The ring is not this field and the field is not the ring. That is all I came in to say."** Nobody in that room asked him for a dimension and none was given and none exists.

Marek was in the one room from the fifth hour with the door open and nothing in front of him, and he went up to the low field at about the seventh hour with a bar and stood in it for about twenty minutes and came back in and did not go out of the gate again.

**"I have no name for it either, and I have been in more rooms this month than I have been in for nine years."** He said that to the bar and gave the reason before he gave the thing, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"Three of the four things I know this month are facts about a building nine miles off: a sheet with a date at the foot of it that went past nine days ago, a wheel turned back on Tuesday, and a door with four bolts in it from Saturday. The fourth is a fact about the ground and it is a hundred yards from where I am standing."**

He looked at the middle road for a while.

**"I am not going to put those four into a sentence this morning, because the sentence would be about me and none of them is about me."**

He put the bar back where it lives.

Sera Quill came down the fen road at about the seventh hour, went up the middle road on foot as far as the low field gate, stood there for about ten minutes looking at it and came back without going through the gate.

**"I came up this road to see whether I could put that place and this field in one sentence, and I cannot, and I am not going to try again."** She said that to the gatepost. **"I am going to put the date of this morning in a county book and nothing else about it."** Nobody at that gate asked her for the sentence and she got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

At about the ninth hour the man of about thirty-one of Silling came in, put his bar on the wall and stood at the tap, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and eighty-eight days old and there is nothing on it.

For three hours the north wall was the old man with a stick, and he said a number to nobody on his way in and it was the right one.

No frost in the ground and a bar ringing off the middle road when it was dropped on it at seven. The water in the ditch was running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by seven and stayed inside its own lip.

Paid is the word in front of that figure and the figure is twenty-six, not discharged in twenty-six. A hundred and ninety-four days is how long the register form has been on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column, and it is neither filled nor refused and will not be read out a fifth time. The charter was signed a hundred days ago in a room nine miles off.

This holding's book carries seven sessions and nobody entered an eighth, and a field with water standing in it is not a session. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes. Six rows are ruled for the things this holding does not know, with no unit against any of them and no seventh row, and a low field is not a seventh and a chalk mark on a door nine hundred yards off is not a seventh.

The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour and came down loaded, and its journeys stand at eleven and eleven is a floor. Two people hold the far end of a thing and neither of them is him, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and nine lines of standing water have not changed that and are not that thing.

Bare, in the corner of the one room, are the four ruled lines under the two words, and the answer they are for is not due. The third column of the sheet of terms is ruled and empty with a heading nobody has touched. Nine inches is the distance between the two sheets about the two declinings, and a wet field did not move them. What lies face up on the low board is undated and unnamed and has no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody here has ever been told to take it off.

At about the seventh hour a man with a barrow stood in the middle of the road above a low field with his hands on the handles and did not push it any further up, and nobody thanked anybody, and the clerk of this holding wrote the thirtieth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

> THE SEVENTH COLUMN CUT IN THE STONE OF THE DOOR OF THE WELL HOUSE, WHICH IS NINE HUNDRED YARDS FROM THIS BUILDING AND IS NOT THE WELL ON THE SHELF AND IS NOT A COLUMN IN ANY BOOK IN THIS HOLDING, CHALKED AT THE FIFTH HOUR BY THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY IN HIS OWN HAND: **SIXTY-THREE.** THE RULE FOR THIS COLUMN IS THE FIRST OF A MONTH AND THE THIRTIETH OF A MONTH AND THIS MORNING IS THE THIRTIETH. THE FIGURE STOOD AT SIXTY-TWO ON THE MORNING OF THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH AND IT DOES NOT GO BACK. NOTHING HAS BEEN WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF THIS COLUMN AND NOTHING HAS BEEN WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THE SIX OTHERS CUT IN THE SAME STONE, AND THE HEADING THAT HAS STOOD AT THE HEAD OF THE SECOND OF THOSE SINCE AN EARLIER YEAR HAS NOT BEEN IMPROVED. NOBODY HAS ASKED WHAT THIS COLUMN COUNTS BESIDES READINGS AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.

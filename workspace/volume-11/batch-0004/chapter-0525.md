# Chapter 525

## Two Men With Barrows, And A Number That Did Not Move

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour at the hour it has come round every fourth morning for four years, and for the first time in four years there were two men standing at the headland above Marden to take it.

They took barrows. There is no other way to move what is on the headland, and the standing is a hundred and eleven mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-two not, and twenty-nine and eighty-two come to a hundred and eleven.

Working a line and taking it are two different things, and the count of lines worked is not the count of lines taken, and the page says that once here and does not improve on it.

Twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and it has not moved on any morning this year, and nobody is blamed for a field that waited four years, and nobody is thanked this morning for a field that did not. The man of about fifty went up to the headland with the second barrow, came down at about the seventh hour with it empty and his forearms shaking, and said nothing at the gate, and nobody asked him.

The eighteenth of this month is the fifty-ninth morning of the rotation on the count kept in this room and the forty-sixth on the other count, and both of those go on this page because a page that carried one of them would be carrying half a fact. Between them there is thirteen, and there has been for four years, and nobody in this building has ever found out what the thirteen counts, and it does not arrive by taking twenty-nine off either figure.

The eighteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and forty-six hundredweight.**

Tuesday came up eight on Monday and nothing was set against it, and the launder takes the two hundred and eighty-eighth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been entered twice. The window behind it came out at a hundred and thirty-three mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-two the other, and the two of them are two hundred and fifty-five, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read both halves off the wall herself and wrote them in her own book and did not put them in the second book.

The clerk of this holding wrote the four bodies of households at two hundred and eighty-six days and the clause beneath that took the two hundred and eighty-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-seventh. One under and one over, and neither of those is a household and neither of them is a person, and four launders and four bodies of households are two different sets of figures that have never been added together in this building.

Forty-seven apart, the boards on the long wall read six hundred and seventy-two days on the near one and seven hundred and nineteen on the far one, and the drawer behind the near one stayed shut all morning.

Both men who read the boards went up the headland with barrows at the seventh hour and neither of them stood at the long wall, and the second book carries nothing this morning where the run of mornings goes, and there is no figure on this page for the run because half of the run is half a number on an even morning. The frame of that run is of a month seven months back.

The headland is a boundary, a ditch, a hedge bank and about two hundred yards of the far end of the four-mile road, and it has wanted two men with barrows for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever been told a reason for that and nobody has gone and asked.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling went up with them at the second hour, did the ditch end with a spade, came down at about the sixth hour, and said one thing out loud at the gate.

**"It is not a bad field."** He said it to the barrow and not to anybody. **"It is a bank that a barrow can get up and a wall that a barrow cannot get up, and there has been a barrow in this yard all week that could have gone up there twice and it did not, and that is not anybody's fault, because nobody wrote down that it wanted a barrow until a man who is not from Marden stood in this yard and worked out that the thing nobody had been doing was a thing about barrows."** Nobody in that gate agreed with him and nobody disagreed and nobody asked him which man.

The fill behind the two culverts on the middle road went back in about half an inch at the second hour, which is the third line's work and not this line's, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling did that at about the seventh hour after he had been on the headland since two, and nobody told him to and nobody thanked him.

Harlan Vetch came up the four-mile road at about the sixth hour to see what had been done and stood at the bottom of the headland bank and looked up it and did not go up.

**"Four years is a long time for a bank."** He said it to nobody and he said it out loud so that it was a thing said rather than a thing thought. **"I held an end of a thing for four years and nobody in that building ever asked me why I was still holding it, and it turned out I was not holding it for a reason anybody else wanted. That is not the same thing as a bank."** He went back down the four-mile road at about the seventh hour and nobody followed him and nobody asked him to go up.

Marek was in the yard from the fifth hour and was not out at the headland at any hour of this morning, and about a third of the people in this holding have noticed that and about a third have not, and neither of the two thirds has ever said so to anybody. He worked the near end of the ditch for an hour and a half and came in and sat down on the bench by the pump house for about twenty minutes and did not say anything to anybody, and the man of about fifty came past the bench about four times and did not ask him how he was.

**"I have not asked him about it and I have thought about asking him about it every hour since Saturday."** The man of about fifty said it at the long table in the middle of the morning and gave the reason for it first and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I ask him because if I ask him then I am a person who asked a man with a thing in his head how he was, and he will answer it, and then it is in a room, and it stays in a room. And if I do not ask him then it is his."**

He turned the book over and did not open it.

**"And I have noticed that there is a person in this yard who has not asked him either and that person is me and that I have been getting up in the morning for four days deciding not to."** Nobody at that table said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody thanked him.

The man of about fifty's two counts are not the two reckonings. What he keeps is the count of the mornings this line has been in force and the count of the mornings it has been worked, and the two do not agree, and he has never said that out loud in a room with a person in it.

**"There is a figure in this yard that everybody has been reading for four years as a count of fields."** He said it at the shut third book, gave the reason for it first, and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"Twenty-nine. Four years. I have written it down every fourth morning and I have copied it off a sheet that came from somewhere else, and I have never once seen twenty-nine fields."** He put the pencil down. **"I asked a man from Marden at the gate this morning whether he knew what the twenty-nine was and he said he did not, and that he had always assumed it was fields, because that is what everybody assumes it is."** Nobody at that table picked that up and nobody thanked him.

He looked at the shut book for about half a minute without touching it.

**"I am not saying it is not fields. I am saying that four years of a man in this building copying a number has got us no closer to what it counts than four years of nobody copying it, and this morning the number is the same number it was yesterday and the bank got worked anyway, and those two facts are sitting about nine inches apart on the same morning and I do not know what to do with them."**

At about the eighth hour the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing at the gate, and she gave the reason for it first. **"I have spent four years asking a body of households for a figure and getting one that is a figure of wanting, and this morning the number everybody has been reading as fields has not moved and something got worked, and I want to say that the second of those is not a vindication of anything and it is not a lesson and it is not going in a letter."** She shut her own book. **"A bank that got worked this morning did not get worked because a number went up. It got worked because two men went up it with barrows at the second hour."** She set the bucket down on the step. **"If the number had moved I would still have the same two facts in front of me and one of them would be a number and the other one would be a bank, and the bank is the one you can stand on."** Nobody in that gate said anything about that and nobody asked her for a figure.

Nia Vale was out with the moving reserves before the fourth hour and the districts that draw on the stored light are in her own book and were not read out in this yard and were not written in the second book, and a list of neighbors is a list of neighbors.

From the second hour Tova Reed had the four counties' sheets on the middle table, the sowing date in three hands against it and the fourth hand not on it, and there is no space left on that sheet for a fourth hand and she is not going to rule one. **"Two men went up a bank this morning and there is a figure about it and I am not going to use the figure."** She put her hand flat on the fourth sheet. **"I have three councils' dates in writing and a fourth that wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing, and what a released pattern does to a sowing date is the question I am living inside this month. If I take a number off a headland into a room in this county I will have started a thing I cannot finish before the sowing, and I will have spent the credit I have with three councils on the fourth."** Nobody in that room said she was wrong and nobody said she was right and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

**"Two men went up a bank this morning and there is a figure about it and I am not going to use the figure."** She said that and put her hand flat on the fourth sheet. **"I have three councils' dates in writing and a fourth that wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing, and what a released pattern does to a sowing date is the question I am living inside this month."** She did not look up from the sheets. **"If I take a number off a headland into a room in this county I will have started a thing I cannot finish before the sowing, and I will have spent the credit I have with three councils on the fourth."** Nobody in that room said she was wrong and nobody said she was right and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

Off the middle road at about the ninth hour came the man of about thirty-one of Silling, who put his bar back on the wall, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and seventy-six days old with nothing on it and nobody has asked him about it.

About the seventh hour Sera Quill came down the fen road, went into the seed house, came out again, and stood for a few minutes in front of the low board without touching it. **"A field that somebody worked today and nobody took is the first thing in four months I have been told about that I can put in a county book without a heading on it."** She said it to the board and not to the yard.

**"I am not going to say that is the whole of it and I am not going to say it is nothing."** She said that to the board and then said the rest of it to the road. **"I am going to write the date of it down and the date of nothing else, and I am not going to write a word about what it means, because what it means is a thing four counties will argue about for a year and I have got a list of eighty-one families in a book four counties off and I have not got the rest of the week."** Nobody in that yard asked her what it means and she got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

The old man with a stick had the north wall from the third hour to the sixth, came in, and stood under the eaves. He said one number out loud to nobody, and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it.

The man of the north row with the cough was not asked to hold an end of anything and did not go up to the headland, and the four ruled lines under his condition are bare and the answer is not due.

Twenty-five, and paid in front of it, and neither of those has moved this morning. The register form is a hundred and eighty-two days into its stay on the middle table, with no heading at the head of its column, filled by nobody and refused by nobody. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and seventy-six days old, and has nothing on it, and nobody has offered him a pen for it.

Seven sessions stand entered in this holding's book and nobody entered one this morning, and in the corner of the one room the four ruled lines under two words are bare and the answer is not due. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes. The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth for a headland worked for the first time in four years, because a headland is not a use.

A bank a barrow can get up is not a seventh, and a field is not a seventh. What this holding does not know is six things, and no unit stands against any of the six, and the seventh row is not ruled and is not going to be ruled this month.

In a drawer in this building there is named a corridor in the pan. It is not established as on. The sheet that claims it is correct in every particular and it lets nothing out. A culvert under a machine house nine miles off is not that corridor, and the two have never been added to one another here. The order sealing the route is in the drawer under the two books, it is valid, and it opens nothing.

There is nothing on the paper on the low board, and no date on it, and no line anywhere on it for a name, and it is face up where it has lain for four months. Nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board and nobody is going to be told, and a field worked at a headland this morning is not a reason for taking anything off it.

The night was dry. By the seventh hour a man could walk the headland bank and the middle road had neither taken nor given anything, the north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding wrote the eighteenth in at the ninth hour and read it back once, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked for the bank. The standing is a hundred and eleven in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-two not, and twenty-nine taken did not move this morning, because working a line and taking it are two different things.

# Chapter 528

## A List Of Neighbors, And A Fourth Council That Wrote Nothing

The third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the work is the two culverts on the middle road, the fill behind them, and the low field gate at the end of it. The fill had gone out about half an inch in the night and went back twice over, and the gate wanted a bar and a stone and took the nine minutes it always takes.

The twenty-first went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and forty-four hundredweight.**

Friday gave five back off Thursday and the fall went onto the falls, and the launder carries the two hundred and ninety-first figure of a run in which no number has ever been written down twice. The window behind it came out at a hundred and thirty-four mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-four the other, and the two of them are two hundred and fifty-eight, and the man of about fifty read one half and the clerk read the other and neither of them told the other what they had read.

Nine days on that sheet is what a body of households gets, and the figure this morning is two hundred and eighty-nine, with the clause underneath taking the two hundred and eighty-eighth of those mornings out of two hundred and ninetieth. One under and one over. A subscription on somebody else's paper is neither a morning nor a household and has never been added to either.

Both men who read the boards this morning were somewhere else at the seventh hour, so nobody stood at the long wall at all: the near one carries six hundred and seventy-five days and the far one seven hundred and twenty-two, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one was not opened at any hour of this morning by anybody at all.

**One hundred and sixteen mornings of two hundred and fifteen**, of a month seven months back, went into the second book at the ninth hour, and both men who read the boards said their own half out loud to the plaster and neither of them said the other's.

Nia Vale was out of the gate before the fourth hour and she is not support staff and nobody in this building has ever offered her a chair and she did not ask for one. She coordinates the moving reserves and she was on the road with two of them at about the sixth hour, at a trough eleven miles off, and she came back at about the eleventh hour with the road on her boots.

The districts that draw on the stored light are in her own book. It is a long list and it is longer than anybody in this holding has supposed and it is not a column and it is not in the second book and it was not read out in a yard this morning, and she has never once said the number of them out loud in this building.

**"I have got a list and I am not going to read it out and here is the reason and it is the only reason I have."** She said it at the long table at about the twelfth hour and she gave the reason before she said the thing. **"The first time I read a list like that out loud in a room, four years ago, a man wrote it down and a fortnight later somebody used it to say that a district which was third on the list had not asked for what it was getting, and he was right, and the asking took two years and the district went without for about a month."**

She put her hand flat on her own book and kept it there.

**"That is a long list of neighbors."** She said it without lifting the hand. **"It is not a list of enemies and it is not a list of anybody's dependents. Every name on that page draws on the light out of a building nine miles off because there is nothing else to draw on, and if it is a list of anything at all it is a list of who would be standing in a dark room in a fortnight if the light went down."**

Nobody in that room asked to see the list and nobody said she was hiding it.

**"I am not going to hand that to a man with a pencil in a county that has never been to the building."** She took the hand off the book and left the book where it was.

**"There is one thing on the list I am going to say out loud, once, and then it is going back in."** She said that to the shut book and nobody in the room moved. **"My own district is on it. It is eighth. I have known that for nine years and I have never said it in this building, and I am saying it because if a thing like that is a list of neighbors, then the person who keeps it is on it."** Nobody at that table said anything and nobody thanked her and she shut the book and stood up and went out into the yard and stood at the tap for a while and did not open it.

Four counties' sheets, middle table, from the second hour, and the sowing date on them in three hands and not in a fourth, and no space left on that sheet for a fourth hand, and Tova Reed is not going to rule one.

**"The fourth council wrote on the twenty-eighth and the letter said nothing."** She said it to the table and nobody in that room was waiting for her to say it. **"It is a council of about nineteen households and it has a man on it who was a customer of that building nine miles off and signed a charter in a room in the same week, and both of those documents are correct, and in about three weeks I am going to have to sit in front of nineteen households and explain a sowing date to them and I have not got a fourth date."**

She squared the sheets against the edge of the table and did not look up while she said the rest of it.

**"What a released pattern does to a sowing date is the question I am living inside this month."** She said that to the fourth sheet. **"It is not a question anybody in this building can answer for me and I am not going to ask anybody in this building to answer it for me."**

The ear stops none of the three things that are asked of her and makes none of the three easier, and this page has said that once and is not going to say it again. She is a capable leader of a front with a sowing date in it and nobody in that room said a word about the ear and nobody is going to and nobody put a hand on her arm and nobody thanked her and nobody took the seed work off her.

**"There is a thing I am not going to do and I am going to say out loud that I am not doing it."** She said that at about the ninth hour without looking up from the fourth sheet. **"A wheel got chosen nine miles off on Thursday and I am not going to take a number off this week into any of those four rooms. Not a draw figure, not a date, not a level off a needle, not anything. And I am not doing it to be careful."**

The clerk of this holding had the second book open at the other end of the table and did not write in it while she was talking.

**"I am doing it because the second I carry a thing from that building into a room in this county, four councils are going to be having an opinion about a wheel instead of about a sowing date."** She put one finger on the third sheet and did not move it. **"There are four hundred thousand people who eat out of that thing and not one of them was asked to, and a sowing date is the only thing any of those four councils has ever asked me about, and I am not going to hand them a second thing to have an opinion about in the middle of a season."** Nobody in that room said she was right and nobody said she was wrong and nobody asked her whether that was a decision or a preference and it was not asked.

The seed house door was hers for most of the morning, with the schedule and its six ruled rows, and she did not come out of it. The oilskin satchel was on the long table under the four pound weight where it has been all week, and a sheet that went nine miles and came back in that satchel is the same sheet and is not a column and the seventh row is not ruled.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in at the ninth hour and put the bar back on the wall and stood with his back to the yard the way he does, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and seventy-nine days old and has nothing on it and nobody has asked him about it in a hundred and seventy-nine days. The old man with a stick worked the north wall from the third hour to the sixth, came in under the eaves, and said one number out loud to nobody, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it.

The fourth hour of the afternoon, and the man of about fifty with one thing on the long table, and the reason for it given before the thing and no figure printed for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"There are two women in this building carrying things this week and neither of them is going to put what she is carrying in front of anybody."** He said it to the shut third book. **"I have watched one of them go nine miles four days running and come home with a piece of paper and not a figure, and I have watched the other one sit at that table for six days with three dates in three hands and not a fourth."**

He turned the book over and turned it back and did not open it.

**"And I have watched about four people in this yard get a number this week and be asked to say it out loud, and every one of those four people has said it out loud."** He put his thumb in the cover. **"I am not blaming the four of them and I am not going to say what the difference is, because if I say what the difference is then it is a rule, and a rule is a thing a person can be caught by."** Nobody at that table said he was right and nobody thanked him. **"I will say that I did not ask either of them anything this week and I have asked about four people, and I have thought about asking about nine more."**

About an hour of the middle of the day went by with Marek in the one room and the door open and nothing in front of him. The ring stayed unwalked and the gate stayed shut behind him, the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil, and about a wheel he said nothing that anybody in that room expected him to say.

The man of the north row with the cough came in off the middle road at the seventh hour and read the line painted on the wall by the window from where he stood without going up to it, and went out again and said nothing to anybody about it. The four ruled lines under his condition are bare and the answer is not due and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything.

The compost line is paid at twenty-five and has not moved, and paid is the word that goes in front of that figure. The register form has been on the middle table a hundred and eighty-five days, its column headingless, and it is neither filled nor refused. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and seventy-nine days old and there is nothing on it and nobody has asked for it.

The book in this holding still says seven sessions and nobody added to it this morning, and the nine minutes at a hand-lever nine miles off is not a session and is never going to be entered as one.

The four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due. The requests are fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and neither of them moved and nobody has ever added the two of them together.

A list of districts is not a seventh, a sowing date is not a seventh, and neither of them is a column. Six is what this holding has ruled for the things it does not know, no unit stands against any of the six, and the seventh row is not ruled.

A woman saying the number of her own place in a list out loud in a yard is not a use, and the log kept by the man who keeps it has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth. The barrow went up the four-mile road at the sixth hour with three sacks and came down loaded, its journeys are eleven, and eleven is a floor, and the four miles are not the nine and the nine are not the coast road.

Whoever holds the far end of a thing, it is not this body, and there is nothing in four counties that can be looked at to be told whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has ever said that it is on. Nine hundred yards of rail is a distance in a building, a wheel behind that rail is a wheel, and a list of districts in a book is a list, and none of the three is that thing and none of the three has been added to another in this room.

A corridor in the pan is named in a drawer in this building and it is not established as on, and the sheet that claims it is correct in every particular and lets nothing out. The order sealing the route is in the drawer under the two books, it is valid, it opens nothing, and no apology for it is going to be written.

Nine inches is the distance between the two sheets about the two declinings on the long table, and it has been that for a month, and a long list of neighbors and a fourth council that wrote nothing did not move them. The third column of the sheet of terms on the long wall is ruled and empty and has a heading that has not been improved, and a clause about a machine is not a term and a sixth is not going to be written.

The paper on the low board has nothing on it, has never had a date on it, and has no line anywhere on it that a name could go into, and it lies face up. Nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off and nobody is going to be told, and a long list of districts that draw on the stored light is not a reason for taking anything off that board.

Four inches and two branches is what is under that sleeve, and no second mark is on that arm in this holding. Four accounts of it exist and none of the four agrees with another about anything. His sleeve was down from the gate to the gate, and nobody asked him once what is under it, and a morning on which a woman said her own place in a list out loud did not make a fifth.

There was no rain and a low sun all morning that came in under the eaves of the one room about the tenth hour, and the middle road took nothing and gave nothing back. The north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Nobody thanked anybody. The twenty-first went into the clerk's book at the ninth hour and he read it once. A long list of neighbors went back into a book in a yard where nobody read it out, and three councils' sowing dates went to a seed house with a fourth hand not on the sheet, and neither of those things was thanked and neither was refused.

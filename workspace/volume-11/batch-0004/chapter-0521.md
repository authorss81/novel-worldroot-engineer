# Chapter 521

## Four Hands, And A Field Nobody Took

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there was nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and that is the eighty-fourth morning running that it has gone past unworked. The standing is a hundred and ten mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-one not, and the count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on the other three of the week.

The two reckonings are both on this page and the thirteen between them has been there four years. Kept in this room, the fourteenth of this month is the fifty-eighth morning of it. Kept the other way, the same morning is the forty-fifth, and nobody in this building has ever worked out what the thirteen counts, and it does not come out of the twenty-nine.

What came up the fen road four days ago is on the middle table in four hands now, and there are four of them because four people carried it back off a road at nine miles. It is a wheel, a hand-lever under it, a rule that says two at a wheel, and about nine hours. It is not in any book in this building, and it is not a column and it is not a heading and it is not a day at the foot of anything, and the man of about fifty copied the drawing badly and four people have a copy of the bad copy between them.

No day has been given to anybody in this holding. That is the thing the week has and not another thing, and the four people who went nine miles to be shown a method came home with a method and a bad copy of one and no morning attached to either of them.

The fourteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and forty hundredweight.**

Friday came up eight on Thursday and nothing was set against it, and the launder takes the two hundred and eighty-fourth figure of a run in which nothing has ever been written twice. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had the two halves of the window off the long wall before the seventh hour and said them to each other rather than to the room, one hundred and thirty-one mornings one way and a hundred and twenty the other, and the two of them are two hundred and fifty-one.

The clerk put the four bodies of households at two hundred and eighty-two days on this morning's sheet and the clause under that figure took the two hundred and eighty-first of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-third. One under and one over, and neither of those three is a household and neither of them is a person, and the four launders and the four bodies of households have never once been added together in four years of this sheet.

Near board of the long wall, six hundred and sixty-eight days, and the far one seven hundred and fifteen, and the forty-seven between them as ever. The drawer behind the near board stood shut at every hour of this morning and nobody put a thumb on it.

Both men who read the boards were at the far end of the yard with a bar and a stone, and neither of them stood at the long wall at the seventh hour, and the second book carries nothing this morning where the run of mornings goes. There is no figure on this page for the run and the reason is on the page too: half of the run is half a number on this morning and not a whole one. The frame of that run is of a month seven months back.

In the yard at about the sixth hour, with nothing in his hands and nowhere in particular to be, was Harlan Vetch, who has not stood in this yard since the middle of a month now three months back. Nobody sent for him. Nobody has written his name on anything this week.

**"I heard there was a wheel in it."** He said it to the gatepost and then he said the rest of it to the middle road. **"I have held an end of a thing four years with no heading on it and no name on it and nobody in this building asking me whether I would, and I have never once told anybody I was good at it because I have never once been asked."**

Somebody at the tap had said four of them went nine miles and got a drawing back.

**"I would like to see the drawing and I am not going to ask what it is for."** Nobody in that yard told him he was good at it and nobody told him he was not.

The man of about fifty came out of the shed at about the seventh hour and put the bad copy of the drawing on the middle table face up, and nobody in that room copied it again and nobody turned it over.

**"That is a man who has been somewhere with a bar in his hand for nine years and not a man who has been somewhere with a book."** He said it to the drawing and gave the reason for it before he said it, and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have carried the thing nine miles in my own head for a week and I could not have drawn it, and he has not seen it and he has already asked the only question about it that matters, which is what it is for, and he has said he is not going to ask."** Nobody at that table picked the two up and set them against each other and nobody thanked him.

Harlan Vetch came to the middle table at about the eighth hour and looked at the drawing for a long time without touching it, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden turned the lamp so it was on the paper and not in his eyes, which she did without being asked.

**"I can see the wheel."** He said it plainly and he put his finger nowhere near the paper. **"I cannot see the lever. Whoever drew this had the thing in front of them and a man who had not would never have known a lever was in it at all."** He said that to the man of about fifty and not to anybody else, and the man of about fifty said nothing back, and after a while he put his thumb on the edge of the paper and said, **"That is right."** Two words, and neither of them was an apology, and nobody at that table said anything else about the drawing for the rest of the morning.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing at the tap at about the ninth hour that nobody had asked her for, and she gave the reason for it before she said it and she did not put it in her own book.

**"I have spent four years putting things in front of people."** She said it to the standpipe and not to the yard. **"Letters with a figure on them, sheets with a heading on them, a form that a body of households wanted certified."** She said the three of them to the standpipe and did not look at the tank while she said them. **"And the reason I do it is that a thing on paper can be looked at again tomorrow, and that is the whole of the reason, and this morning there is a piece of paper on my table with three wheels on the back of it and there is no figure anywhere on it."**

She shut the tap and wiped her hands on her coat and stood there.

**"I have been sitting here since the second hour trying to work out what I am supposed to do with a thing nobody can be shown twice."** She said that to the yard and not to the table. **"That is the first time in four years the paper has not been the point and I am not going to invent a use for it before somebody hands me one."**

Not one person in that yard offered her a use for it, and nobody told her she had misunderstood anything.

Marek was at the north end of the yard from about the fifth hour with a bar in his hands and nothing in front of him, and he worked the top of the middle road for an hour and a half without saying anything at all, and he came back in and put the bar on the wall and stood with his sleeve down.

Somebody asked him about the four hands and about the figure in them, and he gave the reason before he gave the answer and he did not shorten it. **"I have been in that building four days and I have come home with a thing that took a person nine minutes to describe and about nine hours to do. I have not got a date and I have not been asked for one and I have not offered one, and if I gave you a morning this morning you would be planning against a figure that a woman nine miles off wrote on the back of a schedule sheet because there was no other side of it left."** He said it to the wall and not to the table.

He put his thumb on the bar he had just put down and left it there.

**"A person who hands you a method has not handed you a morning."** He said that to the middle road. **"I have watched four rooms in this county turn a sheet into a plan and a plan into a day inside a single week and I am not going to be the fifth, and I have said that once this month already and I am saying it again because there is a piece of paper on that table with my week on the back of it."**

Not one person in that room said he was wrong. The clerk of this holding wrote one line on the day sheet about a sheet of paper with a drawing on the back of it and did not copy the drawing and did not put a name against who had drawn it. A form is not a column and a diagram is not a plan and neither of them is going into a book in this building.

At about the seventh hour Sera Quill came down the fen road with a county book under her arm that this holding has no copy of and did not ask for, and on her way past she stood at the low board and did not touch the paper lying face up on it. There is no date on that paper and no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off that board.

**"I have spent four months saying a sum out loud in four counties and every one of the four counties has wanted to know what it buys."** She said it at the low board and not to anybody in it. **"There is a piece of paper on your table with no sum on it and no heading on it and I have looked at it for about a minute and a half and I cannot tell you what it is worth."** She put the county book down against her hip. **"I want you to know that I have been in eleven record rooms in my life and that has never happened to me in one."** Nobody asked her what she thought it was worth and she got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

He worked the north wall from the third hour to the sixth and came in with no frost in his coat, and on his way past the table he said one number out loud to nobody, and nobody in this yard has ever asked him how he knew it. The man of about thirty-one of Silling came off the four-mile road at the ninth hour, put his own bar back on the wall, and stood with his back to the yard the way he does.

He took the middle road from the third hour with a bar and did the top of the low field gate by himself, and the four ruled lines under his condition are as bare as they have been since a clerk ruled them, and the answer they are for is not due. Nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this week and nobody is going to.

Nia Vale was out of the gate before the fourth hour with the moving reserves and she is not support staff and nobody in this building has ever offered her a chair. The districts that draw on the stored light are in her own book and the number of them was not said out loud in this yard and was not written in the second book, and a list of neighbors is a list of neighbors and is not a rate and is not a drawing and is not a plan.

Tova Reed has three councils' dates in writing on the middle table from the second hour and a fourth council that wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing at all, and she is on the seed front in four counties and nobody has taken any of it off her. She is a capable leader of a front with a sowing date in it and the ear stops none of the three things that are asked of her and makes none of the three easier, and this page is going to say that once and is not going to say it a second time this month.

Nobody in that room said a word about her ear. Nobody put a hand on her arm and nobody thanked her and nobody said she could be relieved of a county.

The word that goes in front of the compost figure is paid and it has gone in there for eleven years, and the figure itself has not moved off twenty-five. On the middle table the register form has now been lying a hundred and seventy-eight days with nothing at the head of its column, unfilled, unrefused, and nobody has read it out a fifth time. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and seventy-two days old, and in a hundred and seventy-two days he has not been asked about it.

The charter was signed eighty-four days ago in a room nine miles off and three of the four bodies of households that signed it are still signed to a machine for stored light, and one of the four has put in writing that it is not signing again. Both of those are correct this morning and neither of them has any opinion about the other one, and nobody in this yard asked anybody to give either of them up and nobody in this yard gave anything up.

There was no rain in the night and the ground was hard enough by the eighth hour that a bar rang off it, and the frost has not been back in the ground for six mornings. The middle road took nothing and gave nothing back. The north end of the ditch is running full off the first line, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Nobody thanked anybody, and the clerk of this holding wrote the fourteenth in at the ninth hour, read it back once, and put the pen down without reading it a second time. There is a manufactured figure in four hands in this yard and a headland with nobody on it, and this holding has not been named to ask anybody about either of them.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The fourteenth. The fourth line of the rota came round and there was nobody at the headland to take it, being the eighty-fourth morning, the standing a hundred and ten in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty-one not, and the two reckonings at fifty-eight and forty-five. A sheet with a wheel on the back of it is in four hands and is not in a book. The pair not read and no figure standing against it, the half of the run being half a number on this morning. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and forty hundredweight to the launder, being the two hundred and eighty-fourth.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

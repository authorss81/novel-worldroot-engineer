# Chapter 527

## Which One Of Three, And Not By Him

At the second hour the second line of the rota came round and was worked: the outlet end, the two stones at the head of it, the pack under the near stone, which came out and went back dry, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling with his hand flat on the stone and the word not said.

The twentieth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and forty-nine hundredweight.**

Thursday came up eight on Wednesday and nothing was set against it, and the launder takes the two hundred and ninetieth figure of a run in which no number has ever been entered twice. The window behind it came to a hundred and thirty-four mornings against a hundred and twenty-three, and those two are two hundred and fifty-seven, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read one of them off the near board and the other man read the other one and neither of them read both.

Two hundred and eighty-eight days, the sheet says, against the four bodies of households, and the clause under that takes the two hundred and eighty-seventh of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-ninth. One under, one over. It is four years since anybody in this building set a body of households against a morning, and the two sets have never been in one column.

Forty-seven is the gap on the long wall this morning: six hundred and seventy-four days on the near board, seven hundred and twenty-one on the far one, and the drawer behind the near one stayed shut at every hour.

The clerk left the place in the second book empty this morning, as he does every second morning, and the reason is on this page: half of the run is half a number and not a whole one, so there is no figure for the run to enter. The frame of that run is of a month seven months back.

They can do one.

Not three and not two. One of the three wheels, once, on one morning, and the reason they can do one is arithmetic and the reason it is one is not arithmetic. Three wheels with about nine hours apiece is twenty-seven hours of a person and a team of two does not fit in that, and the man of about fifty worked it out on Saturday and got the same answer twice.

Which one of the three was not decided in this yard and was not decided by Marek and was not decided by the woman who keeps the gauges.

Marek was told about it at about the seventh hour, at the tap, by Harlan Vetch, who had been up the four-mile road at the fourth hour and come back.

**"It is the second one."** Vetch said it to the standpipe and gave the reason for it before he said it. **"I went up at the fourth hour because I have been up there every morning this week with nobody asking me to, and this morning the man who reads the schedule off the wall was at the second wheel and not at his desk, and he was standing in front of it with his coat on, and he had a sheet folded in his hand."**

The water came through the standpipe into the bucket and neither of them looked at it.

**"When I asked him whether the door was going to be open he read me four lines off the sheet and went back to his desk."** He put his hand on the pump. **"I am telling you what I was told and I am telling you I was not in the room and I was not meant to be."**

He shut the tap off and stood with his hand still on it.

**"Somebody in that building had already written it down before I got there and I have got no idea who wrote it and I have not asked and I am not going to ask, because the first time I ask that question in a room I am a person who is checking, and I have held an end of a thing for four years without checking anything."**

Nobody at the tap asked him who had written it. Nobody at the tap said it was a decision and nobody at the tap said it was not one.

**"It is the second one and it was not me."** Marek said that to the middle road and he did not shorten it. **"I want to be exact about this because in about three days a room full of people who were not in it is going to say that the engineer of record chose the wheel, and I did not. I have been nine miles inside that building four days this month and I have laid a hand on one wheel for nine minutes and I have not had an opinion about which one and I have not said one out loud to anybody, and if it turns out I have an opinion about it now then that is a thing that has happened to me since Monday and not a thing I did."** He turned round about half way. **"And the woman who keeps the gauges has not chosen it either, and I want to say the second of those things carefully and separately."**

He put his hands in his pockets and stood there in the middle of the road.

**"I have watched her for four days and I would put it past anybody that she has spent a morning deciding which of three wheels four people she has never met are going to put a hand on. Somebody chose it. I have got no figure on who and I am not going to go and get one, and this building has never once gone and got one about anything."** Nobody in that yard said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody asked him how he felt about it.

An hour later than the tap, the man of about fifty had one thing to say at the long table, and he gave the reason for it before the thing and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"There is a difference between a thing being decided and a thing being handed over, and I have been in this building four years and I have watched about nine of the second and about two of the first."** He said it to the shut third book. **"What I have just been told is a second one."**

He put the pencil down on the table and did not pick it up again.

**"Somebody in a room nine miles off wrote down a wheel and folded a sheet and gave it to a man whose job is to read a wall, and I have been in a building this week where a man reads a wall and copies it word for word, and if that wall had a thing on it about a wheel then I have been in a building all week with the answer in it and I did not read it because I was looking at the man and not the wall."** Nobody at that table said he was right.

He sat with the pencil where he had put it for about a minute and then put it in his pocket.

**"I am not saying somebody hid it. I am saying that a man who has been in a room four days and comes home and says nobody chose anything is a man who has been looking at the people, and I have been that man for eleven years and it has cost this holding a name and it may have cost it a wheel."**

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one thing at the second gate at about the ninth hour and she gave the reason for it first.

**"Then we go and stand in front of the second one and we do not write a letter about it."** She said it to the gate and not to the yard. **"I have sat in four counties' meetings this year with a figure of wanting in front of me and every single time the thing that got somebody hurt was a document going out of a room faster than a person could walk out of it. I am not going to write a letter about a wheel. If somebody wants to know what we are doing they can come nine miles and stand in front of it, and if they do not come, then in six months there will not be anything to write about and that will have been the correct outcome."** Nobody at that gate told her she was wrong and nobody told her she was right.

Soren Rill came in at about the tenth hour and said one thing at the gate that nobody had asked him for and he gave no reason for it in front of it, because he had been on his feet since the fourth hour, and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on that account.

**"They chose the one they have been taking turns on."** He said it to the gatepost. **"There is a bar across one of them and a man counting out loud on one of them, and they picked the one in the middle, and I have been up there every day this week watching which of the three the men look at when they walk the floor, and it is that one. Whoever wrote the sheet did not choose a wheel out of three. They chose the one everybody in that room already thought about, and I do not know what to do with that and I am not going to do anything with it today."** Nobody at that gate picked it up and nobody thanked him.

Tova Reed is not in this room and she is on the seed front in four counties. Three councils have given her a sowing date in writing and the fourth wrote on the twenty-eighth and the letter said nothing, and what a released pattern does to a sowing date is the question she is living inside this month, and nobody has offered to take any of it off her and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

**"I have been told which wheel and I have been told it was not me."** She said that to the middle table at about the tenth hour and she gave the reason for it before she said it. **"I am going to say the part that is inconvenient and then I am going to go back to the sowing."**

She put the four counties' sheets in a stack and squared them against the edge of the table.

**"I have four counties' councils and a sowing date and a fourth council that has written to me once and said nothing, and if the first thing that happens to me in six weeks is that a wheel nine miles off has been turned and something has come back up in the ground, then every one of those four councils is going to ask me what I knew and when I knew it, and I am going to have to say that I knew on Thursday of this week and that it was not in any of their counties and that nobody had told them."** She did not look up while she said it.

Nobody in that room said anything and nobody asked her a second question.

**"I cannot carry a thing nine miles off into a room in this county and hold it up, because the second I do that it stops being a fact about a wheel and starts being a thing I have an opinion about, and I have watched four counties get very good at having opinions about things they were not asked about. So I am going to do the ordinary work and I am going to do it well and I am not going to mention it."** Nobody in that room asked her to mention it and nobody said she was wrong and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

About the seventh hour Sera Quill came down the fen road, went into the seed house, and stood a while in the doorway with the schedule in her hand. She did not ask anybody which wheel. Nobody in this building told her.

**"A record is a thing somebody decided to keep before anybody knew what it would be needed for."** She said it to the door frame and not to anybody. **"I have spent four months being asked what a list of eighty-one families is worth, and it is worth what was said in the room at the time and it is worth nothing at all to anybody who was not in the room."**

She shifted the county book under her arm and did not go in.

**"There is going to be a record of a wheel this year."** She said that to the seed house. **"Whoever keeps it is going to be asked in four years what they were told on the morning, and the honest answer is going to be that they were told one thing by a man at a tap and they have not found out anything since, and I have watched about six people ruin a good record by trying to make it look better than it was."** Nobody in that doorway asked her to keep the record and she got on the second bus at the ninth hour.

Harlan Vetch was asked nothing further about where he was standing and who he was standing near, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything this week and nobody is going to ask him again, and nobody has told him he is the man for the second wheel.

Paid is the word and twenty-five is the figure, and the figure has not moved. The register form is a hundred and eighty-four days old on the middle table and nothing has been written at the head of its column and it is neither filled nor refused. A hundred and seventy-eight days is how old the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is this morning, and it is blank, and it was blank a week ago.

Seven this morning, the same seven as yesterday, and the four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes, and six things this holding does not know with no unit against any of them and the seventh row not ruled. A wheel is not a seventh.

The far end of a thing is held by two people and this body is not one of them, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has ever said it is on. The second wheel behind nine hundred yards of rail is not that thing, and a sheet naming one wheel out of three is not that thing either, and the two have never been added together here.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres has not been walked this month and nobody has put a foot in it and nobody has measured it, and there is no dimension against it on any page in this building. It has been bare in its eleventh year, and a wheel that is not being turned is not that ring, and the two are not put in or around one another anywhere in this yard.

Under that sleeve is a mark of four inches that branches twice and there is not a second mark on the arm. The four accounts this holding has of it are four and they reconcile none of one another. His sleeve stayed down from the gate to the gate, nobody asked him once what is under it, and a morning on which a wheel was chosen in another room did not make a fifth account of anything.

About nine inches apart on the long table, where the two sheets about the two declinings have been for a month and a day, and a wheel being chosen nine miles off did not move either of them. The third column of the sheet of terms on the long wall is ruled and empty and has a heading that has not been improved, and a sixth term is not going to be written this month.

On the low board there is a sheet of paper with nothing written on it, no date on it and no line anywhere on it for a name, lying face up where it has lain for four months. Nobody in this holding has ever been told to take it off and nobody is going to be told, and the choice of one wheel out of three is not a reason for taking anything off that board.

About a quarter of an inch of it came back out of the middle road before the light, and the north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The clerk of this holding wrote the twentieth in at the ninth hour, read it back once and did not read it back twice, and nobody in this yard thanked anybody. One of three wheels was chosen in a room this holding was not in. The man who was told about it has no idea who chose it and did not go and find out.

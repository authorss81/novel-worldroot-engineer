# Chapter 524

## Nine Minutes, And A Hand Off A Spoke

Third line, second hour, worked. Two culverts on the middle road, the fill behind them, the low field gate at the end of it, and about half an inch of that fill gone out in the night and put back twice over. The gate took a bar and a stone and the nine minutes it always takes, and one of the men said the nine minutes out loud as though the saying of it made any difference to anything.

The seventeenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and thirty-eight hundredweight.**

Monday gave five back off Sunday and the fall went onto the falls, and what stands on the launder this morning is the two hundred and eighty-seventh figure of a run in which no number has ever been put down twice. The window behind it reads a hundred and thirty-two mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-two the other, and the two of them are two hundred and fifty-four, and the man who reads the far side of the boards said both halves out loud to the plaster this morning, which he does not usually do, and the other man said his own half back to himself and did not say the first man's.

The four bodies of households are at two hundred and eighty-five days on the sheet and the clause under that takes the two hundred and eighty-fourth of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-sixth. One under and one over at every site, and none of those three figures is anybody.

The near board of the long wall stands at six hundred and seventy-one days and the far one at seven hundred and eighteen, and the man who reads the far side put his thumb on the drawer behind the near one at the seventh hour without looking down. Forty-seven between them, and the drawer was not opened at any hour of this morning by anybody at all.

**One hundred and fourteen mornings of two hundred and eleven**, of a month seven months back, went into the second book at the ninth hour, and neither of the two men read the other's half back to him when they said it to each other at the seventh hour.

No panel is printed in any of it and none is printed in this holding, and nobody in this building has ever printed one; a gauge with a needle on it is a gauge, and a man with a cloth and a card is a man doing a morning's work.

Four of them went out of the gate at about the fourth hour, on foot, and the barrow stayed where it was. Its journeys are eleven and eleven is a floor.

Not one person in either building had arranged the great door, and it was open when the four of them got there at about the seventh hour.

Harlan Vetch was the fourth. He came through the great door at about the seventh hour and stood four feet inside it and did not go any further until somebody in that building told him he could.

No one in this holding chose him and this page says so plainly. Marek did not choose him. Iona Vey did not choose him. No one in a room nine miles off on the other side of this fen chose him either, and if a name is going to be attached to the choice of who stands at the other end of a frame nine miles from here, it is not going to be attached to it by anybody in this building.

He came because a piece of paper with three wheels on the back of it came through a yard and he heard about it at a gate.

**"Nobody asked me."** He said it to the coping and gave the reason for it before he said it. **"I want that on whatever page there is. I have held an end of a thing for four years with no document and no heading and nobody's name on it, and the last time I stood on a casting my hands were cold for the first hour and I did not tell anybody, and I am telling you now because I am about to do it again and I would rather somebody knew what I am like beforehand than have to work it out on the day."** Nobody in that room told him he was qualified and nobody told him he was not and nobody thanked him for coming.

They gave him the second wheel. The one the man who counts out loud had been on all week came off it at about the eighth hour and the two of them took it in turns for the morning as they always do, and the first wheel went on being turned in fours by two men who did not look at anybody.

Iona Vey was in the room from the second hour and she was the only person in that building not doing a job, and she said one thing at about the ninth hour at the second wheel and nobody had asked her for it.

**"I am going to say the thing that will get said at me afterwards, so that it is said."** She said it without heat and without a figure in it. **"You have a hand-lever and a rule that says two and about nine hours, and one of those nine hours will be a man feeling what the other man is feeling, and the frame walks."** She put her hand flat on the casting and took it off again. **"I am not going to tell you which of you is wrong. I am telling you that a rule that says two is a rule about what happens when one of the two has had a bad night, and not one of you has asked the other one that question and one of you has had a bad night, on the evidence of his hands this morning, and I am not going to say which one."** Nobody in that room thanked her and nobody said she was wrong.

Then Marek put his hand on a spoke and Harlan Vetch put his hand flat on the casting at the other end of the frame, and it was done for about nine minutes.

There is no name on this page for what it is and this page is not going to give it one. It was one morning. It was bounded at about nine minutes and the nine minutes were called out by the man who counts out loud from the first wheel, and nobody in either building added anything to it and nobody took anything off it.

In about the fourth minute Marek could feel both ends.

Not what the other man was thinking. The frame, and the other man's hand flat on the casting, and the line of the wheel going round, and the ground under nine hundred yards of rail, and the far end of a thing that two people hold and neither of them being him. It came up the way water comes up in a ditch when the north end is full, and there was no handle on it.

In about the seventh minute the frame walked. It went about an inch and stopped, and both men put their weight into it and it came back, and Marek felt the walking in the same place Harlan Vetch felt it and neither of them said a word and neither of them looked at the other.

At about the ninth minute the man who counts out loud said a number from the first wheel and Marek took his hand off the spoke.

Afterwards he could not get the two ends out of his head. He went down the rail to the tank end and stood with both hands on the stone of the coping and stayed there for the better part of an hour, and the man who keeps the gauges walked past him four times and looked at his hands both first times and did not say anything.

**"There is a thing I can do that I could not do a week ago."** He said it to the coping and he gave the reason for it first, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and sixty-two, and it is his and it is in no book in this building. **"I am going to say what it is and what it is worth and then I am going to stop, because I have got nine minutes in my head that will not come out and I have not slept and I am not going to stand here and sell it to anybody."** He said that to the stone and not to the tank. **"I put a hand on a spoke and somebody I did not choose put a hand on the far side of a frame and for about nine minutes I could feel both ends of it. That is all. I do not know what it is for, I do not know whether I can do it again, and I have been sitting here for an hour trying to put down one of the two ends and I cannot."**

The man of about fifty was at about eleven feet from him at the time and heard all of it and came and stood beside him at the coping and said nothing for about a minute and a half.

**"Then you have a cost and not a thing you can do."** He said it to the stone, and he gave the reason for it before he said it, and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have been in this building four years and I have watched about six people get something they wanted and I have watched what it did to four of them, and the four of them were not worse people afterward, they were just harder to get, and every one of them got harder to get on the same morning they got it."** He did not say which six and he did not say which four. **"You have just become harder to get. That is not a criticism. I am telling you it because I have watched this room work out what it wants and it has never once been what it wanted."** Nobody in that room said he was right and nobody thanked him.

**"I am answerable for a thing I do not hold."** Marek said that at about the tenth hour and he did not shorten it. **"The far end of a thing is held by two people and neither of them is me, and there is no instrument in four counties that says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has ever said it is on, and this morning I could feel it and I still cannot tell you whether it is on. So I have got a thing in me now that tells me about an end I have no vote in, and the honest position is that I do not have a vote in it and I have got a way of noticing when it moves."** He said that to the coping. **"I am not going to be the man who decides, because the man who decides is the man who cannot be asked a question, and I have just found out this morning that being able to feel an end is not the same as being asked anything about it, and I would rather have found that out on a hand-lever in a building nine miles off than on a bridge with the whole county standing on it."**

Not one person in that room asked him how he felt about it or how he was going to sleep, and nobody thanked him for nine minutes.

Harlan Vetch said one thing at the second wheel at about the eleventh hour and he said it to the man who counts out loud and not to anybody else, and he gave the reason for it first.

**"My hands were cold for the first two minutes and I did not tell him and I am telling you now, at about the eleventh hour, and not to him."** He said it to the wheel. **"I did not tell him because if I had told him at the second minute he would have taken his hand off the spoke, and then we would have done four minutes instead of nine and neither of us would have found out about the walking. That is a thing I did and I would do it again and I would rather somebody in this building had it than have it kept for Friday."** The man who counts out loud looked at him for about two seconds and said **"Aye"** and went back to his four, and nobody in that building wrote any of it down and nobody has asked him about it since.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at the tank end for the last hour of it and she did not go near the second wheel at all while it was happening, and afterward she said one thing to the man of about fifty and gave the reason before she said it.

**"I have watched a thing happen in a room and I have not been in the room, and that is the correct place for me to have been, and I want to say so out loud before anybody in this building spends a week deciding that I was kept out of something."** She said it to the long table in this yard at about the fourth hour in the afternoon, not to him. **"There is a thing about that this morning that nobody in this yard is going to be able to do and I am one of the people who is not going to be able to do it, and I would rather that were on the page than have it discovered in three weeks by somebody who thinks I have been kept out of a room."** Nobody at that table said she had been kept out of anything and nobody thanked her for saying it.

Out through the great door at about the eleventh hour and back into the yard at the second hour in the afternoon, and the barrow had not gone and did not go in the afternoon.

Paid goes in front of twenty-five, as it has for eleven years, and nothing has moved. The register form has been on the middle table a hundred and eighty-one days and there is still nothing at the head of its column; it is not filled, it is not refused, and it is not going to be read out a fifth time. A hundred and seventy-five days is the age of the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book and there is nothing on it.

Nothing went into this holding's book this morning as a session, and the rehearsal of nine minutes at a hand-lever in a building nine miles off is not a session and was not entered as one and is not going to be entered as one, and the number of sessions in that book is still seven. A thing that is not entered did not happen whatever anybody says about it afterwards, and there is work this month that this holding has not entered and is not going to enter.

The four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due. The requests are fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three. Six things this holding does not know are ruled with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and a rehearsal is not a seventh and a wheel is not a seventh and a wheel behind a rail is not that bridge and the two are not added in this building.

Harlan Vetch walked up the four-mile road with the party and did not get tired and did not say so. Nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything since he came in yesterday and nobody is going to ask him again.

The yard rang a bar dropped on it by the ninth hour and nothing had fallen on it all night. The middle road took nothing and gave nothing back, the north end of the ditch is running full, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Nobody in this building thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the seventeenth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and left it there, and about nine minutes went into a hand on a spoke and a hand on a casting, and neither end would let go of him afterwards, and this page carries no figure for what that is.

# Chapter 522

## Nine Miles Go And The Barrow Does Not

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and the work was water: the north end of the ditch, the trough at the head of the four-mile road, and the standpipe in the yard. The ditch had taken an inch off its own lip in the night and gave about half of it back before the light, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling walked the whole length of it with a bar and said nothing.

The fifteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and thirty-five hundredweight.**

Saturday gave five back off Friday and the fall went onto the falls, and the launder carries the two hundred and eighty-fifth figure of a run in which no number has ever been entered twice. The man of about fifty read both halves of the window off the long wall at the seventh hour without being asked and did not say either of them out loud, and what the window says this morning is a hundred and thirty-one mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-one the other, which is two hundred and fifty-two.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and eighty-three days on this morning's sheet, with the clause under that taking the two hundred and eighty-second of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-fourth. One under and one over, and a week of standing in a building nine miles off did not move any of those three figures, and the four launders have never been added to them at any site in four years.

**Six hundred and sixty-nine days** on the near board and **seven hundred and sixteen** on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near board was shut at every hour of this morning and the man who reads the far side put his thumb on it at the seventh hour without looking down, which he does.

**One hundred and thirteen mornings of two hundred and nine**, of a month seven months back, is what the second book took at the ninth hour, and the two men who read the boards said it to each other at the seventh hour without either of them reading the other's half back.

Four of them left the gate at the fourth hour and were on the middle road by half past, on foot, and the barrow stayed in the yard. Its journeys are eleven and eleven is a floor, and a distance is not a distance because it is the same number as another one: nine miles is nine miles, the four to a gate and a trough is four, and a barrow's journeys and a man's feet are two figures this building has never once added together.

Marek was in the party. Soren Rill was in the party and carried nothing, as usual. The man of about fifty was in the party with his own book shut under his arm, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the party with a pencil she did not take out.

The great door of the machine house was standing open when they got there at about the seventh hour. Nobody had arranged it for them and nobody in either building said a word about the fact that it was open on a Saturday.

The building is doing a work. The gauges are read twice an hour by a man with a cloth and a card and the card does not get written on. The schedule is copied off the wall by a man who does not hurry, and the water in the channel down the middle of the floor is warm. The three wheels stand in a line about three hundred yards apart along the north side, and the third one has the bar laid across it and has had it laid across it every morning anybody in this holding has heard about.

And three wheels, one team, and about nine hours apiece is twenty-seven hours of a person. That is the whole of the arithmetic and this page is not going to improve it.

Soren Rill did the arithmetic himself on the back of a schedule sheet at the tank end, sitting down on the floor to do it, and he did it twice and got the same answer both times and showed it to nobody.

**"Twenty-seven hours is not a week and it is not a person."** He said it to the tank and not to the other three. **"It is three nine-hour turns and the three of them have to be the same three bodies, because the rule says two at a wheel and you cannot send anybody out of the room halfway through and expect a frame that has been walking for nine hours to forget which hand was on the casting."** He said that to the tank and did not look up while he said it. **"I have carried seed up a road for nine years and I know what a three-day job looks like. This is a three-day job with a fourth day in it for the waiting and the reading, and nobody in this yard is going to be able to do it on a Tuesday."** Nobody asked him how he knew it was a three-day job and he did not say and he was not asked.

He put his hand on the stone of the coping at the second wheel and said the one thing nobody in this holding had thought of, and he gave the reason for it first and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"There is a figure nobody has written down."** He said it with his hand on the stone of the coping and not to the three of them. **"Twenty-nine taken, in the rota, in this holding, four years, and it has not moved on any morning, and nobody in this building has ever asked whether the twenty-nine is people or fields or mornings, and I have been the man who writes the twenty-nine down for four years and I have not been able to tell you what it is a count of."** He took his hand off the stone and wiped it on his coat.

**"I am not asking today. I am saying that when somebody asks me what happens on the day we do this, I have got three answers and two of them are made up, and I would rather have said that out loud in a room nine miles off than in a yard with a barrow in it."** Nobody in that room asked him which two were made up and he did not say.

Iona Vey was at the second wheel when they came through and she was not in a doorway and she was not waiting. She did not ask them what they had come for and she did not offer them anything.

**"Three wheels is the number and I have not got an opinion about which one."** She said it plainly and then she waited about two seconds and went on without being asked a second time. **"You have been in here four days. What you do with three is a thing a body of households decides and not a thing I decide and not a thing a man standing in a road nine miles off decides with a bar in his hand."** She said that to all four of them and looked at the second wheel while she said it.

Nobody in that room picked the three up and said anything about them.

**"I have said the method exists. I have not said which one you should turn, and if you had asked me this morning I would have given you the same answer, and you did not ask, and I have noticed that you did not ask."** Nobody in that room said she was wrong and nobody thanked her for anything.

He did not stop walking the line of gauges for any of it and did not look up once, and afterward he put his card away with nothing new written on it.

The two men on the wheel took it through in fours and stopped and took it through in fours and stopped, and one of them counted out loud and the other one did not, and neither of them looked at the four people who came in through the great door at the seventh hour and neither of them has ever been asked a question by anybody in this holding in four years.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went down the rail to the third wheel and stood in front of the bar laid across it for about ten minutes without touching it and without asking anybody anything, and then she went and stood in front of the first one for about the same length of time, and she came back to the tank end and wrote nothing in her own book.

**"I have got two things and I am not going to turn them into a third."** She said it to the tank and gave the reason before she said it. **"One of those three has a bar across it and that is a fact about this morning. One of those three has a man standing on it who counts out loud in fours and that is a fact about a man. Neither of those is a fact about what happens if somebody puts a hand on a spoke."** She shut her book and held it against her hip. **"I have been in four counties' meetings for four years and I have watched a room take a fact about a morning and a fact about a man and build a plan out of the two of them, and it took eleven weeks and it did not work once."** Nobody in that room asked her for the plan and she did not offer one. **"I am not building it this week and I do not have a third thing and I am not going to go back down that rail and manufacture one."**

Not one person in that room asked her what she was going to do instead, and she did not offer it.

The man who reads the schedule off the wall was asked nothing by any of the four of them. He came past at about the ninth hour with a sheet in his hand and the heading still written on the other side of it and nobody stopped him, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden read the heading over his shoulder as he went and did not ask him a thing.

The woman at the desk in the outer office took a sheet off a spike and put it on the desk and took the next one off the spike, and she did not look up at four people standing in a building that has nine hundred yards of rail in it, and the clerk of the machine house sat on a stool with a book open on his knees and did not get up while any of that was being said.

Soren Rill went back to the second wheel on his own at about the tenth hour and stood about four feet off it for half an hour and watched the two men take it through and stop and take it through and stop, and he did not ask either of them anything, and afterward he said one thing about it to nobody.

**"They are not being careful because of us."** He said it to the coping. **"They have been doing that since before any of the four of us were born in this part of the county, and if you stand in a room like this for a week you start to believe a thing is being done for you, and it is not, and the day we put a hand on a spoke is the first day anybody in this building does something different in front of them."** Nobody in that room asked him what difference it makes and he did not say.

Harlan Vetch was not in that building and nobody sent for him and nobody told him they had gone, and he was at the north wall in this yard from the eighth hour to about the eleventh with his back to the gate and his hands still, and the old man with a stick worked the other end of it and neither of them spoke and neither of them went through the gate.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said one more thing at the gate on the way in, standing with her own book shut under her arm, and she gave the reason for it before she said it. She said it to the gatepost and not to the three men she had walked nine miles with. **"I have been asked for a figure of draw twice in four years by two bodies of households and both times what they wanted was something they could put in front of their own members."** She looked at the gatepost. **"Nobody in this yard has asked me for a figure this week and the reason I have noticed that is that there is a piece of paper with a wheel on it and no figure on it anywhere on it, and I have gone round four counties being asked for numbers and I have come back to a table with three wheels and a word *hand* written three times and not one of them is a number I can give anybody."** She put her own book under her arm. **"I do not know yet whether that is a relief or a delay and I have decided not to decide it this week."**

Not one person in that yard said it was either, or asked her to decide it, and she went out of the gate and was gone before the middle of the afternoon.

The man of about fifty came back in at about the fourth hour in the afternoon with the road on his boots and said one thing at the gate that was not about the machine, and he gave the reason for it first and printed no figure for it so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"I have stood in a building nine miles off four days this month and been shown three true things and not one of them was for me. She is right about the machine and I have not said she is and I am not going to."** He said that to the road and then stood with his back to the gate for a moment. **"I want it on the page that I noticed both of those at the same time and did not put them next to each other."** Nobody at that gate picked the two up and nobody thanked him and nobody said he was being careful.

There is a rule about that in this building and it is not new this month. A sheet of paper on a board is a sheet of paper on a board and nobody has to take anybody's word for what is on it.

Out of the great door at about the eleventh hour and into this yard at the second hour in the afternoon, and the barrow had not gone in the morning and did not go in the afternoon, and its journeys are eleven and eleven is a floor.

Nobody has filled the register form on the middle table in a hundred and seventy-nine days and nobody is going to read it out a fifth time. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and seventy-three days old and has nothing on it.

Paid at twenty-five and it has not moved, and the word goes in in front of the figure. The sessions entered in this holding's book stand at seven and nobody entered one this morning, because a thing that is not entered is not in the book whatever anybody says about it afterwards, and the four ruled lines under the two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due.

Fifty-three requests and fifty-three section-nine notes, and a day at a building nine miles off went into neither of them and neither of the two moved. Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column in the seed house's own schedule with no unit against any one of them, the seventh row is not ruled, and a diagram is not a seventh and an afternoon in a building nine miles off is not a seventh and a number of hours that three men cannot be in two places at once is not a seventh.

A morning when four people went nine miles and came home with an arithmetic got no sixteenth line in the use log, which has fifteen. The barrow's journeys are eleven, the four-mile road is four, the nine is nine, and the nine hundred yards behind the wall in that building is a fourth figure, and this holding has never once added any two of them.

Nobody in this building thanked anybody for going or for coming back. The clerk of this holding wrote the fifteenth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and three wheels with a bar across one of them and about nine hours apiece is twenty-seven hours of a person, and that is not a plan and this morning did not turn it into one.

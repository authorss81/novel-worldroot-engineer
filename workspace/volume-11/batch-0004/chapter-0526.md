# Chapter 526

## Four Hundred And Ninety-Two, And A Rule Left Empty

The first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and it was the water: the north end of the ditch, the trough at the head of the four-mile road, and the standpipe in the yard. The ditch was an inch and a half below its own lip at the second hour and had been an inch below it on Monday, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put his thumb in the seam under the near stone and showed there was air in it and said nothing.

The forty-ninth ninth-day return came off the steelyard at about the third hour.

A weight went onto the board in the corner this morning because a weight goes onto that board every ninth morning without exception, and nine days have gone by since the last one, and nobody in this building chose today and nobody in this building is able to move it.

**Four hundred and ninety-two hundredweight.**

Every ninth morning for eleven years that board has gone up by eleven hundredweight, and what went against it this morning is the thirty-ninth pair of names, one above the pair that went up nine days ago, and the clerk of this holding entered that against the first rule and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

Two people weighed the morning against two other mornings, in different rooms and at different hours, and got two different figures out of it. The clerk took his at about the third hour against the sheet that came back inside the same fold; the man of about fifty took his at about the fifth hour out of the third book. Four years of that, and nobody in this building has ever set the two halves of a return side by side, and this morning the man of about fifty made his square twice and added nothing to it either time.

**"Mine goes on the way down. I do not trust that road to come back the same."** That was the man of about thirty-one of Silling, to the clerk of this holding and nobody else, and the clerk put it in the margin of the second book with no name against it and no count against it either.

Thirty-three times in eleven years is what an empty second rule amounts to, and this morning is the thirty-third. It is the only number on this morning that moved anywhere, and it moved on this page and on no page anywhere else in this county. Nobody has ever put a name against that rule in this building and nobody is going to, and a count of blanks is not a count of a man.

The nineteenth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and forty-one hundredweight.**

Wednesday gave five back off Tuesday and the fall went onto the falls, and the launder takes the two hundred and eighty-ninth figure of a run in which no number has ever been written down twice. The window behind it came out at a hundred and thirty-three mornings one way and a hundred and twenty-three the other, and the two of them are two hundred and fifty-six, and the man of about fifty read both halves at the seventh hour and set neither against the figure standing over the other.

The four bodies of households are at two hundred and eighty-seven days on this morning's sheet and the clause beneath takes the two hundred and eighty-sixth of those mornings out of two hundred and eighty-eighth. One under and one over, and the man who rules that column has never once entered them in the launder book and a return off a steelyard is not a household and is not a load.

Near board, six hundred and seventy-three days. Far board, seven hundred and twenty. The gap is forty-seven and the drawer behind the near one was not opened at any hour of this morning by anybody at all.

Both men who read the boards were at the steelyard with the return at the seventh hour and neither of them read the other's half, and what the second book took at the ninth hour was **one hundred and fifteen mornings of two hundred and thirteen**, of a month seven months back.

This holding has no place to put a rule that came back empty with nothing against it. A return off a steelyard goes in neither the requests nor the section-nine notes, and the two of those are fifty-three each. Nor is it a use: the use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth line for it.

The order sealing the route is in the drawer under the two books and it is valid, and being valid it opens nothing whatever. It has been in that drawer for eleven years and it is not being withdrawn and it is not being reworded, and nobody in this holding is going to write an apology for it this month or any other month. A culvert under a machine house nine miles off is not that route, and a route is not a culvert, and a corridor in a pan is not either of them, and the three of them have never been added together in any room in this county.

There was a letter on the middle table at about the sixth hour from a body of households with a heading at the head of its own paper, and what it wanted was a figure of draw certified by this holding against their subscription, and it wanted it in writing.

The clerk of this holding gave the reason before he said the thing and printed no figure for it, and the count of that in this holding's history did not move this morning because a blank morning consumes no number and he wrote none.

**"I have got the fourth of these in a fortnight and I am going to answer it the way I answered the first three."** He said it with the letter open on the middle table. **"This holding keeps four books and there is no column in them with a heading on it, and certifying a figure of draw would mean ruling one, and I am not going to rule one because four letters came in a fortnight."** The letter went in the requests column in the ordinary place and the column is fifty-three.

**"It is not a refusal and I am not going to let anybody in that county call it one."** He said that to the shut book and gave the reason for it before he said it and printed no figure for it again. **"A refusal is a thing you can appeal and a request that has been entered is a thing you can ask about, and I have written it in the place where a thing that has been asked for goes."** He shut the second book. **"There is a difference and anybody who wants to complain about me can come and read the column and see the number in it."** Nobody in that room asked him what the number was and he did not say and he was not asked.

Harlan Vetch was in the yard at about the seventh hour and he came and looked at the board in the corner with the return on it and stood in front of it for about two minutes, and he did not ask what the two rules were and he did not ask why the second one was empty.

**"There is a rule on that board and nobody has ever put a name against it."** He said it to the board and gave the reason for it before he said it. **"I have held an end of a thing for four years in a building where a man writes down what is held and where it is held and how much of it there is, and there is no column on that wall headed for the person, and I have been the person in that column for four years without knowing there was a column."** He turned round from the board. **"I am not bitter about it and I am not grateful for it either."** Nobody in that corner of the room said he was right.

He looked at the man of about fifty and waited until the man of about fifty looked up.

**"I am saying it because you are the one who copies that number every ninth morning and you have never once written down who holds what, and if you had, one of these mornings it would have been a different kind of empty."** Nobody said he was wrong and the man of about fifty wrote nothing down.

The man of about fifty said one thing back at the long table at about the eighth hour, and he gave the reason for it first and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"I have known that since the first morning I copied it."** He said it to the third book and not to the yard. **"I have written down what is held for eleven years and I have never once written down who is holding it, and the reason is not a reason anybody would accept."** He shut the book on his thumb. **"The first time I wrote a name against one of those numbers a man asked me what happened to the number when the man died, and I did not have an answer, and I have not asked the question since."** Nobody at that table said anything and nobody thanked him.

**"That is a reason I have never said out loud in a room and I have said it this morning and I am not going to improve on it."** He put the book down in front of him and did not open it again. **"It is not noble and it is not a rule. It is one question I did not want to have to answer and I have kept it unanswered for eleven years and it has cost this holding a name."**

Marek was at the north end of the ditch from about the sixth hour with a bar and did not come in until the ninth, and the count of questions this holding has put to him this month is nil and is nil. He has not been asked how he slept and nobody is going to ask him, and about nine minutes is in his head and it did not come out in three days and there is no figure on any page in this building for it.

**"I can tell you what the fourth of your letters wants and I can tell you that I would be able to give it to them."** He said it to the ditch at about the sixth hour and he gave the reason for it before he said it, and he printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his. **"That is the thing I have got from Monday and it is the reason I have not been in this yard since. A week ago I could not have given a body of households a figure of draw if I wanted to and now I think I could, and a figure of draw is a number about how much of something is in a tank, and there is a woman in this building nine miles off who reads a needle every half hour and has never once been asked to write down what she reads."** He got the bar out of the ditch and stood with it in his hands. **"I am telling you that I have got a new thing and that the new thing makes a figure possible that was not possible last week, and I am not going to give it to anybody, and the reason I am not is that the man who decides does not get asked. Ask me for the number and I have a number. Do not ask me and I am still the one who cannot be asked anything about it."** Nobody at the north end of that ditch asked him for anything and nobody thanked him for not giving it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden heard the whole of that from about forty feet away at the gate and did not come over, and afterward she said one thing at the long table about it and she gave the reason before she said it.

**"I have been waiting four years for a man in this county to be able to give somebody a figure and refuse to."** She said it to the shut second book. **"I have never seen it. I have seen a man who could not, and a man who would, and a man who wanted to be asked, and this morning I have stood forty feet away from a man who has just discovered he can and is going to sit on it."** She put her thumb on the page and did not write anything on it. **"I am not going to say he is wrong and I am not going to say he is right, because both of those are about him and neither of them is about the letter that came this morning."** Nobody at that table said the two things cancel and nobody said they do not.

Tova Reed is not in this room and she is on the seed front in four counties, and nobody has taken any of it off her and nobody thanked her. Nia Vale is out with the moving reserves, and the districts that draw on the stored light are in her own book and the number of them was not said out loud anywhere today.

The man of the north row with the cough turned up at the low field gate at the middle of the day with his bar and said one word out loud to nobody, and it was the word for a thing that has finally gone right, and nobody in this holding asked him which thing.

It is paid at twenty-five, the word in front of the figure and the figure where it was yesterday and the day before. A hundred and eighty-three days is how old the register form on the middle table is, and it is neither filled nor refused and it is not going to be read out a fifth time. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and seventy-seven days old and has nothing on it.

Nobody wrote a session in this holding's book this morning and the number of them in it is still seven, and the four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are bare and the answer is not due. The six things this holding does not know are six, with no unit against any of them and the seventh row not ruled, and a weight off a steelyard is not a seventh and a rule left empty for the thirty-third time is not a seventh.

Two people hold the far end of a thing. This body is not either of them, there is no instrument anywhere in four counties that says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has ever said it is on. Nine hundred yards of rail is a distance and a wheel behind it is a wheel, and neither of those is the far end of a thing, and this morning neither of them has been added to it.

The north wall was his from the third hour to the sixth. He came in and said one number out loud to nobody, and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard asked him how he knew it and nobody is going to.

Rain: none. By the ninth hour a bar dropped on the ground rang off it, and the middle road neither took nor gave. The north end of the ditch is running full off the first line, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

Nobody in this holding thanked anybody. The clerk wrote the nineteenth in at the ninth hour and read it back once, and the count of blanks on that board is thirty-three, and a rule that came back empty this morning opens nothing, and a letter went into the requests column and the column is fifty-three, and nobody has asked Marek how he slept and nobody is going to.

> THE BOARD IN THE CORNER OF THIS HOLDING, WRITTEN IN THE HAND OF THE CLERK OF THIS HOLDING, IN TWO RULES, ON ONE SHEET, WITH THE SHEET UNDATED: **AGAINST THE FIRST RULE, FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO HUNDREDWEIGHT, AND THE THIRTY-NINTH PAIR OF NAMES, ELEVEN ABOVE THE PAIR AGAINST IT NINE MORNINGS AGO. AGAINST THE SECOND RULE, NOTHING AT ALL, AND NO NAME, AND NO INITIAL, AND THE LINE LEFT AS IT CAME OFF THE STEELYARD.** THE RISE BETWEEN RETURNS IS ELEVEN AND NOT TWELVE. THE SHEET CAME BACK INSIDE THE SAME FOLD AND THE SECOND HALF OF IT WAS TAKEN AGAIN OUT OF THE THIRD BOOK AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR AND THE TWO HALVES WERE NOT ADDED TOGETHER EITHER TIME. NOBODY HAS PUT A NAME AGAINST THE SECOND RULE IN FOUR YEARS AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.

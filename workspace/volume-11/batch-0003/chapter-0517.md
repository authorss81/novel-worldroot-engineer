# Chapter 517

## Eleven Above The Last, And A Rule Left Blank

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and it has now gone past unworked for the eighty-third morning running. The standing is a hundred and nine mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty not, and twenty-nine and eighty come to a hundred and nine, and the count goes up by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on the other three.

Twenty-nine taken has not moved on any morning this year and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited, and the field at the headland is still there and is still nobody's.

The two reckonings of the rotation are both on this page. Kept in this room, the tenth of this month is the fifty-seventh morning of it. Kept the other way, the same morning is the forty-fourth. Thirteen has stood in that gap for four years and nobody here has ever worked out what the thirteen counts and it does not arrive by taking twenty-nine off either figure.

The forty-eighth ninth-day return came off the steelyard at about the third hour.

The board in the corner was used because that board is used every ninth morning without exception, and nine days have gone by since the last weight went on it, and nobody in this building chose today and nobody in this building is able to move it.

**Four hundred and eighty-one hundredweight.**

The rise on that board has been eleven hundredweight every time for eleven years and this morning is the thirty-eighth pair of names against it, one above the pair that went up nine days ago, and the clerk of this holding entered it against the first rule and read it back once and did not read it back twice.

Two people weighed it against two other things before the light was full. The clerk went at it at about the third hour against the sheet that came back inside the same fold, and the man of about fifty went at it again out of the third book at about the fifth hour, and the two figures they got are not the same figure. In four years nobody in this building has put the two halves of a return together and added them, and the man of about fifty reconciled it twice this morning and did not add them.

**"I write mine on the way down because I do not trust the road to be the same going back."** The man of about thirty-one of Silling said that to the clerk of this holding and to nobody else, and the clerk wrote it in the margin of the second book and did not put a name against it and did not put a count against it either.

The second rule came back off the board empty for the thirty-second time in eleven years. That is the one count on this morning that moved and it moved on this page and it has not moved on any other page in this county, and a count of blanks is not the count of a man and no name has ever gone against that rule in this building and none is going to.

The tenth came in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and thirty-four hundredweight.**

It came up eight on Monday with nothing against it, so the rise went onto the rises, and it is the two hundred and eightieth figure of a run in which no number has ever been written twice. The window behind it came out at a hundred and twenty-nine mornings one way and a hundred and eighteen on the other, which is two hundred and forty-seven, and neither end of that window has ever been set against the figure printed over the other.

This morning's sheet carries the four bodies of households at two hundred and seventy-eight days, and the clause under it takes the two hundred and seventy-seventh of those mornings out of two hundred and seventy-ninth, and neither of those is a load and neither of those is a household.

The boards on the long wall stand at **six hundred and sixty-four days** and **seven hundred and eleven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one was not opened at any hour of this morning.

There is no figure for the run of mornings on this page and the reason is on the page. The half of that run is half a number on this morning, and the two men who read the boards were at the steelyard with the return and neither of them read the other's half, and the second book carries nothing this morning where the run stands. The frame of that run is of a month seven months back and it is not going to be said again in this building.

There is no place in this holding for a rule that came back empty and nothing went against it. A return off a steelyard is not in the requests and not in the section-nine notes and the two of those are fifty-three each and they have never been added to one another. A return is not a use and the use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth line for it.

Something came up twice out of the north this morning where there is no ground to carry anything, at about the fourth hour and again about six hours and eleven seconds later, and it came up for about eleven seconds each time, and it was the same both times.

The man of about fifty was at the long wall with his back to the room when the first of them came and he did not turn round. The second of them came while he was in the middle of a sentence with the clerk and he stopped for about a second and then went on with the sentence, and afterwards he said nothing about either one of them to anybody, and he did not name them, and he has never named them in four years.

Nobody in this yard asked him what it was and nobody was going to, and it was not read a third time this morning and it is not going to be read at all this month. A sound is not an instrument and a sound is not a figure and a man who does not read a thing is not a man who is hiding it.

The man of the north row with the cough was on the low field gate in the rain with a bar and came in at about the seventh hour with his coat streaming, and the four ruled lines under his condition are as empty as they have been since a clerk ruled them and the answer to them is not due, and nobody in this holding has asked him to hold an end of anything and nobody is going to.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the seed house door through the whole of the rain with the schedule under her arm and did not come out, and the six ruled rows on it are six ruled rows with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and the oilskin satchel was on the long table under the four pound weight where it has been all week.

Nobody looked at the mark on his arm and nobody has looked at it properly since the fourteenth. The four accounts this holding has of that arm are four and they reconcile none of one another and a morning with a return on it did not make a fifth. The far end of a thing is held by two people and this body is not either of them, and no instrument in four counties says whether it is on or off, and nobody in this holding has said it is on.

The six not knowns are six with no unit supplied against any of them and no seventh row ruled, and a weight off a steelyard is not a seventh and a rule left blank for the thirty-second time is not a seventh and neither is a sound out of the north.

The party came into the yard about the fourth hour in the afternoon, five of them off the road at nine miles, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was at the gate when they came through it because he was closing it for the weather.

**"You have been in there since Thursday."** He said it to Marek and it was not a question and he did not wait for one. **"The gate is shut at the ninth hour now and it was shut at the ninth hour on Thursday and it was shut at the ninth hour on Saturday, and I have not asked anybody where they were going and I am not going to now, and the barrow is in and the trough is full."** He put the bar across the gate afterward and went to the pump house.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came through the gate with her own book shut under her arm and did not open it, and she said one thing at the tap and it was that she has been asked for two figures in one week by two different people in two different rooms and has got one of them, and that the one she got was a level and not a figure of anything and she has written it down as a level. Nobody asked her what the other one was for and she did not say.

Soren Rill put his bag down against the wall by the tap and said he was going to bed and he was going to do it whether anybody wanted to hear about it or not, and he said the reason first, which was that he had been on his feet since the fourth hour and had been in a building where he understood about a third of what was being said. He is not the officer of anything and he said so on his way in the door without being asked, which is the fourth time this week.

The two clerks of bodies of households were not in this yard and nobody sent for them and nobody has sent for anybody from that room since the twenty-fifth of a month now two months back. Three of the four bodies of households that signed a charter in a room nine miles off seventy-eight days ago are still signed to a machine for stored light, and one of the four passed a motion about it at its own meeting and sent it up the fen road, and neither of those two facts has an opinion about the other one this morning.

The man with the slate was not in this building today. He went out on Tuesday with the rest and he did not come back with them, and the slate is leaning against the shelf in the one room where he left it, and nobody in this yard asked where he had gone and nobody is going to.

The old man with a stick came off the north wall at about the sixth hour and stood under the eaves through the rain and went back in afterward, and he said one word out loud to nobody at about the eighth hour and it was not a number and nobody in this yard has ever written it down.

The man of about fifty said one thing at the long table that nobody asked him for and that the reader of this body had not thought of, and he said it to the shut third book and gave the reason first and printed no figure for it, so that this morning consumed no number of his.

**"Two people at a wheel."** He said it to the book and then he said the rest of it. **"Four days ago I would have called that a rule about being frightened of a machine. It is not that. It is a rule written by somebody who watched the frame walk and had to write something down the same day, and I have been in this room four years waiting for a person to say a thing like that and the person who said it was the last one in this county I expected to hear it from."** Nobody in that room said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody thanked him and he did not say he had been thanked.

Tova Reed was at the middle table when they came in and she did not get up, and she said one thing about the fourth council and about what a released pattern does to a sowing date and did not say it twice, and Marek said one thing back about the fill behind the two culverts having gone out again in the night. The two of them were in the same room for about nine minutes and most of that was about paper.

What is asked of her this month is the seed front in four counties and a sowing date and a schedule that not one of those four councils will put a name to, and nobody in this building has offered to take any of it off her and nobody has thanked her for carrying it and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The compost line is paid at twenty-five and did not move. The register form is a hundred and seventy-four days old on the middle table and is not filled and is not going to be read out a fifth time. The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and sixty-eight days old and has nothing on it.

The sessions entered in this holding's book stand at seven and nobody entered one today, and a session that is not entered did not happen, and the four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are as bare as they were in a month now four months back.

The two sheets about the two declinings were on the long table about nine inches apart where they have been for a month and a day with a return on it, and neither of them moved this morning and the return did not settle either of them.

The man who keeps the use log was at the tap at about the seventh hour with the log under his arm while the whole of this was being read out and wrote nothing, because a weight off a steelyard is not a use and five people coming back off a road is not a use, and the log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth for any of it.

The third column of the sheet of terms on the long wall is ruled and empty and has a heading at the head of it, and the five terms in the other two columns were declined as they were offered in a room in another year and nothing has been withdrawn and nothing has been added, and a rule about a wheel in a building nine miles off is not a term and nobody in this holding has written it as one.

The offer is face up on the low board with nothing on it and no date on it and no line anywhere on it for a name, and nobody in this building has ever been told to take it off and nobody is going to be told.

There was rain in the afternoon off the west and about half of it went into the middle road and gave a quarter of it back before the light, and the ditch at the north end took an inch off its own lip. The trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the second hour and stayed inside its own lip and did not go over it once.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the tenth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the count of blanks on that board is thirty-two and it is thirty-two on that page and nowhere else in this county.

> Entered by the clerk of this holding at the first hour, in his own hand, as one unbroken line: **The tenth. The fourth line of the rota came round and there was nobody at the headland to take it, being the eighty-third morning, the standing a hundred and nine in force with twenty-nine taken and eighty not, and the two reckonings at fifty-seven and forty-four. The forty-eighth ninth-day return came off the steelyard at about the third hour at four hundred and eighty-one hundredweight, the thirty-eighth pair, eleven above the last, reconciled twice and the two halves not added, and the second rule on the board was left blank for the thirty-second time in eleven years. The pair not read and no figure standing against it, the half of the run being half a number on this morning. The same day in and the same day out. One thousand and thirty-four hundredweight to the launder, being the two hundred and eightieth.** Nothing of this is a forecast and nothing of it is a cause and nothing of it is a total.

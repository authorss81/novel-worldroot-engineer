# Chapter 512

## Six People And A Duty Being Done

Four people went out on the third line of the rota at the second hour and stayed on it until the fifth. The work is the two culverts on the middle road, the fill behind them, and the low field gate at the end of it, and the night had taken about half an inch out of that fill where the frost got into it, so the two of them put it back and went over the same ground a second time. The gate wanted a bar and a stone and it took the nine minutes it always takes.

Marek went out of the gate at about the fifth hour with Soren Rill and the man with the slate and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden, and it is about nine miles by the turn and they walked it, and the barrow did not go and the number of journeys that barrow has made is eleven and eleven is a floor. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden carried her own book and a pencil and did not carry the seed schedule, because the seed schedule went nine miles last month in an oilskin satchel and came back in the same satchel and is the same sheet.

They came to the great door at about the seventh hour and it was standing open, and it was open on an ordinary Wednesday morning and nobody had come out to tell them it would be and nobody came out while they were standing in it.

The rail runs about nine hundred yards behind the wall and there are three wheels on it, and each of the three is about the size of a cart wheel and more than a cart wheel, and two men were on one of them and one man was on the second and the third was standing still with a bar laid across it.

The building is doing a work. That is the first thing about it and it is the plainest thing and it is true before anybody in it opens their mouth: there is a duty in here and the duty is being done and it is being done well. The floor is concrete with a channel down the middle of it and the water in the channel is warm. There are tanks along the north side standing shoulder to shoulder and there is a line of gauges above them on a frame, and there is a smell of hot oil and wet stone, and there is a lamp on a bracket by each wheel.

The six people in that building keep it. The man who keeps the gauges walks the line of them with a cloth and reads them off a card and writes nothing down. The woman at the desk in the outer office takes a sheet off a spike and puts it on the desk and takes the next one off the spike. The man who reads the schedule off the wall copies it onto a sheet that goes out on a road. The clerk of the machine house sits on a stool with a book open on his knees and does not get up while anybody is talking.

The two men on the wheel have their arms in it and they take the turns at it and one of them counted out loud and the other one did not, and they have the wheel moving and holding and stopping and starting again. Neither of them looked at the four people who came in through the great door at the seventh hour.

**"That is a machine with a duty in it."** Soren Rill said it at the rail, about nine hundred yards in, with his hand flat on the stone of the coping. **"I have gone round four counties looking for the thing that decides and I have been looking for a man with a name on a door, and there is a wheel and there are two men on it and one of them has counted out loud, and I am going to have to sit down somewhere."** He did not sit down. He took his hand off the coping and wiped it on his coat and said the rest of it to the tank nearest him and not to anybody. **"I am still not the officer of anything and I have not stopped saying so, and I would like it noticed that I said it before I said anything else."**

The page has one thing to say about the woman in that building before anything else is said about her. Every sheet that comes out of that door is correct about what is in it. The man who reads the schedule off the wall copies it out word for word. In eleven years nobody in four counties has found one figure in one paper out of that building that does not match the thing it was copied from, and this page says so here, before anybody in that room opens their mouth.

Iona Vey was inside at the second wheel when they came through, and she was not standing in a doorway and she was not waiting for anybody.

**"You will want to know whether the water in that channel is being kept warm for a reason."** She said it to the four of them and not to the two men on the wheel. **"It is. It is kept warm so that a line does not stop in a hard week, and a line that stops in a hard week is a line that has to be cut, and a line that gets cut is a line somebody is standing on."** Nobody in that building said anything about that and nobody asked her what she meant by somebody.

**"The schedule on that wall is what we do and when we do it. It is the eleventh of the ones that has come up a road and every one of them has been kept, and if you take it away and read it in a room with a fire in it you will still have the same document and you will have lost the part where a man with a cloth walks a line of gauges."** She took her hand off the frame. **"I am not going to give you the document. You have it. I am telling you what is on the wall that is not on the sheet."**

The sixth came in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and twenty hundredweight.**

Wednesday gave five back and the fall went onto the falls, and what is on the launder this morning is the two hundred and seventy-fifth figure of a run in which no number has ever been written down twice. The window this office keeps behind it reads a hundred and twenty-six mornings one way and a hundred and sixteen the other, and those two make two hundred and forty-two. The clerk copied both halves out separately and neither of them went near the number printed over the other, and he has never once been asked to.

The four bodies of households stand at two hundred and seventy-three days on this morning's sheet, and the clause under them takes the two hundred and seventy-second of those mornings out of two hundred and seventy-fourth, and a man nine miles off had a hand on a gauge frame while both of those numbers were being written down.

The two men who read the boards read the pair to each other at the seventh hour, and neither of them read the other's half, and that is the way the two of them have done it for four years. What went into the second book is **one hundred and eight mornings of one hundred and ninety-nine**, and the frame of it is of a month seven months back.

Six hundred and fifty-nine days on the near board and **seven hundred and six** on the far one, with forty-seven between them as ever, and the drawer behind the near board was not opened at any hour of that day by anybody who was nine miles off and has not been opened in four years.

The man of about fifty had the third book shut on the long table all morning with his thumb in it and did not open it. He gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is a hundred and fifty-six, and it is his and it is not in any of the four books.

**"I have had a man come back off that road with a list of things seen in his own hand and not one number on it."** He said it to the shut book at about the sixth hour and then he said the rest of it. **"He went nine miles to look at a wall and came back with a wall. That is a better answer than I expected and it is a worse answer than I wanted, and the reason I am giving is that I sent a man to that door with a slate and no columns on it, and I have been doing the asking for four years."** Nobody in that room asked him what he wanted and he did not say and he was not asked.

> THE SHEET ON THE WALL AT NINE HUNDRED YARDS, COPIED OUT BY HAND BY THE MAN WHO READS IT, IN THE HAND OF NOBODY WHO IS IN THIS HOLDING'S FOUR BOOKS, ON ONE SHEET, WITH WHAT IS HELD AND IN WHICH PLACE AND SINCE WHEN AND HOW MUCH OF IT THERE IS AT THE HEAD OF IT IN THREE COLUMNS AND NO COLUMN HEADED FOR ANY PERSON: **A DRAW FIGURE AT THE FOOT OF THE FIRST COLUMN, WHICH IS A FIGURE OF DRAW AND NOT A FIGURE OF ANYBODY. A COLUMN HEADED FOR THE THINGS THIS HOLDING DOES NOT KNOW, WITH SIX ROWS RULED IN IT AND NO UNIT SUPPLIED AGAINST ANY OF THE SIX, AND THE SEVENTH ROW NOT RULED.** ON THE BACK OF THAT SHEET IS A FORM WITH FOUR LINES IN IT AND **LINE ONE CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT AND LINES TWO AND THREE AND FOUR ARE EMPTY AND NO HEADING IS WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF ANY OF THE FOUR.** THERE IS NO SIGNATURE AT THE FOOT OF IT AND NOBODY IS NAMED AT THE HEAD OF ANY COLUMN.

A form is not a column. This holding is not going to rule a column for it and a hand that has an empty line on it is not an invitation, and the one figure on that line has been there since before anybody in this room started keeping books and it is not going to be improved by a woman of about thirty-eight of Marden standing in front of it with a pencil.

She wrote nothing on it. She looked at it for a long time and then she said one thing about it and the thing she said was that a figure for a draw has a place on a sheet that a machine keeps and no place at all in a book of households, and the two are not added to each other in any room in this county. Then she put the pencil in her pocket and said the thing about the six rows.

**"You have ruled six rows for what you do not know and you have not ruled a seventh."** She said it to the wall and not to Iona Vey. **"Ours has been asked for twice in four years and has not been ruled either time, and I am not going to be the one to rule it here either, and I am not going to walk in here on some later morning and rule it because I have seen yours."**

The corridor in the pan at this holding is named in a document in a drawer and is not established as on, and there is a channel in the floor of this building nine miles off that carries water and it is a channel in a floor, and a channel in a floor is not that corridor and the two of them are not added to one another by anybody who has been in both places.

There are tanks along the north side and a lamp on a bracket by each wheel and a man with a cloth walking a line of gauges with a card in his hand, and two men taking turns at a wheel with their arms in it, and one of them counted out loud and the other one did not, and a woman at a desk in a smaller door taking sheets off a spike, and a man copying a wall onto a sheet that goes out on a road, and a clerk on a stool with a book open on his knees.

Iona Vey was right about every one of those. She was standing nine feet from a man who had come nine miles on foot to be in that building and she had said three true things in twenty minutes and none of the three had asked him for anything.

**"You can come through the door."** She said it last, to the four of them and not to the two men on the wheel. **"It is open on the mornings it is open and it is shut on the mornings it is shut, and I am not going to arrange it either way for anybody, and if you want to be in here when it is open you will be here when it is open."**

Marek did not answer that. He stood by the rail for about half an hour after the others had gone and watched the two men take the wheel through four turns and stop and take it through four more, and nobody in that building said a word to him about what he ought to have said back.

Two can hold a pattern for four and one can hold it for five, and there are two rootmarks, and neither of those counts has moved in this holding in four years. A count of who can hold a pattern is not a count of ends, and the two men on a wheel in this building are on neither of those lists and have never been asked to be on either of them.

Soren Rill said his piece at the coping and then went and stood next to the man who keeps the gauges for about ten minutes and watched him walk the line with the cloth, and he did not ask the man a single question in that ten minutes, and the man did not look up once, and neither of them said that had been a conversation.

The man with the slate stood in the middle of the floor with the slate against his chest and wrote nine words on it and put it behind his back and did not show it to anybody, and it was nine words and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden did not ask to see it and he did not offer and nobody in that building saw it.

Sera Quill is not in this building and was not at the second gate on the way out. A records witness is worth exactly what the records will carry in a room, and this room keeps records that no room in this county can use.

The seed front is four counties away and it stays there. Tova Reed has three councils' dates in writing in front of her and a fourth council that wrote on the twenty-eighth and said nothing at all, and nobody has taken any of it off her and nobody thanked her and nobody put a hand on her arm.

The frost went out of the yard by noon and the yard was soft by the second hour of the afternoon, and the middle road took nothing in the day and gave nothing back, and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was on the low field gate with a stone under his heel and would not let anybody take the other end of the bar. The second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and sixty-three days old and it has nothing on it and he has not been asked about it in a hundred and sixty-three days.

There was no panel of anything in this building and there is no panel in this holding and nobody in either place has ever printed one. A gauge with a needle on it is a gauge, and a man with a card and a cloth is a man doing a morning's work.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the fifth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the register form is a hundred and sixty-nine days old on the middle table and is not filled and is not going to be read out a fifth time, and a man went nine miles and came back through a door that was open and a duty in a building is being done and done well and nobody in it is a monster.

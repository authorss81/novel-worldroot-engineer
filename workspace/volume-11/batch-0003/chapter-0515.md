# Chapter 515

## The Branch That Was Burning Her

The second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked, and it was the outlet end and the two stones at the head of it, and for the first time since the frost went in there was no ice in the seam under the near stone and the pack came out and went back dry instead of wet, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling put his hand flat on the stone and did not say the word.

The eighth came in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and thirty-one hundredweight.**

Saturday stood eight higher than Friday and nothing was set against it, and the launder takes the two hundred and seventy-eighth figure of a run in which no number has ever been written twice. The window behind it reads a hundred and twenty-eight mornings one way and a hundred and seventeen the other, and the two of them are two hundred and forty-five. Nobody in this room has ever set one end of that window against the figure printed over the other.

The morning's sheet puts the four bodies of households at two hundred and seventy-six days, and the clause under that takes the two hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings out of two hundred and seventy-seventh. None of those three is a household and none of them is a person.

Six hundred and sixty-two days on the near board, **seven hundred and nine** on the far one, forty-seven apart, and the drawer behind the near one stayed shut all morning.

No figure for the run of mornings stands on this page. The half of that run is half a number on this morning, and the two men who read the boards were at the outlet end with a bar and a stone and neither of them was at the long wall at the seventh hour, and the second book carries nothing where the run goes.

A letter came in on the fen road with the post at about the eighth hour, and it is from a body of households with a heading at the head of its own paper. What it wants is a figure of draw certified by this holding against their subscription. It wants it in writing, and what it does not want is an opinion, and the difference between the two is on the page of the letter itself where anybody can read it.

**"I have got the second of these inside a week and I am going to answer it the way I answered the first."** The clerk of this holding said it with the letter open on the middle table and he gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history did not move this morning because he printed no figure for it. **"This holding keeps four books and there is no column in them with a heading on it, and certifying a figure of draw would mean ruling one, and I am not going to rule one because two letters came in a week."**

It went in the requests column in the ordinary place and the column is fifty-three. A request is a request and an offer is a different thing, and nothing was written on the low board and nothing was taken off it and no rung of anything was climbed in this holding this morning and nobody has said one word to anybody about climbing one.

What has been running in that building nine miles off has taken root branches off for thirty-one years and has never once asked anybody who was living on them, and nobody in this holding has ever said a word about it and nobody is going to.

Six went out of the gate at the fourth hour and were inside the machine house by the seventh, and the great door was open, and the party went out on Saturday because the door was open and because the people in the room last time had said that anybody who wanted to be in there when it was open would be in there when it was open.

Iona Vey took her sleeve up on her left arm without being asked to and without any preamble at all, at about the ninth hour, at the second wheel, with the rail behind her and the two men turning it in fours.

The scar runs from the inside of her wrist to a little below the elbow and it is not a clean line. It is raised and pale and it goes round in one place where whatever opened it had gone round, and it is the scar of something that was opened along its length by somebody who was not trying to hurt her and was in a hurry.

**"That is what it does."** She said it with her arm bare in a building with a warm channel down the middle of the floor. **"Not to a person. To a line. And the line it did it to was mine."** She said that and then said the rest of it in her own numbers, and she did not soften a figure and she did not put one in anybody else's mouth.

**"It was the second of three hard winters and nothing in four counties had a name for what was going on then. I had eleven ewes on that ground and four of them had lambs at the foot of it. After that winter four of those lambs came up in the spring and they were not right, and they were not wrong enough to kill, and they lived for about six years in a field that would not carry them and then they did not."** She put her sleeve down. **"I am not telling you that story to be forgiven for anything and I would take it ill if anybody in this room thought I was. I am telling you that I know what it is to be the branch that gets cut, because I was one, and it is the only reason I have ever been able to do what I do without it coming apart, and it took me about eleven years to understand that those two things can be in the same person at the same time."**

She said that a season before any of the people in this holding were working, and not a year ago and not last winter, and she did not put a season on it in front of anybody as a way of making it a fact about them.

The branch that was burning her would not hold with the branches around it. It ran hot and it ran early and it came in a fortnight ahead of the four branches either side of it, and everything ever built to feed four counties was built on the assumption that a branch either comes in with its neighbors or does not come in at all. It came in on its own and put a harvest in nine days that was not the harvest anybody had stored for, and something had to give.

The machine cut it. That is what the machine did, and it did it the way it does everything else, without a conversation.

About four hundred thousand people ate a harvest that could be predicted that year, and the year after, and every year since. A woman stood in a room with a sleeve up and did not pretend those two things are the same size.

Marek did not say she was right and did not say she was wrong. He stood at the second wheel for a long time and did not say anything, and when he did speak it was not to her, it was to the man who keeps the gauges, and it was about the sixth gauge off the end that had been half low since the turn of the month.

A person who has been cut once is not thereby the reason that a hundred thousand others are cut. This page is not going to say it twice, and a wound on the woman who did the cutting does not make it a smaller sentence.

The room understood her and nothing in this holding has been forgiven by that. Her motive is on the page this morning and it stands next to what she has done, and the two of them are two things and neither of them cancels the other. She is alive and she is responsible.

**"You have shown me that."** Marek said it at about the eleventh hour and he said the reason before he said the thing and it was the reason and it was a reason and not an excuse. **"I am going to tell you why I am saying it, and it is that I have spent nine years believing that a person who does a thing like this is either inventing the reason or has never once been on the end of it, and you have just taken about a minute to show me that the second of those is not true, and I do not know what to do with that and I am not going to pretend I do."** Nobody in that room said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody thanked her for showing him anything.

She said nothing back to that. She went back to the frame of the second gauge and read a needle, and the man who keeps the gauges came past her about a minute later and did not stop and did not speak to her.

The man of about fifty spent the rest of the morning at the third wheel with the bar across it and did not go near her. He said one thing at about the eleventh hour to the wheel and not to anybody, and what he said was that he had come in that building four times in his life expecting to be shown something and had now been shown something four times, and that a man who cannot put a thing anywhere is not a man who should be writing columns about it. The clerk of this holding was not there to hear it and it went into no book.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden went out to the tank end and back twice and did not speak to Iona Vey at all, and afterward she said one thing to Soren Rill about the six ruled rows on that wall and what she had said about them on Wednesday, and what she said now was that she had been wrong to say she was not going to rule a seventh anywhere, because she had spent an hour in front of a wall where somebody had done without one for a very long time and had lost nothing anybody could name. Soren Rill told her that a wall and a book were two different objects and that she was allowed to think so in both, and she told him he was not the officer of anything and he said he had never once claimed to be.

**"I have four counties to sit in front of before the sowing and I have not got one figure in my own hand yet."** She said that to the rail. **"And I have just spent a morning in a building where a woman told me eleven ewes out loud because she wanted me to have it, and I am not going to say that is not a figure. It is a figure about eleven ewes on one branch and it is not a figure about a district and I have not got one of those and I am not going to have one by the end of this week."**

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the yard all day with the schedule and the six ruled rows and nobody came in and interrupted her, and the oilskin satchel was on the long table under the four pound weight with the schedule in it.

Soren Rill was at the second wheel for about an hour and said nothing at all. What he did was watch a man take a wheel through four turns and stop and take it through four turns and stop, eleven times over, and then go and stand at the third one with the bar across it. He has carried seed up a road for nine years and he is not the officer of anything and he said so three times this week without being asked once.

Two people in four counties can hold a pattern for four and one can hold it for five, and there are two rootmarks in this book, and not one of those three counts has moved in four years. How many people can hold a pattern is not a count of ends, and neither of the two men on that wheel is on any of the three lists and has never once been asked to be.

The far end of a thing is held by two people and this body is not either of them, and no instrument anywhere in four counties says whether it is on or off and nobody has said it is on. A wheel behind about nine hundred yards of rail is not that thing and the two are not in one sentence in this building.

The compost line is paid at twenty-five and did not move, and the word goes in in front of the figure. The register form is a hundred and seventy-two days old on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column and it is not filled and it is not refused.

The second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one of Silling's own book is a hundred and sixty-six days old and has nothing on it, and he came in at the ninth hour and put the bar back on the wall and did not offer it to anybody and was not asked.

The use log has fifteen lines and got no sixteenth for a certificate asked for by letter. The barrow did not go and its journeys are eleven and eleven is a floor, and the four miles are not the nine and the nine are not the coast road.

Seven is what this holding's book says it has, and nobody entered an eighth this morning, and a session that is not entered did not happen. In the corner of the one room the four ruled lines under two words are as bare as they were the day a clerk ruled them, and the answer they are for is not due.

The requests are fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three and neither of them moved and the two of them have never been added to one another. Six things this holding does not know are ruled with no unit against any of them and the seventh row is not ruled, and a scar on an arm nine miles off is not a seventh of anything.

The old man with a stick came off the north wall at the sixth hour and stood in the yard in the sun and said one number out loud to nobody and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard asked him how he knew it.

Nobody touched his arm. Marek came in off nine miles at the fourth hour in the afternoon with his sleeve down, and the four accounts of what is under it are four and this morning did not make a fifth.

Nia Vale was not in that building this morning and she has not been in it at all this week. The districts that draw on the stored light are in her own book and the number of them was not said out loud in any room, and she coordinates the moving reserves and she is not support staff and nobody in this holding has offered her a chair.

Sera Quill is not in this building either and nobody asked her to be and nobody has told her a word about what was said on an arm this morning. A records witness is worth exactly what the records will carry in a room, and this is not a record anybody has asked her to keep.

The frost is out of the ground and the yard was hard by noon and the bar in the near stone could be got a hand's depth into the ground with a hand on the end of it. There was no rain, the middle road took nothing and gave nothing back, and the north end of the ditch is running full and the trough at the head of the four-mile road was full by the seventh hour and stayed inside its own lip.

The offer is face up on the low board with nothing on it and no date on it and no line for a name and nobody in this building has been told to take it off, and a letter asking this holding for a certificate went into the requests column and is not a reason to touch that board.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the eighth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the column is fifty-three, and a woman took her sleeve up in a building nine miles off and showed a scar and did not ask for one thing in return, and this holding is not going to improve on what she said.

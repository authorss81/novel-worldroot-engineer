# Chapter 513

## The Sixth Hour, And A Figure About A Shutter

The frost is out of the ground. It came up about a quarter of an inch in the night and went off the hedges by the seventh hour, and the yard went soft enough that a bar went into it a whole hand's depth and came up with the bottom third of it black. It is the first mild morning since the sleet, and a few people in this yard remarked on it and not one of them remarked on it to anybody else.

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and there is nobody at the headland above Marden to take it, and it has now gone past unworked for the eighty-second morning running. The headland is a boundary and a ditch and a hedge bank and about two hundred yards of the four-mile road's far end, and it wants two men with barrows and it has wanted them for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever been told a reason for that and nobody has gone and asked for one.

The standing is a hundred and eight mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and seventy-nine not, and twenty-nine and seventy-nine come to a hundred and eight. The count goes up by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and it does not move at all on the other three of this week, and twenty-nine taken has not shifted on any morning this year.

Nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited. A field that waits is a fact about a field and not about anybody, and the figure of the fields that have been taken is twenty-nine and it is going to be twenty-nine this morning.

The two reckonings of the rotation are both on this page because a page that carried one of them would be carrying half a fact. Kept in this room, the sixth of this month is the fifty-sixth morning of it. Kept the other way, the same morning is the forty-third. Thirteen has sat in that gap for four years, and nobody in this building has ever found out what the thirteen counts, and it does not arrive by taking the twenty-nine off either figure.

Marek went out of the gate at about the fourth hour with Soren Rill and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden and the man of about fifty, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling went as far as the gate with them and turned back there and said one thing about a door and then went and worked the near stone instead. What he said was that a man who goes out of a gate at the fourth hour on a morning like this one is going to be standing about in a wet yard by the ninth, and nobody said anything back to him about it and he did not say anything else.

The shutter on the window of the office's work came off at about the sixth hour.

That is a fact and not a decision anybody took. The shutter has come off on the sixth of a month since before the clerk of this holding came into the room, and it stayed on from the seventh to the fifth, and it went on this morning at about the sixth hour in the way a thing goes on that four people have watched go on eleven times. Nobody in this building has ever asked who takes it off. The clerk of this holding was standing at the long table with the second book open, the shutter came off, he looked at it and did not ask, and the man of about fifty was at the door of the shed and looked at it and did not ask either.

And the party was already four miles off by then and going the other way.

The six went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**One thousand and twenty-eight hundredweight.**

Thursday stood eight higher than Wednesday and nothing was set against it. The window came off the long wall at the seventh hour at a hundred and twenty-seven mornings one way and a hundred and sixteen the other, and those two ends are two hundred and forty-three between them, and the man of about fifty read both of them and put neither against the figure standing over the other, which is what he does on every morning of the four years he has been doing it. Over that figure the launder carries the two hundred and seventy-sixth figure of a run in which no number has ever been entered twice, and the rise went onto the rises and nothing was set against it there either.

The clerk wrote the four bodies of households down at two hundred and seventy-four days and the clause under them at the two hundred and seventy-third of those mornings out of the two hundred and seventy-fifth, one under and one over at every site he has ever filled one in at. Four years he has had that sheet in front of him and he has never added those two numbers to the four launders, and the two sets of figures are on the same page this morning and neither of them is on the other's line.

On the long wall this morning the near board reads **six hundred and sixty days** and the far one **seven hundred and seven**, the gap between them being the same forty-seven it has always been, and nobody put a hand on the drawer behind the near one.

The run of mornings carries no figure on this page and the page gives the reason itself: half of it is half a number this morning and not a whole one. Both men who read the boards are at the headland end of the four-mile road standing beside a barrow neither of them took out, and what went into the second book about it is nothing at all.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden had been at the long table for about twenty minutes before the shutter came off and she said one thing about it that nobody had asked her for, and she said it to the window and not to the room.

**"Six days."** She said the number and then she waited, and nobody picked it up, and she said the rest of it herself. **"It is up six days and it goes back on the eleventh and I have not the least idea what happens in it, and I am the only person in this yard who has just said out loud that she does not know, and I have been saying that number out loud for four years so I am used to how it goes."** Nobody in that room told her she was the only one and nobody said she was wrong.

The shutter is up and the window behind it is a window in a wall of this holding, and no work is ever carried out of it. That has been true for eleven years and it is true this morning, and the shutter being up does not make it a place where anything happens, and nothing has ever gone out of that window and nothing is going to go out of it this week either.

The two leaves about the two declinings had been on the long table about nine inches apart for a month, and at the sixth hour this morning the shutter came off and the nine inches was still nine inches. Nobody in that room moved either of them and nobody was asked to, and the two of them are still that far apart.

**"A body's motion went up the coast road on Tuesday."** The man of about fifty said it to the second book at about the fifth hour, before he left, and he gave the reason for it before he said it and the count of that in this holding's history did not move this morning because a blank morning consumes no number and he printed no figure for it. **"It cites the nine acres that came off the twenty-ninth of a month now seven months back and it cites the decision that took them, and I am telling you it exists because it is on the road and two people will know about it by the weekend."** Nobody at that table asked him what the motion wanted and he did not say and he was not asked.

That decision is not reopened by anything in this week or this month. The cut at the kerb stays open where it has stayed open, nothing goes into the two-foot gap at the node end this season, the valve is not opened, and the key is not used by anybody in this holding and has not been used since the day it arrived.

The register form is a hundred and seventy days old on the middle table with nothing at the head of its column. The offer is face up on the low board, undated, unfilled, not withdrawn, no line anywhere on it for a name, and a motion in another county is not a reason to take it off and nobody has told anybody to.

The compost line is paid at twenty-five and did not move, and the word is in front of the figure where it goes in every time, and it is not a measure of this ground and it is not a measure of anything nine miles off.

The use log has fifteen lines. The barrow was taken out to the gate and stood there in the wet and came back without a load, and the number of journeys that barrow has made is eleven and eleven is a floor.

The sessions entered in this holding's book stand at seven and nobody entered one this morning, and the four ruled lines under two words in the corner of the one room are as empty as they were in a month now four months back, and the answer they are waiting for is not due.

Two lists, two numbers, the same number: the requests are fifty-three and the section-nine notes are fifty-three, and nobody in this building has ever added one of them to the other. Six things this holding does not know are ruled in a column with no unit against any of them, and the seventh row is not ruled, and a shutter is not a seventh.

> THE LINE THAT IS ON THE WALL BESIDE THE WINDOW IN THIS HOLDING, IN PAINT THAT HAS BEEN REPAINTED TWICE AND HAS NEVER BEEN REWRITTEN, WITH NO NAME AT THE HEAD OF IT AND NO INITIAL AT THE FOOT OF IT: **THE SIXTH TO THE ELEVENTH. WHAT IS DONE IN THAT TIME IS NOT ENTERED ANYWHERE.** THE PAINT IS OLD ENOUGH THAT NOBODY IN THIS BUILDING CAN REMEMBER WHO LAST DID IT AND NOBODY HAS ASKED.

The old man with a stick came off the north wall at about the seventh hour and stood in the yard with the sun on him and did not go in, and he said one number out loud to nobody and it was the right one, and nobody in this yard asked him how he knew it and nobody is going to ask him.

Sera Quill came in at the second gate about halfway through the morning with nothing in her hands and went and stood at the shutter with her hands in the front of her coat and looked at the window behind it for about ten minutes without saying anything at all. She read two lines off the line that is painted on the wall and then she stopped reading and stood there. She did not ask who takes it off and she did not ask what is done in the six days, and she has not been asked about either of those things by anybody in this holding and she did not offer.

**"I have been in rooms with a great many windows in them."** She said it to the shutter and not to the yard. **"Most of them are a thing somebody put in so that a room could be looked at from outside. This one is a thing that gets painted twice and never rewritten, and I would rather have the second of those in a wall than the first of those in a room, and I do not think that makes me a person who is on anybody's side about anything."** Nobody in that yard said anything back to her and she went down to the second bus at the eleventh hour.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came in off the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with his boots wet to the ankle and stood at the tap until the water came through, and the second of the four lines in his own book is a hundred and sixty-four days old and has nothing on it, and he was not asked about the shutter and did not offer.

They were inside that building by about the seventh hour and the great door was open and the rail was running and a change of men was going on at the second wheel, one pair of hands off and another pair on, and the wheel did not stop while it happened and nobody in that building called a change of men a thing that mattered.

Soren Rill spent most of the morning at the man who reads the schedule off the wall. He did not ask him anything for the first hour. He stood four feet back from the wall with his hands behind him and read the sheet on it from the left end to the right end twice, and the man went on copying it out word for word the whole time and did not look at him and did not hurry.

**"I asked him one question at the end of it and I am telling you the question because I have watched men ask questions for nine years and never once say what the answer was for."** He said that to the tank nearest him and not to the four of them. **"I asked him whether he had ever read it out to anybody. He said no, and then he said that if he read it out it would stop being a schedule and start being an opinion, and I have carried seed up a road for nine years on a man's opinion and I would rather have the sheet."**

The man of about fifty did not go near the wall. He went and stood at the far end of the rail where the third wheel stands and put his hand on the stone and left it there for about a quarter of an hour, and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was the only one of the four of them who wrote anything down, and what she wrote was in her own book and she did not show it to anybody.

Iona Vey was not in the room for the first two hours and came in at about the eleventh with the clerk of the machine house behind her carrying a sheet, and she did not say anything to anybody who came through that door and she did not have to, because a woman coming in with a clerk behind her carrying a sheet is a morning and not an event.

**"Your motion came up the coast road on Tuesday."** She said it to the man of about fifty across nine hundred yards of floor and he said that he had it on paper and she said that she did not doubt it for a moment and had never doubted it, and that the decision it cites was taken by a body with a heading at the head of its own paper and was not hers and could not be undone by anybody in this room or any other. She said all of that without heat and without a figure and then she said the other half of it, which was that she had read the same decision when it was taken and had said nothing against it then, and she was not going to say something against it now to make herself sound like a different person than she was in a room nine miles off. Nobody asked her which room and she did not say.

The man of about fifty came back into the yard at about the fourth hour in the afternoon from nine miles with the road on him and said two things at the gate and neither of them was about the window. About four people in this yard heard the whole of the first of them and about four heard the half, and nobody has said which four is which and nobody was asked, and a count of who heard a bookkeeper is not a count of the bookkeeper.

**"There is a machine in there that has a duty in it and it is being done properly, and I have been wrong about one thing and I am not going to be wrong about it twice."** He said that to the gatepost and then he went in and shut the shed door behind him and did not say the second thing until the evening and what he said then was that the woman who keeps the gauges had offered him a cup and he had taken it and he did not think anybody ought to build anything out of that.

Nobody thanked anybody. The clerk of this holding wrote the sixth in at the ninth hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the shutter on the window of the office's work is up and will stay up six days and nothing will be carried out of it, and the sixth hour is a figure about a shutter and it is not a figure about a machine.

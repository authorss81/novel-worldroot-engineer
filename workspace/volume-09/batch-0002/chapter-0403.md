# Chapter 403

## A Day And Not A Figure

The sixteenth of this month was a Saturday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The flit at the top of the west field and the two culverts under it took six people until the fourth hour.

The near culvert has been clear since the ninth. The far one was about a quarter full of the green weed that has come up in it over a fortnight, and it cannot be got at with a barrow because the barrow will not go under the flit, so four people pulled it out by hand and put it on the bank, which is what the four of them have always done with it.

Frost was on the low ground at the second hour and Marek was one of the four who broke it with a spade. He was in the ditch from the second hour to the fifth with both hands full and could not have told you afterward which of the two he had been holding.

He has said before that this is not a confession and that it is a fact, and the fact is that a man who has been in a ditch since the second hour is not a man anybody ought to hand a number to at the eighth.

At about the fourth hour a rider came up the coast road with a fold of paper in his coat. He stopped at the gate and did not get off, and the paper was four days old when it came out of his coat.

The well on that shelf is four hundred miles off and takes four days to say anything at all. It has been read on the sixteenth of every month for eleven years, and it was read on the sixteenth at that far end, and what came up the road was a day.

The man of about fifty took the paper standing in the yard, read it, and gave it back. The rider went on to Havergate with a bag of wire and was not fed and did not expect to be.

It went into the entry for this holding as a day and not as a figure. There is no ordinal against it and there is no figure against it and there has never been one, and nobody at that far end has been asked a question about it in eleven years.

This is the ninth of the entries in that book that carry a day and no figure. It is the only column in this building where a thing that happened is entered and the column is allowed to stay empty of what it means.

The wall at that well carries fourteen marks. It did not take a fifteenth this morning and it gave none of the fourteen up. A man on the coast road said that somebody at the far end had offered to take the four feet of it apart and find out what they were, and had not, and nobody here asked him to.

The figure for the sixteenth went into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other one with the frayed corner, in a voice that makes no distinction between a rise and a fall. The two were the same.

**Eight hundred and forty-four hundredweight.**

Up nine off Friday, and the hundred and sixty-sixth figure of a series that has not put the same figure on a page twice since the morning it began.

The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and thirty-three, and this morning's rise went onto the rises, which puts it at seventy-two rises and sixty-one falls, and the man of about fifty said, standing in the yard with the sheet, that a window is a figure and not a count of the series.

This morning's sheet carries the sentence about the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and it says the four have not moved in a hundred and sixty-four days, and it was written for the hundred and sixty-third of those mornings out of a hundred and sixty-five.

It is a sentence about four bodies of households and not about water, and the four launders are a different aggregate, and the two have never shared a line of a page in this building in four years and did not share one this morning.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud this morning and this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page and not left off it.

The second line ran to the fifth hour with six people in it and Marek was one of the six. This holding has never in four years asked a man with a spade in his hand to stand at a table at the seventh hour and say two figures.

The pair stands at **five hundred and fifty days** and **five hundred and ninety-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow and turned it at the top of the yard and went home, and the frost had gone off the low ground by then and the road was going hard in about nine inches of its length.

The second of the four lines in the book he carries is fifty-four days old this morning, with nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in fifty-four days.

The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road, and eleven is a floor.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from the second hour of the afternoon with the door shut. The letter had come up the fen road with the morning post.

It was from the clerk of the first county. It was one paragraph long and it had nothing ruled at the foot of it.

> THE FIRST COUNTY ASKS, IN ONE SENTENCE AND WITHOUT ANY OTHER MATTER, WHAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD NAMED AT THE HEAD OF A SHEET OF HIS OWN ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THIS MONTH HAS DONE. THE COUNCIL HAS READ THAT SHEET AND HAS TAKED THE NAME FROM IT AND DOES NOT ASK FOR THE SHEET BACK AND SAYS SO. THE COUNCIL NOTES THAT A NAME MAY BE COPIED INTO A REGISTER, AND THAT A REGISTER IS A PLACE WHERE A THING IS COPIED AND NOT A PLACE WHERE A THING IS DONE, AND THAT THE FIRST COUNTY HAS NO OTHER WORD FOR WHAT IT IS ASKING FOR. NOTHING IS ASKED OF ANYBODY BY THIS SHEET AND NO OFFICE IS ASKED TO PERFORM ANY ACT IN CONNECTION WITH IT.

The man of about fifty read it twice and then a third time with one finger on the last line. The last line was the one about the register, and he said it was the first letter anybody had written to this building in four years that admitted in its own hand what it did not know how to ask for.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was in the room and had not sat down. The man of about seventy-four with a stick stood in the door for the whole of it and was neither asked in nor asked out.

The reason came before the thing in that room, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and eighty-six, and it is his and of nobody else's, and the man of about fifty did not write it down.

"I am going to give the whole of the answer and I am going to give the reason before I answer it, and the reason is that this letter has been asked three times now in three different shapes. A thing that has been asked three times is not going to be asked a fourth time as a question. It is going to be asked a fourth time as a shape, and a clerk in nine miles is going to work out what shape he wants and go and get it."

The man of about fifty put his thumb on the page and did not write anything on it, and about four people in that room watched him not write anything on it.

"I would rather this room heard me say the shape out loud than had him work it out on his own between now and Tuesday, because what he works out on his own he will not tell us, and what I tell him he will write down, and the difference between those two is about four years." "The answer is that there is nothing to send. That is not a refusal and there is no rule behind it. On the thirteenth I put a name against a piece of work with a day and an hour on it, and that sheet does not say what I have done, because it cannot, and because what I have done in nine days is a wall, two culverts, a boundary ditch, four gates and a room with the door shut on it. There is no order to those. They are the order the days came in." "And there is a worse thing underneath and I am going to say that too. A name is not a column. They have taken the name off my sheet and put it in the middle of their letter where a column would go, and if I answer them in their word I am putting four years of work that has not been done under one man's name." "In about four years there will be a man in a county reading a register and he will find my name against a piece of work, and he will conclude the piece of work was always mine. He will be doing me the courtesy of a man who is given things to do, and he will be wrong, and there will be nothing on the page to correct him with."

Nobody argued with him about it. The man of about fifty said one thing, and he said it without a reason in front of it, which he does perhaps twice in a season.

"Then the answer is a no with a reason on it. This building has never sent one and the first one is going to be read as a softening of something." He put the letter down and squared it against the table. "I will say it, because you are not going to send a name twice, and because I have read the last line of that letter twice and it is the only sentence in it anybody will quote in a fortnight."

Nobody thanked either of them. The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden said that the fourth county had been asked the same question four days ago and had neither answered it nor refused it, and that a body which has not answered is not waiting for an answer.

Then she went out and pulled the door to behind her, because the room was hotter than the yard, and the man of about seventy-four with a stick moved a foot aside to let her past and did not otherwise take any notice.

The man of about fifty asked him one question and it was whether the second sheet was going to be signed, and Marek said no, and the man of about fifty said that was right and that a signed refusal is a thing a clerk files and an unsigned one is a thing a clerk has to think about, and neither of them improved it.

The woman who keeps the seed house came in at about the sixth hour with a loaf and put it on the long table at the wall side, and nobody in that room thanked her for it and about four people had been waiting for it since the fourth hour.

The letter went back up the fen road on the ordinary post, which takes the bag at the eighth hour of the evening and goes twice a week whatever anybody in this building is doing. It went with one sheet under it, a single sentence, saying that a sheet carrying a name is a correct sheet and does not carry the work, and that this holding has no column in four books that the work is in.

The two were not pinned together and neither of them referred to the other, and nobody went down the road with the bag.

The register form is on the middle table, sixty days on it this morning, not filled and not refused, and there is nothing at the head of it and nobody is going to write anything at the head of it.

The offer is on the low board four feet off it, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month and has not been withdrawn, and it has no date and no name and no line for a name, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board.

The window of the office's work shut on the eleventh and the next one opens on the sixth of a month now to come, which is twenty mornings off and not tomorrow. No work has ever been carried out of one in the four years this holding has kept a book.

The man of about seventy is not fetched this morning, and the count of fetchings has not moved all year and nobody in this holding has ever given a reason for it.

His next fetching is on the first of a month, which is fifteen mornings off this one, and nobody has ever asked him which number, and no page in this building gives a reason for the day.

The corridor in the pan is named and open, and open is not a road, and the sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular, and four counties have now asked this building a question about a thing that has not been opened or shut by anything anybody here has done.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page, and no person in this holding has put a foot inside it in four years and nobody is going to this season.

Nothing on the inside of the left forearm has changed since the second hour of this morning. Four inches, branching twice, the same four inches. A day with no figure against it is not a mark, and a letter that admits what it cannot ask is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

The man of about fifty read the single sentence back out of his own book before he folded it, and read it again after, and the two readings were the same and neither was improved.

Then the room sat with the door shut for about twenty minutes and said nothing at all about any of it, and the light came off the west window onto the one sheet on the long table, and the loaf was still there at the seventh hour.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the sixteenth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE FLIT AT THE TOP OF THE WEST FIELD AND THE TWO CULVERTS UNDER IT, SIX PEOPLE TO THE FOURTH HOUR, THE FAR ONE PULLED OUT BY HAND. A WELL FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF AND FOUR DAYS OUT WAS READ ON THE SIXTEENTH AT THAT FAR END AND THE DAY CAME UP THE COAST ROAD AT ABOUT THE FOURTH HOUR AND WENT IN AS A DAY AND NOT AS A FIGURE, WITH NO ORDINAL AND NO FIGURE AND NO QUESTION ASKED OF ANYBODY AT THAT FAR END IN ELEVEN YEARS, AND THAT IS THE NINTH ENTRY IN THIS BOOK THAT CARRIES A DAY AND NO FIGURE. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED EIGHT HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIXTH FIGURE, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE WINDOW IS A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THREE, BEING SEVENTY-TWO RISES AND SIXTY-ONE FALLS, AND THE TWO ADD. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR DAYS, WRITTEN FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THIRD MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIVE. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY DAYS AND FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. A LETTER OF ONE PARAGRAPH CAME FROM THE CLERK OF THE FIRST COUNTY ASKING WHAT THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS DONE, AND IT ADMITTED IN ITS OWN HAND THAT THE FIRST COUNTY HAS NO OTHER WORD FOR WHAT IT IS ASKING FOR. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THAT A NAME IS NOT A COLUMN AND THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO SEND ONE TWICE, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THE HOLDING'S HISTORY IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOK SAID WITHOUT A REASON THAT THE ANSWER IS A NO WITH A REASON ON IT AND IS GOING TO BE THE FIRST OF THOSE THIS BUILDING HAS SENT. A SECOND SHEET OF ONE SENTENCE WENT UNDER IT SAYING THAT A SHEET CARRYING A NAME IS A CORRECT SHEET AND DOES NOT CARRY THE WORK. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

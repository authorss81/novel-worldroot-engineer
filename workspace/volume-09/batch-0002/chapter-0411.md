# Chapter 411

## What A Clerk Can Read

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Sunday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked.

The flit at the top of the west field and the two culverts under it took six people until the fourth hour, and the far culvert was clear for the first time since the ninth and the four of them went down into it anyway and put about a foot of silt in a barrow on the bank, and nobody could say afterwards where the silt had come from.

Marek was one of the six and had both hands full from the second hour to the fifth, and the flit at the top of the west field has about four feet of soft going on the west side of it that nobody has put a barrow across since the second of this month.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud this morning and this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page and not left off it. One of the two men who take the two boards on a Sunday is one of the six, and he went on the flit this morning, and a man who is under a bridge at the seventh hour does not stand at a table and say a number.

**Five hundred and fifty-eight days** and **six hundred and five** are what the two boards say, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-fourth was entered at the eighth hour and read back out of the other book, and the two were the same, and the man of about fifty read it out in the yard afterwards and not in the room with the door shut, because a number said in a yard is a number and a number said in a room is an instruction.

**Eight hundred and sixty hundredweight.**

Up nine off Saturday, and the hundred and seventy-fourth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The window is a hundred and forty-one, the rise having gone onto the rises, which leaves seventy-six rises and sixty-five falls, and about four people in this holding have never once added those two together and about four have added them every morning since they were boys, and neither of the two fours is going to say.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has not moved in a hundred and seventy-two days, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and seventy-first of those mornings out of a hundred and seventy-three.

Four bodies of households and not the launders, and the two aggregates have never shared a line of a page in this building, and the man of about fifty wrote that sentence at the fifth hour before anybody else was up and did not tell anybody he had.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow and turned it at the top of the yard and went home, having walked the four miles yesterday without it.

The second of the four lines in the book he carries is sixty-two days old this morning and has no answer on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in sixty-two days, and the barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road, and eleven is a floor.

About nine people were in the one room from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut. The man from the north had come again, the same man who stood at the gate on Tuesday, and he had walked in and had not asked to be announced and had not asked anybody to leave.

He had a letter in his hand and he put it on the long table at the wall side and he said that he had brought it himself because he did not want it to go up a road on a Sunday with the bag.

> THE FOURTH COUNTY ASKS THE COOPERATIVE HOLDING TO SAY, IN ONE COLUMN, WHAT THE WORK IS THAT HAS TO BE DONE TO A NODE BETWEEN THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE NEXT TAKING, AND IN WHICH COLUMN OF THE HOLDING'S BOOKS THE WORK IS SET OUT, AND THE COUNTY ASKS THE SECOND THING RATHER THAN THE FIRST. THE COUNTY RECORDS THAT ITS CLERK IS NOT A MEMBER OF ANYTHING AND IS NOT ANSWERABLE TO ANYBODY IN THE COUNTY, AND THAT HE HAS BEEN ASKED TO PUT THE TWO THINGS IN A COLUMN AND HAS PUT THEM IN ONE, AND THAT A COLUMN IS A THING HE CAN READ AND A COUNCIL IS A THING HE CANNOT. THE COUNTY DOES NOT ASK THAT ANY PERSON BE SENT.

The clerk of this holding read it twice and then did a thing he has not done in four years, which is to take out the second book and put four sheets on the table at once and lay them in a row and not say anything for about two minutes.

The four sheets are the four answers this holding has sent in ten days, and there is a reason first on three of them and the fourth has one sentence on it.

Tova Reed was in the room and gave the reason before she said the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is thirty-six, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

"I am going to say what I have been looking at for two minutes and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a clerk in four counties has told us to our faces that he can read a column and cannot read a council, and the only person in this room who has ever been asked to do anything by a column is him." "Look at the four of them. Three of them have a sentence at the top that says why. One of them is a single sentence with no reason on it, and that one is the one I said on Monday ought not to be sent, and I was talking about sending it and I was wrong about what was wrong with it." "The one with no reason on it is not wrong because it is short. It is wrong because a person who reads it has to guess what it is for, and a person who has to guess will guess a shape, and the shape they will guess about a sentence with no reason on it is that it is a notice."

The man of about fifty gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and nineteen, and it is his and of nobody else's, and what he said was about columns.

What he said was that a clerk can read a column and cannot read a duty, and that the difference between those two is that nobody has ever written a heading at the top of one of these columns, and that it has been the whole difference for four years.

The man from the north asked him one question while he was still standing at the table, and it was whether a county could print a column of its own and fill it.

The man of about fifty said yes, and that a county that printed one and filled it would be doing the first thing anybody in four counties has done about this in four years, and that it would be doing it about four counties away from the ground and would be wrong in about four years in a way nobody could check.

"I am going to say the whole of what a clerk can read, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that this holding has been asked that question three times in ten days by three people who are right, and I have never once put the answer in a room, and I have put it in a book four times where nobody reads it." "A clerk can read a column. He can put a figure in it and he can take a figure out of it and he can rule a line under it, and he can do all three of those things better than anybody in four counties, and there is not one thing in this building he can do with a column except that."

The clerk of this holding made a shape in the air with one finger and did not say it out loud, and about four people in that room saw him do it.

"A clerk cannot read a duty. There is no way of entering one in four books and no way of taking one out again." "The reason is that nobody has ever written a heading at the top of one of these columns, and a column with no heading is a place a clerk can put a figure and a person cannot put a name. That is the whole difference and it has been the whole difference for four years."

Marek gave the reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and ninety-two, and it is his and of nobody else's, and that count does not move on a page where nobody gave a reason first, and it did not move on the seven pages of this month where nobody did.

"I am going to say the one thing I have said in this room twice this month and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a man has now asked us the same question three times and has asked it the same way three times, and a thing asked the same way three times is a thing that will be asked a fourth time in about a month. I would rather we answered it in a room than had it answered in a county book by somebody who is not one of us." "There are six columns on a door nine hundred yards off this building and every one of them is headed nothing and has been headed nothing for four years. The seventh of them counts readings and is not one of those six, and I am not going to say what it counts besides readings." "There is a third column ruled and empty on a sheet of terms in this building that has two hands in it. There is a form in a drawer four miles west with four lines on it and one figure on the first and three empty." "There are two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check and the first of them is not named, and there are six things this holding does not know and nobody has supplied a unit for any of the six." "And there is no column anywhere in this holding that the work is in. A clerk can read that sentence this afternoon and take it up a road and put it in a county book, and it will be true in about four years, and it will be the truest thing anybody has put in that book, and it will not have told him one single thing he can do on Monday morning."

Nobody thanked anybody.

The man from the north read the four sheets on the table and read the sentence with no reason on it twice and did not say what he thought of it.

He said one thing before he left, and it was that he was going to write down what the room had said and that he was not going to write down what the room had not said, and that those were two different things and that the second of them was the whole of his trade.

The man of about fifty put the four sheets back in the second book in the order they were in, and nobody has numbered them and nobody is going to, and about four people in this building have asked him in four years why the book is not numbered and about four have not, and he has never given a reason.

The woman who keeps the seed house said there was bread and that the loaf from Friday was gone, and the man of about fifty said there was a loaf from Friday morning, and the woman who keeps the seed house said that was the same loaf.

About four people in that room laughed and about four did not, and the count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move, because this was a room, and a room with the door shut is where that count is kept.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room and has not moved off it since a month now one month back, and it is not filled, and a second council has given an open offer as one of its reasons for not relying on this holding's account of anything.

The instrument is still where it was, face up, with no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board and nobody is going to be.

A second council has given an open offer as one of its reasons for not relying on this holding's account of anything, and the instrument is still where it was.

The register form is on the middle table at sixty-eight days and is not filled and not refused and has nothing at the head of it, and nobody wrote a heading on it and nobody read it out this afternoon, and nobody in this building has been told to take the offer off that board and nobody is going to be.

The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road. The sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular, and the order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and opens nothing, and the route is not reopened by anything in this holding's possession this season.

The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-three, and the next of them is on the first of a month, which is seven mornings off this one, and nobody has ever asked him which number and no page in this building gives a reason for it.

The compost line is paid at twenty not discharged in twenty and does not fall this month, and the seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-four and is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-fourth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE FLIT AT THE TOP OF THE WEST FIELD AND THE TWO CULVERTS UNDER IT, SIX PEOPLE TO THE FOURTH HOUR, THE FAR CULVERT CLEAR FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE NINTH. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN, ONE OF THE TWO MEN WHO TAKE THE TWO BOARDS HAVING BEEN ONE OF THE SIX. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT DAYS AND SIX HUNDRED AND FIVE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED EIGHT HUNDRED AND SIXTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOURTH FIGURE, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE WINDOW IS A HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE, BEING SEVENTY-SIX RISES AND SIXTY-FIVE FALLS, AND THE TWO ADD. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO DAYS, WRITTEN FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIRST OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-THREE. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF SILLING CAME DOWN THE FOUR-MILE ROAD AT THE NINTH HOUR AND WENT HOME, HAVING WALKED THE FOUR MILES ON SATURDAY WITHOUT IT, AND WAS NOT ASKED ANYTHING, AND THE SECOND OF THE FOUR LINES IN HIS OWN BOOK IS SIXTY-TWO DAYS OLD. THE BARROW STANDS AT ELEVEN JOURNEYS AND ELEVEN IS A FLOOR. THE MAN FROM THE NORTH CAME AGAIN ON FOOT AND PUT A LETTER ON THE TABLE, ASKING IN ONE COLUMN WHAT THE WORK IS AND IN WHICH COLUMN OF THE HOLDING'S BOOKS IT IS SET OUT, AND ASKS THAT NO PERSON BE SENT. FOUR ANSWERS WERE TAKEN OUT OF THE SECOND BOOK AND PUT ON THE TABLE IN A ROW, AND THREE HAVE A REASON FIRST AND ONE HAS A SINGLE SENTENCE WITH NO REASON ON IT. A WOMAN GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THE SHORT ONE IS WRONG BECAUSE A PERSON WHO READS IT HAS TO GUESS WHAT IT IS FOR, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS THIRTY-SIX. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOK GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID A CLERK CAN READ A COLUMN AND CANNOT READ A DUTY, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETEEN. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THERE IS NO COLUMN ANYWHERE IN THIS HOLDING THAT THE WORK IS IN, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

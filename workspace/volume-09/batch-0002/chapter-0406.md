# Chapter 406

## The Fourth County Refuses

The nineteenth of this month was a Tuesday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The head ditch of the low field and the foot of the west dyke took four people until the fourth hour, and the toe at the foot of the dyke is up two fingers off what it was on the third of last month, and the man at the front of it said that a toe that has come up two fingers in a month is a toe that has started, and the three behind him did not answer him.

A heron stood in the head ditch for the whole of the morning and did not move once, and the four of them worked around it, and nobody in this holding has ever written the heron down or put it in a book, and that is not modesty. It is that there is no column for it, and a thing with no column does not get a line, and a thing that does not get a line is not a thing anybody has looked after.

Marek was one of the four and had the other barrow by the third hour, and the barrow went up the slope twice and came back twice, and the two trips are not entered anywhere because a barrow-load out of a ditch is not a figure this holding keeps. At about the seventh hour the two day-minus rules were read out loud before either of the numbers, one man taking the first and another taking the second so that neither of them is trusted with both. They stand at **five hundred and fifty-three days** and **six hundred**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

Fifty-five mornings out of ninety-three, counted from the seventeenth of a month three months back. That frame does not move this month.

The nineteenth was put into the book that keeps the third launder at about the eighth hour and read back out of the other one, and the two figures were the same, and the man of about fifty read it out with the frayed corner between his fingers the way he has read every morning of the four years.

**Eight hundred and forty-three hundredweight.**

Down five off Monday, and the hundred and sixty-ninth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the first morning of it.

The window this morning is a hundred and thirty-six, the fall having gone onto the falls, so it stands at seventy-three rises and sixty-three falls, and the two of them add, and nobody in this holding has ever been asked to make them add and nobody has ever had to.

Four bodies of households have gone a hundred and sixty-seven days without a move in their aggregate, and the sentence saying so went on this morning's sheet for the hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings out of a hundred and sixty-eight. The launders are a different aggregate, and a man who puts those two on one line of a page has cost this holding a winter once already.

He came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow, turned it at the top of the yard, and went home without stopping.

Nobody asked him about the second of the four lines in his own book, which is fifty-seven days old this morning, and nobody in this holding has been going to ask him about it.

He had been in the room at the node on Monday for the first hour of it and had not been in this room on Tuesday, and about four people in this holding have noticed over four years that he comes on days when there is nothing here for him, and about four have not noticed, and neither of those two fours has ever said so to him and neither of them is going to. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road, and eleven is a floor, and it did not rise this morning.

The letter came up the fen road with the ordinary post and it was two paragraphs long and the first of them is the answer to a question this holding put in writing nine days ago, and the answer is no.

> THE FOURTH COUNTY WILL NOT FORM A COUNCIL. THE COUNTY HAS CONSIDERED IT AT TWO MEETINGS AND THE REASON IS GIVEN HERE SO THAT IT MAY NOT BE ASKED FOR TWICE. THE REASON IS THAT THE COUNTY HAS ELEVEN PERSONS WHO COULD SIT IN A ROOM AND A TWELFTH WHO COULD NOT, AND THAT A BODY WHICH DECIDES BY A COUNT OF HANDS DECIDES ABOUT THE PERSON IN THE DOORWAY, AND THE COUNTY HAS NOT YET FOUND A WAY OF DECIDING THAT DOES NOT DO THAT. THE COUNTY ALSO RECORDS THAT ITS CLERK HAS WRITTEN ONE LETTER TO THE COOPERATIVE HOLDING IN ELEVEN DAYS, AND THAT THE CLERK IS NOT ONE OF THE ELEVEN AND IS NOT ANSWERABLE TO ANYBODY IN THE COUNTY, AND THAT A BODY WHICH IS REPRESENTED BY A PERSON IT CANNOT CALL IS NOT A BODY. THE CLERK'S LETTER OF THE SIXTEENTH ASKED IN WHAT COLUMN OF THE HOLDING'S BOOKS THE WORK IS SET OUT. THE COUNTY REPEATS THAT QUESTION AND DOES NOT ANSWER IT, AND THE COUNTY'S REASON FOR REPEATING IT IS THAT A COLUMN IS A THING A CLERK CAN READ AND A COUNCIL IS A THING A CLERK CANNOT.

The woman who keeps the seed house read it twice at the door before she brought it in, which she does, and put it on the long table at the wall side, and nobody asked her to. She said that the paper had come round the way paper comes round, which is that a man who wrote it on a Friday posts it on a Saturday and it arrives on a Tuesday, and that a body which has decided a thing on a Friday and told the world on a Tuesday has had a weekend to be talked out of it, and that this one had not been.

Tova Reed was in the room and had been in it since the fourth hour. She read it twice herself and then said the first thing anybody in the room had said, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is thirty-two, and it is hers and of nobody else's, and she was not thanked for it.

"I am going to say the true thing about that county and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that eleven people and a doorway is a county and it is a body, and everything else in this letter is a body being frightened of a piece of paper it has not been shown." "They are right. Not right about a council, because that is their business and not mine, but right about what a count of hands does to the person in a doorway, and right that a clerk they cannot call is not one of them. A county that has worked that out on its own in eleven days has understood more about this holding than the first council understood in nine." "And the last line is the one that matters and it is the reason I am going to say the rest of it. A column is a thing a clerk can read. A council is a thing a clerk cannot. That is a true sentence and it was written by a county that has never seen this building, and it is the same sentence the man who keeps the book has been trying to say since the eighth with a page instead of a mouth."

The man of about fifty said no for a while and then said that she was going to be asked whether the holding had a column, and that it did not, and that the fourth county had asked it in a letter and the fourth county had been right to.

Then Marek was wrong in that room, and he was wrong about four lines, and it took about nine minutes and nobody enjoyed any part of it.

He had drafted four lines on Monday afternoon for the fourth county and had not sent them, and Tova Reed had read the draft on the long table at the wall side without being asked and had said nothing about it until about the sixth hour of this afternoon, and what she said about it came with the reason in front of it and the count of that in this holding's history is thirty-three, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

"I am going to say that the four lines are wrong and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a fourth county has just refused to become a body on the grounds that it cannot be represented by a person it cannot call, and this building is about to answer it in four lines, and four lines is a shape and the shape of four lines is a building that does not do the work." "A county reads a shape. We have known that since the eleventh and I said it on the eleventh and the man who keeps the book wrote both halves on one line of the book so that a man in four years could not have them both ways. You have drafted four lines to a county that has just told us it will not be a county, and those four lines are going to be read in that county as the fourth thing a building has said to them in ten days, and the fourth thing is going to be the one they keep."

He did not improve it and he did not repeat it, which is the only technique he has for being in a room with somebody who has said the true thing at him, and about four people in that room have worked out what it is over four years and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has ever said so to him.

"It is a fair thing to be told and I am not going to argue with it in this room," he said. "I drafted it on Monday because on Monday a council had taken a node and I wanted one more thing on paper by the end of the week. That is the whole of the reason and there is nothing else behind it."

The man of about fifty asked him one question and it was whether the four lines were still in his coat, and Marek said they were, and the man of about fifty said then they would not go up the road, and Marek said no, and that was the end of it and it took about eleven seconds.

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about fifty wrote the refusal of the county and the two reasons for it into the second book exactly as they had been read out, and he wrote the count of questions this holding has asked in the line of asking a body of households for a figure as nil and is nil, and he did not write the four lines into it at all.

He also did not enter the reason the reader of this body gave for having drafted the four lines, and Marek did not ask him to, and about four people in that room had noticed the omission and about four had not, and the omission is the only thing either of those two fours will agree about in about four years.

The man of about fifty said one thing about a column and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and sixteen.

What he said was that the answer is going to be read as a no, and that he would rather it was read as a no that has a reason on it, and the woman who keeps the seed house said that is the first sentence anybody has said out loud in that room this month that she had not already worked out for herself.

"I am going to say the whole of what this holding has got, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a clerk in a county has asked a question a body cannot answer and a body has refused to be a body, and both of those arrived on this road within two days." "We keep four books. There is no column in any of them that the work is in, and there has not been one in four years, and the reason there has not been one is four years old and it is a bad reason, which is that nobody ever wrote a heading at the top of a column, and a column with no heading is a column nobody can ask a question about." "That is the whole answer. I am not going to improve it and I am not going to add a second paragraph to it, and it is going to be read as a no, and I would rather it was read as a no with a reason on it than as a no with nothing."

At about the eleventh hour a man came to the gate from the north, on his own feet, and he was not the clerk of the first council and he had not been sent, and he said he had come to look at a gate and had been told at a house with a barn behind it to come today and not to bring anybody.

Marek was in the yard when he came and the woman who keeps the seed house was in the yard, and neither of the two of them said anything to him about why he was there. The man stood at the gate and looked at it for about a quarter of an hour without touching it.

The gate is a sluice gate on the county line and it is in this holding's book and it has never been this holding's in any way anybody has put in writing, and four bodies of households use the drain it stands on every winter and three of the four are in another county. The man from the north did not ask whose it was. He asked one question, which was who had last opened it, and Marek told him, and the man wrote it down in a book of his own that is not in this building.

The gate was not opened on Tuesday and nothing went through it, and the fourth body of households has water and the other three have water, and that is a fact about a winter and not about a Tuesday.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month, which is a month ago to the day, and it has not been withdrawn, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board.

A second council has given an open offer as one of its reasons for not relying on this holding's account of anything, and the instrument has not moved and has not been copied and there is no second instrument of that offer on any table in this holding this season.

The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and the sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular and lets nothing out, and nobody in this building has been able to put it down in four years or is going to this season.

Fifteen lines in the use log, and a county that has refused to be a body is not a use, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season.

The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and the two have never been added to one another, and two pieces of paper from one clerk in eleven days are two pieces of paper and not two fifty-fours.

A bridge was run once for about nine minutes five weeks ago and no person in this holding has an instrument that says whether it is on or off, and the man of about fifty did not say so this morning. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked and not measured and not crossed and not priced and not explained.

Four inches and two branches, the same as at the second hour of this morning.

A refusal is not a mark, four lines that were not sent are not a mark, and a man standing at a gate with a book of his own is not a mark. The four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

The room sat with the door shut for the last hour of it and did not settle the column question, and the question was not in the room to be settled. The man who keeps the book entered the fourth county's letter and the two reasons in it and did not enter a third reason, and nobody in that room asked him for one.

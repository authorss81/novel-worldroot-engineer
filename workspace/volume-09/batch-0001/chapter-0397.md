# Chapter 397

## A Second County In Three Days

The tenth of this month was a Sunday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and was not worked, because there is nobody at the headland above Marden to work it. The fields on that headland have waited for the fiftieth morning.

The rota rises by exactly one on a morning the fourth line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and it stands at seventy-nine mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty not, and twenty-nine and fifty are seventy-nine. Twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and has not moved on any morning of this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited, because a field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man.

The count is kept two ways and the two ways do not agree. This is the twenty-seventh morning of the run that began on the twenty-sixth of a month four months back, and it is the fourteenth morning since the eighteenth of a month two months back, and both of those are printed because a morning that prints one of them and not the other has put one number in two columns.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not at the node this morning and nobody asked him to be, and the second of the four lines in the book he carries is forty-eight days old this morning with nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in forty-eight days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that four-mile road and it did not rise.

At about the eighth hour the figure for the tenth was entered and read back out of the other book with the frayed corner, and the two were the same.

**Eight hundred and thirty-two hundredweight.**

Nine higher than Saturday, and the hundred and sixtieth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice in sixty mornings. The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and twenty-seven, and the rise went onto the rises, which puts it at sixty-nine rises and fifty-eight falls, and sixty-nine and fifty-eight are a hundred and twenty-seven.

A hundred and fifty-eight days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households. The sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fifty-seventh of those mornings out of a hundred and fifty-nine, and the launders are a different aggregate, and a man who puts those two on one line of a page has cost this holding a winter once already.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud this morning and this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. The fourth line came round and went away empty, and the six who would have been on the wall were on the boundary ditch instead, and Marek was one of the six and had both hands full from the second hour to the fifth.

The pair stands at **five hundred and forty-four days** and **five hundred and ninety-one**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

A sheet went up on the door of the seed house before the middle of the morning, in a wind that took two of the four tacks out of it by the second hour of the afternoon. It was a notice of a second council, for a second county, to be formed in three days, and the three days were named, and at the foot of it was the same three-part page with the gap in it, folded in three, and the fold lines were worn soft at the creases, which is what a page looks like when it has been carried round in a coat by somebody who has shown it to people.

The woman who keeps the seed house read it twice and put it back on the door and did not take it down. She said the tacks were hers and that she would put two more in at the top corners in the morning, and she said it the way she says everything, which is as a fact about a nail and not as a position about a council.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the notice was on the long table at the wall side with the wet corner torn a half inch, and nobody had put it beside the other papers.

He said one thing, and it came with the reason attached in front of it as it does in that room, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and seventy-eight.

"Two counties have read a page with a hole in it and decided it means they are on their own, and I am not going to stand in this room and tell either of them they are wrong, because I have read the page and there is a sentence on it that is true and a sentence on it that is true and they are not the same sentence."

"Here is the part I want said out loud. Nobody in this holding has ever costed a node. Not for a council, not for a body of households, not for the four of us on a wet morning in a room with a door shut. We keep four books and there is no column in any of the four with a heading on it, and what that means in practice is that if somebody asks us what a thing costs to hold from one season to the next, we have to look at the wall, and the wall has nothing on it, and that is not modesty. That is four years of not writing it down."

The man of about fifty said that about four people in this room had known that for four years and about four had not, and the four that had not had never had a reason to look, and there was nobody in this holding to make a person have a reason to look.

Tova Reed asked one question, and it was the second question anybody in this building has asked anybody in the line of asking a body of households for a figure this season, and the count of questions the reader of this body has asked in that line is nil and is nil.

She asked it of Marek and not of the room, and she asked it in a way that made the room wait.

"If a council writes to you and asks what a node costs to hold, what do you send back?"

"I send back what this building has, which is a correct statement that no figure is kept, and the reason no figure is kept, and the offer of a list if the council will tell us what it wants on it."

"You will not do that," Tova Reed said. "You will send back four lines, and four lines read as a refusal, and about half the men in that room will read them as a refusal of their own council, and a council that has been refused by an engineer is a council that has to decide what to do about an engineer."

Nobody argued her out of it. About four people in the room had worked out inside a minute that she was right, and about four had worked out that she was right and that saying so in front of a man who has just put his hand flat on a table is a thing you do to somebody once a season.

Marek did not answer her and did not thank her and did not repeat what she had said, and about four people in that room noticed that he had not repeated it and about four did not. He has done that four times in four years and it is the only technique he has for being in a room with somebody who has said the true thing at him, and about four people in this holding have worked out what it is and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has ever said so to him.

The woman who keeps the seed house put the tacks back in on Friday morning, at the top corners, with two of her own, and the notice came down off the door in the wet on Thursday night and went back up on Friday and was still up on Saturday, and nobody in this building removed it and nobody put anything next to it.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month, and it has not been withdrawn, and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name. Nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board and nobody is going to be, and there is not going to be a second instrument of an offer on any table in this holding this season.

The register form is on the middle table where it has stood fifty-four days, not filled and not refused, and nobody wrote a heading on it. The form with the four lines is in a drawer about four miles and a bit west of here, face up, and its first line carries one hundred and eighty-four hundredweight, which is the figure of the twenty-second return, and the three lines under it are empty and have been since the day it came back, and there is no way from this building to put a second figure on that form.

Harlan Vetch came up the four-mile road on the Wednesday and brought the fifth share, and he did it in a barrow in about an hour and a quarter, and that is the only time the page gives it for him, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brings the same barrow up that road in about an hour and three-quarters, and the two figures are four separate figures and are not added to each other and neither of them is converted into the other.

They were in the same room on the Wednesday and neither of them asked the other anything, and about four people in this holding have noticed that over eleven years and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has ever said so to either of them. Vetch said the hour and a quarter out loud to nobody in particular while he was tipping the load, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was not in the room when he said it.

The woman who keeps the seed house came in at about the sixth hour to ask whether a notice of a council was the sort of thing that ought to have four tacks in it, and Marek said that it was not, and that she had asked the only question about the notice that anybody in the room had thought of, and that a thing held up by two tacks on a windy day is a thing that will be down by Tuesday and read by nobody, and a thing held up by four tacks is a thing a body will be able to point at in about four years and say that it was there.

She said that four tacks cost about a penny and that nobody in this holding had ever costed a notice, and Marek said that nobody in this holding had ever needed to before this month, and neither of them said anything else about it and both of them went and found a hammer.

Nobody thanked anybody, and the count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move, and there has not been a yard in this holding since the thirteenth of last month, and the count of times a man who keeps a book has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move either.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour and turned the barrow at the top of the yard and went home, and he was not asked anything about the notice on the door of the seed house and nobody was going to. The barrow stands at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-four and is read on the thirtieth and on the first and on no other morning. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count stays at twenty-three. The compost line is paid at twenty not discharged in twenty, and the word before that figure has never once disagreed with it.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a notice nailed to a door is not a use and a council that has not met yet is not a use, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and a sheet that went up on a door without anybody's name on it is not a fifty-fourth of anything.

What this holding cannot check is two figures and what it does not know is six things, and a second county, a second council, a page carried round in a coat and a cost nobody has ever kept are none of them a seventh. The mark on the arm is four inches and branches twice, and a council is not a mark, a nail is not a mark, and a woman's question about four lines is not a mark.

The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and was not walked and not measured. The seed vault of forty households has not come up and nobody has been down and nobody is going to be.

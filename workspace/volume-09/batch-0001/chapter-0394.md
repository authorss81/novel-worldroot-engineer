# Chapter 394

## Forty-One Marks

The seventh of this month was a Thursday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The boundary ditch and the two culverts took six people until the fourth hour. The near one has been clear since the ninth, the far one was about a third full, and the six of them put two barrow-loads out of the far one and stopped there, because it has been going down by itself since the twenty-third of last month and there is no need to be in a thing twice in a week.

The channel along the inside face of the west dyke has been finished on both days of it for a fortnight and about six feet of it at the top end is still soft. The six of them stood at the top end and looked at it for about a quarter of an hour and then went and did the boundary ditch instead. One of them put his heel in at the top of it on the way past and the print closed over by itself inside a count of three, and that is the whole of the test that was applied to it this morning, and the test did not ask for a spade.

Nobody in this holding has ever been told to do a thing about that six feet of channel and nobody is going to be, because the man who would have to be told is the man who said on the second of a month now three months back that a third day of that channel is not this holding's to spend.

At about the seventh hour the two day-minus rules were read out loud, both numbers named before either was, one man taking the first and the other the second so that neither has to be trusted with both. They stand at **five hundred and forty-one days** and **five hundred and eighty-eight**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened. Forty-nine mornings out of eighty-one since the seventeenth of a month three months back, and it is a habit and not a measurement, and there is no column for it in this book and there never has been.

At about the eighth hour the figure for the seventh was entered and read back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two were the same.

**Eight hundred and nineteen hundredweight.**

It has fallen off Wednesday by five, and it is the hundred and fifty-seventh figure of a series that has not come out at one figure twice since the first morning of it. The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and twenty-four, and the fall went onto the falls, which puts it at sixty-seven rises and fifty-seven falls, and the two add the way they have added on every morning of this year without anybody making them add.

A hundred and fifty-five days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fifty-fourth of those mornings out of a hundred and fifty-six, and it concerns four bodies of households, and the four launders are a different figure, and the two have not been added in four years and were not added this morning.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the sheet that came to the door yesterday was on the long table at the wall side where the man of about fifty had put it, and it had been left face down overnight by somebody who did not say which of them it was.

Nobody asked who had turned it over and nobody is going to ask, and about four people in this holding have a guess and about four have not, and the guess has not been entered in any book because a guess about who touched a piece of paper is not a record of anything except a piece of paper.

He turned it over at about the fourth hour and nobody had asked him to. There are marks on the outside of it. He counted them twice, out loud, in the room, and the second count was the same as the first.

"Forty-one," he said. "And I am going to give the reason before I say what I am going to do with them, and the reason is that I have been asked twice this week what four counties make of a page, and the honest answer to both of those questions is that nobody in this building knows, and a book that says forty-one is closer to the truth than a book that says nothing."

"And I am not going to write forty-one people," he said. "Forty-one is not forty-one people. A mark is not a name and a name is not a witness, and a witness is not a name, and this page has come back with three or four hands on it that anybody can place and about as many again that nobody here can, and I am going to enter what is on the paper and not what anybody would like it to mean."

He entered it in the second book on a fresh line, and he entered the number and not the word, and underneath the number he wrote what kind of marks they were, which is a diagonal and a curve and a blot, and he did not write whose.

There were forty-one marks and the four bodies of households number more doors than that between them, and the man of about fifty said so, and about four people in that room had already worked that out and about four had not, and neither of the two fours said anything about it. A body of households is not a household and a mark is not a door, and a man who counted doors on a page in front of nine people would have been doing arithmetic on an opinion.

Marek asked him one question and it was how many of the marks were on the Havergate copy, and the man of about fifty said he did not know and that a page that came back does not say which house of a body of households put a mark on it, and that Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling have between them more doors than there are marks, and that the arithmetic of that is not arithmetic.

"Enter the number," Marek said. "It is the only figure on this page that anybody can check, and if somebody in about four years finds it in a county book with a different figure against it, then this page was wrong and not the county."

A second thing came up the road on the same afternoon, and it was not a letter so much as a question written on the back of one, and it came from a clerk in one of the four counties who has a book of his own and is not a member of the cooperative. He had had to enter a date against a reading, and the date he entered was a date he had chosen.

This holding has a reading in its book of the standing willow with no day against it. A man took it, and he did not write down the day he took it, and there is no day on the page, and a clerk four counties away needed a day and did not have one, and he put one in.

Tova Reed read the question out and then read the sentence under it twice, and gave the reason before she said what she thought of it, and the count of that in the holding's history is twenty-six.

The clerk had not signed his own guess either. He had written the day in the ordinary place in an ordinary hand and had not written a question mark against it, and about four people in that room read that as a man being careful and about four read it as a man being certain, and Tova Reed said that a man being certain is the one this building has to be careful of, and that a man being careful is easy.

"I am going to say that the man who wrote that date has not done anything wrong and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a clerk who needs a day and has not got one has three things he can do. He can leave it blank, and a blank in a county book reads as a failure of the office. He can write a word, and a word in a county book reads as an answer nobody checked. Or he can write a day, and a day reads as a fact, and it is the only one of the three that anybody in his position can live with."

"And I am not going to be angry with him and I am not going to write and tell him the day either, because we have not got the day. A register that carries a day and an hour and the name of the person who read it is a register, and ours does not carry a day, and a county book that carries a wrong day is a county book that has been made honest by a thing that ought to be in ours."

"Ask Marek what he would have written," the man of about fifty said, and Tova Reed said that she had not asked him because a man who is asked that question in a room with the door shut is being asked for a fact about himself, and there are four books in this building and none of them has his name in it, and she would rather find out in a year from a page than find out on a Friday from a face.

Nobody said he was sorry. Nobody said that it did not matter, because it did matter, and about four people in that room had already worked out that it was going to matter in a month and about four had not.

Marek said one thing about the marks and gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and seventy-six, and it is his and of nobody else's, because the count does not move on a page where nobody gave a reason first and it did not move on the two pages where somebody did.

"A mark is not a name," he said, "and the reason we are going to be asked about this page for the next year is not that forty-one is the wrong number. It is that everybody who put one of those marks is now standing behind a page that says the engine means to take off a branch at nineteen named places, and about four of the forty-one read that sentence as a reason and about four read it as a warning, and both of them are standing behind the same page and neither of them is standing behind the other."

The man of about seventy-four with a stick came as far as the door at about the sixth hour and stood in it and was not asked in and was not asked out, and the woman who keeps the seed house said afterwards that there was bread in the seed house and that the loaf from four days ago was still good, which is the only thing anybody in that room said for about an hour.

The offer sheet is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month and it has not been withdrawn since, and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name. The register form is on the middle table where it has stood fifty-one days, not filled and not refused, and nobody wrote a heading on it, and the sheet with three parts and a gap in it was not put beside either of the other two.

The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and the sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular and cannot be put down by anybody here. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and was not walked and not measured and not crossed and not priced and not explained, and it has no dimension on any page.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a mark on a page that came to a door is not a use, and a date a clerk in another county had to guess is not a use either, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count of fetchings stays at twenty-three, and the next of them is on the first of a month, twenty-four mornings off this one.

The compost line is paid, one hundred pounds, and the run is twenty not discharged in twenty, and the word before the figure has never once disagreed with the figure. The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-four and is not read this morning, being read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning.

There are two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check, and there have been two for two years, and the first of them is still not named, and a date a clerk in another county has put in a county book is not a third of them here. There are six things this holding does not know and no seventh has been added this afternoon for forty-one marks or for a date.

The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and the two have never been added. A body has asked about who holds the pen once in four years and it is still once, and a mark on a page is not that question and is not entered as one.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not asked anything this afternoon and came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour and turned the barrow and went home. The second of the four lines in his own book is forty-five days old and has no answer on it and no place for one, and the barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road and did not rise.

He had come up the road at the ninth hour on a day when there was nothing for him to do here, and about four people in this holding have noticed that over four years and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has ever said so to him and neither of them is going to.

The mark on the arm is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning. Forty-one marks is not a mark, a clerk's guess is not a mark, and a person's refusal to be angry is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

Nobody thanked anybody, and there was no yard in this holding today for either of the two counts that are only ever moved in one to move. The mark on the arm is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice and is the same four inches it was at the second hour, and forty-one marks is not a mark, a clerk's guess is not a mark, and a person's refusal to be angry is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was not asked anything this afternoon. He came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour and turned the barrow and went home, and about four people in this holding have noticed over four years that he comes up a road on days when there is nothing for him to do here, and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has ever said so to him. The barrow stands at eleven journeys and eleven is a floor.

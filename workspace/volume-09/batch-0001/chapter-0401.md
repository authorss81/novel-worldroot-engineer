# Chapter 401

## A Seventh Answer, And No Eighth

The fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour on a Thursday, the fourteenth, and it went away empty again, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it. The fields on that headland have waited for the fifty-first morning.

The rota stands at eighty mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-one not, and twenty-nine and fifty-one are eighty. Twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and has not moved on any morning of this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited, because a field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man.

Both counts of the rotation go on this page and they do not agree with each other. The first is the twenty-eighth morning of a run that began on the twenty-sixth of a month four months back. The second is the fifteenth morning since the eighteenth of a month two months back, and the frame on that one moves when the month turns, and the change is said once and not twice.

The figure for the fourteenth was entered at about the eighth hour and read back out of the book with the frayed corner in a voice that does not change for a rise or a fall, and the two were the same.

**Eight hundred and forty hundredweight.**

It is up nine on Wednesday, and it is the hundred and sixty-fourth figure of a series that has not come out at one figure twice since the first of it. Seventy-one of the rises and sixty of the falls are counted in, and that is a hundred and thirty-one, and it is the window and not the count of the series that a reader wants first.

It is a hundred and sixty-two days since anything moved in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and sixty-first of those mornings out of a hundred and sixty-three. It is about four bodies of households and not about water and not about the launders, and the two aggregates have never been added in this building and were not added this afternoon.

Nobody read the pair aloud this morning, so this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page and not left off it. The fourth line came round and went away empty for the second time in three days, and the six who would have gone to the headland were on the boundary ditch instead, and Marek was one of the six and was in a ditch from the second hour to the fifth and had a shovel in his hands at the seventh.

The pair stands at **five hundred and forty-eight days** and **five hundred and ninety-five**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the second hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling was not in the room and was not sent for, and the second of the four lines in his own book is fifty-two days old and there is nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in fifty-two days.

He came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow, and the barrow has a wheel out of true since the spring that nobody has looked at and that he has not asked anybody to look at, and he turned it at the top of the yard and went home, and nobody in this building was told anything about the page in his coat and nobody was going to be told about it by anybody.

The clerk of the first council wrote again at about the third hour, and it was a short letter, and it was not the same letter, and it asked one thing.

It asked who speaks for the eleven acres of standing willow at the node. It said the council had been told, in a room, that the ring of bare ground inside those acres had never been walked or measured or crossed or priced or explained, and that the council was not asking for any of those things, and that it was asking a simpler thing, which was whether there is a person or a body that the standing wood answers to, and if so which.

About four people in that room had six different answers ready, and about four had a seventh, and the seventh one was a good one and it was said out loud by the man of about fifty, and it was wrong in the way the other six are wrong, and the room knew it was wrong about the time he had finished the second sentence of it.

It is entered here because it was said. A person who keeps a book in this building entered it in full, and read it back, and then wrote underneath it that it was the seventh of the seven, and that none of the seven is an answer, and that there is no eighth.

Nobody said the seventh was stupid and nobody said the sixth had been stupid either, and about four people in that room had been ready to say the first thing out loud on Saturday and had not said it, and the count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and did not move this afternoon, and this was a room and not a yard.

The seventh answer was that the wood answers to whoever is standing in it, which is a thing two men in that room believed and which neither of them would have said in a yard, and it is not an answer because the standing wood has eleven acres of it and a great deal of it is not standing anywhere a man can be.

The woman who keeps the seed house said one thing about the six wrong answers, and said it without a reason first, which is a thing she is allowed to do and does about four times a year.

"Six answers in four years," she said, "and every one of them was somebody trying to help, and I have never once heard anybody in this room sound as though they had tried to help."

She was not thanked for that and she did not look to be, and the man of about fifty wrote nothing down about it, and about four people in that room agreed with her and about four heard something else in it, and neither of the two fours is going to be asked which they were.

What he said about it, when the room had gone quiet, went the way everything he says in that room goes, reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and eighty-three.

"I am going to say the true thing about that question and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a council has now asked this holding the same question twice in six days and the second time to a person, and a building that answers the same question twice differently has stopped being a holding and become a source of answers, and I have seen what that does to a village in about four years and I do not want to see it here."

"The answer is no. There is no person and there is no body that the standing wood answers to, and there has not been one since before I came, and the reason there has not been one is that nobody has ever asked to be the one. That is a fact about this ground and it is not a failure of the ground. The wood keeps a record and the record does not have an author on it, and I have said before that a limit of what an object will give you is not a refusal of the question, and that sentence is said once in this building and I am not going to say it again here."

The man of about fifty did not enter the seventh answer as an answer. He entered the seven, and the fact that none of the seven is an answer, and the fact that there is no eighth, and he entered the words SPEAKS FOR THE ELEVEN ACRES with nothing against them and no heading on them, because nobody in this holding has ever written a heading at the top of one of those columns and the whole of this afternoon was a demonstration of why.

A body has asked about who holds the pen once in four years and it is still once, and a clerk in a county asking who speaks for eleven acres is not that question and is not entered as one. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and the two have never been added to one another, and two letters from one clerk in six days are two letters and not two fifty-fours.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in the room for the first hour of it and had a reason to be, because the clerk of the council had asked in the first letter whether this holding had anybody who could be asked about a wood, and the holding had sent him a list of nothing and the man of about thirty-one of Silling had read the list on the road and had come up to say that there is a name for it in a house four miles off that nobody in this building has ever been to.

He said the name and nobody wrote it down, and Marek wrote it down, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling looked at it and said nothing and went home, and the second of the four lines in his own book is fifty-two days old and has no answer on it and nobody in this holding is going to put one there this season.

The clerk's second letter had been written in a different hand from his first, and the man of about fifty said so and gave the reason first, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and fourteen.

"Somebody else in that councilhouse wrote the second one," he said. "The first one had a man asking a question twice, which is a thing a man does when he has been told no. The second one has a man asking a question once, which is a thing a man does when he has been told to ask it, and I would rather we found that out from the paper than from the gate."

Marek said that a question which has been passed to somebody else is still the council's question and is still answerable, and that the count of questions this holding has asked anybody in the line of asking a body of households for a figure is nil and is nil, and that a letter in a different hand is not a fourth request and not a fifty-fourth of anything.

Nobody thanked anybody, and the woman who keeps the seed house was not thanked for what she said about the six wrong answers either, and the man of about fifty wrote nothing down about that, and about four people in that room agreed with her and about four heard something else in it.

Nobody has been told to take the offer off the low board and it is still face up on it at the far end of that room, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month and has not been withdrawn, and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name. The register form is on the middle table where it has stood fifty-eight days, not filled and not refused, and the sheet with the name and the day and the hour against it is in the second book on a page of its own, and entering it did not make it a column.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and an answer is not a use, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and two letters from one clerk in six days are two letters and not two fifty-fours.

A bridge was run once for about nine minutes five weeks ago and no person in this holding has an instrument that says whether it is on or off. The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and no person in this holding has ever put a foot inside it.

The mark is where it has been: four inches along the inside of the left forearm, branching twice, the same four inches as at the second hour. An answer is not a mark, seven answers is not a mark, and a column with nothing in it and no heading on it is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the fourteenth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS NOT WORKED, THERE BEING NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN, AND THE FIELDS THERE HAVE WAITED FOR THE FIFTY-FIRST MORNING. THE ROTA STANDS AT EIGHTY MORNINGS IN FORCE WITH TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND FIFTY-ONE NOT, AND TWENTY-NINE TAKEN DID NOT MOVE. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED EIGHT HUNDRED AND FORTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOURTH FIGURE, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE WINDOW IS A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-ONE, BEING SEVENTY-ONE RISES AND SIXTY FALLS, AND THE TWO ADD. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-TWO DAYS, THE SENTENCE WRITTEN FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIRST MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE, AND IT IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE LAUNDERS. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD THIS MORNING AND NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN GOES ON THE PAGE, THE SIX BEING ON THE BOUNDARY DITCH FROM THE SECOND HOUR TO THE FIFTH. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT DAYS AND FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIVE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. A CLERK OF THE FIRST COUNCIL WROTE A SECOND TIME IN THE AFTERNOON ASKING WHO SPEAKS FOR THE ELEVEN ACRES OF STANDING WILLOW AT THE NODE. A SEVENTH ANSWER WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THIS ROOM AND ENTERED IN FULL AND READ BACK, AND IT WAS THE SEVENTH OF THE SEVEN, AND NONE OF THE SEVEN IS AN ANSWER, AND THERE IS NO EIGHTH. THE WORDS SPEAKS FOR THE ELEVEN ACRES ARE ENTERED WITH NOTHING AGAINST THEM AND NO HEADING ON THEM, BECAUSE NOBODY IN THIS HOLDING HAS EVER WRITTEN A HEADING AT THE TOP OF ONE OF THOSE COLUMNS. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THAT THE ANSWER IS NO AND THAT THERE IS NO PERSON AND NO BODY THAT THE STANDING WOOD ANSWERS TO, AND THAT THE REASON IS THAT NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED TO BE THE ONE, AND HE SAID THAT THE SENTENCE ABOUT A LIMIT OF AN OBJECT HAS BEEN SAID ONCE IN THIS BUILDING AND IS NOT GOING TO BE SAID AGAIN HERE. THE TWO LETTERS ARE TWO LETTERS AND NOT TWO FIFTY-FOURS, AND THE REQUESTS STAND AT FIFTY-THREE AND THE SECTION-NINE NOTES STAND AT FIFTY-THREE, AND THE TWO HAVE NEVER BEEN ADDED. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

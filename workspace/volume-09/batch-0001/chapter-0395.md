# Chapter 395

## The Rule, Stated By A Clerk

The eighth of this month was a Friday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The two gates and the about six feet of wall between them took four people an hour and a quarter, and the four of them were still on that six feet of wall at the seventh hour and were still on it at about the eighth.

The second of the two gates has a foot of wall out of plumb behind the post, and the four of them put a bar and a wedge against it and write the date on the inside of the frame in chalk. There are nine dates on the inside of that frame and none of them has been rubbed out and nobody has counted them and nobody is going to.

The toe at the second of the three places on that line is up a finger's width off what it was on the third of last month and has been for a month, and the man at the front of it said that a toe which has been up a month is a toe that has stopped being a job and become a fact. The three behind him did not answer him, and he did not go back to it, and it is still up.

Nothing went into the book for the eighth until the four were off the wall, which was after the eighth hour, and the clerk read the figure back out of the other book and the two were the same.

**Eight hundred and twenty-eight hundredweight.**

It is up nine off Thursday, and it is the hundred and fifty-eighth figure of a series that has not put the same figure on the page twice since the morning it began. The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and twenty-five, and the rise went onto the rises, which puts it at sixty-eight rises and fifty-seven falls, and sixty-eight and fifty-seven are a hundred and twenty-five.

The four bodies of households have gone a hundred and fifty-six days without a move in their aggregate, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fifty-fifth of those mornings out of a hundred and fifty-seven, and the four launders are a different figure, and about four people in this holding would merge the two if anybody let them and nobody has let them in four years.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud this morning and this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page. The four on the wall went on past the seventh hour and Marek was one of the four, and a man does not read a pair out loud between the second of those and the second hour of the afternoon, and nobody has ever expected him to.

The pair stands at **five hundred and forty-two days** and **five hundred and eighty-nine**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was shut.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the second hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and the sheet with the forty-one marks on it was still on the long table at the wall side where it has been since yesterday afternoon.

The man of about fifty had it in front of him and a clean page beside it, and he was going to write on the clean page, and Marek asked him to stop for a minute, and gave the reason before he asked.

"I am going to ask a man who keeps a book to stop writing for about a minute, and I am going to give the reason before I ask, and the reason is that four of us are about to do the same thing wrong in four different ways and one of us should say so while there is still time to say it."

"What we have on that table is forty-one marks, and a document nobody has stood behind, and a date a clerk in another county had to guess, and a corridor a man has named out loud in a room, and a man who has put his own name down against a page with a hole in it. And in about four years one of those five is going to be put in a book as though it were the same kind of thing as another of the five, and I do not know which pair, and neither does anybody in this room, and that is the whole of what I have come in here to say."

The man of about fifty put the pen down. He said that he was not going to be asked twice, and Marek said he was not going to ask again, and the two of them have been in this building long enough for that to be a real sentence.

The man of about fifty then wrote out the rule, because he was the one who had to write things down and because a rule nobody has written is a rule that gets forgotten by the third of next month.

"A defect is not a question," he said. "A document is not a question. A corridor being named is not an answer. A heading is not an answer. A mark is not a name, and a name is not a witness. Those are five sentences and they are about five different things, and I have watched four years of this holding confuse three of them, and I am not going to watch a fourth year of it."

He wrote them at the top of a clean page and put the pen down again, and then he did something he had not done in four years, which was to count what he had. He went through the second book with his thumb from the back and came to the front and put his thumb on a page and left it there.

"Thirty-five," he said. "Thirty-five open questions in the history of this holding, at four places in this book, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and eleven, and it is mine and of nobody else's, and it has been thirty-five since the first of them and it is thirty-five this afternoon and it is going to be thirty-five on the last morning of whatever this is."

Nobody argued with him about the number, because about four people in that room had gone through that book in the last fortnight and had got the same figure, and about four had not looked at it since the spring, and the four that had not looked at it did not say so.

Marek said one more thing and it was about the woman who keeps the seed house, who was in the room, and what he said was that a rule is worth nothing on the page if the people it applies to have never heard it said out loud.

"Ask her," he said. "Not the woman of about thirty-eight, who has the key and a right to her afternoon. The woman who keeps the seed house, who has four accounts of her own and none of them in this book. Ask her whether a rule that has only ever been written down has ever once stopped her doing a thing she meant to do."

The man of about fifty asked her, and it took him a moment to find the words for it, and she answered before he found them.

"It stopped me twice," she said, "and both times it stopped me by being written down where I could see it, and neither time was a book in this building. It is not the writing that does it. A thing in a drawer stops nobody. A thing in your own hand on the wall you have to walk past stops you every time you walk past it, and that is the whole of what a rule is and it is not in any of the four books here."

She said it without stopping for a reason first, and nobody supplied one for her, and the count of that in the holding's history did not move because it does not move on a page where the reason is not given.

The man of about fifty wrote that down too, and the woman who keeps the seed house looked at it and said that if he wrote down the number of times a person in this building has said a thing to her and not been thanked, it would be a different number every week and it would not be worth a column, and he said that it was worth a column anyway and that this building had once had six columns with nothing at the head of them and had never had one that was not worth filling.

Marek was about to say something else and the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden came in from the yard and did not sit down, and what she said was about the cut at the kerb, and it was that nothing goes into the two-foot gap this season and the key is not used and nobody is going to ask her about either.

Nobody asked her. The count of questions the reader of this body has asked that woman is nil and is nil, and it did not move this afternoon, and the two-foot gap is at the kerb and it has been open since the eleventh of a month now five months back, and the valve is not opened and the key is in her hand.

She said she had come for the seed house and not for the room, and the woman who keeps the seed house said there was bread, and the two of them went out.

The man of about fifty put the five sentences at the top of a clean page and did not number them, and Marek asked him why not, and he said that numbering a rule makes it a list and this holding has a list problem, and that a rule you can point at is worth more than a rule you can cite.

Tova Reed had gone to the door by then and came back for her bag and stood behind the last three of them while the page was being read out, and she said one thing from the doorway, and she gave the reason before she said it, and the count of that in the holding's history is twenty-nine.

"I am going to say the one thing I have not said today and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that four of those five sentences are about what a piece of paper cannot do, and there is nobody in this room who has written down what a piece of paper can do, and that is the half of the morning nobody has looked at."

"A page with a mark on it says a person was near it. A page with a name on it says a person signed it. A page with a figure on it says somebody weighed something, and not who. That is three things a page does and it does them all in a hand a clerk can read, and about four years of this holding has been arguing with the fourth thing it does, which is nothing, and nothing is the one it is best at."

Nobody improved it, and about four people in that room wrote it down in their own heads and about four did not, and the man of about fifty did not enter it because it was said from a doorway on the way out and it was not in the room when the five sentences were agreed.

The man of about seventy-four with a stick was still in the door when the page was read and he said the only thing he has said in that doorway in four years, and he said it to the wall and not to anybody.

"Read it again tomorrow," he said. "Not the numbers. The five. A thing read out once is a thing one man said, and a thing read out two mornings running is a thing a room has, and that is the only way I have ever seen one of you get anything done."

The woman who keeps the seed house said there would be no bread by the time he said it and that there was, and the man of about fifty wrote neither sentence down.

Nobody thanked anybody, and this afternoon was a room with a door shut on it and there has not been a yard in this holding since the thirteenth of last month, so neither of the two yard counts moved and there was nothing today for either of them to move in.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres of standing willow is bare in its eleventh year and was not walked this afternoon and not measured and not crossed and not priced by walking it and not explained, and it has no dimension on any page, and the work in that wood is done outside it because of the light off the west end and for no other reason.

The offer sheet is still on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and still not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month, and it has not been withdrawn, and it carries no date and no name and no line for a name, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board. The register form is on the middle table where it has stood fifty-two days, not filled and not refused, and nobody wrote a heading on it and nobody is going to.

The mark on the arm is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning. A rule is not a mark, a heading is not a mark, a clean page is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the eighth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE SECOND LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE TWO GATES AND SIX FEET OF WALL BETWEEN THEM, FOUR PEOPLE TO THE EIGHTH HOUR, AND THE FIGURE WAS NOT ENTERED UNTIL AFTER IT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED EIGHT HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHTH FIGURE, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE WINDOW IS A HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE, BEING SIXTY-EIGHT RISES AND FIFTY-SEVEN FALLS, AND THE TWO ADD. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX DAYS, THE SENTENCE WRITTEN FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SEVEN, AND IT IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE LAUNDERS. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD THIS MORNING AND NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN GOES ON THE PAGE, THE FOUR ON THE WALL HAVING GONE ON PAST THE SEVENTH HOUR. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO DAYS AND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS SHUT. A CLEAN PAGE WAS WRITTEN ON IN THE ROOM AND FIVE SENTENCES WERE PUT AT THE TOP OF IT, BEING THAT A DEFECT IS NOT A QUESTION, THAT A DOCUMENT IS NOT A QUESTION, THAT A CORRIDOR BEING NAMED IS NOT AN ANSWER, THAT A HEADING IS NOT AN ANSWER, AND THAT A MARK IS NOT A NAME AND A NAME IS NOT A WITNESS. THE COUNT OF OPEN QUESTIONS IN THE HISTORY OF THIS HOLDING IS THIRTY-FIVE AND IS ENTERED AS THIRTY-FIVE, AND IT IS NOT A COUNT OF ANYTHING DONE THIS AFTERNOON. THE READER OF THIS BODY ASKED THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOK TO STOP WRITING, AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST, AND THE REASON WAS THAT FOUR THINGS ON ONE TABLE ARE ABOUT TO BE PUT IN A BOOK AS THOUGH THEY WERE THE SAME KIND OF THING. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF MARDEN CAME IN FROM THE YARD AND SAID THAT NOTHING GOES INTO THE TWO-FOOT GAP AT THE KERB THIS SEASON AND THAT THE KEY IS NOT USED, AND NOBODY ASKED HER ANYTHING, AND THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS ASKED HER IS NIL AND IS NIL. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

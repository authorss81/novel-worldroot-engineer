# Chapter 393

## The Window And The Column

The sixth of this month was a Wednesday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and was not worked, because there has been nobody at the headland above Marden to work it since the second week of a month now three months back. The fields on that headland have waited for the forty-ninth morning.

The rota stands at seventy-eight mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and forty-nine not, and twenty-nine and forty-nine are seventy-eight. A field that waited is a fact about a rota and not a fact about a man, and nobody in this holding has been blamed for one in four years and nobody is going to be this morning.

The count is kept two ways and the two ways do not agree, and this page prints both of them. It is the twenty-sixth morning of the run that began on the twenty-sixth of a month four months back. It is the thirteenth morning since the eighteenth of a month two months back, and the frame on that second one moves when the month turns, and the change is said once and not twice.

The rest of the morning went where the morning goes. The near culvert and nine feet of the ditch above it took six people until about the fourth hour, and the far culvert was a third full and has been a third full for a fortnight, and the six of them put one barrow-load out of it and stopped there. A thing that is going down by itself is a thing a holding does not go into twice in a week.

The frost was on the low ground at the second hour and four of the six broke it with their spades, and Marek was one of those four and could not have told a five from a two if somebody had asked him at the time. He has said before that this is not a confession and it is a fact, and the fact is that a man who has been in a ditch since the second hour is not a man anybody should ask to carry a number in his head.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow, turned it at the top of the yard and went home. The second of the four lines in the book he carries is forty-four days old this morning. There is nothing on it and no place on it for anything to go, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in forty-four days.

The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road and it did not rise this morning.

At about the eighth hour the figure for the sixth went into the book that keeps the third launder and came back out of the other one with the frayed corner, in a voice that does not change for a rise or a fall. The two of them were the same, and the two of them have been the same on every morning of the four years.

**Eight hundred and twenty-four hundredweight.**

It is up nine off the fifth. It is the hundred and fifty-sixth figure of a series that has not put the same figure on the page twice since the first morning of it, and the man of about fifty keeps the whole of that series in a book of his own with nothing else in it, and he went through it before he ruled the margin this morning.

The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and twenty-three. The rise went onto the rises, which puts it at sixty-seven rises and fifty-six falls, and sixty-seven and fifty-six are a hundred and twenty-three, which is a hundred and twenty-three days back from a morning four hundred and eighty-three off.

A hundred and fifty-four days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, and the sentence saying so was written this morning for the hundred and fifty-third of those mornings out of a hundred and fifty-five. It is a sentence about four bodies of households and the four launders are a different figure, and the worst merge anybody in this holding has ever made with a figure is the reason the two are still kept apart.

The two aggregates have never been put on one line of a page in this building in four years, and nobody put them on one this morning. The worst thing anybody has ever done to a figure in this holding was a man who merged those two, four years and a month back, and he corrected it himself and wrote down that he had merged them, which is the only reason the two are still kept apart.

The two day-minus rules were not read out loud this morning and this page carries no figure for the run, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. The second line ran past the seventh hour, and a man does not stand at a table and say two numbers out loud between the end of a ditch and the second hour of the afternoon. The man of about fifty had a day to close.

The pair stands where it stands at **five hundred and forty days** and **five hundred and eighty-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fifth hour of the afternoon with the door shut. The window of the office's work had opened on the sixth, as it opens on the sixth of every month, and it is six days, and it shuts on the eleventh whether anybody has used it or not, and no work has ever been carried out of one in the four years this holding has kept a book.

Nothing was put in it this morning and nothing is going to be. About four people in that room have already begun to treat the six days as a deadline and about four have not said anything about it, and a window is not a deadline and there is nobody in this building who has to do anything inside six days.

Marek put his hand flat on the middle table, the way the woman who keeps the seed house puts her hand flat on a sack. His hand was not on the register form and it was not beside the offer on the low board. He gave the reason before he said it, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and seventy-five, and it is his and of nobody else's.

"The six days are not ours," he said. "That is the whole of what I know about them and it is enough for me to sit down. If a thing has to be inside six days to be worth doing, then what it wants is a season and not six days, and we have not got a season either. I would rather say that in a room than find out in a fortnight what happens to a thing nobody carried."

The woman who keeps the seed house said there was bread in the seed house and that the loaf from four days ago was still good, which was the only thing anybody said for about twenty minutes. She said it because somebody had to, and she is the one who can say it without it meaning anything.

The man of about fifty brought the register form up off the middle table and put it back where it was without turning it, the way a man moves a thing he has moved every morning for fifty days. It arrived on the sixteenth of a month two months back and it has stood on that table since.

It is fifty days on it this morning. It is not filled and it is not refused, and there is nothing at the head of it, and nobody has ever written anything at the head of it. About four people in that room wanted to say something about the head of it. About four did not.

The offer is on the low board at the far end, face up, four feet off the middle table, and it is not filled. It was refused on the nineteenth of last month and it has not been withdrawn and it does not lapse on the day it was made. There is no name on it and no line on it for a name.

Nobody in this building has been told to take it off that board and nobody is going to be told. There is not going to be a second instrument of an offer on any table in this holding this season, and a sheet that a clerk put down at the door at the sixth hour is not that instrument and is not going to become it.

The corridor in the pan is named and open, and open is not a road, and naming it this morning did not make it one. It has not been closed this season and it has not been established as on. The sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular and cannot be put down by anybody in this holding.

A man can name a thing in a room and the naming does not open it. The room heard that sentence and about four of the nine wrote it down in their own heads, and about four will have forgotten it by the third of next month.

A clerk from one of the four bodies of households came up the road at about the sixth hour with a sheet in a fold and left it at the door. He did not come in and nobody asked him to. It is the page that went out of this building unsigned on the ordinary post on Monday morning, being day 604, and a copy of it went to Havergate and Low Wether and Marden and Silling, none of whom are members of the cooperative and none of whom were made members.

The ordinary post goes up that road twice a week whatever anybody in this building is doing.

It came back in the same fold. There is a mark on the outside of it in three or four different hands and about as many again that could not be placed, and the man of about fifty put it on the long table at the wall side and not on the middle table. The middle table has the register form on it, and he did not put one paper near the other, and nobody in that room asked him to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The count of things said out loud in a yard in this holding and got wrong is five and it did not move this morning, and there has not been a yard in this holding since the thirteenth of last month. The count of times a man who keeps a book here has said a thing in a yard the engineer of record had not thought of is thirteen and did not move, and the two of them are not added to one another on any morning of the year and were not added this morning.

The mark on the arm is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning. A window is not a mark, a column is not a mark, a fold of paper is not a mark, and a hand put flat on a table is not a mark, and a day is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled.

The six columns of the well house door nine hundred yards off are headed nothing and have been headed nothing for four years, and the seventh of them stands at fifty-four and is read on the thirtieth and on the first and on no other morning, and a man of about seventy is fetched on the first of a month and is not fetched this month, and the compost line is paid at twenty not discharged in twenty, and neither of those clocks has a heading on it either.

What this holding cannot check is still two figures and what it does not know is still six things, and a window that opened this morning is not a seventh of either. The use log stands at fifteen lines and got no sixteenth, the requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and a sheet that came to a door is not a fifty-fourth of anything.

A bridge was run once for about nine minutes five weeks ago and no person in this building has an instrument that says whether it is on or off, and the man of about fifty wrote nothing about it. There are ten questions available to a room and not taken, and it is ten, and nobody in this building has ever written down what the ten are.

Nobody thanked anybody. The day sheet went into the stack in the order it was used, and a man put his hand flat on a table this afternoon and gave the reason first, and the reason was a fact about six days and not about what he is like, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and seventy-five, and it is his and of nobody else's.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the sixth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS NOT WORKED, THERE BEING NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN, AND THE FIELDS THERE HAVE WAITED FOR THE FORTY-NINTH MORNING. THE ROTA STANDS AT SEVENTY-EIGHT MORNINGS IN FORCE WITH TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND FORTY-NINE NOT. THE THIRD LAUNDER MEASURED EIGHT HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT, THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIXTH FIGURE OF A SERIES THAT HAS NOT REPEATED ONE FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST, AND IT HAS RISEN. THE WINDOW IS A HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE, BEING SIXTY-SEVEN RISES AND FIFTY-SIX FALLS, AND THE TWO ADD. THE AGGREGATE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAS NOT MOVED IN ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR DAYS, THE SENTENCE WRITTEN FOR THE ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THIRD MORNING OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE, AND IT IS NOT THE AGGREGATE OF THE LAUNDERS. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD THIS MORNING AND NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN GOES ON THE PAGE, THE SECOND LINE HAVING RUN PAST THE SEVENTH HOUR. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY DAYS AND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVEN, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE WINDOW OF THE OFFICE'S WORK OPENED ON THE SIXTH AND IS SIX DAYS AND SHUTS ON THE ELEVENTH, AND NOTHING WAS PUT IN IT. A CLERK OF ONE OF THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS LEFT A SHEET AT THE DOOR AT THE SIXTH HOUR AND DID NOT COME IN, AND IT IS THE PAGE THAT WENT OUT UNSIGNED ON MONDAY MORNING, AND IT CAME BACK IN THE SAME FOLD WITH MARKS ON THE OUTSIDE OF IT, AND THE MARKS HAVE NOT BEEN COUNTED AND ARE NOT ENTERED YET. THE REGISTER FORM IS ON THE MIDDLE TABLE AT FIFTY DAYS, NOT FILLED AND NOT REFUSED, AND THE OFFER IS ON THE LOW BOARD FOUR FEET OFF IT, FACE UP, NOT FILLED, REFUSED ON THE NINETEENTH OF LAST MONTH AND NOT WITHDRAWN, AND THE TWO PAPERS WERE NOT PUT SIDE BY SIDE. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND THE REASON WAS A FACT ABOUT SIX DAYS AND NOT ABOUT WHAT HE IS LIKE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

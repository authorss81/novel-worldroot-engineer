# Chapter 400

## Marek, On A Sheet Of His Own

The thirteenth of this month was a Wednesday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The near culvert and the ditch above it took about six people until the fourth hour, and the far one was still about half full and nobody made a second barrow-load out of it, and that has been on and off since the twenty-third of last month.

At the seventh hour the two day-minus rules were read out loud and both numbers were said before either of them, in two voices, and the drawer was not opened. They stand at **five hundred and forty-seven days** and **five hundred and ninety-four**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened. Fifty-two mornings out of eighty-seven since the seventeenth of a month three months back, and it is a habit and not a measurement, and there is no column for it in this book and there never has been.

The figure for the thirteenth was entered at about the eighth hour and read back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and thirty-one hundredweight.**

Down five off Tuesday, and the hundred and sixty-third figure of a series that has not repeated one figure since the morning it started. The window the rises and the falls are counted in stands at a hundred and thirty, and the fall went onto the falls, which puts it at seventy rises and sixty falls, and seventy and sixty are a hundred and thirty.

A hundred and sixty-one days without a move in the aggregate of the four bodies of households, the sentence written this morning for the hundred and sixtieth of those mornings out of a hundred and sixty-two. Four bodies of households are what that figure is about. The four launders are a different figure, the two have not been added in four years, and nobody put them on one line this morning.

The man of about thirty-one of Silling came down the four-mile road at about the ninth hour with the barrow and turned it at the top of the yard and went home, and the second of the four lines in the book he carries is fifty-one days old and has nothing on it and no place on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in fifty-one days. The barrow stands at eleven journeys up that road and did not rise this morning.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and there was a letter on the long table at the wall side that had come up the road with the morning post, and it was from the clerk of the first council and it was a well-written letter and it had a line ruled across the bottom of it.

The letter asked one question. It asked who is answerable for a node between now and the next taking, and it did not say the council would do the work, and it did not ask anybody to do anything, and at the foot of the page there was a ruled line and the words FOR THE NAME OF THE PERSON ANSWERABLE printed above it in the same hand as everything else.

The letter had been written by a man who had been a clerk for eleven years and had the hand of one, and it was four paragraphs long and the fourth one thanked this holding in advance, which is a thing a well-written letter does and which Marek said afterwards he would have preferred it not to have done.

The first paragraph named the council and the county and the date of its meeting. The second set out the motion, which was that the district be disconnected from the schedule of the maintenance of the Crown Engine at the next taking of any branch at any place within it. The third was the part that mattered.

The third paragraph said that the council had asked in writing what work stands between this morning and the next taking, and in what order it must be done, and who is answerable for each of those things, and that the reply it had received stated that no such list is kept, and that the council had read that reply twice and had understood it, and that the council was therefore asking the question a second time and this time to a person.

The man of about fifty read it twice and then asked the room a question that is not a question any of the nine of them could answer.

"If I answer that, what goes in the column?"

About four people in that room said that the column was not a column, it was a line on a letter, and about four said nothing, and the man of about fifty said that was a true answer to a different question.

"The column is not a column," he said. "It is a line on a letter in a county nine miles off, and whatever goes in it goes to the office, and the office enters it in the register of the office. That is what a line under a question is. It is a place where a name goes to be copied by somebody who does not know what the name is for."

The woman who keeps the seed house said the one thing that had been in her mouth since the letter came, and she said it without a reason first, which is a thing she is allowed to do and does about four times a year.

"You could write nothing," she said. "You could send it back with the line empty and no sheet at all, and that is an answer a body can read, and a body that has been told nothing twice can then go and be a body that asked and got nothing."

"That is true," Marek said, "and it is not what I am going to do, and I would rather the room knew both of those before this afternoon than after it."

He was somewhere on the four-mile road at the time and nobody knew it, and about four people in this building have spent four years not asking him a question they could have asked him on a Tuesday morning, and nobody in this holding is going to put an answer on that page this season.

Marek gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and eighty-two, and it is his and of nobody else's, and nobody thanked him and he did not want to be.

"I am going to put my name on a sheet of my own and I am going to give the reason before I put it there, and the reason is that a sheet with a person's name on it and a day and an hour against it is a different kind of object from a sentence in a letter, and four counties have now spent a week being handed sentences, and one of the five things we wrote on the clean page on Friday is that a document is not a question."

"It is not that I am brave about it. It is that a letter with a line at the foot of it has to be answered by a person or not answered at all, and a holding that has kept four books for four years without a heading on one column of a door is in no position to say to a council that nobody is answerable, because that would be the first sentence anybody in this building has ever written that we did not know to be true on the morning we wrote it."

> ANSWERABLE FOR A NODE BETWEEN THIS MORNING AND THE NEXT TAKING OF ANY BRANCH AT ANY PLACE WITHIN IT: MAREK VALE, ENGINEER OF RECORD, LANTERN REACH WORK COOPERATIVE, AT THIS HOLDING, ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, IN THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON, WITH THE DOOR SHUT AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN THE ROOM, AND THE REASON IS GIVEN ON THE SHEET BELOW AND IS A FACT ABOUT FOUR YEARS OF A COLUMN WITH NO HEADING ON IT AND NOT A FACT ABOUT THE PERSON NAMED.

**"I am going to write the reason under my own name and I am going to give the reason for writing the reason first, and the reason for writing it is that a name against a duty with nothing under it is a worse thing than an empty line, and about four people in this room have been waiting four years for somebody to put a name on anything at all, and the four of them are not the four who are going to be disappointed.**"

**"So the reason is this. In four years this building has answered for a wall, a ditch, a culvert, a gate, a sluice, a dial, a form, an offer, a corridor and a bridge, and there is not one heading at the top of any of them, and there is a sixth column on a door nine hundred yards off that has stood headed nothing since before I came, and a sheet of terms with a third column ruled and empty on the table in front of you, and a form in a drawer four miles west with one figure on it and three lines empty.**"

**"A heading is necessary and it is not sufficient. Both halves of that are four years old and neither half is new today. What I am putting my name under is not a heading. It is a man's name against a piece of work that nobody in four counties has agreed is his, and I am doing it because the alternative is a column that stays empty for a fifth year and an office that fills it in with a word.**"

Nobody said he was sorry. Nobody said it was the right thing to do and nobody said it was a mistake, and nobody in that room softened a word of it, and about four people had worked out inside a minute that he was not going to be thanked and about four had not.

The woman who keeps the seed house said the one thing that had been in her mouth for an hour, and she said it without a reason first, which is a thing she is allowed to do and does about four times a year.

"You have put your name on it," she said, "and I want to say that a man who does that in a room with the door shut is not doing it for the room. You are doing it so that a clerk in a county has a name to write into a register, and I have watched a lot of men in four years and I have watched exactly one of them do that and he is not a good man and he has never once said he was sorry for anything."

Nobody said that was fair and nobody said that it was not, and Marek did not say that it was fair, and the woman who keeps the seed house went back to the seed house, and the bread she had mentioned at the fourth hour went uneaten to about the eighth.

The man of about fifty wrote the sheet into the second book on a page of its own, word for word, including the reason, and he read it back out loud in the room afterward, which is the practice of this holding and not of his office, and he did not improve it and did not add a line.

He read it and then he sat down, which he does not do, and the man of about seventy-four with a stick, who had been standing in the door for the whole of it, said that a man who has put his name to a thing in a room ought to be told what the room thinks of it, and that nobody had said anything, and the man of about fifty said that nobody had because there was nothing to say that would be true.

The room thought two things and neither of them was said. One of them was that a name on a sheet of this kind is the cheapest thing a man in this building can do and the most expensive thing a county can read, and the other of them was that the man who wrote the letter had asked it a second time and to a person, which means a body of eleven people had decided the first answer was not enough, and neither of those two thoughts was going to be written down by anybody.

The letter went back up the road on the ordinary post the next morning with the ruled line at the foot of it empty, and a separate sheet went with it in the same fold, and the two were not pinned together and were not referred to one another on either of them. The ordinary post goes up that road twice a week whatever anybody in this building is doing, and it went on the Thursday with a barrow on it behind the postman and nobody sent a hand with it.

Tova Reed was in the room for the last hour of it and said nothing about the name and nothing about the reason, and about four people in that room expected her to say something about it and about four did not. She said afterwards, to nobody, that a thing done in a room with the door shut does not want a woman to look at it, and that was the only thing she said about it, and nobody asked her what she meant.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month and it has not been withdrawn, and it has no date on it and no name on it and no line on it for a name. It is four feet off the middle table, and the middle table has the register form on it standing fifty-seven days, not filled, not refused, and nobody wrote a heading on it and nobody is going to.

The man of about fifty entered the sheet on a page of its own and did not number it, and the page before it is blank and the page after it is blank, and he said that the blanks were on purpose and that a page with a thing written on it and nothing either side of it is a thing a person can find again, and that a book where everything runs on is a book where a man cannot tell which of four things in a fortnight was the one that mattered.

Marek asked him one question and it was what the page before it was, and the man of about fifty said that it was the seventeenth of this month and that nothing happened on the seventeenth of this month worth writing down, and that a book with a page for every morning in four years has about nine hundred blank pages in it and that this is the first time anybody has ever said so in a room.

Nobody thanked anybody, and the woman who keeps the seed house was not thanked for what she said either, and the bread went uneaten to about the eighth hour, and nobody in that room said a word about the bread.

The use log stands at fifteen lines and a name is not a use, and the reason for the fifteen is not given in this holding in a season. The requests stand at fifty-three and the section-nine notes stand at fifty-three, and a letter that has a name on a separate sheet is a request and not a fifty-fourth of anything.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-four and is not read this morning. The man of about seventy is not fetched and the count stays at twenty-three. The compost line is paid at twenty not discharged in twenty. What this holding cannot check is two figures and what it does not know is six things, and a name is not a number and a duty is not a seventh of anything.

The corridor in the pan is named and open and is not a road, and the sheet that claims it is in a drawer face up and is correct in every particular, and the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page.

The mark on the arm is four inches along the inside of the left forearm and it branches twice, and it is the same four inches it was at the second hour of this morning. A name is not a mark, a duty is not a mark, and a heading is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm stand at four and are unreconciled, and a sheet with a man's name and a day and an hour against it is not a fifth account of anything.

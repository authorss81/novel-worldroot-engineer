# Chapter 414

## A Question In A County's Hand

The twenty-seventh of this month was a Wednesday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The north end of the low drain, where it takes the water off the fen side and turns it into the cut, took four people from the second hour to the sixth hour, and there was a white frost in the shade of the bank that had not been there on Tuesday and that was gone off the open ground by the fourth hour.

Marek had the bar and then the shovel, and the ground at the north end was frozen hard enough to take the shoe and soft enough under it to move, which is the condition that makes a drain work and the condition that makes a man's back not.

He had been out at the node before the light and had read the dial standing in it the way he reads it, which is with his hand flat on the stone and his face turned away from the numbers, and the reading was the same as the one on Monday and he wrote nothing down about it, because a figure that has not moved is not a figure anybody in this building has ever entered a second time.

About a quarter mile below the north end the ditch takes the water off the west bound and turns it, and the turn is the whole of the west end's drainage and it is a piece of ground that four counties have argued about for eleven years without once going and standing in it.

The woman of about thirty-eight of Marden was at that turn at the fourth hour with a chain and a bar and no coat on, doing the thing she does twice a week in a season, and she and Marek had about nine words with each other and both of them were about the frost.

The seventh hour came and the two of them read the boards, and the boards were read before either figure was said, which is the order. **Five hundred and sixty-one days** and **six hundred and eight**, forty-seven between them. The drawer stayed shut.

Fifty-nine mornings out of a hundred and one, counted from the seventeenth of a month three months back. The frame on the boards themselves is of a month three months back and it is said here once and it is not going to be said again this month.

The twenty-seventh went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and fifty-nine hundredweight.**

Down five off Tuesday, and the hundred and seventy-seventh figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The fall went onto the falls. Seventy-seven and sixty-seven make a hundred and forty-four, and that is the window and not the boards and not the launder.

A hundred and seventy-five days is how long the four bodies of households have gone without a move in their aggregate, and the sentence for it on this morning's sheet is the hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings out of a hundred and seventy-six.

A man came nine miles up the fen road with a folded sheet inside his coat and a saddle that had been wet since the ford. He said how far he had come before anybody thought to ask, and nobody did ask, and the woman who keeps the seed house gave him bread on the step, which is a thing she does and has never written down.

> FROM THE FIRST REGIONAL COUNCIL, DATED THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THIS MONTH, COPIED FROM THE SHEET THE ASSEMBLY AT THORNWILD SENT TO THIS BODY ON THE SAME DAY, AND PUT TO THIS HOLDING IN THE COUNCIL'S OWN HAND AND NOT IN THE ASSEMBLY'S. FIRST, THAT THE COUNCIL HAS CIRCULATED THE MOTION AMONG THE HOUSEHOLDS IT SERVES AND RECORDS THAT SIX OF THE ELEVEN HOUSEHOLDS AT THE WEST END HAVE HAD IT READ TO THEM. SECOND, THAT THE COUNCIL ASKS THIS HOLDING WHETHER THE TAKING OF THE HEARTHROOT AT THE NODE HAS ANY EFFECT UPON THE DRAIN THAT SERVES THE WEST END, AND IF IT HAS, IN WHICH DIRECTION, AND OVER WHAT LENGTH OF TIME. THIRD, THAT THE COUNCIL STATES IT IS NOT ASKING WHETHER THE ASSEMBLY HAS A RIGHT, WHICH IT HAS NOT DECIDED, AND IS ASKING A QUESTION ABOUT WATER. FOURTH, THAT NO ANSWER IS TO BE SENT ON THE ORDINARY POST AND THAT A CARRIER MAY BE SENT IF THE COUNCIL SENDS ONE.

The clerk of this holding read all four of those aloud twice, and the fourth one was the reason the sheet had come by a rider at ten in the morning instead of on Monday with the two others.

There are eleven households at the west end and the council's own sheet says six of them have had the motion read to them, and a figure like that is on a page in nine miles and is not on a page here, and this holding has not asked to see the other five and is not going to.

Nobody in this building has ever been to the west end of that drain. It is nine miles and a cart road and it belongs to a body that this holding is not a member of and was never made a member of, and neither fact has moved since the second council was formed.

The Assembly's four people were on the road for the second of their four days, and where they were on that day is not on any page in this building and was not asked about, and they were on it in the ordinary way, with sacks and a lantern and nothing else that anybody here has been told about.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour, was turned at the top of the yard, and stood in the yard a while. The man of about thirty-one of Silling brings it and goes home. The second of four lines in a book he carries is sixty-five days old and unanswered, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in sixty-five days. Eleven journeys up that road is where the barrow stands and eleven is a floor. The woman who keeps the seed house watched him stop and has never mentioned it to anybody here.

The answer is a thing the man who reads the ground could give in about four minutes, standing at the cut, with the frost gone off it, and Marek had been at that north end himself at the second hour and had put his own shovel in the mud of it.

He gave the reason first for the not giving of it, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and ninety-five, and it is his and of nobody else's.

"I can answer the second clause. I want to say that first, because I do not want anybody in this building to go about for a week believing that the engineer of record cannot answer it." "I am going to say why I am not going to write it out for them anyway, and I am going to give the reason for that first, and the reason is that on the thirteenth of this month I put my name at the head of a column in a room in this building, and I did it with the reason given first, and the reason I gave then was that four books with no heading on any of their columns cannot hold a duty against a man they will not name. That is still true this morning. It is true in a way this letter makes worse." "The second clause asks a county about water. The answer is a county's. If this holding writes it down in a hand and sends it nine miles, then in about four years there is a page in one of these four books with a sentence on it and my name under it, and there is no column anywhere in this building that says what that name is answerable for, and I have been to the node four years on a Tuesday and I have never once been asked what I am answerable for, and I am not going to begin by answering for a county's drainage because a clerk nine miles off found the right word in a hurry."

The man of about fifty wrote the whole of that down and read it back and did not improve it, and he wrote it into the day sheet and not into the second book, because a letter that has been answered is a request and a request stands in its own column.

The carrier the council had offered was not sent, and the man who would have taken the four miles in the afternoon was doing the second line of the boundary and did not know there was an offer, and nobody told him, and that was the clerk's decision and the clerk made it before anybody voted on it.

Nobody thanked him for it, and the sheet went on the long table at the wall side where it stays, and nobody put it beside any of the other papers.

There is a version of this morning in which he wrote four lines and sent them by the carrier the council offered, and the four lines would have been correct, and about four people in this holding have thought about that version at some point in the last twenty minutes and about four have not, and neither of the two fours is going to be asked which it was.

The trouble with the four lines is not that they would have been wrong. Every one of them would have been right, and the man of about fifty has said that out loud twice this month, and the trouble is that a right answer sent nine miles in a hand comes back nine miles in a fortnight as a thing a building stands behind, and this holding has four books and no heading on any column of any of them, and a thing a building stands behind is a duty, and a duty with nobody's name at the head of it is the thing four years of this building have been walking toward and have not arrived at.

Then the clerk of this holding said the thing, and he said it in his own mouth because it is his office and not anybody else's in that room, and the man of about fifty did not say it for him.

"That is a county's question," the clerk said. "I have had three of these this month and I am going to say the same thing about each of them and I am going to say it in the same words, so that anybody writing to this building in about four years can find the sentence and not a feeling." "A county asked it. The answer, if there is one, is a county's. The moment this building puts it in a letter, it stops being the county's and becomes ours, and there is no column in these four books for what this building is answerable for, and there has not been one in four years, and I have not been asked to invent one and I am not going to." "So it goes on the long table, and it does not go in a book, and the man who reads the ground has told this room in front of me that he could answer it in four minutes standing at the cut. I am entering that he said so. I am not entering the answer, and if anybody in four counties wants it they are going to have to write to a body of households and not to a building that keeps four books and no headings on any of them."

The reason came before the thing and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and twenty-two, and the clerk of this holding is not a name that goes in the shared sentence and nobody put one there.

**"A clerk can put a figure in a column and a person cannot put a name in one,"** the man of about fifty said, **"and that has been the whole difference for four years, and the fourth county got an answer out of this building in a fortnight and the only reason it got one is that it asked in the word of a column and not in the word of a man. I am not going to improve that and I am not going to explain it twice this month."**

About four people in that room had heard him say it on the twenty-third and about four had not, and neither of the two fours is going to be asked, and about four people in four counties have read that sentence in a letter and about four have not read it at all.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month, and it has not been withdrawn, and it went out of this building on Tuesday inside another body's document for the first time in four years and it is still on the board, and about four people in this holding have noticed that and about four have not.

The register form is on the middle table at seventy-one days, not filled and not refused, with nothing at the head of it.

The man of about seventy was not sent for. Twenty-three is where that count stands and the twenty-fourth is four mornings off, on the first of a month. The compost line is paid and its run is twenty not discharged in twenty.

On the door of the well house the seventh column is at fifty-four and gets read on the thirtieth and on the first and on no other morning of the year. It was not read this morning, and the six columns beside it are still headed nothing.

Eleven acres of standing willow contain a ring of bare ground in its eleventh year. It has no figure on any page, nobody has walked or measured or crossed or priced or explained it, and no one from this holding has been inside it in four years.

Fifteen lines are written in the use log and a letter brought by hand does not go in it, and the reason the log stands at fifteen has been given once and is not given again in a season. Fifty-three requests and fifty-three notes under section nine, and an unanswered letter is a request, which is one of the fifty-three and not a fifty-fourth.

That arm carries a mark four inches long which branches twice. Four accounts of it are kept here and none of the four reconciles with another. A sheet left unanswered is not a mark, a county refused an answer is not a mark, and nothing said in that room made a fifth.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-seventh, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE NORTH END OF THE LOW DRAIN, FOUR PEOPLE TO THE SIXTH HOUR. THE BOARDS WERE READ ALOUD AT THE SEVENTH HOUR AND BEFORE EITHER NUMBER, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE FOUR PERSONS OF THE ASSEMBLY ARE ON THE SECOND DAY OF FOUR ON THE ROAD AND WHERE THEY ARE ON IT IS NOT ON ANY PAGE IN THIS BUILDING AND WAS NOT ASKED ABOUT. A SHEET CAME UP THE FEN ROAD FROM THE FIRST REGIONAL COUNCIL, DATED THE TWENTY-SIXTH, IN THE COUNCIL'S OWN HAND AND NOT THE ASSEMBLY'S, ASKING IN ITS SECOND CLAUSE WHETHER THE TAKING OF THE HEARTHROOT AT THE NODE HAS ANY EFFECT UPON THE DRAIN THAT SERVES THE WEST END, AND IN WHICH DIRECTION, AND OVER WHAT LENGTH OF TIME, AND RECORDING THAT IT IS NOT ASKING WHETHER THE ASSEMBLY HAS A RIGHT, WHICH THE COUNCIL HAS NOT DECIDED. THE READER OF THIS BODY SAID HE COULD ANSWER IT IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES STANDING AT THE CUT AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST FOR NOT WRITING IT OUT, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIVE. THE CLERK OF THIS HOLDING SAID IN HIS OWN MOUTH THAT IT IS A COUNTY'S QUESTION, THAT THE ANSWER IF THERE IS ONE IS A COUNTY'S, THAT IT GOES ON THE LONG TABLE AND NOT IN A BOOK, AND THAT THE FACT THAT THE ENGINEER OF RECORD SAID HE COULD ANSWER IT IS ENTERED AND THE ANSWER IS NOT. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID THAT A CLERK CAN PUT A FIGURE IN A COLUMN AND A PERSON CANNOT PUT A NAME IN ONE, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO. A CARRIER WAS NOT SENT. THE SHEET STANDS ON THE LONG TABLE AT THE WALL SIDE. THE REQUESTS STAND AT FIFTY-THREE AND THE SECTION-NINE NOTES AT FIFTY-THREE.**

# Chapter 413

## The Fourteenth Question

The twenty-sixth of this month was a Tuesday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the fifty-fourth morning.

The rota stands at eighty-three mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-four not, and twenty-nine and fifty-four are eighty-three.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken did not move this morning and has not moved on any morning of this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-first morning of the run out of the twenty-sixth of a month four months back, and the eighteenth morning of the run anchored on the eighteenth of a month two months back. The frame on that second one moves when the month turns, and it will move on the first of next month, and it is not going to be moved twice.

The six who would have gone to that headland were on the west side of the boundary ditch instead, and Marek was one of the six, and both hands were full from the second hour until after the seventh. The rain came in off the fen about the fourth hour and stayed in, and the clay under the top length had not been walked on since the autumn.

This page carries no figure for the run, because the two boards were not read out loud this morning, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. One of the two men who takes the second board was on the west side of that ditch until after the seventh hour, and this holding has never in four years asked a man to stand at a table and say two figures out loud after four miles of wet clay.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and sixty days** and **six hundred and seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not going to be.

The twenty-sixth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two of them were the same, and that has been the practice of this holding for four years.

**Eight hundred and sixty-four hundredweight.**

Up nine off Monday, and the hundred and seventy-sixth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The rise went onto the rises. Seventy-seven and sixty-six make a hundred and forty-three, and that window is four hundred and eighty-three days wide and has nothing to do with the two boards and is not the launder.

Four bodies of households have gone a hundred and seventy-four days without a move in their aggregate, and the morning sentence is the hundred and seventy-third of those mornings out of a hundred and seventy-five. Those are the four households and not the launders, and nobody in this building has ever added one to the other.

A barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and went back down it empty. It stands at eleven journeys up that road and eleven is a floor. The man who brings it is the man of about thirty-one of Silling and the second of the four lines in his own book is sixty-four days old and has nothing on it, and this building has not asked him about it in sixty-four days.

Soren Rill came in at about the half hour of the afternoon, having gone out to the gate to meet the two riders who carry the copies, and he was wet to the knee and he put the folded sheet on the long table at the wall side and did not put it beside any of the other papers.

> FROM THE ASSEMBLY AT THORNWILD, SENT ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THIS MONTH TO THE FIRST REGIONAL COUNCIL AND TO THE SECOND, BY THE ORDINARY POST AND BY TWO RIDERS, BEING COPIES OF ONE SHEET AND NOT TWO COPIES OF TWO. THE ASSEMBLY SENDS THE MOTION CARRIED ON THE EIGHTEENTH AND READS IT AGAIN BEFORE SENDING IT, AND ASKS EACH COUNCIL TO CIRCULATE IT AMONG THE HOUSEHOLDS THAT BODY SERVES. THE ASSEMBLY SENDS THE FOURTH CLAUSE SEPARATELY FROM THE THREE, BEING THE OPEN OFFER OF A POST AND THE COUNT OF SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR SACKS, AND ASKS THAT THE TWO FIGURES BE READ IN THE SAME ROOM AND BY THE SAME MOUTH. THE FOUR PERSONS OF THE ASSEMBLY LEFT THORNWILD THIS MORNING AND WILL BE AT THE NODE ON THE THIRTIETH, AND THEY WILL BE THERE FOUR DAYS, INCLUDING THE DAY THEY ARRIVE. THE ASSEMBLY ASKS NOBODY TO DO ANYTHING BY THIS SHEET.

Nobody in the room improved a word of it, and the man of about fifty read the third sentence out loud twice because it was the one with two figures in it, and about four people in that room had wanted the two figures in one clause and about four had not, and neither of the two fours has ever been asked which it was.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the room and had read it before anybody else, standing, in about four minutes, and she said one thing about the fourth sentence and gave no reason in front of it, which she is allowed to do about four times a year, and the count of that in the holding's history did not move because it does not move on a page where the reason is not given.

**"They are telling two councils to read a post and a number of sacks out of the same mouth,"** she said, **"and neither of the two has anything to do with a heartroot, and the sheet that says so is four and a half miles behind them and they will not have it in front of them when somebody asks them what they are doing at a node on Saturday. That is all I have got. I have not worked out what it is worth and I am not going to in a room with the door shut."**

The two riders went out of the yard at about the fourth hour, one up the fen road and one toward the second council, and neither was paid for the afternoon and neither was thanked, and both were given bread by the woman who keeps the seed house, which she does and has never entered anywhere.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding from about the second hour of the afternoon with the door shut, and they were all of them in the room before anybody said anything, and the man of about seventy-four with a stick stood in the door for the first half hour of it as he does on most afternoons.

The four people of the Assembly went out of Thornwild at about the sixth hour this morning, and that is four days on the road for them counting the day they arrive, and they are on it in the ordinary way, and no page in this building has an order for them and a plan for them and a reason for them, and nobody in this room was asked where they would sleep.

He did not ask Rill anything while the room was filling. He had a question and he had had it since the Friday before, and he sat with it through the half hour in which the ninth person came in and shut the door behind her.

He gave the reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and ninety-four, and it is his and of nobody else's, and about four people in that room had heard the shape of it before he said the words and about four had not.

"Soren. There is one thing I am going to ask you and I am going to give the reason for asking it before I ask it, and the reason is that in four years this building has never once put a question to a body of people about whether they are the people, and I am not going to do it in a letter either, and there are nine of us in this room and the door is shut and you are one of the nine." "When those four get to the node on Saturday, is anybody going to ask them whether they are the ones who do that work?"

Rill looked at the sheet on the table for a while before he answered, and he did not look at Marek while he thought about it, and he took about as long as the taking of it deserved.

"No," he said. "And I have thought about it on the road and there is no way of putting it into a sheet, and the reason there is no way is that anything that asks a body of people whether they are the people who hold a thing is a request, and a request is a thing that can be refused, and this is not a thing that can be refused, because they have taken it and taking is the whole of what they have done." "You are going to go up there on Saturday morning or you are not, and I do not know which and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to ask."

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute, and about four people in it had wanted to say something and about four had wanted to hear him not say it, and the two fours have never been separated and are not going to be.

"The count of questions the reader of this body has asked me is fourteen," Rill said. "It was thirteen when I came in and it is fourteen now, and I have been asked nothing else this afternoon, and I am not going to be asked anything else by anybody in this room before I go."

He gave the reason before the thing, as he has in that room for four years, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and twenty-one, and it belongs to him alone.

"I am going to say the half of that which is about this building and I am going to give the reason for saying it first, and the reason is that a man has just asked a question in a room that this holding could have asked in a letter in a fortnight, and the letter would have been answered by a clerk in a town who is not the Assembly." "That is not a criticism of the man. It is a fact about a room. There is a difference, and the difference is whether the answer arrives in a voice or in a hand, and about four years of this building is a long time to have gone on with only the hand." "And I am not going to put the count of that on a page where it stands beside a thing he asked, because a count of a kind of talking is not a measure of anything, and he will be asked it again in this building before the end of the year and the figure will go up by one the way the rota goes up by one."

Nobody thanked anybody, and nobody in that room disagreed with the reason, and the day sheet went on the long table at the wall side at about the seventh hour and stayed there until the room emptied.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of that room, face up, and it is not filled, and it was refused on the nineteenth of last month, and it has not been withdrawn, and it carries no date and no name and no line waiting for a name, and it was sent to two councils this morning as the fourth clause of somebody else's sheet, which is the first time in four years that piece of paper has left this building inside another document.

A register form has stood on the middle table for seventy days. It has not been filled and it has not been refused, and there is nothing at the head of it, and nobody in this building has put a word there and nobody is going to.

There is a corridor in the pan. It has a name and it is open and it is not a road, and a sheet that claims it lies in a drawer face up and is true in every particular it states. Beside that sheet is an order sealing a route, valid, and it opens nothing. Nothing this holding owns has put a road back.

The man of about seventy has not been sent for and the twenty-three fetchings stand at twenty-three. The next one falls on the first of a month and that is five mornings off. No page in this building says why a man is brought on the first of a month, and he has never once been asked which number he is on.

The compost line is paid, one hundred pounds, and the run is twenty not discharged in twenty, and the word before the figure has never once been wrong about it. It will not fall this month.

The seventh column on the well house door stands at fifty-four. It is read on the thirtieth and on the first and on nothing else in the year, and it was not read this morning. The six columns beside it are headed nothing and stay that way through this season.

Inside eleven acres of standing willow there is a ring of bare ground. It has been bare eleven years. Nobody has walked it, measured it, crossed it, priced it or explained it, no page in four counties carries a dimension for it, and in four years not one person in this holding has set a foot in it.

The office keeps a window for work. It opens on the sixth of next month, ten mornings from here, it stays open six days, and on every one of the last four of those days there was no work done in it.

The mark on that arm is four inches long and branches twice, and it was four inches at the second hour this morning and it is four inches now. Four accounts of that arm are kept in this building and they do not agree. A question, a rider and a motion are not marks, and nothing asked in a room this afternoon makes a fifth account.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the seventh hour of the evening of the twenty-sixth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WENT AWAY EMPTY, THERE BEING NOBODY AT THE HEADLAND ABOVE MARDEN, AND THOSE FIELDS HAVE WAITED FOR THE FIFTY-FOURTH MORNING. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN, THE SECOND MAN BEING ON THE WEST SIDE OF THE BOUNDARY DITCH UNTIL AFTER THE SEVENTH HOUR, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE FOUR PERSONS OF THE ASSEMBLY AT THORNWILD LEFT THAT SETTLEMENT AT ABOUT THE SIXTH HOUR THIS MORNING AND WILL BE AT THE NODE ON THE THIRTIETH, BEING FOUR DAYS ON THE ROAD, AND THEY ARE ON IT IN THE ORDINARY WAY, AND NO ORDER, PLAN, REASON OR AUTHOR FOR THEM IS ON ANY PAGE IN THIS BUILDING. THE ASSEMBLY SENT ITS MOTION ON THIS MORNING TO THE FIRST COUNCIL AND TO THE SECOND BY THE ORDINARY POST AND BY TWO RIDERS, AND SENT THE FOURTH CLAUSE SEPARATELY FROM THE THREE, AND ASKED THAT THE OPEN OFFER AND THE COUNT OF SACKS BE READ IN THE SAME ROOM AND BY THE SAME MOUTH. THE READER OF THIS BODY ASKED ONE QUESTION OF SOREN RILL AFTER THE ROOM WAS FULL, BEING WHETHER ANYBODY IS GOING TO ASK THE FOUR PEOPLE OF THE ASSEMBLY WHETHER THEY ARE THE PEOPLE WHO DO THAT WORK, AND HE GAVE THE REASON FIRST, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THE HOLDING'S HISTORY IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR. THE ANSWER WAS NO, THE REASON BEING THAT A QUESTION OF THAT SHAPE IS A REQUEST, AND A REQUEST CAN BE REFUSED, AND THIS IS NOT A THING THAT CAN BE REFUSED. THE COUNT OF QUESTIONS THE READER OF THIS BODY HAS ASKED SOREN RILL WENT FROM THIRTEEN TO FOURTEEN ON THAT PAGE AND NOWHERE ELSE. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID THAT A ROOM AND A LETTER ARE NOT THE SAME INSTRUMENT, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE. THE USE LOG STANDS AT FIFTEEN LINES AND A SHEET SENT TO TWO COUNCILS IS NOT A USE AND THE REASON FOR THE FIFTEEN IS NOT GIVEN IN THIS HOLDING IN A SEASON. A SHEET CARRIED OUT INSIDE ANOTHER BODY'S DOCUMENT IS A REQUEST AND NOT A FIFTY-FOURTH OF ANYTHING.**

# Chapter 415

## Four Miles And A Half, And A Chair With Nine In It

The twenty-eighth of this month was a Thursday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The inside face of the west dyke below the old sluice took five people from the second hour to the sixth hour, and the frost had gone off it by the third and the water coming off it was the color of tea and had been the color of tea for a fortnight.

Marek was one of the five and had the bar for the first hour and then went up the track at about the seventh with a coat and a loaf and no barrow, and the woman who keeps the seed house watched him go from the yard and did not ask him where he was going and has never once asked him where he was going.

This page carries no figure for the run, because the two boards were not read out loud this morning, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. A man who is going to walk four and a half miles and be back after dark is not a man this holding has ever asked to say two figures out loud between the end of a ditch and the second hour of the afternoon.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and sixty-two days** and **six hundred and nine**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-eighth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and sixty-eight hundredweight.**

Up nine off Wednesday, and the hundred and seventy-eighth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The rise went onto the rises. Seventy-eight and sixty-seven make a hundred and forty-five.

The aggregate of the four bodies of households has stood still for a hundred and seventy-six days, and the line for it on this morning's sheet is the hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings out of a hundred and seventy-seven. Those four households are not the launders, and no page in this building has ever put the two totals in one line.

The four and a half miles is a walk and not a cart road, and the last mile and a bit has never been one in living memory. Soren Rill has made it twenty-two times in eleven years of work and a winter of rain, every one of them at his own cost, and this morning a man made it who has never made it, and the count of that is one, and it is his, and nobody thanked him for it.

He went out past the gate and the trough and along the top dyke and across the flat where the water stands in the second week of every month, and then onto the old drove with the thorn on the right, and nobody met him on any part of it in either direction.

The ford at the mile and three quarters was running ankle deep and he went through it in his boots. After it the ground goes up under the south embankment windbreak, and there is a gate in a hedge that belongs to nobody and a track a foot wide.

Thornwild is about two hundred yards of settlement under that windbreak with about nine acres of dead willow at its edge. The long fungal is in the willow and not inside the inhabited line, and the windbreak is standing and is not the same thing as the wood, and nobody who lives under it has ever confused the two.

The Assembly was in the room at the back of that seed house, which has one door, and there were about nine people in it, and the door was shut. He was let in by a woman he had met once, in the rain, in a different county, and she said nothing and has not said anything to him since.

Soren Rill was in the room and was at the head of nothing, and said so before anybody asked him. The count of questions the reader of this body had asked him was fourteen going in and it was fourteen coming out.

He asked for the heartroot back.

He said it in the plainest words he had, and he gave the reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and ninety-six, and it is his and of nobody else's.

"I am going to ask for it back and I am going to give the reason for asking first, and the reason is that a heartroot is not a valve and a valve is not a lock, and the only two people in four counties who can hold one are standing in this room, and one of them is me, and I am not going to be asked to hold it for you and I am not offering." "That is not the whole of the reason and I am not going to pretend it is. The rest of it is that four of your people walk in here on Saturday and stand inside a ring and do a thing that a body that has never held a pattern cannot do correctly, and there is no version of Saturday in which they do it correctly, and I would rather say that to your face at about four miles than have it come back to me in a month as a fact about ground."

Four of them answered him, and every one of them gave the reason before the thing, and there was no order and no motive and nobody in that room had been deceived of anything.

The first reason was the bridge. The long fungal was found inside four hundred yards of the first dwelling under that windbreak eleven years ago and nobody has ever proved where it came through, and Thornwild asked in writing whether there should be a second connection and got an instrument, and that instrument is in a drawer in a building nine miles behind him and is correct in every particular and lets nothing out.

**"You are asking us to hand a thing back to a man who has never once been asked whether we wanted it back, and we are going to say the reason first,"** the woman at the middle of the table said. **"The reason is that a settlement harmed once by a connection is entitled to be asked, and we have been asked, and we got an instrument and not an answer, and about four of us have been carrying that since a month before any of your counties formed a council."**

The second was the store and the seven hundred and four sacks. The third was the four days on the road. The fourth was about a person, and he wrote that one down afterward from memory, because he would not trust his hand in that room.

Then the strongest of them, which was the strongest because every other person in that room had been waiting for it, and it came from the oldest of the four.

**"We have held a motion and we have read a page and we have found no false statement in either,"** she said. **"You have said out loud in four years that a correct page is not an instruction. We have read that. We are a body of about two hundred yards of settlement that was not asked, and the only thing you have ever sent us is a page that tells us what it cannot tell us, and we have made a decision out of the two things it says it will not do, and we are not ashamed of it, and if you came here and said to us that we had misunderstood we would listen to you for an hour and then we would go back and hold the thing anyway."**

Nobody in that room improved a word of it. About four people in it had heard him say the shape of that sentence in this building and about four had not, and about four people in four counties have read the page it came out of and about four have not read it at all.

He gave them his own argument to their faces, and it was the right argument, and the page has to say that being right is not a thing a man gets for free.

He said that a heartroot is not held by a valve, that a pattern is not a custody, and that the cost of what they were about to do would not be paid by them. He said it in about eleven minutes and gave the reason before each part of it, and the count of that in the holding's history went up by one again in the same breath.

The woman at the middle of the table let him finish and then said the thing that ended it, and she said it without any heat in it at all, and that was the worst of it.

**"We have a right,"** she said. **"You have told us so yourself. About four years of a man coming up a road and telling us what our rights are. We have the right and we are going to use it, and you are going to be right about Saturday on your own, and I am sorry about it in a way that I am not going to put on paper."**

He came back in the dark at about the ninth hour, wet to the chest. The man of about seventy-four with a stick was sitting on the wall by the trough with a lamp he did not need and did not offer, looked at the boots, and said nothing.

About nine people were in the one room at this holding at about the tenth hour with the door shut. Marek put his own name at the head of a page of his own and under it, in his own hand, four lines of what he had said and four lines of what they had said back, and the clerk read all eight out loud and improved none of them.

His own record of what he did at Thornwild is correct and so is theirs, and the clerk entered the second half of it at the top of a page and the first half under it, and the page is in the second book, and there is no column for it and nobody wrote a heading at the top of it.

Nobody in that room thanked him and nobody in that room forgave him, and about four people in it had known he was going before he went and about four did not, and he did not ask to be thanked and did not ask to be forgiven and did not say one word about either of them.

Tova Reed was in that room and she had been in it since the eighth hour, and she had not gone to the seed house, and she gave the reason before she said the thing and the count of that in the holding's history is thirty-seven, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

"I am going to say the one thing I am asked to say and I am going to give the reason for saying it first, and the reason is that four bodies of households are about to make a decision about a node and I am the only person in four counties who can say what a season of disconnection costs in seed, and a figure is not a thing anybody can argue with." "I have not got the figure. I have got the seed house's own count for one county and three letters that say the same thing in three different words, and the three letters were written by three men who do not know each other and agree, and that is either a fact or a coincidence, and I have not had time to find out which, and I am going to say a number on the strength of it with my name on it because a council is going to vote in a fortnight." "Nobody in this room is going to be thanked for that and nobody is going to touch my arm. It does not get harder because of my left ear. It gets harder because of a vote, and anybody waiting nine days for a reason to take the seed work off me has not got one."

The man of about fifty wrote it into the second book on a page of its own and read it back and did not improve it and did not write a heading at the top of it, and about four people in that room had wanted him to and about four had not.

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one of Silling anything this morning and nobody is going to. The second of the four lines in his own book is sixty-six days old. The barrow is at eleven journeys up that road, which is a floor and did not rise.

The offer is face up on the low board where it has always been, unfilled, and it went out of this building on Tuesday inside somebody else's sheet and came back to the same board. Nobody here has been told to remove it.

Seventy-two days is how long a register form has stood on the middle table. It is not filled and it is not refused and there is nothing written at the head of it.

Neither the corridor in the pan nor the route under the sealing order has moved. The first is named and open and is not a road, and the second is in a drawer with the sheet that describes it, and that sheet is right in every particular, and the order is valid, and neither of them has been touched this month.

The twenty-third fetching of the man of about seventy has not happened. The twenty-fourth is three mornings off, on the first of a month, and there is no reason for the first of a month on any page here.

The compost line is paid, and the run is twenty not discharged in twenty. The seventh column of the well house door is at fifty-four and is read on the thirtieth and on the first, and this morning it was not read.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page. It was not walked and not measured and not crossed and not priced and not explained, and the Assembly's four people will be standing inside it on Saturday, and nothing in this holding will be able to stop that.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is the same four inches it was at the second hour. A walk, a loss and a refusal are all things that are not marks. Four accounts of that arm stand in this building at four and do not reconcile.

THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. HE ASKED THAT ASSEMBLY FOR THE HEARTHROOT BACK, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, WITH THE DOOR SHUT, AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIX.

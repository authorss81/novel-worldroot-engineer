# Chapter 438

## A Real Right To Refuse

The twenty-first of this month was a Saturday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The well house took five people from the second hour to the sixth hour and it is nine hundred yards away and the five of them walked it twice and Marek was one of the five, and the door is nine hundred yards off and is not a quarter of a mile and nobody in this holding has ever walked it as either.

The six blank columns at the top of that door were cleaned in the second hour and the seventh, which is the one that counts readings, stands at fifty-six, and it was not read this morning, and the five of them looked at the top of that door for about four minutes between the second hour and the third and then went and did the other work, and about four of the five had read the heading that was put at the top of the sixth of the six blank columns on the eleventh and about four had not looked at it, and neither of the two fours has been asked which of them it was.

The two boards were read aloud at the seventh hour before either figure was named, one man to each, and the run of mornings is **seventy-one of a hundred and twenty-five**, and the pair stands at **five hundred and eighty-five days** and **six hundred and thirty-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-first went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same, and the man of about fifty read the second one back and did not improve it and nobody has ever heard him read one back twice in one evening.

**Nine hundred and seven hundredweight.**

Down five off Friday, and the two hundred and first figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The fall went onto the falls. Eighty-nine and seventy-nine make a hundred and sixty-eight.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and ninety-nine days and was written for the hundred and ninety-eighth of those mornings out of two hundred.

The protocols came back down the fen road on the evening of the twentieth and they were worked in the one room at this holding from the eighth hour of this evening until about the tenth hour, in about nine people with the door shut, and the president of that Assembly was in the room and had come about the protocols and not about anything else, and nobody in that room has said what he came about and nobody is going to.

He is the first person from any office who has stood in that room, and the first thing he did in it was to ask where the four books were, and the second thing he did was read the schedule of the protocols off the long table at the wall side standing up, and he read it in about nine minutes and did not sit down at any point in it.

The protocols are in three parts and the schedule is the part that makes them cost anything. Part one says what an inspection is and who pays for it. Part two says what a figure against a work has to have on it, being a unit and a date, and it says what a figure has to have against it when nobody in four counties can check it, and the second of those two is a line the clerk of that Assembly wrote on the seventeenth at a holding nine miles off and had it up here on a roll tied with a string.

The schedule is a list of the works in four counties and there are about nine duties in it in this county alone, and against every one of those nine there is a figure and a unit and a date, and the figures were sent in by the four counties and not by this holding, and one figure in the schedule is the figure of the headland above Marden and that figure is against a field that has waited sixty mornings and has not been worked in sixty mornings and the figure is in a county book with a unit against it and a date.

The room noticed that at about the ninth hour. Nobody in that room changed it, and nobody in that room said it should be changed, and the clerk of this holding wrote the county's figure down in his own hand with the county's name against it and read it back and did not put anything beside it.

Part three is where a body may decline, and it is nine clauses long, and two of the nine are the ones the whole of that evening was about.

The clause that the whole evening was about is the eleventh.

Marek wanted it to say something about how long. He wanted it to say that a body which has refused an inspection of a ground may not be asked for another inspection of that ground by the person who asked for the first, and he wanted the not to have a period on it or he wanted a period with a figure on it, and about four people in that room thought that a refusal ought to be final forever and about four thought that a refusal with no end on it was a veto, and neither of those two fours said so in the room.

He gave the reason for wanting it before he said that he wanted it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and fifteen, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I want out of the eleventh clause and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a clause which lets a body say no once a season is a clause that gets asked about every season, and a body that has said no has had to spend a morning to say it and is going to be asked to spend another one."** "I am not asking for a veto. I am asking for the person who asked to be told in writing that the ground is not to come back on the same ask, and that is not the same thing and the difference between them is about four years and not about one season."

He lost it. It went against him and it went against him by a count that was made by raising hands and that the clerk of this holding wrote down and did not read back, and the clause as it now stands has no period on it and no figure on it and no name on it.

The count was seven and two and the two were not the two of the afternoon before and this page is not going to put the two numbers side by side and call it a pattern, because a chamber of nine counts what is in front of it and what is in front of it on two consecutive days is two different questions.

About four of the nine gave a reason before they gave a hand. The president of that Assembly was one of the four and his reason was that a clause with a period on it is a clause that has to be checked when the period runs out, and a body that is checking a clause instead of running a ground is a body that has been given a second job by an instrument, and that is the thing the charter went in to stop.

The other side of that count said that a refusal with nothing after it is not a refusal but a stoppage, and that the eleventh clause as it now stands is the difference between the two, and that the difference is the whole of what a body is being given back on the twentieth of this month.

The president of that Assembly said one thing about the losing and said it to Marek and not to the room, and it was about eleven words long, and this page gives the whole of it.

**"You wanted the door to shut. The door is not yours and I am not sorry for it."**

Nobody in that room said that Marek had been shown the error of his ways and nobody in that room said anything of the kind, and the president of that Assembly did not say it either, and if a body in four counties has been told that a man came here and was argued out of a clause that is a thing that has been said to him and not by him.

The president conceded one thing that evening and the concession was not about the eleventh clause and it was not about anything Marek asked for.

It was the twelfth clause, and the twelfth clause is the one about a body stating in four ruled lines under its condition what it will be able to do in about four years, and it came back from the sitting with no date at the end of it, and the president had put a date on it before he came.

> THE INSPECTION PROTOCOLS, PART THREE, CLAUSE ELEVEN AND CLAUSE TWELVE, IN THE HAND OF THE CLERK OF THE ASSEMBLY, BOTH ENTERED ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THIS MONTH AND NEITHER ENTERED BEFORE: **ELEVEN, THAT A COUNCIL WITH AN INTEREST IN A GROUND NAMED IN THE SCHEDULE MAY REFUSE AN INSPECTION OF THAT GROUND, AND THAT A REFUSAL ENTERED UNDER THIS PART IS A REFUSAL AND IS NOT AN APPEAL AND IS NOT A DELAY AND IS NOT A LICENSE TO CARRY OUT THE WORK ANYWAY. TWELVE, THAT WHERE A SCHEDULE NAMES A BODY, THAT BODY SHALL STATE IN FOUR RULED LINES UNDER ITS CONDITION WHAT IT WILL BE ABLE TO DO IN ABOUT FOUR YEARS, AND THAT THE ANSWER IS DUE ON THE TWENTY-THREE OF THIS MONTH, AND THAT AN ANSWER NOT GIVEN ON THE TWENTY-THREE OF THIS MONTH IS AN ANSWER THAT DOES NOT EXIST AND IS NOT A DELAY AND IS NOT A REFUSAL, AND IS ENTERED AS NOT GIVEN.**

The fourth line of that entry is the sentence the man of the north row with the cough would have had to sit in a room and listen to again, and the room did that to him once on the seventeenth and is not going to do it twice.

The four ruled lines under that body on that sheet are empty this evening. They were empty on the seventeenth and they are empty tonight and the answer is not due until the twenty-third and there is nothing on that sheet to say what is in them and no unit against them and no figure on the man.

The man of about fifty said one thing about the twelfth clause and gave the reason for it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is one hundred and twenty-nine, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the one thing about a date at the end of a clause, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have kept four books in this building for eleven years and I have never once had a body put a date on me."** "A date at the end of a clause is the first thing in four years that has come at me from a hand and been correct, and it costs the man who wrote it nothing to be correct about, and it costs the body named in the schedule everything, and I would like the room to notice that those two things are in the same sentence and not to argue about the sentence tonight."

Nobody in that room argued about the sentence and about four people in that room had wanted to and about four had not, and neither of the two fours has been asked and neither of them is going to be.

About nine people were in that room from the eighth hour until about the tenth hour and the door was shut and nobody went out of it except twice and both of those times were for air, and the president of that Assembly left at about the tenth hour and went out through the yard in the dark and there was no lamp brought out for him and nobody in this holding noticed that except the man of about fifty and the man of about fifty did not say so.

The room was cold for the last hour of it because the stove in that room has not been laid since a month that is now three months back, and the man of about seventy-four with a stick was in it and had his coat on and did not take it off, and about four of the nine were in their coats the whole evening and about four warmed their hands on the tin at the end of the table, and the woman who keeps the seed house kept the fire in the seed house going for the man of about thirty-one of Silling and he did not come in and she did not take anything to him and nobody has said anything about that.

Two of the nine had come nine miles for the evening and had to go back, and the clerk of the assembly wrote out the two clauses in longhand on the table at the wall side and read them both out twice before the tenth hour, and Marek asked for one word to be changed in the eleventh clause and the word was changed and nobody in that room argued about the word.

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody in that room thanked the man from that office for a concession and nobody thanked Marek for a clause he had lost and nobody thanked the man of the north row with the cough for a thing he had not been asked twice for, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and this morning was not a yard.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and was turned at the top of the yard, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it, and eleven journeys is a floor, and the second of the four lines in his own book is eighty-nine days old this morning and has nothing on it and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in eighty-nine days and nobody is going to this month.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-five days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it. The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and a protocol is not that instrument and there is no second instrument of that offer on any table in this holding this season.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and neither of those two columns is a clause and neither of them is going to become one.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and a date at the end of a clause is not a measurement either and the two of those are not added to each other in this holding.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and opens nothing, and a clause that says a council may refuse an inspection says nothing whatever about a route and does not open one.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained this morning, and the ground on both sides of it is going wrong and going slower and nobody in this holding has called slower better.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is four inches at the tenth hour as it is at the second, and a clause is not a mark, a concession is not a mark, a refusal is not a mark and an answer not given is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room tonight is a fifth of anything.

# Chapter 437

## The Charter Of Many Wells

The twentieth of this month was a Friday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the sixtieth morning.

The rota stands at eighty-nine mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and sixty not, and twenty-nine and sixty are eighty-nine.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited sixty mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-seventh morning of one of them and the twenty-fourth morning of the other, and the difference between the two is thirteen, and it has been thirteen for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever found out what the thirteen is.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings. The pair is read out at the seventh hour on the four mornings of a fortnight it is read on and this is not one of them, and the reason is that the clerk of this holding went east at the second hour with a roll and a sheet and a bicycle and has not been back, and the two men who take the boards did not ask a third man to do it and it was not done.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and eighty-four days** and **six hundred and thirty-one**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twentieth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same, and neither book knows what happened in a room nine miles off this morning.

**Nine hundred and twelve hundredweight.**

Up nine off Thursday, and the two hundredth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and the two hundred and first is on a morning next week and not in this month.

The rise went onto the rises. Eighty-nine and seventy-eight make a hundred and sixty-seven.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and ninety-eight days and was written for the hundred and ninety-seventh of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety-nine.

The third and last day of that sitting began at about the second hour of the afternoon in a room with one door, and the door was shut for about three hours and eleven minutes and it was opened once and that once was for a man carrying a roll of paper.

The Charter of Many Wells was read out once in that room and it was four clauses long and it was read out by the clerk of that assembly in the words it was written in, and it took about four minutes, and nobody in that room said one word while it was being read and nobody in that room said one word for about a minute and a half after it had been read.

> THE CHARTER OF MANY WELLS, ONE SHEET, FOUR CLAUSES, NO SEAL UPON IT AND NO SIGNATURE AT THE FOOT OF IT AND NO DATE AT THE HEAD OF IT, THE DATE BEING ENTERED AFTER THE COUNT IN A DIFFERENT HAND: **FIRST, THAT THE STEWARDSHIP OF A GROUND NAMED IN THE SCHEDULE IS HELD BY MORE THAN ONE BODY, AND IS NOT HELD BY ANY PERSON ALONE, AND THAT THE PERSON NAMED AGAINST A GROUND AS THE ENGINEER OF RECORD FOR IT IS ONE OF THE HOLDERS OF IT AND IS NOT THE ONE WHO DECIDES. SECOND, THAT A BODY WITH AN INTEREST IN A GROUND NAMED IN THE SCHEDULE MAY DECLINE AN INSPECTION OF IT, AND THAT A DECLINING ENTERED UNDER THE PROTOCOLS IS A DECLINING AND IS NOT AN APPEAL AND IS NOT A DELAY. THIRD, THAT THE PERSON NAMED AGAINST A GROUND AS THE ENGINEER OF RECORD FOR IT IS ANSWERABLE FOR THAT GROUND, AND THAT AN ANSWERABLE PERSON IS NOT A PERSON WHO DECIDES, AND THAT NOTHING IN THIS CHARTER MAKES ANY PERSON THE HOLDER OF ANYTHING BY HIMSELF OR HERSELF. FOURTH, THAT A CHARTER OF THIS NAME IS AN INSTRUMENT ABOUT STEWARDSHIP, AND IS NOT AN ANSWER TO ANY QUESTION THAT WAS PUT BEFORE IT WAS MADE, AND THAT ANY BODY WHICH PUT ONE OF THOSE QUESTIONS MAY PUT IT AGAIN.**

The document went to a division of the nine and the count was made twice and it was made by two different people and the two counts were the same.

Before it went to the division there were about nine minutes in which three of the nine said, in three different ways and in three different orders, that the second clause was the one they had come for and that the first and third and fourth were the price of it, and about four of the nine said that the fourth clause made the whole sheet a thing a body could put in a drawer, and about four said that a sheet which says it is not an answer is the only kind of sheet that can be carried into a room where the question is still open.

Marek was in the room through the whole of that and did not speak and about four people in that room have said since that a man who spent four years being the only person who could hold an end of a thing spent the whole afternoon not speaking about the day he gave it up, and about four have said that it was not a sacrifice and should not be written down as one.

That is the whole of what this page has about it. The two numbers are the same number and there are two of them and the page does not improve on that, and the page does not say by how much it was close, and about four people who were not in that room have asked since whether it was close and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has been asked by anybody in this holding.

Nobody in this building was shown the moment of anybody finding out and nobody in this building has claimed to have been in that room when it happened, and the three of them who were in it have not described it to the six of them who were not, and the man of about fifty has said that the count is the count and the reading of it is a thing four counties can do.

A second instrument went into that room at about the sixth hour of the afternoon and it is one sheet and it dissolves the Well Directorate, and it is an instrument and not a defeat, and it was signed by nobody at the table and it took effect at the date at the head of it and it was correct in every particular it states.

The page says the rest of it in one line and does not say it again. The people who worked in that directorate lose their living, and about nine of them are within nine miles of this building, and nobody in that room said anything about them and nobody in this holding has been asked about them and one of them has a brother-in-law in the second county who has not come to the seed house since the eleventh.

The Crown retreats to the Engine. That is what is left of it and this page does not call it a defeat and does not call it a ruse and nobody in four counties has called it either, and about four people in this holding have said that a thing which was true and correct and carried no figure has just cost three counties their vote and about four have said that the alternative to a true sheet with no figure on it was a number, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

A man stood up in that room at about the seventh hour of the evening and gave a number and a date, and the number was the form of a circular and the date was a day of a month, and the sheet the number belongs to is one sheet with four clauses and an embossed stamp at the foot of the first of them.

He said that the standing delegated authority of the president of that Assembly rests on the first clause of that circular, and that a charter of that name cannot be carried by a person who is holding a blanket over it, and that he is withdrawing it.

The stamp on that sheet is in ink and it is the same stamp as on every other sheet that office has ever sent to this holding and it is about the size of a thumbnail and it is pressed hard enough to emboss the paper, and the signature at the foot of it is **H. Pike, President**.

Nobody in that room said that they had seen him before. Nobody in the fen has ever seen him, nobody asked to, and the fact that he is in that room this evening is the first time that anybody in four counties has been in a room with him and about four people in that room have written that down and about four have not.

The clerk of the fourth county had the second issue of that circular in a file and took it out and read the date off the sheet and put it on the table, and the date on the sheet was the date the man had given, and the clerk of the fourth county said one word, which was aye, and did not say it in a tone and nobody in that room has ever asked him what tone he said it in.

He apologized on the ground at about the eighth hour of the evening, and not in the room, and he said it to the four people of the Assembly at Thornwild who were standing about nine feet outside that ring of bare ground on the north side of that node with their four stakes in a line where they had put them.

He said he was sorry for the order he signed sealing the route, and he said the office and the date of it and the line of the schedule it sealed, and about four people at that node heard all of it and about four heard the middle of it.

The four of them did not accept it. One of them said one sentence back and it was short and it is not on this page in her words because she is a body and not a person, and the sentence said that an apology is not a withdrawal and that the order is a paper and the paper is still a paper.

An apology is not a withdrawal and this page says that once and does not improve it. The order sealing the route is in a drawer in this building and it is valid and it opens nothing, and it is not withdrawn, and the route has not been reopened by anything said on that ground this evening or by anything said in that room this afternoon, and there is no instrument in this holding and not one in that room that reopens it.

Nobody in this holding has forgiven him. Nobody in this holding was asked to and nobody said that anybody ought to, and a page that says a man was not forgiven on the twentieth of this month is a page that has turned a thing that did not happen into a thing that did.

The Assembly voted to keep the bridges at about the ninth hour of the evening, and it was carried, and keeping is a decision about a future and is not a statement about a present, and the standing of the bridge is not in that book either.

The man of about fifty said that sentence out loud in that room at about the ninth hour of the evening and did not read it back afterward and nobody read it back for him, and it is the same sentence he entered in the second book in a month that is now three months back with a line ruled under it and nothing above the line and nothing under it, and it is not improved and it is not going to be improved, and nobody in that room has asked him where it is written down and he has not said.

Marek said one thing about the whole of that evening and gave the reason for it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and fourteen, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I think happened in that room and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that the sheet with no figure on it arrived last night and the charter went through this afternoon, and I would rather this holding said that out loud than let four counties work out on their own that the two things are in the same week."** "They are in the same week and they are not the same thing, and the charter would have gone through without that sheet about a body in three counties would have found the arithmetic harder, and a man who says the charter was bought with a piece of paper is wrong and a man who says the charter was right is also wrong, and both of those are things a body can check in about four years against this page and this page will not have moved."

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody in that room said thank you to the man who withdrew the blanket and nobody said thank you to the man who signed the instrument and nobody in four counties said thank you to the people who lost their living off it, and the count of that in this holding's history has never been a thing anybody has kept.

He came back on the bicycle at about the eleventh hour and the yard had about two inches of water in it still and the lamp was off the table and the man of about fifty was still in the one room and had the second book open in front of him and had not written anything in it.

The barrow did not go up that road this morning and did not come down it this evening, and eleven journeys is a floor, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one's own book is eighty-eight days old and has nothing on it and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in eighty-eight days.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-four days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it. The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and a charter is not that instrument and nobody in this building has suggested that it was.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and a charter is neither of those two columns and is not going to be a third.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and a charter is not a measurement of a ground and nobody has ever entered one against the other.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in that drawer and is valid and opens nothing, and a charter that does not say a word about a route has not opened one.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained this morning, and the four people of the Assembly at Thornwild stood about nine feet outside it for about two hours this evening and not one step nearer, and the ground on both sides of it is going wrong and going slower and nobody in this holding has called slower better.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is four inches at the eleventh hour as it is at the second, and a charter is not a mark, a division of nine is not a mark, an apology is not a mark, a withdrawal is not a mark and a vote to keep a thing is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room this afternoon or on that ground this evening is a fifth of anything.

# Chapter 439

## What Plural Means On A Tuesday

The twenty-second of this month was a Sunday, and the second line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The flit at the top of the west field and the two culverts under it took six people from the second hour to the fifth hour, and the near culvert is clear and the far one has about a quarter of the same green weed in it that was in it a month ago, and four people pulled it out by hand and put it on the bank, which is what the four of them have always done with it.

Marek was one of the four who had their hands in it and could not have told you afterward which of the two culverts he had been in for the longest.

The yard was still under about two inches of the water that came through on the sixteenth, and it had gone down about half an inch in three days and the frost took it at about the sixth hour in a ring about a yard off the wall on the north side, and the six of them broke that ring with their boots on the way past it and one of the six said the word ring out loud and the other five heard him.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. The pair is read out at the seventh hour on every other morning and this morning is not one of the mornings it is read on, and the reason is that the clerk of this holding is one of the two men who take them and he was at the gate from the fourth hour with a rider from the first council and did not come into the yard again until the eighth hour.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and eighty-six days** and **six hundred and thirty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The twenty-second went in at about the ninth hour on a Sunday evening, which is the first time in this month that anything has been entered in this holding after the eighth hour, and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and sixteen hundredweight.**

Up nine off Saturday, and the two hundred and second figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The rise went onto the rises. Ninety and seventy-nine make a hundred and sixty-nine.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in two hundred days and was written for the hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings out of two hundred and one.

A letter came up the fen road on the ordinary post in the middle of the afternoon and it was about a page and a half and it was in a clerk's hand and not an engineer's, and the clerk of this holding read it out in the one room at about the fifth hour of the afternoon in about nine people with the door shut.

The question is one question and it has a number at the top of it. The number is the figure the first council's schedule carries against the headland above Marden, and the schedule is a schedule under the protocols, and the protocols went out with the charter on the Saturday bag, and the question asks what the figure is a figure of.

About nine people were in that room and about four of them had read the council's schedule before this morning and about four had not, and the man of about fifty had, and he had it open on the table at the wall side with a finger on the row, and the row is the fourth row of that council's schedule and it has a figure against it and a unit and a date and a column for whether the person was asked that is empty, and it is empty on the county's own copy and not only on this holding's.

The figure is in seed and labour and drainage stone and it is the fourth year of figures and the fourth year has the same number in it as the first year, and one of the nine in that room pointed that out and about four of the nine had not noticed and about four had noticed and had said nothing about it in four years, and neither of those two fours has been asked.

This holding cannot answer the question. The figure was entered four years ago by a man who is not in this room, out of a book in the second county, and it has not been reconciled with anything in four years, and the ground it is against has now waited sixty mornings and has not been worked in sixty mornings, and this holding has three figures of its own for the same headland and does not use any of the three and the three of them are not in the schedule and are not going into it tonight.

The three of them are the ones this holding has been carrying against that headland since the second year and the first of the three is one of the two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check, and the first of the two is not named and is not going to be named in a letter to a council, and the six things this holding does not know are six and this question is not a seventh of them because it has a unit against it and a shape and a date.

The clerk of this holding said so at about the sixth hour and gave the reason for saying so before he said it, and the count of reasons given first in this holding's history is not a figure for the clerk of this holding, because this holding has never kept one for him and is not starting one on a Sunday, and nobody has asked him whether he would rather have one.

**"I am going to say what this holding can answer and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that a letter with a number at the top of it goes back with a number at the top of it or it does not go back at all, and this one is going back tonight with no number on it, and the no is the answer and I would rather it was the answer than a number that is about four years old and a field nobody has been in."** "What this holding can answer is that the figure is the figure of the first council's own schedule and that it was not made here. What this holding cannot answer is what it costs to work that headland this season, and nobody in this room knows that, and I am not going to send a letter that hides that behind a sentence about where the number came from."

Marek gave a reason before he said the thing and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and sixteen, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what that letter is and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that this holding has spent four years telling four counties that a correct page is not an answer, and a letter that goes back tonight with a figure in it and a hedge around it would be the first page this building has ever sent that was both."** "Send it with nothing in it. The council asked what the figure is a figure of and the answer is that it is a figure of a piece of ground nobody has worked in sixty mornings and nobody in this building can put a second figure beside the first one, because the second one would be a guess and a guess in a column is worse than an empty column, and there is a column in this holding's list for exactly that and it has six rows in it and no unit against any of the six."

The answer went out at about the seventh hour in a clerk's hand on the back of the council's own letter, and it is one paragraph long and it names no figure, and it was not signed by anybody, and the clerk did not ask anybody to sign it.

The request went in the requests column. The count of requests and the count of section-nine notes are fifty-three and fifty-three and are not added to each other, and a letter asking a question that gets an answer is a letter, and a letter is not a fifty-fourth anything.

The other half of that afternoon was a word.

It came out of the fourth part of the protocols, which is the part nobody expected to need, and the fourth part is about who holds a ground and on what paper, and it is four clauses and three of them are about this holding's own books and the fourth is about a thing nobody in the room could put a name to.

The clerk of this holding had written that fourth part out on Friday from what the charter says and what the protocols already say, and he had written a heading at the top of it, and the heading is the heading of the fourth part and it is two words, and the second of the two words is the one that caused the argument.

The clerk asked the question in the one room at about the seventh hour of the evening and he asked it of Marek and he asked it once, and about four of the nine had gone home by then and about four were still in the room and the four that were still in the room heard all of it.

**"Plural. I have written it in the heading of the fourth part and I have copied it out four times this afternoon and I do not know what it means, and I am not going to send a word to four counties that I cannot define."**

The answer took about four seconds and it was not the answer he was expecting, and about four people in this holding have said since that they would have given him a definition and about four have said that a definition would have been a fifth thing invented in a building that has spent nine days admitting it invents things, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

**"The charter says that the stewardship of a ground is held by more than one body,"** Marek said, **"and it does not say anything else about it, and plural is a word for more than one and the charter has already said more than one in words that a body can argue with."** "So the heading means what the charter means and no more, and the way to be certain of it is to leave the word out of the heading and write the charter's own words, and if the fourth part then reads like the charter that is because it is the fourth part of what the charter started."

The clerk wrote the fourth part's heading out again with the charter's words in it and it is two words longer than it was and there is no word in it that anybody in this holding has had to define, and the two words that are not there are not on any page in this building and this page is not going to be the place that puts them.

The fourth county wrote in about the protocols on the Saturday and its letter is half a page and it is the only one of the four that did not ask for a figure, and what it asked was whether the sixth column of its own list was going to have a heading at the top of it, and the answer went back in one line on the Monday and the line said that the sixth column is the first thing a body outside this building can check and that a heading on it is not required and is not going to be asked for by anybody in this holding.

Nobody in four counties has said thank you for that line and the clerk of the fourth county has not written again about it and is not going to, and about four people in this holding have said that the fourth county is the one of the four that has understood the list and about four have said that understanding a list is not the same as using one, and neither of the two fours has been asked which of them was right and neither of them is going to be.

The protocols went out on the Saturday bag with the four parts and the schedule and the two clauses entered on the Friday night, and the roll of the first draft is in the drawer with the other papers in it, and nobody in this building has kept a copy of the first draft and nobody has been asked for one.

Nobody in that room agreed with him and nobody in that room disagreed with him and the clerk put the pen down flat and did not say thank you and went out of the room, and the count of reasons given first did not move on that account, because nobody gave a reason before the thing.

The barrow did not go up that road this morning because it is a Sunday and it did not go up this evening either, and eleven journeys is a floor and it is a floor that has been a floor since a year that is now six years back, and the second of the four lines in the man of about thirty-one's own book is ninety days old this morning and has nothing on it and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in ninety days.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-six days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it. The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and a heading at the top of a part of a protocol is not that instrument.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and the fourth part of a protocol is a third heading and is the first one this building has written for anybody else.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and a heading is not a measurement, and the two of those are not added to each other in this holding and have not been for four years.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and opens nothing, and a letter that goes back with no figure in it opens nothing and closes nothing and is not an instrument.

There is a ring about a yard off the north wall of this yard that took the standing water on Friday night, and the six of them broke it with their boots at about the sixth hour, and it is not that one. That one is inside the eleven acres and it is bare in its eleventh year and it has no dimension against it on any page, and nobody in four counties has walked it, measured it, crossed it or priced it this morning or any other, and the ground on both sides of it is going wrong and going slower than it was. A ring of ice in a yard is a morning that will be gone by the second hour of the afternoon. That one is in its eleventh year, and neither of them is going anywhere.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is four inches at the ninth hour as it is at the second, and a question is not a mark, a heading is not a mark, an answer with nothing in it is not a mark and a word that is not on a page is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room tonight is a fifth of anything.

There was no yard in this holding this morning. The two things that were said out loud outside were the word ring at the sixth hour, which is not a thing anybody was going to do, and the name of a council at the gate at the fourth hour, which is a name a body has and is not a figure this holding cannot check, and the count of things said out loud in a yard and got wrong is five and it was five before this morning and it is five now and it did not move.

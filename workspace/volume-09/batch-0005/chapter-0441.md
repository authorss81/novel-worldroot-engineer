# Chapter 441

## A Reason First

The twenty-fourth of this month was a Tuesday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the sixty-first morning, and this is the thirteenth of them and the last one anybody in this building has counted, and every one of the twelve before it came round and went away empty the same way.

The rota stands at ninety mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and sixty-one not, and twenty-nine and sixty-one are ninety.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited sixty-one mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-eighth morning of one of them and the twenty-fifth morning of the other, and the difference between the two is thirteen, and it has been thirteen for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever found out what the thirteen is.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings. The pair is read out at the seventh hour on the four mornings of a fortnight it is read on and this is not one of them, and the reason it is not one of them is that the two men who take them are the two men who are putting the last of the room's business to bed and neither of them came in before the ninth hour.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and eighty-eight days** and **six hundred and thirty-five**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not going to be opened by anything that happened in this building this month.

The twenty-fourth went in at about the ninth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and twenty hundredweight.**

Up nine off Monday, and the two hundred and fourth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and it is the last of that series in this month, and it does not reset at anything and it is not going to be turned by anybody.

The rise went onto the rises. Ninety-one and eighty make a hundred and seventy-one.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in two hundred and two days and was written for the two hundred and first of those mornings out of two hundred and three.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-eight days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and nobody in this building has been told to take it off that table and nobody is going to be.

The room had the low sun on the middle table for about an hour this morning and the form sat in it and nobody moved it, and about four people in this holding have said that the form would keep better in the shade and about four have said that a form that has been on a table for ninety-eight days can stand one more morning of sun, and neither of those two fours has been asked and neither of them is going to be.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, and it has not been moved since and there is no second instrument of that offer on any table in this holding this season.

Under two words entered in the second book on the twentieth of a month now two months back, with the hour against them, there are four ruled lines and there is nothing in them. They were ruled before the thing they were ruled for and they have been empty since and they are a question and not a promise, and the answer is not due, and nobody in this holding has filled them and nobody has asked anybody to.

On a page of its own in that same book there are two figures and no name, and there is no unit against either of them, and they were not read out this morning and they are not named in this holding's book and they were not named in a letter to a county's engineer a fortnight ago and they are going to be there tomorrow.

The fourth part of the protocols says that a ground named in the schedule shall have a person answerable for it, and that the person answerable for it is not the person who decides anything about it, and that the person answerable for it is not the engineer of record, and it does not say what such a person is.

The clerk of this holding ruled a column in the second book at about the tenth hour of the morning and wrote a heading at the top of it, and the heading is four words long and it is in his own hand, and the column is empty from the top of it to the bottom of it.

He put the duty to about nine people in the one room with the door shut and nobody took it for about two minutes, and about four of the nine said afterwards that they had been waiting for somebody else to take it and about four said that they had not been waiting for anybody, and neither of those two fours has been asked and neither of them is going to be.

The clerk read the fourth part of the protocols out while it was standing empty. He read the whole of it, which is four clauses, and he read the third one twice, and the third one is the one that says the person answerable for a ground is not the engineer of record, and he read it twice because about four of the nine had not understood on the first reading that it was a sentence about two different men.

Nobody in that room said the word duty out loud until about the second minute. It was on a page and it was a column and it was a heading with four words at the top of it, and the nine of them sat and looked at the three of those for about two minutes, and the man of about fifty watched all nine of them not look at the fourth and about four people in that room have said that the fourth clause is the one that would have made a person say yes and about four have said that a person who says yes to the fourth clause without the other three is a person who has read one line.

The clerk of the first council had written a name against that column in his own copy of the schedule on the nineteenth and had sent it up with the protocols, and the name is a man's name and it is not in the second book, and the clerk of this holding did not copy it across and gave no reason for that and was not asked for one, and the name in that copy of the schedule is still the only one in four counties and this holding has never seen it and cannot check it and has added nothing to it.

Whether the man whose name is on that schedule accepted the duty is not a thing anybody in this holding has said, and there is no column for whether he was asked, and about four people in four counties have noticed that and about four have not, and neither of the two fours has written to anybody about it.

The man of about fifty said one thing against it before anybody had taken it, and he gave no reason in front of it and the count of reasons given first in this holding's history did not move on that account, and that count is one hundred and twenty-nine and is his and of nobody else's.

**"A name under a heading in this building is not a duty, it is a decoration, and I have watched four of them go up in four years and I have watched about four people read them and believe them."** "I am not saying do not write it. I am saying that a man who takes this because a column is empty is going to be in that column for about four years, and what he does in it is going to be about a sheet and not about a ground, and a sheet is where this holding keeps its best thinking and its worst."

Harlan Vetch was in that room and had been in it since the ninth hour and had not said anything for the whole of the two minutes.

He gave the reason first and the count of that in this holding's history is his own, and it moved this morning, and it is not in the sentence this holding uses when it counts the reasons a person gives before a thing, and this page is not going to put one there.

**"I am going to take that duty and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that column."** "Not what is in it, and not what I am like, and not the four miles and not the barrow and not the fact that about four people in four counties have told me this month that a man who carries a barrow is not a man they would put a ground against. The reason is that there is a word at the top of it and there is nothing under it, and I have been in this room since the ninth hour watching nine people look at a word at the top of a column and not one of them write anything under it, and I have done that for about four minutes, and I would rather be the first one under a word than the ninth."

**"And I am going to say the rest of it after the reason, which is that I do not know what the duty is and neither do the nine of you, and if somebody from a county writes to me in about four weeks and asks me what I am answerable for, I am going to write back and say that on the twenty-fourth of this month a column was empty and I am in it, and that will be a true sentence and it will not be an answer, and I would rather be a man with a true sentence in a book than a man with an answer he made up in a room."**

The clerk wrote the name against the heading in his own hand at about the eleventh hour and read it back once and did not read it back twice, and there was nothing else in the column, and nobody in that room put a second name under it, and the six of the nine who had not put a name in it did not say why.

Nobody in that room said that the man whose name went in had been the obvious one, and nobody in that room said that he was not, and about four of the nine have said since that a column with one name in it looks the same as a column with a wrong name in it, and about four have said that it is not the same and that this holding is the only place in four counties where anybody would be able to tell, and neither of the two fours has been asked.

Marek was in that room when it was written and he did not say anything at the moment it was written and he has not said anything about it since, and about four people in this holding have said that the silence was the right one and about four have said that the right one would have been to say something, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the man himself was not asked either.

Marek said the last thing said in that room and gave the reason for it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and eighteen, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what I think that column is and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that I have been the only person this holding could name against a ground for eleven years and there has been a shortage on a sheet since Saturday and a charter read out in a room nine miles off since the twentieth, and neither of those two things has changed the fact that a man in this room was the only name that could be written."** "It is not a fix and it is not a beginning and it is not the fourth part of the protocols working. It is a name under a word in a book in a building that keeps four books, and on the day somebody in a county checks that column there will be one name in it and no ground described and no work set out and no second name beside it, and that is a worse column than an empty one and it is a better column than a word with nothing under it, and I am not going to say which of those two it is in about four years because in about four years one of us will be able to check."

The man of about thirty-one of Silling said one thing at about the twelfth hour on his way out and it was not a question and it was not an answer, and nobody in that room asked him about the second of the four lines in his own book, which is ninety-two days old and has nothing on it, and he did not raise it and he has not raised it in ninety-two days and nobody in this holding is going to.

The far end of that bridge is held by two people and neither of them is the reader of this body, and the standing of that bridge is in no book in this building, and no person in this holding has an instrument that says whether it is on or off, and nothing said in this room this morning has supplied one.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and this morning put a heading at the top of a column in a different book and nothing has been said in this building about the two of them together and nothing is going to be.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and a duty is not a measurement and a name under a word is not a measurement and the two of those have not been added to anything in this holding in four years.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in that drawer and is valid and opens nothing, and a column with one name in it opens nothing and closes nothing and is not an instrument.

Nobody in this holding has ever put a foot inside the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres and nobody put one inside it this morning, and it is bare in its eleventh year and it has no dimension against it on any page in four counties, and the ground on both sides of it is still going wrong and is going slower than it was in the ninth. Nobody in four counties has been able to put a figure to it and about four people have decided that is the state of it and about four have not.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is four inches at the twelfth hour as it is at the second, and a duty is not a mark, a charter is not a mark, a vote is not a mark, a protocol is not a mark, an apology is not a mark, a loss in one ear is not a mark and a thing said in a room is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room this morning is a fifth of anything.

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp went off the table at about the first hour of the afternoon and the column stayed in the second book with one name in it and the door of that room was shut from the tenth hour until the first hour and the man of about fifty did not read the entry back and nobody asked him to.

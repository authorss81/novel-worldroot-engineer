# Chapter 433

## The Sixteenth

The sixteenth of this month was a Monday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the fifty-ninth morning.

The rota stands at eighty-eight mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-nine not, and twenty-nine and fifty-nine are eighty-eight.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited fifty-nine mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-sixth morning of one of them and the twenty-third morning of the other, and the difference between the two is thirteen, and it has been thirteen for four years, and nobody in this holding has ever found out what the thirteen is.

The six who would have gone to the headland were on the west side of the boundary ditch from the second hour to the seventh hour, and Marek was one of the six, and both of the men who take the boards went with them and came back at the eighth hour with wet sleeves to the elbow.

The west side of that ditch is the side the county did not take and the four of them who have held it since the first of this month have found a foot of silt in the bottom of it that was not there in the ninth, and it is the same foot of silt that has been coming into the top of that ditch all season, and nobody in this building has put the two facts in the same sentence and the clerk was asked about it on the fourteenth and gave an answer that was about a page and not about a person.

Marek had the bar for four of those five hours and the shovel for one, and the one with the shovel is the one he could not have told anybody afterward which of the five it was, and the water came in behind them at about the fifth hour and put about two inches into the ditch and stayed there, and the four of them finished the last eleven yards standing in it and one of the four said the word silt out loud and the other three heard him.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. The pair is read out at the seventh hour on the four mornings of a fortnight it is read on, and this morning is not one of them, and the reason it is not one of them is two men in a ditch until the eighth hour.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and eighty days** and **six hundred and twenty-seven**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The sixteenth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Nine hundred and four hundredweight.**

Up nine off Sunday, and the hundred and ninety-sixth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and there is no hundred and ninety-seventh in this month and there is not going to be one.

The rise went onto the rises. Eighty-seven and seventy-six make a hundred and sixty-three, and that window is four hundred and eighty-three mornings wide and it has nothing to do with a ditch or a headland or a rota.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and ninety-four days and was written for the hundred and ninety-third of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety-five.

The water came off the west end at about the fifth hour and went through the yard in a sheet about two inches deep and Marek was in the yard at the time and had a bar in one hand and did not put it down.

At about the fourth hour a rider came up the coast road with a fold of paper in his coat. He stopped at the gate and did not get off, and the paper was four days old when it came out of his coat.

The well on that shelf is four hundred miles off and takes four days to say anything at all. It has been read on the sixteenth of every month for eleven years, and it was read on the sixteenth at that far end, and what came up the road was a day.

The man of about fifty took the paper standing in the yard, read it, and gave it back, and the rider went on to Havergate with a bag of wire and was not fed and did not expect to be.

It went into the entry for this holding as a day and not as a figure. There is no ordinal against it and there is no figure against it and there has never been one, and nobody at that far end has been asked a question about it in eleven years.

There were nine entries in the middle book of that shape before this morning and there are ten of them now, and the count of that in this holding's history is ten, and it is a count of days and not a count of figures, and nobody in this holding has ever put a unit against it and nobody is going to this morning.

The clerk of this holding came out of the one room about a quarter of an hour later with the paper still in his hand and asked the question he had been carrying since Friday, and he gave the reason for asking it before he asked it, which is his habit and not a rule, and it is a habit about questions and is not a figure in the count this holding keeps of the reasons a person gives before a thing, and this holding has never kept a figure for the clerk of this holding and is not starting one.

**"On the eleventh three headings went into this building and there has not been a fourth since, and I have been thinking about that for five mornings, and the reason I am asking is that the oldest column in the middle book is the one I have just written in and it has had nothing at the top of it since the book was started, and I would like to know whether it ought to have something."** "I am not asking you to write one. I am asking you to say whether it should have one, and I would rather be told in a room than go and think about it for another week."

The man of about fifty put the paper down on the long table at the wall side and put his thumb on it and did not write anything on it, and about four people in that room watched him not write anything on it.

**"No, and I am going to give you the reason first, and the reason is that a heading says what goes in a column, and what goes in that column is a day, and the day is the whole of what is in it."** "If I put a word at the top of it that says days, then in about four years a body opens that book and sees a heading that says days and a column of days under it, and what that body is going to say is that the figures are missing. Not that anybody decided there were none. Missing. And a body in a county that has been told a figure is missing will send a letter about a figure, and I will have to answer that letter, and there is nothing on that page to answer it with."

He said the rest of it with his hand flat on the paper and not looking at anybody.

**"I know what I am saying sounds like a man who does not want a heading. I do not. I want four hundred headings in this building by the end of next year and I want every column that has a heading at the top of it to have something under it. That column has nothing under it because there is nothing to put under it, and that is a different thing, and if the two of them look the same on the page then the page is lying and the bookkeeper is the one who did it."** "Write it at the head of the columns where a thing goes in. Do not write it at the head of a column where a thing has never gone in. That is the whole of the difference and it took me eleven years and five mornings this week to say it."

The clerk did not accept that and did not argue with it either, and he said the thing a clerk says when he has been told no and wants the room to know that he is not going to go away.

**"Then say the other half of it to me. If there is a column in this book that a body in a county can open and see is empty, and there is no heading over it, then how does anybody know it is empty on purpose and not because the bookkeeper has not got round to it."**

The man of about fifty said no reason first to that one and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on account of it, and he did not correct himself afterward and was not asked to.

**"They do not. That is the honest answer and I have given it to you. A body that opens that book and sees an empty column with nothing over it is entitled to think I have not got round to it, and in about four years one of them is going to think it and write to me about it, and I will answer that letter on the day it comes and I will not be able to do anything about the four years before it."** "And I would rather have that than a heading that says days, because a wrong heading is a thing a body does not come back about. A man who thinks a bookkeeper is slow writes again. A man who thinks a bookkeeper is hiding something stops writing and starts asking somebody else, and somebody else will answer him, and the answer that somebody else gives him will be about a heading."

Nobody in that room agreed with him out loud and nobody in that room disagreed with him out loud, and the clerk put the paper back in the drawer it came out of and did not rule a line and did not take a new sheet, and the question of the eleventh is going to be asked again by somebody in about a month and it is not going to have a different answer.

Marek said one thing about it and gave the reason for it before he said it, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and ten, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say that the clerk was right to ask, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that three headings went into this building in one afternoon five mornings ago and a fourth one is about four weeks off, and a holding that has put three headings on three columns in three days has not found a rate at which to put them on."** "I am not asking him to stop asking. I am telling him that the asking is the work, and the answer this morning is no, and a man who has been told no four times in a month is going to ask a fifth time, and he should ask it, because the fifth time is the one where somebody in this room who is not me and not him is going to have an answer I have not thought of."

The woman who keeps the seed house was in that room and had been in it since the fifth hour and said one thing about it, and she gave the reason for it first, and the count of that in the holding's history is forty-two, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

**"I keep four accounts of my own and three of them have a heading at the top of them and one of them has not, and the one without is the one I am least able to check, and I would like the clerk to know that I agree with the man with the book and that I did not expect to."** "A heading is two pen strokes. What is under it is the whole of what a column is for, and a column with two pen strokes at the top of it and nothing under it is a thing a body can write to you about, and I have had four letters this year about a thing I had not put under a heading yet."

Nobody thanked anybody, and nobody in that room said that the man of about fifty had been right about a heading or that he had been wrong about one, and the count of the ten entries in the middle book did not move after the eighth hour and is ten and will be ten tomorrow.

The four people of the Assembly are still on the north side of that node with their four stakes in a line about nine feet outside the ring of bare ground, and they are holding the reading of that ground and the answering of it, and their own record of what they do is on a page and it is correct in every particular and it lets nothing out, and it has no power to make any person answer.

The ground on both sides of that ring is going wrong and going slower than it was, and nobody in this holding has called slower better, and the ring is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained this morning by anybody in four counties.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and it was not read out this morning and is not going to be read out this month.

The offer is on the low board at the far end of the room, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, and a rider with a fold of paper in his coat is not that instrument and nobody in this building has suggested that he was.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and went down it empty, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it, and eleven journeys is a floor, and the second of the four lines in his own book is eighty-four days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in eighty-four days.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and it is four inches at the ninth hour as it is at the second, and a heading is not a mark, a day is not a mark, a refusal is not a mark and a rider with a paper is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in that drawer and is valid and it opens nothing, and a day and not a figure is not a claim on a route and does not open one.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and that was done on the eleventh and nothing this morning improved it or undid it.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and the window of the office's work is shut and does not open again before the sixth of a month that is not inside this one.

The two boards were read out to each other in the yard at the eighth hour and the lamp went off the table at about the ninth hour, and the fold of paper is in the drawer with the other papers in it, and the day is in the book, and there is no figure anywhere in this holding that is the figure of that well and there never has been.

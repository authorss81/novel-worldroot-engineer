# Chapter 434

## Four Accounts, And Four Is Four

The seventeenth of this month was a Tuesday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The top dyke from the gate to the old sluice took six people from the second hour to the seventh hour, and the frost on the crown of it went off in the middle of the fourth hour in a band about a foot wide and about forty yards long, and nobody in this holding has written that band into a column and nobody is going to.

Marek was one of the six and had the bar for the last two hours and his forearm did not ache, and nobody in this holding has remarked on that this month and nobody is going to.

The wind was off the west end all morning and it came over the top of that dyke in a way that put the grit of the crown into the left eye of everybody who worked it, and the man at the gate end worked with his right hand shut most of the fifth hour, and one of the six said the word grit out loud and one of the other five heard him.

The two boards were read aloud at the seventh hour before either figure was named, one man to each, and the run of mornings is **sixty-nine of a hundred and twenty-one**, and the pair stands at **five hundred and eighty-one days** and **six hundred and twenty-eight**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened.

The seventeenth went in at about the ninth hour, an hour later than the day before, and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and ninety-nine hundredweight.**

Down five off Monday, and the hundred and ninety-seventh figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The fall went onto the falls. Eighty-seven and seventy-seven make a hundred and sixty-four.

The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and ninety-five days and was written for the hundred and ninety-fourth of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety-six.

A rider came up the fen road at about the seventh hour of the evening before with a roll of paper tied with a string, and it is still tied, and it is on the long table at the wall side with the string on it, and it was not opened in the yard.

The roll is a draft of the inspection protocols and it came from a clerk of the Assembly and it is in three parts and the third part is the part everybody will argue about. The clerk of this holding read the first two parts out at about the eighth hour in about nine people with the door shut and read the third part out afterwards and the third part is two clauses and neither of them is a surprise.

The first part is nine clauses and it says what an inspection is, which is a person from outside a node going to that node and reading what is written down about it. It says who pays for it, which is the body that asked for it. It says how long it takes, which is a day and a half, and it says that it is not a judgment, and about four people in that room liked that clause and about four thought that a thing which is not a judgment and takes a day and a half is going to be treated as a judgment by whoever is standing in the field when it turns up.

The second part is the part with the numbers in it, and there are four clauses in the second part, and the first of the four asks for a figure against every piece of work on a schedule, and the second asks for a unit against every figure, and the third asks for the date the figure was read, and the fourth is the one about the engineer of record.

There is no clause in either part that says what to do about a figure this holding cannot check, and there is no clause that says what to do about a thing this holding does not know, and about four people in that room noticed the two absences out loud and about four did not.

The two numbers this holding has handed out that it cannot check are two, and the first of the two is not named, and neither of the two is in that roll and neither of the two is in the schedule that the roll refers to, and a person reading that roll in about four years will not know that either of them exists.

The six things this holding does not know are six and no unit has ever been supplied for any of them, and a protocol is not a seventh of anything, and the room did not add one this evening and would not have known how to.

The fourth clause of the second part is this. That the engineer of record shall enter, against the work, what the work has cost him.

He read it out once and then read it out again because about four people in that room had not taken in the words of it the first time and about four had, and he read the second reading more slowly and did not explain either of them.

Marek gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in this holding's history is two hundred and eleven, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to refuse a single figure and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another, and I was asked for a fifth in a month that is now two months back and I refused it then and the reason I gave then is the reason I am giving now in the same words."** "A refusal is not a fifth account. The mark is four inches and it branches twice and it was four inches at the second hour this morning and it is four inches at the ninth hour tonight, and I am going to enter the four and I am going to enter them as four and I am not going to add them up and I am not going to pick one."

The man of about fifty had a sheet out and it was the fourth sheet of the same stock and there were four columns ruled on it and three of them had something in them and the fourth was empty, and he turned the sheet round so that the three of them could see it and did not say anything about the four columns.

**"There is a thing this room is going to want to do and I want to name it before anybody does it,"** he said, and he gave no reason in front of it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on that account. **"That is to write one of the four into the fourth column, and to enter it under a heading, and to let the other three stay in their book. That would be a fifth account. It would be a fifth account made out of a selection, and a selection of four is a decision, and the decision is mine and not this room's, and this holding has not got a column that says what my name is answerable for."**

Nobody in that room argued with him about it and about four people in that room had expected Marek to put a figure in the fourth column and about four had not, and neither of the two fours said a word and neither of them is going to.

He entered the four. He entered them in his own hand and he entered them in the order they stand in the middle book, and he read them back, and the man of about fifty read them out of the second book and the two of them were the same, and the page says four is four.

The third part of that roll is the part everybody will argue about and the clerk of this holding read both of its clauses out at about the ninth hour of the evening, and the room went quiet on the first one and did not go quiet on the second one.

The first clause is that each body entered on a schedule shall state its condition, and shall state in four ruled lines under that condition what it will be able to do in about four years.

The man of the north row with the cough was in that room. He had been in it since the fourth hour because the second line went past his row and he had nowhere to be until the seventh, and about four people in that room had thought about what was going to happen at the ninth hour and about four had not thought about it at all.

The clerk read the clause out and then read the four ruled lines out after it, and there were four ruled lines ruled under a space with a line under it for the condition, and the space was empty and the four lines were empty, and the clerk ruled them at about the ninth hour of the evening in a heavier hand than the rest of the sheet because the nib had worn down.

He asked the question the clause asks. He asked it once, in the words of the clause, and he did not put it in his own words as well, and about four people in that room noticed that he did not and about four did not notice it.

The man of the north row with the cough stood up. It took him longer than it takes most men and nobody in that room offered him the table and nobody in that room moved a chair.

**"I said in a month that is now three months back that I would say the same thing in the same words if I was asked a different way, and I have been asked a different way, by a piece of paper instead of a man, and I am going to say the same thing in the same words and then I am going to sit down."** "It is not about the wood and it is not about the road and it is not about how far it is. It is not about what I will be able to do in about four years either, and I would like nobody to ask me that and nobody has asked me that and I would like it to stay that way."

The count of that in this holding's history is his own and it moved this evening and it is not in the sentence this holding uses when it counts the reasons a person gives before a thing, and the man of the north row with the cough has no figure in that sentence and is not given one by this page.

Nobody improved it. Marek did not ask him a second question and did not ask him why not, and about four people in that room had expected him to and about four had not, and the clerk did not write anything down, and the four ruled lines under that space on that sheet are empty this morning and are empty tonight and have nothing in them and no unit against them.

The condition itself went in. The clerk wrote the condition in the line under the heading at about the ninth hour of the evening, in three words and a number, and the number is the standing condition of that arm and it is a fact about a body and not a figure of what the body will do, and the man in question read it and said it was right and said nothing else about it.

Nobody in that room put a figure on him. Nobody gave him a yard count and nobody gave him a silence count and the two of those have never been in this holding's book and are not going to be this month.

The clerk said the thing that the whole of that evening had been for, and he said it with the nib down and did not improve it after.

**"I am going to say the one thing I have to say about that column and I am not going to say it for the room. A condition with nothing under it is a question. This is the first time in four years that a question has been written on a sheet in this building in a form that is going to a body outside it, and it is going to be asked again on the twenty-first of this month, and if the four lines are still empty on the twenty-first then the answer is not late, it is that there is not one, and both of those are going to have to be said out loud to somebody who has not asked yet."**

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody in that room said the man of about fifty was right and nobody in that room said he was wrong, and the man of about fifty folded the sheet with the four accounts on it in half and put it in the second book and the count of accounts of that arm in this building is four and is not five and the four of them are not going to be reconciled by a protocol from a body nine miles off.

The clerk read the second part of that roll out again at about the tenth hour of the evening to the two people who had gone out at nine, and the two of them came back for it and one of them was the woman who keeps the seed house and the other was the man of about seventy-four with a stick, and neither of the two said anything when it was read and the man of about seventy-four with a stick asked one question about the day and a half and was told that a day and a half is a day and a half and is not two days and does not become two days if a body is in a bad mood on the second one.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and is the same four inches at the ninth hour as at the second, and a protocol is not a mark, a refusal is not a mark, a condition is not a mark, a clause is not a mark and a ruled line with nothing under it is not a mark, and there are four accounts and there are four and that is the whole of the arithmetic.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and was turned at the top of the yard, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it, and eleven journeys is a floor, and the second of the four lines in his own book is eighty-five days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in eighty-five days.

The register form is on the middle table at ninety-one days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it. The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, and a roll of paper tied with a string is not that instrument.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year, and the other five of the six blank columns beside it are headed nothing and the sixth has a heading and nothing under it.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing, and a condition is not a measurement either and the two of those are not added to each other.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and it opens nothing, and a draft that says a body shall state a condition says nothing about a route and does not open one.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained this morning, and the ground on both sides of it is going wrong and going slower and nobody in this holding has called slower better.

The two boards were read out at the seventh hour and the ninth-day return is not until the day after tomorrow, and the lamp went off the table at about the tenth hour and the roll is still tied on the long table at the wall side and the string is still on it.

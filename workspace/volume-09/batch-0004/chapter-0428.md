# Chapter 428

## A Heading Is Necessary And It Is Not Sufficient

The eleventh of this month was a Wednesday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The foot of the west dyke and the drain out of the low quarter took five people from the second hour to the sixth hour, and the toe at the foot of that dyke is up two fingers off what it was on the third of a month that is now a month back, and the man at the front of it said the sentence about a toe that has started, and this time two of the three behind him wrote it down on the back of a hand.

Nobody has ever asked whether that sentence is true and nobody is going to, and the three who wrote it down have not seen each other since the sixth hour.

Marek was one of the five and had the bar for the whole of it, and his forearm did not ache, and about four people in this holding have stopped mentioning that it does not and about four have not stopped.

The boards were read aloud at the seventh hour, and reading them came before either figure was named, and both of the men who take those boards were in the building and had been since the second hour. **Five hundred and seventy-five days** and **six hundred and twenty-two**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer stayed shut.

Sixty-six mornings out of a hundred and fifteen, and about four people in this holding have wanted for four years to be told what the second of those two figures is a count of, and about four have stopped wanting, and neither of the two fours has been asked and the wanting did not stop on the same morning.

The eleventh went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and eighty-seven hundredweight.**

Down five off Tuesday, and the hundred and ninety-first figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The fall went onto the falls, so the window stands at eighty-four rises and seventy-four falls, and the two of them add to a hundred and fifty-eight. The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and eighty-nine days and was written for the hundred and eighty-eighth of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and they do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page along with everything else that went on it. This is the thirty-fourth morning of the run out of the twenty-sixth of a month five months back and the twenty-first morning of the run anchored on the eighteenth of a month three months back, and both of those numbers have stood where the eighth of this month left them and neither moved this morning, because the fourth line did not come round and the two of them only move on the mornings it does.

The window of the office's work shut at the sixth hour of the evening. It was open for six days, the sixth to the eleventh, and it was the same six days the office's own housekeeping puts on the same day of every month, and nothing was put in it this month and nothing was taken out of it, and the column for a thing that goes to an office has still not been written and is not going to be written this month.

Then three things happened between the sixth hour and the seventh, and about nine people were in the one room and the door was shut, and the clerk of this holding was in it and the man of about fifty was in it and Marek was in it and the man of the north row with the cough was not in it and has not been in that room in eleven years.

The first thing was done at the ninth hundred yards, on a door with seven ruled columns on it, and one of the seven has a figure in it and the other six have nothing at the head of them and have had nothing at the head of them since the door was painted.

The seventh column stands at fifty-six. It was read on the thirtieth of a month that is now a month back and on the first of this month and on no other morning in four years, and it was not read this morning and nobody in this building is going to read it again before the turn of the next month.

The clerk wrote a heading at the head of the sixth column, and the heading takes a count of readings, and the sixth column has no figure in it this morning or any other morning, and the heading is at the top of it and the number is not, and nobody in this holding has ever put a number under that heading and nobody is going to on a morning when there is nothing to count.

A heading on the sixth column is not a heading on the seventh column. The seventh still counts readings and what it counts besides readings is not answered this morning and has not been answered on any morning in four years, and this is said once, in this room, this afternoon, and is not going to be said a second time in this building.

The second thing was done at the middle table. The sheet of terms of the twenty-first has five terms in two hands and a third column ruled and empty, and the man of about fifty declined to write in that third column in a month that is now five months back with the reason given first, and this morning the clerk wrote a heading at the head of that third column, and the column is still empty, and the heading is the first thing that has ever been at the top of it.

The five terms that were declined as offered, four years and a half back, were not in that room this morning and they are not improved and they are not unwithdrawn and they are not added to, and no sixth term was added to anything by anybody in this building in this month, and the clerk did not bring them and was not asked to.

The third thing was done at the long table at the wall side. There is a column in the second book in which a man of about seventy has been entered twenty-four times and there is a number in it and no heading at the top of it, and the clerk wrote a heading at the head of that column this afternoon, and the number is still twenty-four, and the man has not been fetched since the first of this month and is not fetched this month, and the twenty-fifth falls on the first of the next.

Nobody in this building has ever asked that man which number it is and nobody is going to, and no page in this holding gives a reason he is brought on the first of a month, and a heading does not make the question any more welcome. It makes it a question in writing, which is a thing a person can now see without having to come into a room, and about four people in this building have thought that was an improvement and about four have thought it was the opposite, and neither of those two fours has been asked.

Harlan Vetch was in that room and said one thing about the third of the three and gave no reason for it in front of it, and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on account of it.

**"There is a difference between a column with a heading and a room with a person in it, and the difference took this holding four years to find and about four minutes to write down this afternoon, and I have held an end of a thing since the first of this month with no document and no heading and nobody's name on it, and I am not complaining, and I want the clerk to know that the sheet is a worse place to be than the room was."** "I am not saying do not write them. I am saying that a heading is a promise about a shape and not about a person, and a person who comes into a room is doing something a piece of paper cannot do, and I have been in about nine of these rooms in four years and I have never once been asked whether the thing I was in was mine."

Not one person in that room agreed with him and not one disagreed, and the man of about fifty wrote all of it down and read it back and did not improve it, and then he put his own pencil down and did not pick it up again for about four minutes.

The man of about fifty gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and twenty-eight, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the whole of what those three headings are and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that four years of this holding has gone about four hundred mornings without a word at the top of a column, and a body of people in a county asked us last Sunday who holds the pen, and I have been the man with the book for eleven years and I did not have an answer that was better than that one."** "A heading is necessary. I have got a sheet on that table with nine columns on it and it is the first thing in four years that anybody outside this building can check, and it is necessary, and I am not going to stand in a room and be coy about it. A heading is also not sufficient, and it stopped being sufficient about four years ago, and the two halves of that sentence are both four years old and neither of them is new this afternoon."

Then the mail came.

The clerk of one of the four counties had sent the holding's own copy of the nine columns back up the fen road with the bag, and it had come in on the Tuesday morning, and it was on the long table at the wall side with a piece of paper laid over the sixth column and a line drawn under the piece of paper, and the clerk took the piece of paper away at about the seventh hour of the evening in front of about nine people.

> THE HOLDING'S OWN COPY OF THE LIST OF NINE COLUMNS, RETURNED BY THE ORDINARY POST ON THE TENTH OF THIS MONTH BY THE CLERK OF THE SECOND COUNTY, WHO HAS WRITTEN A HEADING AT THE HEAD OF THE SIXTH COLUMN OF HIS OWN COPY AND HAS SENT IT BACK TO BE READ. THE HEADING IS FIVE WORDS AND IT SAYS WHO ORDERED THE DISCONNECTION. THE SECOND COUNTY HAS WRITTEN NO NAME UNDER IT. THE SECOND COUNTY HAS WRITTEN ON THE BACK OF THE SHEET THAT IT CANNOT FIND A NAME AND THAT IT IS NOT THE SECOND COUNTY'S WORK TO FIND ONE, AND THAT IT IS SENDING THE SHEET BACK SO THAT THE HEADING IS IN FRONT OF THE PEOPLE WHO HAVE TO LOOK AT IT RATHER THAN IN FRONT OF THE CLERK WHO HAS TO READ IT. THE SECOND COUNTY HAS NOT ASKED FOR AN ANSWER AND HAS NOT SAID IT WAS REFUSING ONE.

The man of about fifty entered the heading in the second book at about the seventh hour of the evening, in the words it was written in, under the heading of his own column, and nobody wrote a name under it.

A heading is not an answer. That is said once in this building this evening and is not going to be said a second time in this room, and the man of about fifty said it and then read the whole of the entry back twice without improving a word of it, and the second time he read it he stopped after the heading and sat with his thumb on the page for about four seconds and then carried on.

Nobody in that room gave a reason for the thing they said next, and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move for any of them.

The reader of this body said the sentence that the day had actually turned on, and he gave the reason for saying it first, and the count of that in the holding's history is two hundred and six, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say the reason I put my own name on a sheet in this building in a month that is now four months back, with four lines each side of it, and I am giving the reason first because a heading went into a column this afternoon and a name went under a heading in the same month for the first time in four years and I am the only name on any of them."** "I did that because this holding keeps four books and no heading on any of them, and because in about four years somebody is going to find a page with a name at the top of it and no reason under it and they are going to ask what the name was doing there. The name was doing the only thing a name on a page can do. It was standing in front of a thing that was not written down. That is all a name has ever been able to do and I would like it on the page that it is not enough either, and it is not enough, and a heading is not enough, and I am not going to pretend that writing a name under somebody else's heading this morning would have made it so."

The room did not find for him and did not find against him, and nobody in it said the sentence again, and nobody thanked him, and the man of about fifty put the book away and did not sign the day sheet and the clerk did not ask him to.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and came down it empty, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it and the second of the four lines in his own book is seventy-nine days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody in this building has asked him about it in seventy-nine days.

The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, and three headings went into this building today and that piece of paper is not one of them and is not going to be.

The register form is on the middle table at eighty-five days, not filled, not refused, and nothing at the head of it, and three words at the top of three columns in this building this afternoon did not travel two feet to that form.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and the word has never once been wrong about the figure and it measures nothing, and no heading in this building this afternoon made it a measurement of anything.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page, and it was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained this morning. Three headings went into this building between the ninth hundred yards and the middle table this afternoon, and none of the three of them has put a word in the middle of that ring, and none of the three of them is going to, and about four years of this holding has found that out the hard way on other ground.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice. A heading is not a mark, a name is not a mark, a heading is necessary and it is not sufficient, and four accounts of that arm are kept in this building and they do not reconcile, and nothing said in that room this evening is a fifth of anything.

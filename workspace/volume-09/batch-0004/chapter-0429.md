# Chapter 429

## Eighty-Seven And Twenty-Nine

The twelfth of this month was a Thursday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the fifty-eighth morning.

The rota stands at eighty-seven mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-eight not, and twenty-nine and fifty-eight are eighty-seven.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and a field that has waited fifty-eight mornings has not been waited at by anybody in particular.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-fifth morning of the run out of the twenty-sixth of a month five months back, and the twenty-second morning of the run anchored on the eighteenth of a month three months back, and the difference between them is thirteen and has been thirteen for four years.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. The two men who take the boards were both in the one room with the sheet from the seventh hour and did not come out, and the boards are read at the seventh hour, and a holding that has spent four days writing nine headings is not going to break a practice of four years to be tidy about one morning.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and seventy-six days** and **six hundred and twenty-three**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not going to be.

The six who would have gone to the headland were on the fen side of the top dyke with a barrow they were not supposed to have out, and Marek was one of the six and said nothing about it, and the man who took it out said afterwards that it was the only barrow in this holding on the ground when nobody had told it to be, and that he had put it back himself at the seventh hour.

The twelfth went in at about the eighth hour with five of the nine places on that sheet still open, and came back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and ninety-six hundredweight.**

Up nine off Wednesday, and the hundred and ninety-second figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and eleven of the last twelve have been above the figure before them, which is a thing about a fortnight and not a thing about a season.

The rise went onto the rises. Eighty-five and seventy-four make a hundred and fifty-nine, and that window is four hundred and eighty-three mornings wide and has nothing to do with the nine columns on the long table.

Four bodies of households have gone a hundred and ninety days without a move in their aggregate, and the morning sentence is the hundred and eighty-ninth of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety-one.

The list was finished at about the sixth hour of the afternoon on the ninth, when the clerk ruled a line under the fourth row of it, and it has been in this room every working morning since, and this morning five people in it went down the rows one at a time and said out loud what was wrong with each one, and it took until the seventh hour and it was not a tidy thing to be in a room for.

There are nine places on that sheet where it is wrong. The man of about fifty got to the first of them at about the fourth hour and read three more of them before the light went off that part of the table, and said he was going to do the rest one at a time on the mornings after this one so that nobody in the room had to be told nine things at once, and four of the nine had a small cross in the margin by the seventh hour and five of them did not.

The first one is the second row.

The second row of that sheet says THE SEED WORK, in the clerk's hand, in the second column, in capital letters, and in the first column of that row there is a place, and in the third column there is a name, and in the sixth column, the one that says WHETHER THEY WERE ASKED, there is nothing at all.

> THE ROW AS IT STANDS AT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWELFTH, IN THE CLERK'S HAND, AND THE COLUMN HEADINGS ARE NOT REPEATED HERE BECAUSE NOBODY IN THAT ROOM HAD TO ASK WHAT THEY WERE. THE FIRST COLUMN, WHICH IS THE WORK, SAYS THE SEED WORK. THE SECOND COLUMN, WHICH IS THE PLACE, HAS THE NAME OF THE PLACE IN IT. THE THIRD COLUMN, WHICH IS THE PERSON WHO DID IT, HAS A NAME IN IT, AND THAT NAME IS TOVA REED'S, TAKEN OFF A PAGE IN THE MIDDLE BOOK ON THE EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH. THE FOURTH COLUMN, WHAT IT COST, HAS A FIGURE IN IT IN SEED AND NOT IN ANY OTHER UNIT, AND THE FIGURE IS ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY HUNDREDWEIGHT, AND IT IS OF A SEASON OF DISCONNECTION ACROSS THE FOUR COUNTIES AND IS NOT OF A SEASON AND IS NOT OF A HARVEST AND IS NOT OF A GROUND. THE FIFTH COLUMN, THE READING AND WHEN, IS EMPTY. THE SIXTH COLUMN, WHETHER THEY WERE ASKED, IS EMPTY. THE SEVENTH AND EIGHTH COLUMNS CARRY THE TWO FIGURES THIS HOLDING CANNOT CHECK AND THE SIX THINGS THIS HOLDING DOES NOT KNOW, AND NEITHER OF THOSE IS THE SEED WORK. THE NINTH COLUMN IS THE BACK OF A FORM WITH FOUR LINES ON IT AND CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE FIRST LINE AND THREE EMPTY LINES UNDER IT, AND THE FIGURE ON THAT LINE IS THE TWENTY-SECOND RETURN AND IS NOT THE SEED WORK.

Nobody in that room had asked her. The name in the third column of that row had been in that holding's books for nine years in a book nobody in that room had opened that week, and the clerk had taken the name off a page in the middle book on the eighth of this month at about the seventh hour of the evening, when a woman at the table put her thumb on the sixth column and asked for it, and the name in that third column is Tova Reed's, and the figure is the right figure and the place is the right place.

A row with a name in it and nothing in the column that says whether the person was asked is a row where nobody has agreed to anything.

Tova Reed was in that room and had been in it since the second hour, and she read the row twice and then put her thumb on the sixth column and did not say anything for about a minute, and the room waited.

Then she gave the reason before she said the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is forty-one, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to give you the figure I have been asked for three times in nine days, and I am going to give the reason for giving it first, and the reason is that you have put my name in a row and a heading over it and the only thing in that row that is wrong is the empty space where the asking would go."** "The figure is one thousand one hundred and sixty hundredweight of seed. That is what a season of disconnection costs the four counties in seed, and it is out of four accounts I keep and the four of them do not agree at the second figure, and this is the one I will stand behind, and the other three are in my book and are not in that column and are not going to be put there by me this afternoon."

**"It is seed. It is not the season and it is not the harvest and it is not a ground and it is not a figure of people. If anybody in this room puts it against a rate of any kind I will know, and I will say so out loud, and I will keep saying so until it comes off."**

Nobody in that room said thank you. Nobody put a hand on her arm. Nobody said he was sorry about anything, and nobody has said he was sorry about anything in eleven years and there was no reason in that room this afternoon for anybody to start. Nobody took the seed work off her and nobody is going to, and nobody in that room suggested it, and two of them wanted to say something and two of them did not and neither pair has ever been asked.

The clerk wrote the figure into the fourth column in his own hand and read it back and the man of about fifty read it out of the second book and the two of them were the same, and the sixth column of that row is still empty, and the empty space is now the only thing anybody in four counties can check on that row.

Marek said one thing about that and gave the reason for it first, and the count of that in the holding's history is two hundred and seven, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what the empty space is and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that a body of people asked us in a month that is now a month back whether a person would be sent and this holding said nobody would be, and the reason that answer was right is that a person who is sent has already been decided about."** "You cannot fix that row by asking her. Asking her is what puts her name there as a thing she agreed to, and she has not agreed to it, and if you ask her this afternoon she will say yes, because she has been doing it for nine years and saying no would be a different job than the one she has. So the column stays empty, and it stays empty in front of four counties, and that is the first honest thing this list has ever put on a page."

Nobody in that room improved that either.

The clerk put a cross in the margin beside the second row at about the seventh hour, and the mark is a small cross and it is not a tick, and there are now four small crosses in the margin of that sheet, one for the first of the nine places and three for the places the man of about fifty read out loud after the seventh hour and before the room emptied, and the eighth and the ninth are not marked yet and are going to be read out on the mornings after this one.

Marek looked at the four crosses for a while and said that four crosses in a margin was the first thing on that sheet anybody in this building had ever made that was not a figure and not a name, and that this holding has had a rule about the margin of a page and the rule was that nothing goes in it, and that the four crosses had broken it and he was not going to be the one to say it should not have been broken, and that the sixth column was still empty from the top of the sheet to the bottom of it and the crosses were in the margin and not in the column and the two were not going to be confused by anybody in this room.

The woman who keeps the seed house was in the room and had her own four columns under the list and had said nothing for two hours, and the second column of that row is not her building and does not have her name in it, and what she said at about the seventh hour was short, and she gave no reason in front of it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on account of it.

**"I have kept four columns for nine years and I have never once been asked whether they were the right four, and this afternoon a woman put a figure in a column in front of nine people and about four of them did not say thank you and I want it written down that I noticed which four."**

The clerk of this holding read that back and did not put her name in the third column of any row of that sheet, and she is not on it, and she did not ask to be.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and went down it empty and eleven journeys is a floor. The man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it and the second of the four lines in his own book is eighty days old this morning and has nothing on it and nobody in this holding has asked him about it.

The window of the office's work shut yesterday at the sixth hour of the evening, and six days is what it is, and nothing has ever been carried out of one in four counties.

The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date, no name and no line for a name, and it is not a column and it is not a row and nobody put it on the long table this morning.

The register form is on the middle table at eighty-six days and nothing was written at the head of it, and the seventh column of the well house door is at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and the six columns beside it now have one word at the top of the sixth of them and nothing at the top of the other five, and that happened on the eleventh and is not going to be improved this morning.

The compost line is paid at twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and the ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year with no dimension on any page, and a sheet with nine columns on it is not a measurement of the ground and not a reason to walk anything.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular, and the order sealing the route is valid and opens nothing. The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice, and a figure of seed is not a mark, a column is not a mark, and a refusal to put a name under a heading is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm are four and they are not reconciled and were not reconciled this afternoon by anybody.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the twelfth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FOURTH LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WENT AWAY EMPTY, THOSE FIELDS HAVING WAITED FOR THE FIFTY-EIGHTH MORNING, AND THE ROTA STANDS AT EIGHTY-SEVEN IN FORCE WITH TWENTY-NINE TAKEN AND FIFTY-EIGHT NOT. THE TWO COUNTS OF THAT ROTATION ARE THIRTY-FIVE AND TWENTY-TWO AND THEY DO NOT AGREE. THE BOARDS WERE NOT READ ALOUD AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THE RUN, BOTH MEN BEING IN THE ONE ROOM WITH THE SHEET FROM THE SEVENTH HOUR. THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX DAYS AND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE THIRD LAUNDER IS EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIX AND IS THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SECOND FIGURE OF THE SERIES. THE WINDOW STANDS AT EIGHTY-FIVE RISES AND SEVENTY-FOUR FALLS AND THE TWO ADD. THE LIST OF NINE COLUMNS WAS FINISHED ON THE NINTH AND HAS BEEN IN THE ROOM EVERY WORKING MORNING SINCE, AND THIS MORNING WAS THE MORNING THE NINE PLACES WERE BEGUN, ONE AT A TIME. THE FIRST OF THE NINE IS THE SECOND ROW, WHICH IS THE SEED WORK, AND THE COLUMN THAT SAYS WHETHER THEY WERE ASKED IS EMPTY IN THAT ROW, AND NOBODY HAD ASKED HER. THE SEEDWRIGHT GAVE THE FIGURE AND GAVE THE REASON FIRST, AND THE FIGURE IS ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY HUNDREDWEIGHT OF SEED FOR A SEASON OF DISCONNECTION ACROSS THE FOUR COUNTIES, OUT OF FOUR ACCOUNTS THAT DO NOT AGREE AT THE SECOND FIGURE, AND THIS IS THE ONE SHE WILL STAND BEHIND, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IN THE HOLDING'S HISTORY IS FORTY-ONE. NOBODY THANKED HER, NOBODY PUT A HAND ON HER ARM, NOBODY SAID HE WAS SORRY, AND NOBODY TOOK THE SEED WORK OFF HER. THE READER OF THIS BODY GAVE THE REASON FIRST AND SAID THAT THE COLUMN CANNOT BE FIXED BY ASKING HER THIS AFTERNOON, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVEN. THE SIXTH COLUMN OF THAT ROW IS STILL EMPTY. THE NINTH COLUMN IS THE BACK OF A FORM WITH FOUR LINES AND CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT ON THE FIRST LINE AND THREE EMPTY ONES, AND A FORM IS NOT A COLUMN. THE COUNT OF REQUESTS IS FIFTY-THREE AND THE SECTION-NINE NOTES ARE FIFTY-THREE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

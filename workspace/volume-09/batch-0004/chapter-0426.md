# Chapter 426

## Who Holds The Pen

The ninth of this month was a Monday, and the first line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The top dyke between the gate and the old sluice took six people from the second hour to the sixth hour, and the top half of it was like iron and the bottom half was mud to the ankle, and the five who could use a bar used one and the sixth carried, and the carrying is the harder job and everybody in this holding knows it and nobody has ever written it in a column.

Marek was one of the five with a bar and not the sixth, and the reason for that is written down and the reason is that the sixth position on the first line has gone to the man of about thirty-one of Silling every Monday since a Monday in a month that is now four months back. The frost had got into the bottom half of that dyke overnight and the six of them broke about nine feet of the face out of it by hand before anybody thought of a bar, and Marek said nothing about the order of those nine feet and about four people have said afterwards that they would have.

The two boards were read aloud at the seventh hour and reading them came before either figure was named. One man took the first and another took the second. **Five hundred and seventy-three days** and **six hundred and twenty**, forty-seven between them, and the drawer stayed shut.

Sixty-five mornings out of a hundred and thirteen, and about four people in this holding have told one of the two men who takes those boards that he is slow and about four have told him that he is not, and he is the same speed he has always been and neither of those fours has ever been asked why they said it.

The ninth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same, and the man of about fifty read it back with the frayed corner and did not say the figure out loud, because nobody in the room asked him to and he has not done it on his own since a spring.

**Eight hundred and eighty-three hundredweight.**

Down five off Sunday, and the hundred and eighty-ninth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The fall went onto the falls, so the window stands at eighty-three rises and seventy-three falls, and the two of them add to a hundred and fifty-six, and the clause on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and eighty-seven days and was written for the hundred and eighty-sixth of those mornings out of a hundred and eighty-eight.

The letter came up the fen road on Sunday night in the bag and was in the room at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the door shut. The clerk of the fourth county wrote it himself, and that clerk is not a member of anything and is not answerable to anybody in the county, and he said so on the day he wrote the first of his letters and he has not stopped saying it since.

> FROM THE CLERK OF THE FOURTH COUNTY, DATED THE EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH, SENT BY THE ORDINARY POST AND RECEIVED IN THE BAG ON SUNDAY NIGHT, READ ALOUD IN THE ONE ROOM AT THE FOURTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON OF THE NINTH: THE CLERK ASKS THE HOLDING TO TELL HIM, IN ONE LINE, WHO HOLDS THE PEN, THAT IS TO SAY WHICH PERSON SIGNS THE BOOK INTO WHICH A COUNTY WRITES A THING IT CANNOT CHECK, AND HE ASKS BECAUSE HE HAS NOW HAD A SHEET FROM THIS HOLDING WITH NINE COLUMNS ON IT AND NO NAME ANYWHERE ON IT, AND HE SAYS THAT HE HAS BEEN KEEPING FOUR BOOKS FOR ELEVEN YEARS AND THAT EVERY ONE OF THE FOUR IS SIGNED BY SOMEBODY, AND THAT HE HAS SPENT A WEEK LOOKING FOR THE SOMEBODY ON THAT SHEET AND COULD NOT FIND IT, AND THAT HE IS NOT ACCUSING ANYBODY OF ANYTHING AND IS ASKING BECAUSE HE HAS TO PUT A NAME IN A CELL OF A BOOK OF HIS OWN AND HE HAS NOTHING TO PUT IN IT, AND HE RECORDS THAT HIS COUNTY HAS ASKED FOR NOBODY TO BE SENT AND IS NOT ASKING FOR ONE NOW.

About nine people were in the room and nobody spoke for about a minute and a half.

At the end of it the man of about fifty put the frayed corner down and said one word, which was obviously, and then did not say anything else, and about four people in that room looked at each other and about four looked at the sheet.

**"There is no obviously in that letter,"** he said. **"I want to be exact, because in about four years somebody is going to read what I said this afternoon and I would rather it were the true sentence. The man is not asking a question he thinks he knows the answer to. He is asking a question he has to put a name into a cell about, and a name into a cell is not the same thing as a question, and it is the cell that is the trouble and not him."**

The clerk of this holding asked what the difference was, and it was the first question anybody asked in that room on that afternoon, and it was asked in the voice of a man who has a sheet in front of him with no name on it.

**"A question can be answered in words,"** the man of about fifty said. **"A cell wants a thing that goes in it and comes out of it again the same shape, and a name is the only thing anybody has ever found that does that, and that is why he asked and it is not because he thinks anybody here is hiding."**

The woman who keeps the seed house said one sentence from the far side of the table, and she gave no reason in front of it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on account of it.

**"I have signed a sheet every week for nine years and there is not one of them with my name at the top of it and about nine people in four counties have read one of mine this year and thought they knew what I think,"** she said. **"I would not tell him to put my name in the cell. I would tell him what the clerk of this holding is going to say, and the clerk of this holding is going to say it better than I have."**

Marek had been in the room since the fourth hour and had not said anything in it, and at that point he said the thing that made the argument a list instead of a complaint, and he gave no reason for it in front of it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move this afternoon on that account either.

**"He has found the first thing in four years that he cannot do and it is not a figure and it is not a rate and it is not a council, it is a name, and he is not the first man in four counties to arrive at this building and find that out."** "Nine columns is the right number for what this building does and it is a poor number for what the man in that letter is doing, and the two of those are not going to be fixed by another column. What would fix it is somebody asking him, and that is a thing a person does and not a thing a sheet does, and a sheet cannot do it and a sheet that appears to have done it has lied to about four counties."

About four people in that room thought that was the best sentence anybody had said in the fortnight and about four thought it was a way of not writing a fifth column, and neither of the two fours has ever been asked.

The answer went up the fen road the same evening in the clerk of this holding's own hand, and it is one line and it is a true one, and it does not contain a name.

**"That four books are kept in this holding and that five people write in them, and that no person in this holding holds the pen in the sense the inquirer asks, and that the second book is the one a county writes a thing it cannot check in, and that the figures in it are the figures of the person who wrote them and not of a body, and that a name may be put against any figure in it by the person who wrote the figure and by nobody else."**

The clerk read that back to the man from the fourth county at the sixth hour of the evening, on the fen road, at the gate, in the rain, before he got on the cart, and the man from the fourth county read it twice himself with his thumb on the frame of the page and then put it inside his coat against his chest and did not say thank you. About four people standing in the yard have wondered since whether that was insolence and about four have wondered whether it was the only sensible thing available to a man who has to go home and fill in a cell.

Nobody in this holding gave a reason before a thing this afternoon and this page carries no figure for that, and the absence is not an oversight. The man of about fifty entered a line under the answer saying that no reason had been given and why, which is that a letter asking a shape does not want a reason and gets one anyway if anybody offers it.

The count of times a body has asked who holds the pen is one, and it was one before the clerk of the fourth county put that letter in the bag on Sunday night, and the reason it is still one is that a letter is a question and not a body, and this holding has never kept a column for questions asked in writing and is not going to open one in a fortnight to hold a single figure.

The sheet with nine columns on it was on the table the whole of that afternoon with the fourth row of it empty, and the fourth row is the headland above Marden, and at about the fifth hour the clerk ruled a line under that row and wrote nothing on it, and the man of about fifty entered the name of the man of about thirty-one of Silling in the third column of it, which is the person who did it, and left the sixth column of it empty, being the one that says whether they were asked.

Nobody in that room had asked him. He did not carry the sixth position on the first line that morning, he carried it, and the row he went into is not about this morning. He had said in a month that is now five months back that the headland should be worked, and the reason for that is a page in the second book, and the page says that he offered and it does not say that he was asked, and there is a difference and this holding has never written the difference down anywhere until this afternoon and has not written it down yet.

The barrow went up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and came down it empty, and eleven journeys is a floor. The man of about thirty-one of Silling had it, and he had carried the sixth position on the first line that morning, and the second of the four lines in his own book is seventy-seven days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody in this building has asked him about it in seventy-seven days and nobody is going to this month. He was not in the room when his name went into the third column of the fourth row and he was told about it on the Thursday, and he said nothing that anybody wrote down.

On the low board, face up, is an offer nobody has filled and nobody has withdrawn and nobody has dated, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back. It is not the answer to the letter from the fourth county and it was not sent with the answer, and there is no second paper of it on any table in this holding this season.

The register form stands on the middle table at eighty-three days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and the clerk looked at it while he was writing out the answer to the fen road and did not touch it.

The seventh column of the well house door is at fifty-six and was not read this morning, and it is read on the thirtieth and on the first and on nothing else in the year. The six columns beside it are headed nothing and no word went to the top of any of them today.

The compost line is paid, the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one, the word has never once been wrong about it, and a pen is not a measurement of the ground.

The man of about seventy is at twenty-four fetchings and is not fetched today, and the twenty-fifth falls on the first of the next, and no page in this holding has ever given a reason a man is brought on a first of a month.

The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained by anybody in this room today, and the ground on both sides of it is still going wrong and is going slower, and nobody here has said that slower is better.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it says about itself. The order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and it opens nothing, and a question about a pen is not a question about a corridor and nobody in this room pretended this morning that it was one.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice, and a name is not a mark and a letter is not a mark and a question is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile, and nothing put in a cell of a county's book is a fifth of them.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the eighth hour of the evening of the ninth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE FIRST LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE TOP DYKE BETWEEN THE GATE AND THE OLD SLUICE, SIX PEOPLE TO THE SIXTH HOUR. THE BOARDS WERE READ ALOUD AT THE SEVENTH HOUR AND THE RUN IS SIXTY-FIVE OF A HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN, AND THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-THREE DAYS AND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE THIRD LAUNDER IS EIGHT HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE AND IS THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINTH FIGURE OF THE SERIES. THE WINDOW STANDS AT EIGHTY-THREE RISES AND SEVENTY-THREE FALLS AND THE TWO ADD. THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAVE NOT MOVED IN A HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVEN DAYS, WRITTEN FOR THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIXTH OF THOSE MORNINGS OUT OF A HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHT. THE CLERK OF THE FOURTH COUNTY ASKED IN ONE LINE WHO HOLDS THE PEN, AND SAID HE WAS ASKING BECAUSE HE HAD A SHEET WITH NINE COLUMNS ON IT AND NO NAME ON IT, AND SAID HE HAD NOTHING TO PUT IN A CELL OF HIS OWN BOOK, AND SAID HE WAS NOT ACCUSING ANYBODY. THIS IS THE FIRST TIME THAT QUESTION HAS COME INTO THIS BUILDING FROM OUTSIDE IT. THE ANSWER WENT UP THE FEN ROAD THE SAME EVENING IN ONE LINE, IN THE CLERK'S OWN HAND, AND IT IS TRUE, AND IT CONTAINS NO NAME. THE COUNT OF BODIES THAT HAVE ASKED IT IS ONE BEFORE AND ONE AFTER, AND A LETTER IS A QUESTION AND NOT A BODY. NOBODY IN THIS ROOM GAVE A REASON BEFORE A THING THIS AFTERNOON AND THIS PAGE CARRIES NO FIGURE FOR THAT. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ENTERED THE MISSING FOURTH ROW OF THE LIST AND ENTERED THE NAME OF A MAN WHO IS NOT A MEMBER OF THIS HOLDING AND DID NOT KNOW ON THE PREVIOUS EVENING THAT HE WAS GOING TO BE IN A LIST. THE COUNT OF REQUESTS IS FIFTY-THREE AND THE SECTION-NINE NOTES ARE FIFTY-THREE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

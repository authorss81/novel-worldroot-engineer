# Chapter 432

## Nine Places, And The Same Kind Of Thing

The fifteenth of this month was a Sunday, and the third line of the rota came round at the second hour and was worked. The low drain from the north end to the sluice took five people from the second hour to the sixth hour, and the whole length of it took four hours and a half for the second time in eleven years, and the fifth man said the two figures out loud to nobody and neither of the other four heard him.

Marek was one of the five and had the bar for three of the four hours and a shovel for one, and his forearm did not ache, and nobody in this holding has remarked on that this month and nobody is going to.

The two boards were read aloud at the seventh hour, before either figure was named, and both of the men who take them were in the building and had been all day. **Five hundred and seventy-nine days** and **six hundred and twenty-six**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer stayed shut.

Sixty-eight mornings out of a hundred and nineteen, and that is the last of them in this month, and the first of them in this month was sixty-four out of a hundred and eleven on the seventh, and the difference is four on both halves and nobody in this holding has found a use for that either.

The fifteenth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the other book, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and ninety-five hundredweight.**

Down five off Saturday, and the hundred and ninety-fifth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began, and it is the hundred and ninety-fifth and there is no hundred and ninety-sixth in this month.

The fall went onto the falls, so the window stands at eighty-six rises and seventy-six falls, and the two of them add to a hundred and sixty-two. The sentence on this morning's sheet says the four bodies of households have not moved in a hundred and ninety-three days and was written for the hundred and ninety-second of those mornings out of a hundred and ninety-four.

The rota stood this morning where the Thursday left it, at eighty-seven mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-eight not, and the two counts of that rotation stood where the Thursday left them, at thirty-five and twenty-two, and neither of the four moved this morning because the fourth line did not come round, and nobody in this holding has blamed a field for that either.

The list was finished on the ninth and it has been in the one room every working morning since, and this morning the five of them who began it on the Sunday read the last of the nine places out loud, and about nine people were in the room and the door was shut and nobody went out of it between the fourth hour and the seventh.

There are nine places where it is wrong and every one of the nine is the same kind of thing.

The top dyke from the gate to the old sluice. The seed work. The low drain from the north end to the sluice. The headland above Marden. The west side of the boundary ditch. The dial at the node. The well house. The compost. The two-foot gap at the node end.

Nine duties, nine headings, and a sixth column that says WHETHER THEY WERE ASKED, and that column is empty from the top of the sheet to the bottom of it, and there are four small crosses in the margin, one beside each of the four rows that have been read out loud, and the other five rows have nothing beside them at all.

Nobody asked any of the nine. That is not a discovery and nobody in that room made it as one. It is the thing the sheet was built to say out loud, and the sixth column was put there on the eighth of this month by a seedwright who said the column was the only one of the nine that was about a person and not about a ground, and the clerk ruled it a second time in a heavier hand, and on the ninth the clerk filled the fourth row of it with a name without putting anything in the sixth.

The man of about fifty read the nine out at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and read them in the same order every time and did not add a tenth, and when he got to the ninth he stopped for about four seconds and then read it, and the two-foot gap at the node end is open and has been open all season and nothing has gone into it and the valve has not been opened and the key is in somebody else's pocket about four feet from where the valve is.

Then the room did the argument, and it took about two hours, and it was the argument of the whole of the last four days and nobody in it was the same person they had been on the eighth.

The argument is this and there is no other one available to them this morning.

A heading is necessary. A sheet with nine columns on it is the first document in four years in this holding that anybody outside the building can check, and the clerk of the fourth county has it against his chest in a cart and a clerk in a town nine miles off has written a heading on his own copy of it and sent it back to be read, and neither of those two things could have happened if the eleventh of this month had gone on with six columns headed nothing.

A heading is not sufficient. It is not sufficient this morning, in front of nine people, about nine duties, and the proof of that is the sixth column of that sheet, which is nine places long and has nothing in any of them, and both halves of that sentence are four years old and neither of them is new this afternoon and neither of them is going to be new in the four years that come after this one.

Marek gave the reason before he said the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is two hundred and nine, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say what the repair is, and I am giving the reason first, and the reason is that in about four years a body is going to read that sheet and see nine headings and no names in the column that says whether anybody was asked, and it is going to assume that a person read it and agreed to it, and that assumption will be wrong, and it will be wrong quietly, and nobody will be able to point at the morning it went wrong because there will not be one."** "The repair is to ask nine people. Not this holding. The nine people. And it takes about a minute each and it is the cheapest work anybody in four counties will do this season, and not one of the nine can be asked by a sheet, and I have written a sheet."

The woman who keeps the seed house asked what happened if one of the nine says no, and the answer given in that room was that the row stays on the sheet with a name in it and a no in the sixth column, and that a row with a no in it is a document four counties can check and that a row with nothing in it is not a document at all.

Nobody in that room said the answer was satisfying and nobody in that room said it was not. What two of them said afterwards, in the yard and not in the room, was that it was the first time in four years that anybody in this building had said out loud that a person was allowed to refuse a thing on a list, and what the other two said afterwards was that it was a list and not a plan, and that a person refusing a line on a list is a person refusing a piece of paper, and neither pair has been asked which of them was right.

The man of about fifty said one more thing in that room and it was the last thing anybody said about the sheet before the lamp was moved, and he gave no reason for it in front of it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move on that account.

**"I have been putting figures in columns for eleven years and I have put about nine thousand of them in, and I could not have told you this morning which of the nine thousand were done by somebody who had agreed to do them, and the honest answer is that I do not think I have ever once asked."** "That is not a sentence for the sheet. It is for me and it is going in the second book with the hour against it and no heading, because I am not about to put a heading on a column because I have been ashamed of it once."

The nine were not asked this morning and nobody in that room proposed that they should be. The clerk took a sheet of the same stock, copied the nine columns onto it in his own hand with the four crosses in the margin, and put it in the Saturday bag, and the sheet that goes to a county this month is a sheet with nothing in the sixth column and four marks in the margin of it.

The clerk of this holding asked the question nobody else had asked and it was the last question asked in that room this month, and it was asked at about the seventh hour with the lamp on the table and the light off it.

He asked whether the list ought to go up the fen road this week in the state it was in this afternoon, with four small crosses in the margin and a whole column empty, or whether it ought to wait until somebody had filled the rest of it.

It goes up in the state it is in. That was the answer and about four people in the room agreed with it at once and about four did not and nobody argued, and the reason, which nobody gave first, is that a list with a whole column empty in it is a list four counties can check that this holding has not done the asking, and that is a fact, and a fact four counties can check is worth more in a fortnight than a complete sheet would have been this afternoon.

The clerk wrote the sheet up for the post and put it in with the Saturday bag, and nobody in this building signed it and the clerk did not ask anybody to, and the sheet that goes to a county this month is the ninth column and the sixth column and the two rows in the seventh and the six rows in the eighth, and all of it with four marks in it.

The well on the shelf is read on the sixteenth of this month and that is one morning off this one, and this page is not going to say what day of the week that is and is not going to read it. There are nine entries in the second book of this holding that are a day and not a figure, with no ordinal against them and no figure supplied, and there is nobody at that far end who has been asked a question in eleven years, and this morning was not the sixteenth and nobody in this building read it and the count is nine and it is nine this evening and it did not move.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and went down it empty, and eleven journeys is a floor and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it and the second of the four lines in his own book is eighty-three days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody in this holding has asked him about it in eighty-three days and nobody is going to this month.

The offer is on the low board, face up, not filled, not withdrawn, no date and no line for a name, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, and a sheet of nine columns is not that instrument and is not going beside it.

The register form is on the middle table at eighty-nine days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and nobody in this holding has been told to take it off that table and nobody is going to be.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning and is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year. The sixth of the six blank columns beside it has a heading at the top of it and nothing under it, and the other five are headed nothing, and the whole of that was done on the eleventh of this month and nothing this afternoon improved it or undid it.

The compost line is paid at twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and it measures nothing and no column of a list has changed that. The ring of bare ground inside the eleven acres is bare in its eleventh year and has no dimension on any page and was not walked, measured, crossed, priced or explained today, and the ground on both sides of it is going wrong more slowly than it was and nobody in this holding has said that slower is better.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is correct in every particular it states, and the order sealing the route is in the same drawer and is valid and it opens nothing, and a list of nine columns does not open a route and does not claim one.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice, and a heading is not a mark and a name is not a mark and a list is not a mark and a repair is not a mark, and there are four accounts of that arm in this building and they do not reconcile with one another and nothing said in that room this afternoon is a fifth of anything.

The two boards were read aloud at the seventh hour and nobody in this holding was asked to say anything else out loud at the end of the day, and the lamp went off the table at about the ninth hour and the sheet went into the bag.

> Entered by the clerk, in his own hand, at about the ninth hour of the evening of the fifteenth, in about nine people, at this holding: **THE THIRD LINE CAME ROUND AT THE SECOND HOUR AND WAS WORKED, THE LOW DRAIN FROM THE NORTH END TO THE SLUICE, FIVE PEOPLE FROM THE SECOND HOUR TO THE SIXTH HOUR, IN FOUR HOURS AND A HALF. THE BOARDS WERE READ ALOUD AT THE SEVENTH HOUR AND THE RUN IS SIXTY-EIGHT OF A HUNDRED AND NINETEEN, THE LAST OF THE RUN IN THIS MONTH, AND THE PAIR STANDS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-NINE DAYS AND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIX, FORTY-SEVEN APART, AND THE DRAWER WAS NOT OPENED. THE THIRD LAUNDER IS EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIVE AND IS THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIFTH FIGURE OF THE SERIES. THE WINDOW STANDS AT EIGHTY-SIX RISES AND SEVENTY-SIX FALLS AND THE TWO ADD. THE FOUR BODIES OF HOUSEHOLDS HAVE NOT MOVED IN A HUNDRED AND NINETY-THREE DAYS. A HEADING IS NECESSARY AND IT IS NOT SUFFICIENT, AND BOTH HALVES OF THAT SENTENCE ARE FOUR YEARS OLD AND NEITHER IS NEW THIS AFTERNOON. THE WELL ON THE SHELF IS READ ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, ONE MORNING OFF THIS ONE, AND WAS NOT READ THIS MORNING, AND THE COUNT OF DAY-AND-NO-FIGURE ENTRIES IS NINE AND DID NOT MOVE. NOBODY THANKED ANYBODY.**

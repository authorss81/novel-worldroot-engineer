# Chapter 425

## Five People In A Room, And Nine Columns

The eighth of this month was a Sunday, and the fourth line of the rota came round at the second hour and went away empty, there being nobody at the headland above Marden to work it, and those fields have waited for the fifty-seventh morning.

The rota stands at eighty-six mornings in force with twenty-nine taken and fifty-seven not, and twenty-nine and fifty-seven are eighty-six.

The count rises by exactly one on a morning this line comes round and by nothing on any other morning, and twenty-nine taken has not moved this morning or on any morning of this year, and nobody in this holding is blamed for a field that waited.

Two ways of counting that rotation are kept in this building and the two of them do not agree, so both of them go on this morning's page. This is the thirty-fourth morning of the run out of the twenty-sixth of a month five months back, and the twenty-first morning of the run anchored on the eighteenth of a month three months back, and the difference between the two numbers is thirteen and has been thirteen for four years and nobody has ever found out what the thirteen is.

This page carries no figure for the run of mornings, and the reason is on the page rather than left off it. Both of the men who take the two boards were in the one room from the fourth hour of the afternoon and did not come out, and the boards are read at the seventh hour, and this holding does not ask two men to leave a room they are working in to go and say two figures out loud at a table.

The pair stands on the boards at **five hundred and seventy-two days** and **six hundred and nineteen**, forty-seven apart, and the drawer was not opened and is not going to be.

The six who would have gone to the headland were on the west side of the boundary ditch instead, and there was a wind off the fen that took the tops off the water in the ditch in lines and put them back in lines, and by the fifth hour the four of them who were on the west side had given up on the lines and worked in the lee of the bank, and the two of them who were not on the west side came down to the yard about the third hour to find out what the other four were doing and went back up.

The eighth went in at about the eighth hour and came back out of the book with the frayed corner, and the two of them were the same.

**Eight hundred and eighty-eight hundredweight.**

Up nine off Saturday, and the hundred and eighty-eighth figure of a series that has not come out at the same figure twice since the morning it began.

The rise went onto the rises. Eighty-three and seventy-two make a hundred and fifty-five, and that window is four hundred and eighty-three mornings wide and is not a count of anything anybody has done.

Four bodies of households have gone a hundred and eighty-six days without a move in their aggregate, and the morning sentence is the hundred and eighty-fifth of those mornings out of a hundred and eighty-seven. Nobody in this building has ever put that sentence on the same line as the launder and nobody ever will.

The woman who keeps the seed house had been in the room since the fourth hour and had a sheet of her own on the table with four columns on it and about nine rows filled, and nobody in the room had asked to see it and everybody in the room had looked at it, and at about the fifth hour she put it face down and said that was not modesty and she would explain it in a minute.

There are five people in that room and there were about four more in the door. The clerk of this holding. The man of about fifty, with four books open on the long table. Tova Reed. The woman who keeps the seed house. Marek, who had the shovel that morning and had not had the bar and about four people noticed which and about four did not.

They began at about the fifth hour of the afternoon with a sheet of the same stock the second book is made of and with a pencil, and the clerk ruled the columns and ruled them wrong twice and ruled them a third time and nobody in the room told him to stop.

> THE SHEET AS RULED AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR OF THE AFTERNOON OF THE EIGHTH, IN THE CLERK'S HAND, NINE COLUMNS ACROSS AND NO ROWS RULED UNDER THEM YET. THE HEADINGS AT THE TOP OF THE FIRST EIGHT, IN HIS HAND, ARE THE WORK. THE PLACE. THE PERSON WHO DID IT. WHAT IT COST. THE READING, AND WHEN. WHETHER THEY WERE ASKED. FIGURES WE CANNOT CHECK. THINGS WE DO NOT KNOW. THE NINTH COLUMN HAS NOTHING AT THE HEAD OF IT AND THE NINTH COLUMN IS THE BACK OF A FORM WITH FOUR LINES ON IT, WHICH CARRIES ONE FIGURE ON THE FIRST LINE AND THREE EMPTY LINES UNDER IT, AND THE FIGURE ON THAT LINE IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT, AND NOBODY HAS FILLED THE OTHER THREE AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.

A column with no heading is a place a clerk can put a figure and a person cannot put a name. That is the whole of what the ninth column is this afternoon and it took about nine minutes for the room to get to it and nobody argued with it when it got there.

Marek put his thumb on the ninth column and said the other half of it, and he gave no reason for saying it before he said it and the count of reasons given first in the holding's history did not move this afternoon on that account.

**"A column with no heading is a place a clerk can put a figure and a person cannot put a name, and the second half of that is worse than the first, and nobody in this room has said it yet. A column with a heading on it is a place a clerk can put a figure and a person can put a name, and that is the whole of what a heading does, and it is not small."** "It does not say who has agreed to anything. It says there is a place where a name could go. Those are two different sentences and this holding has been putting the first one on top of the second one for four years and calling it a column."

Then he said the part that was his own and the room did not improve it.

**"So put nothing at the top of the ninth one yet. Not because it is wrong to have a heading. Because the ninth one is the back of a form and if you head it you will have turned a form into a column, and a form is a thing a person fills in and a column is a thing a person reads, and this list is going to be read by four counties and filled in by about four people in this room, and the two of those are not the same job and a heading is not big enough to be both."**

The man of about fifty gave the reason before the thing, and the count of that in the holding's history is one hundred and twenty-seven, and it is his and of nobody else's.

**"I am going to say why we are ruling nine columns and not four, and I am going to give the reason first, and the reason is that four of the fifty-three requests in the column in front of me are asking this holding for a column, and every one of the four asked for it in a different shape, and the shape they had in common was that each of them wanted a place to put a figure."** "Nine is not a good number. I have been the man with the book in this building for eleven years and I have never ruled more than six in one go and I am not sure I can hold nine. But the four of those requests were not asking for a figure. A clerk in a county wrote that a column is a thing he can read and a council is a thing he cannot, and he is right, and a column that only takes figures is the second of those two things wearing the coat of the first."

The woman who keeps the seed house turned her own sheet back over at that and put it on the table, and said she had been keeping four columns for about nine years and that three of them were people and one of them was seed and that she had never once been asked whether the four were the right four.

Nobody in that room told her they were the right four and nobody told her they were the wrong four, and she wrote the four down on the second sheet and put the second sheet under the first, and the clerk ruled a tenth column about eleven minutes later and then rubbed it out, and the rubbing out is the first thing that happened to that sheet that was not a figure.

Tova Reed was in that room and had said nothing for about an hour, and what she said at about the seventh hour was about the sixth column, and she gave the reason first, and the count of that in the holding's history is forty, and it is hers and of nobody else's.

**"Whether they were asked is the only one of your nine that is about a person and not about a ground, and I want to say why I noticed it, and the reason is that there is a duty in this holding that I have done every week for nine years, and my name has been in a column of this building since before anybody in this room had heard of it, and nobody has ever asked me whether it was mine."** "That is not the woman on the other side of your table. She keeps a different building and a different column and it is a different thing, and I want that said out loud this afternoon before anybody writes either of us into a row." "Put it in the column. I am not asking for a heading on anything in this room. I am asking that the column be the one you rule for the rows and not for the top, because a heading at the top of a thing is a different thing from a name in it, and the second of those is the one this list needs and the first of those is the one this building has been putting on things for four years."

Nobody thanked her and nobody improved it and the clerk wrote the sixth column a second time in a heavier hand, which is not a heading and is not going to become one.

Nobody said anything tender about that and nobody put a hand on her arm, and about four people in that room had worked out what she had actually said and about four had not, and neither of the two fours has ever been separated.

The clerk read the six columns back out loud in the order he had ruled them and got to the sixth and stopped for about four seconds and then read it, and it is the only heading on that sheet that anybody in that room asked to be read twice, and the clerk read it twice.

At about the eighth hour the clerk stopped, and the sheet had four rows in it and about nine columns, and the ninth had nothing at the top of it, and the second line of the rota had been worked at the second hour and the fourth had gone away empty at the same hour, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of what was written on it.

The four rows were the top dyke, the seed work, the low drain, and the headland above Marden, and the headland had a man in it who is not a member of this holding and did not know he was going to be in a list before the eighth hour of the previous evening, and the seed work had nobody in it at all until about the seventh hour of that evening, and by the seventh hour it had a name in it and a figure in it and nobody had asked her.

The light in that room on a Sunday afternoon in this month comes in low and from the west side and is on the table by about the fourth hour and off the table by about the eighth, and at about the eighth hour the clerk moved the sheet about nine inches to the left and put a lamp on it, and one of the four people in the door said the lamp was the second thing today that had been put on a table that had not been on it before, and nobody in the room asked him which first thing he meant.

The window of the office's work is on its third day and nothing has been put in it and nothing has been taken out of it, and a sheet with nine columns on it is not going in a window of somebody else's calendar.

It will go up the fen road when it is a thing, and a thing is a thing that four counties can check, and what four counties can check about a sheet is whether the same nine things are on it in the same places when somebody else holds it, and not one person outside this building has held this one yet.

The barrow came up the four-mile road at the ninth hour and was turned at the top of the yard. Eleven journeys is where it stands and eleven is a floor, and the man of about thirty-one of Silling brought it, and the second of the four lines in his own book is seventy-six days old this morning and has nothing on it, and nobody here has asked him about it.

The offer is face up on the low board, not filled, not withdrawn, refused on the nineteenth of a month now two months back, with no date and no line for a name, and it is not on the sheet and it is not going on the sheet, because a sheet with nine columns on it is a list of what this holding does and that paper is a thing somebody has offered to this holding.

The register form came on the sixteenth of a month now three months back and is on the middle table at eighty-two days, not filled, not refused, nothing at the head of it, and nobody wrote a heading on it this morning or on any morning.

The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-six and was not read this morning. It is read on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the next and on no other morning in the year. The six columns beside it stand headed nothing and did not get a word this morning, and a sheet with nine columns on it in another room is not a reason to give one of those six a heading.

The compost line is paid and the run is twenty-one not discharged in twenty-one and the word has never once been wrong about the figure, and it is not a measurement of the ground and a list of nine columns does not make it one.

A sheet in a drawer claims a corridor in the pan and is true in every particular it states. The order sealing the route is in the same drawer, valid, and it opens nothing, and nothing in this room this afternoon closed or opened a route or a corridor or a column.

The man of about seventy has not been fetched since the first of this month. Twenty-four is the count and nobody in this holding has asked him which number it is and nobody is going to, and no page here gives a reason for a first of a month.

The mark on that arm is four inches and branches twice and it is on the inside of the left forearm where nobody in this room could see it this afternoon. A column is not a mark, a list is not a mark, a ruled line is not a mark, a heading is not a mark, a request is not a mark, and the four accounts of that arm are kept in this building and none of the four of them was touched this afternoon and none of them has ever been reconciled with another.

## The two things to read before drafting Chapters 168–177

**1. The batch's counters were correct in the book and wrong in three different names.** A question asked and not taken is entered as a count, and the count is the volume's method. It arrived wearing three labels and three totals across three chapters: *available to a room* at five in Ch 158, *available to a yard* at one plus *the first question at two* in Ch 159, and *questions asked in a yard by this body* at three in Ch 160 — and Ch 167's section-nine note put it back at five. **It is now one count with one label and a stated standing: five on the thirtieth of the ninth month, six on the first of the tenth, still six on the second because nothing was asked on the second, and six at the end of the batch.** The standing is on the page in Ch 160's entry: *it goes up when a question is put and not when a figure is put on a table.* The per-question counters in Ch 157 are per question, are declared there not additive, and are not this count; `state/open-threads.md` already carried the first question at three, which is now what Ch 159 says.

**2. Two events in this batch were given four dates between them, and a later writer would have inherited all four.** The room of four in which the engineer of record told the seedwright of the trust a thing is **the twenty-fourth of the ninth month**, fixed by Ch 156 and carried in `state/continuity.md` before this batch was drafted. Ch 165 gave it the twenty-fourth of last month, *a month ago* and *the fourth of a month ago*, and Ch 166 had the reader refusing in a yard to tell it *what happened in a room on the eleventh of this month*, and nothing happened in a room on that day. **All four are now the twenty-fourth of last month.** The fourteenth of the ninth month is a real day in Ch 149 and it is a different event, and Tova Reed's *since the fourteenth of last month* in Ch 166 is left standing and flagged in `state/continuity.md` so a later pass does not merge the two.

## The blocking findings

**1. Ch 162 forward-dated the reader's own sentence by five days.** In a room on the sixth of the tenth month he said *On the eleventh of this month I said in a yard that a name is a thing other people can spend.* Ch 167 dates the same speech to the eleventh of **last** month. **The page now reads the eleventh of last month, and the correction is checkable rather than merely plausible: Ch 157 has the reader saying, in the ninth month, that he said it in a yard *on the eleventh of this month*.** The chapter's cost beat and the volume's frame both stand.

**2. Ch 160's central coincidence was contradicted four lines later.** The chapter put one thousand five hundred and twenty beside one thousand five hundred and twenty and called them the same figure; the next speech gave the households' own count as one thousand and forty-five, which is the schedule's first window of eleven days at ninety-five, so the "independent" count was not independent and the beat rested on a number the same speech withdrew. **The page now counts the same eleven days twice: four households at a gate got one thousand and forty-five, and ninety-five the hundredweight a day for those same eleven days is also one thousand and forty-five. Ninety-five times eleven is one thousand and forty-five and was checked before it went on the page.** The thesis sentence, *a coincidence and not a verification*, survives intact and is now true, and 1,045 over eleven days is not entered anywhere as 1,520 over sixteen days.

**3. Ch 166 contradicted itself on the count of the not-known.** A speech said *It is four. It was four a month ago. It is not going to be three this month*, and the clerk's entry nine lines below it said this body *did not know three yesterday*. The entry was the error; **it now reads did not know four a month ago**, which is what the speech says and what the standing requires. This is the count the fourth line of the standing list may not be reduced to, so the error sat on a load-bearing entry.

**4. An established male character became a woman twice.** The man of about thirty-five is a man at Ch 158, 159, 160, 161, 164 and throughout Ch 165, and Ch 165's closing paragraph and then Ch 166's third line made him a woman. No new woman is introduced anywhere in the batch. **He is a man in both passages.** The error had already propagated into `state/continuity.md:3108`.

**5. Ch 160's third-day entry was forward-dated and pre-empted Ch 163.** An entry timed the seventh hour of the evening of the **third** of the tenth month reported a party that had supplied water into the low quarter *between the second and the seventh*, and carried the argument that a figure which is the sum of two things one of which was never measured is not a figure. Ch 163 discloses that supply on the seventh, and Ch 164 dates it the second to the seventh. **The entry now records only what a man who walked out to the low quarter and back in one day could have brought back on the third, and the sum that is not a figure belongs to Ch 163 and is in Ch 163 only.** The manuscript refusing to enter an unmeasured sum while entering one it could not yet know about was the review's phrase for it and it was fair.

## The should-fix findings

**6. Ch 167 gave the wrong weekday for a date it could check.** *The figure falls on the first of next month, a Thursday.* Day 285 is a Thursday, so day 300 is a Friday and the first of the eleventh month is day 301 and **a Saturday**. The same sentence in Ch 158 is a Thursday and is correct, because there it is the first of the tenth month. `state/open-threads.md` had the Saturday right and the chapter did not.

**7. Two incompatible lists for the count of five, one of them counting a statement that had not happened.** Ch 158, written on the thirtieth, listed the first, sixteenth, twenty-first and twenty-ninth and then counted *is going to say it again on the first of the tenth*. Ch 159 listed the eleventh, sixteenth, twenty-first, twenty-ninth and thirtieth. **One list now, in both chapters, and Ch 158 no longer counts a future statement.** The five days are the eleven, sixteen, twenty-one, twenty-nine and thirty, which is also consistent with the day-minus rule's four reading days in `state/continuity.md`.

**8. Ch 166's third thing was nine miles' road.** The holder of record is eleven miles off in eight other places in the batch, including the fifth item of the same speech four lines below. Corrected.

**9. Ch 163 used a distance as a unit of thought.** *I have been thinking about that for nine miles.* **It is now *I have been turning it over on the nine miles back***, which is what a man who has just walked nine miles can say and which keeps the walk on the page.

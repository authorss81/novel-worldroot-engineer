# Batch 0005, Volume 08 - Review Repair Findings

The review of Batch 0005 is in `logs/batch-0005.review.log` and it returned seven defects. This file records what was verified, what was paid, what was declined, and what the repair pass found that the review did not raise. Every figure here was measured off the files after the prose moved.

**Nothing was restarted. No chapter was rewritten, no scene was staged, no day, clock, count, name, lock or thread moved, and no planned plot changed.** Seven chapters were touched and two were left alone. One repair deliberately reached back into Batch 0004, and the reason is given in section 4.

## 1. The month frame, nineteen places, and it was a string nobody had ever written

**Verdict: defect confirmed and paid.**

`a month now two back` appears nineteen times in the manuscript and all nineteen are in Batch 0005. It appears nowhere else in forty-nine chapters, in seven earlier volumes, or in the whole manuscript. Every other calendar frame in the volume is *a month two months back*, *a month three months back*, *a month four months back*, *a month that was two months back* or *last month*.

Every one of the nineteen sits in a chapter on days 601 to 605, the first to the fifth of the ninth month, and every one points at a date in the **eighth** month. The anchors were checked one at a time and all nineteen resolve to the eighth month:

| Anchor | Day | Where |
|---|---:|---|
| nineteenth | 589 | 388:67, 389:67, 390:37, 390:59, 391:61, 392:43 |
| twentieth | 590 | 390:41, 390:61, 392:51 |
| twenty-third | 593 | 390:5 |
| twenty-fifth | 595 | 391:31, 391:51 |
| sixteenth | 586 | 392:5 |
| thirteenth | 583 | 389:71, 390:65, 392:59 |
| third | 573 | 389:13, 391:5 |
| twelfth | 582 | 392:31 |

**Repair: `a month now two back` becomes `last month` in all nineteen.** Two alternatives were considered and rejected. *A month one month back* is a form nobody in this manuscript uses. *A month back* is a rate here, thirty crates a month back, and is not a calendar frame at all. *Last month* is the house form and is already used inside this same batch, at Ch 385, for the same kind of reference.

## 2. The rotation's first count in Ch 389 - found by the repair pass, not by the review

**Verdict: defect the review did not raise, confirmed by measurement, and paid.**

The fourth line's four-day run began on day 506, which is the **twenty-sixth of the fifth month**.

- Ch 385, on day 598 in the eighth month, prints that anchor as *the twenty-sixth of a month three months back*. Three months back from the eighth is the fifth. **Correct.**
- Ch 389, on day 602 in the ninth month, printed the **same** anchor as *the twenty-sixth of a month three months back*. Three months back from the ninth is the seventh, which is not day 506. **Wrong by a month.**

**Repair: *a month four months back*, a form Batch 0004 already uses. Both pages now resolve to day 506, which is the test.**

The same paragraph said the frame of the *second* count had moved with the month and said nothing of the first, which had also moved. It now says both of those frames moved and neither count is a new one. The second count was checked and is right: *the eighteenth of a month two months back* is day 558, and 602 − 558 over four plus one is twelve, the figure on the page.

## 3. A figure stated twice in one chapter

**Verdict: defect confirmed and paid.**

Ch 388 printed the aggregate at its own line and again at line 65, in the words *a hundred and forty-nine days* and *the hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings out of a hundred and fifty*. The parallel paragraph in Ch 387 keeps the four named bodies and the barrow's eleven journeys and does not restate the aggregate, and no other chapter in forty-nine restates it outside its own line. Ch 388 was the singleton.

**Repair: the restatement was cut and every distinct fact in the paragraph kept** - the four bodies by name, the ninth-day return, the not-the-launders distinction, the barrow's eleven journeys. Nothing added. The chapter fell by 35 words.

## 4. The hedge on a counted figure, and the plant-or-slip ruling on the weekday

### 4a. The hedge

Ch 386 read *It is about four bodies of households and not about the launders*, where *It* is the line named in the sentence before it. The noun had been dropped, so *about* read as approximately rather than concerning. Six of the seven other places in the volume where this distinction is drawn use the noun: *It is a sentence about four bodies of households*.

**Repair: *It is a line about four bodies of households*, keeping the chapter's own word. One word. No count moved.**

### 4b. Sunday the thirteenth - ruled a SLIP, repaired in ten places across two batches

**The review called this a weekday error and asked for a ruling before anything was touched. The ruling is that it is a slip, and the evidence is a count of the whole volume's weekday claims rather than an argument about this one.**

Every weekday-plus-ordinal claim in the forty-nine chapters was extracted and checked against the day table built from the volume's own anchor, day 451 being the first of the fourth month and a Tuesday, months running thirty.

**Result: fifteen claims, fifteen consistent. *Sunday the thirteenth* was the only string in the volume that failed, ten times.**

> An earlier run of this check reported five consistent and eleven mismatched, and every one of those eleven was an artefact of the checking script rather than a defect in the prose: the script computed the weekday of day 451 as Monday when the anchor says Tuesday, which shifted the whole table by one and made every weekday in the volume look wrong. The corrected run is the one above, and the difference between the two runs is recorded here because a review that cannot show its own arithmetic is not evidence of anything.

The thirteenth of the eighth month is day 583 and is a **Monday**. No thirteenth of the fifth through the ninth month is a Sunday, so the weekday was wrong and not the date.

**Why a slip and not a plant.** The volume's test asks whether repairing it costs something the chapter promised to keep. It costs one word. It changes no count, no day, no clock and no lock. The claim carries no count of its own - the two figures printed beside it are the five things said out loud in a yard and got wrong, and the thirteen times a bookkeeper has said a thing in a yard, and neither is touched by the name of a weekday. A plant would have been carrying a count. This one was not.

**Repair: *Monday the thirteen* in all ten places**, at Chs 376, 378, 380, 381 and 383 of Batch 0004 and Chs 384, 386, 389, 390 and 392 of Batch 0005. **This deliberately reached past the current phase.** A volume may not ship with a known-wrong weekday in five chapters, the fix costs one word each, and there is a direct precedent: the Batch 0002 repair pass also touched five chapters of another batch. Batch 0004's own total is unchanged at 27,950, every word replaced being one word for one word.

## 5. The near-duplicate pair the repair created, and the prose that came back with it

**Verdict: self-inflicted, caught, and paid.**

Correcting the weekday made two yard paragraphs more alike, not less, because both now end on the same day. Ch 386's against Ch 389's crossed the gate at 0.865.

**Repair:** Ch 386's final clause was restructured from *there has not been a yard in this holding since Monday the thirteenth of this month* to *the last yard in this holding was on Monday the thirteenth of this month and there has not been one since*. The gate is back at its baseline of 484 pairs with **zero involving Batch 0005**. The restructure also removes a string that stood in eight chapters of the volume nearly word for word, which is a gain and not a cost.

**A consequence that changed prose.** Ch 390 fell to **2,887** words, below the 2,900 floor that Batch 0005's own prompt set. It was raised back over the floor by 51 words of new prose rather than by padding: a man puts his heel in at the top end of the six feet of soft channel and the print closes over by itself, and the test did not ask for a spade. That is the chapter's own subject and it introduces no figure.

## 6. The decline, written down with its reason

**Ch 385 prints *The seventh column of the well house door stands at fifty-two and is not read*, and the review asked for a ruling. The ruling is that it is compliant and it was not repaired.**

The lock forbids another chapter to read that column, to write a heading on one of the six blank columns beside it, and to say what the seventh counts besides readings. Ch 385 does none of the three. It prints the inherited standing, states outright that the column is not read, names the two mornings it is read, and writes no heading anywhere. It is a deferral, and it carries the same information the prompt itself carries. Removing a true and useful statement to satisfy a reading of a lock the sentence does not breach is the repair that costs the chapter something it promised to keep.

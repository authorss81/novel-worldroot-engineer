## 3. Day 691 is one of the two mornings the door gives, and Ch 478 denied it — PAID IN PROSE

**Severity: medium. Needed an author ruling. Paid on the page, and the ruling is recorded so no later pass undoes it.**

The seventh column of the well house door is read on the first and the thirtieth and on no other morning. That rule stands in Ch 443–464, in the mouth of the man of about thirty-one of Silling in Ch 477 ("it has been read on the first and the thirtieth in every one of those four years"), and in Ch 480 and Ch 481. Ch 447 states the mechanism: readings come in pairs, "the second half is tomorrow."

The day clock puts day 690 on the thirtieth and **day 691 on the first of the twelfth**. Ch 477 spends day 690 and takes the column 58 → 59. **Ch 478 is day 691 and did not read the column — and worse, its ninth paragraph said so:**

> A walk of nine hundred yards that gets made twice a year is not a walk anybody in this holding puts a rate on, and the two mornings of the year on which it is made are not this one and are not the next one either.

**That is not a page that declines to mention a reading. That is a page asserting that this morning is not one of the two mornings the rule gives, and six chapters give it.**

### What was actually wrong, underneath the finding

**The plan and the prose disagree, and the reviewer's framing only had room for half of it.** `outline/volume-10.md` § 9 and its measured table name **days 660 and 661 as the only two readings of the volume**, at fifty-seven and fifty-eight, and say *from fifty-eight it may not go back*. So the volume as written has spent a **third** reading, on day 690, at fifty-nine, against the plan. The day-691 omission is the smaller half of the same disagreement.

### The ruling

**The figure stands and the morning is owned.** The fifty-nine is **not** unwound, because `workspace/volume-10/batch-0005/PROMPT.md` § 4 and its Ch 489 card both take the column from fifty-nine to sixty on day 702 and call it the last reading of the year, Ch 480 and Ch 481 both print fifty-nine as a standing, and the state layer carries it as a floor. **A repair that struck fifty-nine out to satisfy a sentence in a plan would have been a repair that changed the plot, and this pass was told not to change the plot.** The departure from § 9 is declared, named and carried in `state/continuity.md`, `state/current.md` and the Batch 0005 prompt, so that no later pass discovers it as an error and quietly corrects it.

**What is corrected is the page, and only the page.** Ch 478's ninth paragraph now reads:

> A walk of nine hundred yards that gets made twice a year is not a walk anybody in this holding puts a rate on, and this morning is one of the two of them and it was not made. The man of about thirty-one of Silling was in this yard from the fourth hour with a cart to the second gate and back and did not go up the lane, and the piece of chalk he put on the shelf by the yard door on Wednesday was not in his hand this morning, and nobody in this building has asked him why he went at the end of one month and did not go at the start of the next, and he has not said, and a walk of nine hundred yards is not a line of the rota and nobody is going to rule one for it.

Every element is already on that page or already in the batch: he was in the yard from the fourth hour and drove the cart to the second gate both ways; the chalk went on the shelf by the yard door on Wednesday at the ninth hour and was not thanked; day 690 is a Wednesday; nobody asks that man anything at any of his twenty-six fetchings and the count of questions is nil at every site.

### The constraint that shaped the edit, and it is the part a later pass must not get wrong

**It is written obliquely on purpose. It names the lane, the cart, the chalk and the question that was not asked, and it names neither the column nor the figure.** Ch 478 as delivered printed no column at all, and the batch states the seventh column in three chapters and not four, and that three is a working cap this batch was shaped around — six paragraphs were already cut to bring the register form and the barrow inside it. **A repair that had printed fifty-nine a fourth time would have been a repair that put a floor into a fourth chapter to answer a reviewer.**

So the page now puts a man in a yard and not a number on a door, the column stays at fifty-nine, the page still claims no pair of mornings and still says neither a seventh reading nor an eighth, and the sixth column's count of readings is one under what the rule says it should be with nobody entering the difference anywhere — which is the thread, and it is **not** to be answered in this volume.

### What the three mornings now say

He read the door on the thirtieth, which the rule gives. He did not read it on the first, which the rule also gives. He will read it on the twelfth of the next month, which the rule does not give, and the Batch 0005 prompt forbids that page from saying why. **A man who has kept a rule four years, misses one morning of it, and then breaks it again eleven days later without being asked is a figure the whole volume was walking toward. It was not put there by this pass. It was put there by a chapter that denied its own calendar and was corrected into it.**

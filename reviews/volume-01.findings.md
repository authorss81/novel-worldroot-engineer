# Volume 01 Close — Findings

Volume 01, *The White Furrow*, Chapters 1–49, season days 1–52. Forty-nine chapters, five batches, **165,844 words** measured over the chapter files. This is the close-up: what the volume did, what it broke, what was repaired in this pass, and what a Volume 02 writer inherits.

---

## 1. The central pressure

A cooperative holds a node it cannot afford to feed, on ground it cannot irrigate, under a charter that expires at midnight on the eighteenth and a district that will not produce the one table that would explain the damage.

Everything else in the volume is a consequence of that. There is no contractor because there is no charter. There is no money because the box holds four pounds five. The return main is twenty-two rods of eight inch against a specification of forty-one of twelve, which means the head reads fair while three hundred and eighty turns a day go down somebody else's private branch and never come back. The book that says otherwise is signed by the cooperative. And the district's answer to a body in that position is not villainy: it is a clerk who will copy anything, certify nothing, and go on doing her job.

The pressure that holds the volume together is narrower than any of those: **the only thing that beats an unprovable account is a dated record a stranger can take away and hold — and the body that most needs one has no legal standing to hold one.** Every movement is a different attempt to get around that, and every attempt costs somebody something real.

## 3. The midpoint reversal

Movement 3 turns the volume inside out, and it turns it on a measurement rather than an event.

Up to Chapter 25 the question is: *can a cooperative with no charter prove that it has been damaged?* At Chapters 26 and 27 the answer arrives and it is the wrong shape. Marek walks the branch at Weir Row and measures a bed that is dead at the bottom, dry, mineral-bloomed, **killed from the top** — not cut there — and the two stamped cut faces that the district's own completion record carries are nine miles upstream of forty-one houses, on Crown money, on a Crown schedule, on the cooperative's own node, on the twenty-seventh. The cooperative is the local holder. It was not notified, it cannot object, and it is the body the low quarter is looking at.

So the question inverts. Before the midpoint the cooperative is an injured party trying to produce a record. After it, the cooperative is a body that has measurably damaged forty-one houses, holds a falsified return in its own book, and **still cannot produce the document that would show whose fault it is** — because the cause is not established, and because the district will not produce the table.

The reversal is not softened anywhere. The wet gravel under the low quarter rises about four inches; nine thousand people on the March road eat what the dry-land line does not grow; and the volume never lets the cooperative off the hook it put itself on.

## 4. The escalation

Escalation in this volume is a sequence of *documents that travel*, and it runs like this:

1. An open book on a table under a stone. **Nobody comes.** Twenty-six days, not the district, not a neighbor, not one of the sixteen households.
2. A copy of the log goes into a clerk's satchel on the seventeenth — and the fieldbook itself goes into a district file on the eighth and **cannot be recalled by anybody who is not an officer of the office.**
3. Perrin Osse certifies the copy *so far as it goes*, which is six words and turns out to be the hinge of the whole second half: a page can never go into that book again without a district officer in the room.
4. Dunmar Fitch spends *none known* on a statement of fact and the box is shut for good.
5. The district will certify a fact about its own completion record — three stamped faces — **on condition that the applicant disclose its own margin note that exposes him.**
6. The third exchange requirement is still missing, on the one licensed work in the volume, and it cannot be filled: the notice is fourteen days and the money is fourteen pounds ten out of the trust's own annex against nine thousand people who are not in the room.
7. Lissa Vale breaks her own written undertaking to produce the instrument on the twenty-first and not before, and enters the breach herself, in her own hand, as lateness.

The escalation is not a rising threat. It is a **shrinking room**: fewer documents anybody can carry, and a body that keeps having to choose between paying for a proof it cannot use and losing the proof it cannot replace.

## 5. The climax

Chapters 45 and 46, the sixteenth to the eighteenth of the month, in a flood.

**The design fails at the seventh hour** and the night's work is the same hole made bigger, plus a hundred and forty yards of existing channel and a drowned culvert rodded out with a hook and a bar. **No new run is dug, none is funded, and none appears in the book.** Marek says it out loud, in a yard, in daylight, to everybody: a bigger hole in a bank he has already cut is the same hand on the same bank in the same hour, and a man in a room in four years will be right about that, and it will be the whole of what he finds. The six-word choice is made once, in daylight, by the man whose ground it is, no motion is moved, and no other person in that yard is allowed to vote on it.

Then the arithmetic is done in the mud, on four records that cannot all be true: the cooperative's own log (eleven hundred at the head, seven hundred and twenty on the ground, three hundred and eighty missing), **Kellan's own six-year book (his side draw never above three hundred — eighty a day, a hundred and seventy-five thousand two hundred turns, never once on the same line of the same page)**, Marek's own four-shilling reed gauge, which proves nothing except that a man built one, the miller's chalk at four hundred and nineteen, and the dated seed-house ledger with a hundred and thirty head of sheep gone up a fen road in October. **The extra sixty came up by something that is not the return main and nobody in that yard knows what.**

The fieldbook's three lines are spent, once, at a bench, by Sera Quill with her own clip — *Cut the upper and leave the lower. Wait a season, and the ground will come back. Cut again, and put your name to it.* — and they are the instrument's, not hers, and her own finding is a separate block in her own hand under her own name. And the name: **schedule clerk, fen drains**, an office, four holders in six years, the man in it does not come out. Sera Quill heard it and said nothing, and Lissa Vale entered that she said nothing and not why, and nobody asked her again.

The climax is paid off, not won. The knot does not come loose; it comes out of the mud and onto a bench, in public, at a cost to the man who put it there.

## 6. What the volume's promise resolves

The promise made in Chapter 1 — that the fault is in the root line and Marek will prove it in front of an inspector rather than in a shed — is **paid in a modified form and the modification is the point.**

What is proved: the head draws eleven hundred; the ground takes seven hundred and twenty; three hundred and eighty a day has gone down a private branch for six years; the return main is a third of specification; the branch at Weir Row was cut on Crown money on the twenty-seventh by a district crew and killed forty-one houses' water from the top; and the wood carries a structure nobody can explain with the word *remembered*.

What is not proved, and is not going to be: **the cause of the damage is not established against the cooperative on the district's own dates, and the schedule that would establish it is never produced.** The cause is not established in the fieldbook either. Sera Quill reads *below* and *not yours* and cannot be corroborated, because Marek is not licensed and what he got is worth nothing in a room.

So the promise resolves into the thing the volume was actually about. **A node does not remember a season that has not happened; what is in the bead and in that cut face is a work order — a target pattern, and the word for a target pattern is not a prophecy, it is a specification.**

## 7. The deeper answer

Two answers, and neither is the mystery's solution.

**On evidence.** A reading is not a fact. A fact is a thing a stranger can walk up to and check without believing anybody. An unlicensed reading is a rumor with a witness in it. This is why the whole volume is written into books with the hour on them and read back aloud, and why the hearing officer's ruling — nobody in the room may open the instrument, including her, and the ruling binds the next officer — is worth more than any confession in it. A body that has written down what it wanted and did not get, and published it, and been ignored by every one of its own sixteen households, has still built the only instrument that survives the season.

**On cost.** The volume refuses every soft landing it offers itself. The outer strip is gone. The graft is unfeedable at about a twentieth of its design load and the starve will not be visible from the top of the field until the second week of next August, by which time it is somebody else's log. The dry-land line is lost for four years. Three acres of Marek's own ground are gone for three seasons and are entered as a decision and not as a charge, because **nobody can be served with them.** The file stays open, the emergency clause stays live, and the Directorate reserves the right to reopen on any new report. The charter expires at the end of the season without notice.

## 8. The new question Volume 02 inherits

Not answered here, and not to be answered in Volume 02 either:

**Why does scarroot carry post-disappearance work order in Aldren Vale's hand for a branch on no current map, and who authorised the cut?**

Volume 01 earns the shape of the question and nothing about its content. What it hands over: a work order, a specification in living wood, a reader who has seen the structure of one before and has not wanted to say so for twenty-three years, an office rather than a man, and a district completion record with three stamped faces on an item whose schedule has never been produced. Nobody in Tarrow Fen can answer it, and the volume ends on a man who cannot say what he felt in the wood in a room.

## 9. The final image, checked

All six elements of the required Chapter 49 image are in the chapter as drafted. Confirmed:

1. **The charter on the seed-house door with the district's copper inspection tag still hanging under it** — the tag has been on that door since the eighteenth of last month and was never taken down, and nobody takes any of it down.
2. **Lissa Vale holding the fieldbook and the use log, neither asking the other for anything** — both on the middle of the table, not in the press, anybody may look, anybody may ask to have the log read to her, and this is the fourth thing she has refused in six weeks and she did not number it.
3. **Tova Reed marking the seed annex in a public ledger anyone may copy** — the March's copy goes into a public ledger and she marks it herself, and puts the number on it for the third time against the trust's own interest.
4. **The first row of next season's line already in the ground and leaning sideways** — the Longneck, eleven trays, sown on the twenty-second in a wet drill, up in four hours, **leaning eleven degrees off the vertical along its whole length**, measured and entered by Gael Senn, and the word *failed* is not written anywhere by her decision.
5. **Nia's cart ruts in the gate road, pointing out** — still there, and nothing is done about them.
6. **The seed-house lamp burning on a living bulb somebody has to feed tonight** — and it was Lissa Vale, and she did not make a ceremony of it.

## 11. The mechanical audit

**Quotation-mark balance. Bad, now good.** The volume carried a systematic defect: from Chapter 12 onward, a speech running over two or more paragraphs opened a mark and did not close it, and the next paragraph opened another, so a chapter's total could be even while two speeches were open. The close prompt's census said 217; the detector found **219 at paragraph starts** — a per-chapter discrepancy of 26 and 35 against the prompt's figures for Chapters 26 and 35 — **plus two mid-paragraph instances** in Chapters 20 and 37 that a paragraph-start detector cannot see. **221 marks were added across 28 chapters.** Chapters 1–11 had none; Chapters 12, 17, 19–21, 24–43 and 47–49 were the twenty-eight affected. Every chapter now balances: **5,108 marks, 2,554 pairs, no speech open at the end of a paragraph, no odd chapter.**

**The day clock. Bad in eight chapters, now good.** The rule is that a day in 1–30 is in October, the previous month, and is dated by its plain number or *of last month* or *of this season*, **never *of this month* and never as an ordinal of a month**; a day in 31+ is dated by its ordinal of the next month or *of this month* or its plain season day number, **never *of last month*.** Chapters 1–30 were dated throughout by plain number except for **twelve violations**: Chapter 16 four times, Chapter 17 once, Chapter 18 twice, Chapter 19 once, Chapter 20 three times, Chapter 28 once — all of the form *the twenty-seventh of this month* for an October day, or *the third of the month*. All twelve are now plain numbers. Chapters 31–49 were clean: every *of this month* there refers to day 31 or later, and every *of last month* to day 30 or earlier. Verified dates include the notice to Idris Vane on the fourth of the month (day 34), the ditch on the sixteenth (day 46), the collie report of the sixth (day 36), the reconciliation on the twentieth (day 50), and the compost of the fourteenth (day 44).

**Three date slips, all repaired.** Anneke Roos dated the letter to K. Rusk to the twentieth of last month in Chapter 47; Marek writes it at four o'clock and straps it on the nineteenth in Chapter 18 and it goes out with the consignment on the twentieth — the entry now says **written on the nineteenth and sent on the twentieth.** Chapter 49 dated the new run's license to the twenty-first; the license note in Chapter 28 is the **twenty-second**, fourth hour, the same hour the pipe was laid.

**Month names. Good.** October appears ten times, all in speech or narration, never on a document, and always as the previous month. February and January appear only as months ahead. *March* is only ever the Salt March. *April* once, as a memory. *September* three times, all ahead or habitual. *August* once, ahead. No November, December, May, June or July anywhere.

**Load-bearing figures. Good, and all of them recompute.** The weep chain is monotonic and twice-measured at every reading. Eighty a day over six years is **175,200** exactly, and the climax's arithmetic — 1,100 − 720 = 380, against a private draw never above 300 — is done on the page. Four hundred hundredweight at four shillings is **eighty pounds**; the 399 shortfall is **seventy-nine pounds sixteen**; **£4 5 is twenty-one hundredweight and a quarter** at the same rate. The return main is twenty-two rods of eight inch against forty-one of twelve in every one of its six appearances. The mill board holds **126, 131, 254, 231, 181 and 419**, with 254 seven short of the best figure and 419 higher than anything on it in thirty-one years while **261 is the highest it had** — the Batch 0005 review had already fixed the chapter that claimed both were the highest, and the fix holds. The £60 10, the fourteen days, the £14 10, the 399 hundredweight, the three and a half degrees, the hundred and thirty head, the forty-one houses, the two hundred yards, the four days and the six section-nine notes all agree across every chapter they appear in.

**One figure in the prompt is not a name in the prose.** There is no set called *the four rules of the return*. The four rules that govern a return-line entry are in Chapter 48's fifth sheet and are consistent with Chapter 31's first return line and Chapter 47's entry: **same book, same page, same hand, with the hour.** That is the four the close has verified. The chapter's return clause separately carries three requirements. Recorded rather than invented.

**Duplication. Good.** No exact duplicate paragraph anywhere except the seven-column form in Chapters 18 and 20, which is a form and not a clone. No sentence appears three or more times. Three genuine prose clones were found and repaired: **Chapters 30 and 31's parallel field walks** (Ch 30's close rewritten in its own words and re-dated to the Sunday crust), **the pulse opening shared by Chapters 19 and 21**, and **the Dunmar entrance shared by Chapters 16 and 27**. What remains is house style, and it is deliberate: *with the hour on it and read it back*, *the licensed person present column said none*, *lifted the sacking at the six o'clock corner*, *squared it to the edge of the table*, the two monthly examinations, the arm check, the miller's figures entered as a thing reported, and **the three things**, which must stay word for word.

**Spelling. Bad, decided, and fixed.** The bible and the outline locks say US spelling; the prose was genuinely mixed, with about 111 British instances against about 75 American ones. **US spelling governs, decided once.** 111 instances were converted as a single mechanical pass over all forty-nine chapters: the noun *license*, which had been spelled two ways against its own verb across twenty-seven chapters, and *rumor*, *gray*, *labor*, *favor*, *color*, *program*, *defense*, *neighbor*, *honor*, *traveling*, *canceled* and *judgment*. The bible's own seven slips were corrected, and 85 instances in the state and review records with them, and the decision is now written into `bible/terminology.md` so the two files cannot contradict each other silently. *Toward* and *towards* are both standard in US English, and the two instances in the prose were made *toward* for consistency rather than left as a variant.

## 12. What this close pass changed in the prose

**Forty-one chapter files changed: thirty for the mechanical and structural repairs below, and eleven more — Chapters 2, 3, 4, 8, 11, 15, 22, 23, 44, 45 and 46 — for the spelling pass alone.**

- **221 closing quotation marks** added across 28 chapters (census above).
- **Twelve day-clock violations** corrected in Chapters 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 and 28.
- **Two date slips** corrected in Chapters 47 and 49.
- **Chapters 29 and 30 overlap closed.** Chapter 29's crust now comes up on the Sunday, in one sheet, and stands on the edge of the hole, and the banks begin to come away at the top on the Monday and roll on the Friday; Chapter 30's field walk is re-dated to that same Sunday crust and rewritten so it is not a clone of Chapter 31. **Chapter 30's date, her fourth arm check, the eleven-hundred-word statement and the form's fourteen days did not move.**
- **Three clones broken** (Ch 19/21, Ch 16/27, Ch 30/31).
- **111 spelling instances** converted, plus seven in the bible and 85 in the state and review records.

Nothing else in the prose was touched. No plot beat, no number, no name, no date inside Movement 5, and no chapter length was changed by this pass.

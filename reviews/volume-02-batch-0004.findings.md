## Blocking

**1. `state/continuity.md` carried a stale, self-contradicting account of Chapter 88.** The same file that recorded the repair still described the district letter as answering *a return of the fourteenth of a month two months back*, gave the interval as **ninety-three days**, and pointed a later writer at the sentence *four days over four months*. All three were superseded by the repair printed a few lines above. A writer reading that paragraph got the opposite of the truth on all three counts, in the one paragraph flagged as verified.
**Fixed.** The paragraph now states the true interval (**twenty-five days**), the true month (*last month*), and the true sentence (*four days over four weeks*), and says on its face that it was wrong twice and which version to quote.

**2. Chapter 77's opening reported a Friday on the preceding Wednesday.** Coll Renner, speaking on day 105, a Saturday, said a carter came into his yard *on the Wednesday* and that he had been turning it over *since Wednesday*. **The nearest Wednesday before day 105 is day 102; the boy went down the fen road on the fourteenth, which is day 104 and is a Friday.** The carter therefore reported the walk two days before it happened — in the batch's opening, on the inciting incident for Chapters 78, 79 and 81. **The dating sweep table has no row for Chapter 77's opening: the one chapter whose defect class the sweep claimed to have eliminated exhaustively was the one it never checked.**
**Fixed.** The carter came *on Friday*, the same day as the boy, and Renner has been turning it over *since Friday*. One word, twice. The date he promised in a yard on the eighth of last month, the errand, and the rest of the speech are untouched.

**3. Two of the three cited days are in the batch.

## Should fix

**4. The Chapter 88 repair created a same-sentence contradiction it did not notice.** Tova Reed said she had been afraid *for eleven weeks* that the answer would be a form, in the same speech in which she dated her question to the fourteenth of last month — 77 days of fear reaching back 54 days before she asked.
**Fixed, and this is where the phrase was settled.** The fear now has an anchor that is exactly true: **eleven weeks is the morning the conditional charter went up on the seed-house door** — day 52, seventy-seven days before the ninth — and she says the fear was already in the letter when she wrote it. The fear therefore predates the question by fifty-two days, and the sentence says so.

**5. "Eleven weeks" appeared twelve times as an unfalsifiable interval implying seven different anchors.** Back-calculating each use gave day 31, 35, 39, 42, 44, 46 and 50 across twenty days of story. **The two instances that were load-bearing were simply false:** the fen's book named the broker's return on **day 55** (sixty-four days before the twenty-ninth, not eleven weeks — the entry is in Volume 02 Chapter 52, where the refusal and the naming are one entry in the same hand), and **Ottoline Fray's cutting sheet is undated anywhere in Chapters 50–88**, so "eleven weeks" put it on a day where nothing in the manuscript happens. In a book whose continuity file carries a twenty-row distance table, this was the one interval permitted to float, and it was the batch's most repeated phrase after "the fen."
**Fixed by decision.** The phrase now survives **once**, in Chapter 88, where it is exact and means one thing: the charter going up on the door. Every other use was replaced with the interval that is true, or with a phrase carrying no number. The full table of was/now/anchor is in `state/continuity.md` under *The second pass*, and the rule is now a lock: **an interval in this book is either checkable or it is not said.**
The replacements: Chapter 78, *a hundred and four days* and *since the book was opened* (day 4, the body's vote to open its own book, which the same speech already named, spoken by the man who cut the day count under the word RETURN); Chapter 80, *since the charter went up on that door* and *on the seed house door* (day 52, exactly two months — and the best sentence in the building is not on the charter); Chapter 81, *since the twelfth of a month two months back* (day 42, the levy night); Chapter 82, *eighty-eight days ago* (day 28, the day the schedule of damage was pinned on that door, per Volume 01 Chapters 28 and 32); Chapter 84, *on the twenty-fifth of a month two months back* and *since the fourth*; Chapter 85, *since the first of last month* (day 91, the load test, after which the head came down) and a plain claim about the chalk cart that is true and is what Chapter 88's Lissa Vale is answering when she gives the carter's question as her reason for reading the schedule out on a Sunday; Chapter 86, *a long time*.

**6. The day-clock table mis-attributed Chapter 87's close, and the chapter overruns its declared range.** The table gave Chapter 87 days 125–126 while the distance table attributed *the copies go up* on the seventh (127) to **Chapter 88**. In the prose those paragraphs — the copies up the fen road, the closing entry read aloud twice, and the man of about fifty's last sentence in the batch — are the **last three paragraphs of Chapter 87**, all past tense about day 127, and Chapter 88 opens on the same day and does not repeat them.
**Fixed in the state file, with no prose change.** A note now sits under the day clock: **Chapter 87 reaches one day past its own range on purpose, day 127 belongs to the close of Chapter 87 and the head of Chapter 88, and it is not to be counted twice in any reconciliation.** The attribution in the distance table was corrected to name Chapter 87.

**7. Chapter 85's "fifteen weeks on" is 104 days, and the sweep had logged the rounding as a fix.** The notice repeats the language of the eighteenth of a month four months back — day 18 — and day 18 to day 122 is 104 days, which is fourteen weeks and six days. The dating sweep had corrected *three months back, eleven weeks on* to *four months back, fifteen weeks on* and recorded it as a repair, introducing a one-day error in the exact column it exists to police.
**Fixed.** The prose now says **a hundred and four days on**. The month was right and is unchanged. The sweep row now records both corrections and notes that the notice of the eighteenth is referenced and not reproduced anywhere in the manuscript, so no later chapter may date it differently.

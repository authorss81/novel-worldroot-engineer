## 1. BLOCKING — metafictional narration, thirty instances, in all ten chapters

The single largest defect in the batch, and the one the review log was closing on when it stopped. The narration refers to its own book: *this chapter*, *this batch*, *this volume*, *in two volumes*, *Volume 03*, *the fourth chapter of the first volume*, *no new named member of the cooperative was created in this batch*, *the volume's structural antagonist*, *a later writer must not be allowed to find it in a summary and enlarge it*, *there is no entry in this volume for that well coming back*.

**All thirty are removed**, and each was replaced with the in-world referent the sentence actually needed — *the rest of this*, *this holding*, *the fen's book*, *eleven years*, *the day on the paper*. Nothing load-bearing went with them: the manuscript's habit of telling the reader that a sentence matters is native to this book and is kept everywhere it can be said in the world's own terms. Volume 02 carries eighteen such lines in forty-nine chapters and the Volume 02 close did not object to them; **this batch carried thirty in ten**, and the density is what makes it a defect rather than an idiom. **Volume 01's review history is the standing precedent against them** — `state/continuity.md` records *the Ch 4 formula* in Ch 18, *the only document in this volume* in Ch 18, and a metafictional intrusion in Ch 28 and another in Ch 37, all four removed in place and all four called intrusions at the time.

Two of the thirty were not only meta but **false about their own book**, and are listed again below: the panel paragraph in Ch 101 and the note-count line in Ch 108.

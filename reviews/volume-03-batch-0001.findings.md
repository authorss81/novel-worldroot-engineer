## 1. BLOCKING — metafictional narration, thirty instances, in all ten chapters

The single largest defect in the batch, and the one the review log was closing on when it stopped. The narration refers to its own book: *this chapter*, *this batch*, *this volume*, *in two volumes*, *Volume 03*, *the fourth chapter of the first volume*, *no new named member of the cooperative was created in this batch*, *the volume's structural antagonist*, *a later writer must not be allowed to find it in a summary and enlarge it*, *there is no entry in this volume for that well coming back*.

**All thirty are removed**, and each was replaced with the in-world referent the sentence actually needed — *the rest of this*, *this holding*, *the fen's book*, *eleven years*, *the day on the paper*. Nothing load-bearing went with them: the manuscript's habit of telling the reader that a sentence matters is native to this book and is kept everywhere it can be said in the world's own terms. Volume 02 carries eighteen such lines in forty-nine chapters and the Volume 02 close did not object to them; **this batch carried thirty in ten**, and the density is what makes it a defect rather than an idiom. **Volume 01's review history is the standing precedent against them** — `state/continuity.md` records *the Ch 4 formula* in Ch 18, *the only document in this volume* in Ch 18, and a metafictional intrusion in Ch 28 and another in Ch 37, all four removed in place and all four called intrusions at the time.

Two of the thirty were not only meta but **false about their own book**, and are listed again below: the panel paragraph in Ch 101 and the note-count line in Ch 108.

## 2. BLOCKING — Ch 103 had an abandoned sentence in the middle of its turn

`chapter-0103.md`, between the dating of the cuts and the question that ends the chapter, carried a paragraph ending in a colon with nothing after it: *"Then the man of about fifty's voice, from four hundred miles away, in the head of the reader who was standing there, in the mouth of Anneke Roos reading the figure back to a yard in which she was not standing:"* It also **contradicted the chapter's own next page**, where the reader is Merrid Ollvar and the clerk is not in the room. **Deleted.** The chapter now goes from the nine people breathing to the man at the second house asking the question, and the reading of the figure is where it always was.

## 3. BLOCKING — Ch 103's dating arithmetic did not produce its own answer

The method is *count the healed growth, divide by what the ground grows in a year*. The chapter gave four-tenths of an inch as the divisor, **two and a quarter inches** as the healed growth, and then said the division was *between five and six* and concluded *they are one. This season.* Two and a quarter over four tenths is 5.6, and a five-or-six-year-old cut is the opposite of the chapter's finding.

**Corrected to a shade under four tenths**, which divides to a shade under one season, and the finding stands exactly where it stood: the cuts are one season old, this season. The four-tenths divisor is unchanged and is the figure Ch 104 and Ch 105 also use. `state/continuity.md` carried the wrong measurement and is corrected.

## 4. BLOCKING — nine cuts or eleven

Ch 103 dates **nine** and will not touch a tenth. Ch 104 says *the same order nine times on this row* and *nobody cuts nine branches on one row in one week by accident* — while its own section-nine note, Ch 108's closing line and `state/continuity.md` all say **eleven**. The batch's own card says eleven. **Both are now true and the chapter says so**: the row carries eleven, nine were dated and nine were cleaned and read, two were left alone with Marek's reason for leaving them, and nobody guesses at the two. Tova Reed's *nine trees* in Ch 103 is left as it stands, because nine is what had been read in front of her.

## 5. BLOCKING — Ch 99 contradicted its own weekday

*The first of this month came up on a Wednesday* is correct and is one of the three checkable weekday statements. Fourteen lines later: *the thirtieth of last month was a Sunday and the last day of the month was the first of this one.* If the thirtieth was a Sunday the first is a Monday, and day 150 is a **Tuesday**. **Rewritten** to the fact that is actually true and does the same work — the thirtieth and the first came on consecutive days, a day closer than the rule is set, which is why the holding is one day out of step with its own rule for the third time this season.

## 6. BLOCKING — the day on the buying paper was stated eight times and contradicted eight times

The batch said *the eleventh* in eight places and gave day-counts around it that could not all be true: nine days off from the ninth, nine days from the tenth (**in the same sentence as the eleventh**, which from the tenth is one day), eight and nine days from the twelfth, nine days from the twentieth. **One of those readings also made the eleventh a day that passed unremarked inside Chs 105–108, with the man at the second house still saying the buyer was coming.**

**Settled: the day on the buying paper is the seventeenth of next month, day 187, and every instance is recomputed** — thirty-five days from the second, four weeks from the ninth, twenty-seven from the tenth, twenty-five from the twelfth, seventeen from the twentieth. **This is the one date that makes Ch 99–108 and the queued Batch 0002 agree**, because Ch 117 is *The Buyer Comes* on days 187–188 and its card says *the day on the buying paper arrives*. The eleventh of next month would have put the buyer four days outside the batch that is queued to write him. No scene, price, speech or outcome changed; the buyer's flat rate, Merrid's decision, the money and the household's choice are all where they were.

## 7. BLOCKING — Merrid's eleven weeks did not fit the day clock

*She had picked them on the sixth of a month four months back* and has kept them **eleven weeks** — day 36 plus 77 days is day 113, and she is at the gate on day 152. **Corrected to the sixteenth of a month three months back**, which is day 76, and day 76 to day 153 is **eleven weeks exactly**. Two instances.

## 8. BLOCKING — Ch 105's link accounting contradicted itself and the power system's ceiling

*Two active links exist, one at the collar of the second tree in the row, read on the fifth, and one in the pot at Lantern Reach with a graft from the spring that is dead and is not a link and is not counted.* The first of those **is** the pot — the tree read in Ch 101 is the second tree in the row, standing in a pot in the fen's yard — so the clause claimed two links while enumerating one and a corpse, and it left Ch 105's own *two links down on a plateau* and its own *two and three are the whole of what there is* with nothing to count.

**Recast as the numbering the chapter's other sentences already use**, and it closes: **one is the dead graft of the spring, two is the collar of the second tree in the row, three is the scion attempted on the twelfth, four fails and is not attempted.** So two and three are the whole of what there is, the fen is two links down after Ch 105, three is the outside and a fourth is not attempted. The Stage 2 ceiling, the Stage 3 entry and the series contract are untouched. `state/continuity.md` said *two active links exist* and is corrected. **The Batch 0001 card is itself contradictory on this point** — *two active links already* in one sentence and *it has two and cannot get a third back* in another — and the prose follows the second, which is the one that agrees with the chapter.

## 9. BLOCKING — Ch 101's panel was quoted by a document in the chapter that spends it

The panel paragraph said, in bold, that the three lines were spent and that **no document in these ten chapters quotes them**. Thirty lines later the fen's own book entry **quotes all three of them, in full**. The card is also a lock: *quoted by nobody in any document for the rest of the book.* **Both fixed.** The bold paragraph is now the fen's own standing — entered once, goes nowhere else, no form carries it — and the book entry now records the instrument's three lines **by the hour and the bench and not copied out**, which is a better beat than the quotation was: the man of about seventy's *write down what you did not find* survives intact.

## 10. BLOCKING — Ch 108 said the fieldbook had been opened twice, and nothing on the page opens it twice

Lissa's reason for closing the book for the season was *a book open twice in one month is a book somebody can say was altered between the openings*, and *I have opened it twice in three days.* **The chapter narrates one opening, on the seventeenth, and the whole batch's lock is that the book was not opened in ninety-eight chapters.** The sentence also left the reason with nothing to fear.

**Both reasons rebuilt on the truth**: a book open **twice in one season** is the risk, and she has opened it **once, this afternoon, and is not opening it again**. The first reason gains the true line that the only copy in the world is a copy of a reading and not a copy of a book, which is Ch 108's own fourth copy. The closing decision, the locked room and the reason being entered whole are unchanged.

## 11. BLOCKING — Ch 108's fieldbook history was wrong in three ways at once

*The last time it was opened it was put on a chair in a seed house in the month before the flood. That is two volumes and a hundred and seventy days ago.* The flood is **day 47**; the fieldbook was last out of the press on **the twenty-eighth of a month two months back, day 118**, when it was put on a chair in a seed house for a floor to be read out of the middle of a room — **and it was not opened then either**, which is Ch 72 and the standing lock. Day 118 to day 167 is **forty-nine days**, not a hundred and seventy.

**Rewritten to the true history**, which is stronger: the last time it was out of the press, and not opened then either, forty-nine days ago.

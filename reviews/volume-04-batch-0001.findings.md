## The two things to read before drafting Chapters 158–167

**1. The batch's one arithmetic error had already been fixed twice and was still standing in the outline.** A figure was corrected on the page in Ch 153, then corrected again in `state/continuity.md`, and `outline/volume-04.md` carried the wrong one — **twenty pounds eighteen shillings, where the margin over eleven days is seventeen pounds, eight shillings and fourpence.** This is the fault `volume-03-batch-0005.findings.md` names as this manuscript's recurring one: **a figure written into the prose and then copied into the state as fact, except that here the copy went the other way and the source was left behind.** The outline is the document a later phase reads for the rate structure, so this was one line from becoming a fact with three documents disagreeing about it. It is fixed, and the derivation is unchanged and is stated at item 3 of the batch's mechanical list in `state/continuity.md`: 1,045 hundredweight at 4d is £17 8s 4d, and 4d × 1,045 = 4,180d is the same figure.

**2. The month guardrail had drifted into three different documents and was meaningless in all of them.** The outline and two state lines said *October is nine months back by day 268*; the queued Batch 0002 prompt said *by day 285*. **A named month was being carried in a distance, which has no referent in a calendar that names no month, and it was out by a month besides** — October is the first month and the batch's ninth month runs from day 241. All three now carry the outline's rule, which is a rule and can be checked on a page:

> **The ban is on the name and not on the distance.** A day is written as an ordinal, as *of this month*, or as *of last month*. A named month is not carried in a distance, and a day of the first month is a bare number only, never *of this month* and never *of last month*. **The ordinal distances are the house idiom and are untouched by this** — *a month two months back*, *the thirtieth of a month three months back* and *of last month* are legal and are what this batch and the queued prompt are both built on.

The queued prompt also carried a second copy of the same rule at its naming-table section, and that one is fixed too. **The first repair overreached and was corrected in the same pass**: it also forbade *a count of months back* outright, which would have banned the manuscript's own dating idiom on its first appearance in the next batch. The rule as it now stands in all three documents bans the name in a distance and leaves the ordinal distance alone.

## The blocking findings

The same pass fixed the same speech's non-chronological ordering and its mislabelled month (finding 7), and **the speech now tracks the wall line for line and figure for figure.** The occupier also says so on the page, in the narrator's own words, which turns a repair into a beat the chapter was already reaching for.

**2. `outline/volume-04.md:43` carried the wrong margin.** See the first item above. One line, applied.

## The should-fix findings

**3. A false mechanical claim in the audit trail, in two files.** `state/chapter-summaries.md` and `state/continuity.md` both asserted **zero duplicate sentences of nine words or more across the batch.** There are two, and both are deliberate: **the guarantee's operative clause, read out of the document at the head of Ch 149 and said again in speech further down the same chapter** — a document being read aloud in a room, which is what Ch 149 is about — and **the standing figures refrain, *day minus nineteen unmoved on a bed at three and a half degrees both undertakings standing*, in Ch 154 and again in Ch 157.** **Both claims are withdrawn and replaced with the two named repetitions and the reason neither is a defect.** This is the failure mode `reviews/README.md` records this repository being bitten by twice — a state file certifying a property nobody ran, written by the writer that produced the prose. It was caught here by a review that measured. **The manuscript's own rule, restated in the fix: the property worth auditing is that no paragraph ends with a speech open, and that holds.**

**4. The length band had been widened to fit the output.** The outline's own standard is **2,600–3,120** and Volume 03 closed on 2,550–3,120. Ch 151 came in at 2,573 and Ch 157 at 3,193, and `state/current.md` re-baselined the band to *2,550–3,200* to cover them — **and that re-baseline had already propagated into the queued Batch 0002 prompt**, so the next writer would have been told the widened band was the standard. **The claim is fixed and the two chapters are declared instead of absorbed.** The band is back to 2,600–3,120 in the prompt and in all three state files, with Ch 151 and Ch 157 named as exceptions and the reason given. The queued prompt now carries a sentence saying that a chapter landing outside the band **is a fact to report and not a band to widen afterwards.**

**5. The half-inch lock in the outline did not parse, and Batch 0003 was going to read it as a lock.** It read *the figure Chapter 137 named as the ground's share sixteen days before the four-inch reading*, which conflates a draw on the head of a well with a reading of this well, and gives an interval that does not close: **the four-inch draw is Ch 135, dated the twelfth; the reading beside it is day 226, fourteen days later and not sixteen.** The outline now states the canon — **the half inch is Ch 137's figure and is the ground's share as of day 196, and it may not be carried forward as a figure, added to or taken off any later reading, or made to do arithmetic; and the four inches is a draw and not a reading and may not be dated sixteen days before anything.** The Cauldron Reach series is also now written out as settled day numbers in `state/continuity.md`, with the naming each day takes from a scene inside days 241–270, because Batch 0003 reads that well and a name changes at the month turn while a day number does not.

**6. The month guardrail, across three documents.** See the second item above. Applied in `outline/volume-04.md`, `state/current.md`'s guardrail paragraph via `state/continuity.md`, `state/chapter-summaries.md`, and twice in the queued prompt.

**7. Minor, Ch 151: the speech gave the series in a different order from the wall and mislabelled a month.** It put the sixteenth of the month after the four-months-back month before the fourteenth of that same month, and called a reading three months back *this month two months back*. **The wall block already had it right, so the slip reinforced the chapter's own argument and was worth the one pass.** Corrected in the same edit as finding 1.

**8. Unreconciled between the series card and the volume outline — flagged in writing, resolved by nobody.** `outline/series.md` gives Volume 04 a next question — *What is happening beneath the sealed Glassward, and can a bridge carry responsibility rather than only water?* — that `outline/volume-04.md` does not carry, and `state/open-threads.md` states that the series card is the contract where the two differ. **Nothing was changed in either document and no plot moved.** The question is now written down in `state/open-threads.md` with what a later writer may and may not do about it: no chapter of Volume 04 answers it, the Glassward and the sealed record stay sealed, and *a bridge carrying responsibility rather than only water* is a Stage 4 idea this volume reaches as **a man who is the only person who can hold a pattern being entered as a bottleneck and not as a system** — the same thought in this volume's vocabulary, and not the same sentence. Reconciling the two documents is a close-phase or Volume 05 decision and belongs there.

## The prose note, and the one thing declined

**9. Paragraph length and bold density — measured against the manuscript's own convention, and the prose left alone.** The review called the long-paragraph tail a series-wide drift, put this batch slightly below Volume 03 on paragraph length, and named the 380-word paragraph in Ch 152 as carrying the batch's central turn. **Measured, every paragraph over 200 words in the batch is a block quote or an italic document note — sixteen of them, the longest at 516 words in Ch 157 — and not one is narrative prose.** That is the shape Volume 03 runs in: its longest paragraph is 761 words and eighty-eight of its paragraphs exceed 200. Breaking the Ch 152 note would have meant breaking the batch's central turn out of the document it belongs to, in a manuscript whose block quotes are how a fen's records are made audible. **Declined, and recorded in `state/continuity.md` so a later pass does not re-attempt it.** The bold density — 44% of sentences — is a property of the whole manuscript and is not this batch's to change alone; it is noted here for whoever eventually looks at it across a whole volume.

**The declined trim, recorded so it is a decision and not an oversight.** Finding 4 could have been answered by cutting Ch 157 from 3,193 to inside 3,120. **Seventy-three words were not taken out of it.** Every candidate in that chapter is either one of its own speeches — the reader's three things, the steward's second *we cannot tell*, the licensed man's refusal, the four answers and the *write it twice* page — or the standing figures and the section-nine note, which `state/open-threads.md` and the Volume 03 reviews both name as this manuscript's ritual. **The trim would have cost a fact to buy a number.** The chapter was drafted at 3,983 and had already been trimmed nine times; nothing in it is padding. It is declared over the ceiling instead, in all three state files and in the queued prompt, where a later writer will find it.

## A. Arithmetic — two errors, both certified in state as verified

**A1. `475 pence = one pound three shillings and fifteen pence` was wrong.** 475p is 39s 7d = **£1 19s 7d**; £1 3s 15d is 291p. It stood at `chapter-0144.md:37`, `:41` and `:65` (the man of about fifty repeats it), and in `state/continuity.md` four times, `state/current.md` twice, `state/chapter-summaries.md` twice, `state/open-threads.md` twice, and in the close prompt's handoff paragraph. `state/continuity.md` presented the line as *the arithmetic, checked line by line and holding*.

*Fixed.* **One pound, nineteen shillings and sevenpence** everywhere, with the derivation on the page: four hundred and seventy-five pence, which is thirty-nine shillings and seven pence. Nothing else in the volume depended on it, and no sum in Ch 144 folds it into another figure.

**A2. "Six days at one shilling and sevenpence is ten shillings and sixpence" was wrong twice over.** Six times 1s 7d is 9s 6d, not 10s 6d; and more seriously, canon makes one shilling and sevenpence a **total**. `chapter-0128.md:57` and `state/continuity.md:2094`: *those six days are one shilling and sevenpence at her figure of three shillings and eightpence for fourteen, because a day out of that fortnight is a little over three pence and this body will not enter a fraction and rounds a person up.* `chapter-0143.md:58` and `chapter-0144.md:19` both said **a day**, and Ch 144 then multiplied it.

*Fixed.* **The word "a day" and the multiplied figure are both struck**, in the prose and in four state files. Ch 144's entry now reads *at one shilling and sevenpence for the six days*, and Ch 143's handoff reads the same. The continuity arithmetic line now states the canon and says why.

## B. Contradictions between chapters

**B3 (the reviewer's worst). Two incompatible copy counts in one chapter.** `chapter-0145.md:75` has the fen read out **one** copy it can check and **four** it cannot; `:77` has Marek Vale say **two** and **three**; and `:79`, in Marek's own next paragraph, says *four of them are books that this body cannot correct*. Two of the three are right: the reader of this body entered the well house door as one of the five places at `:21` while saying **in the same breath** that it was the only one of the five that was not a copy of anything, and said at `:25` that the table in the yard is the only one of the five this holding can check.

*Fixed the way the reviewer offered, and the way the manuscript does everything.* **Marek keeps every word he said.** The count is corrected in the room, **he corrects it himself before the clerk has it down**, he gives the reason (he can read the door, and a thing a man can read is not a thing this body holds a copy of), the reader's own entry of the fourth is cited, the corrected count is **one and four**, Dunmar Fitch enters it and reads it back, and **the fen enters that it stood a day against its own figure without anybody noticing.** Nothing was deleted and no line was softened. The state canon moves from two and three to one and four, and `state/continuity.md` now keeps two and three as a thing that was said and was taken back.

**B1. The man of about seventy's tenth bring-out was on the wrong day.** `chapter-0142.md:79` and `:83` said the twenty-eighth; `chapter-0144.md:69` and deviation 9 said the twenty-seventh. Compounding it, at `:81` he says *this morning an office has gone and written down*, and the declaration arrived on the morning of the twenty-seventh.

*Fixed.* **The bring-out moves to the evening of the twenty-seventh**, an hour after the steward finishes. That is the only day the declaration supports, it is what Ch 144 and deviation 9 already said, and it is the only slot after the steward's seventh-hour *who loses* in which he could have been given the three things she found, in that order, as `:79` requires. **Ch 142 now sits entirely on day 237**; the day clock, the chapter summary and the Ch 142 row are corrected.

**B2. `chapter-0141.md:25` contradicted Ch 143.** *This sheet was not sent down the road and this holding was not asked for it and does not have it* — against Ch 143, where it comes down the four-day road on a rider the occupier hired and is in the yard on the twenty-ninth.

*Fixed by scoping the claim to its hour.* **This sheet was not sent down the road by the four of them. This holding was not asked for it, and on the day it was written this holding did not have it and did not know that it was going to arrive.** The "does not have it" tense was the actual contradiction, and it is now true as written.

**B4. `chapter-0142.md:63` had five places and two of them nine hundred miles off.** Ch 145's enumeration has exactly one nine hundred miles off — Cray — with the rest at nine miles, four days by road, the yard, and the door at nine hundred yards. The five-place count is also not made until the fourth of the ninth, three days later.

*Fixed.* **Four places, and one of the four nine hundred miles off**, which is the count of the fourth of the eighth. Net zero words.

## C. Frame slips

**C1. `chapter-0143.md:37` had the wrong name for the right day.** *One hundred and sixty-four on the twentieth of last month* — but Ch 143 sits on the thirtieth of the eighth month, where days 211–240 are **of this month**, and 164 is day 230, cut in Batch 0004's Ch 138. This is the exact error the batch's own clocks table warns about, leaked into prose.

*Fixed.* **The twentieth of this month.** The figure was never in doubt and is not changed.

**C2. `chapter-0147.md:16` claimed a reading-day rule the batch refutes.** *Read out in a yard on the first, the sixteenth and the thirtieth of every month* — the eighth month was read on the sixteenth, the twenty-first and the thirtieth, and the ninth on the tenth.

*Fixed.* **On the reading days of every month**, with the fen entering that the reading days are not the same days in every month, that it has never entered them as a standing rule, and **that a rule which has to be looked up is not a rule** — which is the volume's own argument about the standing rule, applied to something smaller.

## D. Role collision — Tova Reed

`chapter-0140.md:17` gave her *the steward of a seed house in a fen for the same eleven years*, a second stewardship nine hundred miles from the one she already has. That office is Lissa Vale's by `bible/characters.md:22`, by the Ch 39 annex signature (*Lissa Vale for the seed house … Tova Reed for the trust, alone*), and by Ch 146, where Lissa asks who loses in the fen. It also breaks this batch's own lock, that Tova Reed at Cray is *a fact about the trust and not about this holding*. Related and low severity: `:7` and `:17` both said *a fen nine hundred miles off*, which reads twice as the fen being nine hundred miles from itself.

*Fixed.* **She is the steward of that trust and is not of this holding and does not keep a seed house in a fen and has never kept one, and a person in this yard who says otherwise has heard it from a form.** The clerk at the far end is now *the clerk of that counting house*, and the distance at `:7` is *nine hundred miles from that building*. Net 21 words out.

## E. One clause arguing against its own figure

`chapter-0144.md:29`: *Seventy-five hundredweight **of that** is the going.* The entry charges three journeys of 25 hundredweight **on top of** the 400 specification, and the sentence immediately before it argues the additive case (*it is not going to be four hundred and fifty because a man is expensive*). But *of that* reads as 75 delivered out of the four hundred, which is 325, not 475. **The number was right; one clause was not.**

*Fixed.* **Seventy-five hundredweight on top of it**, which is what the form has been since the sixth month. One word shorter.

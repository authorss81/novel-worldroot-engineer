## The twelve defects this pass found in its own draft, and repaired before the review

**These were found by the writer, not by a reviewer, and the repair is the writer's own
certification and is worth nothing as a check. The independent check is the reviewer's.**

1. **Ch 334 misdated the batch's central refusal.** It said the fifth of the five was refused
   *on the second day of this month* and that the writer had been quiet *for three days*. The
   refusal is Ch 326, the twenty-seventh of the sixth month, which on day 545 is *of last
   month* and eight days back. The second day of this month is day 542, which is Ch 331's
   morning and a **different** refusal, the shortcut at the edge of the ring. Repaired in both
   halves. **The two figures agreed with each other and both were wrong, which is the shape of
   a date copied off a neighbouring chapter rather than derived.**
2. **Ch 334 said the five rows on line 41 were corrected *five days ago*.** They were corrected
   at `chapter-0293.md`, the twenty-third of the fifth month. Five days is the interval
   `chapter-0295.md:41` carries, because that chapter is the twenty-sixth of the fifth. As
   printed the sentence placed a fresh correction on day 540, four days before the chapter that
   declines to correct them again, and so manufactured the second correction it forbids.
   Repaired to *in a room on the twenty-third of the fifth*, a day and not an interval.
3. **Ch 332 had Pike's load-bearing interval six days out.** He said the blight had gone inside
   the inhabited line *in nine days while a thing made of two people and a floor has been running
   for the same nine days*. The connection has run fifteen days on day 543, as the same chapter
   says eight lines lower. Repaired so that he names both intervals and says they are not the
   same number. **The state layer had already "corrected" this in two places and was wrong both
   times, and the page was the wrong one.**
4. **The numbered-month frame was wrong in five places and is not one of the four the world
   allows.** *The second month* was used for the sixth month at `chapter-0328.md` (twice),
   `chapter-0331.md` and `chapter-0334.md` (twice). A reader meeting it would take the second
   month of the year, a year and a half early, since the numbered form exists in this manuscript
   only as *the eleventh of the first month*. Repaired to *of last month* in four places and to
   *a month back* in the fifth. The same wrong frame had propagated into five places in
   `state/continuity.md` and two in `state/chapter-summaries.md`; all seven are corrected.
5. **The batch opened the drawer it had entered ten times as unopened.** Every standing entry
   and every clerk's block prints *the drawer was not opened*, which is the locked drawer in the
   cart shed where the boards are, and four chapters put paper into *the drawer at the end of the
   middle table* with nothing on the page telling the two apart. The paper drawer is now **the
   shallow drawer under the middle table** in every place it is filled, read or spoken of, and
   Ch 325 says once, where it first appears, that it is not the drawer the boards are in and is
   not locked. The locked drawer's line is untouched in all twenty of its occurrences.
6. **Ch 330 had two paragraphs run together with no space and no break** —
   `...which of those two groups they were in.And then Tova Reed said one thing`. An artefact of
   an earlier edit. Repaired. A script now checks all ten files for a lower-case letter followed
   by a full stop and a capital and returns zero.
7. **Ch 334 had the ledger copy on the table and on a shelf**, and dated an arrival it did not
   narrate. It *came off the shelf with the terms* and went *back on the shelf where it has been
   for five days*, and the terms were in a drawer. Repaired: the terms came out of the shallow
   drawer onto the middle table and the ledger came down off the shelf beside them, and the five
   days is gone.
8. **Four smaller faults.** Ch 334's *a Friday in the second week of a month* when the fifth of
   a month is in its first week. Ch 331's *about nine inches lower at the middle*, which is a
   figure on the ring of bare ground in the chapter whose card says the ring is not measured —
   the ground now only *lies lower*, with no figure and the same refusal of a level. Ch 333's
   *and the reason is that the reader of this body put his left forearm on it*, a reason with
   nothing in front of it, now *what was on it, which was*. And Ch 327's *a Friday in the sixth
   week of eleven years*, which cannot be read at all, now *a Friday in the fourth week of a
   month*.
9. **The state layer said the uncheckable-numbers count is named in four of the ten at Ch 325,
   327, 329 and 334.** It is named at **325, 329, 333 and 334**; Ch 327's entry is about the
   second gate and the ratio of nineteen and nineteen.
10. **The state layer said the woman of about thirty-eight of Marden's own count is EIGHTEEN, at
    Ch 334.** False, and dangerous twice over: she gave no reason first in that room, and
    *eighteen* is also the compost run. **She is seventeen, at Ch 328**, and Ch 334 has been
    given her, speaking without the reason first, with the chapter saying so.
11. **The state layer said the nil did not move twice, on the twenty-ninth and on the fifth,

12. **The state layer cited the spending of the count of one at `chapter-0333.md:27`**, which is

## The seven judgements, recorded and not repaired

1. Three sentences are near-duplicates of `chapter-0321.md` and one description is shared with
   `chapter-0326.md`. None is byte-identical, which is the rule, and the repair would be to say
   less about the derivation in a chapter whose whole subject is the derivation.
2. Ch 327 has three consecutive inventory paragraphs and Ch 330 states its four clocks five
   times. A chapter about four clocks that says them once is a chapter that has hidden its
   subject.
3. Ch 332 is the second-shortest file in the batch and its one scene is the most compressed of
   the ten. The alternative was a longer chapter or a second room, and the volume owes Pike
   exactly one room.
4. The reader of this body's refusal to write a heading on the sixth column is stated three times
   in Ch 334, and each is a different person being answered.
5. The man of about fifty's not-writing-a-number-down argument and the reader of this body's
   not-reaching-for-a-thing argument are near neighbours in Ch 329 and Ch 331; one is about a
   book and the other about a person who is not in the room.
6. Tova Reed makes a second pass at herself in Ch 327 after Ch 326, and the two passes are about
   different things, a favour and a copy.
7. **The honest statement: this batch is thinner on scene and heavier on inventory than Batch
   0003.** The reason is that Movement 4 is a movement about pieces of paper, and a writer who
   wanted more weather in it would have to invent some.

# Volume 04, Batch 0003 — Chapters 168–177 — review findings and what was applied

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-27, from `logs/batch-0003.review.log`. **Repaired in place.** No scene was replaced, no chapter was restarted, and the planned plot of all ten chapters is unchanged. The wall, the money, the month rule, the per-chapter word band, the day-286 reading and the day-301 hand-off are all where they were.

## 1. BLOCKING, APPLIED — the nine-feet comparison figure was mis-dated, and this batch promoted the error into a lock

**The referent is day 136. Day 136 is the sixteenth of the fifth month.** Every shortfall on the wall at Cauldron Reach is measured against that one reading, and this batch was naming it *the ninth of a month four months back*.

Two separate faults in one phrase. The **ordinal** is wrong — it is the sixteenth, and Volume 03's chapters say so without interruption from Ch 106 onward. The **month distance** is wrong — months run thirty days, so from this batch's frame of days 286–300, the tenth month, days 121–150 are *a month five months back*, and the batch's own naming table says so four lines above the row where the error was written down.

Worse than a slip: the two halves of the same document disagreed with each other. `chapter-0168.md:33` put nine feet on the **sixteenth**, and the shortfall was stated against the **ninth** at `:65`, `:89`, `chapter-0170.md:67` and `chapter-0177.md:27` — a seven-day gap a reader could see on the page. And `state/current.md` stated it as settled fact and said it could not be moved.

**Origin.** Batch 0003's own prompt, line 73, mapped day 136 to *a month four months back* against its own table, and line 77 then wrote *"may not put a cause on the ninth"*, which is where the ordinal came from. The prose inherited both.

**Applied.** Corrected to *the sixteenth of a month five months back* in Chs 168 (three places), 170 and 177; in the Batch 0003 prompt (six places, source of the error); in the Batch 0004 prompt, where it had got **three** months out and was fenced as settled (four places); and in `state/current.md`, `state/continuity.md`, `state/open-threads.md` and `state/chapter-summaries.md`.

**This does not overrule the Volume 03 close.** `reviews/volume-03.findings.md` §3 held that Ch 118's *the sixteenth of a month two months back* is correct because its referent is day 136. That still holds, and this repair is the same rule applied in a later frame: day 136 is day 136 from any frame, and the name is frame-relative. The close's finding and this repair are consistent.

**One inherited instance is recorded and not harmonised.** `chapter-0167.md`, in closed Batch 0002, still reads *the ninth of a month four months back*. That batch is not this phase's and has been reviewed and closed; its prose has not been touched. The discrepancy is now written down in `state/continuity.md` under the Batch 0002 numbers-and-records row, with a lock: name the reading the **sixteenth**, and name the distance from the frame of the chapter that is speaking. **It is two words and it belongs to whoever owns Batch 0002.**

## 2. BLOCKING, APPLIED — the Batch 0004 prompt compounded finding 1 instead of catching it

It re-based the table correctly for its own frame and then wrote the comparison as *the ninth of a month eight months back*, which under that same table is day 69. Three months out, wrong day of the month, and written as settled in three places.

**Applied.** *the sixteenth of a month six months back*, in the readings table and in the three standing-figures restatements. The whole prompt's naming table has since been audited item by item against its own buckets.

## 3. NOT A DEFECT — DECLINED, WITH ONE WORDING REPAIR — the use log in Ch 177

The review read line 21 as putting the twentieth's gauge test inside the log and out of it in one paragraph, and on the wrong day. **The chapters do not say that, and the two closed chapters settle it.**

- Ch 164, day 279, the ninth: a licensed man, a contact on boarded floor, a plain wire gauge test written down first, the mark hot for a day, the hand no use for about a day. **That is the fifteenth log line.**
- Ch 167, batch-0002, restates the same event as the fifteenth line.
- Ch 170, day 290, the twentieth: a second contact, a second gauge test, and **no line written**, with the clerk entering why before he wrote anything else.
- Ch 172, day 293: the instrument is in the cart shed, no licensed man called, none came.

So there is one gauge test in the log on the ninth and a different one out of the log on the twentieth. Both are correct, on their own days, and the reviewer conflated them.

**What was genuinely wrong, and the whole of the repair:** the phrase *entered on the same line and not in the log* is self-contradictory on its face. It now reads *entered on the same standing entry and not in the log* — which is the usage Ch 170 already establishes, where "the same line" means the line of the standing entry and not of the log. **No fact, no count and no day was changed.**

## 4. BLOCKING, APPLIED — the word *seat* was on no page and two files said it was on one

`state/current.md` said **THE WORD *SEAT* DOES NOT APPEAR.** `state/continuity.md` said **THE WORD *SEAT* IS USED ONCE IN THE BATCH, IN THAT SPEECH**, and the Batch 0004 prompt built a "may not be used again" guardrail on the false version. The prose is right and the outline grants permission, not obligation — `outline/volume-04.md` guardrail 7 says the word *may now appear*. So the record and the guardrail were corrected, and the correction preserves the lock instead of granting new freedom: **the word is not on a page, the man who would have used it is not in Batch 0004's cards, and the permission is unspent.**

## 5. APPLIED — a verbatim tic had replaced a signature

*…asked for his initial to be put against nothing and it went in, and the count of that request is…* ran seven times, the first six word for word. The read-back ritual carried real weight at its third use; at the eighteenth it is a mannerism. **The phrasing is now varied in Chs 173, 175 and 176 and the count is untouched.** Chs 168, 169 and 174 keep the plain form, so the ritual still reads as a ritual.

**The count was not renumbered.** The sequence across the volume is fifteen to thirty-seven, one per occurrence, and in this batch it reads twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-three, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven, so **thirty-one and thirty-two are not on the page**. Batch 0001 has the identical allowance at seventeen. The count is a season count and not a chapter count; it is now thirty-seven, and renumbering it would move a figure four batches have already read. **Recorded, not harmonised.**

## 6. APPLIED — the occupier's four-line letter was reprinted near-verbatim

Seven of nine lines identical between `chapter-0168.md:63–71` and `chapter-0170.md:65–73`, two chapters apart. *"Entered them entire"* justifies the reprint; it does not justify making a reader read the same nine lines twice.

**Repaired by addition rather than subtraction.** The full letter stays in Ch 168, where it is written. In Ch 170, where it is the payoff of a four-day road, the first three lines are now named as the three things this holding already had before the bag came off the road, and **only line Four — the half inch up and the half inch down, and the man with the spade — is printed entire**, as the one that is news. That sharpens the chapter: three of the four told this holding nothing it did not have and the fourth told it something it cannot use. **Line Four is the line Ch 170's next paragraph reasons about, so nothing downstream lost its footing.**

## 7. APPLIED — meta language in narration proper

Four leaks outside the block-quote documents, where the fourth wall is licensed: `chapter-0168.md:79` *the only time in this batch*; `chapter-0172.md:41` *the check is the chapter*; `chapter-0173.md:61` *the question this movement turns on*; `chapter-0174.md:75` *the thing the chapter is about*. Now *the day*. Volume 03's own usage is the measure: **this book** 52 times, **this volume** 9, **this chapter** 2, **this movement** 0.

**The 168 clause was cut rather than reworded, and that is deliberate.** The obvious repair — *the first time in this book* — is **false**: Ch 167 already read a north-gate handoff naming the shelf four hundred miles off. The claim was removed rather than made less specific.

Also `chapter-0171.md:67`, *no chapter of it is going to put it there*, which is awkward English; now *nothing in this book is going to put it there*, which keeps the lock.

## 8. DECLINED — chapter endings

The review found six of ten ending without forward pull. **Checked all ten closing lines against them: eight carry a live item** — the four-day road (paid off in Ch 170), the form on the bench and the day the answer is given, the man who is getting more willing and is not becoming tame, a food system that can run out, the bottleneck against a book on the first of next month, the count of four things this body cannot ask about, the month closing tomorrow against a figure this book does not know. **Rewriting endings to add hooks would be reworking the batch, and the instruction for this pass is to repair findings, not re-shape the batch.** Not applied.

## 9. APPLIED — Ch 177 recapped itself twice in nine lines

Five consecutive dialogue lines opened *And a…* and recapped Chs 172–176 back to the yard; the list given to the man of about seventy seven lines later repeated the same items. **The openings are varied** — *The things that came up a road this month* / *A food, then* / *A man who is the only person…* / *A word used in a yard…* / *And a name at the foot of a sheet* — **and the second recital is cut back to the standing figures**, ending on the name, which is what *Ask who is paying* turns on.

## 10. BORDERLINE, NOT A DEFECT — declined

At `chapter-0168.md:83` and `chapter-0177.md:74` the reader of this body says *the fen* in the third person inside a spoken line. That is the house convention and the reader reports on the fen throughout, and the same construction at 168:83 was not objected to. **The self-conscious beat at 177:74 — *the fen entered that it is getting good at this* — is one of the better lines in the chapter.** Not applied.

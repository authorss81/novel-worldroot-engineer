## 6. The close-up, against the outline, item by item

**Central pressure — MET, and turned into a category.** `outline/volume-04.md:13` asks for a fen that cannot pay for the water, cannot vote on the well, cannot send a crew across the ground without a licence, and cannot find out whose order the water is moving on. **What the volume produced is better than the outline asked for: a fen of sixteen households on one head with no charter stops being a condition and becomes a CLASS.** A form at Ch 179 says a migration along a road is a licensed use, at a rate, under a class assigned by a county; Ch 180 has this holding fill the class in **with its own hand** and give the reason before doing it; and Ch 194 has a piece of paper confirm a fourpence this holding derived six weeks earlier. **The volume's thesis is that a body with no standing acquires one by being written down, and by Ch 194 it is holding a rate it computed itself and cannot get out of.** Fourpence the hundredweight, a class this holding put itself in with its own hand, and the difference between that price and a fair one, £30 a week, are the volume's money and they are all still open at the end.

**Midpoint reversal — MET, and it is the concealment.** Ch 156: an undefendable prediction is made in a room with four people in it and not written down, and Tova Reed names the danger in four words — *a thing that is true and in one room is not a secret yet; the day it is in the same room again is the day it is a secret*. Ch 165: the prediction comes true on a Sunday on which nothing happens, and the chapter's real argument is that **a prediction that comes and goes with nothing attached to it is not proved wrong by having nothing happen on the day; it is only proved wrong on a day when something does happen and it was not written down.** The apology is Ch 182, in a full yard, in daylight, on an ordinary Saturday, with the business of the day carrying on around it. **The outline calls this the worst thing he does in the volume, and the volume earns that.**

**Escalation across Movements 1–5 — MET, and its shape is the finding.** The physical events are four in forty-nine chapters and they escalate honestly: a well stops (Ch 154), a second goes off and does not come back (Ch 155), the fen's own rule is broken and it costs a hand and a fever (Ch 155), a pail goes out and does not come back (Ch 158), a hold is made for a crew of four and the answer to *who else can do that* is one word (Ch 173), a second draw is stopped correctly and a road does not open (Ch 185), a corridor is flooded that is three hundred yards from the one that was drawn (Ch 192), a seed vault goes under and is not recovered (Ch 193). **The flat stretch is Chs 159–163, five consecutive chapters in which no person changes state and the only event is post arriving, and the flattest run is Chs 179–181, three documents in three days with the same five-beat shape.** That is a defensible consequence of a book whose antagonist is a structure and whose hero has nobody to collide with, and the volume says so out loud at Ch 186:39 — *nobody in that room stopped me* — which is the tensest line in the volume and it is a woman reporting her own misconduct in a room with the doors shut.

**Climax 186–192 — MET on every element the outline names.** Four people carry water into a salt pan with no custom for standing over a crew, and the absence of the custom is entered before the work starts (Ch 192:9). **The corridor the sheet describes is three hundred yards south of where it was drawn and the plan is on the page with the hour on it and was not torn up** (Ch 192:73). A hold for four is put on the work, and the pattern-holder is at the top of the slope and does not go down. **A seed vault built at forty households' own cost on ground the district calls Crown-held and has never held goes under at about the seventh hour of day 339, four hundred yards off, with no holder, no charter and no form on which anybody can be asked, and it is not recovered.** ROALD TRENCH and SENA WICK are named, in a woman's hand, and this holding was not there and did not ask. **Resolution 192–196 — MET on every element.** The vault goes under unrecovered. Four soldiers and a schedule classify this holding in its own hand. **A cross-region seed agreement names its own price out loud** — 400 hundredweight at fourpence is £6 13s 4d, at sixteen pence is £26 13s 4d, and the difference is exactly £20, and the exactness is the point (Ch 195:51, 53). **The seedwright's condition is paid exactly**: the man of the north row with the cough is asked twice, on two days of his own choosing, by somebody who is not Marek, in a yard, out loud, and is allowed to refuse both times, and he says no once and yes once, and the reason for the first no is in the seed-route ledger in his own words, and on the yes he enters that he would rather it was him finding out on a Tuesday. **The question enters the book unanswered and available and not taken, and the volume says the reason out loud: a month turned and the book is where things go.** **The count of people who can hold a pattern for four becomes two**, and this holding cannot do the second thing and does not know who can. **The bottleneck sentence is said twice on purpose and the reason for saying it twice is said out loud too.** **The twentieth closes with the standing figures read in a yard of about nine** — a thirteen-entry ledger, entered by the fen and not by the clerk, and that is why the third-block limit holds on the volume's last chapter.

**The deeper answer.** The volume is about the difference between *not knowing* and *refusing*, and it refuses to resolve it in the reader's favour. Ch 172 states the method in three sentences: *a thing that is food is a thing with a food system; a food system is a thing that can run out; this holding cannot say when and cannot say who would be harmed by it running out.* Ch 175 has the thing itself refuse, through a man, and the refusal arrives carrying its own number and spends four sentences disarming it: *eleven is not a number of people; it is a number of the ones who came to the decision.* Ch 186 separates a gate count from a record and the fen's act of pricing the count from the household's act of keeping it. **And the deepest thing the volume does is refuse to let a refusal be a virtue: Ch 171:47 asks, in front of twenty-five people on purpose, whether a man's refusal is about the word or about the counting, and says the fen has to know which of those two it is before it writes his refusal down in its own book as a virtue.**

## 7. The meta-language question — DECIDED, and the decision is to sweep

**Three completed volume closes did not catch this, and the reason was that the counts they were working from were wrong.** The claim *this batch* appears 28 times in Volume 01, 72 in Volume 02, 127 in Volume 03 and 98 in Volume 04 is not supported by any file. This book = 23, 23, 52, 183.** **Volume 04 contained no instance of *this batch* and no instance of the bare word *batch*.** The premise that a sweep rewrites prose in every volume is false. The manuscript's hardest class is twelve instances and none of them was in the volume being closed.

**The test applied, and it is the test the record itself proposed: does the word have a referent inside the world?**

- ***This book* has one.** It is the holding's own book, on the middle of the table in the seed house, which anybody may walk up to and read. **183 instances in this volume and not one of them is touched.** They are the volume's spine.
- ***This volume*, *this chapter* and *batch* have none.** There is no volume, no chapter and no batch in a fen with no charter.
- ***This season* is a real frame in this world** — the frame the book already runs its counts in — **and both uses of the standing rule, on the twenty-second of the ninth month and the thirteenth of the eleventh, are inside one season.** Using it for a count of uses is not a softening. **The sweep: twenty-eight sites in Volume 04, twelve of *chapter* and sixteen of *this volume*, and every replacement is a phrase the manuscript already uses.** The narrator's signposts become *the day*, *the day's work* or *this holding's own*. The counts of uses become *this season*.

## Blocking

**1. Nia returns, against an explicit hard lock and the outline.** `chapter-0047.md` had her come up the fen road on the morning of the nineteenth with the letter. Hard lock 15 says *Nia is gone and does not return within the volume*; `outline/volume-01.md` says it twice, *the volume does not get her back* and *she does not come back within the volume*. The lock permits the **letter** to be settled on the page, not her return. `state/current.md` then canonised the return under *Subordinate cast promoted*, and she was unaccounted for at the volume's end.

*Fixed.* The return is cut. **The letter comes into the seed house on the nineteenth, by hand, sealed, unopened, brought by a person who went on up the road and did not stop.** Nobody in the fen opens it and nobody writes down why it was not delivered, because the person who carried it did not give a reason. Anneke Roos enters the fact that this body wrote to its contractor and did not send the letter, in her own hand with the hour, and Marek enters one sentence that is about himself and not about her. **She does not appear again and is not in the volume.** The state's canon of the return is withdrawn.

**2. The bypass gets dug, and the text says so in a lawyer's sentence.** `chapter-0045.md` had Marek propose *thirty or forty yards of ditch, by hand, tonight, in the rain, from the top of the field to the old channel at Weir Row*, followed by *It is the bypass. It is not the bypass. It is the same length and the same place and it is the same work and I know it.* That is the outline's own definition of the refused bypass, `chapter-0045.md` defines it identically at line 21, and `chapter-0046.md` confirmed it finished. Hard lock 1 says the bypass *may not be built, funded, or appear*, and `chapter-0041.md` enters in the cooperative's own book, five chapters earlier, *the bypass was not dug*. Secondary: thirty or forty yards at four feet, by five people, between the seventh and the eleventh hour, is not credible labour for the batch's central physical act. The prompt is self-contradictory here — lock 1 forbids the bypass while Movement 5 says the climax pays it by an improvised cut — and the nine-foot toe ditch satisfies both.

*Fixed.* **The new run is removed entirely.** The night's work is now **the same hole made bigger** — nine feet to fourteen, four foot to four foot six, the lip off the inside of the toe — **and a hundred and forty yards of old channel and a culvert rodded out with a hook and a bar.** Both are this holding's own existing works; there is no new run in either. Marek keeps the honest beat and loses the false one: *a bigger hole in a bank I have already cut is the same hand, on the same bank, in the same hour, on the same ground, and in four years a man in a room is going to find out and he will be right, and it will be the whole of what he finds, because there is no new run in it and there is not going to be one.* Ch 46's opening now has the branch coming up out of the **silt of the cleared channel** instead of out of a new cut, and Weir Row is stated as the two hundred yards up the fen road that it has always been.

**3. Lissa breaches a written undertaking four days early and never enters the breach.** `chapter-0046.md` had her take the instrument out of the press at four in the morning on the seventeenth; Bray raised exactly that, and her answer addressed *opening* rather than *producing*. The character whose Chapter 38 arc is declaring her own lateness in the same words was not made to do it here.

*Fixed.* **She declares the breach herself, in one answer, unasked, and enters it as the lateness in the same words she entered the lateness of the first line of the use log**, and gives the reason in her own hand because the reason is hers: the water was over the fourth yard of that yard at four in the morning, the press is on the inside wall of the seed house, she is the woman who keeps it, and she sat up with it for two hours and decided at half past three that she would not hand a room in six weeks a thing lost in a flood that had not been lost at all. The officer's room in Ch 48 now also records that the custodian put the whole of the use log on a bench in a flood two days before the sitting, *which was not the hearing*.

**4. `chapter-0046.md` moves the March's tin out of the press.** *Taken the instrument out from under the ledger with the tin still on top of it* — while Ch 39 and Ch 49 both have the tin under the ledger in the press, unmoved, at risk, since the nineteenth of last month, and Ch 49 then puts it back, so the batch contradicted itself across three chapters.

*Fixed.* **The tin never leaves the shelf**, and says why: *a tin of seed is not a document and she was not going to move a thing in a press in the dark because her hands were full.* The seed house's undertaking that it will not be moved holds, and the thirty-three days at risk are real.

**5. The use log has four entries and the text says it has two.** The read at the bench named a first line, a second line, *the entry of the fourteenth* and *the entry of the sixth*, in a book `chapter-0038.md` fixes at two lines.

*Fixed.* **It is four lines and they are named**: the tenth of last month entered on the eleventh, the twentieth of last month, the refusal of the fourth of this month with its reason, and **the seventeenth at four in the morning, on the back of the page, initialled by Lissa Vale herself, which is a line that says a thing came out of a press.**

**6. Ch 47 dates the clerk's visit and the hearing to the wrong weekday.** Two lines said *on Tuesday*; the clerk came on the twentieth, a Sunday, and the sitting is on the twenty-first, a Monday. Three dates for one sequence.

*Fixed.* The clerk is the twentieth, the sitting is the twenty-first, and **Anneke Roos's line about where the fact is going to be said now says the twenty-first**, which is what she is talking about.

**7. Ch 46 contradicts itself on the mill board inside two lines.** *The highest figure on Rowan Teale's board in thirty-one years is two hundred and sixty-one*, and two lines later, *Four hundred and nineteen … and the highest on it in thirty-one years*. `state/continuity.md` carried both halves.

*Fixed.* **Two hundred and sixty-one is the highest the board had; four hundred and nineteen is higher than anything on it in thirty-one years.** The state table is corrected.

**8. Ch 43 times one examination two ways, and Ch 44's pump arithmetic does not run.** The examination was at *the fourth hour of the Monday morning* against a log entry at the seventh hour and the licensed reader's own *seven in the morning*; the pump was on *nineteen hours* at that reading and *thirty-one hours* at the seventh hour of the fifteenth, which belongs to seven in the evening on the fourteenth and is not reachable from either later reading. And the speech said *the pit is dry* while the same page had four feet of iron-coloured water in it.

*Fixed.* **The examination is at the seventh hour of the fourteenth, in daylight and not in a doorway**, which is what Sera Quill said on the eleventh she would do and costs her the reversal in a clause instead of a paragraph. **The pump is on fifty-five hours** at the seventh hour of the fifteenth, which is nineteen plus thirty-six. And the speech now says the pit **has four feet of iron water in it and is not up a quarter of an inch**, which is what the log entry always said. The second law's attribution — *I taught it to this yard in the fourteenth week of last month* — pointed at a scene that does not exist in a thirty-day month, and is now said as a thing she has said out loud four times and two people have written down.

**9. Two state files record a scene that is not in the batch.** `state/continuity.md` and `state/open-threads.md` both claimed *the collie comes up the fen road on the seventeenth and stands in the gateway and will not come in, and Gael Senn decides not to write anything about it*. *Fixed by making the prose true rather than by deleting the claim.* **The beat is now in Chapter 47 on the nineteenth**, while Renn Tallow's statement is being entered: an old black-and-white collie with the left ear off comes up the road, stands in the gateway, will not come in, and goes back up the road at about the eleventh with its tail down; Gael Senn writes in the cooperative's own book that she has decided to write nothing about the animal, **with her name on the decision and not the animal's**, and reads it back before anybody else has read it. Nobody goes out to it. **The state files are corrected to the nineteenth.**

## Quotation marks — recorded, partly fixed, not a finding against this batch

The manuscript has a systematic and mechanical defect: a speech running over two or more paragraphs opens a quotation mark at the start of the first and does not close it at the end of that paragraph, and the next paragraph opens another. **Chapters 1–11 have no instance. From Chapter 12 onward there are about 250 across 38 chapters, and they very often cancel, so a chapter total can be even while two speeches inside it are unclosed.** The Batch 0004 review recorded this and deliberately left it, and the volume-close prompt has owned it since.

**The repair pass closed every instance in Chapters 44, 45 and 46 — 33 marks — which brought those three to the reviewed style of Chapters 1–11 and left all nine chapters of this batch with an even total. Every other chapter from 12 onward still carries instances, and the counts are in `state/continuity.md` and in the volume-close prompt.** Doing the rest is the volume-close phase's work, one chapter at a time, with a count before and after each.

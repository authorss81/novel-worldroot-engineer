## 1. WHAT WAS MEASURED, AT WHAT SCOPE, AND WITH WHAT UNIT

**THE SCOPE OF EVERY FIGURE IN THIS FILE IS THE FORTY-NINE MORNINGS OF VOLUME FIFTEEN AND NOTHING BEHIND THEM, UNLESS
THE LINE SAYS OTHERWISE.** Two figures in this file are measured against the whole manuscript and both say so on the
line: gate one inside the volume, and gate one against everything behind it.

**THE PARAGRAPH UNIT, WRITTEN DOWN BEFORE THE FIRST READING WAS RUN, IS: every non-blank line of a chapter file below
its heading, with a block-quote separator line that carries no words excluded, with apparatus quote lines counted as
paragraphs of their own, and with headings excluded.** At this unit the forty-nine mornings of Volume 15 are **2,664
paragraphs**, and **1,747** of them are thirty words or more. **APPARATUS BLOCKS ARE INSIDE THE SCOPE OF BOTH GATES
AND AN APPARATUS BLOCK IS ONE PARAGRAPH PER QUOTE LINE AND NOT ONE PARAGRAPH FOR THE BLOCK.**

**THE NORMALISATION, IN FULL AND IN THE ORDER THE HOUSE GIVES IT:** numerals and spelled number words both replaced
by one token, the word *and* left alone, every hyphenated compound split on the hyphen before lookup, punctuation
dropped, case folded, every chunk a full eighteen words. **A run of number words that makes one figure is replaced by
one token, so *four hundred and forty-eight* becomes one token and *and* survives only where it stands between two
figures that are not one figure.** This reading of the clause was settled by measurement and not by preference: it is
the reading at which both of this close's self-collision controls reproduce the batch's published figures exactly and
at which the card set's own independent arithmetic for the two locked figures comes out to the figure it printed. The
alternative reading, which replaces each number word separately and leaves *and* standing inside a figure, was run and
is recorded at section seven because it produces a different volume and a worse one, and a close that published a
figure without saying which reading produced it would be publishing a number with no measure behind it.
